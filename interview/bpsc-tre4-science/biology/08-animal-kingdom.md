This chapter covers the animal kingdom from NCERT class 9 (Diversity in Living Organisms) and class 11 (Animal Kingdom) up to the B.Sc. zoology points that TRE actually asked: larval forms, metamerism, parasites like *Cimex* and *Schistosoma*, and the chick embryo's membranes. TRE asks it as one-liners: "which larva is found in Crustacea", "which mammal lays eggs", "which is a blood fluke".

> **In simple words:** Zoologists sort animals by a few body-plan questions. How organised is the body (cells only, tissues, organs)? Is it symmetrical? How many germ layers does the embryo have? Is there a body cavity (coelom)? Is the body divided into repeated segments? Is there a notochord? Answer these questions and you can place almost any animal in its phylum. Then learn two or three examples and the larva of each phylum. That is most of the exam.

## 1. Basis of classification

### Levels of organisation

| Level | Meaning | Phylum |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellular** | Cells loosely grouped; no true tissues | **Porifera** (sponges) |
| **Tissue** | Cells doing the same job form tissues | **Cnidaria**, **Ctenophora** |
| **Organ** | Tissues grouped into organs | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Organ system** | Organs work together as systems | Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

- **Digestive system**: *incomplete* (one opening, mouth also works as anus) in Cnidaria, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes; *complete* (mouth and anus) from Aschelminthes onwards.
- **Circulation**: *open* (blood flows in open spaces, the haemocoel) in Arthropoda and most Mollusca; *closed* (blood always in vessels) in Annelida, cephalopod molluscs (octopus, squid) and Chordata.

### Symmetry

| Symmetry | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Asymmetrical** | No plane divides the body into equal halves | Most sponges |
| **Radial** | Any plane through the central axis gives two equal halves | Cnidaria, Ctenophora, **adult** Echinodermata |
| **Bilateral** | Only one plane gives equal left and right halves | Platyhelminthes onwards; **larvae** of echinoderms |

> **Common mistake:** Echinoderms are *not* "bilateral animals" in the adult stage. The adult starfish is **pentamerous radial** (five parts), but its larva (bipinnaria) is **bilateral**. This shows they evolved from bilateral ancestors.

### Germ layers

- **Diploblastic**: two layers, outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm**, with a jelly-like **mesoglea** between — Cnidaria, Ctenophora.
- **Triploblastic**: three layers, with **mesoderm** in between — Platyhelminthes to Chordata.

### Coelom (body cavity)

The **coelom** is a body cavity lined on all sides by mesoderm. It lets organs move and grow freely.

| Type | Meaning | Phylum |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoelomate** | No body cavity; space filled with mesoderm cells (parenchyma) | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Pseudocoelomate** | Cavity present but mesoderm only in scattered pouches, not a complete lining | **Aschelminthes** (Nematoda) |
| **Coelomate (eucoelomate)** | True coelom fully lined by mesoderm | Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

**B.Sc. points on the coelom:**
- **Schizocoelom** — coelom forms by *splitting* of mesoderm: Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca (the **protostomes**: blastopore becomes the mouth).
- **Enterocoelom** — coelom forms from *pouches of the embryonic gut*: Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata (the **deuterostomes**: blastopore becomes the anus).
- In arthropods and molluscs the true coelom is small; the main cavity is the blood-filled **haemocoel**.

### Segmentation and metamerism

**Metamerism** (metameric segmentation) means the body is divided, outside and inside, into a series of similar segments (**metameres**), with at least some organs (nephridia, ganglia, muscles, blood vessels) **serially repeated** in each segment.

- Best example: **earthworm** (Annelida). Also found in **Arthropoda** and, in a modified form, **Chordata** (repeated muscle blocks, vertebrae, spinal nerves).
- **Pseudometamerism**: the tapeworm's proglottids look like segments but are budded off from the neck and are not true metameres.
- **Metagenesis** is a different thing: alternation of a polyp (asexual) generation and a medusa (sexual) generation, as in *Obelia*.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "In some animal groups, the body is found divided into compartments with at least some organs serially repeated. This feature is named as — (A) segmentation (B) metamerism (C) metagenesis." The textbook term for serial repetition of organs is **metamerism** (NCERT calls it "metameric segmentation"). Metagenesis is a trap. No official key is in our files; since "segmentation" is loosely used as a synonym, read the options carefully in the exam.

### Notochord

The **notochord** is a rod-like support of mesoderm on the dorsal side of the embryo. Animals with it are **chordates**; those without are **non-chordates**. In vertebrates it is replaced by the **vertebral column** in the adult.

## 2. Non-chordate phyla

### Porifera (sponges) — "pore bearers"

- Mostly **marine**, mostly asymmetrical, **cellular level**.
- **Water canal system**: water enters through tiny pores (**ostia**) into a central cavity (**spongocoel**) and leaves by the **osculum**. **Choanocytes** (collar cells) line the cavities and drive water; they also capture food. Digestion is **intracellular**.
- Skeleton of **spicules** (calcareous or siliceous) or **spongin** fibres.
- Hermaphrodite; asexual reproduction by fragmentation; **gemmules** (internal buds) in freshwater sponges; indirect development with larvae (**amphiblastula**, **parenchymula**).
- Examples: ***Sycon*** (Scypha), ***Spongilla*** (**freshwater** sponge), ***Euspongia*** (**bath sponge**), *Euplectella* (Venus's flower basket).

### Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

- Aquatic, mostly marine; **radial**, **diploblastic**, **tissue level**.
- **Cnidoblasts** (cnidocytes) on tentacles contain stinging capsules (**nematocysts**) for defence and catching prey.
- Central gastro-vascular cavity (**coelenteron**) with one opening, the **hypostome**; extracellular and intracellular digestion.
- Two body forms: **polyp** (cylindrical, sessile, e.g. *Hydra*, *Adamsia*) and **medusa** (umbrella-shaped, free-swimming, e.g. *Aurelia*). In *Obelia* both alternate — **metagenesis**.
- Corals have a skeleton of **calcium carbonate**.
- Larva: **planula** (also ephyra in *Aurelia*).
- Examples: ***Physalia*** (**Portuguese man-of-war**), ***Adamsia*** (sea anemone), ***Pennatula*** (sea pen), ***Gorgonia*** (sea fan), ***Meandrina*** (brain coral), *Hydra*, *Aurelia* (jellyfish).

### Ctenophora (comb jellies, sea walnuts)

- Exclusively **marine**, radial, diploblastic, tissue level.
- **Eight external rows of ciliated comb plates** help in swimming.
- **Bioluminescence** (giving out light) is well marked.
- Only sexual reproduction; sexes not separate; external fertilisation; **indirect development** (NCERT) through a **cydippid** larva.
- Examples: ***Pleurobrachia***, ***Ctenoplana***.

### Platyhelminthes (flatworms)

- **Dorso-ventrally flattened**; bilateral, triploblastic, **acoelomate**, **organ level**.
- Mostly **endoparasites** in animals including humans. **Hooks and suckers** for attachment; parasites absorb food directly through the body surface.
- **Flame cells** help in osmoregulation and excretion.
- Sexes not separate (hermaphrodite) in most; **exception: *Schistosoma*** (the blood fluke has separate male and female).
- *Planaria* shows great power of **regeneration**.
- Larvae of the liver fluke *Fasciola*: **miracidium → sporocyst → redia → cercaria → metacercaria** (snail is the intermediate host). Tapeworm: **onchosphere (hexacanth)** and **cysticercus** (bladder worm).
- Examples: ***Taenia*** (tapeworm), ***Fasciola*** (liver fluke), ***Schistosoma*** (blood fluke), *Planaria*.

### Aschelminthes (Nematoda, roundworms)

- **Circular in cross-section**; bilateral, triploblastic, **pseudocoelomate**, organ-system level.
- Free-living, aquatic, terrestrial, or parasitic in plants and animals.
- Complete alimentary canal with a **muscular pharynx**; excretory tube removes wastes through an excretory pore.
- **Sexes separate (dioecious)**; often **females longer than males** (sexual dimorphism); internal fertilisation; direct or indirect development.
- Examples: ***Ascaris*** (roundworm), ***Wuchereria*** (filarial worm), ***Ancylostoma*** (hookworm), ***Enterobius*** (pinworm), *Dracunculus* (guinea worm).
- **Plant nematodes**: most are **root parasites** — e.g. root-knot nematode *Meloidogyne*, cyst nematode *Heterodera*. They feed with a needle-like **stylet**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "The majority of phytophagous nematodes are — (A) root parasites (B) stem parasites (C) tissue parasites." Phytophagous means "plant-eating". Most plant-feeding nematodes attack **roots** (root-knot, cyst, lesion nematodes), so the expected answer is **(A) root parasites**. (No official key in our files.)

### Annelida (segmented worms)

- Aquatic or terrestrial; free-living or sometimes parasitic (leech).
- **Metamerically segmented** body (Latin *annulus* = little ring); bilateral, triploblastic, **true coelom** (schizocoel), organ-system level.
- Longitudinal and circular muscles help locomotion; aquatic *Nereis* has paddle-like **parapodia** for swimming.
- **Closed circulatory system**; **nephridia** (sing. nephridium) for excretion and osmoregulation; paired ganglia and a double ventral nerve cord.
- Earthworm and leech are **monoecious** (hermaphrodite); *Nereis* is **dioecious**. Larva of polychaetes (*Nereis*): **trochophore**. Earthworm and leech have direct development (no larva).
- Examples: ***Nereis***, ***Pheretima*** (earthworm), ***Hirudinaria*** (blood-sucking leech; saliva contains **hirudin**, an anticoagulant).

### Arthropoda — the largest phylum

- About **two-thirds of all named species** on Earth are arthropods (insects form the biggest group).
- **Jointed appendages** (*arthros* = joint, *poda* = feet); **chitinous exoskeleton**; body of head, thorax and abdomen.
- Bilateral, triploblastic, segmented, coelomate (body cavity mostly **haemocoel**); **open circulatory system**.
- Respiration by **gills** (prawn), **book gills** (*Limulus*), **book lungs** (scorpion, spider) or **tracheal system** (insects).
- Excretion by **Malpighian tubules** (insects); **green (antennal) glands** in crustaceans.
- Sense organs: antennae, compound and simple eyes, statocysts (balance).
- Mostly dioecious, internal fertilisation, mostly oviparous; development direct or indirect.
- **Larvae**: **nauplius** (most Crustacea; the first larva of prawns and crabs), then **zoea**, **mysis**, **megalopa** (crab); insects have caterpillar (butterfly), maggot (housefly), grub (beetle), wriggler (mosquito), and **nymph** (cockroach — incomplete metamorphosis).

| Group | Examples | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Economically important insects | ***Apis*** (honey bee), ***Bombyx*** (silkworm), ***Laccifer*** (lac insect) | Honey, silk, lac |
| Vectors | ***Anopheles***, ***Culex***, ***Aedes*** (mosquitoes) | Malaria; filaria/JE; dengue |
| Gregarious pest | ***Locusta*** (locust) | Crop damage |
| Living fossil | ***Limulus*** (king crab) | Book gills |
| Ectoparasite of man | ***Cimex*** (bed bug) | Sucks human blood at night |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "*Cimex hemipterus* parasitizes on — (A) sheep (B) man (C) elephant." *Cimex lectularius* (common bed bug) and *C. hemipterus* (tropical bed bug) are blood-sucking **ectoparasites of man** (order Hemiptera, true bugs). Expected answer **(B) man**. Bed bugs are not proven vectors of any human disease; they cause itching bites and loss of sleep.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Which type of larva is found in majority of Crustacea? — (A) Tornaria (B) Bipinnaria (C) Nauplius." **Nauplius** (crustacean). Tornaria belongs to Hemichordata and bipinnaria to starfish.

### Mollusca — second largest phylum

- Terrestrial or aquatic; bilateral, triploblastic, coelomate, organ-system level.
- Body **unsegmented**, with a distinct **head**, a muscular **foot** and a **visceral hump**, covered by a soft spongy **mantle**, above which is a **calcareous shell**.
- Space between hump and mantle = **mantle cavity**, with feather-like gills (**ctenidia**) for respiration and excretion.
- Mouth has a file-like rasping organ, the **radula** (absent in bivalves).
- Usually dioecious, oviparous, indirect development. Larvae: **trochophore** and **veliger**; freshwater mussels have a parasitic **glochidium** larva on fish gills.
- **Cephalopods** (octopus, squid, cuttlefish) have a closed circulation and the best-developed invertebrate eyes and brains.
- Examples: ***Pila*** (apple snail), ***Pinctada*** (**pearl oyster**), ***Sepia*** (cuttlefish), ***Loligo*** (squid), ***Octopus*** (devil fish), ***Aplysia*** (sea hare), ***Dentalium*** (tusk shell), ***Chaetopleura*** (chiton).

> **Common mistake:** Cuttlefish, silverfish, jellyfish, starfish, crayfish and devil fish are **not fish**. Cuttlefish and devil fish (octopus) are molluscs, silverfish an insect, jellyfish a cnidarian, starfish an echinoderm, crayfish a crustacean.

### Echinodermata (spiny-skinned)

- **Calcareous ossicles** in an endoskeleton give the spiny skin.
- **All marine**; organ-system level; triploblastic, enterocoelous coelomate.
- **Adults radial (pentamerous), larvae bilateral.**
- **Water vascular system** (unique): helps locomotion through **tube feet**, capture of food and respiration.
- Digestive system complete, mouth on the lower (ventral) side and anus on the upper (dorsal) side.
- **No excretory system.**
- Sexes separate; external fertilisation; indirect development with free-swimming larvae.

| Class | Example | Larva |
|---|---|---|
| Asteroidea (starfish) | ***Asterias*** | **Bipinnaria** → brachiolaria |
| Echinoidea (sea urchin) | ***Echinus*** | **Echinopluteus** |
| Ophiuroidea (brittle star) | ***Ophiura*** | **Ophiopluteus** |
| Holothuroidea (sea cucumber) | ***Cucumaria*** | **Auricularia** |
| Crinoidea (sea lily) | ***Antedon*** | **Doliolaria** |

### Hemichordata

- Earlier placed as a sub-phylum of Chordata; now a **separate phylum** of non-chordates (they have a short **stomochord**, not a true notochord).
- Worm-like, marine; body of **proboscis, collar and trunk**; open circulation; respiration by gills; excretion by a **proboscis gland**.
- Sexes separate; external fertilisation; indirect development with a **tornaria** larva. Tornaria looks very like the bipinnaria of starfish — evidence that hemichordates and echinoderms are related.
- Examples: ***Balanoglossus*** (tongue worm), ***Saccoglossus***.

## 4. Phylum Chordata

### Three fundamental chordate features (plus one)

1. **Notochord** (at least in the embryo).
2. **Dorsal, hollow, single nerve cord** (non-chordates have a ventral, solid, double nerve cord).
3. **Paired pharyngeal gill slits**.
4. **Post-anal tail**; heart on the **ventral** side (non-chordates: dorsal heart, if any).

### Sub-phyla

| Sub-phylum | Notochord | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Urochordata (Tunicata)** | Only in the **larval tail** | *Ascidia*, *Salpa*, *Doliolum*; *Herdmania* shows **retrogressive metamorphosis** (larva more advanced than adult) |
| **Cephalochordata** | From head to tail, **throughout life** | ***Branchiostoma*** (Amphioxus, lancelet) |
| **Vertebrata** | Embryo only; replaced by **vertebral column** | Fishes to mammals |

> **Remember:** "All vertebrates are chordates, but all chordates are not vertebrates." Urochordates and cephalochordates are the **protochordates** — marine, no backbone.

### Vertebrate classes

| Class | Key features | Heart | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyclostomata** | **Jawless**, circular sucking mouth; no scales, no paired fins; cartilaginous cranium; 6–15 pairs of gill slits; **ectoparasites on fishes**; marine but migrate to fresh water to spawn and die; larva ammocoete | 2-chambered | ***Petromyzon*** (lamprey), ***Myxine*** (hagfish) |
| **Chondrichthyes** | **Cartilaginous** endoskeleton; **ventral mouth**; gill slits separate, **no operculum**; tiny **placoid scales**; teeth are modified placoid scales; **no air bladder** (must swim to avoid sinking); males have **claspers**; internal fertilisation; many viviparous | 2-chambered | ***Scoliodon*** (dogfish), ***Pristis*** (sawfish), ***Carcharodon*** (great white shark), ***Trygon*** (stingray, poison sting), ***Torpedo*** (electric ray) |
| **Osteichthyes** | **Bony** skeleton; terminal mouth; **operculum** covers 4 pairs of gills; cycloid/ctenoid scales; **air bladder** for buoyancy; external fertilisation, mostly oviparous | 2-chambered | Marine: ***Exocoetus*** (flying fish), ***Hippocampus*** (sea horse); freshwater: ***Labeo*** (rohu), ***Catla***, ***Clarias*** (magur); aquarium: ***Betta*** (fighting fish), *Pterophyllum* (angel fish) |
| **Amphibia** | Live in water and on land; moist skin **without scales**; tympanum for ear; **cloaca**; respiration by gills, lungs and skin; cold-blooded; external fertilisation; tadpole larva | **3-chambered** (2 auricles, 1 ventricle) | ***Bufo*** (toad), ***Rana*** (frog), ***Hyla*** (tree frog), ***Salamandra***, ***Ichthyophis*** (limbless amphibian) |
| **Reptilia** | Creep or crawl; **dry cornified skin** with epidermal scales or scutes; no external ear; snakes and lizards shed skin; cold-blooded; internal fertilisation; **shelled (cleidoic) eggs**, direct development | 3-chambered; **crocodile 4-chambered** | ***Chelone*** (turtle), ***Testudo*** (tortoise), ***Chameleon***, ***Calotes*** (garden lizard), ***Hemidactylus*** (wall lizard), ***Crocodilus***, ***Alligator***; poisonous snakes ***Naja*** (cobra), ***Bungarus*** (krait), ***Vipera*** (viper) |
| **Aves** | **Feathers**, **beak**; forelimbs → wings; hind limbs have scales; **pneumatic** (air-filled) long bones; **crop** and **gizzard**; **air sacs** with lungs; **warm-blooded (homoiothermous)**; oviparous; no urinary bladder | **4-chambered** | ***Corvus*** (crow), ***Columba*** (pigeon), ***Psittacula*** (parrot), ***Struthio*** (ostrich, flightless), ***Pavo*** (peacock), ***Aptenodytes*** (penguin), ***Neophron*** (vulture) |
| **Mammalia** | **Mammary glands**; **hair** on skin; external ear (**pinna**); different types of teeth; muscular **diaphragm**; warm-blooded; mostly viviparous | **4-chambered** | See below |

> **Common mistake:** Fish heart is **two-chambered and carries only deoxygenated blood** (a "venous heart"); blood goes heart → gills → body once per round (**single circulation**). Birds and mammals have **double circulation**.

### Mammals — three groups

| Group | Reproduction | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Prototheria (Monotremata)** | **Egg-laying (oviparous)** | ***Ornithorhynchus*** (duck-billed **platypus**), ***Tachyglossus*** (spiny anteater, **echidna**) |
| **Metatheria (Marsupials)** | Young born very immature, grow in a **pouch** | ***Macropus*** (kangaroo), koala, opossum |
| **Eutheria (Placentals)** | Viviparous with a true placenta | ***Pteropus*** (flying fox), ***Camelus***, ***Macaca*** (monkey), ***Rattus***, ***Canis***, ***Felis***, ***Elephas***, ***Equus***, ***Delphinus*** (dolphin), ***Balaenoptera*** (blue whale), ***Panthera tigris***, ***Panthera leo*** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following mammals lays eggs? — (A) Echidna (B) Kangaroo (C) Loris." **Official answer: (A) Echidna.** Kangaroo is a marsupial (pouched), loris a placental primate.

**Mammal facts often asked:**
- Mammalian RBCs have **no nucleus** (camel and llama RBCs are oval but still enucleate).
- Most mammals have **7 neck (cervical) vertebrae** — giraffe and human alike. Exceptions: manatees and some sloths.
- **Bat** (*Pteropus*) — only mammal capable of true flight. **Whale and dolphin** — aquatic mammals that breathe air through lungs.
- **Blue whale** — largest animal ever; **African elephant** — largest land animal.
- Genus ***Panthera*** includes tiger, lion, leopard, jaguar and snow leopard (TRE 3.0 touched "genus Panthera"). The cat is *Felis*, the cheetah *Acinonyx*.

### Chick embryo and its extra-embryonic membranes

Reptiles, birds and mammals are **amniotes**: their embryo grows inside four membranes formed outside the embryo body.

| Membrane | Function in chick |
|---|---|
| **Amnion** | Encloses the embryo in **amniotic fluid**; protects from shock and drying |
| **Chorion** | Outermost membrane under the shell; fuses with the allantois |
| **Allantois** | **Respiration** (gas exchange through the porous shell, as the **chorio-allantoic membrane**) and **storage of nitrogenous waste** (uric acid) |
| **Yolk sac** | Encloses the yolk; **nutrition** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "The embryonic respiratory organ in chick is — (A) amnion (B) chorion (C) allantois." The standard textbook answer is **(C) allantois** — its blood vessels, pressed against the chorion under the shell, exchange O₂ and CO₂. (No official key in our files; some might argue chorion + allantois → "more than one", but zoology texts name the **allantois** as the respiratory organ.)

In mammals the chorion and allantois together form the **placenta** (see [Control, Movement and Reproduction](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/human-physiology-control-movement-and-reproduction)).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Echinoderm adult radial, larva bilateral.** Do not mark echinoderms as "bilaterally symmetrical" without reading "adult" or "larva".
- **Sponges** are the only animals at the cellular level; their digestion is intracellular.
- ***Spongilla*** is a **freshwater** sponge; most sponges are marine.
- **Ctenophores** are entirely marine and are known for **bioluminescence**; *Physalia* is a cnidarian, not a ctenophore.
- ***Schistosoma*** is a flatworm with **separate sexes** — an exception among hermaphrodite flatworms.
- **Earthworm is hermaphrodite but cross-fertilises**; *Nereis* has separate sexes.
- **Leech** has no parapodia; *Nereis* has parapodia.
- **Nauplius** = Crustacea; **trochophore** = annelids **and** molluscs; **tornaria** = hemichordates; **bipinnaria** = starfish.
- **Octopus, squid, *Sepia*** — molluscs with closed circulation; cuttlefish is not a fish.
- **Limulus** (king crab) is not a true crab — closer to spiders (book gills); called a **living fossil**.
- **Crocodile** has a **4-chambered** heart though it is a reptile.
- **Platypus and echidna lay eggs** yet are mammals (they have hair and mammary glands; milk oozes from glands without nipples).
- **Bat** flies but is a mammal; **penguin and ostrich** cannot fly but are birds; **whale and dolphin** are mammals.
- **Balanoglossus** is now a **non-chordate** (Hemichordata) — older books put it under Chordata.
- **Amphioxus** keeps the notochord throughout life; in **urochordates** it is only in the larval tail.
- **Shark** has no air bladder; bony fish has one. Shark gills have no operculum.
- **Metamerism vs metagenesis**: repeated segments vs alternation of polyp and medusa.

## Quick revision

1. Largest phylum — **Arthropoda**; second largest — **Mollusca**.
2. Choanocytes (collar cells) — **Porifera**.
3. Cnidoblasts with nematocysts — **Cnidaria**.
4. Comb plates (8 rows) — **Ctenophora**.
5. Flame cells — **Platyhelminthes**; nephridia — **Annelida**; Malpighian tubules — **insects**; green glands — **crustaceans**.
6. Acoelomate — flatworms; pseudocoelomate — roundworms; true coelom — annelids onwards.
7. Metamerism best seen in **earthworm** (Annelida).
8. Water vascular system and tube feet — **Echinodermata**; they have no excretory system.
9. Larva of most Crustacea — **nauplius**; Hemichordata — **tornaria**; starfish — **bipinnaria**; *Nereis* — **trochophore**; lamprey — **ammocoete**.
10. Portuguese man-of-war — ***Physalia*** (Cnidaria).
11. Pearl oyster — ***Pinctada***; apple snail — ***Pila***; devil fish — ***Octopus***.
12. Hirudin (anticoagulant) — leech ***Hirudinaria***.
13. Blood fluke — ***Schistosoma***; liver fluke — ***Fasciola***.
14. Filaria — ***Wuchereria***, spread by ***Culex***; pinworm — ***Enterobius***.
15. Bed bug ***Cimex*** — blood-sucking ectoparasite of **man**.
16. Notochord throughout life — ***Branchiostoma*** (Amphioxus).
17. Jawless vertebrates — **Cyclostomata** (lamprey, hagfish).
18. Cartilaginous fish have **placoid scales**, **no air bladder**, males with **claspers**.
19. Amphibian and reptile hearts — 3-chambered; **crocodile 4**.
20. Birds: pneumatic bones, air sacs, crop and gizzard, no urinary bladder.
21. Egg-laying mammals — **platypus** and **echidna** (Prototheria).
22. Chick embryo respiratory organ — **allantois** (with chorion).
23. Echinoderms and chordates are **deuterostomes** (enterocoelous).

## Practice with answers

1. Which phylum shows the cellular level of organisation? — **Porifera** (no true tissues).
2. Name a freshwater sponge. — ***Spongilla*** (most sponges are marine).
3. Alternation of polyp and medusa in *Obelia* is called? — **Metagenesis** (not metamerism).
4. Which animals are known for bioluminescence and comb plates? — **Ctenophores** (*Pleurobrachia*).
5. Why are flatworms called acoelomate? — **No body cavity** (space filled with mesoderm parenchyma).
6. Which flatworm has separate sexes? — ***Schistosoma*** (blood fluke).
7. Larval stages of *Fasciola* in order? — **Miracidium, sporocyst, redia, cercaria, metacercaria** (snail as intermediate host).
8. Organ of excretion in earthworm? — **Nephridia** (segmentally repeated).
9. Anticoagulant in leech saliva? — **Hirudin**.
10. Respiratory organs of a scorpion? — **Book lungs** (king crab has book gills).
11. Larva of prawn? — **Nauplius** (crustacean larva).
12. Rasping organ in the mouth of molluscs? — **Radula**.
13. Larva of a starfish? — **Bipinnaria** (bilateral, though the adult is radial).
14. Excretory organ of *Balanoglossus*? — **Proboscis gland**; larva **tornaria**.
15. Which chordate keeps its notochord for life? — ***Branchiostoma*** (Amphioxus).
16. Which fish lacks an air bladder? — **Cartilaginous fish** such as sharks (*Scoliodon*).
17. Which reptile has a four-chambered heart? — **Crocodile**.
18. Name two egg-laying mammals. — **Platypus** (*Ornithorhynchus*) and **echidna** (*Tachyglossus*).
19. Which extra-embryonic membrane of chick stores uric acid and helps respiration? — **Allantois**.
20. How does *Schistosoma* enter the human body? — **Cercaria larvae bore through the skin** in snail-infested fresh water.
