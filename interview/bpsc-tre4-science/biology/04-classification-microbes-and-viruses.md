This chapter covers NCERT class 9 "Diversity in Living Organisms", class 11 "The Living World" and "Biological Classification", and B.Sc. microbiology and mycology points (Gram staining chemistry, plant-pathogenic bacteria, fungal classes, viroids and prions).
TRE asks: "genus Panthera includes", "which phylum", "most plant-pathogenic bacteria are", "Albugo nutritionally is", "botulism is", "dengue is spread by", and discoverers (Ivanowsky, Diener, Prusiner, Whittaker, Woese).

> **In simple words:** Biologists sort living things into groups, from big (kingdom) to small (species), and give each species a two-part Latin name. Whittaker's five kingdoms are Monera (bacteria), Protista (one-celled eukaryotes), Fungi, Plantae and Animalia. Viruses, viroids and prions are not placed in any kingdom — they are not cells.

## 1. Taxonomy, systematics and naming

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Taxonomy** | Characterisation, identification, nomenclature (naming) and classification of organisms; term by **A. P. de Candolle** (1813) |
| **Systematics** | Taxonomy + evolutionary relationships (phylogeny); term by Linnaeus (*Systema Naturae*) |
| Taxon | Any rank or group in classification (e.g. "Mammalia", "Panthera") |
| Category | The rank itself (class, genus …) |

- **Carolus Linnaeus** (Sweden) — **father of taxonomy**; introduced **binomial nomenclature**; *Species Plantarum* (**1753**, starting point of plant names) and *Systema Naturae* (10th edition **1758**, starting point of animal names).
- Codes: **ICBN** (International Code of Botanical Nomenclature; now ICN — algae, fungi, plants), **ICZN** (zoological), ICNB (bacteria), ICVN (viruses).

### Rules of binomial nomenclature

1. Two words: **generic name + specific epithet**. *Mangifera indica* (mango), *Homo sapiens*, *Panthera leo*.
2. In Latin (or latinised), **italics** when printed; **underlined separately** when handwritten: <u>Mangifera</u> <u>indica</u>.
3. **Genus starts with a capital letter**, species with a **small letter**.
4. Author's name in short after the species, not in italics: *Mangifera indica* Linn., *Pisum sativum* L.
5. If a genus and species name are the same (*Naja naja*, *Rattus rattus*) it is called a **tautonym** — allowed in zoology, not in botany.

## 2. Taxonomic hierarchy

**Kingdom → Phylum (animals) / Division (plants) → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**
(7 obligate categories; going up, the number of common characters **decreases**; going down, it increases.)

> **Memory trick:** "**K**ing **P**hilip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup."

| Category | Human | Housefly | Mango | Wheat | Lion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kingdom | Animalia | Animalia | Plantae | Plantae | Animalia |
| Phylum / Division | Chordata | Arthropoda | Angiospermae | Angiospermae | Chordata |
| Class | Mammalia | Insecta | Dicotyledonae | Monocotyledonae | Mammalia |
| Order | Primata | Diptera | Sapindales | Poales | Carnivora |
| Family | Hominidae | Muscidae | Anacardiaceae | Poaceae | Felidae |

| Species | *sapiens* | *domestica* | *indica* | *aestivum* | *leo* |

## 3. Systems of classification

| System | Proposed by | Year | Kingdoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two kingdom | **Linnaeus** | 1758 | Plantae, Animalia |
| Three kingdom | **Ernst Haeckel** | 1866 | + **Protista** |
| Four kingdom | **H. F. Copeland** | 1956 | + **Monera** |
| **Five kingdom** | **R. H. Whittaker** | **1969** | **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia** |
| Six kingdom | Carl Woese and others | 1977 / 1990 | Monera split into Archaebacteria and Eubacteria |
| **Three domain** | **Carl Woese** | **1990** | **Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya** (based on **16S rRNA** sequences) |
| Seven kingdom | Cavalier-Smith | 1998/2004 | Includes Chromista (variant system) |

**Problems with the two-kingdom system:** it did not separate prokaryotes from eukaryotes, unicellular from multicellular, or photosynthetic (green algae) from non-photosynthetic (fungi).

**Whittaker's five criteria:** cell structure, body organisation (thallus organisation), **mode of nutrition**, reproduction, phylogenetic relationships.

| Feature | Monera | Protista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell type | **Prokaryotic** | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic | Eukaryotic |
| Cell wall | Present, non-cellulosic (peptidoglycan) | Present in some | Present (**chitin**) | Present (**cellulose**) | Absent |
| Nuclear membrane | Absent | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| Body | Cellular | Cellular (unicellular) | Multicellular / loose tissue (yeast unicellular) | Tissue / organ | Tissue / organ / organ system |
| Nutrition | Autotrophic or heterotrophic | Autotrophic or heterotrophic | **Heterotrophic** (saprophytic / parasitic) | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) | **Heterotrophic** (holozoic) |

### Three domains (Woese, 1990)

| Domain | Cell type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Archaea** | Prokaryotic | No peptidoglycan; **ether-linked branched lipids**; extremophiles; histone-like proteins; RNA polymerase similar to eukaryotes |
| **Bacteria** | Prokaryotic | Peptidoglycan wall; ester-linked lipids |
| **Eukarya** | Eukaryotic | Protists, fungi, plants, animals |

> **Exam angle:** Archaea are genetically closer to Eukarya than to Bacteria (based on rRNA and transcription machinery).

## 4. Kingdom Monera — bacteria

- Most abundant micro-organisms; found everywhere — hot springs, deserts, snow, deep oceans, and inside other organisms.
- **Shapes**:

| Shape | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Spherical | **Coccus** (cocci) | *Streptococcus* (chains), *Staphylococcus* (bunches), *Diplococcus pneumoniae* (pairs) |
| Rod | **Bacillus** (bacilli) | *E. coli*, *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*, *Lactobacillus*, *Mycobacterium* |
| Comma | **Vibrio** | *Vibrio cholerae* |
| Spiral | **Spirillum** | *Spirillum*; *Treponema pallidum* (spirochaete, syphilis) |

- Bacteria were first seen by **Leeuwenhoek (1676)**; term "bacterium" by **Ehrenberg (1829)**. **Louis Pasteur** — germ theory, pasteurisation, vaccines for rabies and anthrax. **Robert Koch** — Koch's postulates; discovered *Bacillus anthracis*, TB bacterium (1882) and cholera vibrio (1883). Father of bacteriology: Leeuwenhoek (or Koch, depending on the book).

### Gram staining (B.Sc. favourite)

- Developed by **Hans Christian Gram (Danish), 1884**.

| Step | Reagent | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Crystal violet** | Primary stain — all cells purple |
| 2 | **Gram's iodine** | **Mordant** — forms crystal violet–iodine complex |
| 3 | **Alcohol (ethanol) or acetone** | **Decolouriser** — removes stain from Gram-negative cells |
| 4 | **Safranin** | **Counterstain** — colours decolourised cells pink/red |

| Feature | Gram-positive | Gram-negative |
|---|---|---|
| Colour after staining | **Purple / violet** | **Pink / red** |
| Peptidoglycan | **Thick** (many layers) | **Thin** (one or two layers) |
| Outer membrane | Absent | **Present**, with **lipopolysaccharide (LPS = endotoxin)** |

| Penicillin sensitivity | More sensitive | Less sensitive |

### Plant-pathogenic bacteria

- **Most plant-pathogenic bacteria are rod-shaped and Gram-negative** (*Xanthomonas, Pseudomonas, Erwinia, Agrobacterium, Ralstonia*). Gram-positive exceptions: *Clavibacter*, *Streptomyces* (filamentous).
- Important diseases:

| Disease | Bacterium |
|---|---|
| **Citrus canker** | *Xanthomonas citri* (*X. axonopodis* pv. *citri*) |
| **Bacterial blight of rice** | *Xanthomonas oryzae* pv. *oryzae* |
| Black rot of crucifers | *Xanthomonas campestris* |
| **Crown gall** | *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* (natural genetic engineer — Ti plasmid) |
| Soft rot of vegetables | *Erwinia carotovora* (*Pectobacterium*) |
| Bacterial wilt of potato/tomato | *Ralstonia solanacearum* |
| Fire blight of apple and pear | *Erwinia amylovora* (first plant disease proved to be bacterial) |
| Angular leaf spot of cotton | *Xanthomonas* |
| Ring rot of potato | *Clavibacter michiganensis* (Gram +) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Most bacteria that cause plant diseases are members of the group of (A) rod-shaped and gram-positive bacteria (B) rod-shaped and gram-negative bacteria (C) filament-shaped and gram-positive bacteria …". Answer: **(B) rod-shaped and gram-negative**.

### Nutrition of bacteria

| Type | Energy source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Photoautotrophs | Sunlight | Cyanobacteria; purple and green sulphur bacteria (no O₂ released — H₂S is the donor) |
| **Chemoautotrophs** | Oxidation of inorganic substances (NH₃, NO₂⁻, H₂S, Fe²⁺) | *Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrobacter*, *Thiobacillus*, *Beggiatoa* — recycle N, P, Fe, S |
| Heterotrophs (most abundant) | Organic matter | Decomposers, parasites, symbionts |

### Reproduction in bacteria

- Mainly **binary fission**; under bad conditions some form **endospores** (resistant; *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*).
- No true sexual reproduction, but **DNA transfer** occurs:
  - **Transformation** — uptake of free DNA from surroundings (Griffith, 1928).
  - **Conjugation** — DNA transfer through a sex pilus (**Lederberg and Tatum, 1946**, *E. coli*).
  - **Transduction** — DNA transfer by a bacteriophage (**Zinder and Lederberg, 1952**).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Botulism is (A) food-borne infection (B) food-borne intoxication (C) water-borne infection …". Official answer: **(B) food-borne intoxication**. The illness is caused by eating the pre-formed **botulinum toxin** (a neurotoxin, the most toxic natural substance) made by *Clostridium botulinum* in badly canned or preserved food — not by the bacteria growing inside us. Botulinum toxin blocks acetylcholine release → flaccid paralysis.

### Archaebacteria

- Live in the harshest places — "**extremophiles**". Cell wall **without peptidoglycan** (some have pseudomurein), membranes with ether-linked branched lipids.

| Group | Habitat | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Halophiles** | Extremely salty water (salt pans, Dead Sea) | *Halobacterium* (purple membrane, bacteriorhodopsin) |
| **Thermoacidophiles** | Hot sulphur springs, acidic and hot | *Thermoplasma*, *Sulfolobus* |
| **Methanogens** | Marshy areas, **gut (rumen) of cattle** | *Methanobacterium* — produce **methane (biogas / gobar gas)** |

### Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae)

- Photosynthetic prokaryotes with chlorophyll *a* (like plants), phycocyanin (blue) and phycoerythrin; release O₂. Gelatinous sheath; colonial or filamentous.
- *Nostoc* and *Anabaena* fix N₂ in special cells called **heterocysts**. *Anabaena azollae* lives in the fern *Azolla* (biofertiliser in paddy). *Oscillatoria*, *Spirulina* (protein-rich food supplement; it is a cyanobacterium, not a true alga).
- Form **water blooms** in polluted water (eutrophication).

### Mycoplasma

- **Smallest living cells**; **no cell wall** → pleomorphic (any shape); can live without O₂; resistant to penicillin; cause **pleuropneumonia** in cattle (PPLO = pleuropneumonia-like organisms) and plant diseases like **little leaf of brinjal**, witches' broom, sandal spike.

## 5. Kingdom Protista

All single-celled eukaryotes. Link between plants, animals and fungi. Reproduction asexual and sexual (cell fusion and zygote formation).

| Group | Key features | Examples / importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Chrysophytes** (diatoms, desmids) | Cell wall of **silica** in two overlapping shells (like a soap box); chief **producers in oceans** | Deposits = **diatomaceous earth** (used in polishing, filtration of oils and syrups) |
| **Dinoflagellates** | Marine; two flagella (one longitudinal, one transverse); stiff cellulose plates | *Gonyaulax* — **red tides** (toxins kill fish); bioluminescence (*Noctiluca*) |
| **Euglenoids** | Fresh water; **no cell wall, protein-rich pellicle**; two flagella; photosynthetic in light, heterotrophic in dark | *Euglena* (plant–animal link) |
| **Slime moulds** | Saprophytic; form **plasmodium** (aggregates); spores with true walls, dispersed by air | *Physarum*, *Dictyostelium* |
| **Protozoans** | Heterotrophic, predators or parasites; "primitive relatives of animals" | Four groups below |

| Protozoan group | Movement | Example | Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amoeboid** (Sarcodina) | Pseudopodia | *Amoeba*, *Entamoeba histolytica* | Amoebic dysentery (amoebiasis) |
| **Flagellated** (Mastigophora) | Flagella | ***Trypanosoma*** | **Sleeping sickness** (tsetse fly); *Leishmania* — kala-azar (sand fly; endemic in Bihar), *Giardia* — diarrhoea |
| **Ciliated** (Ciliophora) | Cilia | *Paramecium* (slipper animalcule; gullet; macro- and micronucleus) | — |
| **Sporozoans** | No locomotory organ; infectious spore stage | ***Plasmodium*** | **Malaria** (female *Anopheles*) |

## 6. Kingdom Fungi

- Heterotrophic, eukaryotic, mostly multicellular (**yeast is unicellular**). Body = thread-like **hyphae** forming a network called **mycelium**. Hyphae may be **coenocytic** (aseptate, multinucleate) or **septate**.
- Cell wall of **chitin** (and polysaccharides); reserve food **glycogen** and oil — never starch.
- Nutrition: **saprophytes** (dead matter), **parasites** (living hosts), **symbionts** (lichens, mycorrhiza).
- Study of fungi = **mycology**; father of Indian mycology — **E. J. Butler**; father of mycology — **P. A. Micheli**.
- Sexual cycle has three steps: **plasmogamy** (fusion of cytoplasm) → **karyogamy** (fusion of nuclei) → **meiosis** in zygote → haploid spores. In ascomycetes and basidiomycetes a **dikaryon** (n + n) stage comes between plasmogamy and karyogamy.

| Class | Mycelium | Asexual spores | Sexual spores | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Phycomycetes** ("algal fungi") | **Aseptate, coenocytic** | Zoospores (motile) or aplanospores (non-motile), endogenous in sporangium | **Zygospore** (isogamy) or oospore | ***Mucor*, *Rhizopus* (bread mould), *Albugo* (white rust of mustard)**; also *Phytophthora*, *Pythium* (now oomycetes) |
| **Ascomycetes** ("sac fungi") | Septate, branched | **Conidia** (exogenous on conidiophores) | **Ascospores** (endogenous, in sac-like **asci**, inside fruiting body **ascocarp**) | ***Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Claviceps* (ergot), *Neurospora* (pink bread mould, "*Drosophila* of plant kingdom"), yeast *Saccharomyces***, morels and truffles (edible) |
| **Basidiomycetes** ("club fungi") | Septate, branched | Usually absent; fragmentation common | **Basidiospores** (exogenous, on club-shaped **basidium** in **basidiocarp**) — no sex organs; plasmogamy by fusion of two hyphae | ***Agaricus* (mushroom)**, ***Ustilago* (smut)**, ***Puccinia* (rust of wheat)**, bracket fungi, puffballs |
| **Deuteromycetes** ("fungi imperfecti") | Septate, branched | **Conidia only** | **Unknown** (only asexual phase known; when found, moved to Asco/Basidio) | ***Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma*** (biocontrol) |

> **Memory trick:** "**P**hyco = **P**lain (no septa)". "**A**sco = s**A**c". "**B**asidio = **B**at/club". "**D**eutero = **D**on't know sexual stage."

### *Albugo* (white rust) — B.Sc. point asked in TRE 1.0

- *Albugo candida* causes **white rust of crucifers** (mustard, radish, cabbage) — white blister-like pustules on leaves and stems; swelling (hypertrophy) of floral parts ("stag-head").
- **Obligate parasite** — can grow only on a living host; cannot be cultured on artificial media. Intercellular mycelium with knob-like **haustoria** that enter host cells.
- Asexual: sporangia in chains (basipetal) → zoospores (biflagellate). Sexual: **oogamous** — antheridium and oogonium → **oospore**.
- Other obligate parasites: **rusts (*Puccinia*)**, **powdery mildews (*Erysiphe*)**, downy mildews (*Peronospora*, *Sclerospora*). Facultative saprophytes/parasites: *Phytophthora*, *Pythium*.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Nutritionally *Albugo* is (A) saprophyte (B) facultative saprophyte (C) obligate parasite …". Answer: **(C) obligate parasite**.

### Useful fungi and symbioses

- **Yeast** (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) — bread, beer, wine (alcoholic fermentation); baker's yeast.
- ***Penicillium notatum*** — penicillin (**Alexander Fleming, 1928**; first antibiotic; Florey and Chain made it usable). *Penicillium roquefortii* and *P. camembertii* — cheese ripening.
- *Aspergillus niger* — citric acid; *Trichoderma polysporum* — **cyclosporin A** (immunosuppressant); *Monascus purpureus* — **statins** (cholesterol-lowering).
- **Lichens** — symbiosis between an **alga** (phycobiont: food maker) and a **fungus** (mycobiont: water, shelter). **Pollution indicators** — they do not grow in SO₂-polluted areas. Pioneers of succession on bare rock. Reindeer moss (*Cladonia*) is a lichen; litmus from *Roccella*.
- **Mycorrhiza** — fungus + higher plant roots; helps absorb **phosphorus** and water (e.g. *Glomus*; in *Pinus* mycorrhiza is essential).

## 7. Viruses

- **Dmitri Ivanowsky (1892)** — found that the **tobacco mosaic** disease agent passed through bacteria-proof filters.
- **M. W. Beijerinck (1898)** — called it "**contagium vivum fluidum**" (infectious living fluid).
- **W. M. Stanley (1935)** — **crystallised TMV**; showed viruses can be crystallised (non-living character).
- Viruses are **obligate intracellular parasites**; inert outside a host; have **either DNA or RNA, never both**; protein coat = **capsid** (made of **capsomeres**); some have a lipid **envelope** (HIV, influenza).
- "Living" features: reproduce inside host, mutate, have genetic material. "Non-living" features: can be crystallised, no metabolism or cell structure outside host. So called "**link between living and non-living**".
- **Virus classification by genome:**

| Genome | Examples |
|---|---|
| **ssRNA** | **TMV** (most plant viruses), poliovirus, influenza, measles, **dengue**, rabies, **SARS-CoV-2** |
| RNA retrovirus (ss RNA → DNA by reverse transcriptase) | **HIV** |
| **dsDNA** | **Bacteriophage T2, T4**, herpes, smallpox (variola), adenovirus, cauliflower mosaic virus |
| ssDNA | φX174 phage |
| dsRNA | Rotavirus, wound tumour virus, reovirus |

- **Plant viruses**: usually **ssRNA**; symptoms — **mosaic**, leaf rolling and curling, yellowing, vein clearing, dwarfing, stunting. TMV is rod-shaped (helical); bacteriophage T4 has a polyhedral head and a tail (complex symmetry).
- **Bacteriophages** — viruses that infect bacteria; discovered by **Twort (1915)** and **d'Hérelle (1917)**. Lytic cycle (bursts cell) and lysogenic cycle (phage DNA integrated as prophage).

| Human viral disease | Vector / spread |
|---|---|
| **Dengue** | **Aedes aegypti** (and *Aedes albopictus*) — day-biting, breeds in clean stored water |
| **Chikungunya**, Zika, yellow fever | *Aedes* |
| **Japanese encephalitis** | *Culex* (pigs are amplifying hosts; occurs in Bihar — Muzaffarpur, Gaya) |
| Rabies | Bite of infected dog (hydrophobia) |
| Polio | Faeco-oral |
| AIDS | Sexual contact, blood, mother to child; HIV attacks helper T-cells (CD4) |
| Common cold, influenza, measles, mumps, chickenpox, COVID-19 | Droplets / air |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The dengue fever is caused by the bite of which of the following mosquitoes? (A) Culex (B) Aedes (C) Anopheles …". Official answer: **(B) Aedes**. (Anopheles → malaria; Culex → filaria and Japanese encephalitis.)

### Viroids, prions and satellites

| Agent | Discovered by | What it is | Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Viroid** | **T. O. Diener (1971)** | **Free, small, circular ssRNA without a protein coat**; RNA of low molecular weight; smaller than viruses | **Potato spindle tuber disease** (first known); citrus exocortis, coconut cadang-cadang |
| **Prion** | **Stanley Prusiner (1982)** — Nobel 1997 | **Abnormally folded protein** (no nucleic acid); converts normal protein into the misfolded form | **Mad cow disease (BSE)** in cattle; **Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease (CJD)** and **kuru** in humans; **scrapie** in sheep |
| Satellite virus / satellite RNA | — | Needs a helper virus to multiply | Tobacco necrosis satellite virus |
| Virusoid | — | Circular RNA inside a virus capsid | — |

> **Memory trick:** Vir**o**id = **o**nly RNA (no coat). Pri**o**n = **o**nly protein (no nucleic acid).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Genus capital, species small**; both italic. Author name is not italic.
- **Cheetah is not *Panthera*** (it is *Acinonyx*). Domestic cat is *Felis*.
- Linnaeus gave **two kingdoms**; **Whittaker** gave five (1969); **Woese** gave three domains (1990) using 16S rRNA.
- **Haeckel** proposed Protista; **Copeland** proposed Monera.
- **Gram-positive = purple (thick wall)**; Gram-negative = pink (thin wall + outer membrane). Iodine is the mordant, not the stain; safranin is the counterstain.
- **Most plant pathogenic bacteria are Gram-negative rods.**
- **Botulism = intoxication** (pre-formed toxin), not infection.
- **Cyanobacteria are prokaryotes** (Monera), not algae; *Spirulina* is a cyanobacterium.
- **Mycoplasma has no cell wall**, so penicillin does not affect it.
- **Fungal wall = chitin**, reserve = glycogen; fungi never store starch.
- **Yeast is an ascomycete** and is unicellular; **mushroom (*Agaricus*) is a basidiomycete**; *Albugo* is a phycomycete.
- **Albugo, rusts and powdery mildews are obligate parasites.**
- *Neurospora* is the "Drosophila of the plant kingdom" (used in Beadle and Tatum's one gene–one enzyme experiments).
- A virus has **DNA or RNA, never both**. **TMV = RNA**; **bacteriophage T4 = dsDNA**.
- **Viroid = RNA without coat (Diener)**; **prion = protein only (Prusiner)**.
- **Dengue — Aedes**; malaria — female *Anopheles*; filaria and JE — *Culex*.
- Kala-azar is caused by a **protozoan** (*Leishmania donovani*), spread by the **sand fly** — not a virus.

## Quick revision

1. Linnaeus — father of taxonomy; *Species Plantarum* 1753; *Systema Naturae* 10th ed. 1758.
2. Hierarchy: kingdom, phylum/division, class, order, family, genus, species.
3. Lion *Panthera leo*, tiger *P. tigris*, leopard *P. pardus* — family Felidae, order Carnivora.
4. Five kingdoms — Whittaker 1969; criteria include mode of nutrition.
5. Three domains — Woese 1990 — Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya.
6. Gram stain: crystal violet → iodine → alcohol → safranin; Gram 1884.
7. Gram +ve purple, thick peptidoglycan; Gram −ve pink, outer membrane with LPS.
8. Most plant-pathogenic bacteria: rod-shaped, Gram-negative.
9. Citrus canker — *Xanthomonas*; crown gall — *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*.
10. Methanogens in cattle gut → biogas; halophiles in salt water.
11. Heterocysts of *Nostoc*/*Anabaena* fix nitrogen.
12. Mycoplasma: smallest, no wall, PPLO.
13. Diatoms: silica walls, diatomaceous earth; *Gonyaulax*: red tides.
14. *Euglena*: pellicle, no wall; *Trypanosoma*: sleeping sickness; *Plasmodium*: malaria.
15. Fungal wall chitin; Phycomycetes aseptate; Ascomycetes ascospores; Basidiomycetes basidiospores; Deuteromycetes conidia only.
16. *Albugo candida*: white rust of mustard, obligate parasite, oospores.
17. Penicillin from *Penicillium notatum* — Fleming 1928.
18. Lichen = alga + fungus; pollution indicator (SO₂).
19. Ivanowsky 1892 (TMV); Beijerinck "contagium vivum fluidum"; Stanley crystallised TMV 1935.
20. Plant viruses mostly ssRNA; bacteriophages mostly dsDNA.
21. Viroid: Diener 1971, potato spindle tuber, RNA without coat.
22. Prion: Prusiner, misfolded protein, mad cow disease, CJD, kuru.
23. Dengue — *Aedes aegypti*; JE — *Culex*.
24. Botulism — food-borne intoxication by *Clostridium botulinum* toxin.
25. Conjugation — Lederberg and Tatum 1946; transduction — Zinder and Lederberg 1952.
