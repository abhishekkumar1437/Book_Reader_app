This chapter covers how the body is controlled (nerves and hormones), how it moves (muscles and bones) and how it reproduces. It builds from NCERT class 10 (Control and Coordination, How do Organisms Reproduce) and class 11–12 (Neural Control, Chemical Coordination, Locomotion, Human Reproduction) to B.Sc. points such as hormone chemistry, sliding filament theory and types of eggs and cleavage. TRE asked: "which hormone regulates blood calcium", "quick response to stress", "chemically insulin is a".

> **In simple words:** Nerves send fast electrical messages; hormones send slow chemical messages through blood. The brain and spinal cord decide, the sense organs collect information, and the muscles act. Muscles pull on bones at joints to move us. In reproduction, the testes make sperm and the ovaries make eggs; they fuse in the oviduct and the embryo grows in the uterus, fed through the placenta.

- **Dendrites** bring impulses **towards** the cell body; the **axon** carries them **away**. The cell body has **Nissl's granules** (ribosome clusters).
- **Myelinated** fibres (sheath made by **Schwann cells**; in the brain by oligodendrocytes) conduct faster because the impulse **jumps from node to node** — **saltatory conduction** (up to about 100–120 m/s). Non-myelinated fibres are slower.
- Types by number of processes: **multipolar** (cerebral cortex), **bipolar** (retina), **unipolar** (embryo).
- Mature neurons **do not divide**; the nervous tissue is supported by **neuroglia**.

### Nerve impulse

- **Resting potential** about **−70 mV** (inside negative): high K⁺ inside, high Na⁺ outside, kept by the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, using ATP).
- On stimulation, Na⁺ channels open, Na⁺ rushes in → **depolarisation** (inside becomes about +30 to +40 mV) = **action potential**. Then K⁺ moves out → **repolarisation**.
- **All-or-none** law: a stimulus below threshold gives no impulse; above it, the impulse is full size.
- **Synapse**: junction between two neurons. Usually **chemical** — the impulse releases a **neurotransmitter** (e.g. **acetylcholine**, dopamine, serotonin) into the synaptic cleft; **one-way** transmission. Electrical synapses are rare and faster.

### Central nervous system (CNS)

The brain lies in the **cranium**, covered by three **meninges**: outer **dura mater**, middle **arachnoid**, inner **pia mater**. **Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)** cushions it.

| Part | Region | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cerebrum** | Forebrain; largest part (two hemispheres joined by **corpus callosum**) | Thinking, memory, intelligence, voluntary actions, speech, sensation |
| **Thalamus** | Forebrain | Relay centre for sensory and motor signals |
| **Hypothalamus** | Forebrain, below thalamus | **Body temperature, hunger, thirst**, emotions; makes **releasing hormones**; links nervous and endocrine systems |
| Limbic system | Forebrain (with amygdala, hippocampus) | Emotions, motivation, memory |
| Midbrain | Between hypothalamus and pons | Corpora quadrigemina — visual and auditory reflexes |
| **Cerebellum** | Hindbrain | **Balance, posture, coordination** of voluntary movements |
| **Pons** | Hindbrain | Relay; **pneumotaxic** (breathing) centre |
| **Medulla oblongata** | Hindbrain, joins spinal cord | **Breathing, heartbeat, BP**, swallowing, coughing, sneezing, vomiting |

- **Brain stem** = midbrain + pons + medulla.
- **Peripheral nerves**: **12 pairs of cranial** nerves and **31 pairs of spinal** nerves.
- **Autonomic nervous system**: **sympathetic** (fight or flight — raises heart rate, dilates pupils, inhibits digestion) and **parasympathetic** (rest and digest — opposite effects).

### Reflex action

A quick, automatic, involuntary response controlled by the **spinal cord** (e.g. knee-jerk, pulling the hand from a hot plate). **Reflex arc**: receptor → **sensory (afferent) neuron** → spinal cord (interneuron) → **motor (efferent) neuron** → effector (muscle/gland). Conditioned reflexes (learned, e.g. mouth watering at the smell of food) were studied by **Pavlov**.

### Sense organs

**Eye:**

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Transparent front; most of the light bending |
| Iris | Coloured; controls pupil size |
| Lens | Focuses; shape changed by ciliary muscles (accommodation) |
| **Retina** | Light-sensitive layer with **rods** and **cones** |
| **Rods** | Dim light (scotopic) vision; pigment **rhodopsin** (from **vitamin A**) |
| **Cones** | Bright light and **colour** vision; three types (red, green, blue); pigment iodopsin |
| **Blind spot** | Where the optic nerve leaves; **no rods or cones** |
| **Fovea** (in yellow spot/macula) | Only cones; **sharpest** vision |

**Ear:**

| Part | Contains | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Outer ear | Pinna, auditory canal | Collects sound |
| Middle ear | Tympanic membrane; **ear ossicles** — **malleus, incus, stapes** | Amplifies vibrations; **Eustachian tube** equalises pressure with the throat |
| Inner ear (labyrinth) | **Cochlea** with **organ of Corti** | **Hearing** |
| Inner ear | **Vestibular apparatus** (semicircular canals, utricle, saccule) | **Balance** and posture |

- **Stapes** is the **smallest bone** in the body.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "In human body, which one of the following hormones regulates blood calcium and phosphate? — (A) Glucagon (B) Growth hormone (C) Parathyroid hormone." **(C) Parathyroid hormone.** PTH and calcitonin together control blood calcium; PTH raises Ca²⁺ and makes the kidney excrete phosphate.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Quick response towards stress is done by — (A) androgen (B) epinephrine (C) corticosteroid." **(B) epinephrine** (adrenaline) from the adrenal medulla — released in seconds through sympathetic nerves. Corticosteroids (cortisol) handle **long-term** stress, more slowly.

### Chemical nature of hormones — a favourite one-liner

| Class | Examples | Solubility | Receptor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peptide / polypeptide / protein hormones** | **Insulin**, glucagon, GH, TSH, FSH, LH, prolactin, **PTH**, calcitonin, **oxytocin, ADH** (9 amino acids each), hypothalamic hormones | Water-soluble | **On the cell membrane** → second messenger (**cAMP**, Ca²⁺, IP₃) |
| **Steroid hormones** (from **cholesterol**) | **Cortisol**, **aldosterone**, **testosterone**, **oestrogen**, **progesterone**; calcitriol (active vitamin D) | Lipid-soluble | **Inside the cell** (intracellular/nuclear) → change gene expression |
| **Iodothyronines** (thyroid hormones) | **Thyroxine (T₄)**, T₃ — derived from the amino acid **tyrosine** plus iodine | Lipid-soluble | Intracellular (like steroids) |
| **Amino-acid derivatives (amines)** | **Adrenaline, noradrenaline** (from tyrosine), melatonin (from tryptophan) | Water-soluble (catecholamines) | Membrane |

- **Insulin is a protein**: 51 amino acids in two chains — **A chain (21)** and **B chain (30)** — joined by **disulphide bonds**. It is made as **pro-insulin**, which has an extra **C-peptide** removed during maturation. Insulin was the **first protein whose amino-acid sequence was found** (**Frederick Sanger**, Nobel 1958). Discovered by **Banting and Best (1921)**. Human insulin made by rDNA technology was marketed by **Eli Lilly** (1982–83). See [Biotechnology](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/biotechnology-and-applied-biology).
- **Thyroxine is not a steroid** — it is an iodinated amino-acid derivative, even though it acts inside the cell like a steroid.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Chemically insulin is a — (A) protein (B) steroid (C) carbohydrate." **Official answer: (A) protein.**

> **Common mistake:** "All lipid-soluble hormones are steroids" is wrong — thyroxine is lipid-soluble but an amino-acid derivative. "All hormones from the adrenal gland are steroids" is wrong — the **cortex** makes steroids, the **medulla** makes catecholamines (adrenaline).

### Feedback control

Most hormones work by **negative feedback**: e.g. high thyroxine → hypothalamus releases less TRH → pituitary releases less TSH → less thyroxine. An example of **positive feedback**: **oxytocin** during childbirth (contractions → more oxytocin → stronger contractions).

## 3. Muscles and movement

### Types of muscle

| Type | Look | Control | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal (striated)** | Long, cylindrical, **multinucleate**, striped | **Voluntary** | Attached to bones by **tendons** |
| **Smooth (unstriated / visceral)** | Spindle-shaped, single nucleus, no stripes | **Involuntary** | Walls of gut, blood vessels, uterus, iris |
| **Cardiac** | Branched, striated, uninucleate, joined by **intercalated discs** | **Involuntary** | Heart only; never tires |

- **Sarcomere** (Z line to Z line) is the functional unit of contraction. **Thin filaments = actin** (with troponin and tropomyosin); **thick filaments = myosin**.
- **I band** (light, only actin) and **A band** (dark, myosin length).
- Nerve signal → acetylcholine → **Ca²⁺ released from sarcoplasmic reticulum** → binds **troponin** → actin sites exposed → **myosin heads** (with ATP-ase) form cross-bridges and pull actin inwards → **sarcomere shortens**. **I band and H zone shorten; A band length stays the same.**
- Proposed by **H.E. Huxley and A.F. Huxley (with colleagues), 1954**.
- **Muscle fatigue**: repeated contraction → anaerobic breakdown of glycogen → **lactic acid** builds up.
- **Red muscle fibres**: rich in **myoglobin** and mitochondria, aerobic, slow, for endurance. **White fibres**: less myoglobin, anaerobic, fast.

### Skeleton

- Adult human skeleton: **206 bones** (a newborn has about 300; many fuse). **Axial skeleton 80**, **appendicular 126**.

| Part | Bones |
|---|---|
| Skull | **22** (8 cranial + 14 facial); plus hyoid (1) and 6 ear ossicles → 29 in skull region |
| Vertebral column | **26** — cervical **7**, thoracic **12**, lumbar **5**, sacrum 1 (5 fused), coccyx 1 (4 fused) |
| Sternum | 1 |
| Ribs | **12 pairs = 24** — true ribs 1–7 (attached to sternum), false ribs 8–10 (vertebrochondral), **floating ribs** 11–12 |
| Each limb | 30 bones (arm: humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges; leg: femur, tibia, fibula, **patella**, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges) |
| Girdles | Pectoral (clavicle + scapula, each side); pelvic (two coxal bones, each from ilium, ischium, pubis fused) |

- **Longest and strongest bone**: **femur** (thigh). **Smallest**: **stapes** (ear).
- **Tendon** joins muscle to bone; **ligament** joins bone to bone.
- First vertebra = **atlas**; second = **axis**.

### Disorders

**Myasthenia gravis** (autoimmune, attacks neuromuscular junction → weakness); **muscular dystrophy** (genetic degeneration of skeletal muscle); **tetany** (low Ca²⁺); **arthritis** (inflamed joints); **osteoporosis** (low bone mass, more fractures; oestrogen fall after menopause); **gout** (uric acid crystals in joints).

## 4. Human reproduction

### Male reproductive system

- **Testes** in the **scrotum**, about **2–2.5 °C below body temperature** (needed for sperm formation). Each testis has ~250 lobules with **seminiferous tubules**.
- **Sertoli cells** nourish developing sperm; **Leydig (interstitial) cells** make **testosterone**.
- Duct path: rete testis → vasa efferentia → **epididymis** (sperm mature and are stored) → **vas deferens** → ejaculatory duct → **urethra**.
- Accessory glands: **seminal vesicles**, **prostate**, **bulbourethral (Cowper's)** — their secretions + sperm = **semen** (fructose gives energy).

### Female reproductive system

- **Ovaries** (make ova and oestrogen, progesterone) → **fallopian tube (oviduct)** — infundibulum with fimbriae, **ampulla** (site of **fertilisation**), isthmus → **uterus** (wall: perimetrium, myometrium, **endometrium**) → **cervix** → vagina.
- **Mammary glands**: milk from alveoli; colostrum in the first days contains **IgA antibodies**.

### Gametogenesis

| | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Starts | At **puberty** | In the **foetus** (before birth); oogonia stop at prophase I as primary oocytes |
| From one primary cell | **4 sperms** | **1 ovum + 2–3 polar bodies** |
| Hormones | GnRH → FSH (Sertoli) and LH (Leydig) | GnRH → FSH, LH |
| Meiosis II | Completed in testis | Completed **only after a sperm enters** the secondary oocyte |

- Mature follicle = **Graafian follicle**. At **ovulation** the **secondary oocyte** is released (not a fully mature ovum).
- Sperm: head (acrosome with enzymes + nucleus), middle piece (**mitochondria** — energy), tail. About 200–300 million sperms per ejaculation; at least 60% should have normal shape and 40% vigorous motility for normal fertility (NCERT).

### Menstrual cycle (~28 days)

| Days (approx.) | Phase | Events |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | **Menstrual phase** | Endometrium breaks down; bleeding (because no fertilisation) |
| 6–13 | **Follicular (proliferative) phase** | FSH grows follicle; **oestrogen** rebuilds endometrium |
| ~14 | **Ovulation** | **LH surge** → Graafian follicle ruptures, secondary oocyte released |
| 15–28 | **Luteal (secretory) phase** | Ruptured follicle → **corpus luteum** → **progesterone** keeps endometrium ready; if no pregnancy, corpus luteum degenerates |

- First menstruation = **menarche**; stop at about 50 years = **menopause**. Cycle occurs only in **primates**; others have an **oestrous cycle**.

### Fertilisation to birth

1. **Fertilisation** in the **ampulla** of the oviduct → **zygote** (sex fixed by the sperm: X or Y).
2. **Cleavage** (mitotic divisions) → **morula** (8–16 cells) → **blastocyst** (outer **trophoblast** + **inner cell mass**, which becomes the embryo; inner cell mass cells are **stem cells**).
3. **Implantation** of the blastocyst in the endometrium about **7 days** after fertilisation.
4. **Placenta** forms from **chorionic villi** (foetal) and uterine tissue (maternal). It supplies O₂ and food, removes CO₂ and wastes, and is an **endocrine gland**: **hCG** (detected in **pregnancy tests**), **hPL**, oestrogen, progesterone; **relaxin** comes from the ovary later in pregnancy. Connected to the foetus by the **umbilical cord**.
5. **Gestation** ~**9 months** (about 266 days from fertilisation, 280 days from the last period). Heart forms after 1 month; limbs and digits by 2 months; most organ systems by 3 months (end of first trimester).
6. **Parturition** (birth): foetal ejection reflex → **oxytocin** → strong uterine contractions (positive feedback).
7. **Lactation**: prolactin (milk production), oxytocin (milk ejection).

| Germ layer | Forms |
|---|---|
| **Ectoderm** | Epidermis of skin, hair, nails, **nervous system** (brain, spinal cord), lens of eye, enamel of teeth |

| **Endoderm** | **Lining of gut and respiratory tract**, liver, pancreas, thyroid, lungs' epithelium, urinary bladder lining |

### Reproductive health

- **Contraception**: natural (periodic abstinence, lactational amenorrhoea), barriers (condom, diaphragm), **IUDs** (Copper-T — Cu ions reduce sperm motility; hormone-releasing), **oral pills** (progestogens/oestrogen; *Saheli* — a **non-steroidal** once-a-week pill from **CDRI Lucknow**), surgical (**vasectomy** in males, **tubectomy** in females).
- **MTP** (Medical Termination of Pregnancy) Act **1971**, amended **2021**: up to **20 weeks** on one doctor's opinion; **20–24 weeks** for special categories with two doctors' opinions.
- **Amniocentesis** — checks chromosomal disorders; banned for sex determination (PCPNDT Act).
- **ART**: **IVF** (test-tube baby; first — Louise Brown, UK 1978; India's first — Durga/Kanupriya Agarwal, Kolkata 1978), **ZIFT** (zygote/early embryo up to 8 cells into fallopian tube), **IUT** (embryo of more than 8 cells into uterus), **GIFT**, **ICSI** (sperm injected into ovum), **AI/IUI** (artificial insemination).
- **STIs**: gonorrhoea, syphilis, genital herpes, chlamydiasis, trichomoniasis, hepatitis B, **HIV/AIDS**. Hepatitis B, genital herpes and HIV are **not fully curable**.

### Ageing

Ageing shows a **fall** in metabolic rate and O₂ consumption, **more catabolism than anabolism**, loss of cells and collagen elasticity, and lower hormone levels.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following characterizes aging? — (A) An increase in the consumption of oxygen (B) Increased anabolism (C) Increased metabolic activity." All three are the **opposite** of ageing, so the expected answer is **(E) None of the above**. (No official key in our files.)

## Traps and exceptions

- **ADH and oxytocin are made in the hypothalamus**, only stored and released by the posterior pituitary.
- **Calcitonin lowers**, **PTH raises** blood calcium.
- **Insulin lowers**, **glucagon raises** blood glucose; insulin is a **protein**, not a steroid.
- **Thyroxine is not a steroid** (iodinated tyrosine derivative), although it acts on intracellular receptors.
- **Adrenaline** (medulla) is a catecholamine, not a steroid; **cortisol and aldosterone** (cortex) are steroids.
- **Thyroid** is the largest endocrine gland; **liver** the largest gland overall; **pancreas** is both exocrine and endocrine.
- **Cretinism** (child) vs **myxoedema** (adult) — both hypothyroidism; **Graves'** is hyperthyroidism.
- **Diabetes insipidus** (ADH) vs **diabetes mellitus** (insulin).
- **Gigantism** before bone growth stops, **acromegaly** after.
- In muscle contraction the **A band does not shorten**.
- **Stapes** smallest bone; **femur** longest.
- Floating ribs are **11th and 12th pairs**.
- **Knee** is a hinge joint, **shoulder and hip** ball-and-socket, **atlas–axis** pivot.
- **Oogenesis begins before birth**; meiosis II completes only after sperm entry.
- Fertilisation occurs in the **ampulla** of the oviduct, not in the uterus.
- **hCG** (placenta) is detected in pregnancy kits, not LH.
- **Saheli** is a **non-steroidal** pill.

## Quick revision

1. Resting membrane potential — about **−70 mV**.
2. Saltatory conduction in **myelinated** fibres (node of Ranvier).
3. Largest part of brain — **cerebrum**; balance — **cerebellum**.
4. Breathing and heartbeat centre — **medulla oblongata**.
5. Temperature, hunger, thirst — **hypothalamus**.
6. Cranial nerves — **12 pairs**; spinal — **31 pairs**.
7. Rods — dim light, rhodopsin (vitamin A); cones — colour.
8. Organ of Corti — **cochlea**; balance — **vestibular apparatus**.
9. **PTH** raises blood calcium; deficiency → **tetany**.
10. **Adrenaline** — emergency hormone (quick stress response).
11. **Cortisol** — long-term stress; Cushing's (excess), Addison's (deficiency).
12. Insulin — **protein**, 51 amino acids, β-cells.
13. Steroid hormones — cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone.
14. Melatonin — pineal; biological clock.
15. Thymus — **T-cell** maturation.
16. Sarcomere — functional unit of muscle; Ca²⁺ binds **troponin**.
17. Lactic acid → **muscle fatigue**.
18. Adult bones **206**; vertebrae **26** (33 in child before fusion).
19. Fertilisation — **ampulla**; implantation ~**7 days**.
20. Ovulation ~day **14**, caused by **LH surge**.
21. Corpus luteum secretes **progesterone**.
22. Bird egg — **telolecithal**, **meroblastic discoidal** cleavage.
23. Lens and nervous system from **ectoderm**; heart and kidney from **mesoderm**.

## Practice with answers

1. Which part of the neuron carries the impulse away from the cell body? — **Axon**.
2. Which ion rushes in during depolarisation? — **Na⁺** (sodium).
3. Which part of the brain controls posture and balance? — **Cerebellum**.
4. Which cells of the retina give colour vision? — **Cones**.
5. Which tube connects the middle ear with the throat? — **Eustachian tube**.
6. Which gland is called the "master gland"? — **Pituitary** (though it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus).
7. Deficiency of thyroxine in children causes? — **Cretinism**.
8. Which hormone lowers blood calcium? — **Calcitonin** (from thyroid).
9. Which hormone is released in seconds during fear and danger? — **Adrenaline (epinephrine)**.
10. Is aldosterone a steroid or a protein? — **Steroid** (mineralocorticoid from adrenal cortex).
11. Number of amino acids in insulin? — **51** (A chain 21 + B chain 30).
12. Which ion triggers muscle contraction by binding troponin? — **Ca²⁺**.
13. Which band does not change length during contraction? — **A band**.
14. Joint between atlas and axis? — **Pivot joint**.
15. Which cells of the testis make testosterone? — **Leydig (interstitial) cells**.
16. What is released from the ovary at ovulation? — **Secondary oocyte**.
17. Which hormone is detected in a pregnancy test kit? — **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin).
18. Type of cleavage in a hen's egg? — **Meroblastic discoidal**.
19. Which germ layer forms the nervous system? — **Ectoderm**.
20. Which extra-embryonic membrane stores uric acid in the chick? — **Allantois**.
