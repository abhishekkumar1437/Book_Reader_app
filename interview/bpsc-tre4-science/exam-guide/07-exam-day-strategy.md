On exam day you will not learn anything new. What you *can* control is time, order, the decision to answer or skip, and the OMR sheet. Candidates with the same knowledge often differ by 5–8 marks only because of these. Practise this plan in every full mock from Day 61 so that it becomes automatic.

> **In simple words:** Do the easy language part quickly and safely, then science in two passes (sure answers first, 50–50s later), GS in between, and keep the last 15 minutes for checking that every row of the OMR has **exactly one** bubble, with **E** for every skip.

## 1. The 150-minute Mains plan

<div class="formula">

| Clock (minutes) | Block | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Check booklet (series, page count, no missing pages); fill roll number and **booklet series** on the OMR; read the cover instructions, especially the **D and E rules** | Zero OMR errors |
| 5–17 | **Part I Language** (30 Q) — sure answers only; E for any doubt | 20+ sure answers; safely above 9 net |
| 17–77 | **Part III Science, pass 1** (80 Q) — answer every question you know; mark 50–50s with "?" in the booklet; skip the rest for now | 55–65 answered |
| 77–107 | **Part II GS** (40 Q) — one pass; maths questions last within GS | 30–34 answered |
| 107–127 | **Science, pass 2** — return to "?" questions; guess if two options remain; E otherwise | 5–10 more answered |
| 127–142 | **OMR check** — every row from 1 to 150 has exactly one bubble; darken E on every empty row | No blank rows |
| 142–150 | Buffer | Calm finish |

</div>

Why this order:

- **Language first** — it is quick, it warms you up, and it secures the qualifying 30% before fatigue.
- **Science next, while your mind is fresh** — 80 of your 120 merit marks.
- **GS after science** — gives the science "?" questions time to settle; often the answer comes back to you while you do GS.
- **Science pass 2 after GS** — fresh eyes on the doubtful ones.

> **Exam angle:** If your mocks show you are stronger and faster in GS than in science, an alternative order is Language → GS → Science (pass 1 and 2). Choose **one** order by Day 70 and never change it in the hall.

### Inside the science part

| Order | Subject | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Biology** (Q≈126–150 in TRE 2.0 numbering) | Pure recall, fastest; about 35–40 seconds per question |
| 2 | **Physics** | Units, facts and a few small calculations |
| 3 | **Chemistry** | Counting and structure questions take longest (oxidation states, σ/π, isomers) |

You do not have to follow booklet order. Start with your strongest subject if it is different — but write the question number carefully on the OMR when you jump.

> **Common mistake:** Getting stuck on one calculation for three minutes. In pass 1, give any question a maximum of **60 seconds**. If not done, put "?" and move on.

## 2. The two-pass method

| Pass | What you do with each question |
|---|---|
| **Pass 1** | Know it → mark the OMR now. Two options left → put "?" in the booklet. No idea → put "×" in the booklet and move on. |
| **Pass 2** | "?" questions → think once more; if still two options, **guess** (expected value +1/3). "×" questions → read once more; if you can now remove two options, guess; otherwise **darken E**. |

> **Remember:** With four answer options left, the expected value of a guess is **0**; with three it is **+1/9**; with two it is **+1/3**. Full working in [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking).

## 3. When to darken E

Darken **E** ("question not attempted") when:

1. You cannot remove even one option.
2. The question is on a topic you have never studied.
3. You keep switching between three options.
4. You have an answer, but it depends on a fact you are unsure of **and** the other options are equally plausible.
5. You reach the end and any row is still empty.

Do **not** darken E when:

- You have narrowed it to two options and one of them is "More than one of the above" that you cannot confirm — choose the single content option you believe in.
- The question is a calculation you can finish in 60 seconds in pass 2.

> **Exam angle:** In Part I Language, the rule is stricter: darken E for **every** doubt, because extra marks there give you nothing once you pass 30%.

## 4. OMR care

Based on the instructions printed in TRE 1.0 and 2.0 booklets (expect the same):

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Black/blue ballpoint pen only | Carry two working pens of the same colour |
| Encode roll number and **booklet series** (A/B/C/D) | Wrong or missing series = answer sheet "will not be evaluated correctly". Do it in the first 5 minutes |
| Only one circle per question | Two bubbles = wrong = −1/3 |
| No erasure or change allowed | Decide first, darken second |
| Do not tear pages; rough work only on the given pages | Use the rough-work pages for calculations |
| OMR is sealed in a self-adhesive LDPE bag in your presence | Watch it being sealed before you leave |

Bubbling method:

1. **Bubble after each page or block of 10 questions**, not at the very end. Many candidates lose everything because time runs out before transfer.
2. Every 10 rows, check that the **question number in the booklet = row number on the OMR**. One shift can make 20 answers wrong.
3. When you skip ahead (for example, starting with biology), put your finger on the OMR row before darkening.
4. Fill the circle completely and darkly; no ticks, no half-fills.

> **Common mistake:** Leaving rows blank "to fill later" and then forgetting. Under TRE 4.0's reported rule, a fully blank row costs 1/3. That is why the plan has a 15-minute OMR check.

## 5. The 120-minute Prelims (GS screening) plan

| Clock (min) | Block |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Booklet check, roll number, series, read instructions |
| 5–80 | Pass 1: all 150 questions; answer what you know; "?" for 50–50s; "×" for blanks (about 30 seconds per question) |
| 80–100 | Pass 2: "?" questions; maths questions you skipped |
| 100–115 | OMR check; E on every empty row |
| 115–120 | Buffer |

> **Exam angle:** Prelims is screening only. Do not take wild risks to score high; aim for a **comfortably safe** score with high accuracy.

## 6. The night before and the morning

| Item | Check |
|---|---|
| Admit card (printed, two copies) | Read the reporting time and gate-closing time |
| Photo ID (original) as listed on the admit card | Aadhaar/voter ID etc. |
| Two passport photos (if asked) | Same as in the application |
| Two black/blue ballpoint pens | Test both |
| Watch | Only if allowed (most centres do not allow smartwatches) |
| Water, light food | Eat a normal breakfast; avoid heavy or new food |
| Route to the centre | Visit a day before if possible; leave with a 1-hour margin |
| Prohibited items | Phone, calculator, smartwatch, notes — leave them at home |

> **Remember:** The admit card lists the exact rules for the centre. Read it fully the day it is released, not on the morning of the exam.

## 7. The last-week routine

| Day | Science (4 h) | GS / Language (1.5 h) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| −7 | Physics question bank + your physics error log | Current affairs: last 6 months revision | One full mock (morning) |
| −6 | Chemistry question bank + error log | Bihar facts | Mock analysis |
| −5 | Biology question bank + error log | History: national movement + Bihar | Units and formula sheets |
| −4 | Last mock (same time as the exam) | — | Analyse; no new topics after this |
| −3 | Revise *Traps and exceptions* of all 37 chapters | Language: one 30-Q set | Sleep by 10:30 pm |
| −2 | Revise the *last-week revision box* of each question bank | Current affairs: last 2 months | Prepare documents and pens |
| −1 | Light: error log only (2 h) | Glance at GS notes | Walk, early sleep; no mock |
| 0 | Exam | — | Follow this chapter |

> **In simple words:** In the last week you only **revise what you already know** and train the exam routine. A new book or a new topic in the last week usually adds confusion, not marks.

## Quick revision

- Mains: 150 min — check 5, Language 12, Science pass 1 60, GS 30, Science pass 2 20, OMR check 15, buffer 8.
- Language first (secure 30%), science while fresh, GS in the middle, science pass 2 later.
- Inside science: biology (fastest) → physics → chemistry (slowest), or your strongest first.
- Pass 1: max 60 seconds per question; "?" for 50–50, "×" for blanks.
- Guess with two options left (+1/3); E with four left (0).
- E for every doubt in Language.
- Encode booklet series in the first 5 minutes.
- Bubble every page/10 questions; check row number every 10.
- Two bubbles = wrong; no erasure; blank row = −1/3.
- Prelims: 120 min — pass 1 75, pass 2 20, OMR 15.
- Last mock on Day −4; no new topics after it.
