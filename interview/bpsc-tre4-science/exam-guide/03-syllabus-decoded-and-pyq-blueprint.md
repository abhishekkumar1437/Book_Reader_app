BPSC does not print a detailed chapter list for the TRE science paper. It says the questions are of the standard expected of a graduate teacher in the subject. So we decode the syllabus from two sources: (1) the NCERT Class 9–12 science books that a Class 9–10 teacher must know, and (2) the **actual questions** BPSC asked in TRE 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0. Section 1 is the topic list. Section 2 is the blueprint — every science question of TRE 1.0 and TRE 2.0 counted by subject and by chapter of this collection.

> **In simple words:** About **60%** of the science questions come from NCERT Class 9–12 facts. About **40%** are standard B.Sc. points (coordination compounds, semiconductors, wave optics, plant families, invertebrate larvae). Every question is a one-liner. You need breadth and accuracy, not long derivations.

> **Remember:** Check the official TRE 4.0 syllabus PDF on [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in) when it is released. If it lists a topic missing below, add it to your notes under the nearest chapter.

**Level key used below:** **9–10** = NCERT Class 9–10 · **11–12** = NCERT Class 11–12 · **B.Sc.** = standard graduation topic beyond NCERT.

## 2. The PYQ blueprint

### 2.1 Method

We took the two science papers we have in full:

- **TRE 1.0** (Aug 2023), Class 9–10 "General Studies and Science", Part II Science: **Q41–120** (80 questions).
- **TRE 2.0** (8 Dec 2023, Advt. 27/2023), Class 9–10, Part III Science: **Q71–150** (80 questions), with BPSC's official final answer key.

Each question was placed in the **one** chapter of this collection that teaches it. A few questions could fit two chapters (for example, a unit question can be "units" or the topic chapter); we put them in the topic chapter. A few TRE 1.0 and TRE 2.0 questions are garbled in our copy (formulas lost), but the topic is still clear enough to count.

### 2.2 By subject

| Subject | TRE 1.0 (Q41–120) | TRE 2.0 (Q71–150) | Total | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics | 27 (Q41–67) | 27 (Q71–97) | 54 | 33.75% |
| Chemistry | 27 (Q68–94) | 28 (Q98–125) | 55 | 34.4% |

> **Exam angle:** The split is almost exactly **one-third each**. Expect about 26–28 questions per subject in TRE 4.0. You cannot "drop" any subject — a weak subject costs you a third of the paper.

## 3. Conclusions on weightage

1. **One-third per subject.** Plan equal time for Physics, Chemistry and Biology. The [90-Day Plan](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/roadmap/how-the-plan-works) does this (about 30% each).
2. **Physics:** electricity, magnetism–EMI–AC, modern physics and electronics together gave **28 of 54** questions (about 52%). Mechanics was light in TRE 1.0/2.0, but TRE 3.0 asked Class 9 mechanics, so cover it quickly but fully.
3. **Chemistry:** physical chemistry of reactions (rate, order, cells, redox) is the single biggest block in TRE 1.0; **coordination compounds and organic functional groups** dominated TRE 2.0. Learn oxidation-state and coordination-number calculation and the standard organic "name reactions and tests".
4. **Biology:** cell + biomolecules + genetics = **14 of 51**. Then come animal diversity, economic botany and diseases. Biology questions are mostly pure recall — the fastest marks in the paper if you have revised.
5. **Units questions** appear every year (dioptre, henry = Ω·s, A·m², decibel, SI unit of force). Keep a one-page units sheet.
6. **"Which is NOT"** questions are frequent (not a vector, not EM, not a greenhouse gas, not a steroid hormone, not a plant hormone, not autoimmune). See [How Questions Are Asked](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/how-questions-are-asked).
7. **Level swings between papers** (TRE 1.0 hard B.Sc. physics; TRE 2.0 easy physics but hard chemistry). Prepare both levels in every subject: NCERT first, then the B.Sc. points listed in each chapter.
