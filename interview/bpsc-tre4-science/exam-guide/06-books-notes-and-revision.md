You do not need a shelf of books. You need **one main source per topic**, read several times, plus a system that makes facts stay in memory until exam day. This chapter gives the minimum book list and the system: one-line notes, spaced revision, mocks and an error log.

> **In simple words:** This collection is your main text. Use NCERT to fill gaps and a few standard B.Sc. books only for the B.Sc. points. Write every fact you get wrong into an error log and revise it again and again. Revision, not new reading, wins this exam.

## 1. The book list

### 1.1 Rule of one source

| Need | Main source | Use other books for |
|---|---|---|
| Science teaching | Physics, Chemistry and Biology books in this collection | Diagrams and exact NCERT lines when a chapter sends you there |
| Science facts at school level | **NCERT Class 9–12** | The "base" of about 60% of questions |
| B.Sc.-level points | The B.Sc. sections of each chapter here | One standard book per subject, only for topics the PYQs touched |
| GS | BPSC CCE shelf on this app (history, polity, geography, economy, Bihar, current affairs, mental ability) + quant book | One GK book for quick facts |
| PYQs | The official question papers and answer keys on [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in) | — |

> **Common mistake:** Buying three "objective science" guides and reading none fully. They repeat the same facts with errors. Finish the collection once, then revise it — do not start a new book in the last 30 days.

### 1.2 NCERT books by subject

| Subject | Class 9 | Class 10 | Class 11 | Class 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics | *Science* (motion, force and laws of motion, gravitation, work and energy, sound) | *Science* (light — reflection and refraction, human eye and colourful world, electricity, magnetic effects of current) | *Physics* Part I and II | *Physics* Part I and II |
| Chemistry | *Science* (matter, is matter pure, atoms and molecules, structure of the atom) | *Science* (chemical reactions, acids–bases–salts, metals and non-metals, carbon compounds) | *Chemistry* Part I and II | *Chemistry* Part I and II |
| Biology | *Science* (cell, tissues; older editions also had diversity in living organisms, why do we fall ill, natural resources) | *Science* (life processes, control and coordination, reproduction, heredity and evolution, our environment) | *Biology* | *Biology* |

> **Remember:** NCERT removed some chapters in the 2023 "rationalised" editions (for example, diversity in living organisms, why do we fall ill and natural resources in Class 9; periodic classification and sources of energy in Class 10). BPSC still asks them — TRE 3.0 asked phylum, bryophyte body and genus *Panthera*. If your new NCERT lacks a topic, read the older edition (available free on the NCERT website archive) or rely on the chapter in this collection.

| Subject | Book (author) | Use only for |
|---|---|---|
| Physics | *Concepts of Physics* Vol. 1–2 (H.C. Verma) | Class 11–12 concepts and short numericals |
| Physics | *Concepts of Modern Physics* (Arthur Beiser) | Fine structure, nuclear physics, X-rays, semiconductors — the TRE 1.0 type |
| Physics | *Principles of Electronics* (V.K. Mehta) | Rectifiers, transistors, modulation |
| Physics | *Optics* (Ajoy Ghatak) | Interference, diffraction, polarisation |
| Chemistry | *Concise Inorganic Chemistry* (J.D. Lee) | Periodicity, s/p/d-block, coordination compounds |
| Chemistry | *Organic Chemistry* (Morrison and Boyd) | Mechanisms, carbocations, nucleophilicity, name reactions |
| Chemistry | *Principles of Physical Chemistry* (Puri, Sharma and Pathania) | Kinetics, photochemistry, electrochemistry, solid state |
| Biology | *Trueman's Elementary Biology* Vol. 1–2 | Class 11–12 depth with objective facts |
| Biology | A B.Sc. botany text (e.g. B.P. Pandey) and zoology text (e.g. R.L. Kotpal's invertebrate/chordate series) | Plant families, algae, fungi, invertebrate larvae and parasites |

> **Exam angle:** Do **not** read these references cover to cover. Open them only for the topics marked in [Syllabus Decoded and PYQ Blueprint](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/syllabus-decoded-and-pyq-blueprint). For example, read Beiser only for photoelectric effect, Bohr spectra, fine structure, radioactivity and nuclear reactions.

## 2. How to make notes

### 2.1 One-line, question-ready notes

Each chapter in this collection already ends with *Quick revision* and *Traps and exceptions*. Your own notes should only add what you **did not know** or **got wrong**. Write them so that each line can become an MCQ:

| Weak note (do not write) | Strong note (write) |
|---|---|
| "Inductance is related to EMI." | "Unit of inductance = henry = Ω·s = V·s/A (TRE 2.0)." |
| "Carbocations — stability depends on many factors." | "(C₆H₅)₃C⁺ > (CH₃)₃C⁺; resonance beats hyperconjugation." |
| "Renin is an enzyme." | "Renin = kidney → blood (angiotensinogen). Rennin = gastric juice (milk). Not the same." |

### 2.2 Notebook layout

Use **one notebook per subject** with these sections:

1. **Units and constants** (one page per subject).
2. **Orders and trends** (IE, radius, acidity, basicity, stability, speed of sound, conductivity).
3. **Exceptions** (one line each).
4. **Formulae** (Physics and physical chemistry) — in a box, with the units of each symbol.
5. **"X — discovered/given by — year"** tables.
6. **Error log** (Section 4) — last pages, growing backwards.

> **Memory trick:** Make the left margin 3 cm wide and write the **chapter slug** there (for example "physics/magnetism-emi-and-ac"). When you revise, you can jump back to the chapter in seconds.

## 3. Spaced revision

Memory fades fast unless you revise at growing gaps. Use the **1–3–7–21 rule**:

| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (same day) | Study the chapter; do its *Practice with answers* | Main slot |
| Day +1 | Read your notes + *Quick revision* of the chapter | 15 min |
| Day +3 | Redo the *Practice* questions without looking | 15 min |
| Day +7 (weekly review day) | Redo all practice of the week; take a 40-question test | Review day |
| Day +21 | Re-read *Traps and exceptions* + error log entries for that chapter | 20 min |
| Last 30 days | Question-bank chapters + full mocks | Phase 3 |

## 4. The error log

The error log is the single most valuable document you will make. Every wrong or guessed answer — from chapter practice, quizzes and mocks — goes into it.

| Date | Source (chapter / mock no. / Q no.) | Question in one line | My answer | Correct answer | Why I was wrong (tag) | Fix (one line) | Revised on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 05 Nov | Physics ch10 practice Q7 | Unit of inductance | ohm/second | ohm-second | **F** (formula not written) | L = e ÷ (dI/dt) → V·s/A = Ω·s | 08 Nov, 26 Nov |
| 12 Dec | Mock 2, Q131 | Renin found in | gastric juice | None of the above | **T** (trap: renin vs rennin) | Renin = kidney; rennin = stomach | 13 Dec |

**Tags** (use one letter so you can count them):

| Tag | Meaning | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| **K** | Knowledge gap — never studied it | Read the chapter section again |
| **F** | Forgot a fact/formula I had studied | Add to the revision sheet; revise at +1, +3, +7 |
| **T** | Fell for a trap (not, except, spelling, unit) | Add to *Traps* page; circle trap words in future |
| **R** | Read the question wrongly / hurried | Slow down; read each option to the end |
| **G** | Bad guess (guessed with 3–4 options left) | Follow the E rule |

> **Exam angle:** After every mock, count the tags. If **R + G** are more than a quarter of your errors, your problem is exam behaviour, not knowledge — and it can be fixed in a week.

## 5. Mock tests

| Phase | Mock type | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–60 | Chapter practice + a 40-question sectional test on each weekly review day (Quiz tab where available, or the subject question bank) | Weekly |
| Days 61–90 | **Full Mains mock**: 150 Q, 150 min (30 language + 40 GS + 80 science) | Twice a week |
| Days 61–90 | **Prelims GS mock**: 150 Q, 120 min | Once a week |
| Every mock | Analyse the same day: score, net, tags, time per part | Always |

How to take a mock properly:

1. Same time of day as the real exam (check the admit card; earlier TRE papers ran morning and afternoon shifts).
2. A printed or hand-made OMR sheet; darken E for skips; no erasing.
3. Strict timer; no phone.
4. Score with **+1 / −1/3** and compare net with your target from [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking).
5. Spend **as long analysing as you spent writing**. Every wrong and every guessed question goes into the error log.

> **Common mistake:** Taking mocks back-to-back without analysis. A mock without analysis only measures you; a mock with analysis improves you.

## 6. PYQs — how to use them

- Solve the **TRE 2.0 and TRE 3.0** Class 9–10 papers as full mocks around Days 61–70 (download from [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in) with the final answer keys).
- Solve TRE 1.0 science (Q41–120) as a "hard B.Sc." sectional test.
- Also solve the **Class 11–12 Physics/Chemistry/Biology** TRE papers if you can get them: the B.Sc.-level questions overlap with your paper.
- Tag every PYQ topic in your notes with "PYQ" — these are the facts BPSC already considers important.

## Quick revision

- One main source per topic: this collection + NCERT 9–12.
- B.Sc. references only for PYQ-marked topics (Beiser, Ghatak, V.K. Mehta, J.D. Lee, Morrison–Boyd, Puri–Sharma, Trueman).
- Old NCERT editions for topics removed in 2023 (diversity, natural resources).
- Notes: one line, question-ready, with the chapter slug in the margin.
- Spaced revision: +1, +3, +7, +21 days.
- Error log tags: K, F, T, R, G.
- Days 61–90: two full Mains mocks + one Prelims GS mock per week.
- Analyse each mock as long as you took to write it.
