Knowing a fact is one thing; recognising it inside a BPSC one-liner, with five options and a negative mark waiting, is another. This chapter shows the real shapes of TRE science questions so that nothing in the hall looks new. Every TRE 2.0 example below is quoted from the Class 9–10 paper of 8 December 2023 (Advt. 27/2023, Series A) with the **official answer** from BPSC's final answer key. TRE 1.0 examples are given for their *shape*; we have no official key for them, so any answer there is marked "answer by reasoning".

> **In simple words:** BPSC asks short, direct questions: a fact, a unit, an order, a "which is NOT", a tiny calculation, or a set of statements. Read every word, test each option separately, and do not fall for "More than one of the above" unless two options are really correct.

## 1. The answer-option pattern

Every science question in TRE 1.0, 2.0 and 3.0 looked like this:

```
(A) …
(B) …
(C) …
(D) More than one of the above
(E) None of the above
```

So the "real" content options were only A, B and C. D and E were logical options.

> **Remember:** In TRE 4.0, **E is reported to mean "question not attempted"** (the skip bubble, as in BPSC CCE). Then the paper will probably have A, B, C as content options and D as *either* "More than one of the above" *or* "None of the above", or four content options A–D. Read the instructions on the cover page of your booklet before you start, and see [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking) for the scoring.

### What the official key tells us about D and E

| Part of TRE 2.0 | Questions | Answer A | Answer B | Answer C | "More than one" (D) | "None" (E) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Physics Q71–97 | 27 | 11 | 8 | 8 | **0** | **0** |
| Chemistry Q98–125 | 28 | 14 | 6 | 8 | **0** | **0** |
| Biology Q126–150 | 25 | 9 | 7 | 7 | **0** | **2** (Q131, Q144) |
| Language + GS Q1–70 | 70 | — | — | — | 9 | 2 |

> **Exam angle:** In 80 science questions, "More than one of the above" was **never** the official answer and "None of the above" was correct only **twice**. In science, a single option is right almost every time. In language and GS, D appeared more often (nine times), because those questions are often "which of these are…" with two true items.

> **Common mistake:** Choosing "More than one of the above" as a safe-looking answer when you are unsure. It is not a hedge; it is a claim that **at least two** options are correct. If you choose it and only one is correct, you lose 1/3.

## 2. The ten question types (with real examples)

### Type 1 — Direct fact

The most common type: a one-line fact from NCERT or a standard B.Sc. book.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q71 asked: "The human eye forms the image of an object on its (A) cornea (B) retina (C) pupil". **Official answer: B.** The cornea and lens refract light; the image forms on the retina, a light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. See [Ray Optics and Optical Instruments](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/ray-optics-and-optical-instruments).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q76 asked: "The phenomenon used in optical fibre for transmission of light energy is (A) total internal reflection (B) diffraction (C) refraction". **Official answer: A.** Light inside the glass core hits the core–cladding boundary at more than the critical angle and is reflected back every time.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q133 asked: "Geocarpic fruit is (A) potato (B) groundnut (C) onion". **Official answer: B.** In groundnut the flower stalk bends into the soil after fertilisation and the fruit ripens underground ("geo" = earth, "carpic" = fruit). Potato is a stem tuber and onion is a bulb — neither is a fruit. See [Plant Morphology, Anatomy and Families](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/plant-morphology-anatomy-and-families).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q137 asked: "Sun hemp jute is obtained from (A) leaf (B) phloem (C) xylem". **Official answer: B.** Sun hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*) and jute are **bast fibres**, which come from the phloem region of the stem. See [Plant Kingdom and Economic Botany](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/plant-kingdom-and-economic-botany).

### Type 2 — Units

Asked every year. A one-page units sheet pays for itself.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q88 asked: "The unit of inductance is (A) ohm-second (B) ohm/second (C) second/ohm". **Official answer: A.** From e = L × (dI/dt), L = e ÷ (dI/dt) = volt × second ÷ ampere = (volt/ampere) × second = **ohm × second**. The henry is the same as Ω·s. See [Magnetism, EMI and AC](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/magnetism-emi-and-ac).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q87 asked: "The unit of magnetic dipole moment is (A) newton/(ampere-metre) (B) ampere/metre (C) ampere-metre²". **Official answer: C.** For a current loop, m = I × A (current × area) → A·m². Option A (N/(A·m)) is the tesla, the unit of magnetic field; option B (A/m) is the unit of magnetising field H and of magnetisation.

> **Memory trick:** Decode a unit by writing the defining formula. Most unit questions are solved in 10 seconds this way.

### Type 3 — "Which of the following is NOT …"

The word **not** flips the question. Many candidates lose marks by answering the opposite.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q81 asked: "Which of the following is not a vector quantity? (A) Speed (B) Displacement (C) Velocity". **Official answer: A.** Speed has only magnitude; displacement and velocity have magnitude and direction. See [Kinematics and Laws of Motion](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/kinematics-and-laws-of-motion).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q90 asked: "Which of the following is not an electromagnetic wave? (A) Gamma ray (B) X-ray (C) Beta ray". **Official answer: C.** Beta rays are fast electrons (particles with charge −e). Gamma rays and X-rays are photons. See [Modern Physics: Atoms and Nuclei](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/modern-physics-atoms-and-nuclei).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q128 asked: "Which of the following is not a greenhouse gas? (A) Methane (B) Carbon dioxide (C) Argon". **Official answer: C.** Argon is a monatomic noble gas; it does not absorb infrared radiation. See [Ecology, Environment and Biodiversity](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/ecology-environment-and-biodiversity).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q149 asked: "Which of the following is not an autoimmune disease? (A) Psoriasis (B) Alzheimer's disease (C) Vitiligo". **Official answer: B.** Alzheimer's is a neurodegenerative disease (loss of brain neurons with amyloid plaques), not an attack of the immune system on the body's own cells. See [Health, Disease and Immunity](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/health-disease-and-immunity).

> **Memory trick:** When you read "not", circle it in the booklet. Then mark each option **T** (is) or **F** (is not). The answer is the single **F**. Two F's → "More than one of the above".

### Type 4 — "Based on" / principle behind a law

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q80 asked: "Kepler's second law is based on (A) Newton's first law (B) Newton's second law (C) conservation of angular momentum". **Official answer: C.** Equal areas in equal times means areal velocity dA/dt = L ÷ (2m) is constant, and that is true because gravity is a central force, which exerts no torque, so angular momentum L is conserved. See [Gravitation and Properties of Matter](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/gravitation-and-properties-of-matter).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q85 asked: "Lenz's law is based on the conservation of (A) energy (B) charge (C) momentum". **Official answer: A.** The induced current opposes the change that causes it; if it helped the change, we would get electrical energy without doing work.

### Type 5 — Orders and trends

These test whether you know the exceptions, not just the general trend.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q98 asked: "The decreasing order of second ionization potential of C, N, O and F is (A) O > F > N > C (B) C > N > O > F (C) O > N > F > C". **Official answer: A.** For the *second* IE we remove an electron from the positive ion. O⁺ is 2s² 2p³ (half-filled, extra stable) → highest. F⁺ is 2p⁴, N⁺ is 2p², C⁺ is 2p¹. Values (kJ/mol, approx.): O 3388, F 3374, N 2856, C 2353. See [Periodic Table and Periodicity](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/periodic-table-and-periodicity).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q72 asked: "Soundwaves travel fastest in (A) solids (B) liquids (C) vacuum". **Official answer: A.** Speed of sound is roughly 5000+ m/s in steel, about 1500 m/s in water, about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C, and **zero** in vacuum (sound needs a medium). See [Oscillations, Waves and Sound](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/oscillations-waves-and-sound).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q106 asked: "Which of the following is the most stable carbonium ion? (A) (CH₃)₃C⁺ (B) (C₆H₅)₃C⁺ (C) CH₂=CH–CH₂⁺". **Official answer: B.** In the triphenylmethyl cation the positive charge is spread by resonance over three benzene rings. Tertiary butyl is stabilised only by hyperconjugation and induction; allyl by resonance over one double bond. See [Organic Basics: IUPAC, Isomerism and Mechanisms](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/organic-basics-iupac-isomerism-and-mechanisms).

### Type 6 — Counting and structure (oxidation state, coordination number, σ/π, nodes)

These look hard but are pure method. Learn the method once.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q118 asked: "The oxidation number of Cr in [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is (A) +3 (B) +2 (C) +1". **Official answer: A.** NH₃ is neutral (0), each Cl⁻ is −1. So x + 4(0) + 2(−1) = +1 → x = **+3**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q121 asked: "The coordination number of Co in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl is (A) 4 (B) 5 (C) 6". **Official answer: C.** Ethylenediamine (en) is bidentate (two donor N atoms): 2 × 2 = 4, plus 2 Cl inside the bracket = **6**. The Cl outside the bracket is a counter-ion and does not count. See [d-, f-Block and Coordination Compounds](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/d-f-block-and-coordination-compounds).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q103 asked: "How many σ and π bonds are in tetracyanoethylene molecule? (A) 9σ and 9π (B) 5σ and 9π (C) 9σ and 7π". **Official answer: A.** (NC)₂C=C(CN)₂: the C=C gives 1σ + 1π; four C–C single bonds give 4σ; four C≡N give 4σ + 8π. Total **9σ, 9π**. See [Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/chemical-bonding-and-molecular-structure).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q124 asked: "In which of the following species number of σ and π bonds is same? (A) (CN)₂ (B) CH₂(CN)₂ (C) XeO₄". **Official answer: C.** XeO₄ has four Xe=O bonds = 4σ + 4π. (CN)₂, N≡C–C≡N, has 3σ + 4π. CH₂(CN)₂ has 2 C–H + 2 C–C + 2 C≡N σ = 6σ and 4π.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q100 asked: "The number of nodal planes in pₓ-orbital is (A) 1 (B) 2 (C) 3". **Official answer: A.** A p orbital has one nodal plane (for pₓ it is the yz-plane). A d orbital has two. See [Atomic Structure](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/atomic-structure).

### Type 7 — Identify the compound / organism / example

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q110 asked: "Isomer of diethyl ether is (A) (CH₃)₃C–OH (B) (CH₃)₂CHOH (C) (C₂H₅)₂CHOH". **Official answer: A.** Diethyl ether is C₄H₁₀O. tert-Butyl alcohol is also C₄H₁₀O (a functional isomer). Option B is C₃H₈O and option C is C₅H₁₂O — wrong formulas. **Always check the molecular formula first.**

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q109 asked: "Markownikoff's rule is applicable to which of the following reactions? (A) HCl + CH₂=CH₂ (B) Cl₂ + CH₃CH=CH₂ (C) HBr + CH₃CH=CH₂". **Official answer: C.** The rule needs an **unsymmetrical alkene** and an **unsymmetrical reagent** (HX). Ethene is symmetrical (A); Cl₂ is symmetrical (B). See [Hydrocarbons and Functional Groups](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/hydrocarbons-and-functional-groups).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q119 asked: "Which of the following exhibits optical isomerism? (A) trans-[Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ (B) [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (C) cis-[Co(NH₃)₂(en)₂]³⁺". **Official answer: C.** A cis complex with two chelating (en) rings has no plane of symmetry, so it has non-superimposable mirror images. Trans isomers and [M(H₂O)₆] have planes of symmetry.

### Type 8 — Short numericals

TRE 2.0 had very few (Q96 on speed of sound and temperature; Q120 on electrode potentials). TRE 1.0 had many. They are always one- or two-step.

TRE 1.0 Q48 shape: "A light of 600 nm wavelength is incident on a single slit. The first minimum of the diffraction pattern is obtained at a distance of 4 mm from the centre. The distance between the screen and the slit is 2 m. What is the width of the slit?"

- First minimum: a sin θ = λ, and for small angles sin θ ≈ y/D.
- a = λD/y = (600 × 10⁻⁹ m × 2 m) ÷ (4 × 10⁻³ m) = 1200 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 4 × 10⁻³ = 3 × 10⁻⁴ m
- **a = 0.3 mm** (answer by reasoning; the options are not legible in our copy). See [Wave Optics](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/wave-optics).

TRE 1.0 Q60 shape: "An atom with proton number 84 and nucleon number 216 decays by emitting an α-particle. What is the structure of the new nucleus?" Options included "Proton number 82, Nucleon number 212".

- α = ⁴₂He → Z falls by 2, A falls by 4 → Z = 82, A = 212. **Answer by reasoning: (A).**

TRE 1.0 Q47 shape: "In an interference pattern produced by two identical slits, the intensity at the site of maxima is I. If either of the slits is closed, the intensity at the same spot is I₀. What is the relation between I and I₀? (A) I = I₀ (B) I = 2I₀ (C) I = 4I₀"

- At a maximum the amplitudes add: 2a → intensity ∝ (2a)² = 4a². One slit gives a². **Answer by reasoning: (C) I = 4I₀.**

> **Exam angle:** Numericals in an MCQ are best solved by **estimation and units**. You rarely need a calculator-level answer; one option is usually clearly right in order of magnitude.

### Type 9 — "Which statement is true / correct"

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q111 asked: "Which of the following statements is true? (A) PH₃ is less alkaline as compared to NH₃. (B) PH₃ is more alkaline as compared to NH₃. (C) PH₃ is amphoteric while NH₃ is alkaline." **Official answer: A.** P is larger than N, so its lone pair is spread over a bigger, more diffuse orbital and is less available to accept H⁺; basicity falls down group 15 (NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃). See [s- and p-Block Elements](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/s-and-p-block-elements).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q92 asked: "Internal energy of a gas in a cyclic process (A) increases (B) decreases (C) remains unchanged". **Official answer: C.** Internal energy is a **state function**; after a full cycle the gas is back in its starting state, so ΔU = 0 (heat absorbed = work done). See [Heat, Thermodynamics and Kinetic Theory](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/physics/heat-thermodynamics-and-kinetic-theory).

### Type 10 — Statement-code questions

TRE 1.0 used numbered statements with codes. The trick is to judge each statement alone, then find the code.

TRE 1.0 Q55: "Which of the following statements is/are true regarding Biot-Savart law?
(i) Magnetic field is directly proportional to the length of element.
(ii) Biot-Savart law deals with electric field.
(iii) Magnetic field is directly proportional to the current through the conductor.
Codes: (A) (i) only (B) (i) and (ii) (C) (i) and (iii) (D) More than one of the above (E) None of the above"

| Statement | Judge | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | True | dB = (μ₀/4π) × I dl sin θ / r² — dB ∝ dl |
| (ii) | False | It gives the **magnetic** field of a current element |
| (iii) | True | dB ∝ I |

Only code (C) contains exactly (i) and (iii). **Answer by reasoning: (C).** Note that (D) "More than one of the above" would be wrong here, because only one *code* is correct, even though more than one *statement* is true.

> **Common mistake:** Confusing "more than one **statement** is true" with "more than one **option** is correct". In code questions, D means more than one of the **codes A–C** is correct, which is almost never possible.

TRE 1.0 Q87: "Exposure of silver chloride to sunlight for a long duration turns it grey due to the (i) formation of silver by decomposition of silver chloride (ii) sublimation of silver chloride (iii) decomposition of chlorine gas from silver chloride (iv) oxidation of silver chloride. Codes: (A) (i) only (B) (i) and (iii) (C) (ii) and (iv) …"

- (i) True: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ in light (photolytic decomposition; the grey colour is metallic silver).
- (ii) False. (iv) False (silver ions are *reduced* to silver).
- (iii) is badly worded: chlorine gas is *released*, it is not "decomposed". So the choice is between (A) and (B).
- With two options left, a guess has expected value +1/3, so you should mark one. A strict reading of the wording supports (A). This is an **answer by reasoning, not official** — and it shows why BPSC questions must be read word by word. See [Redox, Electrochemistry and Kinetics](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/chemistry/redox-electrochemistry-and-kinetics).

## 3. Handling "More than one of the above" and "None of the above"

Use this routine on every question that has these options:

1. **Test each content option (A, B, C) independently** as True or False. Write T or F next to it in the booklet.
2. Exactly one T → mark that option.
3. Two or more T → "More than one of the above".
4. No T → "None of the above" (if it is offered as an answer option).
5. If you cannot decide one of the options, use the guessing rule from [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking): guess only if two choices remain.

### Real cases where D or E was the official answer

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q131 asked: "The proteolytic enzyme renin is found in (A) intestinal juice (B) gastric juice (C) bile juice". **Official answer: E (None of the above).** The trap is spelling: **rennin** (chymosin) is the milk-curdling enzyme of gastric juice in young mammals; **renin** is secreted by the kidney (juxtaglomerular cells) into the blood, where it acts on angiotensinogen. Renin is in none of the three juices. See [Human Physiology: Digestion to Excretion](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/human-physiology-digestion-to-excretion).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q144 asked: "Which of the following is not a plant hormone? (A) Gibberellin (B) Indole acetic acid (IAA) (C) Florigen". **Official answer: E (None of the above).** All three are treated as plant hormones: IAA is the main auxin, gibberellins promote stem elongation, and florigen is the flowering hormone made in leaves (now identified with the FT protein). Since all three *are* hormones, none of them is "not a hormone". See [Plant Physiology](/interview/bpsc-tre4-science/biology/plant-physiology).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q4 (language) asked: "Which of the following is not a part of drawing room? (A) Washing machine (B) Chimney (C) Sofa set". **Official answer: D (More than one of the above)** — both the washing machine and the chimney are not drawing-room items.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q55 (GS) asked: "In which of the following states is black soil found? (A) Karnataka (B) Gujarat (C) Uttarakhand". **Official answer: D** — black (regur) soil is found in both Karnataka and Gujarat (Deccan lava region), not in Uttarakhand.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q6 (language) asked: "November comes after the month of (A) September (B) December (C) January". **Official answer: E** — November comes after October, which is not offered.

> **Exam angle:** D and E answers appear when the question writer deliberately includes **two correct items** (GS lists) or a **spelling or definition trap** (renin/rennin). In science, if you find yourself choosing D or E, re-read the stem once more for such a trap — then commit.

## 4. Reading habits that save marks

| Habit | Example of the trap it avoids |
|---|---|
| Circle **not**, **incorrect**, **false**, **except** | Q81, Q90, Q128, Q149 above |
| Read units and powers of ten in options | "10⁻¹³ cm" vs "10⁻¹⁵ cm" for nuclear radius (TRE 1.0 Q71) — 1 fm = 10⁻¹⁵ m = 10⁻¹³ cm |
| Check molecular formula before structure | Q110 isomer of diethyl ether |
| Spot near-identical words | renin/rennin, Culex/Aedes/Anopheles, absorption/adsorption, metamerism/metagenesis |
| Inside vs outside the coordination bracket | Q121 coordination number |
| First vs second ionisation energy | Q98 |
| Count only what is asked (σ **and** π) | Q103, Q124 |
| In "based on" questions, give the deepest principle | Q80 (angular momentum), Q85 (energy) |

> **Remember:** Questions are printed in English and Hindi. If the two versions seem to differ, the cover page of the booklet (or the notice) states which version is final — read that line before the exam starts, and raise a written objection during the answer-key window if a question was wrongly translated.

## Quick revision

- Old pattern: (A)(B)(C) + (D) More than one of the above + (E) None of the above.
- TRE 4.0: E reported as "not attempted"; read the cover page.
- TRE 2.0 science key: D never correct, E correct twice (Q131 renin, Q144 florigen).
- Ten types: fact, units, NOT, based-on, order, counting, identify, numerical, true-statement, statement-code.
- Henry = Ω·s; magnetic moment = A·m²; tesla = N/(A·m).
- Kepler's 2nd law ↔ angular momentum; Lenz's law ↔ energy.
- Second IE order O > F > N > C.
- (C₆H₅)₃C⁺ is the most stable of the three carbocations.
- Markovnikov needs unsymmetrical alkene + unsymmetrical reagent.
- Oxidation state: sum of ligand charges + x = charge of the bracket.
- Coordination number counts donor atoms inside the bracket (en = 2).
- XeO₄: 4σ + 4π.
- In code questions, D means more than one *code* correct — almost never.
- Renin (kidney) ≠ rennin (gastric juice).
- Circle "not" every time.
