> **In simple words:** In TRE chemistry, about half the questions are straight NCERT facts and half are B.Sc.-flavoured one-liners (coordination compounds, mechanisms, kinetics). Almost none need long calculation. If you can answer the questions on this page in under 20 seconds each, you are ready.

## 4. Attempt strategy for chemistry under 1/3 negative marking

- **The arithmetic:** each question has four answer choices (A, B, C, D = "More than one of the above"); option E means "not attempted". A blind guess among four: expected marks = ¼ × 1 − ¾ × ⅓ = **0** — no gain, no loss on average, but high variance. If you can **eliminate one** option: ⅓ − ⅔ × ⅓ = **+1/9 per question**. Eliminate **two**: ½ − ½ × ⅓ = **+1/3**. So: never guess blind; always guess after eliminating at least one option.
- **Never leave a bubble completely blank** — under TRE 4.0 rules (as reported) leaving all five blank also costs 1/3. Darken **E** to skip.
- **Read option D carefully.** In chemistry it is often correct when two listed items share a property (both biodegradable, both amphoteric, both paramagnetic, both optically active). Before marking (A), check whether (B) or (C) is also true.
- **Order of attack in the 80-question science block:** first sweep the one-line facts (aspirin, B₁₂, CaO, picric acid) in about 10–15 seconds each; then the "orders and exceptions" questions; numericals last. Most TRE chemistry numericals (half-life, rate change, ON, CN, σ/π count) take under a minute if formulas are ready.
- **Unit and wording traps:** cm vs m (nuclear radius), "increasing" vs "decreasing" order, "not" in the stem, "second" ionisation enthalpy, "aqueous" vs "alcoholic" KOH, "ligands" vs "donor atoms".
- **Target for chemistry:** about 25–28 questions out of 80 in science are chemistry; aim for 20+ correct with at most 3–4 wrong. With 1/3 penalty, 20 right and 4 wrong = 18.67 marks.
