## What the exams test

All three exams test **quantitative aptitude**: school-level arithmetic and reasoning-with-numbers, done fast and without a calculator. The syllabus is small; the difficulty is speed and traps.

| Exam | Where maths appears | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **UPSC CSAT (Prelims Paper II)** | Basic numeracy and data interpretation, roughly 25 to 35 of 80 questions | Class 10 level; word problems, percentages, ratios, averages, DI tables and charts, sets, sequences, clocks, probability basics. Qualifying paper (33% needed), so accuracy on the easy questions matters more than reach. |
| **SSC CGL / CHSL / CPO** | Quantitative Aptitude section in Tier 1 (25 questions) and a heavier paper in Tier 2 | Arithmetic (percentage, profit and loss, interest, time and work, speed), algebra identities, geometry theorems, mensuration, trigonometry, DI. Speed-heavy, negative marking, scoring section for toppers. |
| **BPSC (Prelims and Mains GS)** | Basic numeracy inside General Studies, plus the CSAT-style paper where applicable | Class 10 arithmetic, number system, percentages, averages, ratio, simple geometry, DI, elementary reasoning. |

Always check the latest notification for the current pattern and marking; the topics above have been stable for years.

## Weightage, roughly

| Topic group | SSC | CSAT / BPSC |
|---|---|---|
| Percentages, profit and loss, interest | high | high |
| Ratio, averages, mixtures | high | high |
| Time and work, speed and distance | high | medium |
| Number system, simplification | medium | medium |
| Algebra | high (SSC style identities) | low |
| Geometry, mensuration | high | low |
| Trigonometry, heights and distances | high | very low |
| Data interpretation | medium | very high |
| Sets, Venn diagrams, sequences, clocks, calendars | low | high |
| Permutation, combination, probability | low | medium |

Chapters in this book are ordered so the highest-value shared topics come first. If you are preparing only for CSAT or BPSC, chapters 11, 13, and 14 (algebra, mensuration, trigonometry) can be skimmed; if only for SSC, chapter 18 can be skimmed.

## The method

1. **Learn the fraction and square tables first** (chapter 3). Nearly every percentage and ratio shortcut depends on knowing that 1/8 is 12.5% and 17² is 289 without thinking.
2. **For each chapter, memorise the formula box**, then do the worked examples with the solutions covered. The "hint" line under each example tells you what to notice before computing.
3. **Practise by pattern, not by count.** Every question in an exam is a known pattern with changed numbers. This book names the patterns; your job is to recognise them in under ten seconds.
4. **Time yourself from day one.** Arithmetic without a clock is a different skill from arithmetic under one. Aim for 45 to 60 seconds per easy question, 90 for a medium one.
5. **Skip early.** In the exam, a question that does not yield a method in fifteen seconds is skipped and returned to. Negative marking makes a guess worse than a blank.
6. **Keep an error log**: a line per mistake with the cause (misread, arithmetic slip, wrong formula, trap). After two weeks the log shows which of the four you are, and the fix is different for each.

## How to read a chapter

Each chapter has the same parts:

- **Where it appears**: which exam asks it and in what form.
- **Formulae**: the box to memorise, nothing extra.
- **Methods and hints**: the fast ways, with the observation that triggers each.
- **Diagrams** where the topic has a picture worth keeping in your head.
- **Worked examples**: the question, the hint, the solution, in exam style.
- **Traps**: the ways the setters make you lose marks.
- **Practice set**: eight to twelve questions with answers at the end.

Every answer in this book has been checked by calculation. Where a method gives an approximate answer (common in CSAT and DI), the text says so.

## Mental arithmetic habits worth building

- Multiply by 5 as "halve and add a zero"; by 25 as "quarter and add two zeros"; by 11 as "sum adjacent digits"; by 9 as "times 10 minus once".
- Break awkward numbers: 47 × 12 = 47 × 10 + 47 × 2 = 470 + 94 = 564.
- Percentages as fractions: 37.5% of 640 is 3/8 of 640 = 240.
- Check the last digit and the rough size of every answer against the options before moving on; a wrong option is usually off by a factor or a sign.
- Use the options: substitute them into the condition when direct solving is slow.

## Tools

Nothing beyond pen and paper is allowed in the exam, so practise with nothing else. A stopwatch app and a notebook for the error log are the entire kit.
