## Where it appears

Directly (two or three questions in every exam) and inside almost every other arithmetic topic. CSAT and DI questions are mostly percentage questions in disguise.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| x % of N | N × x ÷ 100 · use the fraction: 12.5 % = 1/8, 16.67 % = 1/6, 33.33 % = 1/3 |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 · the base is always the original |
| a as a % of b | a ÷ b × 100 |
| a is x % more than b | a = b × (1 + x/100) |
| Successive changes a %, then b % | net = a + b + ab/100 (use signs; a decrease is negative) |
| Multiplying factors | +20 % → × 1.2 · −25 % → × 0.75 |
| +x % then −x % | net −x²/100 % (always a decrease) |
| Price up x %, same spending | cut consumption by 100x ÷ (100 + x) % |
| Price down x %, same spending | consumption can rise 100x ÷ (100 − x) % |
| A is x % more than B | B is 100x ÷ (100 + x) % less than A |
| Population after n years at r % | P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ · n years ago: P ÷ (1 + r/100)ⁿ |

</div>

## Fraction thinking

Convert the percentage to a fraction, then multiply. 62.5% of 480 is 5/8 of 480 = 300. 83.33% of 720 is 5/6 of 720 = 600. For awkward percentages, split: 27% of 300 = 25% (75) + 2% (6) = 81.

**Hint:** if the numbers are chosen to be divisible, the setter intends the fraction route. 37.5% and 640 together mean "3/8 of 640".

## Percentage change and the base trap

"A's salary is 25% more than B's. B's is what percent less than A's?" The base changes. A = 1.25B, so B = A/1.25 = 0.8A, which is 20% less. Formula: 100 × 25/125 = 20.

**Example.** A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. Net change?
Net = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = −4%. (Always a loss of x²/100 = 4%.)

**Example.** Price of sugar rises 25%. By what percent must a family cut consumption to keep spending unchanged?
100 × 25/125 = 20%.

**Example.** If the length of a rectangle increases 10% and breadth decreases 10%, the area changes by?
+10 − 10 − 1 = −1%.

## Successive percentages

**Example.** A shop gives discounts of 20% and then 10%. Single equivalent discount?
20 + 10 − 200/100 = 28%. (Multipliers: 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72.)

**Example.** Population grows 10% in year one and 20% in year two. Overall?
10 + 20 + 2 = 32%.

Three changes: apply the formula twice, or multiply the factors.

## "What percent" and reverse questions

**Example.** 45 is what percent of 60? 45/60 = 3/4 = 75%.

**Example.** 30% of a number is 96. The number? 96/0.3 = 320.

**Example.** After spending 40% of his money and then 50% of the remainder, a man has ₹360 left. How much did he have?
Left = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3 of total → total = 360/0.3 = ₹1,200.

**Example.** In an election with two candidates, the winner got 60% of votes and won by 4,000 votes. Total votes?
Margin = 60% − 40% = 20% = 4000 → total 20,000.

**Example.** In an exam a student needs 40% to pass. He scores 178 and fails by 22 marks. Maximum marks?
Pass mark 200 = 40% → maximum 500.

## Population and depreciation

**Example.** A town's population is 50,000 and grows 8% a year. Population after 2 years?
50,000 × 1.08² = 50,000 × 1.1664 = 58,320.

**Example.** A machine worth ₹80,000 depreciates 10% a year. Value after 2 years?
80,000 × 0.81 = ₹64,800.

## Mixtures of percentages

**Example.** A solution of 40 litres has 25% alcohol. How much alcohol must be added to make it 40% alcohol?
Alcohol now 10 L. Let x be added: (10 + x)/(40 + x) = 0.4 → 10 + x = 16 + 0.4x → 0.6x = 6 → x = 10 L.

**Example.** 30% of students in a class are girls and 40% of girls and 50% of boys passed. What percent of the class passed?
0.3 × 0.4 + 0.7 × 0.5 = 0.12 + 0.35 = 47%.

## Traps

- Percentage of what? Always identify the base before computing; "25% more than B" has base B.
- Percentage points versus percent: going from 20% to 25% is +5 percentage points but +25%.
- Two equal successive changes in opposite directions never cancel; the net is a decrease.
- Reversing a percentage increase needs division by the factor, not subtraction of the same percentage.
- "Increased to" versus "increased by".

4. Two successive discounts of 15% and 20% equal a single discount of?

6. Price of petrol rises 12.5%. To keep expenditure fixed, consumption must fall by?
7. In an election 10% of voters did not vote, 60 votes were invalid, and the winner got 47% of the total voters and won by 308 votes... (skip; too long for exam) Instead: the winner gets 55% of valid votes and wins by 1,200 votes. Valid votes?
8. Population of a town falls 10% then rises 10%. Net change?
9. A student scores 75% in three papers of 100 marks each and 60 in the fourth of 100. Overall percentage?
10. 20 litres of a 30% acid solution: how much water must be added to make it 20%?
11. A man spends 30% on rent, 20% of the remainder on food, and saves the rest, ₹5,600. His income?
12. If 40% of x = 60% of y, then x : y = ?

**Answers**

1. 1/12 of 1440 = 120.
2. 828/1.15 = 720.
3. B = 2.5A → 150% more.
4. 15 + 20 − 3 = 32%.
5. A = 0.8B → B = 1.25A → 25% more.
6. 100 × 12.5/112.5 = 11.11%.
7. Margin = 55% − 45% = 10% = 1200 → 12,000 valid votes.
8. −1%.
9. (225 + 60)/400 = 71.25%.

12. 0.4x = 0.6y → x/y = 3/2 → 3 : 2.
