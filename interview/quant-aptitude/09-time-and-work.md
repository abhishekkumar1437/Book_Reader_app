## Where it appears

Every exam, one to three questions. The variants are limited: combined work, one leaves midway, alternate days, efficiency ratios, wages in proportion to work, and pipes with an outlet.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| LCM method | total work = LCM of the times · rate = total ÷ own time · time = total ÷ combined rate |
| A in a days, B in b days, together | ab ÷ (a + b) days |
| Efficiency | ∝ 1 ÷ time · A is k times as efficient ⇒ A takes 1/k of the time |
| Men, days, hours | M₁D₁H₁ ÷ W₁ = M₂D₂H₂ ÷ W₂ |
| Wages | split in the ratio of work done = rate × days worked |
| Pipes | inlet +rate, outlet −rate · tank fills when the net rate is positive |

</div>

## The LCM method, step by step

**Example.** A does a job in 12 days, B in 15 days. Together?
Total = LCM(12, 15) = 60 units. A does 5/day, B 4/day, together 9/day → 60/9 = 6⅔ days.

**Example.** A and B together take 8 days; A alone 12. B alone?
Total 24. Together 3/day, A 2/day → B 1/day → 24 days.

**Example.** A can do it in 10 days, B in 15. They start together; A leaves after 3 days. Total time?
Total 30. A 3/day, B 2/day. First 3 days: 15 done. Remaining 15 by B at 2/day = 7.5 days. Total 10.5 days.

**Example.** A, B, C take 20, 30, 60 days. A works every day; B and C help him on alternate days (B on day 1, C on day 2, ...). Days to finish?
Total 60. A 3, B 2, C 1. Two-day block: (3 + 2) + (3 + 1) = 9. Six blocks = 54 in 12 days; remaining 6; day 13: A + B = 5 → 59; day 14: A + C = 4 → done partway. So a little over 13 days; exactly 13 + 1/4 = 13.25 days.

**Example.** A is twice as efficient as B and together they finish in 12 days. A alone?
Rates 2 : 1, together 3 units/day finishing 36 units. A alone: 36/2 = 18 days.

**Example.** 12 men do a job in 20 days. After 8 days, 4 men leave. Total days?
Work = 12 × 20 = 240 man-days. 8 days: 96 done. Remaining 144 by 8 men = 18 days. Total 26.

**Example.** 6 men or 10 women complete a job in 15 days. How long for 4 men and 5 women?
1 man = 10/6 women. 4 men + 5 women = 4 × 10/6 + 5 = 6.67 + 5 = 11.67 women. Time = 10 × 15/11.67 = 12.86 days. Cleaner with LCM: total = 15 × 6 = 90 man-days = 150 woman-days; a man is 1 unit/day and a woman 0.6; 4 + 3 = 7 units/day → 90/7 = 12.86 days.

## Wages

**Example.** A, B take 6 and 8 days; with C they finish in 3 days. They earn ₹3,200. C's share?
Total 24. A 4, B 3, together with C 8/day → C 1/day. Shares in ratio of rates 4 : 3 : 1 → C = 1/8 × 3200 = ₹400.

## Pipes and cisterns

**Example.** Pipe A fills a tank in 6 h, B in 9 h, and outlet C empties it in 18 h. All open: time to fill?
Total 18. A 3, B 2, C −1 → net 4/h → 4.5 hours.

**Example.** Two pipes fill in 10 h and 15 h. Both are opened, but after 3 h the first is closed. Total?
Total 30; rates 3 and 2. 3 h: 15 done. Remaining 15 at 2/h = 7.5 h. Total 10.5 h.

**Example.** A leak empties a full tank in 20 h. An inlet at 5 L/min fills it in 30 h despite the leak. Capacity?
Net rate 1/30 = 1/x − 1/20 → 1/x = 1/30 + 1/20 = 5/60 = 1/12 → inlet fills in 12 h = 720 min × 5 = 3,600 L.

## Traps

- "A leaves 3 days before completion" means B works alone for the last 3 days: count from the end.
- Alternate-day problems: find the two-day block, then handle the remainder day by day, with the right person on the last day.
- Efficiency ratio is inverse to time ratio.
- Outlets subtract; a tank that is "half full" needs only half the total units.

## Practice set

1. A in 15 days, B in 10; together?
2. A + B in 12 days, B + C in 15, C + A in 20. All three together?
3. A does a job in 18 days; B is 50% more efficient. B alone?
4. A and B in 8 days; B alone in 12. A alone?
5. 20 men in 30 days; after 10 days, 10 more men join. Remaining days?
6. A takes 12 days, B 16; they work on alternate days starting with A. Total time?
7. A, B, C in 10, 12, 15 days. A leaves after 2 days, B leaves 3 days before the end. Total time?
8. Two pipes fill in 12 min and 16 min; an outlet empties in 8 min. All open on an empty tank: time to fill?
9. A tap fills in 4 h; with a leak it takes 5 h. The leak alone empties in?
10. 4 men and 6 women do a job in 8 days; 3 men and 7 women in 10 days. How long would 10 women take?
11. A and B earn ₹1,500 for a job A alone does in 10 days and B in 15. A's share?
12. A cistern is 2/5 full. A pipe fills it in 10 min and a drain empties it in 6 min. With both open, in how long is it empty?

**Answers**

1. 30 units; 2 + 3 = 5/day → 6 days.
2. Total 60: A+B 5, B+C 4, C+A 3 → 2(A+B+C) = 12 → 6/day → 10 days.
3. Rates 2 : 3 → B takes 18 × 2/3 = 12 days.
4. Total 24: together 3, B 2 → A 1 → 24 days.
5. 600 man-days; 200 done; 400 by 30 men = 13⅓ days.
6. Total 48: A 4, B 3; two-day block 7; 6 blocks = 42 in 12 days; day 13 A does 4 → 46; day 14 B needs 2 of his 3 → 2/3 day. Total 13⅔ days.
7. Total 60: A 6, B 5, C 4. Let total time T; A works 2 days (12), C works T days (4T), B works T − 3 days (5T − 15): 12 + 4T + 5T − 15 = 60 → 9T = 63 → T = 7 days.
8. Total 48: 4 + 3 − 6 = 1/min → 48 min.
9. 1/4 − 1/5 = 1/20 → 20 h.
10. 8(4m + 6w) = 10(3m + 7w) → 32m + 48w = 30m + 70w → 2m = 22w → 1 man = 11 women. Work = 8 × (44 + 6) = 400 woman-days → 10 women take 40 days.
11. Rates 3 : 2 → A gets 3/5 × 1500 = ₹900.
12. Net emptying rate: total 30 units; fill 3/min, drain 5/min → −2/min; 2/5 of 30 = 12 units → 6 min.
