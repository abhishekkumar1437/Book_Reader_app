## The plan in one paragraph

Six weeks at about ninety minutes a day. Weeks one and two build the arithmetic core, which is most of the marks in every exam. Week three is the SSC-specific block (algebra, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry); CSAT-only and BPSC-only candidates use the week for extra DI and reasoning instead. Week four is data interpretation and reasoning-with-numbers. Weeks five and six are timed practice, mocks, and revision from the error log. Repeat weeks five and six as long as time allows; the score comes from timed practice, not from reading.

## Weeks 5 and 6: Timed practice

- Alternate days: one full previous-year paper (the maths portion) under exam timing, then a review day that redoes every wrong or slow question and updates the error log.
- Keep a running tally: accuracy (correct/attempted) and coverage (attempted/total). Raise coverage only after accuracy is above 90%; with negative marking, an extra guess costs more than it earns.
- On review days, re-read the chapter for any topic with two or more errors in the week.
- The last three days: Formula Sheet, error log, one light paper, sleep.

## Practising for speed

1. **Cover the solution, set a timer, write the answer**, then compare. Reading a solution feels like learning and is not.
2. **Batches of ten** from one chapter at first (recognition), then mixed batches (retrieval), then full papers (stamina).
3. **Track seconds per question.** Easy arithmetic should reach 45 seconds; DI sets about 90 seconds per question.
4. **Do mental arithmetic daily** for five minutes: squares, fraction-percent conversions, two-digit multiplication.
5. **Mark, do not erase.** Circle a wrong step rather than rubbing it out; the error log needs to see what happened.

## The error log

One line per mistake:

```
Day 9   Time & work   Read "leaves 3 days before end" as "after 3 days"    → misread
Day 9   Percentages   Base wrong in "B is what % less than A"              → trap
Day 11  Simplification  47 × 12 computed as 554                           → arithmetic slip
Day 11  CI            Used yearly rate for half-yearly compounding          → wrong formula
```

After two weeks, count the four categories. Misreads: slow down on the first read and underline the question's ask. Traps: re-read the chapter's trap list. Arithmetic slips: more daily mental arithmetic and a habit of checking the last digit. Wrong formula: rewrite the formula sheet by hand.

## Exam-hall strategy

**CSAT.** The paper is qualifying: you need 33%, so the goal is a safe margin, not a maximum. Do the comprehension passages and the easy numeracy first, skip any question that needs more than two minutes, and come back. Never guess with negative marking unless you can eliminate two options. Attempt DI sets fully once you start them; the reading cost is paid up front.

**SSC.** Quant is a scoring section and time is the constraint. Do the chapters you are fastest at first (usually percentages, ratio, profit and loss, simple geometry), leave heights-and-distances and long DI for the end, and keep a strict per-question limit. Use the options: substitute, check unit digits, check magnitude.

**BPSC.** Maths is a small part of a broad paper; do not let it consume disproportionate time. Take the direct questions, skip anything multi-step, and return only if time remains.

**All three:** read the question twice, write the one or two numbers you need in the margin, compute, check the answer against the options for size and last digit, mark, move on.

## The night before

- Read the Formula Sheet once and the error log once.
- Redo five questions you previously got wrong, from memory.
- Pack admit card, ID, pens; know the route and the reporting time.
- Stop by 9 pm. Speed and attention are what the paper tests, and both come from sleep.

That is the whole book. The syllabus is small; the practice is what makes it yours.
