## Where it appears

SSC: two or three questions, trains and boats most common. CSAT and BPSC: one or two, usually average speed, meeting problems, or a simple race.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| Basic relation | distance = speed × time |
| km/h ↔ m/s | × 5/18 to get m/s · × 18/5 to get km/h |
| Average speed | total distance ÷ total time · equal distances at u, v: 2uv ÷ (u + v) |
| Relative speed | opposite directions u + v · same direction |u − v| |
| Train crossing a pole or person | distance = train length |
| Train crossing a platform or bridge | distance = train + platform |
| Two trains crossing | distance = sum of lengths, at relative speed |
| Boats | downstream b + s · upstream b − s |
| From down and up speeds | boat b = (down + up) ÷ 2 · stream s = (down − up) ÷ 2 |
| Meet, then finish in t₁ and t₂ | u ÷ v = √(t₂ ÷ t₁) |
| Speed becomes a/b of usual | time becomes b/a of usual (fixed distance) |

</div>

## Unit conversion first

72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s. 25 m/s = 90 km/h. Convert to m/s when lengths are in metres (trains), to km/h when in kilometres.

## The basic relation and proportion

**Example.** A man walks at 5 km/h and reaches 6 minutes late; at 6 km/h he is 6 minutes early. Distance?
Time difference 12 min = 1/5 h. d/5 − d/6 = 1/5 → d/30 = 1/5 → d = 6 km.

**Example.** A car covers a distance in 4 h at 60 km/h. To cover it in 3 h?
Distance 240; speed 80 km/h. (Or: time becomes 3/4, so speed becomes 4/3 × 60.)

**Example.** Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man is 10 minutes late. Usual time?
Time becomes 4/3 of usual → extra 1/3 of usual = 10 min → 30 min.

**Example.** A 150 m train at 54 km/h crosses a pole in? 54 km/h = 15 m/s → 150/15 = 10 s.

**Example.** A 200 m train crosses a 300 m platform in 25 s. Speed?
500/25 = 20 m/s = 72 km/h.

**Example.** A train crosses a pole in 8 s and a 240 m platform in 20 s. Length and speed?
Let length L, speed v: L = 8v, L + 240 = 20v → 240 = 12v → v = 20 m/s, L = 160 m.

**Example.** Two trains 120 m and 180 m long run at 40 and 50 km/h in opposite directions. Time to cross each other?
Relative 90 km/h = 25 m/s; distance 300 m → 12 s.

**Example.** Same trains, same direction? Relative 10 km/h = 25/9 m/s; 300/(25/9) = 108 s.

**Example.** A train 100 m long passes a man walking at 6 km/h in the same direction in 10 s. Train speed?
Relative speed = 10 m/s = 36 km/h → train = 42 km/h.

## Boats and streams

**Example.** A boat goes 24 km downstream in 2 h and returns in 3 h. Boat speed and stream speed?
Down 12, up 8 → boat 10 km/h, stream 2 km/h.

**Example.** Boat speed 9 km/h in still water, stream 3 km/h. Time to go 36 km upstream and back?
Up 6 km/h → 6 h; down 12 km/h → 3 h; total 9 h.

**Example.** A man rows to a place 48 km away and back in 14 h. He can row 4 km downstream in the same time as 3 km upstream. Stream speed?
Down : up speeds = 4 : 3 = 4k, 3k. 48/4k + 48/3k = 14 → 12/k + 16/k = 14 → k = 2 → down 8, up 6 → stream 1 km/h.

## Meeting and catching up

**Example.** A leaves at 8:00 at 40 km/h; B leaves at 9:00 at 60 km/h in the same direction. When does B catch A?
Gap at 9:00 = 40 km; closing at 20 km/h → 2 h → 11:00.

**Example.** Two cities 300 km apart. A car leaves X at 50 km/h and another leaves Y at 70 km/h towards each other, at the same time. When do they meet?
300/120 = 2.5 h, at 125 km from X.

**Example.** Two trains start towards each other from A and B. After meeting they take 9 h and 16 h to reach B and A. Ratio of speeds?
√(16/9) = 4 : 3.

## Races and circular tracks

**Example.** In a 100 m race A beats B by 10 m. In a 200 m race by how much?
Speeds 100 : 90; when A runs 200, B runs 180 → 20 m.

**Example.** Two people run around a 400 m track at 6 m/s and 4 m/s starting together in the same direction. When do they first meet? 400/(6 − 4) = 200 s. Opposite directions: 400/10 = 40 s.

**Example.** A can give B a 20 m start in a 100 m race and still finish together. Speed ratio? A : B = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4.

## Traps

- Mixing units: lengths in metres with speeds in km/h.
- Crossing a platform uses the sum of lengths; "crossing a man on the platform" uses just the train.
- Same-direction relative speed is the difference and can be tiny, giving long times.
- Average speed for the round trip is the harmonic mean, not the arithmetic mean.
- "Reaches 6 min late at speed 1, 6 min early at speed 2": the time difference is 12 minutes.

## Practice set

1. Convert 90 km/h to m/s and 15 m/s to km/h.
2. A cyclist at 12 km/h reaches 20 min late; at 15 km/h, 10 min late. Distance?
3. A 240 m train at 90 km/h crosses a 360 m bridge in?
4. A train crosses a pole in 12 s and a 300 m platform in 27 s. Length?
5. Trains of 150 m and 250 m at 60 and 84 km/h approach each other. Crossing time?
6. A boat covers 15 km downstream in 1 h and the same upstream in 1.5 h. Stream speed?
7. Boat 12 km/h still water, stream 4 km/h. Round trip to 32 km away takes?
8. A goes at 30 km/h at 7:00; B follows at 45 km/h at 8:30. When does B catch A?
9. Two towns 450 km apart; two cars start towards each other at 60 and 90 km/h at the same time. Distance from the slower car's town when they meet?
10. After meeting, two trains reach their destinations in 4 h and 9 h. If the faster runs at 60 km/h, the slower runs at?
11. In a 1 km race A beats B by 100 m and B beats C by 50 m. A beats C by?
12. Walking at 4/5 of usual speed a student is 6 min late. Usual time?

**Answers**

1. 25 m/s; 54 km/h.
2. d/12 − d/15 = 10/60 → d/60 = 1/6 → 10 km.
3. 600 m at 25 m/s → 24 s.
4. L = 12v, L + 300 = 27v → v = 20 → L = 240 m.
5. 144 km/h = 40 m/s; 400 m → 10 s.
6. Down 15, up 10 → stream 2.5 km/h.
7. Down 16 → 2 h; up 8 → 4 h; total 6 h.
8. Gap at 8:30 = 45 km; closing 15 km/h → 3 h → 11:30.
9. 450/150 = 3 h; 60 × 3 = 180 km.
10. Speed ratio = √(9/4) = 3 : 2 → slower = 40 km/h.
11. B runs 900 when A runs 1000; C runs 950/1000 of B → when A finishes, C has run 900 × 0.95 = 855 → 145 m.
12. Time becomes 5/4 → extra 1/4 = 6 min → 24 min.
