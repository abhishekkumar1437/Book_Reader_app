## Where it appears

SSC asks two or three per paper; CSAT and BPSC one. The setters have a fixed repertoire of patterns, all listed below.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit · Loss | SP − CP · CP − SP |
| Profit % · Loss % | profit ÷ CP × 100 · loss ÷ CP × 100  (base is cost price) |
| SP from CP | CP × (1 + p/100) for p % profit · CP × (1 − l/100) for l % loss |
| Discount | on the marked price: SP = MP × (1 − d/100) |
| Successive discounts d₁, d₂ | SP = MP × (1 − d₁/100)(1 − d₂/100) |
| The chain | CP × (1 + p/100) = SP = MP × (1 − d/100) |
| Mark-up needed | MP ÷ CP = (1 + p/100) ÷ (1 − d/100) |
| Same SP, x % profit and x % loss | net loss of x²/100 % |
| False weight w g per kg | gain = (1000 − w) ÷ w × 100 % |
| SP of n articles = CP of m articles | profit = (m − n) ÷ n × 100 % (loss if negative) |
| Buy x for ₹a, sell y for ₹b | compare per unit: CP a/x, SP b/y |

</div>

## The multiplier method

Turn every percentage into a factor and multiply along the chain. It removes the need for separate formulae.

**Example.** An article is bought for ₹800 and sold at 15% profit. SP? 800 × 1.15 = ₹920.

**Example.** Sold for ₹552 at 8% loss. CP? 552/0.92 = ₹600.

**Example.** A shopkeeper marks goods 40% above cost and gives a 20% discount. Profit?
1.4 × 0.8 = 1.12 → 12%.

**Example.** After two successive discounts of 10% and 15%, an article sells for ₹765. Marked price?
765/(0.9 × 0.85) = 765/0.765 = ₹1,000.

**Example.** A trader wants a 20% profit after giving a 20% discount. How much above cost should he mark?
MP/CP = 1.2/0.8 = 1.5 → 50% above cost.

## Repertoire of patterns

**Same selling price, equal percent profit and loss.**
Two watches are sold at ₹600 each, one at 20% profit and one at 20% loss. Net?
Loss of 20²/100 = 4%. (CPs: 500 and 750, total 1250; SP total 1200; loss 50/1250 = 4%.)

**Profit on selling price.** "Profit is 20% of SP" means SP − CP = 0.2 SP → CP = 0.8 SP → profit on CP = 0.2/0.8 = 25%.

**SP of n = CP of m.** If the SP of 12 pens equals the CP of 15 pens, profit % = (15 − 12)/12 = 25%.

**Buy and sell in lots.** Buys oranges at 5 for ₹4 and sells at 4 for ₹5. CP each 0.8, SP each 1.25 → profit 0.45/0.8 = 56.25%.

**False weights.** A grocer sells at cost price but uses a 900 g weight per kg. Gain = 100/900 = 11.11%.

**Two-step trader.** A sells to B at 20% profit, B to C at 25% profit, C pays ₹300. A's cost? 300/(1.2 × 1.25) = ₹200.

**Difference of two selling prices.** An article sold at ₹1,150 gives a profit that is twice the loss at ₹850. CP? Profit = 1150 − c, loss = c − 850, 1150 − c = 2(c − 850) → 1150 + 1700 = 3c → c = 950.

**Percent change in profit.** If CP rises 20% and SP rises 30%, and originally profit was 10%: new SP = 1.1 × 1.3 = 1.43, new CP = 1.2 → profit = 0.23/1.2 = 19.17%.

## Discount details

- A discount of "20% off, plus 10% off" is 28%, not 30%.
- "Buy 4 get 1 free" is a discount of 1/5 = 20% on the batch.
- A dozen for the price of ten: discount 2/12 = 16.67%.
- Cash discount after trade discount: successive.

**Example.** A shop offers 25% off and a further 10% for cash. A customer pays ₹1,350. Marked price? 1350/(0.75 × 0.9) = ₹2,000.

## Traps

- Percent profit is on CP unless told otherwise; discount percent is on MP.
- Two successive changes of the same percentage in opposite directions net to a loss.
- "Overall profit" for two items with different CPs is (total SP − total CP)/total CP, not the average of the two percentages.
- Per-unit comparison for lot questions; do not compare totals across different lot sizes.

## Practice set

1. CP ₹450, SP ₹513. Profit %?
2. Sold at ₹1,080 for a 10% loss. To gain 15%, sell at?
3. Marked 30% above cost, discount 20%. Profit %?
4. Two items at ₹1,200 each, one at 25% profit, one at 25% loss. Overall?
5. SP of 20 articles = CP of 25 articles. Profit %?
6. Buys 6 for ₹5, sells 5 for ₹6. Profit %?
7. A dishonest dealer sells at cost using 800 g per kg. Gain %?
8. Profit is 25% of SP. Profit % on CP?
9. Successive discounts of 20%, 10%, 5% on ₹2,000. Final price?
10. A sells to B at 10% profit, B to C at 10% loss, C pays ₹495. A's cost?
11. Selling at ₹720 gives the same loss as the profit at ₹880. CP?
12. To gain 25% after a 20% discount, mark up by?

**Answers**

1. 63/450 = 14%.
2. CP = 1080/0.9 = 1200 → 1200 × 1.15 = ₹1,380.
3. 1.3 × 0.8 = 1.04 → 4%.
4. Loss 6.25%.
5. (25 − 20)/20 = 25%.
6. CP 5/6, SP 6/5 → (6/5 − 5/6)/(5/6) = (36 − 25)/25 = 44%.
7. 200/800 = 25%.
8. CP = 0.75 SP → 0.25/0.75 = 33.33%.
9. 2000 × 0.8 × 0.9 × 0.95 = ₹1,368.
10. 495/(1.1 × 0.9) = ₹500.
11. (720 + 880)/2 = ₹800.
12. 1.25/0.8 = 1.5625 → 56.25%.
