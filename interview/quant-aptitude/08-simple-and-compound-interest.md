## Where it appears

Every exam, one or two questions. SSC likes the CI − SI difference and rate-finding; CSAT likes doubling time and comparisons; BPSC asks direct computations.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| Simple interest | SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 · amount = P + SI |
| Compound amount | A = P × (1 + R/100)ᵀ · CI = A − P |
| Half-yearly compounding | rate R/2, periods 2T |
| Quarterly compounding | rate R/4, periods 4T |
| CI for 2 years | P × [2R/100 + (R/100)²] |
| CI − SI, 2 years | P × (R/100)² |
| CI − SI, 3 years | P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100) |
| Effective rate, 2 years | 2R + R²/100 % |
| Doubling time, SI | 100 ÷ R years |
| Doubling time, CI (rule of 72) | ≈ 72 ÷ R years · k times in n years ⇒ k² times in 2n years |
| Equal annual instalment x at rate r | P = x/(1 + r) + x/(1 + r)² + … (r = R/100) |

</div>

## Simple interest

**Example.** SI on ₹6,000 at 8% for 3 years = 6000 × 8 × 3/100 = ₹1,440.

**Example.** A sum becomes ₹9,200 in 3 years and ₹10,400 in 5 years at SI. Principal and rate?
Two years' interest = 1,200 → 600/year. In 3 years: 1,800 → P = 7,400. Rate = 600/7400 × 100 = 8.11%.

**Example.** At what rate will a sum double in 8 years under SI? 100/8 = 12.5%.

**Example.** A sum at SI amounts to ₹1,344 in 4 years and ₹1,440 in 6 years. Find the sum.
Yearly interest = 96/2 = 48. Sum = 1344 − 4 × 48 = ₹1,152.

## Compound interest

**Example.** CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years.
A = 10,000 × 1.21 = 11,000 + 1,000 = 12,100 → CI ₹2,100. (SI would be 2,000; the extra 100 is interest on the first year's interest.)

**Example.** CI on ₹8,000 at 5% for 3 years.
1.05³ = 1.157625 → A = 9,261 → CI ₹1,261.

**Example.** ₹20,000 at 20% per annum compounded half-yearly for 1 year.
Rate 10%, 2 periods: 20,000 × 1.21 = 24,200 → CI ₹4,200.

**Example.** Difference between CI and SI on ₹5,000 at 4% for 2 years.
P(R/100)² = 5000 × 0.0016 = ₹8.

**Example.** CI − SI for 2 years at 10% is ₹150. Principal?
P × 0.01 = 150 → ₹15,000.

**Example.** A sum becomes 4 times in 2 years under CI. Rate?
(1 + r)² = 4 → 1 + r = 2 → 100%.

**Example.** A sum doubles in 5 years under CI. In how many years is it 8 times?
Doubles every 5 years: 2 → 4 → 8 needs 15 years.

## Finding the rate from two amounts

**Example.** Under CI a sum amounts to ₹4,840 in 2 years and ₹5,324 in 3 years. Rate?
Third year's interest = 484 on 4,840 → 10%.

**Hint:** consecutive-year amounts under CI give the rate directly as the ratio minus 1.

## Instalments

**Example.** A loan of ₹2,100 at 10% CI is repaid in two equal annual instalments. Each instalment?
x/1.1 + x/1.21 = 2100 → x(1.1 + 1)/1.21 = 2100 → x = 2100 × 1.21/2.1 = ₹1,210.

## Traps

- Under CI the rate is per period; convert the rate and the count together for half-yearly or quarterly.
- "Amount" includes principal; "interest" does not.
- The CI − SI difference formula for 2 years needs R as a fraction (0.04, not 4).
- SI grows linearly, so "doubles in n years" gives the rate as 100/n; CI does not.
- Rounding intermediate values in multi-year CI; keep three decimals.

## Practice set

1. SI on ₹4,500 at 6% for 2.5 years.
2. A sum triples in 12 years under SI. Rate?
3. CI on ₹12,000 at 10% for 2 years.
4. CI on ₹5,000 at 8% compounded half-yearly for 1 year.
5. Difference between CI and SI on ₹8,000 at 5% for 2 years.
6. CI − SI on a sum at 10% for 3 years is ₹31. The sum?
7. Under CI, ₹1,000 becomes ₹1,331 in 3 years. Rate?
8. A sum doubles in 4 years under CI. It becomes 16 times in?
9. A sum at SI gives ₹840 in 4 years; the same at 2% higher gives ₹1,008. Sum?
10. CI amounts are ₹6,050 after 2 years and ₹6,655 after 3 years. Principal?
11. Repay a ₹4,100 loan at 5% CI in two equal annual instalments. Each?
12. What sum at 5% SI gives the same interest in 4 years as ₹8,000 at 10% for 3 years?

**Answers**

1. 4500 × 6 × 2.5/100 = ₹675.
2. Interest = 2P in 12 years → 200/12 = 16.67%.
3. 12,000 × 0.21 = ₹2,520.
4. 4% for 2 periods: 5000 × 1.0816 = 5,408 → ₹408.
5. 8000 × 0.0025 = ₹20.
6. P × 0.01 × 3.1 = 31 → ₹1,000.
7. 1.331 = 1.1³ → 10%.
8. 2⁴ = 16 → 16 years.
9. 4 × 2% of P = 168 → P = ₹2,100.
10. Rate = 605/6050 = 10%; P = 6050/1.21 = ₹5,000.
11. x/1.05 + x/1.1025 = 4100 → x × 2.05/1.1025 = 4100 → x = 4100 × 1.1025/2.05 = ₹2,205. (Check: 2205/1.05 = 2100 and 2205/1.1025 = 2000.)
12. 8000 × 10 × 3/100 = 2400 = P × 5 × 4/100 → P = ₹12,000.
