## Where it appears

All three exams, often. Direct ratio questions (divide ₹1,200 in the ratio 2:3:5), ages, partnership profit shares, mixtures (milk and water), and alligation for "what proportion must be mixed". Alligation also solves average-of-groups and profit-mixing questions, which is why it is worth learning as a tool rather than a topic.

## Formulae

<div class="formula">

| What | Formula |
|---|---|
| Split Q in ratio a : b : c | Q·a/(a+b+c), Q·b/(a+b+c), Q·c/(a+b+c) |
| Combine A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5 | make B equal (12) → A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15 |
| Proportion a : b :: c : d | a/b = c/d, so ad = bc |
| Mean proportional of a, b | √(ab) |
| Third proportional to a, b | b² ÷ a |
| Fourth proportional to a, b, c | bc ÷ a |
| Componendo-dividendo | a/b = c/d ⇒ (a + b)/(a − b) = (c + d)/(c − d) |
| Partnership profit shares | in the ratio of (capital × time) |
| Alligation (cheaper c, dearer d, mean m) | cheaper : dearer = (d − m) : (m − c) |
| Repeated replacement, n times | liquid left = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ |

</div>

**Hint:** any question that mixes two things with different "rates" (price, concentration, speed, marks, profit percent) to get a known average is an alligation. The two rates go on top, the average in the middle.

## Basic ratio questions

**Example.** Divide ₹2,400 among A, B, C in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
Sum 12 → one part ₹200 → 600, 800, 1,000.

**Example.** A : B = 3 : 4, B : C = 5 : 6. Find A : B : C.
Make B = 20: A : B = 15 : 20, B : C = 20 : 24 → 15 : 20 : 24.

**Example.** Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 7. If 8 is added to each, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. Find the numbers.
(5x + 8)/(7x + 8) = 7/9 → 45x + 72 = 49x + 56 → 4x = 16 → x = 4. Numbers 20 and 28.

**Example.** Ages of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5; after 6 years the ratio is 6 : 7. Present ages?
(4x + 6)/(5x + 6) = 6/7 → 28x + 42 = 30x + 36 → x = 3. Ages 12 and 15.

**Example.** A bag has 1-rupee, 50-paise, and 25-paise coins in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 totalling ₹114. Number of 50-paise coins?
Value of one "set" = 2(1) + 3(0.5) + 5(0.25) = 2 + 1.5 + 1.25 = 4.75. Sets = 114/4.75 = 24. 50-paise coins = 3 × 24 = 72.

## Proportion

**Example.** If 15 workers build a wall in 12 days, how many days for 20 workers? Inverse proportion: 15 × 12 = 20 × d → d = 9.

**Example.** Mean proportional between 9 and 16 = √144 = 12. Third proportional to 4 and 12 = 144/4 = 36. Fourth proportional to 6, 9, 20 = 9 × 20/6 = 30.

**Example.** If a/b = 3/5, find (a + b)/(a − b) = (3 + 5)/(3 − 5) = −4.

## Partnership

**Example.** A invests ₹40,000 for 12 months, B ₹60,000 for 8 months. Profit ₹18,000. B's share?
Ratio = 40 × 12 : 60 × 8 = 480 : 480 = 1 : 1 → ₹9,000 each.

**Example.** A starts with ₹50,000; after 4 months B joins with ₹75,000. At year end profit ₹35,000. Share of A?
A: 50 × 12 = 600; B: 75 × 8 = 600. Equal → ₹17,500.

**Example.** A, B, C invest in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 and their time in the ratio 3 : 2 : 1. Profit ratio?
2×3 : 3×2 : 5×1 = 6 : 6 : 5.

## Mixtures with alligation

**Example.** In what ratio must rice at ₹40/kg be mixed with rice at ₹55/kg so that the mixture costs ₹45/kg?
(55 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1.

**Example.** A mixture of 60 L has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 1 : 2?
Milk 40 L stays. Want milk : water = 1 : 2 → water 80 L. Add 80 − 20 = 60 L.

**Example.** A vessel contains 80 L of milk. 8 L are removed and replaced with water, twice. Milk remaining?
80 × (1 − 8/80)² = 80 × 0.81 = 64.8 L.

**Example.** A shopkeeper mixes two varieties of tea at ₹120/kg and ₹180/kg, and sells the mixture at ₹176/kg at 10% profit. Ratio of the two?
Cost of mixture = 176/1.1 = ₹160. Ratio = (180 − 160) : (160 − 120) = 20 : 40 = 1 : 2.

## Alligation beyond mixtures

**Average of groups.** A class of 50 has an average of 60; boys average 65, girls 55. Number of boys?
Ratio boys : girls = (60 − 55) : (65 − 60) = 1 : 1 → 25 boys.

**Profit on parts.** A trader sells 60% of goods at 20% profit and the rest at a loss of 10%. Overall?
Weighted: 0.6 × 20 + 0.4 × (−10) = 12 − 4 = 8% profit. (Alligation cross with 20 and −10 gives the same.)

**Two speeds.** A man walks part of 20 km at 4 km/h and the rest at 6 km/h, taking 4 h. Distance at 4 km/h?
Average speed 5 km/h. Ratio of **times** at the two speeds = (6 − 5) : (5 − 4) = 1 : 1 → 2 h each → 8 km at 4 km/h.

Alligation on speeds gives the ratio of times, not distances; on prices per kg it gives the ratio of weights. The ratio is always of the quantities the rate is "per".

## Traps

- Ratios are of parts, not amounts; find the value of one part first.
- When two ratios share a term, equalise the shared term before combining.
- Alligation's answer is cheaper : dearer, in that order; reading the cross backwards inverts it.
- Replacement questions: the fraction removed each time is of the current mixture, hence the power.
- Partnership time is in months unless stated; a partner who joins later invests for fewer months.

## Practice set

1. Divide 630 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4.
2. A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5, C : D = 6 : 7. Find A : D.
3. Two numbers in ratio 3 : 5; subtracting 9 from each gives 12 : 23. The numbers?
4. Fourth proportional to 5, 8, 15.
5. A and B invest ₹30,000 and ₹45,000; after 6 months A withdraws half. Profit at year end ₹21,000; A's share?
6. In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹60/L to sell the mixture at ₹60/L and gain 20%?
7. A 50 L mixture has milk : water = 3 : 2. How much milk to add for 4 : 1?
8. From 100 L of pure milk, 10 L is replaced by water three times. Milk left?
9. The average weight of a group of 30 is 55 kg; men average 60 and women 50. How many women?
10. ₹1,050 is divided among A, B, C so that A gets 2/5 of what B and C together get. A's share?
11. Salaries of A and B are in ratio 5 : 7; each gets a raise of ₹2,000 and the ratio becomes 3 : 4. A's new salary?
12. Sugar at ₹32/kg and ₹40/kg mixed and sold at ₹43.20/kg at 20% profit. Ratio?

**Answers**

1. 9 parts, 70 each → 140, 210, 280.
2. A/D = (2/3)(4/5)(6/7) = 48/105 = 16/35 → 16 : 35.
3. (3x − 9)/(5x − 9) = 12/23 → 69x − 207 = 60x − 108 → 9x = 99 → x = 11 → 33 and 55.
4. 8 × 15/5 = 24.
5. A: 30 × 6 + 15 × 6 = 270; B: 45 × 12 = 540. Ratio 1 : 2 → A gets ₹7,000.
6. Cost of mixture = 60/1.2 = ₹50/L. Water costs 0: ratio water : milk = (60 − 50) : (50 − 0) = 1 : 5.
7. Milk 30, water 20. Water fixed: need milk 80 → add 50 L.
8. 100 × 0.9³ = 72.9 L.
9. Men : women = (55 − 50) : (60 − 55) = 1 : 1 → 15 women.
10. A = 2/5 (B + C), so A : (B + C) = 2 : 5 → A = 2/7 × 1050 = ₹300.
11. (5x + 2000)/(7x + 2000) = 3/4 → 20x + 8000 = 21x + 6000 → x = 2000 → A new = 10,000 + 2,000 = ₹12,000.
12. Cost = 43.2/1.2 = 36. Ratio = (40 − 36) : (36 − 32) = 1 : 1.
