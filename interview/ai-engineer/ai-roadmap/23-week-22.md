These are the last three days of the plan. You will not learn anything new. Day 148 is a full rehearsal of a real interview loop, Day 149 is light revision, and Day 150 is for rest and planning what comes next. The checklists below are useful for every real interview after this too.

> **In simple words:** Rehearse once under real conditions, revise only your own notes, sleep well, and walk into each interview with a calm routine. After Day 150, keep a small daily habit going while you apply.

## Interview-day checklist

### The day before

- [ ] Confirm time (and time zone), format, and interviewer names from the invite
- [ ] Read the job description again and match 3 of its needs to your projects
- [ ] Look at the company's product and any AI features it has; prepare one thoughtful question about them
- [ ] Re-read your resume; you must be able to defend every line
- [ ] Open your live project links and check they still work
- [ ] Prepare clothes, ID, charger, water; plan travel with extra time for an on-site interview
- [ ] Sleep at a normal time

### For online interviews (1 hour before)

- [ ] Laptop charged and plugged in; internet tested; a phone hotspot ready as backup
- [ ] Camera at eye level, face lit from the front, quiet room, plain background
- [ ] Headphones with a microphone tested in the meeting app
- [ ] Close extra tabs and notifications; keep one plain editor ready
- [ ] Paper and pen for diagrams and notes
- [ ] Join 5 minutes early

### During the interview

- [ ] Listen to the full question, then repeat it in your own words
- [ ] Ask clarifying questions before solving (input size, edge cases, requirements, constraints)
- [ ] Think aloud; never stay silent for more than about 30 seconds
- [ ] Start simple (brute force or a basic design), then improve
- [ ] Test your code by hand on an example and an edge case before saying "done"
- [ ] State complexity and trade-offs without being asked
- [ ] If you do not know something, say so honestly and explain how you would find out
- [ ] Save 5 minutes at the end for your own questions

### After the interview

- [ ] Write down every question you were asked, within an hour
- [ ] Solve or study anything you missed, within a day
- [ ] Add the lessons to your mistakes log
- [ ] Send a short thank-you note if it is usual for that company
- [ ] Do not stop applying while you wait for results

## After Day 150

Finishing the plan is a big achievement, but the job search may take weeks or months. Keep the habit going in a lighter form.

| What | How much | Why |
|---|---|---|
| DSA | 1 problem a day, 1 mock a week | Coding skill fades quickly without practice |
| Applications | 5–10 good applications a week, plus referral requests | Steady volume brings interviews |
| Interview feedback loop | After each interview, study what you missed | Questions repeat across companies |
| One new skill a month | For example a second cloud, an evaluation framework, or a deeper agent framework | Keeps your profile current |
| Improve one project | Add a feature, better evaluation or a blog post about it | Gives you new things to talk about |
| Stay current | 2–3 hours a week reading model release notes, engineering blogs and papers summaries | AI tools change fast; interviewers ask about recent ideas |
| Share your work | A LinkedIn post or short blog about each project | Recruiters find you; writing improves your explanations |

A sample 30-day plan after Day 150:

```text
Week 1: Apply to 10 roles. Daily DSA problem. Write a blog post about the capstone.
Week 2: Apply to 10 more. One mock coding + one mock design. Add one improvement to Project 1.
Week 3: Follow up on referrals. Re-take your weakest question bank. Learn one new tool.
Week 4: Review what interviews asked so far. Update resume with new numbers. Plan the next month.
```

If you get rejected, that is normal. Most people get many rejections before an offer. Ask for feedback when possible, fix one thing, and keep going.

## What each topic means

**Interview loop.** The full set of rounds at a company, often: recruiter screen, coding, ML/LLM concepts, system design, project deep-dive, and behavioural or hiring-manager round.

**Recruiter screen.** A short first call about your background, interest and salary expectations. Have your 1-minute introduction ready.

**Clarifying questions.** Questions you ask before solving, to remove doubt about the problem. They show careful thinking and prevent solving the wrong problem.

**Thinking aloud.** Saying your reasoning while you work. Interviewers grade your process, not only the final answer, and can help if they hear where you are stuck.

**Offer and negotiation.** When you get an offer, it is normal to ask politely about the details (salary parts, joining date) and, if you have a competing offer or strong reasons, to ask whether there is flexibility.

**Mistakes log (final use).** Your personal list of repeated errors. It is the most valuable revision document you have, because it is about you.

## End-of-week self-check

1. What are the first three things you do after hearing an interview question?
2. What do you say when you do not know the answer to a concept question?
3. Give your 1-minute "tell me about yourself".
4. What are the five things you will do after every interview?
5. What is your daily routine after Day 150?
6. In one minute: what is RAG and when would you not use it?

**Answers**

1. Repeat the question in your own words, ask clarifying questions (inputs, constraints, scale, edge cases), and state a simple first approach before improving it.
2. Be honest: "I have not worked with that directly." Then reason from what you do know ("I would expect it to work like X because…") or say how you would find out. Honest reasoning scores better than bluffing.
3. Check it covers: who you are, what you studied and built (the 150-day plan and three projects, with one number), what you are good at, and why this role. Keep it near 60 seconds.
4. Write down all the questions; study what you missed within a day; update the mistakes log; send a thank-you note if appropriate; keep applying.
5. Example: one DSA problem a day, a mock each week, 5–10 applications a week, a few hours on one project improvement or new skill, and a little reading to stay current.
6. RAG retrieves relevant pieces of your documents and puts them in the prompt so the LLM answers from them, with citations. It suits private or changing knowledge. Do not use it when the task needs no external knowledge, when you need to change the model's style or format (fine-tuning fits better), or when all the needed text already fits cheaply in the prompt.

## If you fall behind

If you reach this week late, keep Day 149's light revision and Day 150's rest, and shorten the mock day to a coding mock plus one design mock. If you have a real interview scheduled, treat it as your Day 148 and use the checklists above.

You started with basic Python. You now have three deployed AI projects, a working knowledge of the whole AI stack, and practice for every interview round. Go and get the job.
