This chapter covers ecology and environment from NCERT class 10 (Our Environment) and class 12 (Organisms and Populations, Ecosystem, Biodiversity and Conservation, older Environmental Issues) up to B.Sc. points such as BOD, sewage treatment stages and species–area relationships. TRE asked: "which is not a greenhouse gas", "the purpose of secondary treatment of sewage", "ozone hole refers to", "tropical evergreen forests are found in". It also overlaps with the General Studies part (Bihar's sanctuaries, environmental laws).

> **In simple words:** An ecosystem is a living community together with its non-living surroundings. Plants capture sunlight; animals eat plants or other animals; decomposers recycle dead matter. At every step up the food chain about 90% of energy is lost, so chains are short. Nutrients like carbon and nitrogen go round and round in cycles. Human activity adds greenhouse gases, thins the ozone layer, pollutes air and water and destroys forests — so we protect species in national parks, sanctuaries and seed banks, backed by laws.

## 1. Ecosystem — structure and function

- **Ecosystem** (term by **A.G. Tansley, 1935**) = **biotic** components (producers, consumers, decomposers) + **abiotic** components (light, temperature, water, soil, minerals).
- **Ecology** (term by **Ernst Haeckel, 1866**) — study of interactions between organisms and their environment.
- **Producers** (autotrophs: green plants, phytoplankton) → **primary consumers** (herbivores) → **secondary consumers** (carnivores) → **tertiary consumers** (top carnivores). **Decomposers** (bacteria, fungi) break dead matter.
- **Stratification**: vertical layering of plants (trees, shrubs, herbs) in a forest.

### Productivity

- **Primary productivity** = rate of biomass made by plants per unit area per unit time (g m⁻² yr⁻¹ or kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹).
- **GPP** (gross primary productivity) = total photosynthesis. **NPP = GPP − R** (R = respiration loss). NPP is what herbivores can eat.
- Annual NPP of the whole biosphere ≈ **170 billion tonnes** (dry weight); oceans give only about **55 billion tonnes** though they cover ~70% of the surface (NCERT).
- Plants capture only **2–10% of the photosynthetically active radiation (PAR)**; PAR is less than 50% of incident sunlight.

### Decomposition

Steps: **fragmentation** (by detritivores like earthworm) → **leaching** (soluble nutrients go into soil) → **catabolism** (enzymes of bacteria and fungi) → **humification** (forms dark, colloidal **humus**, very slow to decompose) → **mineralisation** (humus releases inorganic nutrients). Fast in warm, moist conditions; slow when the litter is rich in **lignin and chitin**.

### Food chains and food webs

- **Grazing food chain (GFC)**: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk.
- **Detritus food chain (DFC)**: starts from dead organic matter (detritus) → decomposers. In terrestrial ecosystems more energy flows through the DFC than the GFC.
- **Food web**: many interconnected food chains — more stable.
- **10% law** (**Lindeman, 1942**): only about **10% of energy** passes from one trophic level to the next; the rest is lost mainly as heat in respiration. Energy flow is **unidirectional** (sun → producers → consumers); nutrients **cycle**.

**Worked example:** Producers fix 10,000 J. Energy at primary consumers = 1,000 J; secondary = 100 J; tertiary = **10 J**.

**Worked example:** A lion (tertiary consumer, 4th level) gets 50 J. Energy that must have been fixed by producers = 50 × 10 × 10 × 10 = **50,000 J**.

| Pyramid | Usually | Inverted when |
|---|---|---|
| **Number** | Upright (grassland) | **Inverted** in a **tree ecosystem** (one tree supports many insects) and in parasite chains |

Limitations: pyramids ignore species belonging to two or more levels, food webs and saprophytes.

### Ecological succession

- Gradual, predictable change in species composition of an area until a stable **climax community** is reached. The series of communities is a **sere**; each stage a **seral stage**.
- **Primary succession**: on bare areas with no soil (bare rock, cooled lava, new pond) — slow. **Secondary succession**: where a community was destroyed (abandoned farm, burnt forest, flooded land) — faster because soil exists.
- **Pioneer species**: **lichens** on bare rock (they secrete acids that make soil); **phytoplankton** in water.
- **Hydrarch** (in water) and **xerarch** (in dry areas) successions both lead to a **mesic** (medium-moisture) climax.

## 2. Populations and interactions

- Attributes: **birth rate (natality)**, **death rate (mortality)**, **sex ratio**, **age pyramid** (triangular = expanding, bell = stable, urn = declining).
- **Exponential growth** (J-shaped, unlimited resources): dN/dt = rN. **Logistic growth** (S-shaped, Verhulst–Pearl): dN/dt = rN (K − N)/K, where **K = carrying capacity**.

| Interaction | Effect (A, B) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutualism** | + , + | **Lichen** (fungus + alga), **mycorrhiza** (fungus + plant root), fig and fig-wasp, *Rhizobium* in legume roots |
| **Competition** | − , − | Flamingos and fish for zooplankton; Gause's **competitive exclusion principle** |
| **Predation** | + , − | Tiger and deer; also herbivores on plants |
| **Parasitism** | + , − | Tapeworm in man; **Cuscuta** (dodder) on plants; **brood parasitism** — **cuckoo (koel)** lays eggs in crow's nest |
| **Commensalism** | + , 0 | **Orchid on a mango branch** (epiphyte); **barnacles on a whale**; **cattle egret and cattle**; clown fish and sea anemone (often given as commensalism in NCERT) |
| **Amensalism** | − , 0 | *Penicillium* inhibits bacteria |

## 3. Biogeochemical (nutrient) cycles

- **Gaseous cycles** (reservoir in atmosphere/ocean): carbon, nitrogen, oxygen. **Sedimentary cycles** (reservoir in Earth's crust): **phosphorus**, sulphur.

### Carbon cycle

Carbon is about **49% of the dry weight** of organisms. About **71%** of the global carbon is **dissolved in oceans**. Fixed by photosynthesis (~4 × 10¹³ kg per year); returned by respiration, decomposition, burning of fossil fuels, forest fires and volcanoes.

### Nitrogen cycle

| Step | What happens | Organisms |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen fixation** | N₂ → NH₃ | Free-living ***Azotobacter***, *Clostridium*, cyanobacteria (***Anabaena***, ***Nostoc***); symbiotic ***Rhizobium*** (legume root nodules); also **lightning** and industry (Haber process) |
| **Ammonification** | Dead matter → NH₃ | Decomposers |
| **Nitrification** | NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ | ***Nitrosomonas*** (ammonia → nitrite), ***Nitrobacter*** (nitrite → nitrate) |
| **Denitrification** | NO₃⁻ → N₂ | ***Pseudomonas***, ***Thiobacillus*** |

Nitrogenase (the N₂-fixing enzyme) works only without O₂; root nodules contain pink **leghaemoglobin** to keep oxygen low.

### Phosphorus cycle

Main reservoir **rocks** (phosphates); released by **weathering**; no significant gaseous phase; needed for DNA, ATP, bones and teeth.

## 4. Global environmental issues

### Greenhouse effect and global warming

- **Greenhouse gases** absorb outgoing **infrared** radiation and warm the Earth. Without them Earth's average temperature would be about **−18 °C** instead of **+15 °C**.
- Main GHGs and their share in warming (NCERT figure): **CO₂ ~60%**, **CH₄ ~20%**, **CFCs ~14%**, **N₂O ~6%**. **Water vapour** and **tropospheric ozone** are also GHGs.
- **Not greenhouse gases**: **N₂, O₂, argon** and other noble gases — simple symmetrical molecules or single atoms that do not absorb infrared.
- Global warming potential (100 years, approx.): CO₂ = 1, CH₄ ~28, N₂O ~265, SF₆ ~23,500.
- Effects: melting ice caps and glaciers, sea-level rise, El Niño intensity, extreme weather. Earth has warmed about **1.1–1.2 °C** above pre-industrial levels (IPCC AR6).
- Agreements: **UNFCCC** (Rio, 1992); **Kyoto Protocol** (1997, in force 2005); **Paris Agreement** (2015; limit warming well below 2 °C, aim 1.5 °C).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is not a greenhouse gas? — (A) Methane (B) Carbon dioxide (C) Argon." **Official answer: (C) Argon.**

### Ozone depletion

- **Good ozone** is in the **stratosphere** (ozone layer, roughly 15–35 km up) — absorbs harmful **UV-B**. **Bad ozone** in the troposphere is a pollutant and GHG.
- Thickness measured in **Dobson units (DU)**; normal ~300 DU; an "**ozone hole**" means an area where total ozone falls below **220 DU**.
- Biggest hole: over **Antarctica**, in the southern **spring (September–October)**, first reported in **1985** (Farman and colleagues).
- Cause: **CFCs** (refrigerants, aerosols) release **chlorine** atoms in the stratosphere under UV; Cl acts as a **catalyst** — one Cl atom can destroy many thousands of O₃ molecules (often quoted ~100,000). Halons (bromine) and CCl₄ also deplete ozone.
- UV-B effects: **skin cancer**, cataract, **snow blindness** (inflamed cornea), DNA damage.
- Agreements: **Vienna Convention (1985)**, **Montreal Protocol** (signed 16 September **1987**; in force 1989), **Kigali Amendment (2016)** to phase down **HFCs**. **World Ozone Day — 16 September**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Ozone hole refers to — (A) decrease in ozone concentration in stratosphere (B) decrease in the thickness of ozone layer in stratosphere (C) increase in the thick layer of ozone in stratosphere." NCERT describes it as the **thinning of the ozone layer** (B); technically the measured quantity is a fall in total ozone (A), which is the same thing expressed differently. No official key is in our files — in the exam, consider "(D) More than one of the above" if both (A) and (B) look correct to you. (C) is plainly wrong.

## 5. Pollution

### Air pollution

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Particulate matter (PM2.5, PM10)** | Vehicles, industry, burning | **PM2.5** (≤ 2.5 µm) most harmful; goes deep into lungs |
| **CO** | Incomplete combustion | Binds haemoglobin |
| **SO₂, NOₓ** | Coal power plants, vehicles | **Acid rain** (damages Taj Mahal marble — "stone cancer"), lung disease |
| Hydrocarbons + NOₓ + sunlight | Vehicles | **Photochemical smog** (ozone, PAN) |
| Lead | Old leaded petrol | Nervous damage |

- **Electrostatic precipitator** removes **over 99%** of particulate matter from power-plant exhaust. **Scrubber** (water or lime spray) removes **SO₂**.
- **Catalytic converter** (platinum–palladium and **rhodium**) converts CO and hydrocarbons to CO₂ and H₂O, and NO to N₂; vehicles fitted with it must use **unleaded petrol** (lead poisons the catalyst).
- **Bharat Stage VI** norms nationwide from **1 April 2020**. **CNG** buses in Delhi.
- **Noise** is also an air pollutant under the **Air Act** (amended 1987). Sound above ~**150 dB** can burst eardrums.

### Water pollution, BOD and eutrophication

- **BOD (biochemical oxygen demand)** = amount of O₂ that microbes need to break down the organic matter in a given volume of water (usually over 5 days at 20 °C). **Higher BOD = more organic pollution**. Clean water BOD < 5 mg/L; raw sewage may be 200–600 mg/L.
- **COD** (chemical oxygen demand) — O₂ needed to oxidise all organic matter chemically; always ≥ BOD.
- Sewage → microbes use up dissolved O₂ → **fish die**.
- **Eutrophication**: natural ageing of a lake by nutrient enrichment; **cultural (accelerated) eutrophication** from sewage, detergents (phosphates) and fertiliser run-off → **algal bloom** → O₂ depletion. **Water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*)** — "**terror of Bengal**", the world's most problematic aquatic weed.
- **Biomagnification**: concentration of a toxic, non-biodegradable chemical **increases at each trophic level** — e.g. **DDT** (water 0.003 ppb → fish-eating birds 25 ppm) causes thin eggshells and fall in bird populations; **mercury** → **Minamata disease** (Japan); cadmium → **itai-itai**.
- Groundwater problems: **arsenic** in the Ganga belt of **Bihar** (Bhojpur, Buxar, Patna, Vaishali, Bhagalpur …) → skin lesions, cancer; **fluoride** in south Bihar → **fluorosis**; **nitrate** → **blue baby syndrome** (methaemoglobinaemia).

### Sewage (wastewater) treatment

| Stage | Type | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Physical** | **Screening/filtration** removes floating debris; grit and soil removed by **sedimentation**; settled solids = **primary sludge**; liquid = primary effluent |
| **Secondary (biological)** | **Biological** | Primary effluent goes to **aeration tanks**; air is pumped in; aerobic microbes form **flocs** (bacteria + fungal filaments) that eat organic matter → **BOD is greatly reduced**; effluent goes to a settling tank; settled flocs = **activated sludge** (part returned to the aeration tank as inoculum); rest pumped to **anaerobic sludge digesters**, where anaerobic bacteria make **biogas** (methane, H₂S, CO₂) |
| **Tertiary (advanced)** | Chemical/physical | Removes remaining nutrients (N, P), colour, toxic substances; **disinfection** (chlorine, UV) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "One of the purposes of secondary treatment of waste-water and sewage is — (A) to increase the chlorine content (B) to reduce the BOD (C) to encourage the formation of PCBs." **(B) to reduce the BOD.** Chlorination belongs to the disinfection (tertiary) step; PCBs are toxic pollutants, never "encouraged". (No official key in our files.)

Schemes: **Ganga Action Plan (1985)**, **Yamuna Action Plan (1993)**, **Namami Gange (2014)**; **Swachh Bharat Mission (2 October 2014)**.

### Solid, plastic and radioactive waste

- Municipal waste → sanitary landfills; **e-waste** (computers, phones) — recycling of copper, gold, iron.
- **Polyblend** — fine plastic powder mixed with bitumen for roads (**Ahmed Khan**, Bengaluru). Single-use plastic items banned in India from **1 July 2022**.
- Disasters: **Bhopal gas tragedy** (2–3 December 1984, **methyl isocyanate**, Union Carbide); **Chernobyl** (1986, Ukraine); Fukushima (2011, Japan).

## 6. Biodiversity

- Term popularised by **Edward Wilson**. Three levels: **genetic** (e.g. *Rauwolfia vomitoria* varying in reserpine content; India has **50,000+ rice strains** and ~**1,000 mango varieties**), **species**, **ecological** (India's deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands, estuaries, alpine meadows).
- Estimated global species: ~**7 million** (Robert May); about **1.5 million** described. **Insects** are the largest group (~70% of animals).
- **India**: only **2.4%** of world land area but about **8.1% of global species diversity**; one of **17 megadiverse countries**. ~45,000 plant species and about twice as many animal species are recorded (NCERT).

### Patterns

- **Latitudinal gradient**: diversity **decreases from the equator to the poles**; tropical rainforest of the **Amazon** is the richest. Reasons: tropics were less disturbed by glaciations, have stable climate, more solar energy.
- **Species–area relationship** (**Alexander von Humboldt**): log S = log C + Z log A. **Z** (slope) is about **0.1–0.2** for small areas, and **0.6–1.2** for very large areas such as whole continents.
- **Rivet-popper hypothesis** (Paul Ehrlich): each species is like a rivet in an aeroplane — losing a few may not matter, but losing many (or a key one) makes the ecosystem collapse.
- **David Tilman**: plots with more species show less year-to-year variation in biomass (more stable).

### Causes of loss — "The Evil Quartet"

1. **Habitat loss and fragmentation** — the **most important cause** (tropical rainforests once covered 14% of land, now ~6%; Amazon cleared for soybean and cattle).
2. **Over-exploitation** — **Steller's sea cow**, **passenger pigeon**.
3. **Alien species invasions** — **Nile perch** in Lake Victoria wiped out 200+ cichlid fish species; **Parthenium** (carrot grass), **Lantana**, **water hyacinth**; illegal **African catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)** threatens native catfish.
4. **Co-extinctions** — a plant and its obligate pollinator; a host and its parasite.

Recent extinctions: **dodo** (Mauritius), **quagga** (Africa), **thylacine** (Australia), **Steller's sea cow** (Russia), three tiger subspecies (**Bali, Javan, Caspian**). The **IUCN Red List** (2004) documents **784 extinctions** in the last 500 years (NCERT).

### Biodiversity hotspots

- Concept by **Norman Myers (1988)**. Criteria: at least **1,500 endemic vascular plant species** and loss of **at least 70%** of original habitat. **36 hotspots** worldwide; they cover less than 2.5% of land but hold a very large share of species.
- **India has parts of 4**: **Himalaya**, **Indo-Burma** (North-East), **Western Ghats and Sri Lanka**, **Sundaland** (Nicobar Islands).

## 8. Conservation

| In situ (on site) | Ex situ (off site) |
|---|---|
| **Biosphere reserves** (core, buffer, transition zones; UNESCO **MAB** 1971) | **Zoological parks**, **botanical gardens** |

**People's movements**: **Bishnoi** community — **Amrita Devi Bishnoi** and 363 others died protecting khejri trees (1731, **Khejarli**, Jodhpur) — Amrita Devi Bishnoi Wildlife Protection Award; **Chipko** (1973, Chamoli, Uttarakhand; Gaura Devi, Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt); **Appiko** (1983, Karnataka); Silent Valley (Kerala); **Joint Forest Management** (1980s; resolution 1990).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Argon, N₂, O₂** are **not** greenhouse gases; water vapour **is** one.
- **Pyramid of energy** can never be inverted; pyramid of **biomass** is inverted in the **sea**; pyramid of **number** inverted in a **tree** ecosystem.
- Energy flow is **one-way**; nutrients **cycle**.
- **Phosphorus** cycle has no significant gaseous phase.
- *Nitrosomonas* makes **nitrite**; *Nitrobacter* makes **nitrate**; *Pseudomonas* **denitrifies**.
- **Stratospheric** ozone is good; **tropospheric** ozone is a pollutant.
- **Ozone hole** is over **Antarctica** in **spring** (Sept–Oct), not in winter.
- **Montreal** = ozone; **Kyoto/Paris** = climate; **Cartagena** = biosafety; **Nagoya** = access and benefit sharing; **Ramsar** = wetlands.
- **High BOD = polluted water** (more organic matter), not clean water.
- **Secondary treatment is biological** and reduces BOD; **primary is physical**.
- **Biomagnification** happens with **non-biodegradable** chemicals (DDT, mercury).
- **Lichens** are pioneers on bare rock; **phytoplankton** in water.

## Practice with answers

1. Which pyramid is always upright? — **Pyramid of energy**.
2. Why is the pyramid of biomass inverted in the sea? — **Small phytoplankton biomass supports a larger fish biomass** (fast turnover).
3. Producers fix 20,000 J. How much reaches secondary consumers? — **200 J** (20,000 → 2,000 → 200).
4. Name the pioneer species of a bare rock. — **Lichens**.
5. Orchid growing on a mango branch is an example of? — **Commensalism**.
6. Which bacterium converts nitrite into nitrate? — ***Nitrobacter***.
7. Which cycle has no gaseous phase of note? — **Phosphorus cycle**.
8. Which gas contributes most to global warming? — **CO₂**.
9. Ozone hole is measured in which unit? — **Dobson units** (below 220 DU).
10. Which protocol controls ozone-depleting substances? — **Montreal Protocol (1987)**.
11. What does high BOD indicate? — **Heavy organic pollution** of water.
12. Which stage of sewage treatment produces activated sludge? — **Secondary (biological) treatment**.
13. Gases produced in anaerobic sludge digesters? — **Methane, H₂S and CO₂** (biogas).
14. Minamata disease is caused by? — **Mercury**.
15. How many biodiversity hotspots does India share? — **Four** (Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, Sundaland).
16. Which invasive fish destroyed cichlids of Lake Victoria? — **Nile perch**.
17. Which forest type is most widespread in India? — **Tropical deciduous (monsoon) forests**.
18. Where is the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary? — **Bhagalpur** (Sultanganj to Kahalgaon stretch of the Ganga).
19. Bihar's largest wildlife sanctuary? — **Kaimur WLS**.
20. Which act set up the Pollution Control Boards? — **Water Act, 1974**.
