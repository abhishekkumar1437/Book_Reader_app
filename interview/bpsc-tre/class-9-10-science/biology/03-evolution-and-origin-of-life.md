This chapter follows NCERT class 10 "Heredity and Evolution" and class 12 "Evolution", plus the B.Sc. zoology points (connecting links, recapitulation, Weismann, types of selection, geological eras).
TRE asks very direct one-liners: "According to Oparin, which gas was not present in the primitive atmosphere", "homologous organs are an example of", "Darwin's book", "eugenics is the study of", "industrial melanism".

> **In simple words:** Life began in the early oceans when simple gases, with energy from lightning and UV light, joined into small organic molecules, then bigger ones, then the first cells. Over very long times living things changed — species that suited their surroundings survived and left more children (natural selection). Fossils, similar body plans and DNA show that all living things are related.

## 2. Theories of origin of life

| Theory | Idea | Who / status |
|---|---|---|
| Special creation | God created all species in their present form; earth about 4000 years old | Religious; rejected |
| **Spontaneous generation (abiogenesis)** | Life arises from non-living matter (e.g. maggots from rotten meat) | Aristotle; **disproved** |
| Biogenesis | Life comes only from pre-existing life | Francesco Redi (meat in jars, 1668), Spallanzani, **Louis Pasteur (swan-neck flask, 1859–1864)** |
| **Panspermia (cosmozoic)** | Life (spores) came from outer space | Richter, **Arrhenius** |
| **Chemical evolution** | Life arose from inorganic molecules by gradual chemical steps | **A. I. Oparin (Russia, 1924)** and **J. B. S. Haldane (England, 1929)** — accepted model |

### Oparin–Haldane hypothesis (chemical evolution)

1. Primitive earth was very hot; its atmosphere was **reducing** — it had **methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen (H₂) and water vapour (H₂O)**, plus CO₂, N₂ and HCN by some accounts. **Free oxygen (O₂) was absent.** (Oxygen was locked in water and oxides.)
2. Energy from **lightning (electric discharge), UV rays** (no ozone layer then), volcanic heat and radioactivity made these gases react.
3. Simple organic molecules formed — sugars, amino acids, nitrogen bases, fatty acids — and collected in the oceans: Haldane called this the **"hot dilute soup" (prebiotic soup)**.
4. They joined into polymers (proteins, nucleic acids).
5. Polymers clumped into droplets surrounded by water/lipid layers — Oparin's **coacervates**; Sidney Fox's **microspheres** (proteinoids). These were **protobionts** (pre-cells).
6. First cells: **anaerobic, heterotrophic prokaryotes** in water. Later came **chemoautotrophs**, then **photoautotrophs** (cyanobacteria) which released O₂ → ozone layer → life could move on to land.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "According to Oparin, which of the following was not present in the primitive atmosphere of the Earth? (A) Oxygen (B) Hydrogen (C) Water vapour …". Answer: **(A) Oxygen**. The early atmosphere was reducing — free O₂ came much later, from photosynthesis.

> **Memory trick:** Early atmosphere = "**MAHW**" — **M**ethane, **A**mmonia, **H**ydrogen, **W**ater vapour. No O₂.

### Miller–Urey experiment (1953)

- **Stanley Miller** (student) and **Harold Urey** tested Oparin–Haldane in the laboratory.
- In a closed glass apparatus they put **CH₄, NH₃, H₂ and water vapour** (some guidebooks quote the ratio CH₄ : NH₃ : H₂ = 2 : 1 : 2), boiled water to make vapour (NCERT says the mixture was "at 800 °C"), and passed **electric sparks** (to imitate lightning). A condenser cooled the products.
- After about one week they found **amino acids** (glycine, alanine, aspartic acid) and other simple organic compounds in the collected water.
- Later similar experiments produced sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats.
- It proved that **organic molecules can form from inorganic gases** under early-earth conditions — supporting chemical evolution. Meteorite analysis (e.g. Murchison meteorite) also shows similar compounds in space.

> **Exam angle:** In Miller's experiment **oxygen was not used**, and the energy source was **electric discharge**. The first products were **amino acids**, not proteins or cells.

## 3. Evidence for evolution

### (a) Palaeontological — fossils

- **Fossils** are remains or impressions of past organisms preserved in sedimentary rocks. Deeper layers = older. Age by **radioactive dating** (Carbon-14 for up to about 50,000 years; uranium–lead and potassium–argon for older rocks). Half-life of C-14 ≈ **5730 years**.
- **Archaeopteryx** (Jurassic) — fossil **connecting link between reptiles and birds**: feathers, wings and beak like a bird; teeth, clawed fingers and a long bony tail like a reptile.
- Horse evolution (Eohippus → Mesohippus → Merychippus → Pliohippus → Equus) is a classic fossil series — size increased, toes reduced to one.

**Living connecting links** (B.Sc. favourite):

| Organism | Links |
|---|---|
| ***Euglena*** | Plants and animals (has chloroplasts and moves like an animal) |
| ***Proterospongia*** | Protozoa and sponges |
| ***Peripatus*** | **Annelida and Arthropoda** |
| ***Neopilina*** | Annelida and Mollusca |
| ***Balanoglossus*** | Non-chordates and chordates (Hemichordata) |
| **Lungfish** (*Protopterus*, *Neoceratodus*) | Fishes and amphibians |
| ***Sphenodon*** (tuatara) | Living fossil reptile |
| **Duck-billed platypus** (*Ornithorhynchus*) and **echidna** | **Reptiles and mammals** (egg-laying mammals) |
| *Archaeopteryx* (fossil) | Reptiles and birds |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following mammals lays eggs? (A) Echidna (B) Kangaroo (C) Loris …". Official answer: **(A) Echidna**. Echidna and platypus are monotremes (Prototheria); kangaroo is a marsupial (pouch).

Living fossils: *Ginkgo biloba* (plant), *Limulus* (king crab), *Sphenodon*, *Latimeria* (coelacanth fish), *Peripatus*.

### (b) Morphological and anatomical — homologous and analogous organs

| | **Homologous organs** | **Analogous organs** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin / structure | **Same origin and basic structure** | Different origin and structure |
| Function | Different | **Same** |
| Type of evolution | **Divergent evolution** (adaptive radiation) | **Convergent evolution** |
| Animal examples | **Forelimbs of human, whale (flipper), bat (wing), cheetah** — same bones (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); heart and brain of vertebrates | **Wings of butterfly (insect) and bird**; eye of octopus and mammal; flippers of penguin and dolphin |
| Plant examples | **Thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita*** (both modified stems) | **Sweet potato (root) and potato (stem)** — both store food |

> **Memory trick:** **H**omologous = **H**ouse plan same, rooms used differently. **A**nalogous = **A**ctivity same, plan different.

**Vestigial organs** — reduced, non-functional organs that were useful in ancestors: **vermiform appendix**, **nictitating membrane** (plica semilunaris in the eye), **coccyx (tail bone)**, **wisdom teeth**, **ear muscles (auricular muscles)**, body hair, segmental abdominal muscles. In plants: scale leaves of underground stems; in python — vestigial hind limbs.

**Atavism** — sudden reappearance of an ancestral character: tail in a newborn baby, extra nipples, thick body hair.

### (c) Embryological

- Vertebrate embryos look alike in early stages — all have **gill slits** and a tail at some stage.
- **Recapitulation theory / biogenetic law** — **Ernst Haeckel**: "**ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny**" (the development of an individual repeats the evolutionary history of its group). Disproved in its strong form by **Karl Ernst von Baer**, who noted that embryos never pass through the adult stages of other animals, only resemble their embryos.

### (d) Biochemical and molecular

Same genetic code, same 20 amino acids, ATP as energy currency, similar enzymes (cytochrome c). The fewer the DNA/protein differences, the closer the relationship (human–chimpanzee DNA about 98–99% similar).

### (e) Biogeographical and observed evolution

- **Darwin's finches** of the **Galápagos Islands** — many beak types evolved from one seed-eating ancestor for different foods → **adaptive radiation**.
- **Australian marsupials** (kangaroo, koala, Tasmanian wolf, sugar glider) radiated from one ancestor; placental mammals elsewhere evolved similar forms → **convergent evolution** between the two groups.
- **Industrial melanism** — peppered moth (*Biston betularia*) in **England**: before industrialisation (pre-1850) white moths were more common (lichen-covered pale tree trunks); after soot darkened the trunks, **dark (melanic) moths** increased because birds could not see them. Example of **natural selection (directional)**. When pollution fell, white moths returned.
- **Antibiotic resistance** in bacteria and **DDT resistance** in mosquitoes, **herbicide resistance** in weeds — evolution in our lifetime ("anthropogenic evolution").

## 4. Theories of evolution

### Lamarckism

- **Jean-Baptiste Lamarck** (French), book ***Philosophie Zoologique* (1809)**. He also coined the word "biology" (with Treviranus, 1802).
- Ideas: (i) internal vital force tends to increase size; (ii) environment creates new needs; (iii) **use and disuse of organs** — used organs grow, unused ones weaken; (iv) **inheritance of acquired characters** — changes in one's lifetime pass to children.
- Classic example: **long neck of giraffe** (ancestors stretched to reach high leaves). Others: loss of limbs in snakes, webbed feet of ducks.
- **Criticism**: **August Weismann** cut off the tails of mice for **21–22 generations**; tailless mice were never born → acquired characters are not inherited. His **germplasm theory**: only changes in germ cells (gametes) are inherited, not in body cells (somatoplasm).
- Neo-Lamarckism — revived some ideas (Cope, Spencer).

### Darwinism (theory of natural selection)

- **Charles Darwin** (England), voyage on **HMS Beagle (1831–1836)**, visited the Galápagos. Book ***On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection* (24 November 1859)**.
- **Alfred Russel Wallace** independently reached the same idea from the **Malay Archipelago**; joint paper read in 1858.
- Influences: **Thomas Malthus** (*Essay on Population* — populations grow geometrically, food arithmetically); Charles Lyell (*Principles of Geology*).
- Main points:
  1. **Overproduction** (prodigality of reproduction) — organisms produce far more offspring than can survive.
  2. **Struggle for existence** — intraspecific (most intense), interspecific and environmental.
  3. **Variation** — no two individuals are alike; some variations are heritable.
  4. **Survival of the fittest / natural selection** — individuals with useful variations survive and reproduce more. Term "survival of the fittest" was coined by **Herbert Spencer**. Fitness = **reproductive fitness** (leaving more offspring).
  5. **Origin of new species** — favourable variations accumulate over generations.
- Weakness: Darwin could not explain the **source of variation** or how variations are inherited (he did not know Mendel's work); he also suggested "pangenesis", which was wrong.

### Mutation theory

- **Hugo de Vries** (Dutch), working on **evening primrose (*Oenothera lamarckiana*)**. Coined "mutation".
- New species arise by **sudden, large, heritable changes (mutations)** in a single step — **saltation** — not by slow small variations as Darwin said.
- Mutations are random and directionless. (Many of de Vries's "mutants" were later found to be chromosomal rearrangements, but the theory added the idea of mutation as the source of variation.)

### Modern synthetic theory (Neo-Darwinism)

- Combines Darwin + Mendelian genetics + population genetics. Contributors: **Theodosius Dobzhansky** (*Genetics and the Origin of Species*, 1937), R. A. Fisher, J. B. S. Haldane, Sewall Wright, **Ernst Mayr**, G. L. Stebbins, **Julian Huxley** (coined "evolutionary synthesis").
- Five factors: **gene mutation, chromosomal mutation, genetic recombination, natural selection, reproductive isolation**. Gene flow and genetic drift also change gene frequencies.

| Theory | Key person | Key word |
|---|---|---|
| Inheritance of acquired characters | Lamarck | Use and disuse; giraffe |
| Germplasm | Weismann | Tail-cutting in mice |
| Natural selection | Darwin, Wallace | Survival of the fittest |
| Mutation | Hugo de Vries | *Oenothera*, saltation |
| Modern synthetic | Dobzhansky, Mayr, Huxley, Stebbins | Population genetics |
| Chemical origin of life | Oparin, Haldane | Coacervate, prebiotic soup |

## 5. Hardy–Weinberg principle

- **G. H. Hardy** (English mathematician) and **Wilhelm Weinberg** (German physician), 1908.
- In a large, randomly mating population with **no mutation, no migration (gene flow), no genetic drift, no selection and no recombination effect**, **allele and genotype frequencies stay constant** generation after generation (genetic equilibrium).

<div class="formula">

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Allele frequencies | p + q = 1 |
| Genotype frequencies | p² + 2pq + q² = 1 |
| p² | frequency of AA (homozygous dominant) |
| 2pq | frequency of Aa (heterozygous carriers) |
| q² | frequency of aa (homozygous recessive) |

</div>

**Five factors that disturb the equilibrium** (and so cause evolution): **gene migration (gene flow), genetic drift, mutation, genetic recombination, natural selection**.

- **Genetic drift** — random change in gene frequency by chance, strong in small populations. **Founder effect**: a few individuals start a new population with different allele frequencies (founders). **Bottleneck effect**: population drastically reduced by disaster.

**Worked example 1.** In a population, 16% of people are homozygous recessive (aa). Find p, q and the carrier frequency.
q² = 0.16 → q = **0.4**; p = 1 − 0.4 = **0.6**; carriers 2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = **0.48 (48%)**; AA = p² = **0.36**.
Check: 0.36 + 0.48 + 0.16 = 1.00.

**Worked example 2.** 1 in 10,000 babies has an autosomal recessive disease. Carrier frequency?
q² = 1/10,000 → q = 1/100 = 0.01; p = 0.99; 2pq = 2 × 0.99 × 0.01 = **0.0198 ≈ 2% (about 1 in 50)**.

**Worked example 3.** Frequency of allele A is 0.7. What fraction of the population is heterozygous?
q = 0.3; 2pq = 2 × 0.7 × 0.3 = **0.42**.

### Types of natural selection

| Type | Which individuals favoured | Effect on curve | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stabilising** | Average (middle) | Peak gets taller and narrower | Human birth weight |
| **Directional** | One extreme | Peak shifts to one side | Industrial melanism; antibiotic resistance; giraffe neck (Darwinian view) |
| **Disruptive** | Both extremes | Two peaks form | Beak size in African seed-cracker finches |

## 6. Speciation and isolation

- **Species** (biological concept by **Ernst Mayr**): a group of interbreeding natural populations reproductively isolated from others.
- **Allopatric speciation** — populations separated by a **geographical barrier** (mountain, river, sea), e.g. Darwin's finches. Most common.
- **Sympatric speciation** — within the same area, by reproductive isolation, often **polyploidy in plants** (e.g. bread wheat).
- **Reproductive isolating mechanisms**: pre-zygotic (habitat, seasonal, behavioural, mechanical, gametic) and post-zygotic (hybrid inviability, **hybrid sterility — mule** = male donkey × female horse, sterile).

## 9. Eugenics and related terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|

## Traps and exceptions

- **Oxygen was absent** in the primitive atmosphere; hydrogen, methane, ammonia and water vapour were present.
- Miller–Urey made **amino acids**, not proteins or living cells; energy was electric discharge.
- **Coacervates — Oparin**; **microspheres — Sidney Fox**; "hot dilute soup" — Haldane.
- **Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation**; he did not propose chemical evolution.
- Wings of bird and **bat** = homologous (forelimbs); wings of bird and **butterfly** = analogous.
- Thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita* = **homologous** (both stems). Potato and sweet potato = **analogous**.
- Homologous organs → **divergent** evolution; analogous → **convergent**.
- **Peripatus** links annelids and arthropods, not reptiles and birds (that is *Archaeopteryx*).
- **Echidna and platypus lay eggs**; kangaroo has a pouch but does not lay eggs.
- **Weismann** disproved Lamarck (mice tails); Lamarck's book is *Philosophie Zoologique* (1809), Darwin's is *Origin of Species* (1859).
- "Survival of the fittest" was coined by **Herbert Spencer**, not Darwin.
- **de Vries** worked on *Oenothera*, not pea; he was one of the rediscoverers of Mendel.
- Industrial melanism: **dark moths increased** in polluted areas.
- **Homo habilis did not eat meat** (NCERT); *Homo erectus* ate meat. Neanderthals buried their dead.
- Hardy–Weinberg: 2pq is carriers; q² is affected — do not mix them.
- **Eugenics ≠ euthenics ≠ euphenics.**
