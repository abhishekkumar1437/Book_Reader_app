This chapter covers the cell (NCERT class 9 "The Fundamental Unit of Life", class 11 "Cell: The Unit of Life" and "Biomolecules") and the B.Sc. cell-biology points that TRE papers touch: prokaryotic membranes and mesosomes, ribosome types, basic and acidic amino acids, nucleotides versus nucleosides, and enzyme classes.
TRE asks one-line facts here: "main function of ribosomes", "ATP is formed in", "most abundant protein", "basic amino acid", "basic unit of nucleic acid". Almost every paper has 3–5 questions from this chapter.

> **In simple words:** A cell is a tiny room with a wall or skin (membrane), a control office (nucleus), power plants (mitochondria), factories (ribosomes), a packing-and-dispatch unit (Golgi), roads (ER) and a waste-disposal unit (lysosome). Everything inside is built from four kinds of molecules: sugars, fats, proteins and nucleic acids. Proteins that speed up reactions are called enzymes.

## 1. Discovery of the cell and the cell theory

| Event | Scientist | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Saw dead cork cells, coined the word "cell" (book *Micrographia*) | Robert Hooke | 1665 |
| First saw living cells (bacteria, protozoa) with his own lens | Antonie van Leeuwenhoek | 1674 |
| Discovered the nucleus (in orchid root cells) | Robert Brown | 1831 |
| Coined "protoplasm" | J. E. Purkinje | 1839 |
| Cell theory — plants are made of cells | M. J. Schleiden (botanist) | 1838 |
| Cell theory — animals are made of cells | Theodor Schwann (zoologist) | 1839 |
| "Omnis cellula e cellula" — all cells arise from pre-existing cells | Rudolf Virchow | 1855 |
| Electron microscope | Knoll and Ruska | 1931 |
| Fluid mosaic model of membrane | S. J. Singer and G. Nicolson | 1972 |

**Modern cell theory:** (i) all living organisms are made of cells and cell products; (ii) the cell is the basic unit of structure and function; (iii) all cells arise from pre-existing cells (Virchow's addition). Schleiden and Schwann did not explain how new cells form — that gap was filled by Virchow.

> **Exam angle:** Viruses are the standard "exception" to cell theory — they are not made of cells.

## 2. Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells

**Prokaryote** (pro = before, karyon = nucleus): no true nucleus, no membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), mycoplasma, archaea.
**Eukaryote** (eu = true): true nucleus with nuclear envelope, membrane-bound organelles. Protists, fungi, plants, animals.

| Feature | Prokaryotic cell | Eukaryotic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | No envelope; DNA lies in a region called **nucleoid** | True nucleus with double-membrane envelope |
| DNA | Single circular, naked (no histones); extra small circles = **plasmids** | Several linear chromosomes with histone proteins |
| Ribosomes | **70S** (50S + 30S) | **80S** (60S + 40S) in cytoplasm; 70S inside mitochondria and chloroplasts |
| Membrane organelles | Absent (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosome) | Present |
| Respiration site | Plasma membrane (and mesosomes) | Mitochondria |
| Cell wall | Peptidoglycan (murein) in bacteria | Cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi), absent in animals |
| Cell division | Binary fission (no mitosis) | Mitosis and meiosis |
| Size | 1–10 µm | 10–100 µm |

### The prokaryotic cell envelope and membrane (B.Sc. point, asked in TRE 3.0)

A bacterial cell has a three-layer **cell envelope**: outer **glycocalyx** (a loose slime layer or a tough capsule), then the **cell wall**, then the **plasma membrane**.

- The plasma membrane of prokaryotes is a **phospholipid bilayer with proteins**, structurally similar to the eukaryotic membrane, but bacterial membranes normally **lack sterols** (cholesterol). Mycoplasma is the exception that takes sterols from its host.
- Archaea have a different membrane: **ether-linked** lipids (bacteria and eukaryotes have ester-linked lipids). This is one reason Woese placed them in a separate domain.
- Because there are no mitochondria, the **respiratory enzymes sit on the plasma membrane**.
- **Mesosomes** are infoldings of the plasma membrane into the cytoplasm in the form of vesicles, tubules and lamellae. NCERT lists their functions: cell wall formation, DNA replication and distribution to daughter cells, respiration, secretion, and increasing membrane surface area and enzyme content. (Modern electron microscopy suggests mesosomes may be artefacts of fixation; for the exam, follow NCERT.)
- **Chromatophores**: membrane infoldings carrying pigments in photosynthetic bacteria and cyanobacteria.
- Other surface structures: **flagella** (motility; filament, hook, basal body), **pili** (tubular), **fimbriae** (bristle-like, attachment).
- Inclusion bodies (not membrane-bound): phosphate granules, cyanophycean granules, glycogen granules, gas vacuoles.

> **PYQ:** TRE 3.0 (2024) asked about the prokaryotic cell membrane (as reported in the PW paper analysis). Key facts: phospholipid bilayer, no sterols (except Mycoplasma), carries respiratory enzymes, folds into mesosomes.

## 4. Organelles one by one

### Cell wall
- Plants: cellulose, hemicellulose, pectin, proteins. Fungi: **chitin**. Bacteria: **peptidoglycan**. Algae: cellulose, galactans, mannans, calcium carbonate.
- Layers: **middle lamella** (calcium pectate; glues neighbouring cells) → primary wall (thin, can grow) → secondary wall (inside, thick, sometimes lignified).
- **Plasmodesmata** are cytoplasmic channels that join neighbouring plant cells through the wall.

### Plasma membrane (cell membrane)
- Made of **lipids and proteins** (and a little carbohydrate). In human RBC membrane about 52% protein and 40% lipid.
- **Fluid mosaic model** (Singer and Nicolson, 1972): a fluid phospholipid bilayer with proteins floating in it like a mosaic. Proteins are **peripheral** (on the surface) or **integral** (buried, partly or fully).
- Phospholipid: polar (water-loving) head outside, non-polar tails inside.
- Selectively permeable. Transport: **passive** (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion through carrier/channel proteins — along the gradient, no energy) and **active** (against the gradient, uses ATP, e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ pump).
- Endocytosis (taking in by forming vesicles): phagocytosis = cell eating, pinocytosis = cell drinking. Exocytosis = throwing out.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Plasma membrane is built up of (A) protein (B) lipid (C) carbohydrate (D) More than one of the above (E) None of the above." Both protein and lipid are main components (carbohydrate too, in small amounts), so the best answer is **(D) More than one of the above**. (No official key for TRE 1.0 is in our files.)

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Active transport takes place — (A) against concentration gradient and requires ATP …". Answer: **(A)**. Active transport always goes uphill and costs energy.

### Nucleus
- Discovered by Robert Brown (1831). Double membrane (**nuclear envelope**) with **nuclear pores**; outer membrane continuous with ER.
- Contains **nucleoplasm**, **chromatin** (DNA + histones; condenses into chromosomes during division) and the **nucleolus** (not membrane-bound; site of **rRNA synthesis** and ribosome assembly).
- Euchromatin = loosely packed, active; heterochromatin = densely packed, inactive.
- Cells without nucleus: mature mammalian **RBCs** and plant **sieve tube cells**.
- **DNA is mainly found in the nucleus.** Small amounts are also in mitochondria and chloroplasts (extra-nuclear DNA).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "DNA is mainly found in (A) nucleus (B) cytoplasm (C) nucleolus …". Official answer: **(A) nucleus**.

### Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)
- Network of tubules and sacs (cisternae). Seen by Porter, Claude and Fullam (1945).
- **Rough ER (RER)**: ribosomes on surface → protein synthesis and secretion. Abundant in cells that secrete proteins (pancreas, liver).
- **Smooth ER (SER)**: no ribosomes → **lipid and steroid synthesis**, detoxification (liver cells). In muscle, SER is called **sarcoplasmic reticulum** and stores Ca²⁺.

### Golgi apparatus
- Discovered by **Camillo Golgi (1898)**. Stack of flat, disc-shaped cisternae with a **cis (forming) face** towards ER and a **trans (maturing) face** away from it.
- Packaging and dispatch of proteins; **glycosylation** (adding sugar to proteins and lipids → glycoproteins, glycolipids); forms **lysosomes** and the **acrosome** of sperm; in plants forms the cell plate material.
- In plants Golgi bodies are scattered units called **dictyosomes**.

### Lysosomes
- Discovered by **Christian de Duve (1955)**. Single-membrane vesicles formed by Golgi, full of **acid hydrolase** enzymes (lipases, proteases, carbohydrases) active at acidic pH.
- Intracellular digestion; called **"suicidal bags"** because when the cell is damaged they burst and digest the cell (autolysis).

### Vacuoles
- Bounded by a single membrane called the **tonoplast**. Contain cell sap (water, sap, excretory products).
- Contractile vacuole in Amoeba and Paramecium — **osmoregulation and excretion**. Food vacuole in protists.

### Mitochondria — the powerhouse
- Seen by Kölliker; named "bioblasts" by Altmann (1890); the name **mitochondria** by **Benda (1898)**.
- **Double membrane**: outer smooth; inner folded into **cristae**; inner fluid = **matrix**. The inner membrane carries the electron transport chain and **ATP synthase** (F₀–F₁ particles, also called oxysomes).
- Site of **aerobic respiration** — Krebs cycle in the matrix, ETS and ATP formation on the inner membrane.
- **Semi-autonomous**: own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, divide by fission. Endosymbiotic origin from bacteria.
- Mitochondrial DNA is inherited from the **mother** only.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is formed in (A) mitochondria (B) ribosomes (C) Golgi bodies …". Official answer: **(A) mitochondria**. (Chloroplasts also make ATP in light, and a little ATP is made in cytoplasm during glycolysis — but among the given options only mitochondria fits.)

### Plastids
- Found in plants and some protists. Three types:
  - **Chloroplasts** — green, chlorophyll, photosynthesis.
  - **Chromoplasts** — coloured (carotene, xanthophyll), give yellow, orange, red colour to petals and fruits (tomato red = lycopene, carrot orange = carotene).
  - **Leucoplasts** — colourless storage: **amyloplasts** (starch; potato), **elaioplasts** (oils and fats), **aleuroplasts** (proteins).
- Chloroplast: double membrane; inner system of **thylakoids** stacked into **grana** (singular granum) joined by **stroma lamellae**; fluid **stroma** has enzymes of the Calvin cycle, 70S ribosomes and circular DNA. Light reaction on thylakoids, dark reaction in stroma.
- Chloroplasts are also semi-autonomous.

### Ribosomes
- Discovered by **George Palade (1953)** — called "Palade particles". Made of **rRNA and protein**; **no membrane**.
- **Main function: protein synthesis.**
- 70S in prokaryotes (50S + 30S), 80S in eukaryotes (60S + 40S). S = Svedberg unit, a measure of sedimentation rate — **not additive** (50 + 30 ≠ 70).
- Mg²⁺ is needed to hold the two subunits together.
- Several ribosomes on one mRNA = **polysome (polyribosome)** — the functional unit of protein synthesis.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The main function of ribosomes is (A) photosynthesis (B) protein synthesis (C) fat synthesis …". Official answer: **(B) protein synthesis**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "The functional unit of synthesis of protein is (A) peroxisome (B) polysome (C) lysosome …". Answer: **(B) polysome**.

### Cytoskeleton, centrosome, cilia and flagella
- **Cytoskeleton**: microtubules (tubulin), microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments. Support, movement, shape.
- **Centrosome**: two **centrioles** at right angles; each centriole has **9 triplets** of microtubules arranged like a cart-wheel; no central pair. Forms spindle poles in animal cells; basal bodies of cilia and flagella.
- **Cilia and flagella** (eukaryotic): **9 + 2 arrangement** of microtubules (9 peripheral doublets + 2 central singlets) in the axoneme. Bacterial flagella are structurally different (no 9+2).

### Microbodies
- **Peroxisomes**: contain catalase (breaks H₂O₂); in plants take part in **photorespiration**.
- **Glyoxysomes**: in germinating fatty seeds (castor), convert fat to carbohydrate (glyoxylate cycle).

## 5. Biomolecules — what a cell is made of

Water is the most abundant compound in a cell (about 70–90%). Elements by weight in the human body: O > C > H > N > Ca > P. In the earth's crust: O > Si > Al > Fe.

**Micromolecules** (small, molecular weight < 1000 Da; acid-soluble): sugars, amino acids, nucleotides, lipids. Lipids are small but appear in the acid-insoluble fraction because they form membranes.
**Macromolecules** (polymers, > 10,000 Da): proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides.

### Carbohydrates
General formula Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ. Hydrates of carbon.

| Type | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharide | Glucose, fructose (hexoses), ribose (pentose) | Glucose = blood sugar; fructose = fruit sugar, sweetest natural sugar |
| Disaccharide | Sucrose = glucose + fructose | Cane sugar; non-reducing |
| | Maltose = glucose + glucose | Malt sugar |
| | Lactose = glucose + galactose | Milk sugar |
| Polysaccharide | Starch (amylose + amylopectin) | Plant reserve; blue-black with iodine |
| | Glycogen | Animal reserve ("animal starch"); liver and muscle; more branched |
| | Cellulose | Plant wall; **most abundant organic compound on earth**; no colour with iodine |
| | Chitin | Fungal wall, arthropod exoskeleton; polymer of N-acetylglucosamine |
| | Inulin | Polymer of **fructose**; used to measure GFR |

- In polysaccharides, the right end is the **reducing end** and the left end non-reducing.
- Starch forms helices that hold iodine (blue colour); cellulose cannot.
- **Floridean starch** (reserve of red algae) is structurally similar to **amylopectin and glycogen**. Laminarin and mannitol are the reserves of brown algae.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Floridean starch has the structure similar to (A) amylopectin and glycogen (B) mannitol and algin (C) laminarin and cellulose …". Official answer: **(A)**.

### Lipids
- Insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents. Simple lipids (fats and oils = glycerol + 3 fatty acids = triglycerides), compound lipids (**phospholipids** like **lecithin**, glycolipids), derived lipids (**steroids** — cholesterol, sex hormones, vitamin D).
- **Saturated** fatty acids: no double bond, solid at room temperature (palmitic acid C16, stearic acid C18; ghee, animal fat). **Unsaturated**: one or more C=C, liquid (oleic, linoleic; vegetable oils).
- Fat gives about **9 kcal/g** (37 kJ/g); carbohydrate and protein about **4 kcal/g** (17 kJ/g).

### Amino acids
- Each has a central α-carbon bearing **H, –NH₂ (amino), –COOH (carboxyl) and an R group**. The R group decides its nature.
- **20** standard amino acids in proteins. **Glycine** (R = H) is the simplest and the only one that is **not optically active**.
- Amino acids exist as **zwitterions** (both + and − charges) at their isoelectric pH.
- **Essential amino acids** cannot be made by the body and must come from food: for adults, valine, leucine, isoleucine, lysine, methionine, threonine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, plus **histidine** (histidine is essential in infants; most textbooks now list 9 for adults). Arginine is semi-essential (needed by growing children).

| Class of R group | Amino acids | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** (extra –NH₂, positive) | **Lysine, Arginine, Histidine** | "LAH — basic" |
| **Acidic** (extra –COOH, negative) | **Aspartic acid, Glutamic acid** | "acid in the name" |
| Neutral non-polar | Glycine, alanine, valine, leucine, isoleucine, proline, methionine | — |
| Aromatic (benzene ring) | **Phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan** | "PTT" |
| Sulphur-containing | **Cysteine, methionine** | S–S bridges by cysteine |
| Hydroxyl (alcoholic) | Serine, threonine | — |
| Imino acid (ring) | **Proline** | Breaks helices |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Identify the basic amino acid from the following. (A) Glutamic acid (B) Lysine (C) Tyrosine …". Official answer: **(B) Lysine**. Glutamic acid is acidic; tyrosine is aromatic and neutral.

> **Memory trick:** "Basic Lovers Are Happy" — **L**ysine, **A**rginine, **H**istidine are basic.

### Proteins
- Polymers of amino acids joined by **peptide bonds** (–CO–NH–), formed by dehydration between –COOH of one and –NH₂ of the next. The chain has an **N-terminal** (left) and **C-terminal** (right) end.
- **Structure levels**:

| Level | What it is | Held by | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Sequence of amino acids | Peptide bonds | Insulin sequence (first sequenced, Sanger 1953–55) |
| Secondary | Local folding: **α-helix** (right-handed) or **β-pleated sheet** | Hydrogen bonds | Keratin (α), silk fibroin (β) |
| Tertiary | 3-D folding of one chain | H-bonds, ionic, hydrophobic, **disulphide (S–S)** bonds | Myoglobin, most enzymes |
| Quaternary | Two or more chains together | Same weak bonds | **Haemoglobin** (2α + 2β = 4 chains) |

- **Insulin is chemically a protein** (a small polypeptide hormone of **51 amino acids** in two chains, A = 21 and B = 30, joined by two disulphide bonds). First protein whose sequence was found (Frederick Sanger).

| Record / fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| Most abundant protein in **animals** (and in the human body) | **Collagen** (about 25–35% of body protein; connective tissue, skin, bone, tendon) |
| Most abundant protein in the **whole biosphere** | **RuBisCO** (the CO₂-fixing enzyme of chloroplasts) |
| Most abundant protein in blood plasma | Albumin |
| Protein in hair, nails, horn | Keratin |
| Oxygen carrier in RBC | Haemoglobin (chromoprotein, Fe²⁺) |
| Muscle contractile proteins | Actin and myosin |
| Blood clotting | Fibrinogen → fibrin |
| Plant lectins | Sugar-binding proteins (agglutinins) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is the most abundant protein in animals? (A) Lectin (B) Haemoglobin (C) Collagen …". Official answer: **(C) Collagen**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Chemically insulin is a (A) protein (B) steroid (C) carbohydrate …". Official answer: **(A) protein**.

| Conjugated protein | Non-protein part | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Glycoprotein | Carbohydrate | **Antibodies (immunoglobulins)**, mucin, many hormones (FSH, LH) |
| Lipoprotein | Lipid | LDL, HDL in blood |
| Nucleoprotein | Nucleic acid | Chromatin (DNA + histones), ribosomes |
| Chromoprotein | Coloured pigment | **Haemoglobin, cytochromes, phycobiliproteins**, chlorophyll–protein complexes |

| Metalloprotein | Metal ion | Ferritin (Fe), carbonic anhydrase (Zn) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Antibodies in our body are complex (A) steroids (B) prostaglandins (C) glycoproteins …". Official answer: **(C) glycoproteins**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "What are plant pigments? (A) Chromoprotein (B) Glycoprotein (C) Flavoprotein …". Official answer: **(A) Chromoprotein**. Pigments in plants (chlorophylls, phycobilins) occur bound to proteins as coloured complexes.

**Denaturation:** heat, strong acid/base or heavy metals destroy the secondary and tertiary structure (primary stays). Boiling an egg is denaturation of albumin. Function is lost.

### Nucleic acids — nucleosides and nucleotides

- **Nitrogen bases**: **purines** (double ring) = **adenine (A), guanine (G)**; **pyrimidines** (single ring) = **cytosine (C), thymine (T), uracil (U)**. T only in DNA; U only in RNA.
- **Sugar**: deoxyribose in DNA, ribose in RNA (5-carbon pentoses).

<div class="formula">

| Term | Made of | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleoside | **Base + sugar** | Adenosine, guanosine, cytidine, uridine, thymidine |
| Nucleotide | **Base + sugar + phosphate** (= nucleoside + phosphate) | Adenylic acid (AMP), guanylic acid, ATP |
| Nucleic acid | Chain of nucleotides (polynucleotide) | DNA, RNA |

</div>

- **The basic unit (monomer) of nucleic acid is the nucleotide.** Nucleotides join by **3′–5′ phosphodiester bonds**. Base attaches to sugar by an **N-glycosidic bond**.
- **Nucleoid** is not a molecule at all — it is the DNA region of a prokaryotic cell.
- ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is a nucleotide — the "energy currency" of the cell. Its two terminal phosphate bonds are high-energy bonds (about 7.3 kcal/mol each on hydrolysis).
- Other important nucleotides: NAD⁺, NADP⁺, FAD (coenzymes), cAMP (second messenger).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The basic unit of nucleic acid is (A) nucleotide (B) nucleoside (C) nucleoid …". Official answer: **(A) nucleotide**.

> **Memory trick:** Nucleo**t**ide has the extra **t** — think "**t** for **t**ail of phosphate". Nucleo**s**ide = **s**ugar + base only.

> **Memory trick:** "**Pure As Gold**" — **Pu**rines are **A** and **G**. "**CUT** the **Py**" — **Py**rimidines are **C, U, T**.

## 6. Enzymes

- Enzymes are **biological catalysts**, almost all **proteins**. Exceptions: **ribozymes** — RNA molecules with catalytic activity (e.g. peptidyl transferase in ribosomes, RNase P).
- Word "enzyme" coined by **Kühne (1878)**. First enzyme crystallised: **urease** by **J. B. Sumner (1926)**. Buchner (1897) showed that cell-free yeast extract can ferment sugar (zymase).
- They lower the **activation energy**; they do not change the equilibrium of the reaction, and they are not used up.
- **Active site**: the pocket where substrate binds.
  - **Lock and key model**: Emil Fischer (1894).
  - **Induced fit model**: Daniel Koshland (1958) — active site changes shape on binding.
- Factors: **temperature** (optimum ~37 °C in humans; low temperature inactivates reversibly, high temperature denatures permanently), **pH** (pepsin ~2, trypsin ~8), **substrate concentration** (rate rises then levels off at Vmax).
- **Km (Michaelis constant)**: substrate concentration at which the rate is half of Vmax. Low Km = high affinity.
- **Inhibition**: **competitive** — inhibitor resembles substrate and competes for the active site (e.g. **malonate inhibits succinate dehydrogenase**; Km rises, Vmax unchanged). Non-competitive — binds elsewhere (Vmax falls).
- **Cofactors**: non-protein helpers.
  - **Prosthetic group** — tightly bound organic part (haem in peroxidase, catalase).
  - **Coenzyme** — loosely bound organic part, often made from vitamins (NAD from niacin, FAD from riboflavin, coenzyme A from pantothenic acid).
  - **Metal ions** — Zn²⁺ for carboxypeptidase, Mg²⁺ for many kinases.
  - Protein part alone = **apoenzyme**; apoenzyme + cofactor = **holoenzyme**.

### Six classes of enzymes (IUB classification)

| No. | Class | Reaction | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oxidoreductases (dehydrogenases) | Oxidation–reduction | Lactate dehydrogenase, catalase |
| 2 | Transferases | Transfer a group (not H) | Transaminase, hexokinase |
| 3 | Hydrolases | Break bonds by adding water | Amylase, pepsin, lipase |
| 4 | Lyases | Remove groups without water, making double bonds | Aldolase, decarboxylase |
| 5 | Isomerases | Inter-convert isomers | Phosphohexose isomerase |
| 6 | Ligases (synthetases) | Join two molecules using ATP | DNA ligase |

(A seventh class, **translocases**, was added in 2018; most exam books still say six.)

> **Memory trick:** "**O**ver **T**he **H**ill **L**ives **I**ndian **L**ion" — Oxidoreductase, Transferase, Hydrolase, Lyase, Isomerase, Ligase.

### Digestive enzymes worth one mark

| Enzyme | Source | Acts on | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salivary amylase (ptyalin) | Saliva | Starch → maltose | pH 6.8 |
| Pepsin | Gastric juice | Proteins → peptides | Secreted as pepsinogen; pH ~2 |
| **Rennin** | Gastric juice of **infants / calves** | Milk protein casein → paracasein (curdling) | Used in cheese-making |

| Lipase | Pancreatic juice | Fats → fatty acids + glycerol | Needs bile salts |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The proteolytic enzyme renin is found in (A) intestinal juice (B) gastric juice (C) bile juice …". Official answer: **(E) None of the above**. Note the spelling: **renin** (one n) is a kidney enzyme acting on blood angiotensinogen, so none of the digestive juices fits. **Rennin** (double n) is the gastric milk-curdling enzyme. BPSC rewards this kind of careful reading.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Ribosomes, centrioles and nucleolus have no membrane.** Lysosome, vacuole, ER, Golgi have one membrane. Nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast have two.
- **Prokaryotes do have ribosomes** (70S) and a plasma membrane — they lack only membrane-bound organelles.
- **Mitochondria and chloroplasts have 70S ribosomes**, not 80S, even in eukaryotic cells.
- **Svedberg units do not add up**: 60S + 40S = 80S.
- Mature mammalian RBC and sieve tube cells have **no nucleus**; mammalian RBC also lacks mitochondria.
- Human body's most abundant protein = **collagen**; biosphere's most abundant protein = **RuBisCO**; most abundant organic compound = **cellulose**. Questions mix these.
- **Glycine** is the only non-chiral amino acid; **proline** is an imino acid.
- **Tyrosine** is aromatic, not basic. Glutamic acid and aspartic acid are acidic.
- **Nucleoside ≠ nucleotide ≠ nucleoid.** The unit of nucleic acid is the nucleotide.
- **Renin ≠ rennin.** Renin = kidney (blood pressure); rennin = infant stomach (milk curdling).
- **Not all enzymes are proteins** — ribozymes are RNA.
- **Inulin** is a fructose polymer, not insulin (a protein hormone).
- **Cell wall is absent in Mycoplasma** and in animal cells; fungal wall is chitin, not cellulose.
- **Bacterial flagella are not 9 + 2** — the 9 + 2 axoneme is only in eukaryotic cilia and flagella.
- Lysosomes were discovered by **de Duve**, Golgi by **Golgi**, ribosomes by **Palade**, nucleus by **Brown**.

## Quick revision

1. Cell coined by Robert Hooke (1665) from cork; living cells first seen by Leeuwenhoek.
2. Cell theory: Schleiden (1838) + Schwann (1839); "omnis cellula e cellula" — Virchow (1855).
3. Smallest cell: Mycoplasma (PPLO); largest: ostrich egg; longest human cell: neuron.
4. Prokaryotic DNA lies in the nucleoid; extra DNA circles are plasmids.
5. Prokaryotic membrane infoldings = mesosomes (wall formation, DNA replication, respiration).
6. Bacterial membranes lack sterols (except Mycoplasma); archaeal lipids are ether-linked.
7. Ribosomes: 70S (50S + 30S) prokaryote, 80S (60S + 40S) eukaryote; main job protein synthesis.
8. Polysome = many ribosomes on one mRNA — functional unit of protein synthesis.
9. Fluid mosaic model: Singer and Nicolson (1972); membrane = lipid + protein.
10. Active transport: against gradient, needs ATP.
11. Mitochondria: powerhouse, ATP synthesis, double membrane, cristae, own DNA.
12. Golgi: packaging, glycosylation, forms lysosomes and acrosome.
13. Lysosome: suicidal bag, acid hydrolases, discovered by de Duve.
14. Centriole: 9 triplets; cilia/flagella: 9 + 2.
15. Nucleolus makes rRNA; DNA is mainly in the nucleus.
16. Basic amino acids: lysine, arginine, histidine; acidic: aspartic and glutamic acid.
17. Most abundant protein in animals: collagen; in biosphere: RuBisCO.
18. Insulin: protein of 51 amino acids (A 21 + B 30).
19. Antibodies: glycoproteins; haemoglobin: chromoprotein; casein: phosphoprotein.
20. Nucleoside = base + sugar; nucleotide = base + sugar + phosphate.
21. Purines A, G; pyrimidines C, T, U.
22. Floridean starch ≈ amylopectin and glycogen.
23. Enzymes lower activation energy; ribozymes are RNA enzymes.
24. Lock and key — Fischer; induced fit — Koshland; urease first crystallised by Sumner.
25. Malonate is a competitive inhibitor of succinate dehydrogenase.

## Practice with answers

1. Who coined the term "cell"? — **Robert Hooke** (cork slices, *Micrographia*, 1665).
2. Which scientist added "all cells arise from pre-existing cells"? — **Rudolf Virchow** (1855).
3. Smallest known living cell? — **Mycoplasma** (PPLO, no cell wall).
4. Ribosome type in mitochondria? — **70S** (they are of bacterial origin).
5. Which organelle is called the suicidal bag? — **Lysosome** (bursts and digests the cell).
6. Organelle without any membrane: Golgi, ribosome or lysosome? — **Ribosome**.
7. Site of rRNA synthesis? — **Nucleolus**.
8. In prokaryotes, respiratory enzymes are found on? — **Plasma membrane / mesosomes** (no mitochondria).
9. Arrangement of microtubules in a cilium? — **9 + 2**.
10. Which is a basic amino acid: tyrosine, arginine or aspartic acid? — **Arginine** (extra amino group).
11. The only optically inactive amino acid? — **Glycine** (R = H).
12. Most abundant protein on earth? — **RuBisCO** (in animals it is collagen).
13. Casein of milk is which type of conjugated protein? — **Phosphoprotein**.
14. Adenosine is a nucleoside or a nucleotide? — **Nucleoside** (adenine + ribose, no phosphate).
15. Bond joining nucleotides in a strand? — **Phosphodiester bond** (3′–5′).
16. Which enzyme is an RNA molecule? — **Ribozyme**, e.g. peptidyl transferase.
17. Who proposed the induced fit model? — **Koshland** (1958).
18. To which class does DNA ligase belong? — **Ligases** (class 6; join molecules using ATP).
19. Reserve food of fungi and animals? — **Glycogen**.
20. Enzyme that curdles milk in the infant stomach? — **Rennin** (renin is a kidney enzyme).
