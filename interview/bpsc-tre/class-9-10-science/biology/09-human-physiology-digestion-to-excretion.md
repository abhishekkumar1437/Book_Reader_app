This chapter covers the "maintenance" systems of the human body: digestion, respiration, circulation and excretion. The base is NCERT class 10 (Life Processes) and class 11 (Human Physiology); the B.Sc. points are enzyme activation, oxygen dissociation, cardiac cycle and counter-current mechanism. TRE asks it as one-liners: "rennin/renin is found in", "intercostal muscles occur in", "what happens to breathing when blood O₂ falls".

> **In simple words:** Food is broken into small molecules by enzymes in the mouth, stomach and small intestine, then absorbed into blood. Lungs bring in O₂ and remove CO₂. The heart pumps blood that carries O₂, food and waste around the body. Kidneys filter the blood, keep what is useful, and throw out urea and extra water as urine. Learn each organ, its enzyme or hormone, and its numbers (pH, volumes, rates).

## 1. Nutrition basics

| Nutrient | Main job | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Energy (~4 kcal/g, about 17 kJ/g) | Starch, glycogen, sugars |
| Fats | Energy store (~9 kcal/g, about 37 kJ/g) | Highest energy value |
| Proteins | Body building, enzymes (~4 kcal/g) | 20 amino acids; 9 essential for adults |
| Vitamins | Regulate body processes | Needed in small amounts |
| Minerals | Bones, blood, nerves | Ca, P, Fe, I, Na, K |
| **Roughage (dietary fibre)** | **No nutrient value**, but adds bulk and prevents constipation | Mainly **cellulose** from plants |
| Water | Medium for all reactions | About 60–70% of body weight |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "The component of food that does not provide any nutrient to our body and yet is essential in our food is — (A) fat (B) carbohydrate (C) roughage." **(C) roughage.** Humans have no enzyme to digest cellulose.

### Vitamins and deficiency diseases

| Vitamin | Chemical name | Deficiency disease | Rich sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (fat-soluble) | Retinol | **Night blindness**, xerophthalmia | Carrot, liver, fish-liver oil, milk |
| **D** (fat-soluble) | Calciferol | **Rickets** (children), **osteomalacia** (adults) | Sunlight on skin, fish oil, eggs |
| **E** (fat-soluble) | Tocopherol | Sterility/infertility (in rats), weak muscles | Vegetable oils, germinating seeds |
| **K** (fat-soluble) | Phylloquinone | Delayed **blood clotting** | Green leaves; made by gut bacteria |
| **B₁** | Thiamine | **Beri-beri** | Whole grains, pulses |
| **B₂** | Riboflavin | Cracked lips and tongue (cheilosis) | Milk, eggs |
| **B₃** | Niacin (nicotinic acid) | **Pellagra** (4 Ds: dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia, death) | Groundnut, meat |
| **B₆** | Pyridoxine | Anaemia, convulsions | Cereals, liver |
| **B₉** | Folic acid | Megaloblastic anaemia; neural tube defects in baby | Green leaves |
| **B₁₂** | Cyanocobalamin (contains **cobalt**) | **Pernicious anaemia** | Animal foods only, curd |

> **Memory trick:** Fat-soluble vitamins are "**KEDA**" — K, E, D, A. They are stored in the liver and fat, so overdose (hypervitaminosis) is possible.

**Protein-energy malnutrition:** **Kwashiorkor** — protein deficiency with enough calories (swollen belly, oedema, child after weaning). **Marasmus** — deficiency of both protein and calories (wasting, "skin and bones", infant below one year).

**Collagen** is the **most abundant protein in the animal body** (skin, tendons, bone, cartilage). TRE 2.0 (2023) Q136 asked exactly this — **official answer: (C) collagen** (not haemoglobin). The most abundant protein on Earth is **RuBisCO** (in plants).

## 2. Digestive system

### The alimentary canal

Mouth → buccal cavity → **pharynx** → **oesophagus** → **stomach** → **small intestine** (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → **large intestine** (caecum with vermiform **appendix**, colon, rectum) → anus.

- **Teeth**: humans are **thecodont** (teeth in sockets of jaw), **diphyodont** (two sets — milk and permanent) and **heterodont** (four types). Adult dental formula **2123/2123** (incisors 2, canine 1, premolars 2, molars 3 in each half jaw) = **32** teeth. Milk teeth = **20**. Enamel is the **hardest substance in the body**.
- **Tongue** — muscular; papillae with taste buds.
- **Peristalsis** — wave-like muscle contraction that pushes food down.
- **Epiglottis** — flap that closes the windpipe during swallowing.
- **Sphincters**: cardiac (gastro-oesophageal) and pyloric (stomach to duodenum).

### Digestive glands

| Gland | Secretion | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Salivary glands** (3 pairs: **parotid** — largest, submandibular, sublingual) | Saliva with **salivary amylase (ptyalin)** and **lysozyme** | ~1.0–1.5 L/day; pH about 6.8 |
| **Gastric glands** in stomach wall | Gastric juice | Three cell types (below) |
| **Liver** — **largest gland** (about 1.2–1.5 kg) | **Bile** (no enzyme) | Stored in **gall bladder**; Kupffer cells |
| **Pancreas** — mixed gland | Pancreatic juice (exocrine) + insulin, glucagon (endocrine) | Duct joins bile duct as the **hepato-pancreatic duct** |
| **Intestinal glands** (crypts of Lieberkühn, Brunner's glands) | Succus entericus (intestinal juice) | Brush-border enzymes |

**Cells of gastric glands:**
- **Chief (peptic/zymogen) cells** — **pepsinogen** and **prorennin**.
- **Parietal (oxyntic) cells** — **HCl** and **Castle's intrinsic factor** (needed to absorb vitamin B₁₂).
- **Mucus neck cells** — mucus, which protects the stomach lining from acid.

### Every enzyme, where it acts

<div class="formula">

| Juice | Enzyme | Substrate → product |
|---|---|---|
| Saliva | Salivary amylase (ptyalin) | Starch → maltose (about 30% of starch digested in mouth) |
| Saliva | Lysozyme | Kills bacteria |
| Gastric juice | **Pepsin** (from pepsinogen, activated by **HCl**) | Proteins → proteoses and peptones |
| Gastric juice | **Rennin** (from prorennin; in **infants**) | Milk protein **casein → paracasein** (curdles milk; needs Ca²⁺) |
| Gastric juice | Gastric lipase (small amount) | Fats → fatty acids + glycerol |
| Pancreatic juice | **Trypsin** (from trypsinogen, activated by **enterokinase**) | Proteins, peptones → dipeptides |
| Pancreatic juice | Chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase | Proteins → peptides, amino acids |
| Pancreatic juice | Pancreatic amylase (amylopsin) | Starch → maltose |
| Pancreatic juice | Pancreatic lipase (steapsin) | Emulsified fats → fatty acids + glycerol (diglycerides, monoglycerides) |
| Pancreatic juice | Nucleases (DNase, RNase) | Nucleic acids → nucleotides |
| Intestinal juice | Dipeptidases | Dipeptides → amino acids |
| Intestinal juice | **Maltase, lactase, sucrase** | Maltose → glucose + glucose; lactose → glucose + galactose; sucrose → glucose + fructose |
| Intestinal juice | Nucleotidases, nucleosidases | Nucleotides → nucleosides → sugars + bases |
| Bile | **No enzyme** | **Bile salts emulsify fats** (break into tiny droplets); bile pigments **bilirubin, biliverdin** are waste |

</div>

- Stomach pH about **1.8–2** (very acidic); the small intestine is **alkaline** (pH about 7.8) because pancreatic juice and bile contain **bicarbonate**.
- **Enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** from the intestine activates trypsinogen → trypsin; trypsin then activates the others. Enzymes are released as inactive **zymogens** so they do not digest the gland itself.
- Most digestion is **completed** and most absorption happens in the **small intestine** (ileum and jejunum). Finger-like **villi** with **microvilli** increase surface area. Each villus has capillaries and a **lacteal** (lymph vessel) for fat absorption.
- Fats are absorbed as **micelles**, re-formed into **chylomicrons** in the intestinal cells, and go into the **lacteals**.
- **Large intestine** absorbs **water**, some minerals and drugs; bacteria there make vitamins K and some B.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The proteolytic enzyme **renin** is found in — (A) intestinal juice (B) gastric juice (C) bile juice." **Official answer: (E) None of the above.** Spelling decides the answer: **renin** (one *n*) is made by the **juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney** and acts on angiotensinogen in blood. **Rennin** (double *n*, also called chymosin) is the milk-curdling enzyme of **gastric juice**. If the exam writes "rennin", choose gastric juice; if "renin", none of these juices.

### Gastro-intestinal hormones

| Hormone | Source | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | Stomach (pyloric region) | Stimulates gastric juice and HCl |
| **Secretin** | Duodenum | Pancreas releases water and **bicarbonate**; first hormone ever discovered (**Bayliss and Starling, 1902**) |
| **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** | Duodenum | Gall bladder contracts (bile) and pancreas releases enzymes |
| **GIP** | Duodenum | Inhibits gastric secretion and movement |

### Liver — functions to remember

Bile formation; storage of **glycogen** (glucose → glycogen = glycogenesis); **urea formation** through the **ornithine cycle** (Krebs–Henseleit); detoxification; storage of vitamins A, D, B₁₂ and iron; makes plasma proteins (albumin, fibrinogen, prothrombin) and heparin; destroys old RBCs together with the spleen; produces RBCs in the **foetus**.

### Disorders

- **Jaundice** — liver affected; bile pigments deposit, skin and eyes turn **yellow**.
- **Vomiting** — reflex controlled by the **medulla**.
- **Diarrhoea** — frequent watery stools; treatment **ORS** (oral rehydration solution).
- **Constipation**, **indigestion** (lack of enzymes, anxiety, overeating).
- **Peptic ulcer** — often due to the bacterium ***Helicobacter pylori*** (Marshall and Warren, Nobel 2005).
- **Appendicitis** — inflammation of the vestigial appendix.

## 3. Respiration

### The pathway

Nostrils → nasal chamber → **pharynx** (common passage for food and air) → **larynx** (voice box; cartilage forms the "Adam's apple") → **trachea** (held open by **C-shaped cartilage rings**) → primary **bronchi** → bronchioles → **alveoli** (thin, vascular air sacs where gas exchange happens; about 300–500 million in both lungs).

- Lungs are covered by a double membrane, the **pleura**, with pleural fluid that reduces friction.
- The **diaphragm** separates the thoracic cavity from the abdomen.

### Mechanism of breathing

| Step | Inspiration (active) | Expiration (normally passive) |
|---|---|---|
| Diaphragm | **Contracts** and flattens (moves down) | Relaxes, becomes dome-shaped |
| **External intercostal muscles** (between the **ribs**) | **Contract** — ribs and sternum move up and out | Relax |
| Chest volume | Increases | Decreases |
| Lung pressure | Falls below atmospheric — air rushes in | Rises above atmospheric — air goes out |

- In **forced expiration**, the **internal intercostal muscles** and **abdominal muscles** contract.
- Normal breathing rate: **12–16 per minute** in a healthy adult.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Intercostal muscles occur in — (A) abdomen (B) diaphragm (C) ribs." **Official answer: (C) ribs.** *Inter-costal* literally means "between the ribs" (Latin *costa* = rib).

### Respiratory volumes (healthy adult, approx.)

<div class="formula">

| Volume | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Tidal volume (TV) | Air in or out in one normal breath | ~**500 mL** |
| Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV) | Extra air that can be forcibly inhaled | 2500–3000 mL |
| Expiratory reserve volume (ERV) | Extra air that can be forcibly exhaled | 1000–1100 mL |
| Residual volume (RV) | Air left in lungs even after forced expiration | 1100–1200 mL |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** = ERV + TV + IRV | Maximum air one can breathe out after a forced inhalation | ~3500–4600 mL |
| Total lung capacity (TLC) = VC + RV | Total air lungs can hold | ~5000–6000 mL |

</div>

**Worked example:** TV = 500 mL, breathing rate = 12/min. Air breathed per minute (pulmonary ventilation) = 500 × 12 = 6000 mL = **6 litres per minute**.

**Worked example:** IRV = 2500, TV = 500, ERV = 1000 mL. VC = 2500 + 500 + 1000 = **4000 mL**. If RV = 1200 mL, TLC = 4000 + 1200 = **5200 mL**.

### Exchange and transport of gases

Gases move by **simple diffusion** from higher to lower **partial pressure** (pO₂, pCO₂, in mm Hg).

| | Atmospheric air | Alveoli | Deoxygenated blood | Oxygenated blood | Tissues |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pO₂ | 159 | 104 | 40 | 95 | 40 |
| pCO₂ | 0.3 | 40 | 45 | 40 | 45 |

- CO₂ is **20–25 times more soluble** than O₂, so it diffuses faster per unit pressure difference.
- **O₂ transport**: about **97%** as **oxyhaemoglobin** in RBCs; **3%** dissolved in plasma. Each 100 mL of oxygenated blood delivers about **5 mL O₂** to the tissues.
- **CO₂ transport**: about **20–25%** as **carbamino-haemoglobin**, about **70%** as **bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻) through the enzyme **carbonic anhydrase** in RBCs, about **7%** dissolved in plasma.
- **Chloride shift (Hamburger shift)**: HCO₃⁻ moves out of RBCs into plasma and Cl⁻ moves in, to keep electrical balance.
- **Oxygen dissociation curve** is **sigmoid (S-shaped)**. High pCO₂, high H⁺ (low pH), high temperature and 2,3-BPG shift it to the **right** (O₂ released more easily in tissues) — the **Bohr effect**.
- **Carbon monoxide** binds haemoglobin about **200–250 times** more strongly than O₂ (carboxyhaemoglobin) — so CO is a dangerous poison in closed rooms with angithi/charcoal fires.

### Control of breathing

| Centre | Location | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory rhythm centre** | **Medulla oblongata** | Sets the basic rhythm |
| **Pneumotaxic centre** | **Pons** | Can shorten inspiration and so change the rate |
| **Chemosensitive area** | Next to the rhythm centre in the medulla | Highly sensitive to **CO₂ and H⁺** rise |
| **Peripheral chemoreceptors** | **Aortic arch** and **carotid bodies** | Sense rise in CO₂/H⁺ and **fall in O₂**; signal the medulla |

- The **main stimulus** for breathing is a rise in **CO₂ (and H⁺)**; O₂ plays only a small role under normal conditions.
- When O₂ in the blood falls (for example at high altitude), the peripheral chemoreceptors make the **breathing rate increase**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "When there is a decrease in the concentration of oxygen in the blood, the rate of breathing — (A) decreases (B) increases (C) does not change." **(B) increases** (via carotid and aortic bodies). No official key in our files, but this is standard physiology.

### Cellular respiration in one box

Glucose is oxidised in steps: **glycolysis** (cytoplasm) → link reaction and **Krebs cycle** (mitochondrial matrix) → **electron transport chain** and **oxidative phosphorylation** (inner mitochondrial membrane). Aerobic respiration gives about **36–38 ATP** per glucose (older textbooks) or ~30–32 (modern estimate). In muscle without enough O₂, pyruvate → **lactic acid** (fatigue, cramps). Detail is in [Plant Physiology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/plant-physiology).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is formed in — (A) mitochondria (B) ribosomes (C) Golgi bodies." **Official answer: (A) mitochondria** — the "power house of the cell". (ATP is also made in cytoplasm by glycolysis and in chloroplasts, but of the given options only mitochondria fit.)

### Respiratory disorders

- **Asthma** — narrowing of bronchi and bronchioles due to inflammation (wheezing).
- **Emphysema** — alveolar walls damaged, surface area falls; main cause **cigarette smoking**.
- **Occupational diseases** — **silicosis** (stone crushing, silica dust), **asbestosis** (asbestos), **byssinosis** (cotton dust), **anthracosis** (coal dust, "black lung").
- **Pneumonia** — alveoli fill with fluid (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*).
- **Tuberculosis** — *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

## 4. Blood and blood groups

### Composition

- **Plasma** (~55%): ~90–92% water; proteins 6–8% — **albumin** (osmotic balance), **globulins** (defence), **fibrinogen** (clotting); glucose, amino acids, salts, hormones. Plasma without clotting factors = **serum**.
- **Formed elements** (~45%):

| Cell | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **RBC (erythrocytes)** | **5–5.5 million per mm³** | Biconcave, **no nucleus** in mammals; contain **haemoglobin** (12–16 g per 100 mL); life ~**120 days**; made in **red bone marrow**; destroyed in the **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs") |
| **WBC (leucocytes)** | **6000–8000 per mm³** | Nucleated; life short |
| **Platelets (thrombocytes)** | **1.5–3.5 lakh per mm³** | Cell fragments from **megakaryocytes**; clotting |

**Types of WBC (memory: "Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas" — most to least):** **Neutrophils** 60–65% (most abundant, phagocytes) > **Lymphocytes** 20–25% (B and T cells, immunity) > **Monocytes** 6–8% (become macrophages) > **Eosinophils** 2–3% (allergy, worm infections) > **Basophils** 0.5–1% (least; release **histamine**, **serotonin**, **heparin**).

- **Leukaemia** — blood cancer with excess WBCs. **Anaemia** — low haemoglobin or RBCs. **Dengue** lowers the platelet count.

### Blood clotting (coagulation)

Injury → platelets and damaged tissue release **thromboplastin** → **prothrombin → thrombin** (needs **Ca²⁺**) → **fibrinogen → fibrin** threads trap blood cells → clot. **Vitamin K** is needed to make prothrombin in the liver. **Heparin** prevents clotting inside vessels. **Haemophilia** — X-linked disorder, blood does not clot.

### ABO blood groups

Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner (1900)**; group AB was found later (1902, von Decastello and Sturli).

| Blood group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Can donate to | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | A | anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| **B** | B | anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| **AB** | A and B | **None** | AB only | **All** — **universal recipient** |
| **O** | **None** | anti-A and anti-B | **All** — **universal donor** | O only |

- Genes: the I gene with three alleles **Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i**; Iᴬ and Iᴮ are **codominant**; i is recessive. Six genotypes give four phenotypes (see [Cell Division, Genetics and Molecular Biology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/cell-division-genetics-and-molecular-biology)).
- **Rh factor**: antigen first found in the **rhesus monkey** (Landsteiner and Wiener, 1940). About 80% or more of people are Rh⁺.
- **Erythroblastosis foetalis**: an **Rh⁻ mother** carrying an **Rh⁺ foetus** forms anti-Rh antibodies after the first delivery; in the **second** pregnancy these antibodies destroy the foetus's RBCs. Prevented by giving the mother **anti-D (anti-Rh) antibodies** soon after the first delivery.

### Lymph

Colourless fluid (tissue fluid) with lymphocytes, no RBCs; carries **fats absorbed in lacteals**; returns to blood through the thoracic duct. Lymph nodes filter germs.

## 5. Heart and circulation

### Structure of the human heart

- Made of **cardiac muscle**; size of a fist; between the lungs, slightly tilted to the left; covered by a double membrane, the **pericardium**.
- **Four chambers**: two upper thin-walled **atria (auricles)** and two lower thick-walled **ventricles**. The **left ventricle has the thickest wall** — it pumps blood to the whole body.
- Valves: **tricuspid** (right atrium → right ventricle), **bicuspid / mitral** (left atrium → left ventricle), **semilunar valves** at the openings of the pulmonary artery and aorta. Valves keep blood flowing **one way**.

### Double circulation

- **Pulmonary circulation**: right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** (carries **deoxygenated** blood) → lungs → **pulmonary veins** (carry **oxygenated** blood) → left atrium.
- **Systemic circulation**: left ventricle → **aorta** → body → **vena cavae** → right atrium.
- Blood passes through the heart **twice** per complete round — **double circulation** (birds and mammals). Discovered by **William Harvey (1628)**.
- **Hepatic portal system**: blood from the intestine goes to the **liver** first (via the hepatic portal vein) before entering general circulation. **Coronary circulation** supplies the heart muscle itself.

> **Common mistake:** "Arteries carry oxygenated blood" is **not always true**. Arteries carry blood **away from** the heart; veins carry blood **to** the heart. The **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood and the **pulmonary vein** oxygenated blood.

### Heartbeat and cardiac cycle

- The heart is **myogenic** — the beat starts in the heart muscle itself, at the **SA node** (sino-atrial node, the **pacemaker**) in the right atrium wall. Impulse → **AV node** → **bundle of His** → **Purkinje fibres** → ventricles contract.
- Heart rate **~72 beats per minute**; one cardiac cycle = 60/72 ≈ **0.8 s**.
- **Stroke volume** (blood per beat per ventricle) ≈ **70 mL**. **Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate.
- **Heart sounds**: "**lub**" — closure of the **AV valves** (tricuspid and bicuspid); "**dub**" — closure of the **semilunar valves**. Heard with a stethoscope.
- **ECG** (electrocardiogram): **P wave** = atrial depolarisation (atria contract); **QRS complex** = ventricular depolarisation (ventricles contract); **T wave** = ventricular repolarisation.
- Normal blood pressure **120/80 mm Hg** (systolic/diastolic), measured with a **sphygmomanometer**. **Hypertension** = 140/90 or above.
- Nerves only **change** the rate: sympathetic nerves and adrenaline increase it; the parasympathetic **vagus nerve** decreases it.

**Worked example:** Heart rate 72 per min, stroke volume 70 mL. Cardiac output = 72 × 70 = 5040 mL ≈ **5 L per minute**.

**Worked example:** A person's pulse is 75 per minute. Duration of one cardiac cycle = 60 ÷ 75 = **0.8 s**.

### Heart chambers across vertebrates

| Animal | Chambers |
|---|---|
| Fish | **2** (1 atrium, 1 ventricle), single circulation |
| Amphibians, most reptiles | **3** (2 atria, 1 ventricle), incomplete double circulation |
| Crocodiles, birds, mammals | **4**, complete double circulation |

### Circulatory disorders

**Hypertension**; **coronary artery disease (atherosclerosis)** — deposits of calcium, fat and cholesterol narrow the arteries; **angina pectoris** — chest pain when heart muscle gets too little O₂; **heart failure** — heart cannot pump enough blood (not the same as cardiac arrest or heart attack, which is damage due to sudden poor blood supply).

## 6. Excretion

### Nitrogenous wastes

| Type | Main waste | Toxicity / water needed | Animals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonotelic** | Ammonia | Most toxic; needs most water | Bony fishes, aquatic amphibians (tadpoles), aquatic insects |
| **Ureotelic** | **Urea** | Less toxic | **Mammals**, many terrestrial amphibians, marine fishes |
| **Uricotelic** | **Uric acid** (paste/pellet) | Least toxic; least water loss | **Birds**, reptiles, land snails, insects |

Excretory structures in animals: **protonephridia / flame cells** (flatworms, rotifers, *Amphioxus*), **nephridia** (annelids), **Malpighian tubules** (insects), **antennal / green glands** (crustaceans), **kidneys** (vertebrates).

### Human urinary system

Two **kidneys** (bean-shaped, ~120–170 g each; left slightly higher) → **ureters** → **urinary bladder** → **urethra**. Each kidney has an outer **cortex** and inner **medulla** (with medullary pyramids). Each kidney has about **one million nephrons**.

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Glomerulus** (tuft of capillaries) + **Bowman's capsule** = **Malpighian (renal) corpuscle** | **Ultrafiltration** — plasma minus proteins is filtered under pressure |
| **PCT** (proximal convoluted tubule) | **Reabsorbs most**: ~70–80% of water and electrolytes, **all glucose and amino acids**; secretes H⁺, NH₃; keeps pH |
| **Descending limb of Henle's loop** | **Permeable to water**, almost impermeable to electrolytes — filtrate becomes concentrated |
| **Ascending limb** | **Impermeable to water**; Na⁺, Cl⁻ pumped out — filtrate becomes dilute |
| **DCT** | Conditional reabsorption of Na⁺ and water (aldosterone); secretes H⁺, K⁺ |
| **Collecting duct** | Water reabsorbed under **ADH**; makes concentrated urine |

- **GFR** (glomerular filtration rate) ≈ **125 mL per minute** ≈ **180 litres per day**. Only **1–1.5 L** of urine is passed, so about **99%** of filtrate is reabsorbed.
- **Counter-current mechanism**: the loop of Henle and the **vasa recta** (blood vessels running beside it in opposite directions) build up a high osmotic gradient in the medulla (from ~300 to ~1200 mOsm/L), so humans can make urine about **four times** more concentrated than plasma.
- Urine: pale yellow (colour from **urochrome**), slightly acidic (pH ~6); about **25–30 g urea per day**. Glucose in urine = **glycosuria** (diabetes mellitus); ketone bodies = **ketonuria**.

**Worked example:** GFR = 125 mL/min. Filtrate per day = 125 × 60 × 24 = 180,000 mL = **180 L/day**. If urine is 1.5 L/day, fraction reabsorbed = (180 − 1.5)/180 ≈ **99.2%**.

### Regulation of kidney function

- **ADH (antidiuretic hormone, vasopressin)** — made in the **hypothalamus**, released from the **posterior pituitary**; increases water reabsorption. Lack of ADH → **diabetes insipidus** (large volumes of dilute urine).
- **Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)**: low blood pressure or GFR → **juxtaglomerular (JG) cells** release **renin** → angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → (ACE in lungs) → **angiotensin II** → constricts vessels and stimulates **aldosterone** (adrenal cortex) → Na⁺ and water reabsorbed → BP rises.
- **ANF (atrial natriuretic factor)** from the heart atria — opposes RAAS; lowers BP.

### Other excretory organs

**Lungs** remove CO₂ (~200 mL/min) and water; **liver** excretes bile pigments, cholesterol, steroid hormones, vitamins and drugs through bile; **skin** — sweat (NaCl, small urea, lactic acid) and sebum.

### Disorders

**Uraemia** (urea builds up in blood; treated by **haemodialysis** — artificial kidney with a cellophane tube and **heparin** as anticoagulant); **kidney transplant** (from a close relative to reduce rejection); **renal calculi** (kidney stones, mostly **calcium oxalate**); **glomerulonephritis** (inflammation of glomeruli); **gout** (uric acid crystals in joints).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Renin ≠ rennin.** Renin — kidney (blood pressure); rennin — infant stomach (milk curdling). TRE 2.0 official answer was "None of the above" for "renin".
- **Bile has no enzyme**; it emulsifies fats. It is made by the liver, only stored in the gall bladder.
- **Enterokinase** is not a digestive enzyme for food; it activates trypsinogen.
- **Pepsin** works only in acid; **trypsin** works in alkaline medium.
- **Largest gland** — liver; **largest salivary gland** — parotid (mumps virus affects it).
- **Pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood; **pulmonary vein** oxygenated.
- **Left ventricle** has the thickest wall, not the right.
- **"Lub"** is closure of AV valves, **"dub"** semilunar valves — not opening.
- Fish heart has **only deoxygenated blood**.
- **Neutrophils** are the most abundant WBCs; **basophils** the fewest.
- **O** is universal donor, **AB** universal recipient — not the other way round.
- Erythroblastosis needs **Rh⁻ mother + Rh⁺ baby**, and usually affects the **second** child.
- Main breathing stimulus is **CO₂/H⁺**, not low O₂; but low O₂ still **increases** breathing via carotid/aortic bodies.
- **Inspiration is active, normal expiration passive.**
- **PCT** (not the loop of Henle) reabsorbs most of the filtrate; ascending limb is impermeable to **water**.
- Birds are **uricotelic**, mammals **ureotelic**, bony fishes **ammonotelic**.
- **Haemoglobin** is not the most abundant animal protein — **collagen** is.

## Practice with answers

1. Which enzyme starts protein digestion in the stomach? — **Pepsin** (activated from pepsinogen by HCl).
2. Where is the enzyme "renin" produced? — **Juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney** (not in any digestive juice).
3. Which juice contains no digestive enzyme? — **Bile**.
4. Which enzyme converts lactose to glucose and galactose? — **Lactase** (intestinal juice).
5. Absorption of fat takes place into which vessel of the villus? — **Lacteal** (lymph vessel).
6. Which cells of the gastric gland secrete HCl? — **Parietal (oxyntic) cells**.
7. Vitamin made by gut bacteria and needed for clotting? — **Vitamin K**.
8. Which muscles raise the ribs during inspiration? — **External intercostal muscles**.
9. Name the peripheral chemoreceptors. — **Carotid bodies and aortic bodies** (sense low O₂).
10. Which enzyme in RBCs speeds up CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃? — **Carbonic anhydrase**.
11. A person breathes 15 times per minute with tidal volume 500 mL. Pulmonary ventilation? — **7.5 L/min** (15 × 500 mL).
12. Which blood group has no antibodies in plasma? — **AB** (universal recipient).
13. Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood from lungs to heart? — **Pulmonary vein**.
14. Which wave of ECG shows ventricular depolarisation? — **QRS complex**.
15. What does the "dub" sound mean? — **Closure of the semilunar valves**.
16. Heart rate 80/min and stroke volume 70 mL. Cardiac output? — **5.6 L/min** (80 × 70 = 5600 mL).
17. Which part of the nephron is impermeable to water? — **Ascending limb of loop of Henle**.
18. Birds excrete nitrogen mainly as? — **Uric acid** (uricotelic).
19. Which hormone from the JG apparatus pathway raises Na⁺ reabsorption? — **Aldosterone** (through renin–angiotensin).
20. Most abundant protein in the animal body? — **Collagen**.
