This chapter covers human health and disease from NCERT class 9 (Why Do We Fall Ill) and class 12 (Human Health and Disease) to B.Sc. points such as antibody chemistry, autoimmunity, food microbiology and plant pathology. It is a high-yield chapter: TRE 2.0 alone asked dengue's mosquito, a hereditary disease, botulism, antibodies and autoimmune diseases.

> **In simple words:** A disease is any condition that stops the body from working normally. **Infectious** diseases are caused by germs (bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi, worms) and spread from person to person, often through a carrier (vector) like a mosquito. **Non-infectious** diseases come from genes, lifestyle or environment (diabetes, cancer, genetic disorders). The body fights germs with its **immune system** — skin and white blood cells at first, then specific antibodies and T-cells. Vaccines train this system in advance.

## 1. Basic terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pathogen** | Organism that causes disease |
| **Vector** | Living carrier that transmits a pathogen (mosquito, sandfly, flea) |
| **Incubation period** | Time between infection and appearance of symptoms |
| **Acute / chronic** | Short-lasting / long-lasting (TB, diabetes) |
| **Endemic / epidemic / pandemic** | Always present locally / sudden rise in a region / spread across continents (COVID-19, declared by WHO on 11 March 2020) |
| **Infection vs intoxication** | Germs grow inside the body vs illness from a **toxin already formed** in food |
| **Zoonosis** | Disease passed from animals to humans (rabies, plague, bird flu, JE) |

## 2. Diseases by pathogen

### Bacterial diseases

| Disease | Pathogen | Spread / key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Typhoid** | ***Salmonella typhi*** | Contaminated food and water; intestinal perforation possible; **Widal test**; "Typhoid Mary" — a carrier cook |
| **Cholera** | ***Vibrio cholerae*** | Contaminated water; rice-water stools; treat with ORS |
| **Tuberculosis** | ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** (Robert Koch, 1882) | Droplets; lungs mainly; **BCG** vaccine; DOTS treatment |
| **Leprosy (Hansen's disease)** | ***Mycobacterium leprae*** | Long contact; skin and nerves; multi-drug therapy |
| **Pneumonia** | ***Streptococcus pneumoniae***, ***Haemophilus influenzae*** | Droplets; alveoli fill with fluid; lips/nails may turn grey-bluish |
| **Diphtheria** | ***Corynebacterium diphtheriae*** | Throat membrane; DPT vaccine |
| **Whooping cough (pertussis)** | ***Bordetella pertussis*** | Children; DPT vaccine |
| **Tetanus (lockjaw)** | ***Clostridium tetani*** | Spores enter wounds; toxin; TT vaccine |
| **Plague** | ***Yersinia pestis*** | **Rat flea** (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) |
| **Anthrax** | ***Bacillus anthracis*** | Cattle; spores |
| **Botulism** | ***Clostridium botulinum*** | **Toxin in food** (intoxication) |
| **Syphilis / gonorrhoea** | ***Treponema pallidum*** / ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae*** | Sexually transmitted |

### Viral diseases

| Disease | Virus | Spread / key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Common cold** | **Rhinovirus** | Droplets, contaminated objects; nose and airways, not lungs |
| Influenza | Influenza virus (orthomyxovirus) | Droplets; bird flu H5N1, swine flu H1N1 |
| **Measles, mumps, rubella** | Paramyxo-/togaviruses | Droplets; **MMR** vaccine; mumps affects **parotid** glands |
| **Polio** | **Poliovirus** (enterovirus) | Faecal–oral; paralysis; **Salk** (injectable, killed, 1955) and **Sabin** (oral, live) vaccines; India declared polio-free by WHO on **27 March 2014** |
| **Rabies (hydrophobia)** | **Rabies virus** (Lyssavirus) | Bite of dogs, cats, bats, monkeys; nearly 100% fatal once symptoms start; **Pasteur's** vaccine (1885) |
| **Hepatitis** | A and E (faecal–oral, water); **B, C, D** (blood, sex, mother to child) | **Hepatitis B** vaccine is recombinant (made in yeast) |
| **Dengue ("break-bone fever")** | **Dengue virus** (flavivirus) | **Aedes aegypti** (day-biter, breeds in clean stored water — coolers, tyres); falling **platelet count**; dengue haemorrhagic fever |
| **Chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever** | Togavirus / flaviviruses | Also by ***Aedes*** |
| **Japanese encephalitis** | JE virus (flavivirus) | ***Culex***; pigs and water birds as reservoirs; north Bihar and eastern UP |
| **AIDS** | **HIV** (retrovirus) | Sex, blood, shared needles, mother to child |
| **COVID-19** | **SARS-CoV-2** (coronavirus) | Droplets; RT-PCR test |
| Smallpox | Variola virus | **Eradicated** (WHO 1980); **Jenner's** vaccine (1796, from cowpox) |
| Chickenpox | Varicella-zoster virus | Droplets; one attack usually gives lifelong immunity |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The dengue fever is caused by the bite of which of the following mosquitoes? — (A) Culex (B) Aedes (C) Anopheles." **Official answer: (B) Aedes.**

### Protozoan diseases

| Disease | Pathogen | Vector / spread |
|---|---|---|
| **Malaria** | ***Plasmodium*** — *P. vivax*, *P. falciparum* (most dangerous; cerebral malaria), *P. malariae*, *P. ovale* | Female ***Anopheles*** |
| **Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery)** | ***Entamoeba histolytica*** | Faecal–oral; **houseflies** as mechanical carriers; mucus and blood in stool |
| **Kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis)** | ***Leishmania donovani*** | **Sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*); endemic in **Bihar**; enlarged spleen and liver |
| Sleeping sickness | ***Trypanosoma*** (*T. gambiense*) | **Tsetse fly** (*Glossina*) |
| Giardiasis | ***Giardia*** | Water |

**Malaria life cycle (NCERT):**
1. Infected female *Anopheles* bites and injects **sporozoites** (the infective stage).
2. Sporozoites multiply in **liver cells**, then attack **RBCs**.
3. RBCs burst and release a toxin, **haemozoin**, causing **chill and high fever recurring every 3–4 days** (48 h cycle for *vivax*, 72 h for *malariae*).
4. Some parasites become **gametocytes** in blood; a mosquito sucks them up.
5. **Fertilisation and development** happen in the **mosquito's gut** → sporozoites move to its **salivary glands**.
- So the **mosquito is the definitive (primary) host** (sexual stage) and **man the intermediate (secondary) host** (asexual stage).
- Parasite seen in blood by **Laveran (1880)**; mosquito transmission proved by **Ronald Ross (1897, Secunderabad)**, Nobel Prize 1902. World Mosquito Day — **20 August**.
- Drugs: quinine (cinchona bark), chloroquine, artemisinin (from *Artemisia annua*; Tu Youyou, Nobel 2015).

### Helminth (worm) diseases

| Disease | Worm | Spread |
|---|---|---|
| **Ascariasis** | ***Ascaris lumbricoides*** (roundworm) | Eggs in soil, water, vegetables; internal bleeding, anaemia, blocked intestine |
| **Filariasis (elephantiasis)** | ***Wuchereria bancrofti***, *W. (Brugia) malayi* | Female ***Culex***; swelling of legs and genitals (lymph vessels blocked) |
| Taeniasis, cysticercosis | ***Taenia solium*** | Undercooked pork; contaminated food (eggs) |
| Hookworm | ***Ancylostoma duodenale*** | Larvae bore through skin of bare feet; anaemia |
| **Enterobiasis** | ***Enterobius vermicularis*** (pinworm) | Children; anal itching; eggs swallowed |
| **Schistosomiasis** | ***Schistosoma*** (blood fluke) | Cercaria larvae penetrate skin in water; snail host |

### Fungal diseases

**Ringworm** — *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton*; dry scaly patches on skin, nails, scalp; spreads in warm moist conditions and through towels and combs. Others: athlete's foot, candidiasis (*Candida*), aspergillosis.

| Vector | Diseases |
|---|---|
| **Female *Anopheles*** | Malaria |

| **Tsetse fly** | Sleeping sickness |

> **Memory trick:** "**A**edes **D**ances by **D**ay" — Aedes, Dengue, Day-biter. "**C**ulex **F**ilaria **J**E" — think "**C**ulex **F**rom **J**heel" (Culex breeds in dirty, stagnant water; Aedes in clean stored water; Anopheles in clean standing water).

## 3. Immunity

**Immunity** is the ability of the body to fight disease-causing organisms.

### Innate (non-specific, inborn) immunity — four barriers

| Barrier | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Skin; mucus lining of respiratory, gut and urogenital tracts |
| **Physiological** | Acid in stomach, saliva, tears (lysozyme) |
| **Cellular** | **Neutrophils (PMNL)**, **monocytes/macrophages**, **natural killer (NK) cells** — phagocytosis |
| **Cytokine** | **Interferons** released by virus-infected cells protect nearby cells |

### Acquired (specific) immunity

- Has **specificity** and **memory**. First exposure → weak **primary response**; second exposure → strong, fast **secondary (anamnestic) response**.
- **B-lymphocytes** → plasma cells → **antibodies** in blood = **humoral immunity**.
- **T-lymphocytes** → **cell-mediated immunity (CMI)**; responsible for **graft (organ) rejection**. Helper T-cells (CD4) activate B-cells and other T-cells; cytotoxic T-cells kill infected cells.
- Both are made in **bone marrow**; B-cells mature there, T-cells mature in the **thymus**.

- Each antibody has **four polypeptide chains** — **two heavy (H) and two light (L)** — so it is written **H₂L₂**, joined by disulphide bonds in a **Y shape**.
- Antibodies are **immunoglobulins**, chemically **glycoproteins** (protein + carbohydrate) — the γ-globulin fraction of plasma.

| Class | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **IgG** | **Most abundant** in serum; **only one that crosses the placenta** |
| **IgA** | In secretions — tears, saliva, **colostrum** (mother's first milk) |
| **IgM** | **Largest** (pentamer); **first** to be made in a primary response |
| **IgE** | **Allergy**; binds mast cells |
| IgD | On B-cell surface |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Antibodies in our body are complex — (A) steroids (B) prostaglandins (C) glycoproteins." **Official answer: (C) glycoproteins.**

### Types of acquired immunity

| | Natural | Artificial |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** (body makes antibodies; slow, long-lasting) | Recovery from an infection | **Vaccination** |
| **Passive** (ready-made antibodies; quick, short) | Antibodies from mother via **placenta (IgG)** and **colostrum (IgA)** | **Antiserum** / antitoxin — anti-tetanus serum (ATS), **anti-venom** for snakebite |

### Vaccines

A vaccine contains an inactivated/weakened pathogen or its parts (antigens) and produces **memory B and T cells**.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Live attenuated** | **BCG**, **OPV**, **MMR**, measles, yellow fever, rotavirus, chickenpox |
| **Killed / inactivated** | **IPV** (Salk), rabies (cell-culture), hepatitis A, **Covaxin** |
| **Toxoid** (inactivated toxin) | **Tetanus**, **diphtheria** (the D and T of DPT) |
| **Subunit / recombinant** | **Hepatitis B** (surface antigen made in **yeast**), HPV |
| Viral vector | Covishield (ChAdOx1) |
| **mRNA** | Pfizer–BioNTech, Moderna (COVID-19) |

- **Edward Jenner** — smallpox vaccine (1796), father of immunology. **Louis Pasteur** — rabies (1885), anthrax and chicken cholera vaccines; coined "vaccine" in Jenner's honour.
- India: **Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP) 1985**; **Mission Indradhanush** (2014).

### Lymphoid organs

- **Primary**: **bone marrow** (all blood cells made; B-cells mature) and **thymus** (T-cells mature; large at birth, shrinks with age).
- **Secondary**: **spleen** (filters blood; RBC graveyard), **lymph nodes**, tonsils, **Peyer's patches** of small intestine, appendix.
- **MALT** (mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue) is about **50%** of the lymphoid tissue in the body.

### Allergy and autoimmunity

- **Allergy**: exaggerated response to harmless substances (**allergens**: pollen, dust mites, animal dander). **IgE** antibodies cause **mast cells** to release **histamine and serotonin** → sneezing, watery eyes, wheezing. Drugs: **antihistamines**, adrenaline, steroids.
- **Autoimmune disease**: the immune system attacks the body's **own cells** ("self" not recognised).

| Autoimmune (yes) | Not autoimmune (common distractors) |
|---|---|
| **Rheumatoid arthritis** | **Alzheimer's disease** (neurodegenerative) |
| **Type 1 diabetes** | Osteoarthritis (wear and tear) |
| **Multiple sclerosis** | Type 2 diabetes (insulin resistance, lifestyle) |
| **Myasthenia gravis** | Parkinson's disease |
| **Psoriasis** | AIDS (immunodeficiency, caused by a virus) |
| **Vitiligo** (white patches, melanocytes destroyed) | Leprosy |
| **SLE (lupus)**, Hashimoto's thyroiditis, Graves' disease, coeliac disease | Haemophilia (genetic) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is not an autoimmune disease? — (A) Psoriasis (B) Alzheimer's disease (C) Vitiligo." **Official answer: (B) Alzheimer's disease.**

### AIDS

- **AIDS** = Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome; first reported in **1981**. Caused by **HIV**, a **retrovirus** (RNA genome, enzyme **reverse transcriptase** makes DNA from RNA).
- Path: HIV enters **macrophages** (which act as an **HIV factory**) → enters **helper T-lymphocytes (TH, CD4)** and destroys them → immunity falls → opportunistic infections (TB, *Pneumocystis* pneumonia, fungal infections).
- Diagnosis: **ELISA** (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay); confirmed by Western blot/PCR. Treatment: **anti-retroviral therapy** — not a cure.
- **Not spread** by touch, hugging, mosquitoes or sharing food. **NACO** (1992); World AIDS Day — **1 December**.

## 4. Cancer

- **Cancer** = uncontrolled cell division; cancer cells lose **contact inhibition**.
- **Benign tumour** — stays in place, little damage. **Malignant tumour** — invades tissues and spreads through blood/lymph to new sites (**metastasis**).
- **Carcinogens**: physical — **UV rays**, X-rays, gamma rays (ionising radiation); chemical — **tobacco smoke**, benzene, asbestos, aflatoxin; biological — **oncogenic viruses** (HPV → cervical cancer, hepatitis B → liver cancer, Epstein–Barr virus).
- **Genes**: **proto-oncogenes / cellular oncogenes (c-onc)** become cancer-causing when activated; **tumour suppressor genes** (e.g. **p53**) normally stop it.
- **Types**: **carcinoma** (epithelial — most common), **sarcoma** (connective tissue: bone, muscle), **leukaemia** (blood), **lymphoma** (lymph tissue).
- **Detection**: biopsy and histopathology, blood and bone-marrow tests (leukaemia), X-ray, **CT** (X-rays, 3-D), **MRI** (strong magnetic field and non-ionising radiation), PET; monoclonal antibodies against cancer antigens.
- **Treatment**: surgery, **radiotherapy**, **chemotherapy**, **immunotherapy** — **α-interferon** activates the immune system.

## 5. Genetic and hereditary disorders

A **hereditary disease** is passed from parents to children through genes. Infections (leprosy) and most cataracts (age-related) are not hereditary.

| Disorder | Type | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** | **Autosomal recessive**; inborn error of metabolism | Enzyme **phenylalanine hydroxylase** missing → phenylalanine → **phenylpyruvic acid** builds up, appears in urine; mental retardation; treated by low-phenylalanine diet |
| **Sickle-cell anaemia** | Autosomal recessive | **Glutamic acid → valine** at the **6th position of the β-globin chain** (GAG → GUG); sickle-shaped RBCs; carriers resist malaria |
| **Thalassaemia** | Autosomal recessive | Reduced synthesis of α or β globin chains (quantitative defect; sickle-cell is qualitative) |
| **Haemophilia** | **X-linked recessive** | Blood does not clot; "royal disease" (**Queen Victoria** was a carrier); mostly in males |
| **Colour blindness** (red–green) | X-linked recessive | ~8% of males, ~0.4% of females |
| Cystic fibrosis, albinism | Autosomal recessive | Thick mucus; no melanin |
| Huntington's disease, myotonic dystrophy | Autosomal **dominant** | — |
| **Down's syndrome** | **Trisomy 21** (47 chromosomes) | Langdon Down (1866); short, small round head, furrowed tongue, mental retardation |
| **Klinefelter's syndrome** | **47, XXY** (male) | Tall, sterile, gynaecomastia |
| **Turner's syndrome** | **45, XO** (female) | Short, sterile, rudimentary ovaries |
| Patau / Edwards | Trisomy 13 / 18 | — |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which one of the following is a hereditary disease? — (A) Cataract (B) Leprosy (C) Phenylketonuria." **Official answer: (C) Phenylketonuria.** Leprosy is bacterial; cataract is mostly age-related.

## 6. Food poisoning, toxins and adulteration

| Condition | Agent | Infection or intoxication? |
|---|---|---|
| **Botulism** | Neurotoxin of ***Clostridium botulinum*** (anaerobic; badly canned/preserved food) — the most potent natural toxin; blocks acetylcholine release → **flaccid paralysis** | **Food-borne intoxication** (toxin already formed in the food) |
| Staphylococcal food poisoning | Enterotoxin of ***Staphylococcus aureus*** (cream, meat) | Intoxication — symptoms in 1–6 h |
| *Bacillus cereus* vomiting | Toxin in reheated rice | Intoxication |
| **Salmonellosis** | ***Salmonella*** bacteria grow in the gut (eggs, poultry) | **Food-borne infection** |
| Cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A | Bacteria/virus in water | **Water-borne infection** |
| **Aflatoxin** | Fungus ***Aspergillus flavus*** on stored groundnut, maize | Mycotoxin; liver cancer |
| **Ergotism** | ***Claviceps purpurea*** on rye | Mycotoxin; LSD-like alkaloids |
| **Lathyrism** | **Khesari dal** (*Lathyrus sativus*) — neurotoxin **BOAA (ODAP)** | Spastic paralysis of legs; historically common in Bihar, MP, Chhattisgarh |
| **Epidemic dropsy** | **Argemone oil** mixed in **mustard oil** (alkaloid **sanguinarine**) | Swelling of legs, heart failure |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Botulism is — (A) food-borne infection (B) food-borne intoxication (C) water-borne infection." **Official answer: (B) food-borne intoxication.** The toxin, not the growing bacterium, causes the disease. (Infant and wound botulism are exceptions where bacteria grow in the body — B.Sc. detail.)

## 7. Drugs, alcohol and medicines

| Drug | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioids** — morphine, **heroin (smack, diacetylmorphine)** | Latex of poppy, ***Papaver somniferum*** | Depressant; slows body functions; morphine a pain-killer |
| **Cannabinoids** — marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja | ***Cannabis sativa*** | Affect cardiovascular system |
| **Cocaine (coke, crack)** | Coca plant, ***Erythroxylum coca*** | Stimulant; interferes with **dopamine**; hallucinations |
| **Hallucinogens** — LSD, atropine | LSD from ergot fungus; ***Atropa belladonna***, ***Datura*** | Hallucinations |
| Barbiturates, amphetamines, benzodiazepines | Synthetic | Sedatives/stimulants (medical use for depression, insomnia) |
| **Tobacco** | ***Nicotiana*** — alkaloid **nicotine** | Stimulates adrenaline; raises BP; smoke CO lowers O₂ carrying; cancers of lung, mouth |
| **Alcohol** | Fermentation | Liver **cirrhosis**, nervous damage |

**Antibiotics**: **penicillin** — **Alexander Fleming (1928)** from ***Penicillium notatum***; developed by Florey and Chain (Nobel 1945); **streptomycin** — Waksman, from ***Streptomyces griseus*** (first drug for TB). Antibiotics act on **bacteria, not viruses**. Over-use → antibiotic resistance ("superbugs").

## 8. Plant pathology — basics

| Pathogen group | Key facts | Examples (disease — pathogen) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bacteria** | **Most plant-pathogenic bacteria are rod-shaped and Gram-negative** (non-spore-forming). Gram-positive exceptions: *Clavibacter*, filamentous *Streptomyces* | **Bacterial blight of rice** — *Xanthomonas oryzae*; **citrus canker** — *Xanthomonas citri*; **crown gall** — *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*; soft rot — *Erwinia (Pectobacterium)*; wilt — *Ralstonia solanacearum*; **potato scab** — *Streptomyces scabies* |
| **Fungi and oomycetes** | Largest group of plant pathogens | **Late blight of potato** — *Phytophthora infestans* (Irish famine 1845–47); **white rust of crucifers** — ***Albugo candida*** (**obligate parasite**); **black stem rust of wheat** — *Puccinia graminis tritici* (alternate host barberry); **loose smut of wheat** — *Ustilago*; **red rot of sugarcane** — *Colletotrichum falcatum*; **tikka disease of groundnut** — *Cercospora*; **brown spot of rice** — *Helminthosporium oryzae* (Bengal famine 1943); **blast of rice** — *Magnaporthe (Pyricularia) oryzae*; wilt — *Fusarium* |
| **Nematodes** | **Most phytophagous nematodes are root parasites**; feed with a stylet | **Root-knot** — *Meloidogyne*; **cyst nematode** — *Heterodera*; **ear-cockle of wheat** — *Anguina tritici* (one of few above-ground ones) |
| **Viruses** | Spread by insects (aphids, whiteflies) | **Tobacco mosaic** — TMV (first virus discovered; **Ivanowsky 1892**, named *contagium vivum fluidum* by **Beijerinck**); **yellow vein mosaic of bhindi** — whitefly-borne begomovirus |
| **Viroids** | Naked RNA, no protein coat (**T.O. Diener, 1971**) | **Potato spindle tuber** |
| **Phytoplasma (MLOs)** | Wall-less bacteria | **Little leaf of brinjal**, sandal spike |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Dengue** — *Aedes*, not *Culex* or *Anopheles*. **Filaria and JE** — *Culex*. **Malaria** — female *Anopheles* only.
- In malaria, **mosquito = definitive host**, man = intermediate host; infective stage to man = **sporozoite**.
- **Kala-azar** is protozoan (*Leishmania*) spread by **sandfly**, not mosquito.
- **Common cold** — rhinovirus; **pneumonia** — mostly bacterial. Antibiotics do not kill viruses.
- **Hepatitis A and E** spread by water; **B and C** by blood.
- **Hepatitis B vaccine** is recombinant (yeast), not attenuated.
- **Tetanus and diphtheria vaccines are toxoids**; BCG and OPV are live.
- **IgG** crosses placenta; **IgA** in colostrum; **IgM** largest and first; **IgE** allergy.
- Antibodies are **glycoproteins**, not steroids or prostaglandins.
- **Alzheimer's** is not autoimmune; **psoriasis and vitiligo** are.
- AIDS is an **immunodeficiency**, not an autoimmune disease.
- **T-cells mature in the thymus**, but are made in bone marrow.
- **Graft rejection** is due to **cell-mediated** (T-cell) immunity.
- **Botulism** = intoxication; **salmonellosis** = infection.
- **PKU** — hereditary; **leprosy** — infectious; **cataract** — mostly acquired.
- **Haemophilia and colour blindness** are X-linked; **sickle-cell and PKU** autosomal recessive.
- **Turner** = XO female; **Klinefelter** = XXY male.
- Plant pathogenic bacteria: **Gram-negative rods**; most plant nematodes: **root parasites**; *Albugo*: **obligate parasite**.
