This chapter covers NCERT class 10 "Life Processes" and "Control and Coordination" (plant part), class 11 "Transport in Plants", "Mineral Nutrition", "Photosynthesis in Higher Plants", "Respiration in Plants" and "Plant Growth and Development", plus B.Sc. plant physiology points (water potential, Z-scheme, Kranz anatomy, RQ values, phytochrome).
TRE asks: "nitrogen is not a constituent of", "RQ is", "which is not a plant hormone", "active transport", "ATP is formed in", "C₄ first product", "hormone for fruit ripening", "short-day plant".

> **In simple words:** Plants pull water up from roots to leaves mainly by the "suction" of evaporation from leaves (transpiration). They take minerals from soil — each has a job, for example magnesium sits in the centre of chlorophyll. In light, leaves make sugar from CO₂ and water (photosynthesis); in all living cells, sugar is broken to release energy (respiration). Growth and flowering are controlled by hormones and by day length.

## 1. Transport — basic terms

| Process | Direction | Energy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | High → low concentration | No | Gas exchange through stomata |
| **Facilitated diffusion** | High → low, through **carrier/channel proteins** (e.g. aquaporins for water) | No | Glucose, ions |
| **Active transport** | **Low → high (against gradient)** | **Yes — ATP** | **Ion uptake by roots**, Na⁺/K⁺ pump |
| **Osmosis** | **Water (solvent)** through a **semi-permeable membrane** from high water potential (dilute) to low water potential (concentrated) | No | Water entry into root hairs |
| **Imbibition** | Water absorbed by solid **hydrophilic colloids** (cellulose, starch, proteins) — no membrane needed | No | **Swelling of dry seeds**, wooden doors swelling in rainy season; seedlings break through soil |

- **Symport** (two molecules same direction), **antiport** (opposite directions), **uniport** (one molecule).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Active transport takes place (A) against concentration gradient and requires ATP (B) against concentration gradient and does not require ATP (C) with concentration gradient and does not require ATP …". Answer: **(A)**.

### Water potential (Ψ, psi) — B.Sc. point

<div class="formula">

| Quantity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ψw = Ψs + Ψp | Water potential = solute potential + pressure potential |
| Ψw of pure water | **0** (maximum) at atmospheric pressure |
| Ψs (osmotic / solute potential) | Always **negative** (adding solute lowers Ψ) |
| Ψp (pressure / turgor potential) | Usually **positive** in turgid cells; negative in xylem under tension |
| Unit | **Pascal (Pa)**, usually megapascal (MPa); 1 MPa ≈ 10 bar |
| Water moves | From **higher (less negative) Ψ to lower (more negative) Ψ** |

</div>

**Worked example 1.** Cell A has Ψs = −0.8 MPa and Ψp = +0.3 MPa; cell B has Ψs = −0.6 MPa and Ψp = +0.3 MPa. Which way does water move?
ΨA = −0.8 + 0.3 = −0.5 MPa; ΨB = −0.6 + 0.3 = −0.3 MPa. Water goes from higher to lower Ψ → **from B to A**.

**Worked example 2.** A flaccid cell (Ψp = 0) with Ψs = −1.2 MPa is put in pure water. Initial Ψw of cell?
Ψw = −1.2 + 0 = **−1.2 MPa**; water enters until Ψp builds up to +1.2 MPa (full turgor, Ψw = 0).

### Solutions and plasmolysis

| External solution | Effect on plant cell |
|---|---|
| **Hypotonic** (more dilute than cell sap) | Water enters → cell **turgid** (wall prevents bursting) |
| **Isotonic** | No net movement → flaccid |
| **Hypertonic** (more concentrated) | Water leaves → protoplast shrinks away from wall = **plasmolysis**; the reverse in water = deplasmolysis |

- Salting of pickles, killing of weeds by salt and "wilting after too much fertiliser" are due to **exosmosis / plasmolysis**.
- **Turgor pressure** keeps herbaceous plants erect and opens stomata.

### Pathways of water in the root

- **Apoplast**: through cell walls and intercellular spaces (non-living). Fast. Blocked at the **endodermis** by the **Casparian strip** (suberin).
- **Symplast**: through cytoplasm connected by **plasmodesmata** (living).
- At the endodermis, water must enter the symplast — so the plant controls what enters the xylem.

## 2. Ascent of sap (upward movement of water in xylem)

| Theory | Who | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Root pressure | Stephen Hales (term), Priestley | Positive pressure from active ion uptake pushes water up; seen as **guttation** (water drops at leaf tips/margins through **hydathodes** at night/early morning — **grass blades, tomato, *Colocasia***) and as bleeding from cut stems; too weak (about 1–2 atm) for tall trees |
| Capillarity | — | Only a few cm in narrow tubes |
| Vital theories | J. C. Bose (pulsation theory) | Rejected |
| **Cohesion–tension (transpiration pull) theory** | **H. H. Dixon and J. Joly (1894)** | **Accepted theory**: water evaporates from leaf mesophyll → creates **tension (suction)** in xylem; a continuous water column is held by **cohesion** (water–water attraction), **adhesion** (water–wall attraction) and **surface tension** |

## 3. Transpiration

- Loss of water as **vapour** from aerial parts. About **90% through stomata** (stomatal), rest through cuticle and lenticels. Plants lose about **99% of the water they absorb**; only about 1% is used in photosynthesis and growth.
- Called a "**necessary evil**" (Curtis): it wastes water but helps **create transpiration pull, cools leaves, and carries minerals up**.
- Measured with **Ganong's potometer** (strictly it measures water uptake) or the **cobalt chloride paper** test (blue → pink).
- **Stomatal opening mechanism**: in light, **K⁺ ions enter guard cells** (with malate formation) → water enters by osmosis → guard cells swell; their **inner walls are thick and outer walls thin**, and radially arranged **cellulose microfibrils** make them bow outward → pore opens. Loss of K⁺ → closing. **ABA (abscisic acid) closes stomata** during water stress.
- Factors increasing transpiration: high temperature, light, wind, low humidity. Decreasing: high humidity, still air.
- Antitranspirants: **phenyl mercuric acetate, ABA**, CO₂, silicone emulsions.
- **C₄ plants lose half as much water** as C₃ plants for the same CO₂ fixed.

## 4. Transport of food (phloem translocation)

- Food (mainly **sucrose**) moves in **sieve tubes** from **source** (leaves, storage organs in spring) to **sink** (growing tips, roots, fruits, storage organs) — direction can reverse; **bidirectional**.
- **Pressure flow / mass flow hypothesis — Ernst Münch (1930)**: sugar is **actively loaded** into sieve tubes at the source → water enters by osmosis → high turgor pressure → sap flows to the sink where sugar is unloaded.
- **Girdling (ringing) experiment** — removing a ring of bark (phloem) makes the bark above the ring swell with food; roots below starve → proves phloem carries food.

## 5. Mineral nutrition

- **Hydroponics** (soil-less culture) — **Julius von Sachs (1860)** — used to find essential elements; now used to grow tomatoes, lettuce commercially.
- Criteria of essentiality (Arnon and Stout): needed for normal growth and reproduction, **specific** (cannot be replaced), and **directly involved** in metabolism.
- **17 essential elements** (NCERT): C, H, O (from air and water) + 14 from soil.

| Macronutrients (more than 10 mmol/kg dry matter) | Micronutrients (less than 10 mmol/kg) |
|---|---|
| **C, H, O, N, P, K, S, Ca, Mg** (9) | **Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo, Zn, B, Cl, Ni** (8) |

(Some books place Fe among macronutrients because it is needed in larger amount than other micronutrients — NCERT lists it as a micronutrient. Beneficial (not essential) elements: Na, Si, Co, Se.)

### Roles of important elements

| Element | Main role | Deficiency symptom |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | **Constituent of amino acids, proteins, nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), chlorophyll, ATP, vitamins, hormones (auxin, cytokinin)**; needed most | **Chlorosis of older leaves**, stunting |
| **Phosphorus (P)** | Membranes (phospholipids), nucleic acids, **ATP**, NADP | Poor root growth, purple leaves |
| **Potassium (K)** | **Opening/closing of stomata**, anion–cation balance, enzyme activation, turgidity; meristems | Chlorosis and necrosis of leaf margins (older leaves) |
| **Calcium (Ca)** | **Middle lamella (calcium pectate)**, spindle formation, membranes | Death of meristems; young leaves affected |
| **Magnesium (Mg)** | **Central atom of chlorophyll**; activates RuBisCO, PEPcase; holds ribosome subunits | Interveinal chlorosis (older leaves) |
| **Sulphur (S)** | Amino acids **cysteine, methionine**; coenzyme A, vitamins (thiamine, biotin), ferredoxin | Chlorosis of young leaves |
| **Iron (Fe)** | **Cytochromes, ferredoxin**; catalase; **needed for chlorophyll synthesis** (not part of chlorophyll) | Chlorosis of young leaves |
| **Manganese (Mn)** | **Splitting of water (photolysis)** in PS II | Mn **toxicity** causes Fe, Mg, Ca deficiency — brown spots with chlorotic veins |
| **Zinc (Zn)** | Carboxylases; **synthesis of auxin** | **Little leaf**; **khaira disease of rice** |
| **Copper (Cu)** | **Plastocyanin** (electron carrier) | — |
| **Boron (B)** | **Pollen germination**, Ca uptake, cell differentiation, carbohydrate translocation | Heart rot of beet, brown heart of turnip |
| **Molybdenum (Mo)** | **Nitrogenase and nitrate reductase** (N metabolism) | **Whiptail of cauliflower** |
| **Chlorine (Cl)** | With Na⁺/K⁺ — solute balance; **photolysis of water** | — |
| Nickel (Ni) | Urease | — |

- **Mobile elements** (N, P, K, Mg) move from old to young leaves → deficiency appears **first in older leaves**. **Immobile elements** (Ca, S, Fe) → first in **young leaves**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Nitrogen is not an essential constituent of (A) chlorophyll (B) RNA (C) carbohydrate …". Official answer: **(C) carbohydrate**. Carbohydrates contain only **C, H and O**; chlorophyll (4 N in the porphyrin ring) and RNA (nitrogen bases) both contain nitrogen.

### Nitrogen cycle and nitrogen fixation

- N₂ is about **78%** of air but plants cannot use it directly; the N≡N triple bond is very strong.
- **Biological nitrogen fixation** — enzyme **nitrogenase** (a **Mo–Fe protein**), works only in **anaerobic** conditions; needs **16 ATP per N₂**:

<div class="formula">

N₂ + 8e⁻ + 8H⁺ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi

</div>

| Fixer | Type | Where |
|---|---|---|
| ***Rhizobium*** | **Symbiotic** | **Root nodules of legumes** |
| ***Frankia*** | Symbiotic (actinomycete) | Root nodules of non-legumes, e.g. *Alnus* |
| ***Anabaena azollae*** | Symbiotic cyanobacterium | Leaves of water fern *Azolla* |
| *Anabaena*, *Nostoc* | Symbiotic | Coralloid roots of *Cycas*; lichens |
| ***Azotobacter*** | **Free-living aerobic** | Soil |
| *Beijerinckia* | Free-living aerobic | Soil |
| *Rhodospirillum* | Free-living anaerobic | Soil |
| ***Clostridium*** | Free-living anaerobic | Soil |
| *Nostoc*, *Anabaena* | Free-living cyanobacteria | Paddy fields |

- Nodules look **pink** because of **leghaemoglobin** — an oxygen scavenger that protects nitrogenase from O₂.
- **Nitrification**: NH₃ → NO₂⁻ by ***Nitrosomonas*/*Nitrococcus***; NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ by ***Nitrobacter*** (chemoautotrophs).
- **Denitrification**: NO₃⁻ → N₂ by ***Pseudomonas*, *Thiobacillus***.
- **Ammonification**: decomposition of proteins to NH₃.
- Lightning and industry (Haber process) also fix nitrogen.
- In plants: NH₃ → amino acids by **reductive amination** (glutamic acid) and **transamination** (transfer of amino group; transaminase). **Amides — asparagine and glutamine** — are the transport form of nitrogen in xylem.

## 6. Photosynthesis

<div class="formula">

6CO₂ + 12H₂O → (light, chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O

</div>

### History

| Scientist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Joseph Priestley (1770)** | Candle and mouse under bell jar with mint plant — plants "restore" air (O₂, later named by Lavoisier) |
| **Jan Ingenhousz (1779)** | Only **green parts in sunlight** do this |
| **Julius von Sachs (1854)** | Green parts make **glucose**, stored as **starch**; chlorophyll in chloroplasts |
| **T. W. Engelmann (1882)** | **Action spectrum** using the alga *Cladophora* and aerobic bacteria — bacteria gathered in **blue and red** light |
| **Cornelius van Niel (1930s)** | Studying purple and green sulphur bacteria, proposed that **O₂ comes from water** (H₂S in bacteria → S) |
| **Ruben and Kamen (1941)** | Proved with **¹⁸O** that O₂ released comes from **water**, not CO₂ |
| **Robert Hill (1937)** | Isolated chloroplasts release O₂ in light with an electron acceptor (Hill reaction) — light reaction |
| **Melvin Calvin** | Path of carbon using **¹⁴C** in *Chlorella* — Calvin cycle (Nobel 1961) |
| **Hatch and Slack (1966)** | C₄ pathway |
| **F. F. Blackman (1905)** | **Law of limiting factors** |

### Pigments

| Pigment | Colour | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlorophyll a** | **Bright / blue-green** | **Chief pigment**; **reaction centre** |
| Chlorophyll b | Yellow-green | Accessory |
| Xanthophylls | Yellow | Accessory, photoprotection |
| Carotenoids (carotene) | Yellow to orange | Accessory, protect chlorophyll from photo-oxidation |

- Chlorophyll = **porphyrin head with Mg²⁺ at the centre** + long **phytol tail**. (Haemoglobin has Fe in a similar porphyrin.)
- Absorption peaks of chlorophyll a: **blue (~430 nm) and red (~662 nm)**; green is reflected. **PAR (photosynthetically active radiation) = 400–700 nm**.
- Separated by **paper chromatography**.

### Light reaction (photochemical phase) — in thylakoid membranes (grana)

- Two photosystems, each with a light-harvesting complex (antenna) and a reaction centre:
  - **PS I** — reaction centre chlorophyll a **P700**.
  - **PS II** — reaction centre chlorophyll a **P680**.
- **Photolysis (splitting) of water** at PS II: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + O₂ + 4e⁻ (needs **Mn²⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻**). Source of the oxygen we breathe.
- **Non-cyclic photophosphorylation** (**Z-scheme**): electrons flow H₂O → PS II → plastoquinone → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PS I → ferredoxin → **NADP⁺ → NADPH**. Produces **ATP + NADPH + O₂**.
- **Cyclic photophosphorylation**: only **PS I**; electrons return to PS I; in **stroma lamellae**; produces **only ATP** (no NADPH, no O₂). Occurs when only light of wavelength beyond 680 nm is available.
- **Chemiosmosis** (Peter Mitchell): H⁺ gradient across the thylakoid membrane (high H⁺ inside lumen) drives **ATP synthase (CF₀–CF₁)** to make ATP.

*Three stages: carboxylation of RuBP by RuBisCO gives 3-PGA; 3-PGA is reduced to G3P using ATP and NADPH; most G3P regenerates RuBP using ATP.*

1. **Carboxylation**: CO₂ + **RuBP (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, 5C)** → 2 molecules of **3-phosphoglyceric acid (3-PGA, 3C)** — enzyme **RuBisCO** (ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase; **most abundant protein on earth**). **First stable product = 3-PGA (3-carbon)** → hence "C₃ pathway".
2. **Reduction**: 3-PGA → glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P / PGAL), using ATP and NADPH.
3. **Regeneration** of RuBP, using ATP.

<div class="formula">

| Count | ATP | NADPH | Turns of cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per CO₂ fixed | **3** | **2** | 1 |
| Per glucose (6 CO₂) | **18** | **12** | **6** |

</div>

**Worked example 3.** How many ATP and NADPH are needed to make 2 molecules of glucose by the Calvin cycle?
1 glucose = 18 ATP + 12 NADPH → 2 glucose = **36 ATP and 24 NADPH** (12 turns).

### C₄ pathway (Hatch–Slack pathway)

- Found in plants of **dry tropical** regions: **maize, sugarcane, sorghum, *Amaranthus***, many grasses (also *Euphorbia*, *Atriplex*).
- **Kranz anatomy** (Kranz = wreath): large **bundle sheath cells** around veins, with many chloroplasts, thick walls impervious to gases, no intercellular spaces.
- **Mesophyll cells**: CO₂ fixed by **PEP carboxylase (PEPcase)** onto **phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP, 3C)** → **oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C)** = **first stable product** (hence C₄) → malic acid / aspartic acid → moves to bundle sheath.
- **Bundle sheath cells**: C₄ acid releases CO₂ (decarboxylation) → **Calvin cycle with RuBisCO** here; the 3C acid (pyruvate) returns to mesophyll and is regenerated into PEP using ATP.
- Mesophyll cells **lack RuBisCO**; PEPcase has **high affinity for CO₂ and no oxygenase activity**.
- Advantages: **no photorespiration**, higher yield, higher optimum temperature (30–40 °C), tolerate high light, use water and nitrogen efficiently. Cost: **30 ATP per glucose** (vs 18 in C₃).
- Dimorphic chloroplasts: mesophyll chloroplasts granal; bundle-sheath chloroplasts often agranal (in maize).

### CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism)

- In **succulents of deserts**: ***Opuntia*, pineapple, *Bryophyllum* (*Kalanchoe*), *Agave*, *Aloe***, cacti, *Sedum*.
- **Stomata open at night** (save water): CO₂ fixed by PEPcase into OAA → **malic acid stored in vacuoles** (leaves become acidic at night).
- **Day**: stomata closed; malic acid releases CO₂ → Calvin cycle in the same cells.
- So C₄ separates the two fixations **in space** (two cell types); CAM separates them **in time** (night and day).

### Photorespiration (C₂ cycle)

- At high O₂, high temperature and low CO₂, **RuBisCO acts as an oxygenase**: RuBP + O₂ → one 3-PGA + one **phosphoglycolate (2C)**.
- Involves three organelles: **chloroplast → peroxisome → mitochondria** (glycine → serine; CO₂ released).
- **No ATP or NADPH is produced, no sugar is made — it is a wasteful process** (loses about 25% of fixed carbon in C₃ plants).
- **Absent in C₄ plants** (high CO₂ around RuBisCO in bundle sheath).

### C₃ vs C₄ vs CAM

| Feature | C₃ | C₄ | CAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| First stable product | **3-PGA (3C)** | **OAA (4C)** | OAA (at night) |
| Primary CO₂ acceptor | **RuBP** | **PEP** | PEP |
| Enzyme of first fixation | RuBisCO | **PEPcase** | PEPcase |
| Kranz anatomy | No | **Yes** | No |
| Photorespiration | **High** | **Negligible** | Low |
| Optimum temperature | 20–25 °C | 30–40 °C | Up to 35 °C |
| Stomata open | Day | Day | **Night** |
| Examples | **Rice, wheat, potato, most plants** | **Maize, sugarcane, sorghum, *Amaranthus*** | ***Opuntia*, pineapple, *Bryophyllum*** |

> **Common mistake:** **Rice and wheat are C₃**, even though they are grasses; maize, sugarcane and sorghum are C₄.

### Factors affecting photosynthesis

- **Blackman's law of limiting factors (1905)**: when a process depends on several factors, its rate is limited by the factor **nearest its minimum value**.
- **Light**: rate rises with intensity at low light; saturation at about **10% of full sunlight** (except shade plants). Very high light damages chlorophyll.
- **CO₂**: about 0.04% in air — **the major limiting factor**; C₃ plants respond to higher CO₂ up to about 450 µL/L — greenhouse crops (tomato, bell pepper) are grown in CO₂-enriched air.
- **Temperature** (dark reactions are enzymatic), **water** (stress closes stomata).

## 7. Respiration in plants

- Breaking of C–C bonds of food to release energy as **ATP** ("energy currency"). Occurs in all living cells, day and night.

<div class="formula">

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (about 686 kcal ≈ 2870 kJ per mole of glucose)

</div>

### Stages

| Stage | Site | Input → output | Net yield per glucose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Glycolysis** (EMP pathway — Embden, Meyerhof, Parnas) | **Cytoplasm** | Glucose → **2 pyruvate** | **2 ATP (net) + 2 NADH** (4 made, 2 used) |
| **Link reaction** (oxidative decarboxylation) | **Mitochondrial matrix** | 2 pyruvate → 2 **acetyl CoA** + 2 CO₂ (pyruvate dehydrogenase) | 2 NADH |
| **Krebs cycle** (TCA / citric acid cycle — Hans Krebs) | **Mitochondrial matrix** | Acetyl CoA + OAA → **citric acid (first product, 6C)** → … → OAA; 2 turns per glucose | 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 ATP (GTP) + 4 CO₂ |
| **ETS + oxidative phosphorylation** | **Inner mitochondrial membrane** | NADH and FADH₂ give electrons → **O₂ is the final electron acceptor → water** | 1 NADH ≈ 3 ATP, 1 FADH₂ ≈ 2 ATP (textbook) |

- **Total ATP per glucose (aerobic): 38 ATP** (classical NCERT figure; net 36 in eukaryotes where cytoplasmic NADH enters by shuttle; modern estimate **30–32**).
- **Anaerobic respiration / fermentation** (in cytoplasm; only 2 ATP net):
  - **Alcoholic fermentation** (yeast): pyruvate → **ethanol + CO₂** (enzymes pyruvate decarboxylase and alcohol dehydrogenase). Yeast poisons itself at about 13% alcohol.
  - **Lactic acid fermentation** (some bacteria, **human muscle** during heavy exercise → cramps): pyruvate → **lactic acid** (lactate dehydrogenase).
- **Respiration is amphibolic**: it both breaks down (catabolism) and provides intermediates for synthesis (anabolism) — e.g. acetyl CoA for fatty acids, α-ketoglutarate for amino acids.
- Fats enter as acetyl CoA (fatty acids) and as PGAL (glycerol); proteins enter as amino acids at pyruvate, acetyl CoA or Krebs cycle intermediates.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "ATP is formed in (A) mitochondria (B) ribosomes (C) Golgi bodies …". Official answer: **(A) mitochondria**.

### Respiratory quotient (RQ)

<div class="formula">

RQ = volume of CO₂ evolved ÷ volume of O₂ consumed (during respiration)

</div>

| Substrate | RQ | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** (glucose) | **1.0** | 6 CO₂ / 6 O₂ |
| **Fats** (tripalmitin) | **less than 1 (about 0.7)** | Fats are poor in oxygen → need more O₂ |
| **Proteins** | **about 0.9** (0.8–0.9) | — |
| **Organic acids** (malic, oxalic, tartaric) | **more than 1** | Already rich in oxygen |
| **Anaerobic respiration** | **Infinity (∞)** | CO₂ released but no O₂ used |
| CAM plants at night | **Zero** (or close to 0) | O₂ used partly, CO₂ fixed into malic acid, not released |
| Germinating fatty seeds (castor) | Less than 1 | Fats converted to sugars |

**Worked example 4 (glucose).** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. RQ = 6/6 = **1**.

**Worked example 5 (tripalmitin).** 2(C₅₁H₉₈O₆) + 145 O₂ → 102 CO₂ + 98 H₂O. RQ = 102/145 = **0.70**.

**Worked example 6 (malic acid).** C₄H₆O₅ + 3O₂ → 4CO₂ + 3H₂O. RQ = 4/3 = **1.33**.

**Worked example 7 (oxalic acid).** 2(COOH)₂ + O₂ → 4CO₂ + 2H₂O. RQ = 4/1 = **4**.

**Worked example 8 (alcoholic fermentation).** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂. O₂ used = 0 → RQ = 2/0 = **infinity**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "RQ is (A) C/N (B) N/C (C) CO₂/O₂ …". Official answer: **(C) CO₂/O₂** (volume of CO₂ released / volume of O₂ consumed). C/N ratio is a different idea (soil and compost quality, flowering physiology).

> **Memory trick:** RQ order — "**Organic acid > Carbohydrate > Protein > Fat**": >1, 1, 0.9, 0.7.

## 8. Plant growth and plant growth regulators (hormones)

- Growth is **indeterminate** in plants (meristems keep dividing). Phases: meristematic → elongation → maturation. Growth curve: **sigmoid (S-shaped)** — lag, log (exponential), stationary phase.
- Arithmetic growth (root elongating at constant rate): Lₜ = L₀ + rt. Geometric growth: W₁ = W₀ e^(rt).
- Growth measured by **auxanometer**; **J. C. Bose** invented the **crescograph** (magnifies growth about 10,000 times).
- **Plasticity / heterophylly**: different leaf shapes on the same plant — juvenile vs mature (cotton, coriander, larkspur) or air vs water (buttercup).

### The five classic plant hormones

| Hormone | Discovery | Key functions | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Auxin** (IAA — indole-3-acetic acid; also IBA) | **Charles and Francis Darwin** (1880, phototropism of canary grass coleoptile); **F. W. Went (1928)** isolated it from *Avena* (oat) coleoptile tips (agar block) | **Apical dominance**; **rooting of stem cuttings**; **parthenocarpy** (tomato); **prevents premature leaf and fruit drop**; promotes abscission of older parts; **xylem differentiation**; phototropism and geotropism; **2,4-D kills dicot weeds** (lawns, wheat fields) | Promoter |
| **Gibberellins** (GA; >100 kinds; **GA₃** most common) | **E. Kurosawa (1926)** — "**bakanae**" (foolish seedling) disease of rice caused by fungus ***Gibberella fujikuroi*** | **Stem elongation**; **bolting** (internode elongation before flowering in rosette plants — cabbage, beet); **increases length of grape stalks** and size of fruit (apples elongated); **sugarcane yield** (up to 20 tonnes/acre more); **malting in brewing** (speeds up); delays senescence; breaks seed dormancy; **early seed production in conifers**; parthenocarpy | Promoter |
| **Cytokinins** (kinetin, zeatin) | **Skoog and Miller** — **kinetin** from autoclaved **herring sperm DNA**; **zeatin** from **maize kernels and coconut milk** | **Cell division (cytokinesis)**; new leaves, chloroplasts, lateral shoots; **overcome apical dominance**; **delay leaf senescence (Richmond–Lang effect)**; nutrient mobilisation; with auxin controls root/shoot formation in tissue culture | Promoter |
| **Ethylene** (C₂H₄ — gas) | Cousins (1910) — ripening of bananas by gas from oranges | **Fruit ripening** (climacteric rise in respiration); **triple response** in seedlings; abscission and senescence of leaves and flowers; breaks bud/seed dormancy; **promotes flowering in pineapple (and mango)**; **promotes female flowers in cucumber**; internode elongation in deep-water rice; root hair growth. Source used in farms: **ethephon** | Mostly inhibitor (some promotion) |
| **Abscisic acid (ABA)** — "dormin", "abscisin II" | Addicott and Wareing (1960s) — three independent groups | **Stress hormone**: **closes stomata** in drought; **seed and bud dormancy**; inhibits growth and seed germination; promotes abscission; seed maturation and desiccation tolerance. **Antagonist of gibberellins** | **Inhibitor** |

## 9. Photoperiodism

- Response of plants (especially flowering) to the **relative length of day and night** — **W. W. Garner and H. A. Allard (1920)**, working with **Maryland Mammoth tobacco** (and Biloxi soybean).
- The **critical photoperiod** is the day length that decides flowering. The real controlling factor is the length of the **uninterrupted dark period** (night). A brief flash of light in the middle of the night prevents flowering of short-day plants.
- Day length is **perceived by the leaves**; the stimulus (florigen) moves to the shoot apex.

| Type | Flowers when | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-day plants (SDP) = long-night plants** | Day shorter than critical length (night longer than critical) | **Maryland Mammoth tobacco, soybean (Biloxi), *Xanthium* (cocklebur), *Chrysanthemum*, rice, sugarcane, *Dahlia*, *Cosmos*, poinsettia** |
| **Long-day plants (LDP) = short-night plants** | Day longer than critical length | **Wheat, barley, oats, radish, spinach, sugar beet, lettuce, *Hyoscyamus niger* (henbane)**, carrot, potato (tuberisation in SD) |
| **Day-neutral plants** | Not affected by day length | **Tomato, cotton, sunflower, maize, cucumber**, chilli, *Mirabilis* |

- **Phytochrome** — the pigment that senses light; a blue chromoprotein discovered by **Borthwick and Hendricks** (USDA, 1952). Two inter-convertible forms: **Pr (absorbs red, 660 nm)** and **Pfr (absorbs far-red, 730 nm)**. Pfr is the active form. Red light promotes and far-red reverses seed germination in lettuce.

### Vernalisation

- Promotion of flowering by a period of **low temperature (about 1–10 °C)**. Term by **T. D. Lysenko** (1928).
- Prevents premature flowering in the growing season; lets plants mature first.
- Examples: **winter varieties of wheat, barley, rye** (sown in autumn, flower in spring), **biennials** — cabbage, sugar beet, carrot.
- The cold stimulus is perceived by the **shoot apex (meristem)** — unlike photoperiod, which is perceived by leaves.

### Seed dormancy and germination

- Dormancy due to hard seed coat, chemical inhibitors (**ABA**, phenolic acids, para-ascorbic acid), immature embryo. Broken by scarification, **gibberellins**, cold treatment (stratification), red light.
- Germination needs **water, oxygen, suitable temperature** (light for some seeds — lettuce, tobacco). Water uptake begins with **imbibition**.

### Plant movements (class 10)

| Movement | Stimulus | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Phototropism** | Light (shoot +, root −) | Shoot bends towards light (auxin moves to shaded side) |
| **Geotropism / gravitropism** | Gravity (root +, shoot −) | Roots grow down |
| **Hydrotropism** | Water | Roots grow towards water |
| **Thigmotropism** | Touch (directional) | **Tendrils** coil around support |
| **Chemotropism** | Chemicals | **Pollen tube** grows towards ovule |
| **Seismonasty / thigmonasty** (non-directional) | Touch, shock | ***Mimosa pudica*** (touch-me-not) — leaflets fold due to loss of turgor in **pulvinus** |
| **Nyctinasty** | Day/night | Sleep movements of legume leaves |
| **Photonasty** | Light | Opening of flowers (dandelion in morning) |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Carbohydrates have no nitrogen** (only C, H, O). Chlorophyll has N (and Mg); RNA has N in bases.
- **Mg is the central atom of chlorophyll**; Fe is needed for its synthesis but is **not a part of chlorophyll** (Fe is in haemoglobin).
- **Mn, Ca and Cl** are needed for photolysis of water; **Mo** for nitrogenase; **Zn** for auxin synthesis; **B** for pollen germination.
- Mobile elements (N, P, K, Mg) → old leaves show deficiency first.
- **Leghaemoglobin is an O₂ scavenger**, not an O₂ supplier to the bacteria.
- **O₂ of photosynthesis comes from water**, not CO₂ (Ruben and Kamen, ¹⁸O).
- **First product: C₃ = 3-PGA; C₄ = OAA**; primary acceptor in C₄ = PEP, in C₃ = RuBP.
- **Rice and wheat are C₃**; maize and sugarcane are C₄; pineapple and *Opuntia* are CAM.
- **Cyclic photophosphorylation gives only ATP** — no NADPH, no O₂.
- **Photorespiration produces no ATP and no sugar.**
- **Glycolysis is in cytoplasm**, Krebs cycle in **matrix**, ETS on **inner mitochondrial membrane**.
- **RQ = CO₂/O₂**, not O₂/CO₂. Fat < 1; organic acid > 1; anaerobic = ∞.
- **Ethylene is a gas**; ABA is the "stress hormone"; cytokinin delays senescence; gibberellin causes bolting.
- **2,4-D kills broad-leaved (dicot) weeds**, not grasses.
- **Florigen**: TRE 2.0 official key treats it as a plant hormone (answer E — "None of the above" for "not a plant hormone").
- **Chrysanthemum, rice, soybean, tobacco (Maryland Mammoth) = short-day**; wheat, radish, spinach, sugar beet = long-day; tomato, cotton, sunflower = day-neutral.
- Photoperiod is sensed by **leaves**; vernalisation by the **shoot apex**.
- Phytochrome: **Pr absorbs red (660 nm)**, **Pfr absorbs far-red (730 nm)**.
- *Mimosa* leaf folding is a **nastic** movement (turgor), not a tropism.

## Quick revision

1. Active transport: against gradient, uses ATP; imbibition: dry seeds swell.
2. Ψw = Ψs + Ψp; pure water Ψ = 0; water moves to more negative Ψ.
3. Plasmolysis in hypertonic solution.
4. Casparian strip (suberin) in endodermis forces water into symplast.
5. Cohesion–tension theory — Dixon and Joly (1894).
6. Guttation through hydathodes due to root pressure.
7. Stomata open by K⁺ influx into guard cells; ABA closes them.
8. Phloem flow: Münch's pressure flow hypothesis; sucrose from source to sink.
9. Hydroponics — Julius von Sachs (1860); 17 essential elements.
10. N is in proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll — not in carbohydrates.
11. Mg in chlorophyll; Mo in nitrogenase; Zn in auxin synthesis; Mn in water splitting.
12. Nitrogenase needs anaerobic condition; leghaemoglobin pink.
13. *Nitrosomonas* NH₃ → NO₂⁻; *Nitrobacter* NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻; *Pseudomonas* denitrifies.
14. O₂ in photosynthesis from water (Ruben and Kamen).
15. PS I = P700; PS II = P680; photolysis at PS II.
16. Calvin cycle: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH per CO₂; 18 ATP + 12 NADPH per glucose.
17. RuBisCO: most abundant protein on earth.
18. C₄: PEPcase, OAA first product, Kranz anatomy; maize, sugarcane.
19. CAM: stomata open at night; malic acid; pineapple, *Opuntia*.
20. Glycolysis in cytoplasm: net 2 ATP, 2 pyruvate; Krebs cycle in matrix.
21. RQ = CO₂/O₂: carbohydrate 1, fat 0.7, protein 0.9, organic acids > 1, anaerobic ∞.
22. Auxin — Went (*Avena*); gibberellin — Kurosawa (bakanae); kinetin — herring sperm DNA.
23. Ethylene ripens fruits; ABA closes stomata; 2,4-D is a weedicide.
24. Florigen — Chailakhyan; now known as FT protein; official TRE key counts it as a hormone.
25. Photoperiodism — Garner and Allard (Maryland Mammoth tobacco); phytochrome Pr 660, Pfr 730 nm.
