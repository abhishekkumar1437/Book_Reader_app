This chapter covers NCERT class 9 "Tissues", class 11 "Morphology of Flowering Plants" and "Anatomy of Flowering Plants", and the B.Sc. taxonomy of common angiosperm families.
TRE asks: "edible part of", "potato is a modified", "which statement about Liliaceae / Malvaceae / Compositae is incorrect", "connective tissue", "annual rings", "monocot stem has", "placentation in pea".

> **In simple words:** Morphology is the outer form of a plant (root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed) — many parts are modified for storage, support or protection. Anatomy is the inner structure (tissues). Families group plants with similar flowers — pea family, potato family, sunflower family, grass family and so on.

## 1. The root

- Develops from the **radicle**. **Tap root** (dicots: main root + laterals) vs **fibrous root** (monocots: many equal roots from the stem base) vs **adventitious roots** (from any part other than the radicle — grass, banyan, *Monstera*).
- Regions from tip: **root cap** (protects tip) → **meristematic zone** → **zone of elongation** → **zone of maturation** (bears **root hairs**, which absorb water).

### Root modifications

| Function | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Food storage (tap root) | **Conical** | **Carrot** |
| | **Fusiform** (spindle) | **Radish** |
| | **Napiform** (top-like) | **Turnip, beetroot** |
| Food storage (adventitious) | **Tuberous** | **Sweet potato** (*Ipomoea batatas*), *Dahlia*, *Asparagus* |
| Support | **Prop roots** (hanging from branches) | **Banyan** (*Ficus benghalensis*) |
| | **Stilt roots** (from lower nodes) | **Maize, sugarcane**, screw pine (*Pandanus*) |
| Respiration | **Pneumatophores** (grow upward, vertically) | **Mangroves — *Rhizophora***, *Avicennia* (Sundarbans) |
| Absorb moisture from air | **Velamen** roots (epiphytic) | **Orchids** (*Vanda*) |
| Nitrogen fixation | **Root nodules** | **Legumes** — pea, gram (*Rhizobium*) |
| Parasitism | **Haustoria** | *Cuscuta* (dodder; total stem parasite), *Viscum* |
| Photosynthesis | Assimilatory roots | *Tinospora*, *Trapa* |

## 2. The stem

- Develops from the **plumule**. Has **nodes** and **internodes**, bears buds; positively phototropic.

| Modification | Type | Example (stem identity proof) |
|---|---|---|
| Underground storage | **Tuber** | **Potato** — "eyes" are nodes with buds |
| | **Rhizome** | **Ginger, turmeric**, banana, lotus |
| | **Corm** | ***Colocasia* (arbi)**, *Crocus* (saffron), *Amorphophallus* (zamikand) |
| | **Bulb** | **Onion, garlic** — reduced disc-shaped stem; food stored in fleshy **scale leaves** |
| Climbing | **Stem tendrils** (from axillary bud) | **Cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon (cucurbits), grapevine** |
| Protection | **Thorns** (from axillary bud) | ***Bougainvillea*, *Citrus*** |
| Photosynthesis in deserts | **Phylloclade** (flat, green, fleshy stem) | ***Opuntia***, *Euphorbia* |
| | **Cladode** (one-internode phylloclade) | *Asparagus*, *Ruscus* |
| Vegetative spread (sub-aerial) | **Runner** | **Grass (*Cynodon*)**, strawberry, *Oxalis* |
| | **Stolon** (arches then roots) | Mint, jasmine |
| | **Offset** (one internode, aquatic) | ***Pistia*, *Eichhornia* (water hyacinth)** |
| | **Sucker** (underground, grows obliquely up) | **Banana, pineapple, *Chrysanthemum***, mint |
| Food storage above ground | Fleshy stem | Sugarcane, kohlrabi (*knol-khol*) |

> **Common mistake:** **Potato is a stem (tuber)**; **sweet potato is a root**. **Onion's edible part is fleshy leaves**, not stem. **Ginger is a stem (rhizome)**, not a root.

## 3. The leaf

- Lateral outgrowth of the stem at a node, bearing a bud in its axil. Parts: **leaf base** (sheathing in monocots; swollen **pulvinus** in legumes), **petiole**, **lamina**. Stipules may be present.
- **Venation**: **reticulate** (dicots) vs **parallel** (monocots). (Exceptions: *Calophyllum* — dicot with parallel venation; *Smilax*, *Dioscorea* — monocots with reticulate.)
- **Simple** vs **compound**: **pinnately compound** (leaflets on a common axis, the **rachis** — **neem**, rose), **palmately compound** (leaflets at a common point — silk cotton, *Bombax*).
- **Phyllotaxy** (arrangement on stem):

| Type | Leaves per node | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Alternate** | One | **China rose, mustard, sunflower** |
| **Opposite** | Two, facing | ***Calotropis*, guava** |
| **Whorled** | More than two | ***Alstonia*** (devil tree), *Nerium* |

| Leaf modification | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf tendril** | Climbing | **Pea** (*Pisum*), *Lathyrus* |
| **Spines** | Protection, less water loss | **Cactus**, *Opuntia* (spines are leaves; the flat part is stem), *Aloe* margin |
| **Fleshy storage leaves** | Food | **Onion, garlic** |
| **Phyllode** (flattened petiole; lamina lost) | Photosynthesis | **Australian *Acacia*** |
| **Pitcher** | Catching insects | ***Nepenthes*** (pitcher plant) |
| **Bladder** | Catching insects | ***Utricularia*** (bladderwort, aquatic, rootless) |
| Sticky tentacles | Insects | *Drosera* (sundew) |
| Snap trap | Insects | *Dionaea* (Venus flytrap) |

Insectivorous plants grow in **nitrogen-poor** soil and get nitrogen from insects.

## 4. Inflorescence (arrangement of flowers)

| Type | Main axis | Flower order | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Racemose** | Keeps growing | **Acropetal** (oldest at base) | Mustard, radish (raceme) |
| **Cymose** | Ends in a flower | **Basipetal** (oldest at top) | Jasmine, *Calotropis* |

Special types: **capitulum (head)** — sunflower, marigold (Compositae); **spike / spikelet** — wheat, rice (Poaceae); **catkin** — mulberry; **spadix** — banana, palms, *Colocasia* (with a **spathe**); **umbel** — coriander, onion; **cyathium** — *Euphorbia* (poinsettia); **hypanthodium** — *Ficus* (fig, banyan, peepal); **verticillaster** — *Ocimum* (tulsi), *Salvia*.

## 5. The flower

- A **modified shoot** for sexual reproduction. Four whorls on the **thalamus (receptacle)**: **calyx** (sepals), **corolla** (petals), **androecium** (stamens — male), **gynoecium / pistil** (carpels — female).
- When calyx and corolla are not distinct → **perianth** (tepals), e.g. lily.
- **Symmetry**: **actinomorphic** (radial; can be cut into two equal halves along any radius — mustard, *Datura*, chilli), **zygomorphic** (bilateral; only one plane — pea, gulmohar, bean, *Cassia*), asymmetric (*Canna*).
- **Position of ovary**:

| Type | Ovary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypogynous** | **Superior** (others below) | **Mustard, china rose, brinjal** |
| **Perigynous** | **Half-inferior** (others at same level on a cup) | **Plum, rose, peach** |
| **Epigynous** | **Inferior** (thalamus fused around ovary; others above) | **Guava, cucumber, ray florets of sunflower** |

- **Aestivation** (arrangement of sepals/petals in bud):

| Type | Arrangement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Valvate** | Margins just touch | ***Calotropis*** |
| **Twisted** | Each overlaps the next in one direction | **China rose, lady's finger (bhindi), cotton** |
| **Imbricate** | Irregular overlapping | ***Cassia*, gulmohar** |
| **Vexillary (papilionaceous)** | Large **standard (vexillum)** covers two **wings**, which cover two fused **keel** petals | **Pea, bean** |

- **Androecium terms**: **epipetalous** (stamens fused to petals — brinjal), **epiphyllous** (fused to perianth — lily); **monoadelphous** (filaments in one bundle — **china rose**), **diadelphous** (two bundles, 9 + 1 — **pea**), **polyadelphous** (many bundles — **citrus**); **syngenesious** (anthers fused, filaments free — **sunflower**); **didynamous** (2 long + 2 short — *Ocimum*); **tetradynamous** (4 long + 2 short — **mustard**).
- **Gynoecium**: **apocarpous** (free carpels — lotus, rose), **syncarpous** (fused — mustard, tomato).
- **Placentation**:

| Type | Arrangement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Marginal** | Along one ridge of a one-chambered ovary | **Pea** (legumes) |
| **Axile** | On a central axis of a multi-chambered ovary | **China rose, tomato, lemon** |
| **Parietal** | On the inner wall of a one-chambered ovary (false septum may form) | **Mustard, *Argemone*** |
| **Free central** | On a central axis without septa | ***Dianthus*, *Primrose*** |
| **Basal** | At the base; single ovule | **Sunflower, marigold** |

## 6. Fruits and seeds

- **True fruit**: develops only from the ovary. **False fruit**: other floral parts also take part — **apple, pear** (fleshy **thalamus**), **strawberry**, **cashew** (fleshy peduncle/thalamus).
- **Parthenocarpic fruit**: formed without fertilisation; seedless — **banana**, seedless grapes (can be induced by auxins/gibberellins).
- **Pericarp** (fruit wall) = **epicarp + mesocarp + endocarp**.
- **Simple fruits** (one ovary): drupe — mango, coconut; berry — tomato, grape, banana; legume — pea; caryopsis — wheat, rice; cypsela — sunflower; capsule — cotton; siliqua — mustard; hesperidium — orange; pepo — cucumber; pome — apple.
- **Aggregate fruits** (many free carpels of one flower): custard apple (*Annona*), strawberry, lotus, raspberry.
- **Composite / multiple fruits** (whole inflorescence): **pineapple, jackfruit, mulberry** (**sorosis**), **fig** (**syconus**).

### Edible parts — the most-asked table

| Fruit / crop | Edible part |
|---|---|
| **Mango** | **Mesocarp** (fleshy; drupe — stony endocarp) |
| **Coconut** | **Endosperm** (white kernel and coconut water — liquid endosperm) |
| **Apple, pear** | **Fleshy thalamus** (false fruit) |
| **Banana** | Mesocarp and endocarp |
| **Litchi** | **Aril** (fleshy outgrowth of seed) |
| **Pomegranate** | **Juicy seed coat (testa)** |
| **Orange, lemon** | **Juicy hairs from endocarp** |
| **Wheat, rice, maize** | **Endosperm** (fruit = caryopsis) |
| **Tomato** | Pericarp and placenta |
| **Guava** | Whole fruit (pericarp + thalamus) |
| **Grapes** | Pericarp and placenta |
| **Groundnut, gram, pea** | **Cotyledons** (seed) |
| **Cashew nut** | Cotyledons; the "cashew apple" is the fleshy peduncle/thalamus |
| **Jackfruit** | Perianth (bracts), seeds |
| **Pineapple** | Fleshy axis, bracts and perianth |
| **Mulberry** | Fleshy perianth |
| **Papaya, watermelon** | Mesocarp (papaya); mesocarp + endocarp + placenta (watermelon) |
| **Cauliflower** | Inflorescence (pre-floral) |
| **Cabbage** | Leaves (terminal bud) |
| **Saffron** | Stigma |

### Seeds

- **Dicot seed** (gram, bean, pea): seed coat (**testa** + **tegmen**), **hilum** (scar of stalk), **micropyle**, embryo = **embryonal axis** (radicle + plumule) + **two cotyledons**. Usually **non-endospermic** (food in cotyledons). **Castor** is endospermic.
- **Monocot seed** (maize, wheat): seed coat fused with fruit wall (caryopsis); **endospermic**; **aleurone layer** (protein) separates endosperm from embryo; one shield-shaped cotyledon called **scutellum**; plumule sheathed by **coleoptile**, radicle by **coleorhiza**. Orchids are non-endospermic monocots.
- Germination: **epigeal** (cotyledons come above soil — bean, castor, cotton) vs **hypogeal** (cotyledons stay below — pea, gram, maize). **Vivipary** — germination while still on the parent — mangroves (*Rhizophora*).

## 7. Plant tissues

A **tissue** is a group of cells with a common origin and function. (In animals TRE also asks "connective tissue" — see the animal chapters.)

### Meristematic tissues (dividing cells)

| Type | Location | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Apical** | Tips of root and shoot | **Length (primary growth)** |
| **Intercalary** | **Base of internodes / leaves** in **grasses** | Regrowth of grass after grazing; elongation |
| **Lateral** | Sides — **vascular cambium**, **cork cambium** | **Girth (secondary growth)** |

### Simple permanent tissues

| Tissue | Cells | Wall | Living? | Function / example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Parenchyma** | Isodiametric, thin-walled, intercellular spaces | Cellulose | Living | Storage, photosynthesis (**chlorenchyma**), buoyancy (**aerenchyma** — aquatic plants like *Hydrilla*, lotus) |
| **Collenchyma** | Elongated, **thickened at corners** | **Cellulose, hemicellulose, pectin** | Living | **Flexibility and mechanical support** in young dicot stems and petioles (lets stems bend); usually **absent in monocots** and roots |
| **Sclerenchyma** | Long and narrow (**fibres**) or short irregular (**sclereids / stone cells**) | **Thick, lignified** | **Dead** | Strength — fibres in jute, flax, coconut husk; sclereids in **pear and guava pulp**, nut shells, tea leaves, legume seed coats |

### Complex permanent tissues

| Tissue | Elements | Living elements | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Xylem** | **Tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, xylem parenchyma** | Only **xylem parenchyma** | Water and mineral conduction (upward), support |
| **Phloem** | **Sieve tubes (sieve elements), companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres (bast)** | All except **phloem fibres** | Food (sucrose) conduction in both directions |

- **Vessels** are the main water-conducting elements in angiosperms; absent in gymnosperms (except Gnetales). **Tracheids** in all vascular plants.
- **Sieve tubes** have **sieve plates**; a mature sieve tube **lacks a nucleus**, and its functions are controlled by the **companion cell** (connected by pits). Gymnosperms have **albuminous cells** and sieve cells, no companion cells.
- **Phloem parenchyma is absent in most monocots.**
- **Protoxylem** (first-formed) and **metaxylem** (later):
  - **Exarch** — protoxylem towards periphery — **roots**.
  - **Endarch** — protoxylem towards centre (pith) — **stems**.

### Vascular bundle types

| Type | Arrangement | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | Xylem and phloem on **different radii**, alternate | **Roots** |
| **Conjoint, collateral, open** | Xylem and phloem on same radius, **cambium in between** | **Dicot stem** |
| **Conjoint, collateral, closed** | No cambium | **Monocot stem**, leaves |
| **Bicollateral** | Phloem on both sides of xylem | **Cucurbita** (pumpkin family) |
| Concentric — amphicribral | Phloem around xylem | Ferns |
| Concentric — amphivasal | Xylem around phloem | *Dracaena*, *Yucca* |

### Epidermal tissue system

- Epidermis covered with waxy **cuticle** (absent in roots). **Stomata** — two **guard cells** (bean/kidney-shaped in dicots; **dumbbell-shaped in grasses**) with chloroplasts; surrounded by **subsidiary cells**. Stomatal apparatus = stoma + guard cells + subsidiary cells.
- **Trichomes** (shoot hairs, multicellular) and **root hairs** (unicellular).

*Dotted inner line = hypodermis zone. In the dicot the ring of open bundles surrounds a clear pith; in the monocot the closed bundles are scattered — in real sections the peripheral bundles are smaller and more crowded, the central ones larger.*

| Feature | **Dicot stem** (sunflower) | **Monocot stem** (maize) |
|---|---|---|
| Hypodermis | **Collenchymatous** | **Sclerenchymatous** |
| Ground tissue | Differentiated into cortex, endodermis (**starch sheath**), pericycle, pith | **Undifferentiated** (no distinct cortex, pith) |
| Vascular bundles | **Ring**, conjoint collateral **open** | **Scattered**, conjoint collateral **closed**, each with a **sclerenchymatous bundle sheath** |
| Phloem parenchyma | Present | Absent |
| Water-containing cavities | Absent | **Present** in bundles (from protoxylem breakdown) |
| Medullary rays | Present | Absent |
| Secondary growth | Yes | No |

### Dicot root vs monocot root

| Feature | **Dicot root** (gram, bean) | **Monocot root** (maize) |
|---|---|---|
| Xylem bundles | **2–4 (diarch to tetrarch)** | **More than 6 (polyarch)** |
| Pith | Small or absent | **Large, well developed** |
| Cambium / secondary growth | Formed later; secondary growth occurs | Absent |

Common to both: outer **epiblema** (piliferous layer with root hairs), cortex (parenchyma), **endodermis** with **Casparian strips** (bands of **suberin** — waterproof; force water into the symplast), **pericycle** (gives rise to **lateral roots** and, in dicots, part of the cambium), radial vascular bundles, exarch xylem; passage cells opposite protoxylem.

*Dicot (dorsiventral) leaf: palisade on the upper side, loose spongy cells with air spaces below, more stomata on the lower surface.*

| Feature | **Dicot (dorsiventral) leaf** | **Monocot (isobilateral) leaf** |
|---|---|---|
| Mesophyll | Differentiated: **palisade** (upper, columnar, many chloroplasts) + **spongy** (lower, loose) | **Not differentiated** |

| Special cells | — | **Bulliform cells** (large, empty, colourless) on upper epidermis of grasses — lose water and **make leaves roll** to reduce water loss |
| Venation | Reticulate (bundle sizes vary) | Parallel (similar-sized bundles) |

- Vascular bundles in leaves are surrounded by **bundle sheath** cells. In C₄ plants (maize, sugarcane) these are large with many chloroplasts — **Kranz anatomy** (see Plant Physiology).

## 9. Secondary growth (in dicot stems and roots)

1. **Vascular cambium** forms a complete ring: **intrafascicular cambium** (inside bundles) + **interfascicular cambium** (from medullary ray cells).
2. Cambium cuts off **secondary xylem towards the inside** (more) and **secondary phloem towards the outside** (less).
3. **Spring wood (early wood)**: cambium very active in spring → many xylem elements with **wide vessels, light colour, low density**. **Autumn wood (late wood)**: less active → fewer, narrow vessels, dark, dense. One spring + one autumn wood = **one annual ring (growth ring)**. Counting rings gives the age of a tree → **dendrochronology**. Clear rings form in temperate regions; less distinct in tropics.
4. **Heartwood (duramen)**: central, old secondary xylem, **dark** (tannins, resins, oils, gums, aromatic substances), **dead**, hard, durable, resistant to microbes and insects; does **not conduct water** but gives mechanical support.
5. **Sapwood (alburnum)**: peripheral, light-coloured, **conducts water and minerals**.
6. **Cork cambium (phellogen)** forms in the cortex: cuts **cork (phellem)** outside and **secondary cortex (phelloderm)** inside. Phellem + phellogen + phelloderm = **periderm**. Cork cells are dead, with **suberin** — impervious to water.
7. **Lenticels**: lens-shaped openings in the bark for **gas exchange** in woody trees.
8. **Bark**: non-technical term for **all tissues outside the vascular cambium** (secondary phloem + periderm). Early (soft) bark vs late (hard) bark.
9. Commercial cork comes from ***Quercus suber*** (cork oak).

## 10. Important families

### Floral formula symbols

⊕ = actinomorphic; % = zygomorphic; ⚥ = bisexual; Br = bracteate; K = calyx; C = corolla; P = perianth; A = androecium; G = gynoecium; ( ) = fused; + = separate whorls; ∞ = many. "Superior / inferior" is written as a bar below / above G in books — here written in words.

### Malvaceae (cotton / mallow family) — dicot

- Mucilage present; stellate hairs; leaves simple, alternate, stipulate.
- **Epicalyx** (whorl of bracteoles below the calyx) common.
- Calyx 5, valvate; corolla 5, **twisted aestivation**; stamens many, **monoadelphous** (staminal tube around style), anthers **monothecous (one-celled), reniform**; ovary superior, syncarpous, **axile placentation**; fruit capsule or schizocarp.
- **Floral formula (china rose):** Br ⊕ ⚥ Epicalyx 5–8, K(5) C5 A(∞) G(5) superior.
- Examples: **cotton (*Gossypium*)** — fibre; **china rose (*Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*)**; **lady's finger / bhindi (*Abelmoschus esculentus*)**; **mesta / kenaf (*Hibiscus cannabinus*)** — fibre; hollyhock (*Althaea*); *Abutilon*; **jute (*Corchorus*)** and *Bombax*/silk cotton are now included in an expanded Malvaceae (APG).
- So "**fibre-yielding plants are found in Malvaceae**" is a **correct** statement.

### Fabaceae / Leguminosae — subfamily Papilionoideae (pea family) — dicot

- Trees, shrubs, herbs; **root nodules** with *Rhizobium*; leaves pinnately compound or simple, pulvinate, with stipules; **leaf tendrils** in pea.
- Flower zygomorphic, bisexual; calyx 5 gamosepalous; corolla 5 — **papilionaceous**: standard (1), wings (2), keel (2, fused); **vexillary aestivation**.
- Stamens 10, **diadelphous (9) + 1**; ovary superior, **monocarpellary**, unilocular, **marginal placentation**; fruit **legume (pod)**; seeds non-endospermic.
- **Floral formula:** % ⚥ K(5) C1+2+(2) A(9)+1 G1 superior.
- Examples: **pea, gram, arhar, moong, urad, lentil, soybean, groundnut (*Arachis*), sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*), indigo (*Indigofera*), *Sesbania*, sweet pea (*Lathyrus odoratus*), lupin (*Lupinus*), clover (*Trifolium*), fenugreek (methi, *Trigonella*), sheesham (*Dalbergia*), palash (*Butea monosperma*), liquorice (mulethi, *Glycyrrhiza glabra*)**.
- Other subfamilies of Leguminosae: **Caesalpinioideae** (gulmohar, *Cassia*, tamarind — imbricate aestivation) and **Mimosoideae** (*Acacia*, *Mimosa pudica*, *Albizia*).

### Solanaceae (potato / nightshade family) — dicot

- Herbs, shrubs, small trees; stem sometimes underground tuber (potato); leaves simple, alternate, exstipulate.
- Flower actinomorphic, bisexual; calyx 5, united, **persistent** (stays on the fruit — brinjal); corolla 5, united; stamens 5, **epipetalous**; ovary superior, **bicarpellary, syncarpous**, **obliquely placed**, **swollen placenta with axile placentation**; fruit **berry or capsule**; seeds endospermic.
- **Floral formula:** ⊕ ⚥ K(5) C(5) A5 (epipetalous) G(2) superior.
- Examples: **potato (*Solanum tuberosum*), brinjal (*S. melongena*), tomato (*Lycopersicon esculentum* = *S. lycopersicum*), chilli (*Capsicum*), tobacco (*Nicotiana tabacum*)**, ***Atropa belladonna*** (atropine), ***Datura*** (hallucinogen), **ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*)**, *Petunia* (ornamental), black nightshade (*S. nigrum*), *Physalis* (cape gooseberry).

### Liliaceae (lily family) — monocot

- "A characteristic representative of monocotyledons". Perennial herbs with **underground bulbs, corms or rhizomes**; leaves mostly basal, alternate, linear, exstipulate, parallel venation.
- Flower actinomorphic, bisexual; **perianth tepals 6 (3 + 3)**, often united into a tube, valvate; stamens 6 (3 + 3), **epitepalous (epiphyllous)**; ovary superior, **tricarpellary, syncarpous, trilocular, axile placentation**; fruit **capsule**, rarely berry; seeds endospermic.
- **Floral formula:** Br ⊕ ⚥ P(3+3) A3+3 G(3) superior.
- Examples: **tulip (*Tulipa*), *Gloriosa* (glory lily; colchicine), *Asparagus* (vegetable), *Aloe* (medicine), *Colchicum autumnale* (colchicine), *Smilax* (sarsaparilla), *Lilium*, *Scilla***. **Onion and garlic (*Allium*)** are placed in Liliaceae in NCERT and older systems; modern classification (APG) puts them in Amaryllidaceae — follow NCERT in the exam.
- So "**in Liliaceae, some plants have underground parts**" is a **correct** statement.

### Compositae / Asteraceae (sunflower family) — dicot

- **Largest family of dicots** (about 23,000+ species; Orchidaceae is a close rival as largest overall). Mostly **herbs and shrubs** (few trees).
- Inflorescence **capitulum (head)** surrounded by an **involucre of bracts**. Florets of two kinds: **ray florets** (outer, zygomorphic, strap-shaped/ligulate, often female or neuter) and **disc florets** (inner, tubular, actinomorphic, bisexual).
- Calyx modified into hairs or scales — **pappus** (helps fruit dispersal by wind); corolla 5, united; stamens 5, **syngenesious** (anthers united, filaments free), epipetalous; ovary **inferior**, bicarpellary, syncarpous, unilocular, **basal placentation**, one ovule; fruit **cypsela**.
- **Floral formula (disc floret):** ⊕ ⚥ K pappus C(5) A(5) (syngenesious, epipetalous) G(2) inferior.
- Examples: **sunflower (*Helianthus annuus*), marigold (*Tagetes*), *Chrysanthemum*, *Dahlia*, *Zinnia*, lettuce (*Lactuca sativa*), safflower (*Carthamus* — oil), *Tridax*, *Parthenium hysterophorus* (congress grass — allergic weed), *Artemisia*, *Ageratum*, dandelion (*Taraxacum*), stevia (*Stevia rebaudiana* — sweetener), *Eclipta* (bhringraj)**.
- Plants of this family typically have **small flowers (florets) grouped in heads and small one-seeded fruits** — "trees with large flowers and seeds" is **not** a feature of Compositae.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is incorrect statement? (A) In Liliaceae, some plants have underground parts. (B) Fibre-yielding plants are found in Malvaceae. (C) Trees with large flowers and seeds are found in Compositae. …". Answer: **(C)** is incorrect — Compositae are mostly herbs with tiny florets in a head and small cypsela fruits. (A) bulbs/corms of lilies and (B) cotton and mesta of Malvaceae are correct.

### Poaceae / Gramineae (grass family) — monocot

- Herbs (bamboo is woody); stem a **culm** with **hollow internodes** and **solid, swollen nodes**; **intercalary meristem** at nodes.
- Leaves alternate, distichous, with **leaf sheath** around the stem and a **ligule** at the junction of sheath and blade.
- Inflorescence of **spikelets** (each with glumes); flower small, enclosed by **lemma and palea**; perianth reduced to 2–3 **lodicules**; stamens usually **3**, **versatile anthers** on long filaments; ovary superior, unilocular, one ovule, two **feathery stigmas** — adaptations for **wind pollination (anemophily)**.
- Fruit **caryopsis** (pericarp fused with seed coat); endospermic.
- Examples: **rice, wheat, maize, barley, oats, sorghum, bajra, ragi, sugarcane (*Saccharum*), bamboo (*Bambusa*, *Dendrocalamus* — tallest grasses), doob grass (*Cynodon dactylon*), lemon grass (*Cymbopogon*), *Vetiveria* (khus)**. **Bamboo** flowers once in its life (monocarpic) after many years.
- Economically the **most important family** (cereals feed the world).

### Bonus: Brassicaceae / Cruciferae (mustard family)

Corolla **cruciform** (4 petals in a cross), stamens **tetradynamous** (4 long + 2 short), ovary bicarpellary with a false septum (**replum**), **parietal placentation**, fruit **siliqua**. Examples: mustard, radish, cabbage, cauliflower, turnip.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Potato = stem tuber; sweet potato = root tuber.** Ginger and turmeric = rhizome (stem). Onion bulb: edible part is fleshy **leaves**.
- *Opuntia*: **spines are leaves**, flat green part is **stem** (phylloclade).
- Pea tendril = **leaf**; cucumber/grape tendril = **stem**.
- Thorn (stem, *Bougainvillea*) vs spine (leaf, cactus) vs prickle (epidermal, rose).
- Apple, strawberry, cashew = **false fruits**; banana = parthenocarpic.
- Mango edible part = mesocarp; coconut = endosperm; litchi = aril; pomegranate = seed coat; wheat = endosperm.
- **Collenchyma has cellulose/pectin thickening at corners and is living**; sclerenchyma is lignified and dead.
- Only **xylem parenchyma** is living in xylem; only **phloem fibres** are dead in phloem.
- **Exarch in roots, endarch in stems.**
- **Monocot stem: sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered closed bundles, no pith distinction**; dicot stem: collenchymatous hypodermis, ring of open bundles.
- **Dicot root: 2–4 xylem bundles**; monocot root: polyarch with large pith.
- **Bulliform cells** = grasses (monocot leaves).
- **Heartwood is dead and does not conduct**; sapwood conducts.
- Bark = everything outside vascular cambium, not just cork.
- **Pea: marginal placentation; mustard: parietal; china rose and tomato: axile; sunflower: basal; *Dianthus*: free central.**
- **Fabaceae stamens (9)+1** (diadelphous); Malvaceae monoadelphous; Compositae syngenesious.
- Compositae are **mostly herbs** with small florets — not large-flowered trees.
- Onion/garlic family: Liliaceae per NCERT; Amaryllidaceae per modern APG.

## Quick revision

1. Carrot conical, radish fusiform, turnip/beet napiform, sweet potato tuberous root.
2. Prop roots: banyan; stilt roots: maize, sugarcane; pneumatophores: *Rhizophora*.
3. Potato tuber, ginger rhizome, *Colocasia* corm, onion bulb — all stems.
4. Phylloclade: *Opuntia*; offset: *Pistia*, *Eichhornia*; sucker: banana, pineapple.
5. Phyllotaxy: alternate (china rose), opposite (*Calotropis*, guava), whorled (*Alstonia*).
6. Leaf tendril: pea; pitcher: *Nepenthes*; phyllode: Australian *Acacia*.
7. Hypogynous: mustard, brinjal; perigynous: rose, plum; epigynous: guava, cucumber.
8. Twisted aestivation: china rose, cotton; vexillary: pea; valvate: *Calotropis*.
9. Placentation: marginal (pea), axile (china rose), parietal (mustard), basal (sunflower).
10. Composite fruits: pineapple, jackfruit, mulberry (sorosis), fig (syconus).
11. Maize seed: scutellum, coleoptile, coleorhiza, aleurone layer.
12. Intercalary meristem: grasses — base of internodes.
13. Collenchyma: living, corner thickening of cellulose and pectin.
14. Sclereids (stone cells) in pear and guava pulp.
15. Vessels absent in gymnosperms; companion cells absent in gymnosperms.
16. Casparian strips of suberin in endodermis.
17. Bicollateral bundles: *Cucurbita*.
18. Annual rings = spring wood + autumn wood → dendrochronology.
19. Periderm = phellem + phellogen + phelloderm; lenticels for gas exchange.
20. Malvaceae: monoadelphous, fibres (cotton, mesta).
21. Fabaceae: % ⚥ K(5) C1+2+(2) A(9)+1 G1.
22. Solanaceae: ⊕ ⚥ K(5) C(5) A5 G(2), swollen placenta.
23. Liliaceae: Br ⊕ ⚥ P(3+3) A3+3 G(3).
24. Compositae: capitulum, pappus, syngenesious, inferior ovary, cypsela.
25. Poaceae: culm, ligule, lodicules, versatile anthers, caryopsis.
