This chapter follows NCERT class 9 "Diversity in Living Organisms" and class 11 "Plant Kingdom", adds B.Sc. botany facts (algal pigments and reserves, gametophyte vs sporophyte, heterospory) and the economic botany that TRE loves (source plant + useful part).
TRE has asked: "floridean starch is similar to", "agar-agar is obtained from", "bryophyte plant body", "monocots", "sunn hemp is obtained from", "groundnut oil is obtained from", "geocarpic fruit", "basmati-producing state".

> **In simple words:** The plant kingdom goes from simple to complex: algae (mostly water plants, no roots), bryophytes (mosses — first land plants but still need water), pteridophytes (ferns — first plants with xylem and phloem), gymnosperms (naked seeds, like pine) and angiosperms (flowering plants with seeds inside fruits). Humans use plants for food, oil, fibre, medicine, timber and much more.

## 1. Classification of plants — an overview

| Group | Body | Vascular tissue | Seeds | Dominant generation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Algae** (Thallophyta) | Thallus (no root, stem, leaf) | No | No | Varies | *Spirogyra*, *Sargassum* |
| **Bryophytes** | Thallus or leafy; **rhizoids** | **No** | No | **Gametophyte (n)** | *Riccia*, *Marchantia*, *Funaria* |
| **Pteridophytes** | True root, stem, leaves | **Yes** | **No** | **Sporophyte (2n)** | Ferns, *Selaginella* |
| **Gymnosperms** | Trees/shrubs | Yes | **Naked** (no ovary/fruit) | Sporophyte | *Cycas*, *Pinus* |
| **Angiosperms** | Herbs to trees | Yes | **Enclosed in fruit** | Sporophyte | Mango, wheat |

- Old grouping: **Cryptogams** (hidden reproduction, no flowers/seeds) = algae, bryophytes, pteridophytes; **Phanerogams** (seed plants) = gymnosperms, angiosperms.
- Eichler (1883) divided plants into Cryptogamae and Phanerogamae. Bentham and Hooker's system (1862–1883) is a natural system for seed plants; Engler and Prantl's system is phylogenetic.

### Types of classification

| Type | Basis | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Artificial** | One or few superficial characters | Linnaeus (sexual system, based on stamens) |
| **Natural** | Many characters including internal (anatomy, embryology) | **Bentham and Hooker** |
| **Phylogenetic** | Evolutionary relationships | **Engler and Prantl**, Hutchinson |
| Numerical taxonomy | All characters, computers, equal weight | — |
| Cytotaxonomy | Chromosome number and structure | — |
| Chemotaxonomy | Chemical constituents | — |

*Alternation of generations: a haploid gametophyte (makes gametes by mitosis) alternates with a diploid sporophyte (makes spores by meiosis).*

| Life cycle | Dominant phase | Meiosis in | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haplontic** | Haploid gametophyte; only zygote is 2n | Zygote (zygotic meiosis) | ***Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Chlamydomonas*** (many algae) |
| **Diplontic** | Diploid sporophyte; gametophyte reduced to few cells | Gamete formation | **All seed plants (gymnosperms, angiosperms)**; ***Fucus*** (alga) |
| **Haplo-diplontic** | Both multicellular | Spore formation | **Bryophytes** (gametophyte dominant), **pteridophytes** (sporophyte dominant); *Ectocarpus*, *Polysiphonia*, kelps |

## 3. Algae

- Chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, mostly **aquatic** (fresh and marine); some on moist soil, stones, wood, or with fungi (lichens) and animals (sloth bear).
- Size: from microscopic *Chlamydomonas* to colonial *Volvox*, filamentous *Ulothrix* and *Spirogyra*, to huge **kelps** (marine brown algae; *Macrocystis* can be 60 m+).
- Study of algae = **phycology**; father of Indian phycology — **M. O. P. Iyengar**.
- Reproduction: vegetative (fragmentation), asexual (**zoospores** — flagellated), sexual:
  - **Isogamous** — similar gametes (*Ulothrix*, *Spirogyra* — non-flagellated isogametes).
  - **Anisogamous** — dissimilar size (*Eudorina*, some *Chlamydomonas*).
  - **Oogamous** — large non-motile egg + small motile sperm (***Volvox*, *Fucus***).

### The three classes of algae

| Feature | **Chlorophyceae** (green) | **Phaeophyceae** (brown) | **Rhodophyceae** (red) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pigments | **Chlorophyll a, b** | Chlorophyll a, c, **fucoxanthin** | Chlorophyll a, d, **r-phycoerythrin** |
| **Stored food** | **Starch** (in pyrenoids) | **Mannitol, laminarin** | **Floridean starch** (structure similar to **amylopectin and glycogen**) |
| Cell wall | Cellulose (inner) + pectose (outer) | Cellulose + gelatinous **algin** | Cellulose, pectin, poly-sulphate esters |
| Flagella | 2–8, equal, apical | 2, unequal, lateral | **Absent** |
| Habitat | Fresh water, brackish, salt water | **Mostly marine** | **Mostly marine**, warmer areas; also deep water |
| Examples | ***Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulothrix, Spirogyra, Chara***, *Chlorella*, *Ulva* (sea lettuce) | ***Ectocarpus, Dictyota, Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus***, *Macrocystis* | ***Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium***, *Batrachospermum* (fresh water) |

- Brown algae body: holdfast (attachment), stipe (stalk), frond (leaf-like). Gametes pear-shaped.
- Red algae can live at great depths because **phycoerythrin absorbs blue-green light** that penetrates deep water. Sexual reproduction is oogamous with complex post-fertilisation stages.
- ***Sargassum*** is a floating brown alga; the **Sargasso Sea** (North Atlantic) is named after it.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Floridean starch has the structure similar to (A) amylopectin and glycogen (B) mannitol and algin (C) laminarin and cellulose …". Official answer: **(A) amylopectin and glycogen**. Option (C) mixes a brown-algal reserve (laminarin) with a wall material.

## 4. Bryophytes

- **"Amphibians of the plant kingdom"** — live on land but need **water for fertilisation** (sperms swim). Grow in damp, shady places, hills.
- **Plant body is the gametophyte (haploid, n)** — the main, independent, photosynthetic plant. It is **thallus-like** (prostrate or erect), attached to the soil by unicellular or multicellular **rhizoids**. **No true roots, stems or leaves**, though it may have root-like, stem-like and leaf-like structures.
- **No vascular tissue** (no xylem, no phloem). Hence small.
- Sex organs are multicellular and jacketed: male **antheridium** (biflagellate **antherozoids**), female **archegonium** (flask-shaped; one egg).
- Zygote → **sporophyte** (2n) which is **not free-living** — it is **attached to and dependent on the gametophyte** for food. It has foot, seta and capsule. Spores (n) form by meiosis in the capsule.
- Study of bryophytes = **bryology**; father of Indian bryology — **S. R. Kashyap**.

| | **Liverworts** | **Mosses** |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Flat, dorsiventral thallus | Two stages: **protonema** (creeping, green, filamentous, from spore) and **leafy stage** (upright stem-like axis with spirally arranged leaf-like parts) |
| Rhizoids | Unicellular | Multicellular, branched |
| Asexual reproduction | **Gemmae** (green buds in gemma cups) | Fragmentation, budding of protonema |
| Sporophyte | Simple | More elaborate; elaborate spore dispersal (peristome teeth) |
| Examples | ***Marchantia*, *Riccia*** | ***Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum*** |

**Importance of bryophytes:**
- Mosses with lichens are **pioneers** on bare rocks — make soil (succession).
- ***Sphagnum* (peat moss)** — forms **peat** (fuel); holds lots of water → used as **packing material** for trans-shipment of living material and in nurseries.
- Mosses form dense mats on soil → reduce erosion.

> **PYQ:** TRE 3.0 (2024) asked about the plant body of bryophytes (per PW analysis). Key fact: **the main plant body of a bryophyte is the haploid gametophyte**; it is thalloid or leafy with rhizoids, no true roots, stem or leaves, and no vascular tissue.

## 5. Pteridophytes

- **First terrestrial plants with vascular tissues — xylem and phloem**. Found in cool, damp, shady places; some in sandy soil.
- **Main plant body is the sporophyte (2n)**, differentiated into **true root, stem and leaves**. Leaves: small **microphylls** (*Selaginella*) or large **macrophylls** (ferns).
- Sporophyte bears **sporangia** on leaf-like **sporophylls**; compact strobili/cones in *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*.
- Spores (n) germinate into a small, free-living, multicellular, photosynthetic, thalloid gametophyte called the **prothallus**. It needs cool, damp places. Water is needed for fertilisation.
- **Homosporous** (one kind of spore) — most ferns, *Lycopodium*, *Equisetum*. **Heterosporous** (two kinds — large **megaspores** and small **microspores**) — ***Selaginella*, *Salvinia***, *Marsilea*, *Azolla*. Heterospory and retention of the megaspore = **precursor of the seed habit**.
- Classes: **Psilopsida** (*Psilotum*, a "living fossil"), **Lycopsida** (*Selaginella*, *Lycopodium* — club mosses), **Sphenopsida** (*Equisetum* — horsetail; silica in stem), **Pteropsida** (*Dryopteris*, *Pteris*, *Adiantum* — maidenhair fern).
- Uses: medicinal, soil binders, ornamental; ***Azolla*** (water fern with *Anabaena*) is a **biofertiliser in rice fields**; *Marsilea* sporocarps eaten; Carboniferous pteridophyte forests formed **coal**.

> **Exam angle:** Pteridophytes are the "**first vascular plants**" and are also called "**vascular cryptogams**" (vascular tissue present, but no flowers or seeds).

## 6. Gymnosperms

- *gymnos* = naked, *sperma* = seed. **Ovules are not enclosed by an ovary wall** and stay exposed before and after fertilisation → **naked seeds**; **no fruits**.
- Medium-sized trees to tall trees and shrubs. ***Sequoia* (giant redwood)** is one of the **tallest trees**; smallest gymnosperm ***Zamia pygmaea***.
- Roots: **tap roots**; *Pinus* roots have fungal association (**mycorrhiza**); ***Cycas*** has small specialised **coralloid roots** associated with N₂-fixing cyanobacteria (*Anabaena*, *Nostoc*).
- Stems unbranched (*Cycas*) or branched (*Pinus*, *Cedrus*). Leaves: pinnate, large (*Cycas*), or **needle-like** (*Pinus*) with thick cuticle and sunken stomata — reduce water loss.
- **Heterosporous**: microsporophylls form male **cones** (strobili) with pollen; megasporophylls form female cones with ovules. *Cycas* has no female cone — loose megasporophylls. **Pollination by wind**; pollen tube carries male gametes to the archegonia.
- **Endosperm is haploid (n)** — it is the female gametophyte tissue, formed **before** fertilisation (in angiosperms the endosperm is 3n).
- **Xylem has no vessels** (only tracheids) — except **Gnetales** (*Gnetum*, *Ephedra*); **phloem has no companion cells**.
- *Cycas* has the **largest ovules, largest sperms (motile, multiciliate) and largest egg** in the plant kingdom.

| Gymnosperm | Note / use |
|---|---|
| ***Cycas*** (sago palm) | Coralloid roots, motile sperms; sago (starch) from pith; "living fossil" |
| ***Pinus*** (chir, kail) | Turpentine, resin; needles; wood; pine nuts (*Pinus gerardiana* — chilgoza) |
| ***Ginkgo biloba*** (maidenhair tree) | **Living fossil** |
| ***Cedrus deodara*** (deodar) | Timber; Himalayas |
| *Ephedra* | **Ephedrine** (asthma, bronchitis) |
| *Taxus* (yew) | **Taxol** (anti-cancer) |

## 7. Angiosperms (flowering plants)

- Seeds **enclosed inside fruits** (ovules inside an ovary). Range from tiny ***Wolffia*** (smallest flowering plant, ~0.1 cm) to tall trees of ***Eucalyptus*** (over 100 m — tallest angiosperm).
- Xylem has **vessels**; phloem has **companion cells**.
- **Double fertilisation** (discovered by **S. G. Nawaschin, 1898**, in *Fritillaria* and *Lilium*): one male gamete + egg → zygote (2n) → embryo; second male gamete + two polar nuclei (secondary nucleus) → **primary endosperm nucleus (3n)** = **triple fusion** → **endosperm (3n)**. Unique to angiosperms.
- Embryo sac (female gametophyte) is typically **7-celled and 8-nucleate** (Polygonum type).
- Two classes: **dicotyledons** and **monocotyledons**.

### Monocot vs dicot

| Feature | **Dicot** | **Monocot** |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons in seed | **Two** | **One** |
| Root system | **Tap root** | **Fibrous (adventitious)** |
| Leaf venation | **Reticulate** (net-like) | **Parallel** |
| Flower parts | In 4s or **5s** (tetramerous / pentamerous) | In **3s** (trimerous) |
| Vascular bundles in stem | **In a ring**, open (with cambium) | **Scattered**, closed (no cambium) |
| Secondary growth | Usually present | Usually absent (except *Dracaena*, palms — anomalous) |
| Pollen | Tricolpate (three pores) | Monocolpate (one pore) |
| Leaf anatomy | Dorsiventral | Isobilateral |
| Examples | Pea, gram, mango, neem, rose, sunflower, potato, cotton | **Rice, wheat, maize, sugarcane, bamboo, banana, onion, lily, palm, coconut, orchids, grasses** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 3.0 (2024) asked about monocots (per PW analysis). Remember the five quick identifiers: one cotyledon, fibrous roots, parallel veins, trimerous flowers, scattered closed vascular bundles.

> **Common mistake:** Coconut, banana, palms and bamboo look like trees but are **monocots**; groundnut and gram are **dicots** even though they are small.

### Fibre-yielding plants

Fibres are long, thick-walled **sclerenchyma** cells. Classified by where they come from:

| Fibre type | From | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface fibres** | Hairs on **seed coat** | **Cotton** (*Gossypium* — Malvaceae); kapok / silk cotton (*Ceiba*, *Bombax* — fruit wall hairs) |
| **Bast (soft) fibres** | **Phloem / pericycle of stem** | **Jute** (*Corchorus capsularis*, *C. olitorius* — Malvaceae/Tiliaceae), **sunn hemp** (***Crotalaria juncea*** — **Fabaceae**), flax (*Linum usitatissimum*), hemp (*Cannabis sativa*), **mesta / kenaf** (*Hibiscus cannabinus* — Malvaceae), ramie (*Boehmeria nivea*) |
| **Hard (structural) fibres** | **Leaves** (vascular bundles) | **Sisal** (*Agave sisalana*), Manila hemp (*Musa textilis*), New Zealand flax |
| Fruit fibre | **Mesocarp** of fruit | **Coir** (*Cocos nucifera*) |

- **Jute** — "golden fibre"; mainly West Bengal, Bihar (Purnia, Katihar, Kishanganj), Assam. Fibres separated by **retting** (soaking stems in water so microbes rot the soft tissues).
- **Sunn hemp** (*Crotalaria juncea*) — bast fibre from **phloem**; used for ropes, twine, fishing nets, paper; also a green-manure crop that fixes nitrogen. (The question's "sun hemp jute" refers to this phloem fibre.)
- Cotton fibres are **epidermal outgrowths of the seed coat** — almost pure **cellulose** (about 90%+).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Sun hemp jute is obtained from (A) leaf (B) phloem (C) xylem …". Official answer: **(B) phloem**.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Red algae have no flagella** at any stage; brown algae have two unequal lateral flagella.
- **Floridean starch = red algae**; **laminarin and mannitol = brown algae**; starch = green algae.
- **Agar from red algae (*Gelidium, Gracilaria*)**; **algin from brown algae**; carrageenan from red algae.
- *Spirulina* and *Nostoc* are **cyanobacteria**, not true algae; *Chlorella* is a true green alga.
- *Volvox* and *Fucus* are **oogamous**; *Spirogyra* is isogamous.
- *Fucus* is a **diplontic** alga — exception among algae.
- **Bryophyte plant body = gametophyte (n)**; pteridophyte plant body = sporophyte (2n).
- Bryophytes have **no vascular tissue**; pteridophytes are the **first vascular** plants but have **no seeds**.
- **Gymnosperm endosperm is haploid (n)**, angiosperm endosperm is triploid (3n).
- Gymnosperms lack vessels — **except Gnetales**; they also have no fruits.
- **Coconut, banana, bamboo, palm = monocots.**
- **Groundnut is a geocarpic fruit**; potato (stem tuber) and onion (bulb of fleshy leaves) are not fruits.
- **Sunn hemp and jute are bast (phloem) fibres**; cotton is a seed-surface fibre; coir is a mesocarp fibre; sisal is a leaf fibre.
- Sunn hemp (*Crotalaria*) is in **Fabaceae**; jute and cotton are in **Malvaceae** (jute was earlier placed in Tiliaceae).
- Clove = **flower bud**; saffron = **stigma**; cinnamon = bark; black pepper = fruit; turmeric and ginger = rhizome.
- Coffee and cocoa are **seeds**; tea is **leaves**.
- Quinine from **bark** (*Cinchona*); reserpine from **root** (*Rauwolfia*).
- **Artabotrys** is an ornamental (hari champa), not an oil crop.

## Quick revision

1. Haplontic: *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Chlamydomonas*; diplontic: seed plants, *Fucus*; haplo-diplontic: bryophytes, pteridophytes, *Ectocarpus*, *Polysiphonia*.
2. Green algae: chl a, b; starch; *Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulothrix, Spirogyra, Chara*.
3. Brown algae: fucoxanthin; laminarin, mannitol; algin; *Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus*.
4. Red algae: phycoerythrin; floridean starch ≈ amylopectin and glycogen; no flagella.
5. Agar from *Gelidium* and *Gracilaria*; carrageenan from red algae.
6. *Chlorella* — protein-rich space food.
7. Bryophytes: amphibians of plant kingdom; gametophyte dominant; rhizoids; no vascular tissue.
8. *Marchantia* reproduces by gemmae; moss has a protonema stage.
9. *Sphagnum* gives peat and packing material.
10. Pteridophytes: first vascular plants; sporophyte dominant; gametophyte = prothallus.
11. Heterosporous pteridophytes: *Selaginella*, *Salvinia* — precursor of seed habit.
12. *Azolla* + *Anabaena* = biofertiliser in rice.
13. Gymnosperms: naked seeds, no fruit, haploid endosperm, no vessels (except Gnetales).
14. *Cycas* coralloid roots with cyanobacteria; *Pinus* mycorrhiza; *Ginkgo* living fossil.
15. Double fertilisation (Nawaschin, 1898) — unique to angiosperms; endosperm 3n.
16. Smallest angiosperm *Wolffia*; tallest *Eucalyptus*; largest flower *Rafflesia*.
17. Monocot: one cotyledon, fibrous roots, parallel veins, trimerous flowers, scattered bundles.
18. Groundnut (*Arachis hypogaea*): geocarpic fruit; oil from seed.
19. Mustard oil: *Brassica juncea*; coconut oil from endosperm.
20. Sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*) and jute: phloem (bast) fibres.
21. Cotton: seed-coat hairs; coir: mesocarp; sisal: leaf.
22. Tea: leaf; coffee: seed; clove: flower bud; saffron: stigma.
23. Quinine: *Cinchona* bark; reserpine: *Rauwolfia* root (BP).
24. Rubber: *Hevea brasiliensis* latex.
25. Makhana: *Euryale ferox* — Mithila makhana GI; Shahi litchi — Muzaffarpur.

## Practice with answers

1. Reserve food of brown algae? — **Laminarin and mannitol**.
2. Pigment that lets red algae live in deep water? — **r-Phycoerythrin** (absorbs blue-green light).
3. Agar is obtained from which algae? — ***Gelidium* and *Gracilaria*** (red algae).
4. Which alga gives its name to a sea? — ***Sargassum*** (Sargasso Sea).
5. Which algal class lacks flagellated cells? — **Rhodophyceae** (red algae).
6. Life cycle of *Spirogyra*? — **Haplontic**.
7. The independent plant body of a moss is? — **Gametophyte (haploid)**.
8. Asexual buds of *Marchantia*? — **Gemmae**.
9. First plants with xylem and phloem? — **Pteridophytes**.
10. Gametophyte of a fern is called? — **Prothallus**.
11. Ploidy of gymnosperm endosperm? — **Haploid (n)**.
12. Who discovered double fertilisation? — **S. G. Nawaschin** (1898).
13. Smallest flowering plant? — ***Wolffia***.
14. Is coconut a monocot or dicot? — **Monocot** (one cotyledon, parallel veins).
15. Scientific name of groundnut? — ***Arachis hypogaea***.
16. Why is groundnut called geocarpic? — **Its fruit ripens underground** (gynophore pushes the ovary into soil).
17. Sunn hemp fibre comes from which tissue? — **Phloem (bast)** of *Crotalaria juncea*.
18. Coir is obtained from which part of coconut? — **Mesocarp** of the fruit.
19. Edible part of clove? — **Dried flower bud**.
20. Reserpine is obtained from? — **Roots of *Rauwolfia serpentina***.
