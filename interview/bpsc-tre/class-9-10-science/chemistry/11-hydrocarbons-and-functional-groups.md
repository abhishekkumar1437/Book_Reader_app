This chapter walks through the families of organic compounds, from hydrocarbons to amines. It covers NCERT class 10 ("Carbon and its Compounds"), class 11 ("Hydrocarbons") and class 12 (haloalkanes, alcohols–phenols–ethers, aldehydes–ketones–acids, amines), with B.Sc.-style named reactions. TRE 2.0 asked five questions here: Markovnikov's rule, which alkyne gives no red precipitate with ammoniacal cuprous chloride, CHCl₃ on oxidation, which halide gives acetaldehyde with aqueous KOH, and aspirin (chapter 12).

> **In simple words:** Learn each family by three things: how it is made, its one key reaction pattern (alkanes substitute, alkenes and alkynes add, benzene substitutes, carbonyls add nucleophiles), and the lab test that identifies it. Named reactions are just famous examples of those patterns.

## 1. Alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂)

- Saturated, sp³; "paraffins" (little affinity). Methane = marsh gas; main component of natural gas and **CNG**. **LPG** = mainly butane + propane (ethyl mercaptan added for smell).
- **Preparation:** Wurtz reaction 2RX + 2Na —dry ether→ R–R (even-carbon alkanes; methane cannot be made); decarboxylation RCOONa + NaOH —CaO (soda lime), heat→ RH (one carbon less); Kolbe's electrolysis 2RCOONa → R–R + 2CO₂ (at anode); hydrogenation of alkenes (Ni); reduction of RX with Zn/HCl.
- **Reactions:** free-radical **halogenation** in light (reactivity F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂; 3° H replaced fastest), combustion, controlled oxidation, isomerisation (AlCl₃/HCl), aromatisation (n-hexane → benzene over Cr₂O₃/V₂O₅/Mo₂O₃ at 773 K), **cracking / pyrolysis** (higher alkanes → smaller alkanes and alkenes).
- **Physical:** b.p. rises with chain length; branching lowers b.p. (n-pentane 309 K > isopentane 301 K > neopentane 282.5 K) because of smaller surface area.
- **Fuel quality:** **octane number** (petrol; iso-octane 2,2,4-trimethylpentane = 100, n-heptane = 0; branched alkanes and aromatics raise it; knocking was reduced by tetraethyl lead, now banned, replaced by MTBE etc.). **Cetane number** (diesel; cetane = 100, α-methylnaphthalene = 0).

**Petroleum fractions** (fractional distillation): petroleum gas (C₁–C₄) < petrol/gasoline (C₅–C₁₀) < kerosene (C₁₀–C₁₆) < diesel/gas oil (C₁₅–C₁₈) < lubricating oil, paraffin wax, asphalt/bitumen (residue).

**Worked numerical 1.** Volume of O₂ needed to burn 1 L of propane completely (same T, P)? C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O → **5 L O₂** (and 3 L CO₂ formed). Air (21% O₂) needed ≈ 5/0.21 = 23.8 L.

## 2. Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ)

- One C=C (σ + π); sp²; "olefins" (oil-forming). Show geometrical isomerism if each C=C carbon has two different groups.
- **Preparation:** dehydration of alcohols with conc. H₂SO₄ at 443 K (C₂H₅OH → C₂H₄ + H₂O) or Al₂O₃ at 623 K; dehydrohalogenation with **alcoholic KOH** (Saytzeff product major); partial reduction of alkynes; Kolbe electrolysis of succinate.
- **Characteristic reaction: electrophilic addition** (the π electrons attack an electrophile).

| Reagent | Product with propene CH₃CH=CH₂ |
|---|---|
| H₂ / Ni, Pt, Pd (Sabatier–Senderens) | Propane |
| Br₂ in CCl₄ / bromine water | 1,2-Dibromopropane — **red-brown colour disappears (test for unsaturation)** |
| HBr (no peroxide) | **2-Bromopropane (Markovnikov)** |
| HBr + peroxide (Kharasch effect) | **1-Bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov)** |
| H₂O / H⁺ (acid-catalysed hydration) | Propan-2-ol (Markovnikov) |
| B₂H₆ then H₂O₂/OH⁻ (hydroboration–oxidation) | Propan-1-ol (anti-Markovnikov alcohol) |
| Cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄ (**Baeyer's reagent**) | Propane-1,2-diol; **pink colour disappears** |
| O₃ then Zn/H₂O (**ozonolysis**) | Ethanal + methanal — locates the double bond |
| Hot acidic KMnO₄ | Ethanoic acid + CO₂ |
| Polymerisation | Polypropylene |

### Markovnikov's rule (1869)

In the addition of an unsymmetrical reagent (HX, H₂O, H₂SO₄) to an **unsymmetrical alkene**, the negative part goes to the double-bonded carbon with **fewer hydrogen atoms** ("the rich get richer" — H goes to the carbon that already has more H).

**Why:** H⁺ adds first to give the **more stable carbocation**. For propene, H⁺ on C-1 gives the 2° carbocation CH₃–C⁺H–CH₃ (more stable than the 1° CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺), so Br⁻ attaches at C-2.

- Markovnikov's rule applies only when **both** the alkene and the reagent are unsymmetrical. It is meaningless for **HCl + CH₂=CH₂** (symmetrical alkene — only one product) and for **Cl₂ + CH₃CH=CH₂** (symmetrical reagent — both carbons get Cl).
- **Peroxide (Kharasch) effect** works with **HBr only** — H–Cl is too strong to break homolytically; the I· radical is too unreactive to add to C=C.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Markownikoff's rule is applicable to which of the following reactions? (A) HCl + CH₂=CH₂ (B) Cl₂ + CH₃CH=CH₂ (C) HBr + CH₃CH=CH₂". Official answer: **(C)**.

**Worked numerical 2.** 2.8 g of an alkene adds exactly 8.0 g of Br₂. Molar mass and formula? Moles Br₂ = 8/160 = 0.05 = moles alkene (one C=C). M = 2.8/0.05 = 56 → CₙH₂ₙ with 14n = 56 → **C₄H₈ (butene)**.

## 3. Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂)

- C≡C (σ + 2π), sp, linear. Ethyne (acetylene) — oxyacetylene flame (~3000 °C) for welding and cutting; artificial ripening of fruits (from CaC₂).
- **Preparation:** **CaC₂ + 2H₂O → C₂H₂ + Ca(OH)₂** (calcium carbide from CaO + 3C in electric furnace); double dehydrohalogenation of vicinal dihalides with alc. KOH then NaNH₂.

### Acidic hydrogen of terminal alkynes

The sp carbon (50% s-character) is the most electronegative, so **H on a triple-bonded carbon (≡C–H) is weakly acidic** (pKa ≈ 25). Such terminal alkynes react with:
- Na or NaNH₂ (in liquid NH₃) → sodium acetylide + H₂/NH₃.
- **Ammoniacal silver nitrate (Tollens' reagent) → white precipitate** of silver acetylide: HC≡CH + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ → AgC≡CAg↓.
- **Ammoniacal cuprous chloride (Cu₂Cl₂ in NH₃) → red (red-brown) precipitate** of copper(I) acetylide: HC≡CH → CuC≡CCu↓.

**Internal alkynes (no ≡C–H), such as but-2-yne CH₃–C≡C–CH₃, do not give these precipitates.** Alkenes and alkanes do not either. This test distinguishes but-1-yne (positive) from but-2-yne (negative), and ethyne from ethene.

| Compound | ≡C–H present? | Red ppt with ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂? |
|---|---|---|
| C₂H₂ (ethyne) | Yes, two | Yes |
| C₃H₄ as propyne CH₃–C≡CH | Yes, one | Yes |
| C₃H₄ as allene CH₂=C=CH₂ (isomer) | No | No |
| **CH₃–C≡C–CH₃ (but-2-yne)** | **No** | **No** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following does not give red precipitate with ammoniacal cuprous chloride? (A) C₂H₂ (B) C₃H₄ (C) CH₃–C≡C–CH₃". Official answer: **(C) but-2-yne**. (C₃H₄ is read as propyne, which has a terminal H.)

**Other alkyne reactions:** H₂ with **Lindlar's catalyst** (Pd/BaSO₄ or CaCO₃ poisoned with quinoline) → **cis-alkene**; Na in liquid NH₃ (Birch-type) → **trans-alkene**; adds 2 mol Br₂ or HX (Markovnikov twice → gem-dihalide); **hydration with dil. H₂SO₄ + HgSO₄ at 333 K**: ethyne → **ethanal (acetaldehyde)** (via enol, the only alkyne giving an aldehyde), propyne → **propanone**; cyclic polymerisation through red-hot iron tube at 873 K: 3C₂H₂ → **benzene**; ozonolysis gives diketones/acids.

## 4. Benzene and aromatic compounds

- Discovered by Faraday (1825); Kekulé structure (1865) — alternating single and double bonds that resonate. All six C–C bonds are equal (139 pm); planar; every C sp².
- **Hückel rule (aromaticity):** cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, with **(4n + 2) π electrons** (2, 6, 10, 14 …). Aromatic: benzene (6), naphthalene (10), pyridine, pyrrole, furan, thiophene, cyclopentadienyl anion (6), tropylium cation (6), cyclopropenyl cation (2). **Antiaromatic** (4n, planar): cyclobutadiene, cyclopentadienyl cation. Cyclooctatetraene (8) is non-planar (tub) → non-aromatic.

**Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS)** — benzene prefers substitution (keeps aromaticity) over addition.

| Reaction | Reagents | Electrophile | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitration | Conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ (nitrating mixture), ~330 K | **NO₂⁺** | Nitrobenzene |
| Halogenation | Cl₂ / FeCl₃ (or Fe), dark | Cl⁺ | Chlorobenzene |
| Sulphonation | Fuming H₂SO₄ (oleum) | SO₃ | Benzenesulphonic acid |
| Friedel–Crafts alkylation | CH₃Cl / anhydrous AlCl₃ | CH₃⁺ | Toluene |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | CH₃COCl / AlCl₃ | CH₃CO⁺ | Acetophenone |

Benzene also adds: 3H₂ (Ni, 500 K) → cyclohexane; 3Cl₂ in UV light → **benzene hexachloride (BHC, gammexane — lindane is the γ-isomer, an insecticide)**.

**Directive influence of substituents**

| Ortho/para directing + activating | Ortho/para directing + deactivating | Meta directing + deactivating |
|---|---|---|
| –O⁻, –NH₂, –NHR, –NR₂, –OH, –OCH₃, –NHCOCH₃, –CH₃, –R, –C₆H₅ | **Halogens (–F, –Cl, –Br, –I)** | **–NO₂**, –CN, –CHO, –COR, –COOH, –COOR, –SO₃H, –NR₃⁺, –CF₃, –CCl₃ |

Activating groups donate electrons (+R/+I) into the ring; meta directors withdraw (−R/−I). Aniline is so activated that bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline at once; Friedel–Crafts does not work on aniline (it complexes AlCl₃) or nitrobenzene (too deactivated — nitrobenzene is used as the solvent).

Polynuclear hydrocarbons such as **3,4-benzopyrene** (in tobacco smoke, coal tar) are carcinogenic.

## 5. Haloalkanes and haloarenes

- Classification: primary/secondary/tertiary; allylic, benzylic, vinylic, aryl. b.p. order R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F; density of CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄ > water.
- **Preparation:** from alcohols (HX with ZnCl₂, PCl₅, PCl₃, **SOCl₂ — best, by-products SO₂ and HCl are gases**), from alkenes (HX), Finkelstein (R–Cl + NaI in acetone → R–I), Swarts (R–Cl + AgF/Hg₂F₂ → R–F). Haloarenes: Sandmeyer reaction from diazonium salts.
- **Reactions:** nucleophilic substitution (chapter 10) — with aq. KOH → alcohol, KCN → nitrile, AgCN → isocyanide, KNO₂ → nitrite, AgNO₂ → nitroalkane, NH₃ → amines; elimination with alc. KOH; **Wurtz** (2RX + 2Na → R–R), **Wurtz–Fittig** (ArX + RX + 2Na → Ar–R), **Fittig** (2ArX + 2Na → Ar–Ar); Grignard formation RX + Mg (dry ether) → RMgX.

### What aqueous KOH does to different chlorides

| Halide | With aqueous KOH | Product |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃CH₂Cl (ethyl chloride) | Substitution | **Ethanol** |
| **CH₃CHCl₂ (1,1-dichloroethane, gem-dihalide on terminal C)** | → CH₃CH(OH)₂ (unstable gem-diol) → loses H₂O | **Ethanal (acetaldehyde)** |
| CH₃CCl₂CH₃ (2,2-dichloropropane) | → gem-diol → loses H₂O | **Propanone (acetone)** |
| CH₃CCl₃ (1,1,1-trichloroethane) | → CH₃C(OH)₃ → loses H₂O | **Ethanoic acid** (as acetate) |
| ClCH₂CH₂Cl (vicinal dihalide) | Substitution | **Ethane-1,2-diol** (glycol) |
| CH₃COCl (acetyl chloride) | Hydrolysis | **Acetic acid** (potassium acetate) |
| CHCl₃ | With alc. KOH + 1° amine | Isocyanide (carbylamine test) |

**Rule:** two OH groups on one carbon are unstable (Erlenmeyer rule) and lose water to give C=O. Gem-dihalides at the end of a chain give **aldehydes**; in the middle give **ketones**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following reacts with aqueous KOH to produce acetaldehyde? (A) CH₃CH₂Cl (B) CH₃CHCl₂ (C) CH₃COCl". Official answer: **(B) CH₃CHCl₂**.

### Chloroform, iodoform and other polyhalogen compounds

- **Chloroform (CHCl₃, trichloromethane):** made by chlorination of methane or from ethanol/acetone with bleaching powder (haloform reaction). Once used as an anaesthetic (Simpson, 1847), now abandoned (liver damage).
- **Oxidation in air and light gives phosgene**, a highly poisonous gas: **2CHCl₃ + O₂ —light→ 2COCl₂ + 2HCl**. So chloroform is stored in **dark-coloured bottles filled to the brim**, and **about 1% ethanol** is added, which converts any phosgene into harmless diethyl carbonate.
- Chloroform with conc. HNO₃ gives **chloropicrin** CCl₃NO₂ (tear gas, insecticide) — this is nitration, not oxidation.
- **Purity test:** pure CHCl₃ gives no white ppt with AgNO₃; if phosgene/HCl has formed, AgCl precipitates.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "CHCl₃ on oxidation gives (A) phosgene (B) formic acid (C) chloropicrin". Official answer: **(A) phosgene (COCl₂)**.

- **Iodoform (CHI₃):** yellow solid, antiseptic smell; **iodoform test** (I₂ + NaOH) is positive for compounds with **CH₃CO–** or **CH₃CH(OH)–**: ethanol, propan-2-ol, ethanal, acetone, acetophenone. Methanol, propan-1-ol, propanal, benzaldehyde, pentan-3-one are negative.
- **CCl₄:** fire extinguisher (Pyrene), solvent; ozone-depleting. **Freons (CFCs, e.g. CCl₂F₂)** — refrigerants, aerosols; deplete ozone. **DDT** (p,p′-dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) — insecticide (Müller, Nobel 1948), non-biodegradable, biomagnifies; banned in many countries.
- **Haloarenes are less reactive** to nucleophilic substitution than haloalkanes (resonance, sp² C) but undergo EAS at ortho/para positions (slower than benzene).

## 6. Alcohols, phenols and ethers

**Alcohols (R–OH)**
- **Ethanol:** by fermentation of molasses/starch (yeast enzymes **invertase** and **zymase**); rectified spirit 95.6%; absolute alcohol 100%; **denatured spirit** = ethanol + methanol/pyridine/CuSO₄ (unfit to drink). **Power alcohol** = ethanol blended with petrol (E20 programme in India).
- **Methanol (wood spirit):** from CO + 2H₂ (ZnO–Cr₂O₃, 200–300 atm); highly poisonous — small amounts cause **blindness**, larger amounts death.
- **Lucas test** (anhydrous ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl): **3° → turbidity immediately; 2° → within ~5 min; 1° → no turbidity at room temperature**.
- **Oxidation:** 1° → aldehyde → carboxylic acid (PCC stops at aldehyde); 2° → ketone; 3° → resistant (only under drastic conditions). Over hot Cu at 573 K: 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone, 3° → alkene (dehydration).
- **Dehydration ease:** 3° > 2° > 1° (via carbocation).
- Reaction with Na → RONa + H₂ (acidic O–H; order 1° > 2° > 3°).
- Glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol): sweet, viscous; by-product of soap making; nitroglycerine (dynamite, Nobel 1867).

**Phenols (Ar–OH)**
- Phenol = carbolic acid (Lister used it as antiseptic). Made by the **cumene process** (cumene → cumene hydroperoxide → phenol + **acetone**), from chlorobenzene (Dow process: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm), from benzenesulphonic acid, from diazonium salts (warming with water).
- **Tests:** neutral **FeCl₃ gives violet colour**; **bromine water gives white ppt of 2,4,6-tribromophenol**.
- **Kolbe's reaction:** sodium phenoxide + CO₂ (400 K, 4–7 atm), then H⁺ → **salicylic acid** (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) → acetylation gives **aspirin**.
- **Reimer–Tiemann reaction:** phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH (via dichlorocarbene) → **salicylaldehyde** (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde). With CCl₄ → salicylic acid.
- Nitration: dilute HNO₃ → o- and p-nitrophenol (separated by steam distillation); **conc. HNO₃ → picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol)** — yellow, explosive, used in dyeing and as an antiseptic for burns; stronger acid than phenol (pKa ≈ 0.4).
- Phenol + Zn dust → benzene; phenol + HCHO → **Bakelite** (chapter 12).

**Ethers (R–O–R′)**
- **Williamson synthesis:** RONa + R′X → ROR′ (best with 1° alkyl halide; 3° halides give alkenes).
- Diethyl ether — anaesthetic (historical), solvent for Grignard; forms explosive peroxides on standing in air. Cleaved by HI: ROR + HI → RI + ROH (with mixed ethers, I goes to the smaller alkyl; for aryl alkyl ethers → phenol + alkyl iodide). Anisole (methoxybenzene) is o/p directing.

## 7. Aldehydes and ketones (carbonyl compounds)

- **Preparation:** oxidation of 1°/2° alcohols; **Rosenmund reduction** (RCOCl + H₂, Pd/BaSO₄ → RCHO); **Stephen reaction** (RCN + SnCl₂/HCl, then H₂O → RCHO); **Etard reaction** (toluene + CrO₂Cl₂ → benzaldehyde); **Gattermann–Koch** (benzene + CO + HCl, AlCl₃ → benzaldehyde); ozonolysis of alkenes; hydration of alkynes; Friedel–Crafts acylation (ketones).
- **Nucleophilic addition** (carbonyl C is δ+): HCN → cyanohydrin; NaHSO₃ → crystalline bisulphite adduct (purification); Grignard reagent → alcohols (HCHO → 1°, other aldehydes → 2°, ketones → 3°); NH₂–Z derivatives: hydroxylamine → oxime, hydrazine → hydrazone, **2,4-DNP → orange/yellow 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (test for any C=O)**, semicarbazide → semicarbazone. Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones (less steric hindrance, less +I).

**Distinguishing tests**

| Test | Reagent | Positive for | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tollens'** | Ammoniacal AgNO₃, [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | All aldehydes (aliphatic and aromatic), HCOOH, glucose, terminal alkynes (different ppt) | **Silver mirror** |
| **Fehling's** | CuSO₄ (A) + alkaline sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) (B) | Aliphatic aldehydes, glucose, fructose; **not benzaldehyde**, not ketones | **Red-brown ppt of Cu₂O** |
| Benedict's | CuSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ + sodium citrate | Reducing sugars (glucose in urine) | Red / yellow / green ppt |
| **Schiff's** | Rosaniline decolourised by SO₂ | Aldehydes | **Pink/magenta** colour restored |
| 2,4-DNP (Brady's) | 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine | Aldehydes and ketones | Orange-yellow ppt |
| **Iodoform** | I₂ + NaOH | CH₃CO– and CH₃CH(OH)– | Yellow ppt CHI₃ |
| Sodium nitroprusside | — | Ketones (acetone in urine, alkaline) | Red colour |

## 8. Carboxylic acids and derivatives

- Formic acid (HCOOH) — in ant and nettle stings; the only acid that **reduces Tollens' and Fehling's** (it has a –CHO part). Acetic acid — vinegar is 6–8% (5–8%) acetic acid; pure acid freezes at 16.6 °C (**glacial acetic acid**).
- Carboxylic acids exist as **H-bonded dimers** (high b.p.; molar mass appears doubled in benzene).
- **Reactions:** with NaHCO₃ → CO₂ (test that distinguishes acids from phenols); esterification with alcohol + conc. H₂SO₄ (Fischer; fruity smell — ethyl acetate); **Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky (HVZ)**: X₂ + red P → α-halo acid (needs α-H); decarboxylation with soda lime; Kolbe electrolysis; reduction with LiAlH₄ → 1° alcohol; with PCl₅/SOCl₂ → acid chloride.
- **Reactivity of acid derivatives towards nucleophilic acyl substitution:** **acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide** (leaving-group ability Cl⁻ > RCOO⁻ > RO⁻ > NH₂⁻).
- **Saponification:** ester + NaOH → soap/alcohol (second order — chapter 6). Esters are used as flavours (isoamyl acetate — banana; octyl acetate — orange; methyl salicylate — oil of wintergreen).

## 9. Amines and diazonium salts

- 1° RNH₂, 2° R₂NH, 3° R₃N; quaternary R₄N⁺X⁻. Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂) darkens on standing (air oxidation).
- **Preparation:** reduction of nitro compounds (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl — Fe preferred industrially); reduction of nitriles (LiAlH₄ or Na/C₂H₅OH — Mendius) and amides; **Gabriel phthalimide synthesis** (only **pure 1° aliphatic** amines; cannot make aniline); **Hofmann bromamide degradation** RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → **RNH₂ (one carbon less)** + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O; ammonolysis of RX (mixture of 1°, 2°, 3°, 4°).
- **Tests:** **Carbylamine (isocyanide) test** — 1° amine (aliphatic or aromatic) + CHCl₃ + alc. KOH → **foul-smelling isocyanide** (2° and 3° do not respond). **Hinsberg test** (benzenesulphonyl chloride): 1° → sulphonamide soluble in alkali; 2° → sulphonamide insoluble in alkali; 3° → no reaction. **Nitrous acid:** 1° aliphatic → N₂ gas + alcohol; 1° aromatic at 273–278 K → diazonium salt; 2° → yellow oily N-nitrosamine; 3° aliphatic → soluble nitrite salt.
- **Benzenediazonium chloride** C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ — made by diazotisation (aniline + NaNO₂ + HCl at 0–5 °C); stable only when cold.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Markovnikov applies only to unsymmetrical alkene + unsymmetrical reagent**; peroxide effect only with HBr.
- **Only terminal alkynes** (≡C–H) give white ppt with ammoniacal AgNO₃ and red ppt with ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂; but-2-yne gives neither.
- Hydration of ethyne gives **acetaldehyde**; every other alkyne gives a ketone.
- **CHCl₃ + O₂ (light) → phosgene**; CHCl₃ + HNO₃ → chloropicrin. Ethanol (1%) is added as a stabiliser.
- **CH₃CHCl₂ + aq. KOH → acetaldehyde**; CH₃CH₂Cl → ethanol; CH₃CCl₂CH₃ → acetone.
- **Halogens are deactivating but ortho/para directing.**
- **Benzaldehyde does not give Fehling's test** but gives Tollens' test.
- **Formic acid reduces Tollens' and Fehling's**; acetic acid does not.
- Cannizzaro needs **no α-H**; aldol needs α-H.
- Lucas test: 3° fastest; 1° no turbidity at room temperature.
- Gabriel synthesis cannot make aromatic amines.
- Hofmann bromamide gives an amine with **one carbon less**.
- Phenol: violet with FeCl₃, white ppt with bromine water, but **no CO₂ with NaHCO₃**.
- Iodoform positive: ethanol, ethanal, acetone, propan-2-ol; negative: methanol, propanal, pentan-3-one.
- Methanol causes blindness; ethanol is the drinking alcohol.

## Quick revision

1. Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R; methane cannot be made this way.
2. Soda-lime decarboxylation gives alkane with one C less.
3. Branching lowers b.p. of alkanes.
4. Octane number: iso-octane 100, n-heptane 0.
5. Bromine water / Baeyer's reagent decolourise → unsaturation.
6. Markovnikov: H to carbon with more H; via more stable carbocation.
7. Peroxide effect (Kharasch) only with HBr.
8. CaC₂ + H₂O → C₂H₂.
9. Terminal alkyne: white ppt (Ag), red ppt (Cu); but-2-yne negative.
10. Lindlar → cis-alkene; Na/liq NH₃ → trans-alkene.
11. Ethyne + H₂O (HgSO₄/H₂SO₄) → ethanal.
12. 3C₂H₂ (red-hot Fe, 873 K) → benzene.
13. Hückel: 4n + 2 π electrons.
14. Nitrating mixture → NO₂⁺.
15. –NO₂, –CHO, –COOH, –CN are meta directors; halogens o/p but deactivating.
16. CHCl₃ + O₂ (light) → COCl₂ (phosgene); store in dark, add 1% ethanol.
17. CH₃CHCl₂ + aq KOH → CH₃CHO.
18. Iodoform test: CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)–.
19. Lucas test: 3° immediate.
20. Cumene process → phenol + acetone.
21. Kolbe's reaction → salicylic acid; Reimer–Tiemann → salicylaldehyde.
22. Tollens' — silver mirror; Fehling's — red Cu₂O (not benzaldehyde).
23. Cannizzaro: HCHO, C₆H₅CHO with conc. NaOH.
24. Clemmensen: Zn-Hg/HCl; Wolff–Kishner: N₂H₄/KOH.
25. Carbylamine test → 1° amines only; Hinsberg distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3°.

## Practice with answers

1. Product of HBr + propene in presence of peroxide? — **1-Bromopropane**.
2. Does Markovnikov's rule apply to HCl + ethene? — **No** (symmetrical alkene).
3. Reagent to distinguish but-1-yne from but-2-yne? — **Ammoniacal AgNO₃ or ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂** (only but-1-yne reacts).
4. Gas from calcium carbide and water? — **Acetylene (ethyne)**.
5. Catalyst giving cis-alkene from alkyne? — **Lindlar's catalyst**.
6. Number of π electrons in naphthalene? — **10** (aromatic, n = 2).
7. Is the –OH group o/p or m directing? — **Ortho/para, activating**.
8. Poisonous gas from chloroform in air and light? — **Phosgene (COCl₂)**.
9. Why is ethanol added to chloroform? — **Converts phosgene to harmless diethyl carbonate**.
10. Product of CH₃CCl₂CH₃ with aqueous KOH? — **Acetone**.
11. Which gives iodoform test: methanol or ethanol? — **Ethanol**.
12. Alcohol giving immediate turbidity in Lucas test? — **Tertiary (e.g. 2-methylpropan-2-ol)**.
13. Industrial process giving phenol and acetone together? — **Cumene process**.
14. Phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH gives? — **Salicylaldehyde** (Reimer–Tiemann).
15. Which aldehyde does not give Fehling's test? — **Benzaldehyde**.
16. Reaction converting C=O to CH₂ using Zn-Hg/HCl? — **Clemmensen reduction**.
17. Cannizzaro product of HCHO? — **Methanol + sodium formate**.
18. Most reactive acid derivative? — **Acid chloride**.
19. Test for primary amines with foul smell? — **Carbylamine (isocyanide) test**.
20. Orange dye from benzenediazonium chloride and phenol? — **p-Hydroxyazobenzene**.
