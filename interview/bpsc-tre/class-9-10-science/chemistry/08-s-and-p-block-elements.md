This is the "descriptive inorganic" chapter: facts about elements and their everyday compounds. It covers NCERT class 10 (metals, non-metals, common salts), class 11 ("Hydrogen", "s-Block", "Some p-Block Elements") and class 12 ("p-Block Elements"). TRE 2.0 asked "PH₃ vs NH₃ basicity", "which compound does not give oxygen on heating" and "largest atom in group 2"; TRE 1.0 asked "substance used in whitewashing obtained by heating limestone".

> **In simple words:** In the s-block, everything gets bigger, more reactive and more basic as you go down. In the p-block, the first member of every group behaves oddly (small size, no d orbitals), and heavier members prefer a lower oxidation state (inert pair effect). Learn the group trends first, then the famous compounds.

## 1. Hydrogen and water

- Hydrogen (Cavendish, 1766; named by Lavoisier) — lightest element; resembles alkali metals (1 valence electron, forms H⁺) and halogens (needs one electron, forms H⁻, diatomic).
- **Isotopes:** protium ¹H (99.98%), deuterium ²H or D (0.0156%), tritium ³H or T (radioactive, β-emitter, t½ 12.3 years). Only isotopes with different names.
- **Heavy water D₂O** (Urey, 1932): moderator in nuclear reactors; m.p. 3.8 °C, b.p. 101.4 °C, max density at 11.6 °C.
- **Hydrides:** ionic/saline (NaH, CaH₂), covalent/molecular (CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, HF; electron-deficient B₂H₆, electron-precise CH₄, electron-rich NH₃), metallic/interstitial (PdHₓ — palladium absorbs huge volumes of H₂).
- Preparation: Zn + dilute H₂SO₄; electrolysis of water; water–gas shift CO + H₂O → CO₂ + H₂. **Water gas/syngas** = CO + H₂.
- Hydrogen economy and fuel cells; liquid H₂ used as rocket fuel.

### Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)

Non-planar "open book" structure (dihedral angle 111.5° in gas phase). Acts as oxidant and reductant; decomposes in light (stored in dark coloured plastic/wax-lined bottles with urea as stabiliser). Uses: bleaching (hair, silk, wool, pulp), antiseptic (perhydrol), rocket propellant, restoring old paintings (black PbS → white PbSO₄).

**Volume strength:** "10 volume" H₂O₂ means 1 L of solution gives 10 L of O₂ at STP. Volume strength = 11.2 × molarity.

**Worked numerical 1.** Molarity and % strength of 10-volume H₂O₂.
2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂: 68 g gives 22.4 L O₂. For 10 L: 68 × 10/22.4 = 30.4 g L⁻¹ → **≈ 3% (w/v)**, molarity = 30.4/34 = **0.89 M**.

### Hard water

| Hardness | Cause | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary | Bicarbonates of Ca and Mg | Boiling (Ca(HCO₃)₂ → CaCO₃↓ + H₂O + CO₂); **Clark's method** (adding calculated slaked lime) |
| Permanent | Chlorides and sulphates of Ca and Mg | Washing soda (Na₂CO₃); **Calgon** (sodium hexametaphosphate, Na₆P₆O₁₈); ion-exchange (permutit/zeolite, synthetic resins) |

Hard water does not lather with soap (forms scum: insoluble Ca/Mg stearates); detergents work in hard water. Hardness is expressed as ppm of CaCO₃.

**Worked numerical 2.** 1 L water contains 0.111 g CaCl₂. Hardness in ppm CaCO₃? Moles CaCl₂ = 0.111/111 = 0.001 = moles CaCO₃ equivalent = 0.1 g in 1000 g water → **100 ppm**.

## 2. Group 1 — alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr)

General configuration ns¹; always +1. Soft (cut with knife), low m.p., low density (Li, Na, K float on water; Li is the lightest metal, density 0.53 g cm⁻³), very reactive — stored under kerosene (Li in paraffin wax because it floats on kerosene).

| Property | Trend down the group | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | Increases | — |
| Ionisation enthalpy | Decreases | — |
| Melting point | Decreases (Li 181 °C → Cs 28.4 °C) | — |
| Density | Increases | **K is lighter than Na** |
| Reducing power in water | — | **Li strongest** (E° −3.05 V; high hydration energy) |
| Hydration of ion | Decreases (Li⁺ most hydrated) | Li⁺(aq) is the largest hydrated ion, lowest ionic mobility |
| Reactivity with water | Increases (Li slow, Na vigorous, K catches fire) | — |

**Flame colours** (electrons excited by heat emit visible light):

| Li | Na | K | Rb | Cs | Ca | Sr | Ba | Cu |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crimson red | Golden yellow | Lilac (violet) | Red-violet | Blue | Brick red | Crimson | Apple green | Bluish green |

Be and Mg give **no flame colour** (electrons too tightly held). Cs and K are used in photoelectric cells.

**Oxides with excess oxygen:** Li → **Li₂O** (normal oxide), Na → **Na₂O₂** (peroxide), K, Rb, Cs → **KO₂** etc. (superoxides; paramagnetic, coloured). KO₂ is used in submarines and space suits to absorb CO₂ and release O₂.

**Solutions in liquid ammonia** are deep blue (ammoniated electrons), conducting and paramagnetic; concentrated solutions turn bronze and diamagnetic.

**Anomalous lithium** (diagonal relationship with Mg): forms Li₃N with N₂; Li₂CO₃ and LiNO₃ decompose on heating (LiNO₃ → Li₂O + NO₂ + O₂, unlike NaNO₃ → NaNO₂ + O₂); LiCl is deliquescent and covalent-ish; Li does not form solid bicarbonate.

### Important sodium compounds

| Compound | Formula | Making / facts | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common salt | NaCl | From sea water; pure NaCl by passing HCl gas (common ion) | Food, raw material for NaOH, Na₂CO₃, Cl₂ |
| Caustic soda | NaOH | **Castner–Kellner cell** (electrolysis of brine, Hg cathode); chlor-alkali process | Soap, paper, rayon, refining petroleum |
| Washing soda | Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | **Solvay (ammonia–soda) process** (NaCl + NH₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → NaHCO₃ → heat → Na₂CO₃); efflorescent; anhydrous form = soda ash | Removing permanent hardness, glass, cleaning |
| Baking soda | NaHCO₃ | Intermediate of Solvay; 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ on heating | Baking powder (+ tartaric acid), antacid, soda-acid fire extinguisher |
| Glauber's salt | Na₂SO₄·10H₂O | — | Laxative |
| Borax | Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O | Borax bead test | Glass, flux |

The Solvay process cannot make K₂CO₃ because KHCO₃ is too soluble to precipitate.

**Sodium carbonate with copper sulphate:** 2CuSO₄ + 2Na₂CO₃ + H₂O → **CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂↓ (basic copper carbonate, green)** + 2Na₂SO₄ + CO₂↑. Normal CuCO₃ does not precipitate because Na₂CO₃ solution is alkaline (hydrolysis gives OH⁻).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked what is obtained when Na₂CO₃ is added to copper sulphate solution: (A) basic copper carbonate, sodium sulphate and CO₂ (B) copper hydroxide … (C) copper carbonate …. Correct: **(A) basic copper carbonate, sodium sulphate and CO₂**. (No official key in our folder.)

## 3. Group 2 — alkaline earth metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra)

General configuration ns²; +2 state. Harder, denser and higher-melting than group 1; less reactive. Radium is radioactive (discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie, 1898).

| Property | Trend down the group |
|---|---|
| Atomic and ionic radius | Increases: **Be < Mg < Ca < Sr < Ba < Ra** |
| Ionisation enthalpy | Decreases |
| Solubility of **hydroxides** in water | **Increases**: Be(OH)₂ < Mg(OH)₂ < … < Ba(OH)₂ |
| Basic strength of hydroxides | Increases (Be(OH)₂ amphoteric) |
| Solubility of **sulphates** | **Decreases**: BeSO₄, MgSO₄ soluble; CaSO₄ sparingly; SrSO₄, BaSO₄ insoluble |
| Thermal stability of carbonates | **Increases**: BeCO₃ (least stable) < MgCO₃ < CaCO₃ < SrCO₃ < BaCO₃ |
| Solubility of fluorides | BeF₂ very soluble (high hydration), others sparingly soluble |

**Why sulphate solubility falls:** lattice energy of sulphates stays almost constant (large SO₄²⁻) but hydration energy falls as the cation grows. **Why hydroxide solubility rises:** lattice energy falls faster than hydration energy.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked the largest atom among Mg, Be and Ra. Official answer: **Ra** (radius increases down group 2). See [chapter 3](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/chemistry/periodic-table-and-periodicity).

**Anomalous beryllium** (diagonal with Al): covalent compounds; amphoteric BeO and Be(OH)₂; BeCl₂ is a covalent polymer (chain) in solid state and dimer in vapour; Be does not react with water; maximum covalency 4.

### Important calcium compounds

| Compound | Formula | Preparation / reactions | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limestone, marble, chalk | CaCO₃ | — | Cement, lime |
| **Quicklime** | **CaO** | **CaCO₃ —heat (≈1070–1270 K)→ CaO + CO₂** in a lime kiln | Cement, glass, drying agent, steel making; with water gives slaked lime |
| Slaked lime | Ca(OH)₂ | CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (hissing, very exothermic) | **Whitewashing**, mortar, bleaching powder; clear solution = lime water |
| Whitewash chemistry | — | Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (shiny white coat forms in 2–3 days) | — |
| Bleaching powder | CaOCl₂ (Ca(OCl)Cl) | Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂ → CaOCl₂ + H₂O | Bleaching cotton/linen, disinfecting water, oxidising agent; ~35% available chlorine |
| Plaster of Paris | CaSO₄·½H₂O | Gypsum heated at 373 K (100 °C) loses 1½ H₂O; above 393 K gives dead-burnt plaster (anhydrous) | Casts for fractures, statues, toys; sets by re-forming gypsum |
| Gypsum | CaSO₄·2H₂O | — | Added to cement (2–3%) to slow setting; fertiliser |
| Cement | Portland cement: CaO ~50–60%, SiO₂ 20–25%, Al₂O₃ 5–10% | Joseph Aspdin (1824) | Construction |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "A substance X is used in whitewashing and is obtained by heating limestone in the absence of air. Identify X. (A) CaOCl₂ (B) Ca(OH)₂ (C) CaO". Correct: **(C) CaO** (quicklime). Strictly, CaO is first slaked to Ca(OH)₂ before whitewashing, but only CaO comes directly from heating limestone. (No official key in our folder.)

- **Lime water test** for CO₂: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (milky); excess CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂ (soluble, milkiness disappears).
- Mg is the central atom of **chlorophyll**; Ca is in bones and teeth (hydroxyapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂); fluorapatite in enamel.
- Milk of magnesia = Mg(OH)₂ suspension (antacid); Epsom salt = MgSO₄·7H₂O.

## 4. Group 13 — boron family (B, Al, Ga, In, Tl)

- ns² np¹; +3 state, but **+1 becomes more stable down the group (inert pair effect)**: Tl⁺ more stable than Tl³⁺.
- Boron is a non-metal (metalloid); others are metals. Ga has very low m.p. (29.8 °C) but high b.p. (~2400 °C) — used in high-temperature thermometers.
- **Atomic radius anomaly: Ga < Al** (poor shielding by 3d).
- **Boric acid** H₃BO₃ (orthoboric acid): weak **monobasic Lewis acid** — it accepts OH⁻ rather than giving H⁺: B(OH)₃ + 2H₂O → [B(OH)₄]⁻ + H₃O⁺. Layer structure with H-bonds; mild antiseptic (eye wash).
- **Borax** Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O: on heating forms a glassy bead (NaBO₂ + B₂O₃) — **borax bead test** gives coloured metaborates (Co blue, Cu blue-green, Ni brown, Cr green, Mn violet).
- **Diborane B₂H₆:** electron-deficient; two **three-centre two-electron (3c–2e, "banana") B–H–B bonds**; four terminal B–H bonds. Burns in air with huge energy. Each B is sp³.

- **Boron trihalides** are Lewis acids; acid strength **BF₃ < BCl₃ < BBr₃ < BI₃** (back-bonding pπ–pπ strongest in BF₃ reduces its electron deficiency).
- **Aluminium:** amphoteric (dissolves in acids and alkalis giving H₂); passive in concentrated HNO₃ (oxide layer); extracted by electrolysis of alumina in molten cryolite (Hall–Héroult). **Thermite reaction:** 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe (welding rails). AlCl₃ exists as dimer Al₂Cl₆; anhydrous AlCl₃ is a Friedel–Crafts catalyst.
- **Alums** M₂SO₄·M′₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O (potash alum K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O): water purification, styptic (stops bleeding), dyeing (mordant).
- Corundum Al₂O₃; ruby (Cr³⁺ in Al₂O₃), sapphire (Fe/Ti); bauxite Al₂O₃·2H₂O.

## 5. Group 14 — carbon family (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb)

- ns² np²; +4 and +2; stability of +2 increases down (Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺, so PbO₂ is a strong oxidant; Sn²⁺ is a reducing agent because Sn⁴⁺ is stable).
- **Catenation** (self-linking): **C ≫ Si > Ge ≈ Sn** (Pb negligible), because C–C bond energy is high (348 kJ mol⁻¹).
- Carbon forms strong pπ–pπ multiple bonds; Si does not (so CO₂ is a gas but SiO₂ is a giant covalent solid).

| Allotrope of carbon | Structure | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond | sp³, 3-D tetrahedral network; C–C 154 pm | Hardest natural substance, insulator, high refractive index (2.42), good heat conductor |
| Graphite | sp², hexagonal layers held by van der Waals forces; C–C 141.5 pm, layer gap 340 pm | Soft, slippery (lubricant), conducts electricity (delocalised π electrons), thermodynamically most stable form |
| **Fullerene C₆₀** (Buckminsterfullerene) | sp², soccer-ball cage: **12 pentagons + 20 hexagons**, 60 carbons | Discovered 1985 by Kroto, Curl and Smalley (Nobel 1996); only pure form of carbon (no dangling bonds) |
| Graphene | Single layer of graphite | Strongest known material, excellent conductor (Geim and Novoselov, Nobel 2010) |
| Carbon nanotubes | Rolled graphene | Very strong, used in electronics |

Amorphous forms: coal, charcoal, coke, lamp black (carbon black), activated charcoal.

- **CO:** colourless, odourless, very poisonous — binds haemoglobin about 200–300 times more strongly than O₂ (carboxyhaemoglobin). Neutral oxide. Good reducing agent in metallurgy.
- **CO₂:** acidic oxide; solid CO₂ = dry ice (refrigerant); greenhouse gas; fire extinguishers; photosynthesis.
- **Silicon:** second most abundant element in Earth's crust (O 46.6% > Si 27.7% > Al 8.1% > Fe 5%). SiO₂ (quartz, sand) is a 3-D network — piezoelectric quartz. **Silicones** (R₂SiO)ₙ: water-repellent, heat-resistant polymers (from hydrolysis of R₂SiCl₂). **Silicates:** basic unit SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedron. **Zeolites** (aluminosilicates): water softening (permutit), shape-selective catalyst ZSM-5 (alcohol → petrol). **SiC (carborundum)** is very hard abrasive.
- **Tin:** white tin (β) changes to grey tin (α, powder) below 13.2 °C — **tin plague**. **Lead** compounds are poisonous; red lead Pb₃O₄ (2PbO·PbO₂).

## 6. Group 15 — nitrogen family (N, P, As, Sb, Bi)

- ns² np³; oxidation states −3 to +5; +3 more stable than +5 for Bi (inert pair; NaBiO₃ is a strong oxidant).
- N₂: very inert (N≡N, 946 kJ mol⁻¹); 78% of air. N cannot form pentahalides (no d orbitals) — NCl₅ does not exist but PCl₅ does.
- **Hydrides (EH₃) trends**

| Property | Order |
|---|---|
| **Basic strength** (lone-pair availability) | **NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃ > SbH₃ > BiH₃** |
| Thermal stability | NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃ > SbH₃ > BiH₃ |
| Reducing power | **BiH₃ > SbH₃ > AsH₃ > PH₃ > NH₃** |
| Bond angle | NH₃ 107.8° > PH₃ 93.6° > AsH₃ 91.8° > SbH₃ 91.3° |
| Boiling point | **BiH₃ > SbH₃ > NH₃ > AsH₃ > PH₃** (NH₃ high due to H-bonding) |
| Melting point | NH₃ highest |

**Why PH₃ is less basic than NH₃:** the lone pair on P is in a larger, more diffuse orbital (almost pure s-character, bond angle ~93°), so it is less available to accept H⁺. PH₄⁺ forms only with strong acids (PH₄I). PH₃ is not amphoteric.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following statements is true? (A) PH₃ is less alkaline as compared to NH₃ (B) PH₃ is more alkaline … (C) PH₃ is amphoteric while NH₃ is alkaline". Official answer: **(A)**.

- **Ammonia (Haber process):** N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, Fe catalyst + Mo promoter, ~200 atm, ~700 K. Detected by Nessler's reagent (brown ppt) and by white fumes with HCl. Forms deep-blue [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ with Cu²⁺.
- **Nitric acid (Ostwald process):** 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt–Rh gauze, 500 K, 9 bar) → NO₂ → HNO₃. Conc. HNO₃ is ~68%; turns yellow on standing (NO₂). Passivates Fe, Cr, Al. Aqua regia = 3 HCl : 1 HNO₃ (dissolves Au, Pt). **Brown ring test** for nitrate: [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺.
- **Oxides of nitrogen:** N₂O (laughing gas, anaesthetic, neutral), NO (neutral, paramagnetic), N₂O₃ (blue solid, acidic), NO₂ (brown, paramagnetic, dimerises to colourless N₂O₄), N₂O₅ (acidic, anhydride of HNO₃).
- **Phosphorus allotropes:** **white P (P₄)** — tetrahedral, strained (60° angles), very reactive, glows in dark (chemiluminescence), ignites at ~30 °C so **stored under water**, poisonous, soluble in CS₂. **Red P** — polymeric, less reactive, used in matchbox strikers (safety matches), not poisonous. **Black P** — layered, most stable, conducts.
- PCl₃ (pyramidal) and PCl₅ (trigonal bipyramidal in gas; ionic [PCl₄]⁺[PCl₆]⁻ in solid). Phosphoric acid H₃PO₄ (tribasic), H₃PO₃ (dibasic, reducing), H₃PO₂ (monobasic, strong reducing) — basicity = number of P–OH groups.

## 7. Group 16 — chalcogens (O, S, Se, Te, Po)

- ns² np⁴; O mostly −2; S shows −2, +4, +6.
- **Oxygen:** most abundant element in Earth's crust; paramagnetic. **Ozone O₃:** bent, pale blue gas, strong oxidant (E° +2.07 V), ozone layer in stratosphere (15–35 km) absorbs UV-B and UV-C; destroyed by CFCs (Cl· radicals) and NO; measured in **Dobson units**. Montreal Protocol (1987).
- **Hydrides:** acidity H₂O < H₂S < H₂Se < H₂Te; thermal stability and bond angle decrease down; **b.p. order H₂O (100 °C) > H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S** (H₂O high due to H-bonds). H₂S smells of rotten eggs; used in group analysis.
- **Sulphur allotropes:** rhombic (α, yellow, stable below 369 K) and monoclinic (β, stable above 369 K = **transition temperature 95.6 °C**); both S₈ crown rings. Plastic sulphur by quick cooling of molten S.
- **SO₂:** acidic, bleaches by **reduction** (temporary; colour returns in air), food preservative (fruit juices), turns acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ green.
- **Sulphuric acid — "king of chemicals" — contact process:** S → SO₂ → 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (V₂O₅, ~720 K, 2 bar) → SO₃ absorbed in conc. H₂SO₄ to give **oleum H₂S₂O₇** → diluted to H₂SO₄. (SO₃ is not dissolved directly in water — it forms a mist.) Conc. H₂SO₄ is a strong dehydrating agent (chars sugar to carbon), oxidiser and gives HCl from NaCl.

## 8. Group 17 — halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At)

- ns² np⁵; −1 always for F; Cl, Br, I also +1, +3, +5, +7 (with O or F).
- F₂ pale yellow gas, Cl₂ greenish-yellow gas, Br₂ red-brown liquid, I₂ violet-black solid (sublimes, violet vapour).
- **Oxidising power: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂** — so Cl₂ displaces Br₂ and I₂ from bromides and iodides; F₂ oxidises water to O₂.
- Electron gain enthalpy: **Cl > F > Br > I**; electronegativity: F > Cl > Br > I; bond energy Cl₂ > Br₂ > F₂ > I₂.
- **Hydrogen halides:** acid strength HI > HBr > HCl > HF; thermal stability HF > HCl > HBr > HI; b.p. HF > HI > HBr > HCl. **HF etches glass** (stored in wax or plastic bottles): SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O.
- **Chlorine:** discovered by Scheele (1774); prepared by MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + Cl₂ + 2H₂O or by electrolysis of brine. Bleaches by **oxidation** (permanent), needs moisture: Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HOCl → HCl + [O]. Used in water purification, PVC, bleaching powder; poisonous gas (World War I).
- **Interhalogens** XX′ₙ: ClF, BrF₃ (T-shaped), IF₅ (square pyramidal), IF₇ (pentagonal bipyramidal); more reactive than halogens (except F₂).
- Iodine: iodised salt (KIO₃) prevents goitre; tincture of iodine is an antiseptic; starch–iodine blue colour.
- **Oxyacids of Cl:** acid strength HClO₄ > HClO₃ > HClO₂ > HClO; oxidising power HClO > HClO₂ > HClO₃ > HClO₄.

## 9. Group 18 — noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn)

- ns² np⁶ (He 1s²). Monatomic, very low reactivity; positive electron gain enthalpy, highest IE in each period.
- Discovery: argon by **Rayleigh and Ramsay (1894)**; helium first seen in the Sun's spectrum (Janssen and Lockyer, 1868); Ne, Kr, Xe by Ramsay and Travers (1898); radon from radium decay.
- **Uses:** He — weather balloons and airships (light, non-flammable), diving gas mixtures, cryogenics (liquid He, 4.2 K), MRI magnets; Ne — advertisement signs (orange-red glow), beacon lights; **Ar — filling electric bulbs, inert atmosphere for welding** (most abundant noble gas in air, ~0.93%); Kr and Xe — high-intensity lamps, photographic flash; Rn — radioactive, radiotherapy of cancer, earthquake prediction studies.
- **Xenon compounds (Bartlett, 1962):** first noble-gas compound was Xe⁺[PtF₆]⁻ (because IE of Xe ≈ IE of O₂). Shapes: **XeF₂ linear, XeF₄ square planar, XeF₆ distorted octahedral, XeO₃ pyramidal, XeOF₄ square pyramidal, XeO₄ tetrahedral** (see [chapter 4](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/chemistry/chemical-bonding-and-molecular-structure)).

## 10. What gives oxygen on heating?

| Substance | Decomposition on heating | O₂? |
|---|---|---|
| KClO₃ | 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ (MnO₂ catalyst, lab method) | Yes |
| Zn(ClO₃)₂ (any metal chlorate) | Zn(ClO₃)₂ → ZnCl₂ + 3O₂ | Yes |
| KMnO₄ | 2KMnO₄ → K₂MnO₄ + MnO₂ + O₂ | Yes |
| K₂Cr₂O₇ (strong heating) | 4K₂Cr₂O₇ → 4K₂CrO₄ + 2Cr₂O₃ + 3O₂ | Yes |
| **(NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇** | **(NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ → N₂ + Cr₂O₃ + 4H₂O** ("volcano" — green Cr₂O₃) | **No** (gives N₂) |
| NH₄NO₂ | NH₄NO₂ → N₂ + 2H₂O | No (lab N₂) |
| NH₄NO₃ | NH₄NO₃ → N₂O + 2H₂O | No (gives N₂O) |
| KNO₃, NaNO₃ | 2KNO₃ → 2KNO₂ + O₂ | Yes |
| Pb(NO₃)₂ | 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂ (brown fumes) | Yes |
| HgO, Ag₂O | 2HgO → 2Hg + O₂ (Priestley, 1774) | Yes |
| H₂O₂, BaO₂ | 2BaO₂ → 2BaO + O₂ | Yes |
| CaCO₃ | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ | No (gives CO₂) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following does not give oxygen on heating? (A) Zn(ClO₃)₂ (B) K₂Cr₂O₇ (C) (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇". Official answer: **(C) (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇** — the ammonium ion reduces dichromate internally, so N₂ is released instead of O₂.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Li is the strongest reducing agent in water** but the least reactive with water among alkali metals (kinetics, high m.p.).
- **K is less dense than Na.** Cs, not Fr, is the practical "most electropositive" stable metal.
- Li forms normal oxide, Na peroxide, K superoxide.
- Be and Mg give **no flame colour**.
- Down group 2: hydroxide solubility **increases**, sulphate solubility **decreases**, carbonate stability **increases**.
- **Boric acid is a Lewis acid, monobasic**, not tribasic.
- BF₃ is the **weakest** Lewis acid among boron halides (back-bonding).
- **Graphite (not diamond) is the most stable allotrope** of carbon and conducts electricity.
- **PH₃ is less basic than NH₃**, and BiH₃ is the strongest reducing hydride.
- B.p.: SbH₃ > NH₃ > AsH₃ > PH₃; H₂O > H₂Te > H₂Se > H₂S; HF > HI > HBr > HCl.
- SO₂ bleaches by reduction (temporary); Cl₂ by oxidation (permanent).
- **(NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ gives N₂, not O₂**, on heating; NH₄NO₃ gives N₂O.
- White phosphorus is stored under **water**; sodium under **kerosene**.
- Ga is liquid just above room temperature (29.8 °C) but is not the "liquid metal" — that is mercury.
- XeF₂ is linear, XeF₄ is square planar.
- H₃PO₃ is dibasic; H₃PO₂ is monobasic.

## Quick revision

1. Heavy water D₂O — moderator; discovered by Urey (1932).
2. Tritium is radioactive (t½ 12.3 years).
3. 10-volume H₂O₂ ≈ 3% (0.89 M).
4. Temporary hardness: bicarbonates (boiling, Clark's method); permanent: chlorides/sulphates (washing soda, Calgon, ion exchange).
5. Flame: Na golden yellow, K lilac, Ca brick red, Sr crimson, Ba apple green.
6. Li₂O, Na₂O₂, KO₂ — normal, peroxide, superoxide.
7. NaOH — Castner–Kellner; Na₂CO₃ — Solvay process.
8. Baking soda NaHCO₃; washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
9. CaCO₃ → CaO (quicklime) + CO₂; CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (slaked lime, whitewash).
10. Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O from gypsum at 373 K.
11. Bleaching powder CaOCl₂.
12. Group 2 sulphate solubility decreases; hydroxide solubility increases.
13. Diborane has two 3c–2e B–H–B bonds.
14. Boric acid — weak monobasic Lewis acid.
15. Thermite: Al + Fe₂O₃ (welding).
16. C₆₀: 12 pentagons, 20 hexagons (1985).
17. CO binds haemoglobin ~200–300× more than O₂.
18. Silicones (R₂SiO)ₙ; zeolite ZSM-5.
19. NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃ basicity.
20. White P stored under water; red P in matchboxes.

22. Rhombic ⇌ monoclinic sulphur at 369 K.
23. HF etches glass; oxidising power F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂.
24. Argon fills bulbs; helium in balloons and diving.
25. (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇ on heating gives N₂, not O₂.

## Practice with answers

1. Which alkali metal forms a nitride directly? — **Lithium** (Li₃N).
2. Colour imparted to flame by potassium? — **Lilac (violet)**.
3. Chemical name of washing soda? — **Sodium carbonate decahydrate**.

5. Formula of plaster of Paris? — **CaSO₄·½H₂O**.
6. Most soluble sulphate of group 2? — **BeSO₄**.
7. Most thermally stable carbonate of group 2? — **BaCO₃**.
8. Hybridisation of B in diborane? — **sp³**.
9. Why is boric acid a weak acid? — **It accepts OH⁻ (Lewis acid) rather than releasing H⁺**.
10. Number of hexagons in C₆₀? — **20** (with 12 pentagons).
11. Why does graphite conduct? — **Delocalised π electrons in each layer**.
12. More basic: NH₃ or PH₃? — **NH₃** (official TRE 2.0 answer).
13. Laughing gas? — **N₂O**.
14. Catalyst in contact process? — **V₂O₅**.
15. Which halogen is a liquid? — **Bromine**.
16. Which acid is stored in wax bottles? — **HF** (attacks glass).
17. Gas used in electric bulbs? — **Argon** (sometimes with N₂).
18. First noble gas compound? — **Xe⁺[PtF₆]⁻** (Bartlett, 1962).
19. Which gives no O₂ on heating: KClO₃ or (NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇? — **(NH₄)₂Cr₂O₇** (gives N₂).
20. Precipitate when Na₂CO₃ is added to CuSO₄ solution? — **Basic copper carbonate CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂**.
