This chapter is the single biggest B.Sc.-level scoring area in TRE chemistry. It covers NCERT class 12 ("The d- and f-Block Elements", "Coordination Compounds") and class 10–12 metallurgy, plus B.Sc. points such as EAN rule and colours of analytical precipitates. TRE 2.0 asked six questions from here (oxidation number of Fe in nitroprusside and of Cr in [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺, coordination number of Co in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl, optical isomerism, which complex is not yellow, vitamin with cobalt); TRE 1.0 asked the IUPAC name of sodium nitroprusside and the Mo–W radius pair.

> **In simple words:** A coordination compound is a central metal ion holding a set of molecules or ions (ligands) by lone-pair donation. Count the donor atoms to get the coordination number; add up charges to get the oxidation state; and look at the d-electrons to predict colour and magnetism.

## 1. The d-block (transition elements)

**Transition element:** an element whose atom or common ion has an **incompletely filled d sub-shell**. Groups 3–12; four series: 3d (Sc–Zn), 4d (Y–Cd), 5d (La, Hf–Hg), 6d (Ac, Rf–Cn). General configuration (n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns⁰⁻².

**Zn, Cd, Hg** (d¹⁰ in atom and ions) are **not true transition metals** — they are colourless, diamagnetic, soft, low melting. Sc forms only Sc³⁺ (d⁰), so its compounds are colourless too. **Cu** counts as transition because Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹.

| Property | Explanation / facts |
|---|---|
| Metallic, hard, high m.p. | Strong metallic bonding using both ns and (n−1)d electrons. **W has the highest m.p. of all metals (3422 °C)**; Hg is liquid (−38.8 °C). Highest m.p. in 3d series near the middle (V, Cr). |
| Variable oxidation states | ns and (n−1)d energies are close. Mn shows +2 to +7 (most in 3d series). Highest: **Os and Ru +8** (OsO₄). Sc only +3, Zn only +2. |
| Coloured ions | **d–d transitions** absorb part of visible light; we see the complementary colour. d⁰ and d¹⁰ ions are colourless. |
| Paramagnetism | Unpaired d electrons; μ = √(n(n+2)) BM. |
| Catalytic activity | Variable valency and surface adsorption: Fe (Haber), V₂O₅ (contact), Ni (hydrogenation), Pt (Ostwald), Pd (Lindlar), TiCl₄ (Ziegler–Natta). |
| Complex formation | Small, highly charged ions with vacant d orbitals. |
| Interstitial compounds | Small atoms (H, C, N, B) trapped in metal lattice: TiC, Fe₃C (cementite in steel), VH₀.₅₆ — hard, high m.p., non-stoichiometric. |
| Alloys | Similar atomic sizes: brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni), nichrome (Ni + Cr), German silver (Cu + Zn + Ni), solder (Pb + Sn), gun metal (Cu + Sn + Zn). |

**Trends in the 3d series**
- Atomic radius falls from Sc to Cr, stays almost constant from Cr to Cu (extra d electrons shield the outer 4s), and rises slightly at Zn.
- E°(M²⁺/M) mostly negative; **Cu is the only 3d metal with positive E° (+0.34 V)** — does not liberate H₂ from dilute acids.
- Stability: Mn²⁺ (3d⁵) and Fe³⁺ (3d⁵) are extra stable (half-filled); hence Mn³⁺ and Fe²⁺ are easily changed (Mn³⁺ is oxidising, Fe²⁺ reducing). Cr²⁺ is a strong reducing agent (→ Cr³⁺, t₂g³). Cu⁺ disproportionates in water (2Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu) because Cu²⁺ has much higher hydration energy.

**Colours of common aqueous ions**

| Ion | d-electrons | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺ | d⁰ | Colourless |
| Ti³⁺ | d¹ | Purple |
| V³⁺ | d² | Green |
| VO²⁺ | d¹ | Blue |
| Cr³⁺ | d³ | Violet (hexaaqua) / green |
| Mn²⁺ | d⁵ | Very pale pink |
| Fe²⁺ | d⁶ | Pale green |
| Fe³⁺ | d⁵ | Yellow (brown in many salts) |
| Co²⁺ | d⁷ | Pink |
| Ni²⁺ | d⁸ | Green |
| Cu²⁺ | d⁹ | Blue |
| Cu⁺, Zn²⁺ | d¹⁰ | Colourless |

Vitriols: blue vitriol CuSO₄·5H₂O, green vitriol FeSO₄·7H₂O, white vitriol ZnSO₄·7H₂O. Mohr's salt FeSO₄·(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O (stable source of Fe²⁺ in titrations).

### Potassium dichromate and potassium permanganate

**K₂Cr₂O₇** (orange): made from chromite ore FeCr₂O₄ → fused with Na₂CO₃ in air → Na₂CrO₄ (yellow) → acidified → Na₂Cr₂O₇ → + KCl → K₂Cr₂O₇.
- **Chromate–dichromate equilibrium depends on pH:** 2CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O. Acid → orange; alkali → yellow. Cr is +6 in both.
- Strong oxidant in acid (Cr₂O₇²⁻ → 2Cr³⁺, green); used as a primary standard; breath analyser (ethanol turns orange dichromate green); **chromyl chloride test** for Cl⁻ (red CrO₂Cl₂ vapours).
- Structure: two tetrahedra sharing one O (Cr–O–Cr bridge).

**KMnO₄** (deep purple): pyrolusite MnO₂ fused with KOH in air → K₂MnO₄ (green) → disproportionation or electrolytic oxidation → KMnO₄.
- In acid: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O (n = 5). Oxidises Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺, oxalate → CO₂, I⁻ → I₂, H₂S → S, SO₂ → SO₄²⁻.
- In neutral/weakly alkaline: MnO₄⁻ → MnO₂ (n = 3) — **Baeyer's reagent** (cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄) tests unsaturation: pink colour disappears with alkenes.
- Uses: disinfectant (red potash for wells), titrations, oxidising agent in organic chemistry.
- **Colours of KMnO₄ and K₂Cr₂O₇ are due to charge transfer (ligand-to-metal), not d–d transitions** — Mn(+7) and Cr(+6) are d⁰.

## 2. The f-block

**Lanthanoids** (Ce 58 – Lu 71; La 57 is the prototype): 4f filling; common state **+3**; Ce⁴⁺ (4f⁰, strong oxidant), Eu²⁺ and Yb²⁺ (4f⁷, 4f¹⁴, reducing) also known. Mostly paramagnetic and coloured (f–f transitions), except La³⁺, Lu³⁺.
- **Lanthanoid contraction:** steady decrease in atomic and ionic radius from La to Lu, because 4f electrons shield very poorly. Consequences: (1) **Zr ≈ Hf, Nb ≈ Ta, Mo ≈ W** in size — very similar chemistry, hard to separate; (2) basicity of Ln(OH)₃ decreases from La(OH)₃ to Lu(OH)₃; (3) separation of lanthanoids by ion exchange is possible; (4) 5d elements have higher IE and density than 4d ones.
- **Misch metal** = ~95% lanthanoids + ~5% Fe + traces of S, C, Ca, Al — lighter flints, Mg alloys for bullets.

**Actinoids** (Th 90 – Lr 103; Ac 89 prototype): 5f filling; all radioactive; elements after U (Z > 92) are synthetic (**transuranic**). Show many oxidation states (U, Np, Pu up to +6/+7) because 5f, 6d, 7s are close in energy. **Actinoid contraction** is greater than lanthanoid contraction (5f shields even more poorly). Uses: U and Pu as nuclear fuels, Th in gas mantles.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked which pair has similar atomic radii: Mn and Re, Mo and W, or Sc and Ni. Correct: **Mo and W** (lanthanoid contraction).

## 3. Basic metallurgy (class 10–12 essentials)

| Metal | Main ores |
|---|---|
| Aluminium | **Bauxite Al₂O₃·2H₂O**, cryolite Na₃AlF₆, corundum |
| Iron | **Haematite Fe₂O₃**, magnetite Fe₃O₄, limonite, siderite FeCO₃, iron pyrites FeS₂ (fool's gold) |
| Copper | **Copper pyrites CuFeS₂**, cuprite Cu₂O, malachite CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂, copper glance Cu₂S |
| Zinc | **Zinc blende ZnS**, calamine ZnCO₃, zincite ZnO |
| Lead | **Galena PbS** |
| Mercury | **Cinnabar HgS** |
| Sodium | Rock salt NaCl, Chile saltpetre NaNO₃ |
| Calcium / Mg | Limestone, dolomite CaCO₃·MgCO₃, gypsum; carnallite KCl·MgCl₂·6H₂O, magnesite MgCO₃ |
| Tin | Cassiterite (tinstone) SnO₂ |

**Steps:** concentration (gravity separation, magnetic separation, **froth flotation for sulphide ores** with pine oil, leaching — bauxite in NaOH by **Baeyer's process**, Ag/Au by NaCN — **MacArthur–Forrest cyanide process**) → **calcination** (heating in absence/limited air; carbonates and hydrated ores: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂) → **roasting** (heating in excess air; sulphide ores: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂) → reduction (by C/CO in blast furnace for Fe and Zn; by electrolysis for Na, Mg, Al; self-reduction for Cu and Hg; Al in thermite) → refining.

| Refining method | Used for |
|---|---|
| Liquation | Sn, Pb, Bi (low m.p.) |
| Distillation | Zn, Hg, Cd |
| Electrolytic refining | Cu, Zn, Ag, Au, Al (impure metal anode, pure metal cathode; anode mud has Ag, Au, Pt) |
| **Zone refining** | **Si, Ge, Ga, In** for semiconductors (impurities stay in the melt) |
| **Mond process** | **Ni** via volatile Ni(CO)₄ (formed at 330–350 K, decomposed at 450–470 K) |
| **van Arkel–de Boer** | **Ti, Zr** via volatile TiI₄/ZrI₄ decomposed on hot tungsten filament |
| Chromatography | Elements in minute amounts |

**Blast furnace (iron):** coke, limestone and haematite; CO reduces Fe₂O₃; CaCO₃ → CaO, which removes SiO₂ as **slag CaSiO₃**. Pig iron ~4% C; cast iron ~3% C; wrought iron (purest commercial iron, <0.2% C); steel 0.1–1.5% C. **Ellingham diagram** (ΔG° vs T) shows which reducing agent works at which temperature.

## 4. Coordination compounds — Werner's theory

**Double salt vs complex:** a double salt (Mohr's salt, potash alum, carnallite) dissociates completely into simple ions in water; a complex keeps its complex ion intact ([Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ does not give Fe²⁺ test).

**Werner's theory (Alfred Werner, 1893; Nobel 1913):**
- Metal shows two valencies: **primary** (ionisable, = oxidation state, satisfied by anions, shown outside the square bracket) and **secondary** (non-ionisable, = coordination number, satisfied by ligands, directional → gives geometry).
- Evidence: precipitation with AgNO₃ and conductivity.

| Formula (Werner) | Complex | Cl⁻ precipitated by AgNO₃ per formula | Ions in solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| CoCl₃·6NH₃ (yellow) | [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ | 3 | 4 |
| CoCl₃·5NH₃ (purple) | [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ | 2 | 3 |
| CoCl₃·4NH₃ (green / violet) | [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl | 1 | 2 |
| CoCl₃·3NH₃ | [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃] | 0 | 0 (non-electrolyte) |

**Terms**
- **Ligand:** ion or molecule that donates a lone pair to the metal (Lewis base). Central metal = Lewis acid.
- **Denticity:** number of donor atoms in a ligand.

| Type | Ligands |
|---|---|
| Monodentate | H₂O, NH₃, CO, Cl⁻, CN⁻, OH⁻, NO₂⁻, py (pyridine), PPh₃ |
| Bidentate | **en (ethane-1,2-diamine, H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)**, ox²⁻ (oxalate C₂O₄²⁻), gly⁻ (glycinate), bipy, phen, acac⁻ |
| Tridentate | dien (diethylenetriamine) |
| Hexadentate | **EDTA⁴⁻** (2 N + 4 O) |
| Ambidentate (can bind through two different atoms) | **NO₂⁻** (nitrito-N) / ONO⁻ (nitrito-O); **SCN⁻** (thiocyanato-S) / NCS⁻ (-N); CN⁻ / NC⁻; S₂O₃²⁻ |

- **Chelate:** ring formed when a polydentate ligand binds through two or more atoms; chelates are extra stable (**chelate effect**; entropy gain). EDTA forms very stable 1:1 chelates — used to estimate water hardness and to treat lead poisoning.
- **Coordination number (CN):** number of ligand **donor atoms** bonded to the metal (not the number of ligands). Common CNs: 2 (linear), 4 (tetrahedral or square planar), 6 (octahedral).
- **Coordination sphere:** the metal + ligands inside [ ]; counter ions outside.
- **Homoleptic** (one kind of ligand: [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺) vs **heteroleptic** ([Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺).

**Worked numerical 1 (CN and ON).** [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl.
CN: each en gives 2 N donors → 2 × 2 = 4; two Cl⁻ → 2. **CN = 6.**
ON: complex ion is [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ (one Cl⁻ outside). x + 0 + 2(−1) = +1 → **Co = +3**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The coordination number of Co in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl is (A) 4 (B) 5 (C) 6". Official answer: **(C) 6**. The trap is counting ligands (2 en + 2 Cl = 4) instead of donor atoms.

**Worked numerical 2.** CN and ON in K₃[Fe(C₂O₄)₃], [Ni(CO)₄], [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], K₄[Fe(CN)₆], [Fe(EDTA)]⁻.

| Complex | CN | ON of metal |
|---|---|---|
| K₃[Fe(C₂O₄)₃] | 6 (3 bidentate oxalates) | +3 (3 + x − 6 = 0) |
| [Ni(CO)₄] | 4 | 0 |
| [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | 4 | +2 |
| K₄[Fe(CN)₆] | 6 | +2 |
| K₃[Fe(CN)₆] | 6 | +3 |
| [Fe(EDTA)]⁻ | 6 | +3 (x − 4 = −1) |
| [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ | 6 | +3 |
| Na₂[Fe(CN)₅NO] | 6 | +2 (NO⁺) |
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | 2 | +1 |

## 5. IUPAC nomenclature of complexes

**Rules (IUPAC 2005, used by NCERT):**
1. Name the **cation first**, then the anion (as in simple salts), whether or not the complex is the cation.
2. Inside the complex: **ligands first, in alphabetical order**, then the metal. Prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- are ignored in alphabetising.
3. Multiplying prefixes: di, tri, tetra … for simple ligands; **bis, tris, tetrakis** for ligands whose names already contain a number or are complex (en, PPh₃): bis(ethane-1,2-diamine).
4. **Anionic ligands end in -o / -ido:** Cl⁻ chlorido (old: chloro), CN⁻ cyanido (cyano), OH⁻ hydroxido, O²⁻ oxido, NO₂⁻ nitrito-N (old: nitro), ONO⁻ nitrito-O, SO₄²⁻ sulfato, C₂O₄²⁻ oxalato, SCN⁻ thiocyanato-S.
5. **Neutral ligands keep their names, except:** H₂O aqua, NH₃ ammine (double m), CO carbonyl, **NO nitrosyl**.
6. Oxidation state of the metal in Roman numerals in parentheses: (III), (0).
7. If the complex ion is an **anion**, the metal name ends in **-ate**, using Latin names for some: Fe ferrate, Cu cuprate, Ag argentate, Au aurate, Pb plumbate, Sn stannate; but Co cobaltate, Ni nickelate, Cr chromate, Pt platinate, Zn zincate.

| Formula | IUPAC name |
|---|---|
| [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ | Hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride |
| [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ | Pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride |
| [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl | Dichloridobis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) chloride |
| [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ | Tetraamminedichloridochromium(III) ion |
| K₄[Fe(CN)₆] | Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II) (potassium ferrocyanide) |
| K₃[Fe(CN)₆] | Potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III) (potassium ferricyanide) |
| [Ni(CO)₄] | Tetracarbonylnickel(0) |
| [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | Diamminedichloridoplatinum(II) (cis form = cisplatin) |
| K₃[Co(NO₂)₆] | Potassium hexanitrito-N-cobaltate(III) (potassium cobaltinitrite) |
| [Ag(NH₃)₂]Cl | Diamminesilver(I) chloride |
| K₂[HgI₄] | Potassium tetraiodidomercurate(II) (Nessler's reagent base) |
| Na₃[Ag(S₂O₃)₂] | Sodium bis(thiosulfato)argentate(I) |
| [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄ | Tetraamminecopper(II) sulfate |
| Na₂[Fe(CN)₅NO] | Sodium pentacyanidonitrosylferrate(II) (sodium nitroprusside) |

**Sodium nitroprusside, Na₂[Fe(CN)₅NO]·2H₂O.** NO is present as NO⁺ (N–O stretching ~1940 cm⁻¹, short N–O bond), so Fe is +2 and the complex is diamagnetic (low-spin d⁶). Indian exam books therefore write **sodium pentacyanonitrosylferrate(II)** (2005 style: pentacyanido…). Some older books treat NO as a neutral ligand and write "…ferrate(III)"; "sodium nitroferrocyanide" is only a trade name. Use: test for sulphide ion (violet/purple colour with S²⁻) and medicine (vasodilator).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked the IUPAC name of sodium nitroprusside dihydrate: (A) sodium pentacyanonitrosylferrate(III) (B) sodium nitroferrocyanide (C) sodium pentacyanonitrosylferrate(II). The choice that agrees with BPSC's own official answer in TRE 2.0 (Fe = +2) is **(C)**. (TRE 1.0 has no official key in our folder; option (A) appears in some older books — see the note above.)

## 6. Isomerism in coordination compounds

### Structural isomerism

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ionisation | Exchange between a ligand and the counter ion | [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ (red-violet; gives BaSO₄ ppt) and [Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br (red; gives AgBr ppt) |
| Hydrate (solvate) | Water inside vs outside the sphere | [Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃ (violet), [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O (blue-green), [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]Cl·2H₂O (dark green) |
| Linkage | Ambidentate ligand binds through different atoms | [Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]Cl₂ (yellow, nitrito-N) and [Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]Cl₂ (red, nitrito-O) |
| Coordination | Ligands exchanged between cationic and anionic complexes | [Co(NH₃)₆][Cr(CN)₆] and [Cr(NH₃)₆][Co(CN)₆] |

### Geometrical (cis–trans) isomerism

- **Square planar MA₂B₂** (e.g. [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]): cis and trans. **Cisplatin** (cis) is an anticancer drug; trans is inactive. Tetrahedral complexes do not show geometrical isomerism.
- **Octahedral MA₄B₂** (e.g. [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺): cis (green-violet; Cl at 90°) and trans (green; Cl at 180°) — 2 isomers.
- **Octahedral MA₃B₃** (e.g. [Co(NH₃)₃(NO₂)₃]): **fac** (three A on one face) and **mer** (three A around a meridian).
- **[M(AA)₂B₂]** (e.g. [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺): cis and trans.

### Optical isomerism

**Optical isomers (enantiomers)** are non-superimposable mirror images; they rotate plane-polarised light equally in opposite directions (d and l). A complex is optically active only if it has **no plane of symmetry** (and no centre of symmetry).

| Complex type | Optically active? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| [M(AA)₃] | **Yes** (d and l) | [Co(en)₃]³⁺, [Cr(ox)₃]³⁻ |
| **cis-[M(AA)₂B₂]** or cis-[M(AA)₂BC] | **Yes** | **cis-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺, cis-[Co(NH₃)₂(en)₂]³⁺**, cis-[Pt(en)₂Cl₂]²⁺ |
| trans-[M(AA)₂B₂] | **No** (has a plane of symmetry) | trans-[Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ |
| MA₄B₂ (cis or trans) | No | [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ |
| MA₆ | No | [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ |
| Square planar | Generally no (planar = plane of symmetry) | [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] |
| Tetrahedral | Only with four different unsymmetrical ligands (rare) | — |

## 8. Crystal field theory (CFT)

**Bethe and van Vleck (1929–1935):** ligands are treated as point charges. In an octahedral field, the five degenerate d orbitals split:
- **eg** (d(x²−y²), d(z²)) — point directly at ligands → **raised** by +0.6 Δo.
- **t₂g** (dₓᵧ, d_yz, d_zx) — point between ligands → **lowered** by −0.4 Δo.
- In a **tetrahedral** field the order is reversed (e lower, t₂ higher) and **Δt = (4/9) Δo** — so tetrahedral complexes are almost always high spin.
- Square planar: d(x²−y²) is highest; suits d⁸ with strong ligands (Ni²⁺, Pd²⁺, Pt²⁺, Au³⁺).

**Spectrochemical series** (increasing field strength, Δ):
**I⁻ < Br⁻ < SCN⁻ < Cl⁻ < S²⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < C₂O₄²⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < EDTA⁴⁻ < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ < CO**.
Weak-field ligands (left) → small Δo → **high spin**. Strong-field ligands (CN⁻, CO, en, NH₃ with Co³⁺) → large Δo → **low spin** (pairing occurs when Δo > pairing energy P).

**CFSE (crystal field stabilisation energy)** for octahedral = (−0.4 n_t₂g + 0.6 n_eg) Δo (+ pairing energies).

**Worked numerical 3.** CFSE of d⁶ low-spin [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺: t₂g⁶ eg⁰ → 6 × (−0.4) = **−2.4 Δo** (+ 2P extra pairing compared with high spin). High-spin d⁶ ([CoF₆]³⁻): t₂g⁴ eg² → −1.6 + 1.2 = **−0.4 Δo**.

**Worked numerical 4.** Unpaired electrons in d⁴ ion with strong-field ligand vs weak-field? Strong (low spin): t₂g⁴ → **2**; weak (high spin): t₂g³ eg¹ → **4**.

### Colour of complexes

A complex absorbs light whose energy equals Δ (d–d transition) and shows the **complementary colour**.

| Colour absorbed (approx. wavelength) | Colour seen (complementary) |
|---|---|
| Violet (400–430 nm) | Yellow / yellow-green |
| Blue (430–490 nm) | Orange / yellow |
| Green, blue-green (490–560 nm) | Red / purple |
| Yellow (560–590 nm) | Violet / blue |
| Orange (590–620 nm) | Blue |
| Red (620–750 nm) | Green / blue-green |

- [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (d¹) absorbs at about 498 nm (green/blue-green region) → appears **purple (violet)** (NCERT example).
- Anhydrous CuSO₄ is white; with water it forms blue CuSO₄·5H₂O, because the water ligands produce the d–d splitting that gives colour.
- [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ green → add en: [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ violet (stronger field → absorbs shorter wavelength).
- [Cu(H₂O)₄]²⁺ pale blue → [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ deep blue (NH₃ stronger field).
- Colourless: d⁰ (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) and d¹⁰ (Zn²⁺, Cu⁺, Ag⁺) — no d–d transition.
- **Charge-transfer** colours (very intense): MnO₄⁻ purple, CrO₄²⁻ yellow, Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange, Prussian blue.

## 9. Organometallics, EAN rule and uses of complexes

- **Metal carbonyls:** Ni(CO)₄ (tetrahedral), Fe(CO)₅ (trigonal bipyramidal), Cr(CO)₆ (octahedral), Mn₂(CO)₁₀ and Co₂(CO)₈ (M–M bond). Bonding is **synergic**: CO donates a σ lone pair to the metal; the metal back-donates d electrons into CO's π* orbital (**back-bonding**), strengthening M–C and weakening C–O.
- **Effective atomic number (EAN) rule (Sidgwick):** EAN = Z − (oxidation state) + 2 × (CN). Stable carbonyls reach the next noble-gas number (18-electron rule).

**Worked numerical 5.** EAN of Ni in [Ni(CO)₄] = 28 − 0 + 2 × 4 = **36** (Kr). Fe in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻: 26 − 2 + 12 = **36**. Fe in [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻: 26 − 3 + 12 = 35 (not a noble-gas number, so less stable and more reactive).

- Other organometallics: **Zeise's salt** K[PtCl₃(C₂H₄)] (first, 1827); **ferrocene** Fe(C₅H₅)₂ (sandwich compound, 1951); Grignard reagent RMgX; **Wilkinson's catalyst** [RhCl(PPh₃)₃] for hydrogenation of alkenes; **Ziegler–Natta catalyst** TiCl₄ + Al(C₂H₅)₃ for polythene/polypropylene; tetraethyl lead (old antiknock).

## Traps and exceptions

- **CN counts donor atoms, not ligands**: [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl → 6; [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ → 6; [Fe(EDTA)]⁻ → 6.
- Counter ions outside [ ] do not affect the ON calculation except through the charge of the complex ion.
- **Fe in nitroprusside is +2** (NO⁺); CO, NH₃, H₂O, en are neutral ligands.
- **Cr in [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is +3.**
- **trans-[M(AA)₂B₂] is not optically active**; cis is. MA₆ and MA₄B₂ are never optically active.
- Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism; square planar complexes (MA₂B₂) do.
- Zn, Cd, Hg are not typical transition elements; their compounds are white/colourless (ZnSO₄, Zn₂[Fe(CN)₆] white).
- **KMnO₄ and K₂Cr₂O₇ colours are charge transfer**, not d–d (d⁰ metals).
- Δt = 4/9 Δo — tetrahedral complexes are high spin.
- In nomenclature: ammine (NH₃) has double m; amine (organic) single m.
- Anionic complex → metal ends in -ate: ferrate, cuprate, argentate, but cobaltate, nickelate.
- **Ni(CO)₄ is diamagnetic and tetrahedral (sp³)**; [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is square planar (dsp²) and diamagnetic; [NiCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral and paramagnetic.
- Highest oxidation state: Os/Ru +8; Mn +7 in 3d series.
- Highest m.p. metal: W; lowest: Hg.
- Cu is the only 3d metal with positive E°(M²⁺/M).

## Quick revision

1. Transition element: incomplete d sub-shell in atom or common ion.
2. Zn, Cd, Hg — not typical transition metals.
3. Mn shows +2 to +7; OsO₄ has Os +8.
4. Colour from d–d transitions; d⁰ and d¹⁰ colourless.
5. Lanthanoid contraction → Zr ≈ Hf, Mo ≈ W.
6. Ce⁴⁺ oxidant; Eu²⁺ reductant; common lanthanoid state +3.
7. Misch metal — lighter flints.
8. Chromate (yellow) ⇌ dichromate (orange) with pH.
9. Baeyer's reagent = cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄.
10. Zone refining: Si, Ge; Mond: Ni; van Arkel: Ti, Zr.
11. Froth flotation for sulphide ores; calcination for carbonates; roasting for sulphides.
12. Werner (1893): primary = ON, secondary = CN.
13. EDTA hexadentate; en bidentate; NO₂⁻ and SCN⁻ ambidentate.
14. CN of Co in [Co(en)₂Cl₂]Cl = 6.
15. ON of Cr in [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ = +3; Fe in nitroprusside +2.
16. NH₃ = ammine; CO = carbonyl; NO = nitrosyl; H₂O = aqua.
17. Cisplatin = cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], anticancer.
18. Optical activity: [M(AA)₃], cis-[M(AA)₂B₂].
19. [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ has 3 stereoisomers.
20. Δt = 4/9 Δo.
21. Spectrochemical series ends: … NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO.
22. Low spin [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ diamagnetic; [CoF₆]³⁻ paramagnetic.
23. Zn₂[Fe(CN)₆] white; K₃[Co(NO₂)₆] yellow.
