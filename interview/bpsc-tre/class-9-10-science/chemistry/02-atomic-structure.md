This chapter takes the atom from Dalton's solid ball to the quantum-mechanical orbital. It covers NCERT class 9 ("Structure of the Atom"), class 11 ("Structure of Atom") and B.Sc. points such as radial/angular nodes and nuclear radius. TRE 1.0 asked "atomic orbital is …" and "radius of a nucleus is of the order of …"; TRE 2.0 asked "number of nodal planes in a pₓ orbital".

> **In simple words:** An electron is not a planet on a fixed track. We can only say where it is *likely* to be. That region of high probability is an **orbital**. Four quantum numbers are the electron's address: shell (n), sub-shell shape (l), orientation (m) and spin (s).

## 1. Sub-atomic particles

| Particle | Discovered by (year) | Charge | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electron (e⁻) | J. J. Thomson (1897), cathode rays | −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C | 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg (1/1837 of H atom) |
| Proton (p) | Goldstein's canal rays (1886); named by Rutherford (1919/1920) | +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C | 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg |
| Neutron (n) | James Chadwick (1932), by bombarding Be with α-particles | 0 | 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (slightly heavier than proton) |

- **Charge of electron** was measured by **Millikan's oil-drop experiment (1909)**; e/m of electron = 1.758 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹ (Thomson).
- Cathode rays travel in straight lines, carry negative charge, are deflected by electric and magnetic fields, and their nature does **not** depend on the gas or electrode material.
- Canal (anode) rays depend on the gas used; their e/m is largest for hydrogen.
- Other particles: positron (Anderson, 1932), neutrino (proposed by Pauli, 1930), meson (Yukawa, 1935).

### Atomic models

| Model | Main idea | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Thomson (1898/1904) "plum pudding" / watermelon | Positive sphere with electrons embedded | Could not explain α-scattering |
| Rutherford (1911) nuclear model | α-particles on thin gold foil: most passed straight, few deflected, about 1 in 20 000 bounced back → tiny, dense, positive nucleus | Could not explain stability of atom (accelerating electron should radiate and fall in) or line spectra |
| Bohr (1913) | Electrons in fixed circular orbits (stationary states) with quantised angular momentum mvr = nh/2π | Works only for one-electron species (H, He⁺, Li²⁺); fails for Zeeman/Stark effect and for multi-electron atoms |
| Quantum-mechanical (Schrödinger, 1926) | Electron as a wave; ψ² gives probability | — (current model) |

### Size of atom and nucleus

- **Radius of an atom** ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m = 10⁻⁸ cm (1 Å).
- **Radius of a nucleus** ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m = **10⁻¹³ cm** (1 fermi). Formula: R = R₀ A^(1/3), R₀ ≈ 1.2–1.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ m.
- So the atom is about 10⁵ times bigger than its nucleus; nuclear density is nearly constant (~2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg m⁻³) for all nuclei.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "The radius of an atomic nucleus is of the order of (A) 10⁻¹⁰ cm (B) 10⁻¹³ cm (C) 10⁻¹⁵ cm". The correct value is **10⁻¹³ cm** (= 10⁻¹⁵ m). Option (C) is the trap: 10⁻¹⁵ is right only in metres. (No official key in our folder.)

**Worked numerical 1.** If R₀ = 1.2 fm, radius of ²⁷Al nucleus? A^(1/3) = 3 → R = **3.6 fm** (3.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ m). Radius of ⁶⁴Cu nucleus = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 fm. Ratio R(Cu)/R(Al) = 4/3.

### Atomic number, mass number and related terms

- **Atomic number Z** = number of protons (= electrons in neutral atom). Moseley (1913) showed √ν ∝ Z from X-ray spectra, making Z the basis of the modern periodic table.
- **Mass number A** = protons + neutrons. Number of neutrons = A − Z.

| Term | Same | Different | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isotopes | Z | A (neutrons) | ¹H, ²H (D), ³H (T); ¹²C, ¹⁴C; ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl |
| Isobars | A | Z | ⁴⁰Ar, ⁴⁰K, ⁴⁰Ca |
| Isotones | Neutrons | Z and A | ¹⁴C and ¹⁶O (8 neutrons each); ³⁹K and ⁴⁰Ca (20 each) |
| Isoelectronic | Number of electrons | Z | N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ (10 e⁻ each) |
| Isodiaphers | A − 2Z | — | Parent and daughter in α-decay |

Uses of isotopes: ¹⁴C (dating, half-life 5730 years), ⁶⁰Co (cancer therapy), ¹³¹I (thyroid), ³²P (leukaemia, plant uptake studies), ²³⁵U (fission fuel), ²H (heavy water as moderator).

## 2. Electromagnetic radiation and quantum theory

- c = νλ; wave number ν̄ = 1/λ (unit cm⁻¹ or m⁻¹).
- **Planck's quantum theory (1900):** energy is emitted or absorbed in packets (quanta): **E = hν = hc/λ**, h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s.
- Order of wavelength: γ-rays < X-rays < UV < visible (400–750 nm) < IR < microwaves < radio waves. Energy order is the reverse.
- **Photoelectric effect** (Hertz 1887; explained by Einstein 1905): hν = hν₀ + ½mv². No emission below threshold frequency ν₀, whatever the intensity; KE of electrons depends on frequency, number of electrons depends on intensity. Caesium has the lowest work function among stable metals, so it is used in photocells.
- **Black-body radiation** could not be explained by classical physics; Planck's quantum idea solved it.

**Worked numerical 2.** Energy of a photon of 500 nm light?
E = hc/λ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (5 × 10⁻⁷) = **3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 2.48 eV**. (Shortcut: E(eV) = 1240 / λ(nm) = 1240/500 = 2.48 eV.)

## 3. Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom

Postulates: electron moves in fixed circular orbits without radiating; angular momentum mvr = nh/2π; energy is absorbed or emitted only when it jumps between orbits, ΔE = hν.

<div class="formula">

| Quantity (for H-like species, atomic number Z) | Formula |
|---|---|
| Radius of nth orbit | rₙ = 0.529 × n²/Z Å |
| Energy of nth orbit | Eₙ = −13.6 × Z²/n² eV = −2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ Z²/n² J |
| Velocity in nth orbit | vₙ = 2.18 × 10⁶ × Z/n m s⁻¹ |
| Wave number of line | ν̄ = R_H Z² (1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), R_H = 109 677 cm⁻¹ |
| Number of lines from level n down to 1 | n(n − 1)/2 |

</div>

- Energy is negative: the electron is bound. E = 0 at n = ∞. **Ionisation energy of H = 13.6 eV** (from n = 1).
- Ratio KE : PE : total energy = 1 : −2 : −1.
- As n increases, radius increases, energy increases (less negative), and gap between levels decreases.

**Worked numerical 3.** Radius of the 2nd Bohr orbit of H? r₂ = 0.529 × 4 = **2.116 Å**. For He⁺ (Z = 2), n = 2: 0.529 × 4/2 = 1.058 Å.

**Worked numerical 4.** Energy of electron in first orbit of He⁺? E = −13.6 × 4/1 = **−54.4 eV**. Ionisation energy of Li²⁺ = 13.6 × 9 = 122.4 eV.

**Worked numerical 5.** Wavelength of the first Balmer line (n = 3 → 2)?
ν̄ = 109 677 (1/4 − 1/9) = 109 677 × 5/36 cm⁻¹ → λ = 36/(5 × 109 677) cm = 6.565 × 10⁻⁵ cm = **656.5 nm (red Hα line)**.

**Worked numerical 6.** Shortest wavelength of Lyman series (∞ → 1)? λ = 1/R_H = 9.12 × 10⁻⁶ cm = **91.2 nm**. Longest Lyman line (2 → 1): λ = 4/(3R_H) = **121.6 nm**.

**Worked numerical 7.** Electron falls from n = 5 to n = 1 in a sample of H atoms. Maximum number of spectral lines? 5 × 4/2 = **10**.

**Worked numerical 8.** Energy needed to excite H from n = 1 to n = 2? 13.6 (1 − 1/4) = **10.2 eV**.

## 4. Dual nature and uncertainty

- **de Broglie (1924):** every moving particle has a wave: **λ = h/mv = h/p**. For an electron accelerated through V volts, λ = 12.27/√V Å. Wave nature of electrons was confirmed by Davisson–Germer (1927) electron diffraction; the electron microscope uses it.
- For large objects (cricket ball) λ is too small to detect.
- In Bohr's orbit, the circumference holds a whole number of waves: 2πr = nλ.
- **Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (1927):** Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π. Position and momentum of an electron cannot both be known exactly. This rules out fixed orbits.

**Worked numerical 9.** de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 1.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹?
λ = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 10⁶) = **7.27 × 10⁻¹⁰ m ≈ 0.73 nm**.

**Worked numerical 10.** Minimum uncertainty in velocity of an electron if Δx = 1 Å (10⁻¹⁰ m)?
Δv = h/(4π m Δx) = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ / (4 × 3.1416 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 10⁻¹⁰) = **5.8 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** — huge, so the "orbit" idea fails.

## 5. Orbitals and quantum numbers

**Orbit vs orbital**

| Orbit (Bohr) | Orbital (quantum mechanics) |
|---|---|
| Definite circular path | **Three-dimensional region where the probability of finding the electron is maximum** |
| Planar | Has shape (s spherical, p dumb-bell, d double dumb-bell) |
| Can hold 2n² electrons | Holds at most 2 electrons |
| Against uncertainty principle | Agrees with it |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Atomic orbital is (A) the circular path of electron (B) an elliptical shaped orbit (C) the region in which there is a maximum possibility of finding an electron". The correct choice is **(C)**. Circular path is Bohr's orbit; elliptical orbit is Sommerfeld's.

**Schrödinger wave equation (1926):** Ĥψ = Eψ. ψ (wave function) has no physical meaning; **ψ² is the probability density**. Solutions give the quantum numbers.

| Quantum number | Symbol and values | Tells us | Proposed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal | n = 1, 2, 3 … (K, L, M, N) | Shell, size and energy | Bohr |
| Azimuthal (angular momentum) | l = 0 to n − 1 (s, p, d, f = 0, 1, 2, 3) | Sub-shell and **shape**; orbital angular momentum = √(l(l+1)) h/2π | Sommerfeld |
| Magnetic | m = −l … 0 … +l (2l + 1 values) | **Orientation** in space; number of orbitals in a sub-shell | Lande (Zeeman effect) |
| Spin | s = +½ or −½ | Direction of spin; spin angular momentum = √(s(s+1)) h/2π | Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) |

- Orbitals in a shell = n²; electrons in a shell = 2n². Orbitals in a sub-shell = 2l + 1; electrons = 2(2l + 1): s 2, p 6, d 10, f 14.
- **Invalid sets** to spot: l cannot equal n (n = 2, l = 2 is impossible); |m| cannot exceed l.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "The number of nodal planes in pₓ-orbital is (A) 1 (B) 2 (C) 3". Official answer: **(A) 1** (the yz-plane, because l = 1).

- **s** orbitals are spherical and non-directional. **p** orbitals are dumb-bell shaped along x, y, z. **d** orbitals: dₓᵧ, d_yz, d_zx have lobes **between** axes; d(x²−y²) and d(z²) have lobes **along** axes (important in crystal field theory, chapter 9).
- 2s is bigger than 1s; probability plots of 2s show one radial node.

## 6. Filling electrons: Aufbau, Pauli and Hund

- **Aufbau principle:** orbitals fill in increasing energy, decided by the **(n + l) rule**; if (n + l) is equal, the lower n fills first.
  Order: 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < **4s < 3d** < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s < 5f < 6d.
- **Pauli exclusion principle (1925):** no two electrons in an atom have all four quantum numbers the same → an orbital holds at most 2 electrons with opposite spins.
- **Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity:** in degenerate orbitals (same energy), electrons first occupy singly with parallel spins, then pair up. Nitrogen 2p³ has three unpaired electrons.

**Stable half-filled and fully-filled sub-shells** (extra exchange energy and symmetry) cause the famous exceptions:

| Element (Z) | Expected | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Cr (24) | [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² | **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** |
| Cu (29) | [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s² | **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** |
| Mo (42) | [Kr] 4d⁴ 5s² | [Kr] 4d⁵ 5s¹ |
| Ag (47) | [Kr] 4d⁹ 5s² | [Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s¹ |
| Pd (46) | [Kr] 4d⁸ 5s² | **[Kr] 4d¹⁰ 5s⁰** (no s electron) |
| Au (79) | [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d⁹ 6s² | [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ 6s¹ |

- When transition metals form ions, **4s electrons are removed first**: Fe = [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²; Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶; Fe³⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁵ (more stable, half-filled).
- **Magnetic moment** (spin-only) μ = √(n(n + 2)) BM, n = unpaired electrons: n = 1 → 1.73; 2 → 2.83; 3 → 3.87; 4 → 4.90; 5 → 5.92 BM.
- **Paramagnetic** = has unpaired electrons (O₂, Fe³⁺, Cu²⁺). **Diamagnetic** = all paired (N₂, Zn²⁺, Cu⁺, Sc³⁺).

**Worked numerical 11.** Spin-only magnetic moment of Mn²⁺ (3d⁵)? n = 5 → √35 = **5.92 BM**. Of Ti³⁺ (3d¹): √3 = 1.73 BM.

**Worked numerical 12.** Quantum numbers of the last electron of sodium (3s¹)? n = 3, l = 0, m = 0, s = +½ (or −½).

**Bohr–Bury rules (class 9):** maximum electrons in a shell = 2n² (K 2, L 8, M 18, N 32), but the outermost shell cannot hold more than 8 and the penultimate not more than 18. Valency = electrons gained, lost or shared to complete the octet.

## Traps and exceptions

- Nuclear radius 10⁻¹⁵ **m** = 10⁻¹³ **cm**; atomic radius 10⁻¹⁰ m = 10⁻⁸ cm.
- Proton's discovery is credited to Goldstein (canal rays) in most Indian textbooks; Rutherford named and identified it. Neutron = Chadwick 1932, not Rutherford.
- **d(z²) has no nodal plane** (it has two nodal cones), though it has 2 angular nodes.
- pₓ nodal plane is **yz**, not the x-axis.
- Balmer is the only series in the visible region; Lyman is UV.
- Energy levels get closer as n increases, so the n = 2 → 1 gap is the largest.
- Cr and Cu (and Mo, Ag, Au) are exceptions; **Pd has 5s⁰**.
- 4s fills before 3d but is **emptied first** on ionisation.
- Number of orbitals in a shell is n², not 2n².
- The intensity of light controls the number of photoelectrons, not their energy.
