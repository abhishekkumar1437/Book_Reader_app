This chapter collects the "chemistry around us" topics. It covers NCERT class 10 (soaps and detergents, carbon compounds), class 12 ("Polymers", "Biomolecules", "Chemistry in Everyday Life") and class 11 ("Environmental Chemistry"), plus B.Sc. points such as biodegradable copolymers. TRE 1.0 asked "which is a branched polymer" (LDPE) and "which monomers form biodegradable polymers" (PHBV); TRE 2.0 asked "which is a polyamide", "what is aspirin", "which vitamin contains cobalt" and "which is not a steroid hormone".

> **In simple words:** A polymer is a long chain made by joining small units (monomers) again and again — by simple addition (polythene) or by losing a small molecule like water (nylon, terylene). Our body's big molecules — starch, proteins, DNA — are natural polymers too.

## 1. Polymers — classification

- **Polymer:** very large molecule (macromolecule, molar mass 10³–10⁷) made of repeating **monomer** units. **Degree of polymerisation (n)** = molar mass of polymer / molar mass of repeat unit.
- Term "polymer" coined by Berzelius (1833); macromolecule concept by **Hermann Staudinger** (Nobel 1953).

| Basis | Types | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Natural | Starch, cellulose, proteins, natural rubber, silk, wool, DNA |
| | Semi-synthetic | Cellulose acetate (rayon), cellulose nitrate, vulcanised rubber |
| | Synthetic | Polythene, PVC, nylon, terylene, Bakelite |
| Structure | Linear | HDPE, PVC, nylon |
| | **Branched** | **LDPE**, amylopectin, glycogen |
| | Cross-linked (network) | **Bakelite**, melamine–formaldehyde, urea–formaldehyde, vulcanised rubber |
| Mode of polymerisation | Addition (chain-growth) — monomers with C=C, no by-product | Polythene, PVC, PTFE, polystyrene, PAN |
| | Condensation (step-growth) — small molecule (H₂O, HCl, CH₃OH) lost | Nylon-6,6, terylene, Bakelite, glyptal |
| | Homopolymer / copolymer | Polythene / Buna-S, nylon-6,6 |
| Molecular forces | Elastomers (weakest forces, stretch and return) | Natural rubber, Buna-S, Buna-N, neoprene |
| | Fibres (strong H-bonds, high tensile strength) | Nylon-6,6, terylene, silk |
| | Thermoplastics (soften on heating, can be remoulded; intermediate forces) | Polythene, PVC, polystyrene, Teflon |
| | Thermosetting (permanently set on heating, cross-linked) | Bakelite, urea–formaldehyde, melamine |

### LDPE and HDPE

| Feature | Low-density polythene (LDPE) | High-density polythene (HDPE) |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions | Ethene at **1000–2000 atm, 350–570 K**, traces of O₂ or peroxide initiator | 333–343 K, 6–7 atm, **Ziegler–Natta catalyst** (TiCl₄ + Al(C₂H₅)₃) in hydrocarbon solvent |
| Mechanism | Free-radical addition | Coordination (ionic) addition |
| Structure | **Highly branched** | **Linear** |
| Density | ~0.92 g cm⁻³ | 0.95–0.97 g cm⁻³ |
| Properties | Soft, flexible, tough, chemically inert, poor conductor | Harder, stronger, more crystalline, higher m.p. |
| Uses | Squeeze bottles, carry bags, toys, insulation of wires | Buckets, dustbins, pipes, bottles, housewares |

## 2. Important polymers (master table)

| Polymer | Monomer(s) | Type | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polythene (LDPE/HDPE) | Ethene CH₂=CH₂ | Addition, homopolymer | Bags, bottles, pipes |
| Polypropylene | Propene | Addition | Ropes, crates, carpets |
| PVC | Vinyl chloride CH₂=CHCl | Addition | Pipes, raincoats, flooring, cable insulation |
| **Teflon (PTFE)** | Tetrafluoroethene CF₂=CF₂ | Addition | Non-stick cookware, gaskets (very inert) |
| **PAN (Orlon, Acrilan)** | Acrylonitrile CH₂=CHCN | Addition | Synthetic wool, blankets |
| Polystyrene (Styron) | Styrene C₆H₅CH=CH₂ | Addition | Insulation, packaging (thermocol), combs |
| PMMA (Perspex, Plexiglas, Lucite) | Methyl methacrylate | Addition | Unbreakable glass, lenses, aircraft windows |
| **Natural rubber** | **Isoprene** (2-methylbuta-1,3-diene), **cis-1,4** | Addition | Tyres (after vulcanisation) |
| Gutta-percha | Isoprene, **trans** form | Natural | Golf balls, cable coating |
| **Neoprene** | **Chloroprene** (2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene) | Addition | Conveyor belts, gaskets, hoses (oil resistant) |
| **Buna-S (SBR)** | **Buta-1,3-diene + styrene** | Addition copolymer | Tyres, footwear soles |
| **Buna-N (NBR)** | **Buta-1,3-diene + acrylonitrile** | Addition copolymer | Oil seals, tank linings (oil resistant) |
| **Nylon-6,6** | **Hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid** | Condensation, **polyamide** | Bristles, ropes, fabrics, parachutes |
| **Nylon-6 (Perlon)** | **Caprolactam** | Ring-opening, polyamide | Tyre cords, ropes, fabrics |
| **Terylene (Dacron)** | **Ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid** | Condensation, **polyester** | Fabrics (blend with cotton/wool), safety belts |
| **Glyptal** | **Ethylene glycol + phthalic acid** | Condensation, polyester (alkyd resin) | Paints, lacquers |
| **Bakelite** | **Phenol + formaldehyde** (novolac intermediate) | Condensation, cross-linked | Switches, handles, electrical fittings |
| Melamine–formaldehyde | Melamine + HCHO | Condensation | Unbreakable crockery |
| Urea–formaldehyde | Urea + HCHO | Condensation | Unbreakable cups, laminates |
| PHBV | 3-Hydroxybutanoic acid + 3-hydroxypentanoic acid | Condensation copolymer, **biodegradable** | Packaging, orthopaedic devices, controlled drug release |
| **Nylon-2–nylon-6** | **Glycine + aminocaproic acid** (6-aminohexanoic acid) | Condensation, polyamide, **biodegradable** | — |
| PGA, PLA | Glycolic acid, lactic acid | Biodegradable polyesters | Dissolvable sutures |

- **Polyamides** have –CO–NH– links: nylon-6,6, nylon-6, nylon-2–nylon-6, and natural proteins (silk, wool). Polythene and Bakelite are not polyamides.
- **Numbers in nylon names** = carbon atoms in each monomer: nylon-6,6 → 6 C in the diamine, 6 C in the diacid.
- **Vulcanisation** (Charles Goodyear, 1839): heating natural rubber with **sulphur** (and ZnO accelerator) at 373–415 K forms –S–S– cross-links → rubber becomes harder, less sticky, more elastic, works over a wider temperature range. Tyre rubber ~5% S; **ebonite** (very hard) ~30% S or more.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is a polyamide? (A) Nylon (B) Polythene (C) Bakelite". Official answer: **(A) Nylon**.

### Biodegradable polymers

Ordinary plastics are not broken down by microbes. **Biodegradable** polymers have functional groups (ester, amide) that enzymes can hydrolyse.
- **PHBV — poly-β-hydroxybutyrate-co-β-hydroxyvalerate**: copolymer of **3-hydroxybutanoic acid and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid**; made by bacteria; degraded by bacteria.
- **Nylon-2–nylon-6**: alternating polyamide copolymer of **glycine (H₂N–CH₂–COOH) and aminocaproic acid (H₂N–(CH₂)₅–COOH)**; biodegradable.
- Polyglycolic acid (PGA), polylactic acid (PLA), and their copolymers — surgical sutures.
- Not biodegradable: polythene, PVC, terylene, glyptal (ethylene glycol + phthalic acid), Bakelite.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following monomers form biodegradable polymers? (A) 3-Hydroxybutanoic acid + 3-hydroxypentanoic acid (B) Glycine + aminocaproic acid (C) Ethylene glycol + phthalic acid (D) More than one of the above". Both (A) (PHBV) and (B) (nylon-2–nylon-6) are biodegradable, so by NCERT the correct option is **(D) More than one of the above**. (C) gives glyptal, which is not biodegradable. Many candidates mark only (A) — this is exactly the TRE "option D" trap. (No official key in our folder.)

**Molar mass of polymers:** number-average (M̄n, by osmotic pressure) and weight-average (M̄w, by light scattering/ultracentrifuge). **Polydispersity index PDI = M̄w/M̄n ≥ 1**; natural polymers (proteins) are monodisperse (PDI = 1).

**Worked numerical 1.** A sample of polythene has molar mass 28 000 g mol⁻¹. Degree of polymerisation? Repeat unit –CH₂–CH₂– = 28 → n = 28 000/28 = **1000**.

**Worked numerical 2.** Starch of molar mass 162 000. Number of glucose units? Each unit in the chain is C₆H₁₀O₅ = 162 → **1000 units**.

## 3. Carbohydrates

**Carbohydrates** are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones (or compounds that give them on hydrolysis); general formula Cₓ(H₂O)ᵧ (but not all such compounds are carbohydrates: acetic acid C₂(H₂O)₂ is not; rhamnose C₆H₁₂O₅ is a carbohydrate though it does not fit).

| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharides | **Glucose** (aldohexose), **fructose** (ketohexose), galactose, ribose (aldopentose), deoxyribose | Cannot be hydrolysed further |
| Disaccharides | **Sucrose** = glucose + fructose (α1→β2, **non-reducing**); **maltose** = glucose + glucose (α1→4, reducing); **lactose** = galactose + glucose (β1→4, reducing, milk sugar) | — |
| Polysaccharides | **Starch** (amylose 15–20%, linear, water-soluble; amylopectin 80–85%, branched), **cellulose** (β-D-glucose, β1→4, linear; plant cell walls), **glycogen** (animal starch; highly branched; liver and muscles), chitin | Non-reducing, not sweet |

- **Glucose** (dextrose, grape sugar): C₆H₁₂O₆; evidence for its structure — HI gives n-hexane (straight 6-C chain); pentaacetate (5 OH groups); bromine water → gluconic acid (aldehyde group); HNO₃ → saccharic acid. Exists mostly as cyclic **pyranose** forms α and β (**anomers**, differing at C-1). **Mutarotation**: α (+112°) and β (+18.7°) change to an equilibrium value of +52.7°.
- **Fructose** (fruit sugar, laevulose): sweetest natural sugar; furanose ring.
- **Reducing sugars** (free aldehyde/ketone group) reduce Tollens' and Fehling's: all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose. **Sucrose is non-reducing.**
- **Invert sugar:** hydrolysis of sucrose (dextrorotatory, +66.5°) gives glucose (+52.7°) + fructose (−92.4°) — net laevorotatory; enzyme **invertase**.
- Humans cannot digest cellulose (no cellulase); ruminants can (gut microbes).

## 4. Proteins and enzymes

- **α-Amino acids:** H₂N–CHR–COOH; 20 occur in proteins. All except **glycine** are optically active (L-form). Exist as **zwitterions** (H₃N⁺–CHR–COO⁻); amphoteric; high m.p.; **isoelectric point** = pH at which net charge is zero.
- **Essential amino acids** (must come from diet): valine, leucine, isoleucine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, threonine, methionine, lysine, histidine, arginine (10; arginine and histidine are essential in children).
- **Peptide bond** –CO–NH– formed by condensation (water lost). A peptide with n amino acids has (n − 1) peptide bonds.
- **Structure levels:** primary (sequence; Sanger sequenced insulin, Nobel 1958), secondary (**α-helix** — H-bonds within a chain, keratin of hair; **β-pleated sheet** — H-bonds between chains, silk fibroin), tertiary (overall folding; disulphide, H-bonds, ionic, hydrophobic), quaternary (two or more subunits; haemoglobin has 4).
- **Fibrous** (keratin — hair, nails, wool; collagen — tendons; myosin — muscle; fibroin — silk; insoluble) vs **globular** (insulin, albumin, haemoglobin, enzymes; soluble).
- **Denaturation:** loss of 2°/3° structure by heat, pH or chemicals; primary structure intact (boiling of egg — albumin coagulates; curdling of milk — lactic acid from bacteria).
- **Enzymes** are mostly globular proteins; very specific; work at optimum temperature (~37 °C) and pH. Names often end in -ase. Ribozymes are RNA enzymes.

**Worked numerical 3.** Number of peptide bonds in a pentapeptide? 5 − 1 = **4**. Molar mass of a dipeptide of glycine (75): 2 × 75 − 18 = **132 g mol⁻¹**.

## 5. Nucleic acids

| Feature | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | 2-Deoxyribose (β-D-2-deoxyribose) | Ribose |
| Bases | A, G (purines); C, **T** (pyrimidines) | A, G, C, **U** (uracil instead of thymine) |
| Strands | Double helix (Watson and Crick, 1953; X-ray data of Rosalind Franklin, Wilkins) | Usually single |
| Function | Stores genetic information; replicates | Protein synthesis (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA) |

- **Nucleoside** = base + sugar; **nucleotide** = base + sugar + phosphate. Nucleotides are joined by **phosphodiester** links (3′–5′).
- **Base pairing:** A=T (two H-bonds), G≡C (three H-bonds); Chargaff's rule A = T, G = C. DNA helix diameter 2 nm, one turn = 3.4 nm (10 base pairs).
- ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the energy currency of the cell.

| Vitamin | Solubility | Deficiency disease | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (retinol) | Fat | Night blindness, xerophthalmia | Carrot, fish liver oil, milk |
| B₁ (thiamine) | Water | **Beriberi** | Yeast, cereals, milk |
| B₂ (riboflavin) | Water | Cheilosis (cracked lips), dermatitis | Milk, egg white, liver |
| B₃ (niacin) | Water | **Pellagra** | Meat, groundnut |
| B₆ (pyridoxine) | Water | Convulsions | Yeast, cereals, egg |
| **B₁₂ (cyanocobalamin) — contains cobalt** | Water | **Pernicious anaemia** | Meat, fish, egg, curd (not in plants) |
| C (ascorbic acid) | Water | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums) | Citrus fruits, amla, green vegetables |
| D (calciferol) | Fat | **Rickets** (children), osteomalacia (adults) | Sunlight on skin, fish, egg yolk |

| K (phylloquinone) | Fat | Delayed blood clotting | Green leafy vegetables |

Fat-soluble **A, D, E, K** are stored in liver and adipose tissue; water-soluble B and C must be taken regularly (excreted in urine). Vitamin C is destroyed by cooking (heat). Vitamin B₁₂ is the only vitamin containing a metal.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked which vitamin contains cobalt: B₁₂, B₆ or B₁. Official answer: **(A) B₁₂**.

**Hormones** — chemical messengers from endocrine glands.

| Chemical class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Steroid** (derived from cholesterol; four fused rings) | **Estrogen (oestradiol), progesterone, testosterone, aldosterone, cortisol** (cortisone), vitamin D-related calcitriol |
| Peptide / protein | Insulin, glucagon, oxytocin, vasopressin (ADH), growth hormone, TSH, parathyroid hormone |
| Amino-acid derivatives | **Thyroxine** (from tyrosine + iodine), adrenaline (epinephrine), noradrenaline, melatonin |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "Which of the following is not a steroid hormone? (A) Thyroxine (B) Aldosterone (C) Estrogen". Official answer: **(A) Thyroxine** — an iodinated amino-acid (tyrosine) derivative.

**Lipids:** fats and oils are triglycerides (esters of glycerol + fatty acids). Oils (unsaturated, liquid) are converted to vanaspati ghee by **hydrogenation** with Ni catalyst. **Saponification value** relates to molar mass; **iodine value** measures unsaturation. Rancidity = oxidation of fats (prevented by antioxidants BHA, BHT, nitrogen flushing in chips packets).

### Aspirin

- **Aspirin = acetylsalicylic acid** (2-acetoxybenzoic acid), prepared by **acetylation of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride** (conc. H₂SO₄ or H₃PO₄ catalyst). First synthesised in usable form by Felix Hoffmann (Bayer, 1897).
- Action: inhibits synthesis of **prostaglandins** → analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory; also prevents platelet clumping → used in low doses to **prevent heart attacks** (anti-clotting).
- Side effect: can cause **gastric bleeding** (it hydrolyses in the stomach to salicylic acid); not advised for children with viral fever (Reye's syndrome).
- Do not confuse with **methyl salicylate** (oil of wintergreen, used in pain-relief ointments) or salicylic acid itself (used for warts and acne).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) asked: "What is aspirin? (A) Methyl salicylic acid (B) Acetyl salicylic acid (C) Acetyl salicylate". Official answer: **(B) Acetyl salicylic acid**.

## 8. Chemicals in food, soaps and detergents

**Artificial sweeteners** (sweetness relative to cane sugar = 1):

| Sweetener | Sweetness | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Saccharin | ~550 | First (1879); excreted unchanged; for diabetics |
| Aspartame | ~100 | Methyl ester of a dipeptide (aspartic acid + phenylalanine); **unstable at cooking temperature** — used only in cold drinks/foods |
| Alitame | ~2000 | Difficult to control sweetness |
| Sucralose | ~600 | Trichloro-derivative of sucrose; stable at cooking temperature |

**Food preservatives:** common salt, sugar, vegetable oils, vinegar, **sodium benzoate** (metabolised to hippuric acid), salts of sorbic and propanoic acids, potassium metabisulphite (KMS). **Antioxidants:** BHT, BHA, SO₂ and sulphites in wines and beers.

**Soaps:** sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids (stearic, palmitic, oleic). Made by **saponification**: fat/oil + NaOH → soap + **glycerol**; common salt is added to precipitate soap (**salting out**). Potassium soaps are softer (shaving soaps). Transparent soaps contain ethanol; toilet soaps contain perfume; medicated soaps contain antiseptics. Soaps do not work in hard water (Ca/Mg soaps = scum) or in acidic water.

**Cleansing action:** the soap anion has a **hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail** (dissolves grease) and a **hydrophilic –COO⁻ head** (faces water). In water they form **micelles** with grease trapped inside; the charged micelles repel each other and are washed away (emulsification).

**Synthetic detergents** work in hard water (their Ca/Mg salts are soluble).

| Type | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Anionic | Sodium dodecylbenzenesulphonate, **sodium lauryl sulphate** | Household detergents, toothpaste |
| Cationic | **Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide** (quaternary ammonium) | Hair conditioners; germicidal; expensive |
| Non-ionic | Esters of polyethylene glycol (from stearic acid) | Liquid dishwashing detergents |

**Branched-chain** detergents are **non-biodegradable** (foam in rivers, eutrophication with phosphates); **unbranched** chains are biodegradable.

## 9. Glass, fertilisers, explosives and fuels (one-liners)

- **Glass:** ordinary soft (soda–lime) glass = Na₂SiO₃ + CaSiO₃ + SiO₂; **Pyrex** = borosilicate (low expansion, labware); **flint/crystal glass** = lead silicate (high refractive index, lenses, prisms); **crookes glass** contains cerium oxide (absorbs UV); quartz glass transmits UV. **Colouring agents:** Co (blue), Cr₂O₃ (green), Fe²⁺ (green), MnO₂ (violet/purple), Cu₂O (red), CuO (blue-green), gold chloride (ruby red), CdS (yellow). Glass is etched by HF.
- **Fertilisers:** urea CO(NH₂)₂ (46.7% N — highest among solids), ammonium sulphate (21% N), calcium ammonium nitrate (CAN), **superphosphate of lime** Ca(H₂PO₄)₂ + 2CaSO₄·2H₂O, triple superphosphate, NPK, potash (KCl). Urea from NH₃ + CO₂ at high pressure.
- **Explosives:** TNT (2,4,6-trinitrotoluene), **RDX** (cyclonite — most powerful common military explosive), nitroglycerine, **dynamite** (nitroglycerine absorbed in kieselguhr — Alfred Nobel, 1867), gun cotton (cellulose nitrate), picric acid, ammonium nitrate (ANFO), gunpowder (KNO₃ + S + charcoal).
- **Rocket propellants:** liquid H₂ + liquid O₂ (cryogenic), hydrazine/UDMH + N₂O₄ (hypergolic), solid HTPB + ammonium perchlorate + Al powder.
- **Fuel gases:** water gas (CO + H₂), producer gas (CO + N₂), coal gas (H₂ + CH₄ + CO), biogas/gobar gas (~50–75% CH₄ + CO₂), LPG (butane + propane), CNG (CH₄).

**Worked numerical 4.** % nitrogen in urea CO(NH₂)₂ (M = 60)? 28/60 × 100 = **46.7%**. In ammonium sulphate (NH₄)₂SO₄ (M = 132): 28/132 × 100 = **21.2%**.

## 10. Environmental chemistry

| Topic | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Greenhouse gases | CO₂ (largest share), CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, O₃, water vapour. Without greenhouse effect Earth would average about −18 °C instead of ~15 °C |
| Acid rain | pH **below 5.6** (normal rain ~5.6 due to dissolved CO₂); from SO₂ and NO₂ → H₂SO₄ and HNO₃; damages marble — "stone cancer" of the **Taj Mahal** (Mathura refinery) |
| Classical (London) smog | Smoke + fog + SO₂; **reducing**; cool humid weather; London 1952 |
| Photochemical (Los Angeles) smog | NO₂ + hydrocarbons + sunlight → O₃, NO, **PAN** (peroxyacetyl nitrate), formaldehyde, acrolein; **oxidising**; warm sunny weather; eye irritation |
| Ozone depletion | CFCs release Cl· in stratosphere (Cl· + O₃ → ClO· + O₂ chain); **Antarctic ozone hole** in spring (September–October) via polar stratospheric clouds; Montreal Protocol 1987 |
| Particulates | PM₁₀, PM₂.₅; smoke, dust, fly ash; Cottrell precipitator |
| Water quality | **BOD** (oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter): clean water < 5 ppm, highly polluted > 17 ppm; DO in good water ~5–6 ppm (fish need it); COD by K₂Cr₂O₇ |
| Eutrophication | Excess nitrates/phosphates (fertilisers, detergents) → algal bloom → O₂ depletion → fish die |
| Fluoride | ~1 ppm prevents tooth decay; > 2 ppm causes mottling of teeth (dental fluorosis); > 10 ppm harmful to bones (skeletal fluorosis) |
| Arsenic | Groundwater of the Gangetic plain including many **Bihar** districts (e.g. Bhojpur, Buxar, Patna, Vaishali, Bhagalpur); BIS/WHO limit **10 ppb (0.01 mg L⁻¹)**; causes arsenicosis, skin lesions, cancer |
| Nitrate | > 50 ppm in drinking water → methaemoglobinaemia (**blue-baby syndrome**) |
| Lead | Limit 50 ppb (old NCERT value); damages kidney, liver, nervous system |
| Disasters | **Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) — methyl isocyanate (MIC)**; Minamata disease — methylmercury (Japan); Itai-itai — cadmium (Japan) |
| Green chemistry | Reducing hazardous substances; e.g. liquid CO₂ for dry cleaning instead of tetrachloroethene, H₂O₂ for bleaching paper |

## Traps and exceptions

- **LDPE is branched; HDPE is linear.** LDPE uses free radicals at very high pressure; HDPE uses Ziegler–Natta.
- **Both PHBV and nylon-2–nylon-6 are biodegradable** — choose "more than one" when both appear.
- Glyptal (ethylene glycol + phthalic acid) and terylene (ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid) are both polyesters — note phthalic vs terephthalic.
- **Nylon is a polyamide**; terylene is a polyester; Bakelite is phenol–formaldehyde (cross-linked).
- Natural rubber is **cis**-polyisoprene; gutta-percha is trans.
- Neoprene monomer is chloroprene; Buna-N contains acrylonitrile; Buna-S contains styrene.
- Teflon is addition (not condensation); nylon-6 is from caprolactam by ring-opening.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing**; maltose and lactose are reducing.
- Cellulose has β-links; starch α-links.
- Glycine is the only achiral standard amino acid.
- DNA has thymine; RNA has uracil.
- **Vitamin B₁₂ contains cobalt**; it is water-soluble yet stored in liver.
- **Thyroxine and adrenaline are not steroids.**
- **Aspirin = acetylsalicylic acid**; methyl salicylate = oil of wintergreen.
- Aspartame is unstable on heating; sucralose is stable.
- Antiseptic vs disinfectant: 0.2% phenol antiseptic, 1% phenol disinfectant.
- Penicillin is bactericidal; chloramphenicol is bacteriostatic (broad spectrum).
- Classical smog is reducing; photochemical smog is oxidising.
- Normal rain is slightly acidic (~5.6); acid rain is below 5.6.

## Quick revision

1. Staudinger — macromolecules (Nobel 1953).
2. LDPE branched, high pressure, free radical; HDPE linear, Ziegler–Natta.
3. Teflon — CF₂=CF₂; PAN — acrylonitrile; PVC — vinyl chloride.
4. Natural rubber — cis-1,4-polyisoprene; vulcanisation with S (Goodyear 1839).
5. Neoprene — chloroprene; Buna-S — butadiene + styrene; Buna-N — butadiene + acrylonitrile.
6. Nylon-6,6 — hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid; nylon-6 — caprolactam.
7. Terylene — ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid; glyptal — ethylene glycol + phthalic acid.
8. Bakelite — phenol + formaldehyde, thermosetting.
9. PHBV and nylon-2–nylon-6 are biodegradable.
10. PDI = M̄w/M̄n; 1 for natural polymers.
11. Sucrose = glucose + fructose, non-reducing.
12. Lactose = galactose + glucose (milk sugar).
13. Invert sugar is laevorotatory.
14. Peptide bond –CO–NH–; n amino acids → n − 1 bonds.
15. α-Helix (keratin), β-pleated sheet (silk).
16. DNA: deoxyribose, A-T, G-C; RNA: ribose, uracil.
17. Vitamin B₁₂ — cobalt; deficiency pernicious anaemia.

20. Thyroxine — amino-acid derivative with iodine.
