This chapter is about **arithmetic**: how many questions, how many marks, how much you lose for a wrong answer, and when a guess is worth taking. Learn it now, so that in the exam hall you decide in two seconds, not twenty.

> **In simple words:** Right answer **+1**. Wrong answer **−1/3**. Darken **E** = 0 (you skipped). Leave all five bubbles empty = **−1/3** (treated like a wrong answer). A guess is worth it only when you have removed at least one wrong option and you really believe one of the rest.

> **Remember:** These rules are taken from the TRE 4.0 notice as reported on 23 Oct 2026. Confirm them in the official notice and on the cover page of your question booklet.

## 1. Stage 1 — Preliminary (screening) exam

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Paper | General Studies (one paper) |
| Questions / marks | 150 MCQs, 1 mark each = 150 marks |
| Time | 2 hours (120 minutes) → **48 seconds per question** |
| Topics | General science, current affairs, Indian history and national movement, geography, Indian polity, Indian economy, mental ability (reasoning + basic arithmetic), Bihar-specific GS |
| Negative marking | 1/3 per wrong answer |
| Purpose | **Screening only** — marks are not added to the final merit |
| Who goes to Mains | A number of top candidates decided by BPSC (check the notice for the ratio) |

> **Exam angle:** "Screening only" does not mean "easy". If BPSC calls only a few candidates per post to the Mains, the Prelims cut-off can still be high. Aim for a safe score, not the bare minimum — practise the GS chapters listed in [Language and General Studies](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/language-and-general-studies).

## 2. Stage 2 — Mains exam

| Part | Subject | Questions | Marks | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part I | Language (English and Hindi basics) | 30 | 30 | **Qualifying — minimum 30%** (9 marks out of 30) |
| Part II | General Studies | 40 | 40 | Counted in merit |
| Part III | **Zoology** | 80 | 80 | Counted in merit |
| **Total** | | **150** | **150** | **Merit = Part II + Part III = 120** |

Time: **2 hours 30 minutes (150 minutes)** for all three parts together — one minute per question on average. The parts are not timed separately; you choose how to split the time (see [Exam-Day Strategy](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/exam-day-strategy)).

<div class="formula">

| Merit formula |
|---|
| Merit score = (net marks in Part II) + (net marks in Part III) |
| Net marks in a part = (number right) − (number wrong) ÷ 3 − (rows left fully blank) ÷ 3 |
| Part I must be at least 30% (≥ 9 net out of 30), otherwise the rest is not counted |

</div>

> **In simple words:** Language only needs to be **passed**. GS 40 + Zoology 80 make your rank. Zoology alone is two-thirds of your merit.

### How the real TRE 2.0 Zoology booklet was laid out (same shape as TRE 4.0 Mains)

The TRE 2.0 Class 11–12 booklet "Language, General Studies and Zoology" (15 Dec 2023, Advt. 27/2023, Series A) shows exactly what a 30 + 40 + 80 paper looks like:

| Question numbers | Part | What was asked |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–8 | Part I English | Articles ("30 or 31 days in ___ month" → *a*; "My father is ___ engineer" → *an*), parts of a bedroom/room, seasons, the eighth month, sister-in-law, cousins |
| Q9–30 | Part I Hindi | संज्ञा, वचन, शब्दकोश में वर्ण-क्रम, विशेषण के भेद, शुद्ध/अशुद्ध वर्तनी, तत्सम–देशज–विदेशी शब्द, रूढ़ शब्द, अकर्मक क्रिया, 'कुरुक्षेत्र' के लेखक, हिंदी के उद्भव का काल, अविकारी शब्द |
| Q31–38 | Part II GS: arithmetic | Hall walls and painting cost, whole numbers vs integers, average of 7 consecutive numbers, condition for two real roots, work and time, algebra, probability of 53 Sundays, linear equation |
| Q39–46 | Part II GS: general science | Myopia, heredity → genetics, tin vs zinc coating, photosynthesis as reduction, printer dpi, trachoma (eyes), Newton's third law, neutrons |
| Q47–54 | Part II GS: current affairs | Twitter bird designer, *Oppenheimer* director, Commonwealth Games 2026 pull-out (Victoria), INDIA alliance full form, Chandrayaan-2 → Chandrayaan-3 message, Ireland PM, African Union chair |
| Q55–62 | Part II GS: geography | Physiographic units by area, Himalayan ranges north to south, cyclone latitudes, landslide areas, Coromandel coast cyclones, gully erosion, horticulture |
| Q63–70 | Part II GS: history (with Bihar) | Kunwar Singh in 1857, Partition of Bengal date, Quit India resolution date, Divide and Rule, Queen's Proclamation date, reformers, Dadabhai Naoroji, "Charter of Slavery" |
| Q71–150 | Part III Zoology | In NCERT order: digestion → respiration → blood and lymph → excretion → muscle → nerve and eye → hormones → reproduction → contraception and STDs → immunity, drugs, cancer, diseases → environment and biodiversity → cell, genetics, molecular biology → biotechnology → evolution |

> **Exam angle:** The language part was **very easy** (class 5–8 level), but only 8 of 30 questions were English; the other 22 were Hindi grammar and literature. Its only danger now is negative marking: you must still score 9 net. The GS part had **8 maths + 8 science** questions — 16 of 40 GS marks come from things a science graduate can do.

## 3. Negative marking — the basic rule

<div class="formula">

| Your action on a question | Marks |
|---|---|
| Darken the correct option | **+1** |
| Darken a wrong option | **−1/3** (−0.333) |
| Darken **E** ("question not attempted") | **0** |
| Leave all five bubbles empty | **−1/3** (same as wrong) |
| Darken two or more bubbles | treated as wrong → **−1/3** |

</div>

> **Common mistake:** Thinking "if I leave it blank I lose nothing". Under the TRE 4.0 rule (as in BPSC CCE since the 70th exam), a row with **no bubble at all** is penalised. To skip, you must darken **E**.

### Worked score examples

**Example 1 — the standard case (Part III, 80 zoology questions).** 62 right, 12 wrong, 6 marked E.

- Right: 62 × 1 = 62
- Wrong: 12 × (1/3) = 4 → subtract
- E: 6 × 0 = 0
- Net = 62 − 4 = **58 out of 80**

**Example 2 — the same paper, but the 6 skips left fully blank instead of E.**

- Net = 62 − 12/3 − 6/3 = 62 − 4 − 2 = **56**
- You lost **2 marks** only because you did not darken E. With a few hundred posts in a subject, 2 marks can be dozens of ranks.

**Example 3 — over-attempting.** 64 right, 16 wrong, 0 skipped.

- Net = 64 − 16/3 = 64 − 5.33 = **58.67**
- Compared with Example 1, the candidate got 2 more right but 4 more wrong, and gained only 0.67 mark. Extra attempts help only if your hit rate on them is above 25% (see Section 4).

**Example 4 — full merit.** Part II GS: 27 right, 6 wrong, 7 E → 27 − 2 = 25. Part III: 58 (Example 1).

- Merit = 25 + 58 = **83 out of 120** = 69.2%

**Example 5 — failing the language part by negative marking.** Part I: 10 right, 6 wrong, 14 E.

- Net = 10 − 6/3 = 10 − 2 = **8** → 8/30 = 26.7% → **below 30%: disqualified**, even if GS and Zoology are excellent.
- Same paper with 12 right, 6 wrong, 12 E → 12 − 2 = **10** → 33.3% → qualified.

> **Exam angle:** 22 of the 30 language questions in TRE 2.0 were Hindi. If your Hindi grammar (संधि, समास, तत्सम–तद्भव, वर्तनी) is rusty, practise it — you need just **9 net marks**, and twelve sure answers with zero wrong is safer than 25 attempts with 8 wrong.

**Example 6 — Prelims screening (150 questions).** 95 right, 30 wrong, 25 E.

- Net = 95 − 30/3 = 95 − 10 = **85 out of 150**

**Example 7 — the same zoology answers under the old TRE 1.0 penalty.** The TRE 1.0 Zoology booklet printed a **1/4** penalty. 62 right, 12 wrong → 62 − 12/4 = **59**. Under the TRE 4.0 rule the same answers give **58**. The new rule punishes a wrong answer about one-third more than the old printed rule.

<div class="formula">

| Quick mental formula |
|---|
| Net = R − W/3. Every 3 wrong answers cancel 1 right answer. |
| Net % of attempted = (R − W/3) ÷ (R + W) |
| If accuracy is a (fraction right among attempts) and you attempt N: Net = N × (a − (1 − a)/3) = N × (4a − 1)/3 |

</div>

Check with Example 1: N = 74, a = 62/74 ≈ 0.838 → 74 × (3.351 − 1)/3 = 74 × 2.351/3 ≈ **58** ✓

## 4. The expected-value maths of guessing

"Expected value" means the average marks you would get from a guess if you made the same kind of guess many times.

### Case A — four answer options (A, B, C, D) and E reserved for "not attempted"

This is the most likely TRE 4.0 format (it is the BPSC CCE format). If you guess among the options that remain after elimination, each remaining option is equally likely:

<div class="formula">

| Options left | Chance right p | Expected value = p × 1 − (1 − p) × 1/3 | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 (no elimination) | 1/4 | 1/4 − 3/4 × 1/3 = 1/4 − 1/4 = **0** | Mark **E** — no gain, only risk |
| 3 (one eliminated) | 1/3 | 1/3 − 2/3 × 1/3 = 3/9 − 2/9 = **+1/9 ≈ +0.11** | Small gain; guess only if the doubt is genuine |
| 2 (two eliminated) | 1/2 | 1/2 − 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/2 − 1/6 = **+1/3 ≈ +0.33** | **Guess** |
| 1 (you know it) | 1 | **+1** | Mark it |

</div>

**Break-even rule:** a guess helps when p − (1 − p)/3 > 0 → 3p > 1 − p → **p > 1/4**. So any time you honestly believe your chosen option has better than a 1-in-4 chance, the guess has positive expected value.

### Case B — five live options (the old TRE 1.0–3.0 style)

If a question still has five real answer choices and E is *not* a skip option, a blind guess is worse than zero:

| Options left | p | Expected value |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1/5 | 1/5 − 4/5 × 1/3 = 3/15 − 4/15 = **−1/15** |
| 4 | 1/4 | **0** |
| 3 | 1/3 | **+1/9** |
| 2 | 1/2 | **+1/3** |

### A 20-question example

Suppose in Part III you have 20 questions where you have cut it down to two options (a "50–50").

- Expected right = 10, expected wrong = 10
- Expected net = 10 − 10/3 = **+6.67 marks**
- If you marked E on all 20 instead: 0 marks

Now suppose instead the 20 questions are complete blanks (no idea at all, four options):

- Expected right = 5, expected wrong = 15 → 5 − 5 = **0**, and with bad luck you could lose 3–4 marks.

> **In simple words:** Two options left → guess. Four options left → E. Three options left → guess only if one option "feels" right for a real reason (a half-remembered line from NCERT, an enzyme you know belongs elsewhere, a ratio you can check).

### What the zoology keys say about "More than one" and "None"

Unlike the science papers of TRE 1.0/2.0, the zoology keys used D and E as real answers quite often:

| Paper (key) | Q range | A | B | C | D "More than one" | E "None" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRE 1.0 Zoology (BPSC 2nd provisional key) | 41–120 | 21 | 23 | 26 | **6** | **4** |
| TRE 2.0 Zoology (official final key) | 71–150 | 23 | 26 | 25 | **6** | **0** |
| **Both** | 160 Q | 44 | 49 | 51 | **12 (7.5%)** | **4 (2.5%)** |

> **Common mistake:** Treating "More than one of the above" as a safe guess. It was right only 12 times in 160 zoology questions, so as a blind guess it is about a 1-in-13 shot. Choose it only when you have **checked** that two content options are true — as in "hormones may be steroids / peptides / amino-acid derivatives" (TRE 2.0 Q98, official D). The full list is in [How Questions Are Asked](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/how-questions-are-asked).

## 5. The option-E rule in detail

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| You know the answer | Darken that option |
| You have it down to 2 options | Darken your better choice |
| You have no idea, or it is a 4-way guess | Darken **E** |
| You answered but now feel unsure | You **cannot** erase (the booklet forbids erasure or change). Leave it. This is why you must decide before you darken |
| End of exam, some rows still empty | Darken **E** in each one; never submit an empty row |

> **Remember:** Both TRE Zoology booklets said: "Any erasure or change is not allowed" and "If you mark more than one answer for one question, the answer will be treated as wrong." Expect the same in TRE 4.0. A changed answer = two bubbles = wrong = −1/3.

## 6. Qualifying marks

| Category | Minimum overall (reported) | If counted on the 120 merit marks |
|---|---|---|
| General (UR) | 40% | 48 |
| BC | 36.5% | 43.8 |
| EBC | 34% | 40.8 |
| SC / ST / women / PwD | 32% | 38.4 |
| Language Part I (all) | 30% of 30 | 9 |

> **Remember:** The notice decides whether these percentages apply to the 120 merit marks or to the full 150. The table assumes 120. Either way, qualifying is not selection: the **selection** score for Zoology will be far above these minimums.

## 7. What score should you aim for?

There is no official cut-off yet for the new pattern. A sensible target for a well-prepared M.Sc. candidate, adjusted for negative marking:

| Part | Target attempts | Target accuracy | Expected net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part I Language (30) | 22–26 | 95%+ | 20+ (only 9 needed) |
| Part II GS (40) | 30–34 | 85% | about 25–28 |
| Part III Zoology (80) | 68–74 | 88–92% | about 58–66 |
| **Merit (120)** | | | **about 85–92** |

> **Exam angle:** Zoology questions are mostly pure recall — you either know the fact or you don't. Moving accuracy from 80% to 90% on 72 attempts changes your net from 72 × (3.2 − 1)/3 = 52.8 to 72 × (3.6 − 1)/3 = 62.4 — **about 10 marks**. Accuracy beats speed. That is why every subject chapter ends with *Traps and exceptions*.

## Quick revision

- Prelims: 150 GS MCQs, 150 marks, 2 h, screening only, −1/3.
- Mains: 150 MCQs, 2 h 30 min; Part I Language 30 (qualifying 30%), Part II GS 40, Part III Zoology 80.
- TRE 2.0 Zoology booklet: English Q1–8, Hindi Q9–30, GS Q31–70, Zoology Q71–150.
- Merit = Part II + Part III = 120 marks.
- Right +1, wrong −1/3, E = 0, fully blank row = −1/3, two bubbles = wrong.
- Every 3 wrong answers cancel 1 right answer.
- Net = N × (4a − 1)/3 for N attempts at accuracy a.
- Guess with 4 options left: EV 0 → mark E. With 3 left: +1/9. With 2 left: +1/3 → guess.
- Five live options, blind guess: −1/15.
- Break-even: guess if your chance is above 1/4.
- 62 right, 12 wrong, 6 E → 58; same with 6 blank → 56.
- Language: need 9 net of 30; 10 right + 6 wrong = 8 → fail.
- Zoology keys: "More than one" correct 12 times, "None" 4 times in 160 questions.
- No erasing; a changed answer counts as two bubbles → wrong.
- Qualifying (reported): 40 / 36.5 / 34 / 32%; verify base (120 or 150).
