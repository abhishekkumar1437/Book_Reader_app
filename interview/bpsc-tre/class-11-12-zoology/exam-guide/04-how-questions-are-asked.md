Knowing a fact is one thing; recognising it inside a BPSC one-liner, with five options and a negative mark waiting, is another. This chapter shows the real shapes of TRE zoology questions so that nothing in the hall looks new. TRE 2.0 examples are quoted from the Class 11–12 Zoology paper of 15 December 2023 (Advt. 27/2023, Series A) with the **official answer** from BPSC's final key. TRE 1.0 examples are quoted from the paper of 26 August 2023 (2nd sitting, Advt. 26/2023, Series A) with the answer from the **BPSC key (2nd provisional)** — the latest key we have for that paper.

> **In simple words:** BPSC asks short, direct zoology questions: a fact, a "who/when", a "which is NOT", a ratio, a stage, a definition, a list where two options may be right, or a set of statements. Read every word, test each option separately, and remember that in zoology "More than one of the above" really was the answer 12 times in 160.

## 1. The answer-option pattern and what the keys tell us

Every zoology question in TRE 1.0 and 2.0 looked like this:

```
(A) …
(B) …
(C) …
(D) More than one of the above
(E) None of the above
```

So the "content" options were only A, B and C. D and E were logical options.

> **Remember:** In TRE 4.0, **E is reported to mean "question not attempted"** (the skip bubble, as in BPSC CCE). Then the paper will probably have A, B, C as content options and D as "More than one of the above", or four content options A–D. Read the cover page of your booklet before you start; the scoring is in [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRE 1.0 Zoology Q41–120 (2nd provisional key) | 80 | 21 | 23 | 26 | **6** | **4** | D: 63, 79, 84, 90, 95, 111 · E: 50, 73, 82, 87 |
| TRE 2.0 Zoology Q71–150 (final key) | 80 | 23 | 26 | 25 | **6** | **0** | D: 98, 106, 109, 114, 119, 131 |

| TRE 2.0 GS Q31–70 (final key) | 40 | 9 | 13 | 14 | 3 | 1 | D: 36, 45, 68 · E: 70 |
| TRE 1.0 GS Q1–40 (2nd provisional key) | 40 | 20 | 8 | 9 | 1 | 2 | D: 15 · E: 20, 37 |

> **Exam angle:** In zoology, D or E was correct in **16 of 160** questions (10%) — far more than in the Class 9–10 science papers, where "More than one" was never correct in TRE 2.0. A, B and C were used almost equally (44, 49, 51), so there is **no favourite letter** to guess. In TRE 2.0, E ("None") never appeared in zoology; in TRE 1.0 it appeared 4 times, always in questions where all three content options are deliberately wrong (simple protein, mitochondrial outer-membrane enzyme, viruses, criticisms of Darwin).

> **Common mistake:** Choosing "More than one of the above" as a safe-looking answer when unsure. It is a claim that **at least two** options are correct. If only one is correct you lose 1/3.

## 2. The question types (with real examples)

### Type 1 — Direct fact

The most common type: one NCERT line or one standard B.Sc. fact.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q71 asked: "HCl secreting cells of the gastric (glands) are (A) zymogen cells (B) goblet cells (C) oxyntic cells". **Official answer: C.** Oxyntic (parietal) cells secrete HCl and intrinsic factor; chief (zymogen/peptic) cells secrete pepsinogen; goblet cells secrete mucus. See [Digestion and Nutrition](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/digestion-and-nutrition).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q74 asked: "Green colour of bile is due to (A) biliverdin (B) bilirubin (C) bile salts". **Official answer: A.** Biliverdin is green; bilirubin is yellow-orange; bile salts emulsify fat and have no colour role.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q43 asked: "Chromosomes found in the salivary gland of *Drosophila* are (A) lampbrush (B) supernumerary (C) polytene". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** Polytene chromosomes are giant chromosomes made of many DNA copies lying side by side (repeated replication without division); lampbrush chromosomes occur in growing oocytes (diplotene of meiosis). See [Chromosomes, Linkage, Sex Determination and Disorders](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/chromosomes-linkage-sex-determination-and-disorders).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q88 asked: "Which of the following liver cells phagocytizes dead and inactive RBCs? (A) Hepatocytes (B) Kupffer's cells (C) Sinusoids". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** Kupffer cells are macrophages lining the liver sinusoids. Sinusoids are blood channels, not cells.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q122 asked: "Malaria is transmitted by (A) female *Culex* (B) male *Anopheles* (C) female *Anopheles*". **Official answer: C.** Only females suck blood; *Culex* carries filaria, *Aedes* carries dengue. See [Human Diseases, Parasites and Vectors](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/human-diseases-parasites-and-vectors).

### Type 2 — Who, when, which book

About ten of the 160 questions were history of biology. One page of "X — gave — Y — year" pays for itself.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q48 asked for the book in which Darwin published his theory: (A) *The Evolution of Species* (B) *On the Evolution of Species by Means of Natural Selection* (C) *On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** TRE 2.0 Q150 then asked the year: **official answer B, 1859**. See [Origin of Life and Evolution](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/origin-of-life-and-evolution).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q108 asked: "Who proposed the chromosome theory of inheritance? (A) Watson and Crick (B) Walter Sutton and Theodor Boveri (C) T. H. Morgan". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** Sutton and Boveri (1902–03) linked Mendel's factors to chromosomes; Morgan later gave the experimental proof with *Drosophila* and discovered linkage.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q103 asked: "The world's first test tube baby born in 1978 was (A) Louise Brown (B) Kanupriya Agarwal (C) Robert Edwards". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): A.** Louise Brown, born 25 July 1978 in England; Robert Edwards was the scientist (Nobel Prize 2010). Kanupriya Agarwal ("Durga"), born 3 October 1978 in Kolkata, is regarded as India's first IVF baby. See [Reproductive Health and Contraception](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/reproductive-health-and-contraception).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q129 asked: "Who is the father of ecology and environmental laws in India? (A) Ramdeo Misra (B) W. H. Pearsall (C) Indira Gandhi". **Official answer: A.** NCERT Class 12 introduces Professor Ramdeo Misra as the father of ecology in India. See [Ecosystem Structure and Energy Flow](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/ecosystem-structure-and-energy-flow).

### Type 3 — "Which is NOT / which is wrongly matched"

The word **not** flips the question.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q41 asked: "Which of the following is not a correct match? (A) Sertoli cells : Nutritive (B) Corona radiata : Sperm (C) Leydig cells : Testosterone". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** The corona radiata is the layer of follicle cells around the **ovum**, not the sperm. See [Human Reproductive System and Gametogenesis](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/human-reproductive-system-and-gametogenesis).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q113 asked which sexually transmitted disease does not specifically affect the sex organs: (A) Syphilis (B) AIDS (C) Gonorrhoea. **Official answer: B.** HIV destroys helper T-cells everywhere in the body; syphilis and gonorrhoea first attack the genital tract.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q84 asked: "Which of the following is not the type of secondary lysosomes? (A) Phagosomes (B) Residual bodies (C) Granules". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): D (More than one of the above).** Secondary lysosomes are lysosomes that have fused with material: heterophagic (digestive) vacuoles, autophagic vacuoles and residual bodies. A plain phagosome (before fusion) and storage granules (primary lysosomes) are not secondary lysosomes — two "not" options, so D. See [Cell Organelles and Cytoskeleton](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/cell-organelles-and-cytoskeleton).

> **Memory trick:** When you read "not", circle it. Then mark each option **T** (is) or **F** (is not). One F → that option. Two F's → "More than one of the above". No F → "None of the above".

### Type 4 — Ratios and genetics reasoning

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q44 asked: "In the F2 generation, 9 : 7 ratio represents (A) incomplete dominance (B) codominance (C) epistasis". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** 9 : 7 is complementary gene action (duplicate recessive epistasis): both dominant genes are needed for the trait, so 3 + 3 + 1 = 7 lack it. See [Mendelian Genetics and Its Extensions](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/mendelian-genetics-and-its-extensions).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q55 asked: "If both genotype and phenotype show the same ratio of 1 : 2 : 1 in the F2 generation, it shows (A) incomplete dominance in a monohybrid cross (B) complete dominance in a monohybrid cross (C) codominance". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): A.** Snapdragon red × white → pink. (Codominance — for example the AB blood group — also gives 1 : 2 : 1 for both; the key accepted only A, the textbook example.)

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q45 asked: father affected, mother normal, only the daughters inherit the disease and not the sons — the type of inheritance is (A) sex-linked dominant (B) sex-linked recessive (C) autosomal recessive. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): A.** A father passes his X to every daughter and his Y to every son. If every daughter is affected from one affected father, the gene is on the X and **dominant**. See [Chromosomes, Linkage, Sex Determination and Disorders](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/chromosomes-linkage-sex-determination-and-disorders).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q59 asked which two traits are characteristic of a single gene: (A) seed colour and shape (B) flower colour and position (C) colour of flower and seed coat. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** Mendel noticed that violet flowers always came with grey seed coats and white flowers with white seed coats — one gene, two effects (**pleiotropy**). A and B are pairs of Mendel's independent characters.

### Type 5 — Small numericals

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q52 asked how many ova and sperms are produced from 100 secondary oocytes and 100 secondary spermatocytes: (A) 50 ova and 100 sperms (B) 100 ova and 100 sperms (C) 100 ova and 200 sperms. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** Each secondary oocyte completes meiosis II to give **1 ovum + 1 polar body** → 100 ova. Each secondary spermatocyte gives **2 spermatids** → 2 sperms → 200 sperms. See [Human Reproductive System and Gametogenesis](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/human-reproductive-system-and-gametogenesis).

### Type 6 — Stage or site

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q86 asked: "Synapsis takes place during (A) leptotene (B) zygotene (C) pachytene". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** Homologues pair (synapsis, synaptonemal complex) in zygotene; crossing over follows in pachytene — asked in TRE 1.0 Q53 [A] and TRE 2.0 Q133 [official B, pachytene]. See [Cell Cycle, Mitosis and Meiosis](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/cell-cycle-mitosis-and-meiosis).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q96 asked: "In which region of the fallopian tube fertilization takes place? (A) Infundibulum (B) Ampulla (C) Isthmus". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** TRE 2.0 Q107 asked the same at NCERT level: **official answer C, fallopian tube**. See [Menstrual Cycle, Fertilisation and Pregnancy](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/menstrual-cycle-fertilisation-and-pregnancy).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q89 asked where useful substances are reabsorbed from the glomerular filtrate: (A) proximal convoluted tubule (B) distal convoluted tubule (C) loop of Henle. **Official answer: A.** The PCT reabsorbs nearly all glucose and amino acids and about 70–80% of water and electrolytes. See [Excretion and Osmoregulation](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/excretion-and-osmoregulation).

### Type 7 — Name the term

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q143 asked: "During gene cloning, which is called 'gene taxi'? (A) Vaccine (B) Plasmid (C) Bacteria". **Official answer: B.** A vector (plasmid) carries the foreign gene into the host cell. TRE 2.0 Q144 (restriction endonuclease = "biological scissors", official C) and Q146 (radioactive tagged DNA/RNA = probe, official B) are the same type. See [Biotechnology Principles and Tools](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/biotechnology-principles-and-tools).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q97 asked: "Jumping movement of nerve conduction is (A) active phase (B) saltatory conduction (C) neurotransmission". **Official answer: B.** In myelinated fibres the impulse jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next. See [Nervous System and Sense Organs](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/nervous-system-and-sense-organs).

### Type 8 — Lists where two options are right (answer D)

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q98 asked: "By chemical nature, hormones may be (A) steroids (B) peptides (C) amino acid derivatives". **Official answer: D.** All three are true (cortisol, insulin, thyroxine/adrenaline). "May be" in the stem is the clue that more than one fits. See [Endocrine System and Hormones](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/endocrine-system-and-hormones).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q106 asked: "Menstrual cycle is controlled by (A) oestrogen and progesterone of ovary (B) FSH and LH of pituitary (C) FSH of pituitary". **Official answer: D.** Pituitary gonadotropins and ovarian steroids together control the cycle.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q109 asked: "Spermatogenesis and sperm differentiation occur under the control of (A) FSH (B) LH (C) parahormone". **Official answer: D.** LH drives testosterone from Leydig cells; FSH acts on Sertoli cells. Both are needed.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q119 asked: "AIDS is transmitted by (A) sexual intercourse (B) blood transfusion (C) placental transfusion". **Official answer: D.** All three routes are real (mother to child across the placenta).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q131 asked: "Which one of the following is constituent of cell membrane? (A) Cholesterol (B) Glycolysis (C) Phospholipid". **Official answer: D.** Cholesterol and phospholipid both are; glycolysis is a process, not a substance — an obvious distractor. See [Cell Membrane and Transport](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/cell-membrane-and-transport).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q90 asked: "Each actin (thin) filament is made up of (A) two F actins (B) two filaments of tropomyosin (C) troponin". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): D.** NCERT: each thin filament is two F-actin strands helically wound, with two tropomyosin filaments in the grooves and troponin at intervals. See [Muscles, Skeleton and Movement](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/muscles-skeleton-and-movement).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q79 asked: "Facilitated diffusion differs from simple diffusion by (A) concentration gradient (B) stereospecific (C) requirement of a carrier". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): D.** Both use the concentration gradient (so A is not a difference); facilitated diffusion alone needs a carrier protein that is specific (stereospecific) for its molecule.

### Type 9 — All three wrong (answer E)

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q87 asked: "Which of the following is an example of a simple protein? (A) Thyroglobulin (B) Haemoglobin (C) Caeruloplasmin". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): E.** All three are **conjugated** proteins: thyroglobulin is a glycoprotein, haemoglobin a chromoprotein (haem), caeruloplasmin a copper-containing metalloprotein (also glycosylated). Albumins and globulins of the simple kind, histones and protamines are simple proteins. See [Biomolecules and Enzymes](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/biomolecules-and-enzymes).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q82 asked which enzyme is found on the outer membrane of mitochondria: (A) ATPase (B) choline dehydrogenase (C) cytochrome oxidase. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): E.** The marker enzyme of the outer membrane is **monoamine oxidase**; ATP synthase (F₀F₁ ATPase) and cytochrome oxidase are inner-membrane enzymes. (The question's wording "outer membrane of cristae" is confused — cristae are folds of the inner membrane — but either way none of the three is an outer-membrane enzyme.)

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q50 asked: "In which aspects, Darwin's theory is wrong? (A) High efficiency of reproduction (B) Origin of species (C) Survival of the fittest". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): E.** All three are parts of Darwin's theory that still stand. The accepted criticism is that Darwin could not explain the **origin and inheritance of variations** (he did not know Mendel's work).

### Type 10 — Statements true or false

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q63 asked which statement is false: (A) Every honeybee colony comprises a single queen. (B) The drone bees are female and do not have a sting. (C) Both workers and drones develop from unfertilized eggs. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): D.** A is true. B is false (drones are **male**). C is false (workers come from **fertilised** eggs; only drones are parthenogenetic). Two false → D. See [Economic Zoology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/economic-zoology).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q65 asked which statement is true: (A) All immunogens are antigens but all antigens are not immunogens. (B) All immunogens are antigens and all antigens are immunogens. (C) All immunogens are not antigens but all antigens are immunogens. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): A.** An antigen is anything that binds an antibody; an immunogen is an antigen that can also **provoke** an immune response by itself. A hapten is an antigen but not an immunogen. See [Immunology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/immunology).

### Type 11 — Arrange in order and choose the code

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q115 asked to arrange the ingredients of poultry feed for layers in decreasing percentage: 1. decorticated groundnut cake 2. barley 3. polished rice 4. yellow maize 5. corn gluten. Codes: (A) 4, 3, 2, 1, 5 (B) 4, 3, 1, 2, 5 (C) 3, 4, 1, 2, 5. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): A.** Maize (the main energy grain) comes first and is always the largest share; a protein supplement like corn gluten is the smallest. Learn one standard layer ration table from an animal-husbandry text; such questions cannot be reasoned out fully.

> **Common mistake:** In code questions, "More than one of the above" means more than one **code** is correct — almost impossible when the codes are different orders.

### Type 12 — Bihar and applied zoology

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q74 asked in which state the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary is located: (A) Uttar Pradesh (B) Bihar (C) West Bengal. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): B.** It is a 50-km stretch of the Ganga between Sultanganj and Kahalgaon in Bhagalpur district, notified in 1991. See [Biodiversity, Conservation and Wildlife of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/biodiversity-conservation-and-wildlife-of-bihar).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q116 asked: "Which of the following is the largest honeybee? (A) *Apis florea* (B) *Apis indica* (C) *Apis dorsata*". **BPSC key (2nd provisional): C.** *A. dorsata* is the rock (giant) bee; *A. florea* is the little bee; *A. indica* (*A. cerana indica*) is the Indian hive bee.

### Assertion–reason and match-the-list

Neither zoology paper used **assertion–reason** or **match List-I with List-II** questions, but BPSC uses both in its other exams (CCE), and TRE 1.0 Q41 was a one-line version of matching. If they appear:

| Style | Method |
|---|---|
| Assertion (A) and Reason (R) | Judge A alone (true/false), then R alone. Only if both are true ask: does R **explain** A? |
| Match the lists | Start with the pair you are surest of; it usually removes two codes. Then check one more pair |
| "Which of the statements is/are correct" with 1, 2, 3 | Judge each statement alone, write T/F, then find the code |

## 3. Keys that look doubtful — and what to do about them

The rule in this collection: give the key's answer, then explain the disagreement. TRE 2.0 answers are **final**; TRE 1.0 answers are from the **2nd provisional** key, and the final key may have changed some of them.

| Question | Key | Why it looks doubtful | Lesson for the exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRE 1.0 Q73: "Which is not an RNA virus? (A) Epstein–Barr (B) Varicella–Zoster (C) Herpes simplex" | **E** | All three are herpesviruses with **double-stranded DNA**, so all three are "not RNA viruses" — logically D. The key apparently read it as "none of these is an RNA virus" | When all three options share the property asked, BPSC sometimes keys E instead of D. Read the Hindi version too |
| TRE 1.0 Q94: hormone of the adenohypophysis that controls the seminiferous tubules — (A) LH (B) FSH (C) prolactin | **A** | FSH acts directly on Sertoli cells **inside** the tubules (NCERT); LH acts on Leydig cells **between** the tubules, and their testosterone is needed for spermatogenesis | Expect B in most textbooks; know both actions |
| TRE 1.0 Q47: "A chromosome with a centromere near the middle is called (A) metacentric (B) submetacentric (C) acrocentric" | **B** | "At the middle" = metacentric; "near (slightly away from) the middle" = submetacentric. The key chose the strict reading | Read every word: *at*, *near*, *close to the end*, *at the end* |
| TRE 1.0 Q46: "DNA replication is affected (effected) by an enzyme known as (A) ligase (B) polymerase I (C) ribonuclease" | **B** | Ligase also works in replication (joins Okazaki fragments), so D could be argued; the key wants the polymerase | When one option is "the" enzyme of the process, the key usually takes it |
| TRE 1.0 Q114: "Autoantibody DNA fingerprinting was developed by (A) Alec Jeffreys (B) A. K. Francoeur (C) R. Erickson" | **B** | DNA fingerprinting (1984–85) is Alec Jeffreys. The word "autoantibody" seems to refer to *antibody fingerprinting*, a 1988 method published by A. M. Francoeur — as far as we can tell, that is why the key chose B | One unusual word in the stem can change the answer. If you know only Jeffreys, A is the natural choice; the key disagrees |
| TRE 2.0 Q80: "Krebs cycle is important for (A) oxidation of carbohydrates (B) oxidation of amino acids (C) production of ATP by oxidative phosphorylation" | **C** | The Krebs cycle is the common final pathway for carbohydrates, fats and amino acids, so D also looks right; the key stressed the cycle's NADH/FADH₂ feeding ATP synthesis | Final key — accept C, but notice the setter's view: "importance" = energy (ATP) |
| TRE 2.0 Q128: "What type of chemical weathering is enhanced by acid rain? (A) Hydrolysis (B) Dissolution (C) Oxidation" | **A** | Many geography texts would say dissolution/carbonation of limestone; the key chose hydrolysis (H⁺ attacking silicate minerals) | A geography question inside zoology — do not spend time on it |

> **Remember:** If a TRE 4.0 question looks ambiguous, answer what the **NCERT line** says, then raise an objection with evidence during the answer-key challenge window. Keep a list of such questions on the day.

## 4. Reading habits that save marks

| Habit | Example of the trap it avoids |
|---|---|
| Circle **not**, **false**, **incorrect**, **except** | Q41 (T1), Q63 (T1), Q84 (T1), Q113 (T2) |
| Look for "may be", "is/are", plural "hormones/proteins" — signals for D | Q98, Q106, Q109, Q119 (T2) |
| Check whether all three options share the property → think D or E | Q73, Q87 (T1) |
| Spot near-identical words | renin/rennin, *Culex/Anopheles/Aedes*, ampulla/isthmus, metacentric/submetacentric, antigen/immunogen, polytene/lampbrush |
| Watch "secondary" vs "primary" | Secondary oocyte (1 ovum) vs primary oocyte; secondary vs primary lysosome |
| Who vs what: scientist vs company vs product | Eli Lilly (company, Q61) vs insulin (product, Q62) vs Robert Edwards (scientist) vs Louise Brown (baby) |
| Read the Hindi version if the English looks odd | Q82 (T1) wording "outer membrane of cristae" |

> **Remember:** Questions are printed in English and Hindi. If the two versions seem to differ, the cover page or the notice states which version is final — read that line before the exam starts.

## Quick revision

- Old pattern: (A)(B)(C) + (D) More than one of the above + (E) None of the above.
- TRE 4.0: E reported as "not attempted"; read the cover page.
- Zoology keys: D correct 12 times (T1: 63, 79, 84, 90, 95, 111; T2: 98, 106, 109, 114, 119, 131), E 4 times (T1: 50, 73, 82, 87).
- A, B, C used almost equally (44, 49, 51): no favourite letter.
- Types: fact, who/when, NOT/wrong match, ratios, numericals, stage/site, term, D-lists, E-cases, statements, arrange-code, Bihar/applied.
- 9 : 7 = epistasis (complementary genes); 1 : 2 : 1 for both = incomplete dominance.
- Father → all daughters affected = X-linked dominant.
- 100 secondary oocytes → 100 ova; 100 secondary spermatocytes → 200 sperms.
- Synapsis zygotene; crossing over pachytene.
- Simple protein: none of thyroglobulin, haemoglobin, caeruloplasmin.
- Doubtful keys: T1 Q73 (E), Q94 (LH), Q47 (submetacentric), Q114 (Francoeur); T2 Q80 (C), Q128 (hydrolysis).
- "May be"/"is/are" in the stem → check for D.
