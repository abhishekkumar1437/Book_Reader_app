BPSC does not print a detailed chapter list for the TRE subject paper. It says the questions are of the standard expected of a post-graduate teacher in the subject. So we decode the syllabus from two sources: (1) the NCERT Class 11–12 Biology books that a Class 11–12 zoology teacher must teach, plus the standard B.Sc./M.Sc. zoology courses, and (2) the **actual questions** BPSC asked in TRE 1.0 and TRE 2.0. Section 1 is the topic list. Section 2 is the blueprint — every zoology question of both papers counted by book and by chapter of this collection.

> **In simple words:** About **two-thirds** of the zoology questions come straight from NCERT Class 11–12 Biology (the animal half: physiology, reproduction, health, genetics, biotechnology, ecology, evolution). About **one-third** are standard B.Sc./M.Sc. points (polytene chromosomes, 9 : 7 epistasis, unit membrane thickness, Golgi cisternae, secondary lysosomes, Kupffer cells, Cushing's syndrome, type B spermatogonia, cortical reaction, qRT-PCR, apiculture, poultry feed). Every question is a one-liner. You need breadth and accuracy, not long answers.

> **Remember:** Check the official TRE 4.0 syllabus PDF on [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in) when it is released. If it lists a topic missing below, add it to your notes under the nearest chapter.

**Level key used below:** **11–12** = NCERT Class 11–12 Biology · **B.Sc.** = standard graduation/post-graduation zoology beyond NCERT. Chapter links point to this collection.

## 2. The PYQ blueprint

### 2.1 Method

We took the two zoology papers we have in full:

- **TRE 1.0** (26 Aug 2023, 2nd sitting, Advt. 26/2023), Class 11–12 "Paper-4: General Studies and Zoology", Part II Zoology: **Q41–120** (80 questions), Series A, with BPSC's **2nd provisional answer key** (no final key was available to us).
- **TRE 2.0** (15 Dec 2023, Advt. 27/2023), Class 11–12 "Language, General Studies and Zoology", Part III Zoology: **Q71–150** (80 questions), Series A, with BPSC's **official final answer key**.

Each question was placed in the **one** chapter of this collection that teaches it. A few could fit two chapters — for example, DNA fingerprinting (taught in molecular biology and in biotechnology) is counted under biotechnology applications, and reproductive hormones are counted under reproduction when the question is about a reproductive event, and under endocrine when it is about the gland. Key answers are shown in brackets. A few lines of the TRE 1.0 booklet are garbled in our copy, but every question's topic is clear.

In the tables, **T1** = TRE 1.0 and **T2** = TRE 2.0; † marks a question we judge to be beyond NCERT (B.Sc./M.Sc. level).

## 3. Conclusions on weightage

1. **Four big books, one small.** Cell–genetics (25%), physiology (25%), reproduction–health (22.5%) and ecology–biotech–applied (22.5%) carry the paper. The [90-Day Plan](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/roadmap/how-the-plan-works) gives them 13, 11, 10 and 10 of the 52 study days, and animal diversity 8 (one day per chapter).
2. **Genetics is the single biggest chapter cluster.** Mendelism + chromosomes + molecular biology + regulation = **23 of 160** questions. Learn every ratio (3 : 1, 1 : 2 : 1, 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, 9 : 7, 12 : 3 : 1, 9 : 3 : 4, 13 : 3, 15 : 1) and every "X — gave — Y" fact (Sutton–Boveri, Morgan, Mendel, Jacob–Monod).
3. **Human physiology is NCERT Class 11, line by line.** TRE 2.0 asked 34 questions in NCERT chapter order. Every enzyme, cell type, hormone and its gland, and every nephron segment is fair game.
4. **Reproduction is a B.Sc. favourite.** 17 questions on the reproductive system, gametogenesis and the cycle — including M.Sc.-level anatomy (Bartholin glands, mesovarium, epididymal secretions, spermatogonia types).
5. **Biotechnology = 14 questions.** Tools (enzymes, vectors, probes, PCR types) and applications (insulin, Bt cotton, Dolly, fingerprinting) with names and years.
6. **Bihar angle:** Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary (Bhagalpur) appeared in the zoology part, not in GS. Know Bihar's protected areas and state animal/bird.
7. **History of biology:** Linnaeus, Darwin 1859, Sutton–Boveri, Schleiden–Schwann, Jenner, Eli Lilly 1983, Louise Brown 1978, Ramdeo Misra — about 10 questions came from "who/when". Keep a single "X — discovered by — year" page.
8. **Expect D and E answers.** 16 of 160 key answers were "More than one" or "None". Test every option separately (see [How Questions Are Asked](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/how-questions-are-asked)).
