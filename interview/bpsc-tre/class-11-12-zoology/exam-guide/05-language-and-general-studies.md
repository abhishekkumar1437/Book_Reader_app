Zoology gives you 80 of the 120 merit marks, but two other things decide whether those 80 marks ever count: the **Prelims GS screening** (you must clear it to sit the Mains) and **Part I Language** (you must score at least 30%). Part II GS then adds 40 marks to your merit. This chapter tells you exactly what to study for each, and links to the chapters on this shelf that teach it.

> **In simple words:** Language = pass it safely with only sure answers. GS = 40 merit marks + the whole Prelims; give it 1.5 hours every day. As an M.Sc. Zoology candidate you already know the biology part of general science; spend GS time on maths, history, geography, polity, Bihar, current affairs — and on the school **physics and chemistry** you may have forgotten.

## 1. Part I — Language (30 questions, qualifying)

### 1.1 What it is

- 30 MCQs in the Mains; **qualifying only** — you need **30%**, i.e. **9 net marks** out of 30.
- Marks are **not** added to merit.
- In the TRE 2.0 Class 11–12 Zoology booklet the section had **8 English questions (Q1–8) and 22 Hindi questions (Q9–30)**. Check the TRE 4.0 notice for the exact split and for whether the Indian-language part can be Hindi, Urdu or Bangla.
- In TRE 1.0 the language test was a **separate qualifying paper** (we do not have it).
- Level: **Class 5–10**. The English questions were very easy; the Hindi questions included grammar **and** a little Hindi literature.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q1 asked: "There are 30 or 31 days in ___ month. (A) the (B) a (C) an". **Official answer: B.** "A month" — any month, not a particular one; "month" begins with a consonant sound.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q2 asked: "My father is ___ engineer in the Water Works Department. (A) the (B) an (C) a". **Official answer: B** — *an* before a vowel **sound**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q7 asked: "Sister-in-law is (A) husband's sister (B) wife's sister (C) brother's wife". **Official answer: D (More than one of the above)** — all three are sisters-in-law. Q8 "Cousins are (A) maternal (B) paternal (C) siblings" was also **D** (cousins can be on the mother's or the father's side; siblings are brothers and sisters, not cousins).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q6 asked: "The eighth month of the year is (A) July (B) September (C) October". **Official answer: E (None of the above)** — it is August.

> **Exam angle:** Four of the eight English questions in TRE 2.0 had **D** as the answer (Q3, 4, 7, 8) and one had **E** (Q6). In everyday-vocabulary questions BPSC likes lists where several items fit. Test each option.

### 1.2 English topics (Class-10 grammar)

| Topic | What to know | Typical question |
|---|---|---|
| Articles | a/an by sound (an hour, a university, an MLA, an engineer), *the* for specific/unique things, superlatives, rivers, oceans | Fill in the blank (TRE 2.0 Q1–2) |
| Tenses | 12 tenses, *since/for*, present perfect vs past simple | Fill in the blank |
| Subject–verb agreement | each/every/neither + singular; "one of the + plural noun + singular verb" | Spot the error |
| Prepositions | in/on/at, *between/among*, *by/with*, fixed prepositions (*fond of, afraid of, good at*) | Fill in |
| Conjunctions | *although/but*, *unless*, *no sooner…than*, *hardly…when* | Fill in / error |
| Voice and narration | Active ↔ passive; direct ↔ indirect speech | Choose the correct form |
| Vocabulary | Synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution, common idioms, spelling | Choose the meaning |
| Everyday words | Days, months, seasons (rainy season comes before winter — TRE 2.0 Q5, official A), relations, parts of a house | TRE 2.0 Q3–8 |

> **Memory trick:** **Maternal** = mother's side, **paternal** = father's side. Husband's/wife's sister and brother's wife are all **sister-in-law**.

### 1.4 Qualifying strategy under negative marking

1. Do Part I **first, but never in a hurry**. It takes 10–15 minutes.
2. Mark only answers you are **sure** of. You need just 9 net. With 15 sure answers and 0 wrong you are safely through.
3. Darken **E** for every doubtful question. A wrong answer costs 1/3, and here an extra mark gives you nothing (it is qualifying).
4. Example: 14 right, 3 wrong, 13 E → 14 − 1 = **13** (43%) ✓. Example: 11 right, 9 wrong → 11 − 3 = **8** ✗ — failed by greed.

> **Common mistake:** Practising nothing for language because "it is easy". With 22 Hindi questions, a candidate who has not opened a Hindi grammar since class 10 can easily be unsure of half of them. Do one 30-question language set every week (the [90-Day Plan](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/roadmap/how-the-plan-works) has a language slot).

> **Exam angle:** Both Zoology booklets used the same **8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8** recipe: maths, general science, current affairs/culture, geography, history. About **16 of 40** GS questions (maths + science) are "home ground" for a science post-graduate. Polity and economy were absent from these two GS parts but are in the Prelims syllabus.

> **Common mistake:** Skipping the physics and chemistry part of general science because you are a zoologist. In these two papers, **8 of the 16 science GS questions** were physics or chemistry (light flux, Bernoulli, white lead, atomic radius, tin vs zinc, reduction, Newton's third law, neutrons). Use the Class 9–10 Science books listed below.

## 4. How much time to give

| Period | GS + Language per day | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–60 of the plan | 1.5 h | One GS chapter or maths/reasoning topic per day + 20 min current affairs; one language session a week (Hindi and English on alternate weeks, Hindi more often) |
| Days 61–90 | 1.5–2 h | GS question banks, current-affairs revision, one Prelims-pattern GS mock per week |
| If the Prelims date is announced before the Mains | 3 h for the last 3 weeks before Prelims | Shift zoology time to GS temporarily; see [How the Plan Works](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/roadmap/how-the-plan-works) |

> **Exam angle:** Daily **20 minutes of current affairs** is more effective than a 10-hour crash course at the end. Write 5 lines a day in a current-affairs notebook under fixed heads: national, Bihar, international, science and environment, sports and awards, days and reports.
