You do not need a shelf of books. You need **one main source per topic**, read several times, plus a system that makes facts stay in memory until exam day. This chapter gives the minimum book list and the system: one-line notes, spaced revision, mocks and an error log.

> **In simple words:** This collection is your main text. Use NCERT Class 11–12 Biology to fill gaps — every line of it can be a question — and open the standard M.Sc. zoology books only for the deeper points the PYQs touched. Write every fact you get wrong into an error log and revise it again and again. Revision, not new reading, wins this exam.

## 1. The book list

### 1.1 Rule of one source

| Need | Main source | Use other books for |
|---|---|---|
| Zoology teaching | The five zoology books in this collection | Diagrams and exact NCERT lines when a chapter sends you there |
| NCERT-level facts | **NCERT Biology Class 11 and Class 12** (zoology chapters) | The base of about two-thirds of the questions |
| B.Sc./M.Sc. points | The B.Sc. sections of each chapter here | One standard text per area, only for topics the PYQs touched |
| GS | BPSC CCE shelf on this app (history, polity, geography, economy, Bihar, current affairs, mental ability) + quant book + Class 9–10 Science books for physics/chemistry | One GK book for quick facts |
| PYQs | The official question papers and answer keys on [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in) | — |

> **Common mistake:** Reading your old M.Sc. notes cover to cover. They are long, theory-heavy and written for descriptive answers. TRE asks one-line facts. Finish this collection once, then revise it — do not start a new book in the last 30 days.

> **Remember:** NCERT removed some chapters in its 2022–23 "rationalised" editions — as far as we know these include *Digestion and Absorption* (Class 11), *Environmental Issues* and *Strategies for Enhancement in Food Production* (Class 12); check your copy. **BPSC still asks them**: TRE 2.0 had 7 digestion questions (Q71–77) and 4 environmental-issues questions, and TRE 1.0 had 4 apiculture/fisheries/poultry questions. If your NCERT lacks a chapter, read an older edition (free on the NCERT website) or rely on the chapter in this collection.

> **Exam angle:** Do **not** read these references cover to cover. Open them only for the topics marked † in [Syllabus Decoded and PYQ Blueprint](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/syllabus-decoded-and-pyq-blueprint). For example, open Verma–Agarwal only for membrane models, organelle details, chromosome types and gene interactions; open Guyton only for muscle, kidney and endocrine disorders.

## 2. How to make notes

### 2.1 One-line, question-ready notes

Each chapter in this collection already ends with *Quick revision* and *Traps and exceptions*. Your own notes should only add what you **did not know** or **got wrong**. Write them so that each line can become an MCQ:

| Weak note (do not write) | Strong note (write) |
|---|---|
| "Golgi has many cisternae." | "Golgi stack: about 3–8 cisternae (TRE 1.0 key: 3–8)." |
| "Epistasis changes ratios." | "9 : 7 complementary · 12 : 3 : 1 dominant epistasis · 9 : 3 : 4 recessive epistasis · 15 : 1 duplicate · 13 : 3 inhibitory." |
| "Kupffer cells are in the liver." | "Kupffer cells = liver macrophages; eat old RBCs (TRE 1.0 Q88)." |
| "FSH and LH act on testis." | "FSH → Sertoli (inside tubules); LH (ICSH) → Leydig (between tubules) → testosterone." |

### 2.2 Notebook layout

Use **one notebook per book** (five) with these sections:

1. **"X — discovered/gave — year"** table (Linnaeus, Darwin 1859, Mendel 1865/1866, Sutton–Boveri, Morgan, Schleiden–Schwann, Jenner, Eli Lilly 1983, Louise Brown 1978, Alec Jeffreys, Ramdeo Misra).
2. **Ratios and numbers** (genetic ratios, gametogenesis counts, normal values: heart rate, blood pressure, GFR, haemoglobin).
3. **Enzymes and hormones** (source → target → action → disorder).
4. **Examples tables** (animals of each phylum, larval forms, vectors, pathogens).
5. **Exceptions** (one line each).
6. **Error log** (Section 4) — last pages, growing backwards.

> **Memory trick:** Make the left margin 3 cm wide and write the **chapter slug** there (for example "physiology/endocrine-system-and-hormones"). When you revise, you can jump back to the chapter in seconds.

## 3. Spaced revision

Memory fades fast unless you revise at growing gaps. Use the **1–3–7–21 rule**:

| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (same day) | Study the chapter; do its *Practice with answers* | Main slot |
| Day +1 | Read your notes + *Quick revision* of the chapter | 15 min |
| Day +3 | Redo the *Practice* questions without looking | 15–20 min |
| Day +7 (weekly review day) | Redo all practice of the week; take a 40-question test | Review day |
| Day +21 | Re-read *Traps and exceptions* + error-log entries for that chapter | 15–20 min |
| Last 30 days | Question-bank chapters + full mocks | Phase 3 |

## 4. The error log

The error log is the single most valuable document you will make. Every wrong or guessed answer — from chapter practice, quizzes and mocks — goes into it.

| Date | Source (chapter / mock no. / Q no.) | Question in one line | My answer | Correct answer | Why I was wrong (tag) | Fix (one line) | Revised on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 02 Nov | cell-genetics ch6 practice Q9 | 9 : 7 ratio means | codominance | epistasis (complementary genes) | **F** (forgot the ratio list) | 9 : 7 = both dominant genes needed | 05 Nov, 23 Nov |
| 28 Dec | Mock 2, Q87 | Example of a simple protein | haemoglobin | none (all conjugated) | **T** (trap: chromoprotein) | Hb = chromoprotein; thyroglobulin = glycoprotein | 29 Dec |

**Tags** (use one letter so you can count them):

| Tag | Meaning | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| **K** | Knowledge gap — never studied it | Read the chapter section again |
| **F** | Forgot a fact I had studied | Add to the revision sheet; revise at +1, +3, +7 |
| **T** | Fell for a trap (not, except, near-identical words, D/E logic) | Add to *Traps* page; circle trap words in future |
| **R** | Read the question wrongly / hurried | Slow down; read each option to the end |
| **G** | Bad guess (guessed with 3–4 options left) | Follow the E rule |

> **Exam angle:** After every mock, count the tags. If **R + G** are more than a quarter of your errors, your problem is exam behaviour, not knowledge — and it can be fixed in a week.

## 5. Mock tests

| Phase | Mock type | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–60 | Chapter practice + a 40-question sectional test on each weekly review day (the zoology question banks, or the Quiz tab where available) | Weekly |
| Days 61–90 | **Full Mains mock**: 150 Q, 150 min (30 language + 40 GS + 80 zoology) | Twice a week |
| Days 61–90 | **Prelims GS mock**: 150 Q, 120 min | Once a week |
| Every mock | Analyse the same day: score, net, tags, time per part | Always |

How to take a mock properly:

1. Same time of day as the real exam (check the admit card).
2. A printed or hand-made OMR sheet; darken E for skips; no erasing.
3. Strict timer; no phone.
4. Score with **+1 / −1/3** and compare net with your target from [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking).
5. Spend **as long analysing as you spent writing**. Every wrong and every guessed question goes into the error log.

> **Common mistake:** Taking mocks back-to-back without analysis. A mock without analysis only measures you; a mock with analysis improves you.

## 6. PYQs — how to use them

- Solve the **TRE 2.0 Class 11–12 Zoology booklet** (15 Dec 2023) as a full mock around Day 63, with the official final key. Download it from [bpsc.bihar.gov.in](https://bpsc.bihar.gov.in).
- Solve **TRE 1.0 Zoology** (26 Aug 2023, Q41–120) as a "hard M.Sc." sectional test around Day 70, with the latest key published.
- If you can get the **TRE 3.0** Class 11–12 Zoology paper and key, use it as a third full mock.
- The **biology part of the Class 9–10 Science papers** (TRE 1.0 Q95–120, TRE 2.0 Q126–150) is easier but overlaps — use it as a 25-question warm-up; its questions are discussed in the [Class 9–10 blueprint](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/exam-guide/syllabus-decoded-and-pyq-blueprint).
- Your own **Bihar STET Paper-II Zoology** practice papers are good revision sets: similar syllabus, similar one-liners.
- Tag every PYQ topic in your notes with "PYQ" — these are the facts BPSC already considers important.

## Quick revision

- One main source per topic: this collection + NCERT Biology 11–12 (every line).
- Rationalised NCERT dropped digestion, environmental issues, food production — BPSC still asks them; use old editions.
- M.Sc. references only for PYQ-marked topics: Kotpal / Jordan–Verma, Verma–Agarwal, De Robertis, Lehninger, Guyton, Rastogi, Balinsky/Gilbert, Kuby, Odum/Sharma, B.D. Singh, Shukla–Upadhyay.
- Notes: one line, question-ready, with the chapter slug in the margin.
- Notebook pages: X–by–year, ratios and numbers, enzymes and hormones, examples, exceptions, error log.
- Spaced revision: +1, +3, +7, +21 days.
- Error log tags: K, F, T, R, G.
- Days 61–90: two full Mains mocks + one Prelims GS mock per week.
- PYQs: TRE 2.0 as a full mock, TRE 1.0 as a hard sectional.
