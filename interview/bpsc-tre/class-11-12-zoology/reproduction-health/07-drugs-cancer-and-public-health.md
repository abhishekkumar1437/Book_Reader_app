This chapter finishes NCERT class 12 "Human Health and Disease" — drugs and alcohol abuse and cancer — and adds the public-health facts a Bihar teacher is expected to know. TRE 2.0 asked "hashish, ganja and charas are obtained from", "cancer is caused by" and "why cancer cells are damaged more easily by radiation"; TRE 1.0 asked about the acute effects of very high radiation. The GS part of the paper may also ask about Bihar's health programmes.

> **In simple words:** Some plant chemicals act on the brain and are abused as drugs — opium/heroin from the poppy, ganja/charas from the cannabis plant, cocaine from the coca plant. They cause addiction and damage the body. Tobacco's nicotine raises blood pressure and causes cancer; alcohol damages the liver. Cancer is a disease in which cells keep dividing by mitosis without control, form lumps (tumours) and spread. It is caused by radiation, chemicals and some viruses that damage genes. Public-health programmes — vaccination, vector control, free medicines — protect whole populations; Bihar has fought kala-azar and AES (chamki bukhar).

## 1. Drugs of abuse

### 1.1 The NCERT three

| Group | Source | Examples | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Opioids** | **Latex (milky juice) of poppy, *Papaver somniferum*** | Opium, **morphine**, codeine, **heroin (smack)** | Bind to specific **opioid receptors in the CNS and gastrointestinal tract**; **depressant** — slow down body functions |
| **Cannabinoids** | **Inflorescences (flower tops), leaves and resin of *Cannabis sativa*** (hemp) | **Marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja** (and bhang) | Interact with **cannabinoid receptors, mainly in the brain**; affect the **cardiovascular system**; taken by inhalation and oral ingestion |
| **Coca alkaloid** | Coca plant ***Erythroxylum coca*** (native to South America) | **Cocaine** ("coke", "crack") | **Interferes with the transport of the neurotransmitter dopamine**; potent **stimulant** of the CNS — euphoria, more energy; excess causes **hallucinations**; usually snorted |

- **Heroin** is chemically **diacetylmorphine** — a white, odourless, bitter crystalline compound obtained by **acetylation of morphine**. It is taken by snorting and injection.
- **Morphine** is a very effective **sedative and painkiller**, very useful for patients who have undergone surgery.
- Plants with **hallucinogenic** properties: ***Atropa belladonna*** and ***Datura***.
- Drugs normally used as **medicines** for depression and insomnia that are often abused: **barbiturates, amphetamines, benzodiazepines** and **LSD** (lysergic acid diethylamide — from the ergot fungus *Claviceps purpurea*).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q117 asked: "Hashish, Ganja and Charas are obtained from — (A) Cannabis sativa (B) Rauwolfia serpentina (C) Papaver somniferum." **Official answer: (A) Cannabis sativa.** *Papaver* gives opium and heroin; *Rauwolfia* gives the medicine reserpine.

> **Memory trick:** "**P**oppy → **P**ain-killer morphine and heroin; **C**annabis → **C**haras and ganja; **C**oca → **C**ocaine (dopamine)."

### 1.2 Tobacco

- Used by humans for **more than 400 years** — smoked, chewed or used as snuff.
- Contains a large number of chemicals, including the alkaloid **nicotine**.
- **Nicotine stimulates the adrenal gland to release adrenaline and nor-adrenaline** into the blood → both **raise blood pressure and heart rate**.
- Smoking is associated with **cancers of the lung, urinary bladder and throat**, **bronchitis, emphysema, coronary heart disease, gastric ulcer**. Tobacco **chewing** is associated with an increased risk of **cancer of the oral cavity**.
- Smoking increases the **carbon monoxide (CO)** content of blood and reduces the concentration of **haem-bound oxygen** (CO forms carboxyhaemoglobin), causing oxygen deficiency in the body.
- Laws: **COTPA 2003** (no smoking in public places, health warnings on packs), **ban on e-cigarettes (2019)**; National Tobacco Control Programme (2007–08). **World No Tobacco Day — 31 May.**

### 1.3 Alcohol

- **Ethyl alcohol (ethanol)** is a CNS **depressant**.
- Chronic use damages the **nervous system and liver** — **cirrhosis** (liver cells replaced by fibrous tissue); also fatty liver, gastritis, pancreatitis.
- Use during **pregnancy** harms the foetus (**foetal alcohol syndrome**).
- **Disulfiram (Antabuse)** is used in de-addiction — it causes unpleasant reactions if alcohol is taken.
- **Bihar** has had **total prohibition of liquor since April 2016** (Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016).

### 1.4 Adolescence and drug abuse (NCERT)

- **Adolescence** — the period between **12 and 18 years** of age, a bridge between childhood and adulthood.
- Reasons for starting: **curiosity**, need for adventure and excitement, experimentation; **stress** from academics or exams; **peer pressure**; unstable or unsupportive family; television, movies and the internet.
- **Dependence** — the tendency of the body to show a characteristic and unpleasant **withdrawal syndrome** (anxiety, shakiness, nausea, sweating) if regular dose is stopped abruptly.
- **Addiction** — a psychological attachment to certain effects such as euphoria and a temporary feeling of well-being.
- **Warning signs:** drop in academic performance, unexplained absence from school, lack of interest in personal hygiene, withdrawal, isolation, depression, fatigue, aggressive and rebellious behaviour, deteriorating relationships, change in sleeping and eating habits.
- **Effects:** reckless behaviour, vandalism and violence; overdose can lead to **coma and death** due to respiratory failure, heart failure or cerebral haemorrhage. Those who **inject** drugs risk **AIDS and hepatitis B**.

### 1.5 Anabolic steroids in sports (NCERT)

| In females | In males |
|---|---|
| Masculinisation (male features), more aggressiveness, mood swings, depression, abnormal menstrual cycles, excess hair on face and body, enlargement of clitoris, deepening of voice | Acne, aggressiveness, mood swings, depression, **reduction in size of testicles**, **decreased sperm production**, possible kidney and liver dysfunction, **breast enlargement**, premature baldness, enlargement of prostate |

In adolescents they cause severe facial and body acne and **premature closure of the growth centres of long bones**, which may result in **stunted growth**.

### 1.6 Prevention and control (NCERT)

Avoid undue **peer pressure**; **education and counselling**; seeking help from parents and peers; looking for **danger signs**; seeking **professional and medical help** (de-addiction and rehabilitation centres). In India: **NDPS Act, 1985** (Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances); **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** (2020). **International Day against Drug Abuse — 26 June.**

### 1.7 Drugs by their action on the nervous system

| Category | Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Depressants / sedatives** | Slow down brain activity; cause sleep | Alcohol, **barbiturates**, **benzodiazepines** (diazepam), heroin |
| **Narcotic analgesics (opioids)** | Relieve pain, cause sleep and euphoria; highly addictive | **Morphine**, codeine, heroin, pethidine |
| **Stimulants** | Increase alertness and energy | **Cocaine**, **amphetamines**, caffeine, **nicotine** |
| **Hallucinogens** | Change perception; see or hear things that are not there | **LSD**, mescaline (peyote cactus), psilocybin (mushrooms), *Datura*, high doses of cannabis |
| **Tranquillisers** | Reduce anxiety | Benzodiazepines, **reserpine** |

> **Common mistake:** "Narcotic" in law (NDPS Act) covers cannabis and cocaine too, but in pharmacology "narcotic analgesic" means **opioids**.

### 1.8 Medicinal plants worth knowing

| Plant | Product | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ***Rauwolfia serpentina*** (sarpagandha, Indian snakeroot) | **Reserpine** | **Lowers blood pressure**; tranquilliser |
| ***Cinchona*** (bark) | **Quinine** | First anti-malarial drug |
| *Papaver somniferum* | Morphine, codeine | Painkiller, cough suppressant |
| *Atropa belladonna* | **Atropine** | Dilates the pupil; antispasmodic |
| *Digitalis purpurea* (foxglove) | Digitoxin/digoxin | Heart failure |
| *Catharanthus roseus* (sadabahar, periwinkle) | **Vinblastine, vincristine** | Anti-cancer (leukaemia) |
| *Taxus* (yew) | **Taxol** | Anti-cancer |
| *Artemisia annua* | **Artemisinin** | Malaria |
| *Ephedra* | Ephedrine | Asthma |
| *Strychnos nux-vomica* | Strychnine | Poison/stimulant |
| *Penicillium notatum* (fungus) | Penicillin | Antibiotic (Fleming, 1928) |

## 2. Cancer

### 2.1 What it is

- In our body, cell growth and differentiation are **highly controlled and regulated**. In cancer cells these regulatory mechanisms **break down**.
- Normal cells show **contact inhibition** — contact with other cells inhibits their uncontrolled growth. Cancer cells **lose this property**; they keep dividing and give rise to masses of cells called **tumours**.
- Cancer is therefore **uncontrolled cell division by mitosis**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q118 asked: "Cancer is caused by — (A) rupturing of cells (B) uncontrolled meiosis (C) uncontrolled mitosis." **Official answer: (C) uncontrolled mitosis.** Body (somatic) cells divide by mitosis; meiosis happens only in gamete formation.

### 2.2 Benign vs malignant tumours

| Benign tumour | Malignant tumour |
|---|---|
| Normally remains **confined to its original location** | Mass of proliferating **neoplastic (tumour) cells** |
| Does not spread | Grows very rapidly, **invades and damages surrounding tissues** |
| Causes little damage | Cancer cells **starve normal cells** by competing for vital nutrients |
| Usually enclosed in a capsule | Cells sloughed off reach distant sites through blood → new tumours = **metastasis**, **the most feared property** |
| Example: wart, lipoma, fibroid | Example: carcinoma, sarcoma |

### 2.3 Types of cancer

| Type | Origin tissue | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Carcinoma** | **Epithelial** tissue (skin, lining of organs, glands) — most common (about 80–90%) | Lung, breast, oral, cervical, stomach, skin cancers; **adenocarcinoma** = glandular |
| **Sarcoma** | **Connective tissue** — bone, cartilage, muscle, fat (mesodermal) | Osteosarcoma (bone) |
| **Leukaemia** ("blood cancer") | Blood-forming tissue (bone marrow) — huge increase in WBCs | Acute and chronic leukaemias |
| **Lymphoma** | Lymphatic tissue (lymph nodes, spleen) | Hodgkin's lymphoma |
| Myeloma | Plasma cells in bone marrow | Multiple myeloma |
| **Melanoma** | Pigment cells (melanocytes) of skin | Linked to UV exposure |
| Glioma | Glial cells of brain | Brain tumour |

In India, the most common cancers are **oral and lung cancer in men** (tobacco-linked) and **breast and cervical cancer in women**. Cervical cancer is linked to **HPV** and is now preventable by vaccination (India's indigenous HPV vaccine "Cervavac", Serum Institute, 2022).

### 2.4 Causes (carcinogens)

**Transformation of normal cells into cancerous neoplastic cells** may be induced by physical, chemical or biological agents, called **carcinogens**.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | **Ionising radiation** — X-rays, gamma rays (cause DNA damage); **non-ionising** — **UV rays** (skin cancer) |
| **Chemical** | Chemicals in **tobacco smoke** (major cause of lung cancer), benzene, asbestos, aflatoxin (from the fungus *Aspergillus flavus* on stored groundnuts/grains — liver cancer), arsenic in groundwater (skin, bladder cancer — a problem in parts of Bihar's Gangetic belt), vinyl chloride, soot (scrotal cancer of chimney sweeps — first occupational cancer, Percivall Pott 1775) |
| **Biological** | **Oncogenic viruses** with **viral oncogenes** — HPV (cervix), hepatitis B and C (liver), Epstein–Barr virus (Burkitt's lymphoma, nasopharyngeal cancer), HTLV-1 (leukaemia); bacterium *Helicobacter pylori* (stomach) |

**Genes involved:** several genes called **cellular oncogenes (c-onc)** or **proto-oncogenes** are present in normal cells; when **activated** under certain conditions (mutation, extra copies, viral insertion) they lead to oncogenic transformation. **Tumour suppressor genes** like ***p53*** ("guardian of the genome") and ***Rb*** stop uncontrolled division; their loss also causes cancer. Details in [Gene Regulation, Mutation and Cancer Genetics](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/gene-regulation-mutation-and-cancer-genetics).

### 2.5 Detection and diagnosis (NCERT)

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Biopsy and histopathology** | A piece of suspected tissue is cut into thin sections, stained and examined under a microscope by a pathologist |
| **Blood and bone-marrow tests** | Increased cell counts in **leukaemias** |
| **Radiography (X-rays)** | Images of internal organs |
| **CT (computed tomography)** | Uses **X-rays** to generate a **three-dimensional image** of the internals of an object |
| **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)** | Uses **strong magnetic fields and non-ionising radiations** to accurately detect pathological and physiological changes in living tissue |
| **Antibodies against cancer-specific antigens** | Detect certain cancers (tumour markers like PSA for prostate) |
| **Molecular biology techniques** | Detect **genes for inherited susceptibility** to certain cancers (e.g., BRCA1/BRCA2 for breast cancer) — such people can be advised to avoid particular carcinogens |
| Pap smear | Screening for cervical cancer |
| Mammography | Screening for breast cancer |
| PET scan | Detects actively dividing (high glucose-using) tissue |

**Early warning signs (the "CAUTION" list used in health education):** **C**hange in bowel or bladder habits; **A** sore that does not heal; **U**nusual bleeding or discharge; **T**hickening or lump in the breast or elsewhere; **I**ndigestion or difficulty in swallowing; **O**bvious change in a wart or mole; **N**agging cough or hoarseness. India's programme screens adults over 30 for **oral, breast and cervical cancer** at health and wellness centres.

### 2.6 Treatment (NCERT)

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Surgery** | Removes the tumour |
| **Radiation therapy** | Tumour cells are **irradiated lethally**, taking proper care of the surrounding normal tissues |
| **Chemotherapy** | Drugs kill cancer cells; some are specific for particular tumours; most have **side effects like hair loss and anaemia** |
| **Immunotherapy** | Patients are given substances called **biological response modifiers** such as **α-interferon**, which activate their immune system and help destroy the tumour |

Most cancers are treated by a **combination of surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy**. Tumour cells avoid detection and destruction by the immune system, which is why immunotherapy helps. Newer: monoclonal-antibody drugs, CAR-T cell therapy (India approved its first indigenous CAR-T therapy, NexCAR19, in 2023).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q132 asked: "Cancer cells can easily be damaged by radiation than normal cells, because they are — (A) starved on mutation (B) undergoing rapid division (C) different in structure." **Official answer: (B) undergoing rapid division.** Radiation damages DNA; rapidly dividing cells have less time to repair it and die when they try to divide. That is also why hair follicles, gut lining and bone marrow are harmed in radiotherapy and chemotherapy.

### 2.7 Health effects of radiation

| Exposure | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Very high dose, short time (acute)** | **Skin burns** and **acute radiation sickness** (acute radiation syndrome) — nausea, vomiting, hair loss, bone-marrow failure, bleeding, can be fatal within days–weeks |
| Low dose over long time (chronic) | **Cancer** (leukaemia, thyroid cancer), **genetic mutations**, cataract, birth defects |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q120 asked: "Which of the following is the acute health effects due to exposure to very high levels of radiation? — (A) Skin burns and radiation sickness (B) Cancer (C) Cardiovascular disease." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) Skin burns and radiation sickness.** Cancer is a **long-term (late)** effect, not an acute one.

Examples to quote: Hiroshima and Nagasaki (1945), Chernobyl (1986), Fukushima (2011). Ionising radiation is measured in sieverts (Sv).

### 3.2 Bihar — kala-azar elimination

- **Kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis)** is caused by ***Leishmania donovani*** and spread by the **sandfly *Phlebotomus argentipes***. In India it is endemic in **Bihar, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal**, and **Bihar has carried by far the largest share of cases** (north Bihar districts such as Muzaffarpur, Vaishali, Saran, Siwan, Gopalganj, Sitamarhi, Saharsa and the Champarans have been badly affected).
- **Elimination target:** fewer than **1 case per 10,000 population at block level**. India reported reaching this target in all endemic blocks in **2023**; WHO certification needs the level to be sustained for about three years (check the latest status).
- **Tools:** **rK39 rapid diagnostic test**; **single-dose liposomal amphotericin B** treatment; oral **miltefosine** (earlier); **indoor residual spraying (IRS)** with synthetic pyrethroids (alpha-cypermethrin) replacing DDT; active case search; treatment of **PKDL** cases (which act as reservoirs); wage-loss compensation to patients by the state (amounts change — check the latest scheme).
- **Research:** the ICMR's **Rajendra Memorial Research Institute of Medical Sciences (RMRIMS), Patna** works on kala-azar.

### 3.3 Bihar — AES ("chamki bukhar") in Muzaffarpur

- **Acute encephalitis syndrome (AES)** — sudden fever with altered consciousness, confusion or **seizures (chamki = convulsions)** in children. In local speech it is called **chamki bukhar**.
- **Muzaffarpur and nearby districts** see outbreaks in the hot months (**May–June**), the **litchi** season. The worst recent outbreak was in **2019**, when over 150 children died, mostly at **SKMCH, Muzaffarpur**.
- Many cases are not infectious: they are linked to **low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)** in **undernourished children** who **skip the evening meal** and eat litchis. Litchi seeds/fruit contain the toxins **hypoglycin A and MCPG (methylenecyclopropylglycine)**, which block glucose production in the liver overnight (a joint Indian–US CDC study, *The Lancet Global Health*, 2017).
- Other causes of AES: **Japanese encephalitis** (viral, *Culex*; vaccination), scrub typhus, heat stroke.
- Response: the Bihar government's awareness campaign "**Chamki ko Dhamki**" — feed the child before sleep, **wake the child at night** to check, and **rush to hospital** quickly; glucose and ORS at health centres; JE vaccination; ambulances and paediatric ICUs.

### 3.4 Other Bihar points

- **Lymphatic filariasis** — Bihar is highly endemic; annual **mass drug administration** (DEC + albendazole, or IDA) rounds.
- **Arsenic** in groundwater in Gangetic districts (e.g., Bhojpur, Buxar, Patna, Vaishali, Bhagalpur) — skin lesions and cancers.
- **Highest TFR (3.0)** in India (NFHS-5) — Mission Parivar Vikas priority.
- **Total liquor prohibition since 2016**.
- AIIMS Patna (functional since 2012); AIIMS Darbhanga (sanctioned 2020).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Ganja, charas, hashish, marijuana, bhang → *Cannabis sativa***; **heroin, morphine → *Papaver somniferum*** (latex); **cocaine → *Erythroxylum coca***.
- **Heroin = diacetylmorphine** (acetylated morphine) — a depressant, not a stimulant. **Cocaine is a stimulant** (dopamine transport).
- **Cannabinoids** mainly affect the **cardiovascular system** (NCERT phrase).
- **Nicotine** works via the **adrenal medulla** (adrenaline, noradrenaline) → high BP and heart rate.
- **Reserpine (*Rauwolfia*) lowers BP**; it is a medicine, not a drug of abuse.
- **Cancer = uncontrolled mitosis**, not meiosis. Cancer cells lose **contact inhibition**.
- **Metastasis** belongs to **malignant** tumours only.
- **Carcinoma — epithelial; sarcoma — connective tissue; leukaemia — blood; lymphoma — lymph.**
- **CT uses X-rays; MRI uses magnetic fields and non-ionising radiation.**
- **α-interferon** is a biological response modifier (immunotherapy).
- Radiation kills cancer cells more because they **divide rapidly**.
- Acute high radiation → **burns and radiation sickness**; cancer is a **late** effect.
- Chamki bukhar in Muzaffarpur is largely **non-infectious hypoglycaemic encephalopathy** linked to litchi toxins and undernutrition; JE is a different (viral) cause of AES.
- Kala-azar vector is the **sandfly**, not a mosquito.
