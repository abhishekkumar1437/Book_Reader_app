This chapter is the B.Sc. "developmental biology" paper squeezed into MCQ-sized facts: how a single zygote becomes a body. NCERT class 12 gives only morula, blastocyst and the three germ layers; TRE 1.0 showed that the Zoology paper goes deeper, so here we add egg types, cleavage patterns, frog and chick development, extra-embryonic membranes, organisers, regeneration and ageing. Questions are usually one-liners: "meroblastic discoidal cleavage occurs in", "lens develops from which germ layer", "the primary organiser was discovered by".

> **In simple words:** After fertilisation the zygote divides again and again (cleavage) without growing, so the cells get smaller. They form a hollow ball (blastula). Then cells move inward and arrange themselves into three sheets (gastrulation) — ectoderm outside, endoderm inside, mesoderm in between. Each sheet makes fixed organs: ectoderm → skin and nerves, mesoderm → muscles, bones, blood, kidneys; endoderm → gut lining, liver, lungs. How much yolk the egg has decides how it divides: little yolk → whole egg divides (frog, human); lots of yolk → only a small disc on top divides (hen).

## 1. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Embryology** | Study of development from zygote to birth/hatching |
| **Developmental biology** | Wider study — development, regeneration, metamorphosis, ageing |
| **Cleavage** | Rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote without growth |
| **Blastomere** | A cell formed by cleavage |
| **Morula** | Solid ball of blastomeres (8–16 or more cells) |
| **Blastula** | Hollow ball of cells with a cavity, the **blastocoel** |
| **Gastrulation** | Movement of cells to form the three germ layers |
| **Archenteron** | Primitive gut formed during gastrulation; opens by the **blastopore** |
| **Neurulation** | Formation of the neural tube (future brain and spinal cord) |
| **Organogenesis** | Formation of organs from germ layers |
| **Induction** | One tissue directing the development of a neighbouring tissue |
| **Differentiation** | Cells becoming specialised in structure and function |

Founders: **Aristotle** (first embryology observations, chick), **Karl Ernst von Baer** (father of modern embryology; discovered the mammalian ovum, 1827; von Baer's laws), **Wilhelm Roux** (experimental embryology), **Hans Spemann** (organiser, Nobel 1935). The old debate was **preformation** (a tiny ready-made human in the sperm, "homunculus") versus **epigenesis** (gradual formation of new structures — Caspar Friedrich Wolff, 1759); epigenesis is correct.

## 2. Types of eggs (by yolk)

Yolk is stored food in the egg. Its **amount** and **distribution** decide the cleavage pattern.

| Basis | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | **Alecithal / microlecithal / oligolecithal** — very little yolk | **Human and other placental mammals**, Amphioxus, sea urchin |
| | **Mesolecithal** — moderate yolk | **Frog** and other amphibians, lungfish |
| | **Macrolecithal / polylecithal** — large amount of yolk | **Birds**, reptiles, bony fishes (sharks too) |
| Distribution | **Isolecithal / homolecithal** — yolk evenly spread | Mammals, Amphioxus, sea urchin |
| | **Telolecithal** — yolk concentrated at one pole (vegetal pole) | Frog (moderately), **hen** (extremely) |
| | **Centrolecithal** — yolk in the centre, cytoplasm around it | **Insects** and many arthropods |

The pole with the nucleus and less yolk is the **animal pole**; the yolky pole is the **vegetal pole**.

## 3. Cleavage

### 3.1 Features

- Divisions are **mitotic** but **very fast**, with almost no G1 and G2 phases.
- **No growth** between divisions → blastomeres get smaller; the embryo stays about the same size as the zygote.
- Nuclear–cytoplasmic ratio increases.
- Yolk slows cleavage furrows; so the yolky part divides slowly or not at all (**Balfour's law**).

### 3.2 Planes of cleavage

| Plane | Description |
|---|---|
| **Meridional** | Passes through animal–vegetal axis (like a line of longitude) |
| **Vertical** | Parallel to the axis but not through the centre |
| **Equatorial** | At right angles to the axis, at the equator |
| **Latitudinal** | At right angles to the axis, above or below the equator |

### 3.3 Patterns of cleavage

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Holoblastic (total) equal** | Whole egg divides into equal blastomeres | **Mammals**, Amphioxus, sea urchin |
| **Holoblastic unequal** | Whole egg divides, but **micromeres** (animal) and **macromeres** (vegetal) differ in size | **Frog** |
| **Meroblastic discoidal** | Only a small disc of cytoplasm on top of the yolk (blastodisc) divides | **Birds** (hen), reptiles, bony fishes |
| **Meroblastic superficial** | Nucleus divides inside the central yolk; nuclei move to the periphery; cells form only at the surface | **Insects** (Drosophila) |

By the arrangement of blastomeres (B.Sc.):

| Arrangement | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | Blastomeres lie directly above one another; **indeterminate** (early cells can still form a whole embryo) | Echinoderms, chordates (deuterostomes) |
| **Spiral** | Upper blastomeres twisted at 45° over lower ones; **determinate** (each cell's fate fixed early) | Annelids, molluscs, flatworms (protostomes) |
| **Bilateral** | First division sets the only plane of symmetry | Tunicates (Ascidia) |
| **Rotational** | Second division: one blastomere divides meridionally, the other equatorially | **Mammals** (also asynchronous; compaction at 8-cell stage) |

## 4. Blastula

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Coeloblastula** | Hollow sphere with a central or eccentric blastocoel | Amphioxus (central), **frog** (blastocoel shifted to the **animal half**) |
| **Discoblastula** | Flat disc of cells (blastoderm) over the yolk; blastocoel is a narrow space below | **Chick**, reptiles, fishes |
| **Periblastula (superficial)** | Single layer of cells around central yolk; no true blastocoel | Insects |
| **Blastocyst (blastodermic vesicle)** | Outer trophoblast + inner cell mass + cavity | **Mammals** |
| **Stereoblastula** | Solid, no cavity | Some annelids, molluscs |

## 5. Gastrulation

Gastrulation is a period of active cell movements (**morphogenetic movements**) by which the blastula becomes a two- or three-layered **gastrula** with a primitive gut (**archenteron**) opening outside by the **blastopore**.

| Movement | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Epiboly** | Spreading of cells over the surface | Ectoderm cells in frog spreading over the yolky cells |
| **Emboly** | Inward movement of cells (general term) | — |
| **Invagination** | Inward folding of a sheet (like pushing a finger into a ball) | Amphioxus, sea urchin; frog bottle cells |
| **Involution** | Sheet rolling inward over an edge (lip of blastopore) | Frog |
| **Ingression** | Individual cells migrate inward | Sea urchin mesenchyme; chick primitive streak |
| **Delamination** | One sheet splits into two | Hypoblast formation in chick |

**Fate of the blastopore** — in **protostomes** (annelids, arthropods, molluscs) the blastopore becomes the **mouth**; in **deuterostomes** (echinoderms, hemichordates, chordates) it becomes the **anus** and the mouth forms later. This links to classification in [Classification and Basis of Animal Kingdom](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/classification-and-basis-of-animal-kingdom).

Animals with two germ layers (**diploblastic**: cnidarians like Hydra) have only ectoderm and endoderm with a non-cellular mesoglea between; all higher animals are **triploblastic**.

## 6. Germ layers and their derivatives

Remember: **ectoderm = outer things and nerves; mesoderm = movement, support, transport, excretion, reproduction; endoderm = lining of gut and its glands, and respiratory lining.**

| Ectoderm | Mesoderm | Endoderm |
|---|---|---|
| **Epidermis** of skin, hair, nails, feathers, scales, horns | **Dermis** of skin | **Epithelial lining of the alimentary canal** (except mouth and anus ends) |
| Sweat, sebaceous and **mammary glands** | All **muscles** (smooth, skeletal, cardiac) | **Liver**, **pancreas** (glandular parts) |
| **Entire nervous system** — brain, spinal cord, nerves | **Bones, cartilage**, connective tissues | **Lining of trachea, bronchi and lungs (alveoli)** |
| **Retina**, **lens** of eye, cornea epithelium, internal ear (membranous labyrinth) | **Heart, blood vessels, blood**, lymph, spleen | **Thyroid**, **parathyroid**, thymus epithelium |
| **Enamel** of teeth | Dentine of teeth (via neural crest–mesenchyme, B.Sc.) | Lining of urinary bladder and urethra |
| **Pituitary** (anterior from Rathke's pouch, posterior from brain floor), **pineal** | **Kidneys**, ureters, **gonads**, genital ducts | Eustachian tube and middle ear lining |
| **Adrenal medulla** (via neural crest) | **Adrenal cortex** | Tonsils (epithelium) |
| Lining of mouth (stomodaeum) and anus (proctodaeum) | **Notochord** (chordamesoderm), coelom lining | Lining of vagina (part), prostate glandular epithelium |

**Neural crest** — cells that separate from the edges of the neural folds; called the "**fourth germ layer**". It gives pigment cells (**melanocytes**), **adrenal medulla**, sensory (dorsal root) and autonomic ganglia, **Schwann cells**, many facial bones and cartilages, and **odontoblasts** (dentine).

> **Exam angle:** The favourite one-liners are — **lens and retina → ectoderm; heart and kidney → mesoderm; liver, pancreas, lung lining and thyroid → endoderm; adrenal cortex → mesoderm but adrenal medulla → ectoderm (neural crest); notochord → mesoderm.**

> **Memory trick:** "**N**erves, **N**ails, **N**ipples (mammary), e**N**amel" are ectoderm. "**MBBK**" — **M**uscle, **B**one, **B**lood, **K**idney — mesoderm. "**L**iver, **L**ung, **L**ining of gut" — endoderm.

## 7. Development of the frog

1. **Egg** — mesolecithal and telolecithal; animal half dark (pigmented), vegetal half pale and yolky; covered by jelly. Fertilisation is **external** (in water) during amplexus.
2. **Grey crescent** — after sperm entry, the pigmented cortex rotates and a grey crescent-shaped area appears **opposite the point of sperm entry**. It marks the future **dorsal side** and the site of the dorsal lip of the blastopore.
3. **Cleavage** — **holoblastic unequal**. The first two divisions are **meridional** (4 equal cells); the third is **latitudinal**, above the equator → **4 small micromeres** (animal) + **4 large macromeres** (vegetal).
4. **Blastula** — coeloblastula; blastocoel lies in the **animal half**, roof thinner than floor.
5. **Gastrulation** — begins at the **dorsal lip of the blastopore** (just below the grey crescent) with **bottle cells**. Cells involute; epiboly of animal cells over the vegetal cells; the blastopore becomes a circle enclosing a **yolk plug**.
6. **Neurulation** — neural plate → neural folds → neural tube; notochord below it; somites beside it.
7. **Hatching** — a **tadpole larva** hatches: gills, tail with fin, herbivorous, two-chambered heart, ammonotelic.
8. **Metamorphosis** — tadpole → froglet under **thyroxine** (thyroid hormone): tail is **resorbed** (lysosomes; programmed cell death), gills disappear, lungs and limbs develop (hindlimbs first), the heart becomes three-chambered and excretion changes from ammonia to **urea** (ureotelic).

> **Remember:** Tadpoles given extra **iodine/thyroxine** metamorphose early (into tiny frogs); thyroidectomised tadpoles never metamorphose and become giant tadpoles. **Axolotl** (larva of *Ambystoma*) can breed in the larval form — **neoteny/paedogenesis** — because of low thyroid activity; thyroxine makes it metamorphose.

## 8. Development of the chick

1. **Egg** — **polylecithal, telolecithal** (the largest single cell is the yolk of an ostrich egg). Fertilisation is **internal**, in the oviduct, **before** the albumen, shell membranes and shell are added. Layers outward: yolk with blastodisc on top → vitelline membrane → albumen with twisted **chalazae** (keep yolk centred) → two shell membranes (with an **air space** at the broad end) → calcareous **shell** with pores.
2. **Cleavage** — **meroblastic discoidal**; only the blastodisc divides.
3. **Blastoderm** — a disc with a central transparent **area pellucida** (cells over a fluid-filled subgerminal space; forms the embryo) and an outer opaque **area opaca** (over yolk).
4. The blastoderm splits into an upper **epiblast** and a lower **hypoblast** (delamination/ingression).
5. **Primitive streak** — a thickened line in the epiblast; cells move in through it (ingression) to form mesoderm and endoderm. It is the **equivalent of the blastopore**. At its front end lies **Hensen's node** — the chick's **organiser**.
6. **Incubation** — about **21 days** at about 37–38 °C. Laying hens can lay unfertilised eggs too.
7. **Extra-embryonic membranes** form (next section).

## 9. Extra-embryonic membranes

These membranes lie outside the embryo's body and protect and support it. Animals that have them — **reptiles, birds and mammals** — are called **amniotes**; fishes and amphibians lack them (**anamniotes**). They allowed vertebrates to lay eggs on land.

| Membrane | Made of | Function in chick (birds) | In human (placental mammals) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amnion** | Ectoderm + somatic mesoderm (somatopleure) | Encloses the embryo in **amniotic fluid** — protection against shock and drying | Same; "bag of waters"; fluid sampled in **amniocentesis** |
| **Chorion** | Somatopleure (outermost) | Encloses everything; fuses with allantois → **chorio-allantoic membrane** | Forms the **foetal part of the placenta** (chorionic villi); secretes hCG |
| **Allantois** | Endoderm + splanchnic mesoderm (splanchnopleure), outgrowth of hindgut | **Respiration** (gas exchange through shell pores, with chorion) and **storage of nitrogenous waste (uric acid)**; absorbs calcium from shell | Small; its blood vessels become the **umbilical arteries and vein**; base forms part of urinary bladder |
| **Yolk sac** | Splanchnopleure | Surrounds yolk; **digests and absorbs yolk**; first blood cells and vessels | **No yolk**; still makes the **first blood cells** and **primordial germ cells**; small and non-nutritive |

> **Exam angle:** "**Allantois acts as the respiratory organ (and urinary bladder) of the chick embryo**" — this is the most asked membrane fact. In mammals the respiratory exchange is done by the **placenta** (chorion + allantois = chorio-allantoic placenta).

## 10. Human (mammalian) early development

- Egg **alecithal**, cleavage **holoblastic, nearly equal, rotational and asynchronous** (blastomeres do not all divide at the same time).
- **Compaction** at the 8-cell stage — blastomeres flatten and stick tightly together.
- Morula (16 cells) → **blastocyst** (trophoblast + inner cell mass + blastocoel). Details of implantation and placenta are in [Menstrual Cycle, Fertilisation and Pregnancy](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/menstrual-cycle-fertilisation-and-pregnancy).
- Inner cell mass → **bilaminar disc** (epiblast + hypoblast) in the second week → primitive streak → **trilaminar disc** (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) in the third week. The epiblast gives all three germ layers.
- The **heart** is the first organ to function (beats by about day 22).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) asked two development questions from this stage: Q100 — "The fusion of sperm and oocyte membranes causes to occur —" **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) cortical reaction**; and Q101 — "At what interval of pregnancy, the foetus fills the entire uterus?" **Key: (A) By 12 weeks.** Both are explained in chapter 2; the embryology behind Q101 is the growth of the chorionic sac until the decidua capsularis meets the decidua parietalis.

## 11. Stem cells

| Potency | Can form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Totipotent** | All cells of the embryo **and** the placenta/extra-embryonic tissues | Zygote, early blastomeres (up to about the 8-cell stage) |
| **Pluripotent** | All cells of the body, but not the placenta | **Inner cell mass**; embryonic stem (ES) cells |
| **Multipotent** | Several cell types of one family | Adult stem cells — bone marrow (blood cells), umbilical cord blood |
| **Unipotent** | One cell type | Skin basal cells, spermatogonia |

**Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)** — adult skin cells reprogrammed into pluripotent cells using four genes (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) by **Shinya Yamanaka** (2006; Nobel 2012 with John Gurdon, who had cloned frogs from tadpole intestinal nuclei in 1962). Cord-blood banking stores multipotent stem cells.

## 12. Organisers and embryonic induction

- **Embryonic induction** — one group of cells (inducer) influences the developmental fate of another group (responding tissue).
- **Spemann–Mangold experiment (1924)** — **Hans Spemann and Hilde Mangold** transplanted the **dorsal lip of the blastopore** of one newt gastrula into the ventral side of another (of a differently pigmented species). The graft induced the host tissue to form a **second embryo** (secondary neural tube, notochord, somites) — Siamese-twin-like. They called the dorsal lip the **primary organiser**. Spemann received the **Nobel Prize in 1935** (Mangold had died in 1924).
- Equivalent organisers: **Hensen's node** (chick), the **node** (mammals), **embryonic shield** (fish).
- **Secondary induction:** the **optic vesicle** (outgrowth of the brain) induces the overlying ectoderm to form the **lens**; the lens then induces the cornea — a chain of inductions. Spemann's early lens experiments (1901) began this field.
- **Spemann** also did the **hair-loop constriction** experiments on newt eggs showing that the grey crescent is needed in both halves for each to form a whole embryo.
- Molecules: the organiser secretes proteins like **noggin, chordin and follistatin** that block BMP-4 signalling (B.Sc. detail).

> **Remember:** "Primary organiser = dorsal lip of blastopore = Spemann (with Mangold), 1924, Nobel 1935."

## 13. Metamorphosis (brief)

| Animal | Change | Hormone |
|---|---|---|
| Frog | Tadpole → adult | **Thyroxine** (T3/T4) |
| Insects (complete, holometabolous — butterfly, housefly) | Egg → larva → pupa → adult | **Ecdysone** (moulting hormone, from prothoracic gland) promotes moulting; **juvenile hormone** (corpora allata) keeps larval form |
| Insects (incomplete, hemimetabolous — cockroach, grasshopper) | Egg → nymph → adult | Same hormones |

When juvenile hormone falls, the next moult gives a pupa/adult. More on larval forms in [Comparative Anatomy and Larval Forms](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/comparative-anatomy-and-larval-forms).

## 14. Regeneration

**Regeneration** — the ability to regrow lost or damaged body parts. First studied in **Hydra** by **Abraham Trembley (1744)**.

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Morphallaxis** | Remaining tissue **reorganises** itself without much new cell division | **Hydra** (small pieces form whole tiny Hydra) |
| **Epimorphosis** | Cells near the wound **dedifferentiate** and form a mass called a **blastema**, which divides and redifferentiates | **Salamander/newt limb**, axolotl, lizard tail, fish fins |
| **Compensatory (hypertrophy)** | Remaining cells divide but keep their function; shape not restored | **Mammalian liver** — regrows to original mass after partial removal |

Examples worth knowing:
- **Planaria** (flatworm) — each small piece regenerates a complete worm, keeping **head–tail polarity**; it has many neoblasts (stem cells).
- **Starfish** — a single arm with a part of the **central disc** can form a whole animal.
- **Lizards** — **autotomy** (self-breaking) of the tail to escape enemies; the regenerated tail has a **cartilage rod**, not vertebrae, and is often shorter.
- **Crabs, spiders** — regenerate limbs at moults.
- **Earthworm** — can regenerate a few anterior and more posterior segments.
- **Humans** — limited: skin, blood, liver, bone fracture repair, endometrium; nerve cells of the central nervous system regenerate very poorly.

> **Common mistake:** Regeneration is **not** the same as asexual reproduction, although in Planaria and some worms (fragmentation) it serves as one.

## 15. Ageing (senescence)

**Ageing** — progressive decline in body functions with time, leading to death. **Gerontology** is the study of ageing; **geriatrics** is the medical care of the elderly.

Signs: wrinkled skin (loss of collagen and elastin cross-linking), grey hair (less melanin), weak bones (osteoporosis, especially in women after menopause), cataract, hearing loss, lower immunity, slower nerve conduction, fall in basal metabolic rate, memory loss (dementia, Alzheimer's disease in some).

| Theory | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Hayflick limit** (Leonard Hayflick, 1961) | Normal human cells in culture divide only about **50 times** and then stop |
| **Telomere theory** | Telomeres (repeated TTAGGG at chromosome ends) shorten with each division; when too short, cells stop dividing. The enzyme **telomerase** restores them (active in germ cells, stem cells and **cancer cells**). Nobel 2009 — Blackburn, Greider, Szostak |
| **Free radical theory** (Denham Harman, 1956) | Reactive oxygen species damage DNA, proteins and lipids; antioxidants (vitamins C, E) help |
| **Somatic mutation / DNA damage** | Errors accumulate in body cells |
| **Cross-linkage theory** | Collagen and other proteins get cross-linked and stiff |
| **Immunological theory** | Thymus shrinks with age; immunity weakens; autoimmunity rises |
| **Programmed (genetic) theory** | Lifespan set by genes; **apoptosis** (programmed cell death) |
| **Wear and tear** | Parts simply wear out with use |

**Progeria** (Hutchinson–Gilford syndrome) — a rare genetic disease (mutation in the **LMNA** gene, lamin A) with very rapid ageing in childhood.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Human egg = alecithal; frog = mesolecithal; hen = polylecithal/telolecithal; insect = centrolecithal.**
- Frog cleavage is **holoblastic unequal**, not meroblastic. Chick cleavage is **meroblastic discoidal**. Insect is **superficial**.
- The grey crescent forms **opposite** the sperm entry point and marks the **dorsal** side.
- In frog the blastocoel is in the **animal** half.
- **Primitive streak** (chick) = blastopore equivalent; **Hensen's node** = organiser.
- **Allantois** in chick = respiration + uric acid storage; in mammals = umbilical vessels.
- The human yolk sac has no yolk but still makes the first blood cells.
- **Notochord is mesodermal**; neural tube is ectodermal.
- **Adrenal medulla — ectoderm (neural crest); adrenal cortex — mesoderm.**
- **Enamel — ectoderm; dentine — neural crest-derived mesenchyme** (usually listed as mesoderm in simple tables).
- Lining of the lungs and thyroid are **endodermal**; heart and kidneys are **mesodermal**.
- **Deuterostomes** (including us) — blastopore becomes the **anus**.
- Hydra regenerates by **morphallaxis**; salamander limb by **epimorphosis** (blastema).
- Lizard regenerated tail has **cartilage**, not bony vertebrae.
- Telomerase is active in **cancer cells** — why they divide indefinitely.

## Quick revision

1. Cleavage — mitotic divisions without growth.
2. Isolecithal + microlecithal — mammals; mesolecithal — frog; polylecithal — birds; centrolecithal — insects.
3. Holoblastic equal — mammals, Amphioxus; unequal — frog.
4. Meroblastic discoidal — birds, reptiles, fishes; superficial — insects.
5. Spiral determinate cleavage — annelids, molluscs; radial indeterminate — echinoderms, chordates.
6. Mammalian cleavage — rotational, asynchronous, compaction at 8 cells.
7. Blastocyst — mammals; discoblastula — chick; coeloblastula — frog.
8. Gastrulation forms germ layers and archenteron.
9. Protostome — blastopore → mouth; deuterostome — blastopore → anus.
10. Ectoderm — epidermis, nervous system, lens, retina, enamel, mammary glands.
11. Mesoderm — muscles, bones, heart, blood, kidneys, gonads, adrenal cortex, notochord.
12. Endoderm — gut lining, liver, pancreas, lung lining, thyroid, parathyroid.
13. Neural crest — "fourth germ layer": melanocytes, adrenal medulla, Schwann cells.
14. Frog grey crescent — future dorsal side.
15. Frog metamorphosis — thyroxine.
16. Chick incubation — 21 days; primitive streak; Hensen's node.
17. Amniotes — reptiles, birds, mammals.
18. Allantois — respiratory organ and urinary bladder of chick embryo.
19. Primary organiser — dorsal lip of blastopore; Spemann and Mangold, 1924.
20. Lens induced by optic vesicle.
21. Morphallaxis — Hydra; epimorphosis — salamander limb; compensatory — liver.
22. Hayflick limit — about 50 divisions.
23. Telomere shortening and free radicals — ageing theories.
24. Insect moulting — ecdysone; larval state kept by juvenile hormone.

## Practice with answers

1. What type of egg does a frog have? — **Mesolecithal, telolecithal**.
2. What type of cleavage occurs in the hen's egg? — **Meroblastic discoidal**.
3. Cleavage in insects is? — **Superficial (meroblastic)**.
4. In which animals is cleavage spiral? — **Annelids and molluscs** (protostomes).
5. What marks the future dorsal side of the frog embryo? — **Grey crescent**.
6. In mammals, what is the blastula called? — **Blastocyst**.
7. From which germ layer does the lens of the eye develop? — **Ectoderm** (induced by optic vesicle).
8. From which germ layer does the notochord arise? — **Mesoderm**.
9. From which germ layer does the liver arise? — **Endoderm**.
10. Which structure is called the fourth germ layer? — **Neural crest**.
11. What is the respiratory organ of the chick embryo? — **Allantois** (with chorion).
12. Which membrane encloses the embryo in fluid? — **Amnion**.
13. Who discovered the primary organiser? — **Hans Spemann and Hilde Mangold (1924)**.
14. What is the chick's organiser? — **Hensen's node**.
15. Which hormone controls frog metamorphosis? — **Thyroxine**.
16. How does Hydra regenerate? — **Morphallaxis**.
17. What is the mass of dedifferentiated cells in limb regeneration? — **Blastema**.
18. What is the Hayflick limit? — **About 50 divisions** of normal cultured human cells.
19. Which enzyme rebuilds telomeres? — **Telomerase**.
20. Who proposed the theory of epigenesis against preformation? — **Caspar Friedrich Wolff** (1759; the word "epigenesis" itself goes back to William Harvey, 1651).
