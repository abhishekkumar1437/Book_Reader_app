This chapter follows the egg from the monthly cycle to the birth of a baby: menstrual cycle, ovulation, fertilisation, implantation, placenta, pregnancy, parturition and lactation. NCERT class 12 "Human Reproduction" is the base; the B.Sc. layer is cortical reaction and block to polyspermy, decidua, placental types and the hormonal control of labour. TRE asks things like "menstrual cycle is controlled by", "ovulation occurs under the influence of", "hormones involved in parturition".

> **In simple words:** Every month (about 28 days) the brain tells the ovary to ripen one follicle. The follicle makes oestrogen, which thickens the uterus lining. A sudden burst of LH in mid-cycle releases the egg. The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which makes progesterone to keep the lining ready. If no baby comes, progesterone falls, the lining breaks and bleeds out — that is menstruation. If a sperm fertilises the egg, the embryo sinks into the lining, a placenta forms, and its hormone hCG tells the body "do not menstruate, a baby is here."

## 1. The menstrual cycle — basics

- The reproductive cycle in female **primates** (monkeys, apes, humans) is called the **menstrual cycle**. Non-primate mammals (cow, sheep, rat, dog, deer, tiger) have an **oestrous cycle** instead — they show "heat" and do not menstruate.
- **Menarche** — the first menstruation, at puberty.
- Average length — **28 or 29 days**, counted from the first day of one menstruation to the first day of the next.
- One ovum is released (**ovulation**) in the **middle** of each cycle, about day 14 in a 28-day cycle.
- **Menopause** — permanent stopping of menstrual cycles, at about **45–55 years** (average around 50).
- Cycles also stop during **pregnancy**, and often during breastfeeding.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q108 asked: "Cessation of menstrual cycle is called — (A) ovulation (B) puberty (C) menopause." **Official answer: (C) menopause.**

**Lack of menstruation** (amenorrhoea) may indicate pregnancy, but can also be due to stress, poor health, low body weight, thyroid problems or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Painful menstruation is **dysmenorrhoea**.

## 2. The four phases

The cycle is really two cycles running together: the **ovarian cycle** (what happens in the ovary — follicle, ovulation, corpus luteum) and the **uterine cycle** (what happens to the endometrium — bleeding, repair, secretion).

| Phase (days in a 28-day cycle) | Ovary | Uterus (endometrium) | Hormones |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Menstrual phase** (days 1–5; lasts 3–5 days) | Previous corpus luteum has died; new follicles start growing | Lining **breaks down and is shed** with blood | Progesterone and oestrogen **low** |
| **Follicular / proliferative phase** (days 6–13) | Primary follicles grow into a mature **Graafian follicle** | Endometrium **regenerates and thickens** (proliferation) | FSH and LH rise slowly; follicle secretes **oestrogen**, which rises steeply |
| **Ovulatory phase** (about day 14) | Graafian follicle **ruptures** and releases the **secondary oocyte** | Thick lining | **LH surge** (maximum LH), FSH also peaks; oestrogen peak just before |
| **Luteal / secretory phase** (days 15–28) | Ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum** | Glands secrete; lining becomes thick, spongy and ready for implantation | Corpus luteum secretes large amounts of **progesterone** (and some oestrogen); LH and FSH fall |

- **If fertilisation does not occur:** the corpus luteum degenerates after about 10–12 days into the **corpus albicans** → progesterone falls → the endometrium is shed → a new cycle begins.
- **If fertilisation occurs:** the embryo's **hCG** keeps the corpus luteum alive → progesterone stays high → no menstruation. The corpus luteum is maintained for the first ~3 months, after which the placenta takes over progesterone production.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q110 asked: "Ovulation occurs under the influence of — (A) LH (B) … (C) oestrogen." **Official answer: (A) LH.** The mid-cycle **LH surge** triggers rupture of the Graafian follicle. (The second option is not clear in the extracted booklet text.)

How to read it:

- **Oestrogen** rises in the follicular phase and peaks **just before day 14**; a smaller second rise comes from the corpus luteum.
- **LH** (and FSH, less sharply) peaks on about **day 14** — this "surge" causes ovulation. High oestrogen at this moment exerts **positive feedback** on the pituitary — the one important exception to the usual negative feedback.
- **Progesterone** is low in the first half and peaks around **day 21** (luteal phase).
- Fall of progesterone and oestrogen at the end → menstruation.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q106 asked: "Menstrual cycle is controlled by — (A) oestrogen and progesterone of ovary (B) FSH and LH of pituitary (C) FSH of pituitary." **Official answer: (D) More than one of the above.** Both the pituitary gonadotropins and the ovarian steroids control the cycle.

> **Memory trick:** "**F**ollicle — **F**SH — o**E**strogen first; **L**uteal — **L**H then **P**rogesterone." FSH grows the follicle, LH lets the egg go, progesterone "pro-gestation" keeps the lining.

### 2.2 Safe period and fertile window

- A sperm survives about **48–72 hours** in the female tract; an ovulated oocyte remains fertilisable for only about **12–24 hours**.
- So the fertile window is roughly **days 10–17** of a regular 28-day cycle. Avoiding intercourse in these days is the "**periodic abstinence**" or rhythm method (see [Reproductive Health and Contraception](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/reproductive-health-and-contraception)).

## 3. Insemination and fertilisation

### 3.1 Insemination

During copulation, semen is released by the penis into the vagina — **insemination**. Motile sperms swim through the cervix, enter the uterus and reach the **ampullary region** of the fallopian tube. The ovum released by the ovary is also carried to the ampulla. **Fertilisation can occur only if the ovum and sperms reach the ampulla at the same time.**

### 3.2 Capacitation

Freshly ejaculated sperms cannot fertilise. In the female tract they undergo **capacitation** — removal of a glycoprotein and cholesterol coat from the sperm head membrane, making the membrane ready for the acrosome reaction and making the tail beat more strongly (hyperactivation). It takes a few hours.

### 3.3 Steps of fertilisation (B.Sc. level)

1. **Penetration of corona radiata** — helped by hyaluronidase from the acrosome and tail movement.
2. **Binding to zona pellucida** — the sperm binds to a glycoprotein receptor on the zona (**ZP3** in mammals).
3. **Acrosome reaction** — the acrosome membrane fuses with the sperm plasma membrane and releases its enzymes (**acrosin**, hyaluronidase); the sperm digests a path through the zona.
4. **Fusion of sperm and oocyte membranes** — the sperm's head enters the oocyte cytoplasm.
5. **Cortical reaction** — the fusion triggers a wave of **calcium release** in the egg; **cortical granules** under the egg membrane release their contents into the space outside the membrane. This modifies the zona (**zona reaction**) so it hardens and **no other sperm can enter** — the **block to polyspermy**.
6. **Completion of meiosis II** by the secondary oocyte → **ovum (ootid) + second polar body**.
7. **Fusion of pronuclei (amphimixis / karyogamy)** — the haploid male and female pronuclei unite to form a **diploid zygote** (2n = 46).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q100 asked: "The fusion of sperm and oocyte membranes causes to occur — (A) kin selection (B) cortical reaction (C) acrosome reaction." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) cortical reaction.** The acrosome reaction happens **before** fusion (at the zona); the cortical reaction is **caused by** fusion.

| Reaction | When | In which cell | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacitation | In female tract, before reaching egg | Sperm | Makes sperm able to fertilise |
| **Acrosome reaction** | On contact with zona pellucida | Sperm | Releases enzymes to cut through zona |
| **Cortical reaction** | Right after sperm–egg membrane fusion | Egg | Hardens zona; **blocks polyspermy** |

B.Sc. note: in sea urchins there is a **fast block** (electrical depolarisation of the egg membrane within seconds) and a **slow block** (cortical reaction). In mammals the slow block via the cortical reaction is the main one.

**Significance of fertilisation:** restores diploid number, combines genes of two parents (variation), activates the egg to start development, and **decides the sex** of the baby (X or Y sperm).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q107 — fertilisation in humans occurs in the **fallopian tube** (official answer C); more exactly, in the **ampulla** (TRE 1.0 Q96, key B). Both are in chapter 1.

## 4. Cleavage, blastocyst and implantation

- The zygote starts **mitotic division (cleavage)** while it moves through the isthmus toward the uterus.
- Cleavage gives 2, 4, 8, 16 daughter cells called **blastomeres**. Cells become smaller each time because there is no growth between divisions.
- The embryo with **8–16 blastomeres** is the **morula** (looks like a mulberry). It reaches the uterus about 3–4 days after fertilisation.
- The morula continues to divide and becomes the **blastocyst** — a hollow ball. Its outer layer is the **trophoblast**; an inner group of cells attached to it is the **inner cell mass** (embryoblast).
- The **trophoblast** attaches to the endometrium and later forms the chorion and the foetal part of the placenta. The **inner cell mass** becomes the **embryo**. It contains **stem cells** that can give rise to all tissues.
- **Implantation**: the blastocyst becomes embedded in the endometrium of the uterus, about **6–7 days after fertilisation** (completed by about day 10–12). The uterine cells divide rapidly and cover the blastocyst. **Implantation leads to pregnancy.**

**Zygote → (cleavage) → 2, 4, 8 blastomeres → morula (8–16 cells) → blastocyst (trophoblast + inner cell mass) → implantation (day 6–7) → gastrula → embryo → foetus**

</div>

After implantation, the inner cell mass differentiates into an outer **ectoderm**, a middle **mesoderm** and an inner **endoderm**. Embryology and germ-layer derivatives are covered in [Embryology and Developmental Biology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/embryology-and-developmental-biology).

**Decidua (B.Sc.)** — the endometrium of pregnancy is called the decidua: **decidua basalis** (under the embryo, forms the maternal part of the placenta), **decidua capsularis** (covers the embryo on the cavity side) and **decidua parietalis** (rest of the uterine lining). As the embryo grows, the capsularis fuses with the parietalis and the cavity is obliterated, at about the end of the first trimester.

**Ectopic pregnancy** — implantation outside the uterus, most often in the **fallopian tube** (tubal pregnancy); it is dangerous because the tube can rupture.

## 5. The placenta

After implantation, finger-like projections called **chorionic villi** grow from the trophoblast and are surrounded by the uterine tissue and maternal blood. The chorionic villi and uterine tissue become interdigitated and together form a structural and functional unit between the developing embryo (foetus) and the mother — the **placenta**.

- The placenta is connected to the embryo by the **umbilical cord**, which carries **two umbilical arteries** (deoxygenated foetal blood to the placenta) and **one umbilical vein** (oxygenated blood to the foetus).
- Maternal and foetal blood **do not mix**; exchange happens across the thin placental barrier.

**Functions:** supply of O₂ and nutrients to the embryo; removal of CO₂ and wastes (urea); transfer of **IgG antibodies** (passive immunity); acting as an **endocrine gland**; barrier to many (not all) microbes and drugs.

### 5.1 Hormones of pregnancy

| Hormone | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin) | Trophoblast / placenta | **Maintains the corpus luteum** in early pregnancy; detected in urine by **pregnancy test kits**; peaks at about 8–10 weeks |
| **hPL** (human placental lactogen, also called human chorionic somatomammotropin) | Placenta | Prepares breasts for milk; adjusts mother's metabolism (raises blood glucose for foetus) |
| **Oestrogens** | Placenta (and ovary) | Uterine growth, breast duct growth |
| **Progestogens** (progesterone) | Corpus luteum first, then **placenta** | Maintains pregnancy; keeps uterine muscle quiet |
| **Relaxin** | **Ovary** (corpus luteum) in later pregnancy, also placenta | Relaxes pubic symphysis and softens cervix for birth |

During pregnancy the levels of other hormones like oestrogens, progestogens, cortisol, prolactin, thyroxine etc. are also **increased several-fold** in the maternal blood — supporting foetal growth, metabolic changes in the mother and maintenance of pregnancy.

> **Exam angle:** NCERT lists placental hormones as **hCG, hPL, estrogens, progestogens** and says **relaxin is secreted by the ovary in the later phase of pregnancy**. If an MCQ asks "which is not secreted by the placenta?" and relaxin is an option alongside hCG/hPL, choose carefully — by NCERT the answer is relaxin (ovary); some B.Sc. books say the placenta also makes some relaxin.

### 5.2 Types of placenta (B.Sc.)

| Basis | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Shape (distribution of villi) | **Diffuse** — villi all over | Pig, horse |
| | **Cotyledonary** — villi in patches | Cow, sheep (ruminants) |
| | **Zonary** — villi in a band | Dog, cat |
| | **Discoidal** — villi in a disc | **Human**, monkey, rat, rabbit |
| Tissue layers between bloods | Epitheliochorial (most layers) | Pig, horse |
| | Endotheliochorial | Dog, cat |
| | **Haemochorial** — foetal chorion bathed directly in maternal blood | **Human**, rat, rabbit |
| Shed at birth? | **Deciduate** (uterine tissue lost at birth, bleeding) | **Human** |
| | Non-deciduate | Pig, cow |

So the human placenta is **discoidal, haemochorial, deciduate and chorio-allantoic**.

## 6. Pregnancy and embryonic development

- Human pregnancy (gestation) lasts about **9 months** — about 266 days from fertilisation, or **280 days (40 weeks)** counted from the first day of the last menstrual period.
- Gestation of some other mammals: cat about 2 months, dog about 2 months, elephant about 22 months (longest among mammals).
- It is divided into three **trimesters** of three months each.

| Time | Development (NCERT) |
|---|---|
| End of **1 month** | **Heart** forms; the heartbeat is the first sign of a growing foetus (heard with a stethoscope a little later) |
| End of **2nd month** | **Limbs and digits** develop |
| End of **12 weeks** (first trimester) | **Most of the major organ systems are formed**; limbs and external genital organs well developed |
| **5th month** | First **movements of the foetus** (quickening) and **hair on the head** |
| End of **24 weeks** (second trimester) | Body covered with fine hair (lanugo), **eyelids separate**, **eyelashes** form |
| End of **9 months** | Foetus fully developed and ready for delivery |

The **embryo** stage is up to about 8 weeks; from then on it is called a **foetus**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q101 asked: "At what interval of pregnancy, the foetus fills the entire uterus? — (A) By 12 weeks (B) By 14 weeks (C) By 24 weeks." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) By 12 weeks.** This matches the embryology fact that by about the end of the first trimester the growing gestational sac fills the uterine cavity (decidua capsularis fuses with decidua parietalis). In plain terms the foetus itself is still small at 12 weeks; read the question as "the conceptus fills the uterine cavity."

### 6.1 Twins

| Type | Origin | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Monozygotic (identical)** | One zygote splits into two embryos | Same sex, same genes, same blood group; may share a placenta |
| **Dizygotic (fraternal)** | Two ova fertilised by two sperms | May be of different sex; as similar as ordinary siblings |
| **Conjoined (Siamese)** | Incomplete splitting of one embryo | Joined at some part of the body |

## 7. Parturition (childbirth)

- **Parturition** — the process of delivery of the foetus at the end of pregnancy.
- It is caused by vigorous contraction of the **myometrium** and is induced by a complex **neuroendocrine mechanism**.
- The signals for parturition **start from the fully developed foetus and the placenta**, which cause mild uterine contractions called the **foetal ejection reflex**.
- This triggers release of **oxytocin** from the mother's **posterior pituitary**. Oxytocin acts on the uterine muscle and causes **stronger** contractions, which in turn cause **more** oxytocin release — a **positive feedback loop**. Contractions grow stronger and stronger until the baby is expelled through the birth canal.
- Soon after the baby, the **placenta (afterbirth)** is also expelled.
- **Relaxin** softens the cervix and pelvic ligaments; **prostaglandins** and a rise in foetal **cortisol** (from foetal adrenal) and falling progesterone action also help start labour (B.Sc. points).
- Synthetic oxytocin is used by doctors to **induce delivery**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q102 asked: "The hormones involved in the process of parturition are — (A) LH and FSH (B) estradiol and progesterone (C) oxytocin and relaxin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) oxytocin and relaxin.**

> **Remember:** Oxytocin = "quick birth" (Greek). It is made in the **hypothalamus** and **released** from the **posterior pituitary**. Parturition and milk ejection are the classic examples of **positive feedback** in the body.

## 8. Lactation

- The mammary glands differentiate during pregnancy and start producing milk towards the end of pregnancy — **lactation**. This helps the mother feed the newborn.
- **Prolactin** (anterior pituitary) — **milk production (synthesis)**.
- **Oxytocin** (posterior pituitary) — **milk ejection (let-down)** in response to the baby's suckling (neuro-hormonal reflex).
- The milk produced in the **first few days** after birth is **colostrum** — yellowish, rich in protein and **several antibodies, mainly IgA**, which give the newborn **passive (natural) immunity** against infections. Breastfeeding during the initial period of infant growth is recommended by doctors for bringing up a healthy baby.
- Breastfeeding often delays the return of ovulation — the basis of the **lactational amenorrhoea** method (effective only up to about six months after delivery).

| Hormone | Source | Role in pregnancy/birth/lactation |
|---|---|---|
| hCG | Placenta | Keeps corpus luteum alive; pregnancy test |
| Progesterone | Corpus luteum → placenta | Maintains pregnancy |
| Oxytocin | Posterior pituitary | Uterine contraction; milk ejection |
| Relaxin | Ovary (late pregnancy) | Relaxes pelvic ligaments |
| Prolactin | Anterior pituitary | Milk synthesis |
| hPL | Placenta | Breast development, metabolic changes |

## 9. Worked problems on the cycle

The **luteal phase is fairly constant (about 14 days)** because the corpus luteum lives a fixed time. The follicular phase is the part that varies. So **ovulation happens about 14 days before the next menstruation**, not always on "day 14".

**Problem 1:** A woman has a regular 32-day cycle. On which day does she probably ovulate? — 32 − 14 = **day 18**.

**Problem 2:** A woman with a 26-day cycle — ovulation? — 26 − 14 = **day 12**.

**Problem 3:** Her last period began on 1 March and her cycle is 28 days. When is the next period expected, and what is the fertile window? — Next period on **29 March**; ovulation near **14–15 March**; fertile window about **10–17 March** (sperm life 2–3 days before + egg life 1 day after).

**Problem 4:** Expected date of delivery (Naegele's rule, B.Sc./nursing level): add **9 months and 7 days** to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). LMP 1 March → EDD about **8 December**.

**Problem 5:** If pregnancy test kits detect hCG, why is a test done too early sometimes negative? — Because hCG is produced only **after implantation** (day 6–7 after fertilisation) and takes a few more days to reach a detectable level in urine.

## 10. Ovarian cycle and uterine cycle compared

| Point | Ovarian cycle | Uterine (endometrial) cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Organ | Ovary | Uterus |
| Phases | Follicular → ovulation → luteal | Menstrual → proliferative → secretory |
| Controlled by | FSH, LH (pituitary) | Oestrogen, progesterone (ovary) |
| Key event | Release of the secondary oocyte | Building and shedding of the endometrium |
| End product | Corpus luteum → corpus albicans | Menstrual flow (blood + tissue) |

**Changes in the mother during pregnancy (NCERT/B.Sc.):** heart rate and blood volume rise; increased several-fold levels of oestrogen, progesterone, cortisol, prolactin and thyroxine; breasts enlarge; basal metabolic rate rises; the uterus grows from about 50 g to about 1 kg. Iron and folic acid supplements are given in India under maternal-health programmes to prevent anaemia and neural-tube defects.

## 11. Menstrual cycle vs oestrous cycle

| Point | Menstrual cycle | Oestrous cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Found in | Primates — humans, apes, monkeys | Other mammals — cow, sheep, dog, rat, horse, deer |
| Bleeding | **Yes** — endometrium shed at the end of each cycle | **No** — endometrium is reabsorbed |
| Sexual receptivity | Any time in the cycle | Only during **oestrus ("heat")**, near ovulation |
| Cycle names | Menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, luteal | Pro-oestrus, oestrus, met-oestrus, di-oestrus (an-oestrus = resting season) |
| Breeding | Continuous breeders | Many are **seasonal breeders** |

## 12. Common disorders of pregnancy and the cycle

| Disorder | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Ectopic pregnancy** | Implantation outside the uterus, usually in the fallopian tube |
| **Miscarriage** (spontaneous abortion) | Natural loss of pregnancy before about 20 weeks |
| **Pre-eclampsia** | High blood pressure and protein in urine in late pregnancy; can progress to fits (eclampsia) |
| **Gestational diabetes** | High blood sugar first appearing in pregnancy (hPL and other hormones oppose insulin) |
| **Anaemia in pregnancy** | Very common in India and Bihar; prevented by iron–folic acid tablets |
| **PCOS** (polycystic ovary syndrome) | Many small follicles that fail to ovulate, irregular cycles, excess androgens |
| **Endometriosis** | Endometrial tissue growing outside the uterus; painful periods, infertility |
| **Erythroblastosis foetalis** | Rh⁻ mother with Rh⁺ foetus: mother's anti-Rh antibodies (IgG) cross the placenta in a later pregnancy and destroy foetal RBCs; prevented by anti-D injection after the first delivery |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Ovulation is caused by LH**, not FSH and not oestrogen. Oestrogen only triggers the LH surge (positive feedback).
- Progesterone is **highest in the luteal (secretory) phase** (around day 21), not at ovulation.
- **Menopause** = stopping of cycles; **menarche** = first cycle; do not swap them.
- Oestrous cycle (non-primates) vs menstrual cycle (primates only).
- **Acrosome reaction → before fusion (in sperm); cortical reaction → after fusion (in egg)**.
- Polyspermy is blocked by the **cortical (zona) reaction**.
- The **morula** is 8–16 cells; the **blastocyst** has a cavity, trophoblast and inner cell mass. The embryo comes from the **inner cell mass**, the placenta (foetal part) from the **trophoblast**.
- Implantation is about **day 6–7** after fertilisation, in the uterus; tubal implantation = ectopic pregnancy.
- **hCG** (not progesterone) is what pregnancy kits detect.
- By NCERT, **relaxin is from the ovary** in late pregnancy.
- Umbilical **arteries** carry **deoxygenated** blood (from foetus); the umbilical **vein** carries **oxygenated** blood (to foetus).
- Human placenta: **discoidal, haemochorial, deciduate**. Cow: cotyledonary. Pig: diffuse. Dog/cat: zonary.
- Prolactin = milk **production**; oxytocin = milk **ejection**.
- **Colostrum** antibody = **IgA**; antibody crossing the placenta = **IgG**.

8. Which layer of the blastocyst forms the placenta? — **Trophoblast**.
9. Which part of the blastocyst forms the embryo? — **Inner cell mass**.

11. Name a hormone made only during pregnancy. — **hCG** (also hPL).
12. What type of placenta do humans have (by shape)? — **Discoidal**.
13. By which month do limbs and digits develop? — **End of the second month**.
14. When are the first foetal movements usually felt? — **Fifth month**.
15. Which hormone causes strong uterine contractions in labour? — **Oxytocin**.
16. What type of feedback controls parturition? — **Positive feedback**.
17. Which hormone produces milk? — **Prolactin**.
18. Which antibody dominates colostrum? — **IgA**.
19. What is implantation outside the uterus called? — **Ectopic pregnancy** (usually tubal).
20. Twins of different sex are which type? — **Dizygotic (fraternal)**.
