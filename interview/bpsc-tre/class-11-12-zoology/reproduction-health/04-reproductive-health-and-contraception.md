This chapter is NCERT class 12 "Reproductive Health" expanded with the facts TRE 1.0 and 2.0 actually asked: emergency pills within 72 hours, how oral pills work, which contraceptives are for men, the first test-tube babies, and the history of syphilis. Expect 3–5 questions from here in the Zoology section — they are among the easiest marks in the paper if the numbers and names are learnt exactly.

> **In simple words:** Reproductive health means a healthy and safe sexual and reproductive life — physical, emotional and social. India's population grew very fast because deaths fell while births stayed high, so the government promotes small families. Contraceptives prevent pregnancy by stopping sperms and eggs from meeting (condoms, tubectomy), by stopping ovulation (pills), or by stopping implantation (IUDs). Sexually transmitted infections spread by unsafe sex. Couples who cannot have children can be helped by IVF ("test-tube baby") and related methods.

## 1. Reproductive health

- **WHO definition:** reproductive health means a **total well-being in all aspects of reproduction**, i.e., **physical, emotional, behavioural and social**.
- **India was the first country in the world** to start action plans and programmes at a national level for family planning — in **1951** (the First Five-Year Plan; formally launched in 1952).
- These programmes were improved and widened under the name **Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH) programmes** (from **1997**).
- Steps taken: awareness through audio-visual and print media; sex education in schools (to remove myths); educating people about birth control, care of pregnant mothers, post-natal care of mother and child, importance of breastfeeding, equal opportunities for male and female children; preventing child marriage; **ban on amniocentesis for sex determination**; massive child immunisation.
- Signs of improved reproductive health: lower **maternal mortality rate (MMR)** and **infant mortality rate (IMR)**, early detection and cure of STIs, more medical help for deliveries, better post-natal care, small families.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MMR** (maternal mortality ratio) | Mothers dying from pregnancy-related causes per **1,00,000 live births** |
| **IMR** (infant mortality rate) | Deaths of babies under one year per **1,000 live births** |
| **TFR** (total fertility rate) | Average number of children a woman would have in her lifetime; **2.1 = replacement level** |
| **Sex ratio** (India's convention) | Females per 1,000 males |

## 2. Population explosion

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| World population in 1900 | About 2 billion |
| World population in 2000 | About 6 billion |
| World reaches 8 billion | 15 November 2022 (UN) |
| India at Independence (1947) | About 350 million |
| India crosses 1 billion | Around **2000** |
| India, Census 2011 | **1.21 billion (121 crore)**; NCERT says it crossed 1.2 billion in May 2011 |
| Growth rate in 2011 (NCERT) | About **1.7%** per year (17 per 1,000) — population could double in about 33 years |
| India becomes the most populous country | April 2023 (UN estimate), overtaking China |
| India TFR (NFHS-5, 2019–21) | **2.0** (below replacement) |
| **Bihar** | Census 2011: about **10.4 crore**; highest density (**1,106 per km²**) and highest decadal growth (**25.4%**) among states; **TFR 3.0 in NFHS-5 — the highest in India** |

**Causes of rapid growth** (NCERT): sharp **decline in death rate**, **MMR and IMR** (better health care), and an increase in the number of people in the **reproductive age**.

**Worked example — doubling time:** a quick rule is **doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate (%)**. At 1.7% a year, 70 ÷ 1.7 ≈ **41 years**. NCERT's text says the population "could double in 33 years" at this rate; 33 years actually matches a growth rate of about 2.1%. If an MCQ quotes NCERT, answer 33 years; if it asks you to calculate, use the rule of 70.

**Worked example — birth and death rates:** in a town of 1,00,000 people, 2,500 babies are born and 800 people die in a year. Birth rate = 25 per 1,000; death rate = 8 per 1,000; natural growth rate = 17 per 1,000 = **1.7%**. (More population calculations are in the ecology book: [Population, Community and Biogeochemical Cycles](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/population-community-and-biogeochemical-cycles).)

**Steps to control it:** motivating smaller families with contraceptives; raising the **marriageable age** (legally **18 years for females and 21 for males**; a 2021 bill to raise the female age to 21 lapsed — check the current position); slogans like "Hum Do Hamare Do" and incentives to couples with small families. **World Population Day — 11 July.**

## 3. Contraception

### 3.1 Qualities of an ideal contraceptive

It should be **user-friendly, easily available, effective and reversible**, with **no or least side effects**, and it should **in no way interfere with sexual drive, desire or the sexual act**.

### 3.2 Overview

| Group | Methods | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural (traditional)** | Periodic abstinence, withdrawal (coitus interruptus), lactational amenorrhoea | Avoid meeting of sperm and ovum |
| **Barrier** | Condoms, diaphragms, cervical caps, vaults, spermicides | Physical block between sperm and ovum |
| **IUDs** | Lippes loop, CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375, Progestasert, LNG-20 | Kill/slow sperms, stop implantation |
| **Oral pills** | Combined pills, Saheli (Chhaya) | Stop ovulation and implantation, thicken cervical mucus |
| **Injectables and implants** | Progestogen injections (Antara), implants under skin | Same as pills, long-acting |
| **Emergency contraception** | Progestogen or progestogen–oestrogen pills, or IUD, within 72 h | Prevent ovulation/fertilisation/implantation |
| **Surgical (sterilisation)** | Vasectomy (male), tubectomy (female) | Block gamete transport — permanent |

### 3.3 Natural methods

- **Periodic abstinence (rhythm method)** — avoid coitus from **day 10 to 17** of the menstrual cycle, when ovulation is expected (the "fertile period").
- **Withdrawal or coitus interruptus** — the male partner withdraws the penis from the vagina just before ejaculation.
- **Lactational amenorrhoea** — absence of menstruation during intense breastfeeding after delivery; ovulation and the cycle do not occur, so the chance of conception is nearly nil — **effective only up to a maximum of six months after delivery**, and only with full breastfeeding.
- No medicines or devices, so **no side effects**, but **failure rate is high**.

### 3.4 Barrier methods

| Barrier | User | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Condom** | Male (also **female condoms**) | Thin rubber/latex sheath over the penis (or lining the vagina and cervix) just before coitus; brand **"Nirodh"** is the popular free/subsidised male condom in India; **disposable**, can be self-inserted, gives **privacy**; also **protects against STIs and AIDS** |
| **Diaphragm, cervical cap, vault** | Female | Rubber devices inserted into the female tract to **cover the cervix** during coitus; **reusable** |
| **Spermicidal creams, jellies, foams** | Female | Usually used along with barriers to increase efficiency; kill sperms |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q114 asked: "Condoms are one of the most popular contraceptives because of which of the following reasons? — (A) These are effective barriers for insemination (B) They do not interfere with coital act (C) These help in reducing the risk of STDs." **Official answer: (D) More than one of the above.** NCERT stresses (A) and (C) — barrier plus protection from STIs/AIDS — along with easy availability.

### 3.5 Intrauterine devices (IUDs)

Inserted by **doctors or trained nurses** into the **uterus through the vagina**.

| Type | Examples | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-medicated** | **Lippes loop** | Increases **phagocytosis of sperms** inside the uterus |
| **Copper-releasing** | **CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375** | **Cu ions suppress sperm motility and the fertilising capacity** of sperms |
| **Hormone-releasing** | **Progestasert, LNG-20** | Make the **uterus unsuitable for implantation** and the **cervix hostile to sperms** |

IUDs are **ideal for females who want to delay pregnancy and/or space children**. They are one of the **most widely accepted** methods in India.

### 3.6 Oral contraceptive pills

- Contain small doses of **progestogens alone or progestogen–oestrogen combinations**.
- Taken **daily for 21 days**, starting preferably **within the first five days** of the menstrual cycle. After a gap of **7 days** (during which menstruation occurs), the course is repeated as long as the woman wants to prevent conception.
- They **inhibit ovulation and implantation** and **alter the quality of cervical mucus** to prevent or retard entry of sperms.
- Highly effective, with few side effects.
- **Saheli** — the "new" oral contraceptive for females, developed by scientists at the **Central Drug Research Institute (CDRI), Lucknow**. It is a **once-a-week** pill with very few side effects and high contraceptive value. Chemically it is **non-steroidal** — **centchroman (ormeloxifene)**. Since 2016 it is supplied free in the government programme under the name **Chhaya**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q115 asked: "Oral contraceptives prevent pregnancy by — (A) killing the ovum (B) preventing ovulation (C) blocking the fertilization." **Official answer: (B) preventing ovulation.** (They also block implantation and thicken cervical mucus, but the main textbook answer is "inhibit ovulation".)

### 3.7 Injectables and implants

- **Progestogens alone or in combination with oestrogen** can be given as **injections** or **implants under the skin**. Their mode of action is like pills, but **effective periods are much longer**.
- **Antara** — injectable **DMPA** (depot medroxyprogesterone acetate), one shot every **3 months**; added to the national programme in 2016–17.

### 3.8 Emergency contraceptives

**Administration of progestogens or progestogen–oestrogen combinations or IUDs within 72 hours of coitus** has been found very effective as emergency contraception, to avoid possible pregnancy due to **rape or casual unprotected intercourse**. (Commonly known as the "morning-after pill", e.g., levonorgestrel 1.5 mg; it is **not** an abortion pill.)

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q112 asked: "Emergency contraceptives are effective if used within — (A) 72 hours of coitus (B) 72 hours of ovulation (C) 72 hours of menstruation." **Official answer: (A) 72 hours of coitus.**

### 3.9 Surgical methods (sterilisation)

Advised for the male/female partner as a **terminal method** to prevent any more pregnancies.

| Method | In | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **Vasectomy** | Male | A small part of the **vas deferens** is removed or tied up through a small incision on the **scrotum** |
| **Tubectomy** | Female | A small part of the **fallopian tube** is removed or tied up through a small incision in the abdomen or through the vagina |

They block **gamete transport**. They are **highly effective** but **reversibility is very poor**. Hormones and sexual function are unaffected (testes still make testosterone; ovaries still cycle). "No-scalpel vasectomy" (NSV) is promoted in India.

**Methods available to men:** **condoms, withdrawal and vasectomy** only (male hormonal pills/injections such as RISUG are still under trial — RISUG was developed in India at IIT Kharagpur and tested by ICMR).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q104 asked: "The available male contraceptives for male are — (A) oral pills and sterilization (B) injectables and diaphragm (C) vasectomy and condoms." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) vasectomy and condoms.**

### 3.10 Side effects (NCERT)

Possible ill effects of contraceptives include nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding and even breast cancer — though not very significant, they should not be totally ignored. Contraceptives are **not** meant to be a regular requirement for good reproductive health; they are used to avoid unwanted pregnancy or to space children.

| Method | Reversible? | Protects against STIs? | Mainly for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condom | Yes | **Yes** | Male (female condom too) |
| IUD | Yes | No | Female — spacing |
| Pills / Saheli | Yes | No | Female |
| Emergency pill | Yes | No | Female, within 72 h |
| Vasectomy / tubectomy | Very poorly | No | Permanent |
| Natural methods | Yes | No | Couple |

### 3.11 Which step does each method block?

| Step in reproduction | Methods that block it |
|---|---|
| **Gamete formation / release** | Oral pills, injectables, implants (stop **ovulation**); lactational amenorrhoea |
| **Gamete transport** | **Vasectomy** (sperm), **tubectomy** (ovum) |
| **Meeting of sperm and ovum** | Condoms, diaphragm, cervical cap, vault, periodic abstinence, withdrawal, spermicides |
| **Sperm survival / motility** | **Copper IUDs**, Lippes loop (phagocytosis), spermicides, pills (thick cervical mucus) |
| **Implantation** | **Hormonal IUDs**, pills, emergency contraceptives, Saheli |

> **Exam angle:** If a question asks "which contraceptive prevents implantation", look for **hormone-releasing IUDs** or pills; "which kills or slows sperms in the uterus" → **copper IUD**; "which blocks gamete transport" → **sterilisation**.

**Failure rates (approximate, typical use):** natural methods and withdrawal are least reliable; condoms moderately reliable; pills and IUDs highly reliable; sterilisation most reliable (failure well below 1%).

## 4. Medical termination of pregnancy (MTP)

- **Intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term** = MTP or **induced abortion**.
- Nearly **45 to 50 million MTPs** are performed in a year all over the world — about **one-fifth** of all conceived pregnancies (NCERT).
- India legalised MTP with the **MTP Act, 1971**, with strict conditions to prevent misuse.
- **NCERT text (citing the amended Act):** a pregnancy may be terminated within the **first 12 weeks** on the opinion of **one registered medical practitioner**; between **12 and 24 weeks**, **two** registered medical practitioners must agree, on grounds such as risk to the mother's life or grave injury to her physical or mental health, or a substantial risk of serious foetal abnormality.
- **Actual MTP (Amendment) Act, 2021:** up to **20 weeks** on one doctor's opinion; **20–24 weeks** for special categories (rape survivors, minors, etc.) with two doctors; beyond 24 weeks only for substantial foetal abnormality on a **Medical Board's** advice. Know both versions; answer as per NCERT if the question follows its wording.
- MTPs are considered relatively **safe during the first trimester** (up to 12 weeks).
- Problems: misuse for **female foeticide**; majority of MTPs are performed **illegally by unqualified quacks**, which is unsafe and can be fatal.

## 5. Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)

Also called **venereal diseases (VD)** or **reproductive tract infections (RTI)**.

| Disease | Pathogen | Type | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gonorrhoea** | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (gonococcus) | Bacterium | Burning urination, pus discharge; can cause ophthalmia neonatorum in newborns |
| **Syphilis** | ***Treponema pallidum*** | Bacterium (spirochaete) | Primary painless **chancre**, secondary rash, tertiary damage to heart and brain; **VDRL test**; penicillin |
| **Chlamydiasis** | *Chlamydia trachomatis* | Bacterium | Often silent; major cause of infertility (PID) |
| **Genital herpes** | **Herpes simplex virus-2** (HSV-2) | DNA virus | Painful blisters; **not curable** |
| **Genital warts** | **Human papillomavirus (HPV)** | DNA virus | Some types cause **cervical cancer**; HPV vaccine available |
| **Trichomoniasis** | *Trichomonas vaginalis* | Protozoan | Vaginal itching and discharge |
| **Hepatitis B** | Hepatitis B virus | DNA virus | Affects liver; vaccine available; **not completely curable** |
| **AIDS** | **HIV** | RNA retrovirus | Destroys immune system; **not curable** |

- **Except hepatitis B, genital herpes and HIV infections**, the other STIs are **completely curable** if detected early and treated properly.
- Early symptoms are minor (itching, fluid discharge, slight pain, swelling in the genital region); infected females may often be **asymptomatic**.
- Complications: **pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)**, abortions, still births, ectopic pregnancies, **infertility**, even **cancer of the reproductive tract**.
- Highest incidence in the **15–24 years** age group.
- **Hepatitis B and HIV also spread** by sharing injection needles, surgical instruments, transfusion of infected blood and from infected mother to foetus.
- **Prevention (NCERT):** avoid sex with unknown partners or multiple partners; always use condoms during coitus; in case of doubt, go to a qualified doctor for early detection and complete treatment.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q113 asked: "From the sexually transmitted diseases mentioned below, identify the one which does not specifically affect the sex organs. — (A) Syphilis (B) AIDS (C) Gonorrhea." **Official answer: (B) AIDS.** AIDS attacks the immune system (helper T cells) of the whole body.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q105 asked: "Which was the first well-recorded European outbreak of sexually transmitted disease (STD)? — (A) Chlamydia (B) Syphilis (C) Gonorrhoea." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) Syphilis.** The first well-recorded outbreak was in **1494–95** among French troops besieging **Naples** (hence the old name "French disease"); the name "syphilis" comes from a 1530 poem by the Italian physician **Girolamo Fracastoro**.

## 6. Infertility and assisted reproductive technologies (ART)

**Infertility** — inability to conceive or produce children in spite of unprotected sexual cohabitation (usually for one year or more). Causes may be **physical, congenital, diseases, drugs, immunological or even psychological**, and the problem may be in the **male or the female** partner (in India the female is often wrongly blamed). Specialised health-care units — **infertility clinics** — diagnose and correct some of these disorders.

| Causes in the male | Causes in the female |
|---|---|
| Low sperm count (oligospermia) or no sperms (azoospermia) | Blocked fallopian tubes (after infection, PID) |
| Poor motility or abnormal shape of sperms | Ovulation failure (PCOS, thyroid or prolactin problems) |
| Cryptorchidism, varicocele, mumps orchitis | Endometriosis, uterine fibroids |
| Blocked vas deferens or epididymis | Hostile cervical mucus, antisperm antibodies |
| Impotence (erectile dysfunction) | Age-related fall in egg quality |
| Smoking, alcohol, anabolic steroids | Smoking, obesity or very low body weight |

| ART | Full form | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **IVF–ET** ("test-tube baby") | In vitro fertilisation followed by embryo transfer | Ova from the wife/donor and sperms from the husband/donor are made to form a zygote **outside the body**, under conditions similar to the body, and the embryo is then transferred |
| **ZIFT** | Zygote intra-fallopian transfer | The **zygote or early embryo (up to 8 blastomeres)** is transferred into the **fallopian tube** |
| **IUT** | Intra-uterine transfer | Embryos with **more than 8 blastomeres** are transferred into the **uterus** |
| **GIFT** | Gamete intra-fallopian transfer | Transfer of an **ovum collected from a donor** into the fallopian tube of another female who cannot produce ova but can provide a suitable environment for fertilisation and development (fertilisation occurs **inside** the body) |
| **ICSI** | Intra-cytoplasmic sperm injection | A **sperm is directly injected into the ovum** in the laboratory, then the embryo is transferred |
| **AI** | Artificial insemination | Semen from the husband or a healthy donor is introduced into the **vagina** or into the **uterus (IUI — intra-uterine insemination)**; used when the male cannot inseminate or has **very low sperm counts** |

Embryos formed by **in vivo fertilisation** (fusion of gametes within the female) can also be used for such transfer to assist females who cannot conceive.

> **Memory trick:** "**Z**ygote goes to the tube (**Z**IFT, ≤ 8 cells); bigger embryo goes to the **U**terus (I**U**T); **G**amete goes to the tube (**G**IFT)."

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q42 asked: "Test tube baby means a baby born when — (A) developed in a test tube (B) ovum is fertilized externally and thereafter implanted in the uterus (C) developed from a non-fertilized egg." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** Only fertilisation and the first few days happen outside; the baby develops in the mother's uterus.

### 6.1 Test-tube baby history

| Event | Details |
|---|---|
| World's first test-tube baby | **Louise Joy Brown**, born **25 July 1978**, Oldham, England |
| Scientists behind it | **Robert Edwards** (physiologist; **Nobel Prize 2010**) and **Patrick Steptoe** (gynaecologist) |
| India's first (and world's second) test-tube baby | **Durga**, later known as **Kanupriya Agarwal**, born **3 October 1978**, Kolkata — just **67 days** after Louise Brown |
| Scientist | **Dr Subhas Mukhopadhyay** (with Sunit Mukherji and Saroj Kanti Bhattacharya); his work was disbelieved in his lifetime; he died in 1981 and was recognised much later |
| India's first "officially documented" IVF baby | **Harsha Chawda**, born **6 August 1986**, Mumbai — Dr **Indira Hinduja** with ICMR's Dr T. C. Anand Kumar |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q103 asked: "The world's first test tube baby born in 1978 was — (A) Louise Brown (B) Kanupriya Agarwal (C) Robert Edwards." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) Louise Brown.** Kanupriya (Durga) was also born in 1978 but second; Robert Edwards is the scientist.

**Laws in India:** the **Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021** regulates ART clinics and banks; the **Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021** bans commercial surrogacy and allows only altruistic surrogacy. **Adoption** is also suggested by NCERT as a good option for childless couples.

## 7. Amniocentesis and prenatal tests

- **Amniocentesis** — a foetal test in which a small amount of **amniotic fluid** (with shed foetal cells) is drawn through a needle (usually at about 15–20 weeks) and the **chromosomal pattern** of foetal cells is studied.
- Proper use: detecting **genetic disorders** — Down syndrome (trisomy 21), haemophilia, sickle-cell anaemia, metabolic disorders — and foetal survival.
- Misuse: **foetal sex determination** leading to female foeticide. It is **banned for sex determination** under the **PNDT Act, 1994**, renamed the **Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act** in 2003.
- **Chorionic villus sampling (CVS)** — earlier test (about 10–13 weeks) using placental tissue. **Ultrasonography** — imaging; also misusable, hence regulated.

## Traps and exceptions

- Emergency contraception works **within 72 hours of coitus** — not of ovulation or menstruation.
- **Oral pills mainly inhibit ovulation**; **copper IUDs** mainly affect **sperm motility**; **hormonal IUDs** make the uterus unsuitable for implantation.
- **Saheli is once a week**, non-steroidal, from **CDRI Lucknow** — not daily, not hormonal.
- Lippes loop is **non-medicated**; CuT/Cu7/Multiload are **copper**; Progestasert/LNG-20 are **hormonal**.
- Vasectomy cuts the **vas deferens** (not the urethra, not the testis); tubectomy cuts the **fallopian tube** (not the uterus).
- Only the **condom** among contraceptives protects against STIs.
- Lactational amenorrhoea works only up to **6 months** after delivery.
- **AIDS does not specifically affect sex organs** — it affects immunity.
- **Hepatitis B, genital herpes and HIV** are the STIs that are not completely curable.
- **GIFT transfers gametes** (fertilisation inside the body); **ZIFT transfers a zygote** (fertilisation in the lab).
- **ZIFT: up to 8 blastomeres → tube; IUT: more than 8 → uterus.**
- Louise Brown is the baby; **Robert Edwards** and **Patrick Steptoe** are the scientists.
- India's first test-tube baby = **Durga/Kanupriya (Kolkata, 3 Oct 1978)**; officially documented = **Harsha (Mumbai, 1986)**.
- Amniocentesis itself is legal for genetic disorders; only **sex determination** is banned.
