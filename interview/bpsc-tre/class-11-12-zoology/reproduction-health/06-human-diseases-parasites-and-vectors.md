This chapter covers NCERT class 12 "Common diseases in humans" and adds B.Sc. parasitology (life cycles, hosts, infective stages) and the vector table that TRE loves. TRE 2.0 asked "typhoid is caused by" and "malaria is transmitted by"; TRE 1.0 asked "which protozoan causes disease in humans" and a virus-classification question. Expect two to four questions — mostly "agent–disease–vector" matching.

> **In simple words:** Germs that cause disease are called pathogens — bacteria, viruses, protozoa (one-celled animals), worms (helminths) and fungi. Some spread through dirty food and water, some through air (coughs), some through blood and sex, and many through insect bites. The insect that carries a germ from one person to another is a vector. For each disease learn three things — the germ, how it spreads (vector), and the key test or symptom.

## 1. Basics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Health** (WHO) | A state of complete **physical, mental and social well-being**, not merely the absence of disease |
| **Pathogen** | Disease-causing organism |
| **Parasite** | Organism living on/in a host and harming it |
| **Definitive (primary) host** | Host in which the parasite reproduces **sexually** / becomes adult |
| **Intermediate (secondary) host** | Host in which larval or asexual stages live |
| **Monogenetic parasite** | Completes life cycle in **one host** (Entamoeba, Ascaris) |
| **Digenetic parasite** | Needs **two hosts** (Plasmodium, Wuchereria, Schistosoma, Taenia, Leishmania) |
| **Vector** | Living carrier that transmits a pathogen (usually an arthropod) |
| **Mechanical vector** | Just carries germs on its body (housefly) |
| **Biological vector** | Pathogen develops/multiplies inside it (Anopheles for Plasmodium) |
| **Reservoir** | Where a pathogen lives naturally (humans, animals, soil) |
| **Carrier** | Infected person with no symptoms who spreads disease (e.g., "Typhoid Mary") |
| **Endemic / epidemic / pandemic** | Always present locally / sudden rise in an area / worldwide spread |

**Infectious diseases** pass from one person to another (malaria, TB, AIDS). **Non-infectious diseases** do not (cancer, diabetes, hypertension). Cancer is the major non-infectious cause of death (see next chapter).

**Modes of transmission:**

| Route | Examples |
|---|---|
| Contaminated food and water (faeco-oral) | Typhoid, cholera, amoebiasis, ascariasis, polio, hepatitis A and E |
| Air / droplets | Common cold, pneumonia, TB, influenza, measles, COVID-19, diphtheria |
| Insect vectors | Malaria, dengue, filariasis, kala-azar, plague, JE |
| Blood, needles, sex, mother to child | AIDS, hepatitis B and C, syphilis |
| Direct contact | Ringworm, scabies, leprosy (prolonged contact) |
| Wounds / soil | Tetanus |
| Animal bite | Rabies |

## 2. Bacterial diseases

| Disease | Bacterium | Spread | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typhoid** | ***Salmonella typhi*** | Contaminated food and water | Enters small intestine, spreads by blood; **sustained high fever (39–40 °C)**, weakness, stomach pain, constipation, headache, loss of appetite; intestinal perforation in severe cases; **Widal test** |
| **Pneumonia** | ***Streptococcus pneumoniae***, ***Haemophilus influenzae*** | Droplets, sharing glasses/utensils | **Alveoli fill with fluid**; fever, chills, cough, headache; lips and fingernails may turn **grey to bluish** |
| **Cholera** | *Vibrio cholerae* (Koch, 1883) | Water | "Rice-water" stools, severe dehydration; **ORS** |
| **Bacillary dysentery** | *Shigella* | Food, water, flies | Blood and mucus in stool |
| **Tuberculosis** | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Koch, 1882) | Air | Lungs mostly; **BCG** vaccine; sputum test, CBNAAT; **World TB Day 24 March** |
| **Leprosy** (Hansen's disease) | *Mycobacterium leprae* (Hansen, 1873) | Prolonged close contact | Skin patches without sensation, nerve damage; **multi-drug therapy (MDT)** |
| **Diphtheria** | *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* | Droplets | Grey **false membrane** in throat; toxoid in DPT |
| **Tetanus** (lockjaw) | *Clostridium tetani* | Soil into wounds | Muscle spasms; toxoid (TT) |
| **Whooping cough** (pertussis) | *Bordetella pertussis* | Droplets | "Whoop" cough in children; DPT |
| **Plague** | ***Yersinia pestis*** | **Rat flea** *Xenopsylla cheopis* | Bubonic (swollen lymph nodes) and pneumonic forms; Surat outbreak 1994 |
| **Anthrax** | *Bacillus anthracis* | Spores from cattle, wool | First disease linked to a germ (Koch, 1876) |
| **Botulism** | *Clostridium botulinum* | Toxin in canned/preserved food | Food-borne intoxication; paralysis |
| **Leptospirosis** | *Leptospira* | Water with rat urine | Common after floods |
| **Gonorrhoea / syphilis** | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* / *Treponema pallidum* | Sexual | See chapter 4 |
| **Scrub typhus** | *Orientia tsutsugamushi* | Larval mite (chigger) | Eschar (black scab) at bite site; can cause AES |
| **Epidemic typhus** | *Rickettsia prowazekii* | Body louse | War and famine disease |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q121 asked: "Typhoid fever is caused by — (A) Treponema (B) Salmonella (C) Streptococcus." **Official answer: (B) Salmonella.** *Treponema* causes syphilis; *Streptococcus* causes pneumonia and sore throat.

**Typhoid Mary** — Mary Mallon, a cook in New York (early 1900s), a **healthy carrier** who infected many people while showing no symptoms.

## 3. Viral diseases

| Disease | Virus (genome) | Spread | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common cold** | **Rhinoviruses** (RNA) | Droplets; contaminated objects | Infects **nose and respiratory passage, not the lungs**; nasal congestion, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache, tiredness for **3–7 days** |
| Influenza | Influenza virus (RNA; H1N1 swine flu, H5N1 bird flu) | Droplets | Fever, body ache |
| **Measles** | Measles virus (RNA) | Droplets | Rash, Koplik's spots; MR vaccine |
| **Mumps** | Mumps virus (RNA) | Saliva | Swelling of **parotid** glands |
| **Chickenpox** | **Varicella-zoster virus (DNA)** | Droplets, contact | Itchy blisters; reactivates as herpes zoster (shingles) |
| **Smallpox** | Variola virus (DNA) | Droplets | **Eradicated (1980)** |
| **Polio** | Poliovirus (RNA, enterovirus) | Faeco-oral | Paralysis; India declared polio-free **27 March 2014** |
| **Rabies** | Rabies virus (RNA, bullet-shaped) | Bite of rabid dog, bat, monkey | **Hydrophobia**; almost always fatal once symptoms start; post-exposure vaccine |
| **Dengue** | Dengue virus (RNA, flavivirus; 4 serotypes) | ***Aedes aegypti*** (day biter) | "Breakbone fever", **platelet fall**, dengue haemorrhagic fever; NS1 test |
| **Chikungunya** | Chikungunya virus (RNA) | *Aedes* | Severe joint pain |
| **Zika** | Zika virus (RNA) | *Aedes* | Microcephaly in babies |
| **Yellow fever** | Yellow fever virus (RNA) | *Aedes aegypti* | Not found in India; vaccine needed for travel to Africa/S. America |
| **Japanese encephalitis** | JE virus (RNA, flavivirus) | ***Culex*** (*C. tritaeniorhynchus*); **pigs** amplify, water birds are reservoirs | Brain fever in children; part of AES in Bihar/UP |
| **Hepatitis A / E** | RNA viruses | Faeco-oral | Jaundice; E dangerous in pregnancy |
| **Hepatitis B** | **DNA** virus | Blood, sex, mother–child | Vaccine (recombinant); liver cancer risk |
| **Hepatitis C** | RNA virus | Blood | No vaccine; curable with new drugs |
| **AIDS** | HIV (RNA retrovirus) | Sex, blood, mother–child | See [Immunology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/immunology) |
| **COVID-19** | SARS-CoV-2 (RNA coronavirus) | Droplets/aerosols | Pandemic declared March 2020; RT-PCR test |
| **Nipah** | Nipah virus (RNA) | Fruit bats (*Pteropus*), pigs | Kerala outbreaks since 2018 |
| **Kyasanur forest disease** ("monkey fever") | KFD virus | **Ticks** (*Haemaphysalis*) | Karnataka |

**DNA viruses to remember:** **herpes group** (herpes simplex, **varicella-zoster**, **Epstein–Barr**, cytomegalovirus), **pox** (variola), **hepatitis B**, **adenovirus**, **papillomavirus (HPV)**. Most other human viruses are RNA viruses.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q73 asked: "Which of the following is not an RNA virus? — (A) Epstein-Barr virus (B) Varicella-Zoster virus (C) Herpes simplex virus." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (E) None of the above.** Disagreement: all three are **DNA viruses** of the herpes family, so each one is "not an RNA virus" and the logical choice would be **(D) More than one of the above**. Remember the key, but know the biology — EBV, VZV and HSV are all DNA (herpes) viruses. (Only a provisional key was available; check the final key if it is published.)

## 4. Protozoan diseases

| Disease | Protozoan | Vector / spread | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaria** | ***Plasmodium vivax, P. falciparum, P. malariae, P. ovale*** (and *P. knowlesi*) | **Female *Anopheles*** | Chills and high fever recurring every 3–4 days; *P. falciparum* = **malignant tertian**, most dangerous (cerebral malaria) |
| **Amoebiasis** (amoebic dysentery) | ***Entamoeba histolytica*** | Food/water with cysts; **houseflies** are mechanical carriers | Lives in **large intestine**; constipation, abdominal pain and cramps, **stools with excess mucus and blood clots** |
| **Kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis) | ***Leishmania donovani*** | **Sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*, female) | Fever, enlarged **spleen and liver**, anaemia, darkened skin; **Bihar** carries most of India's cases |
| Oriental sore (cutaneous leishmaniasis) | *Leishmania tropica* | Sandfly | Skin ulcers |
| **African sleeping sickness** | ***Trypanosoma gambiense / T. rhodesiense*** | **Tsetse fly** (*Glossina*) | Fever then brain involvement, sleepiness |
| Chagas disease | *Trypanosoma cruzi* | **Reduviid ("kissing") bug** *Triatoma* | South America; heart damage |
| Giardiasis | *Giardia intestinalis (lamblia)* | Water | Diarrhoea ("traveller's diarrhoea"); first protozoan seen by Leeuwenhoek |
| Trichomoniasis | *Trichomonas vaginalis* | Sexual | STI |
| Toxoplasmosis | *Toxoplasma gondii* | Cat faeces, undercooked meat | Danger in pregnancy and AIDS |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q117 asked: "Which is the disease causing Protozoa in human? — (A) Euglena (B) Trichonympha (C) Leishmania." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Leishmania.** *Euglena* is free-living; *Trichonympha* is a harmless symbiont in the gut of termites that digests cellulose.

## 5. Helminth (worm) diseases

| Disease | Worm | Group | How it enters | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ascariasis** | ***Ascaris lumbricoides*** (roundworm) | Nematode | Eggs in contaminated water, vegetables, fruits | **Internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anaemia, blockage of intestinal passage** (NCERT) |
| **Filariasis / elephantiasis** | ***Wuchereria bancrofti, W. (Brugia) malayi*** | Nematode | Bite of **female *Culex*** mosquito | Adults in **lymphatic vessels**, mostly of lower limbs; chronic inflammation; **swelling of limbs and genital organs** (hydrocele) |
| Hookworm disease | *Ancylostoma duodenale* | Nematode | Larvae **bore through skin** of bare feet | Sucks blood → severe **anaemia** |
| Pinworm (enterobiasis) | *Enterobius vermicularis* | Nematode | Eggs via fingers (self-reinfection) | Anal itching in children |
| Guinea worm | *Dracunculus medinensis* | Nematode | Drinking water with infected **Cyclops** | India declared free in **2000** |
| Taeniasis / cysticercosis | ***Taenia solium*** (pork tapeworm) | Cestode | Undercooked pork (cysticerci) or eggs from faeces | Larvae in brain (**neurocysticercosis**) — common cause of epilepsy in India; *T. saginata* = beef tapeworm |
| Hydatid disease | *Echinococcus granulosus* | Cestode | Eggs from dog faeces | Hydatid cysts in liver |
| Liver fluke disease | *Fasciola hepatica* | Trematode | Metacercaria on water plants; snail *Lymnaea* intermediate | Mainly sheep (liver rot) |
| **Schistosomiasis** (bilharziasis) | ***Schistosoma*** (blood fluke) | Trematode | **Cercaria larvae bore through skin** in water; **snail** intermediate host | Blood in urine (*S. haematobium*) or intestinal damage (*S. mansoni*, *S. japonicum*) |

## 7. Vectors — the master table

| Vector | Diseases transmitted | Identification / habits |
|---|---|---|
| **Female *Anopheles*** | **Malaria** | Rests at an **angle** to the surface; eggs laid singly with floats; bites at night; main Indian vectors *An. culicifacies* (rural), *An. stephensi* (urban) |
| ***Aedes aegypti*** (and *Ae. albopictus*) | **Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever** | Black body with **white stripes** ("tiger mosquito"); **day biter**; breeds in clean stored water — coolers, tyres, pots |
| **Female *Culex*** | **Filariasis** (*C. quinquefasciatus*), **Japanese encephalitis** (*C. tritaeniorhynchus*), West Nile | Rests **parallel** to surface; eggs in rafts; breeds in dirty water, drains |
| **Sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*) | **Kala-azar** | Tiny, hairy; breeds in cracks of mud walls and damp, dark places; poor flier (hops near ground) |
| **Tsetse fly** (*Glossina*) | **Sleeping sickness** | Africa only |
| **Housefly** (*Musca domestica*) | **Typhoid, cholera, amoebiasis, dysentery, trachoma** | **Mechanical** carrier |
| **Rat flea** (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) | **Plague**; murine typhus | Leaves dead rats and bites humans |
| **Body louse** (*Pediculus humanus*) | **Epidemic typhus**, relapsing fever, trench fever | Lives in clothes |
| **Bed bug** (***Cimex lectularius***) | **No proven disease transmission**; causes itchy bites, sleeplessness, sometimes anaemia | Hides in beds and cracks; feeds at night. Viruses (hepatitis B) and *T. cruzi* have been found in bed bugs, but they are not established vectors |
| **Reduviid bug** (*Triatoma*) | Chagas disease | South America |
| **Ticks** | KFD, Lyme disease (*Borrelia*) | — |
| **Mites** (chiggers) | Scrub typhus | Scabies is caused by the mite *Sarcoptes scabiei* itself |
| **Cyclops** (water flea) | Guinea worm | Intermediate host |
| **Snails** | Schistosomiasis, liver fluke | Intermediate hosts, not insects |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q122 asked: "Malaria is transmitted by — (A) female Culex (B) male Anopheles (C) female Anopheles." **Official answer: (C) female Anopheles.** Only females suck blood (needed for egg development); males feed on plant juices.

> **Memory trick:** "**A**nopheles — m**A**laria; **AE**des — d**E**ngue (and chikungunya); **C**ulex — **C**an't walk properly (elephantiasis) and **C**erebral JE; **S**andfly — kala-azar (black **S**kin); **T**setse — **T**rypanosoma."

## 8. Life cycle of *Plasmodium*

*Plasmodium* (phylum Apicomplexa/Sporozoa) is **digenetic**. The **female *Anopheles*** is the **definitive host** (sexual phase); **man is the intermediate host** (asexual phase). Discovery: **Charles Laveran** saw the parasite in blood (1880; Nobel 1907); **Ronald Ross** proved mosquito transmission (1897, at Secunderabad; Nobel 1902).

**In man (asexual — schizogony):**
1. Infected female *Anopheles* bites and injects **sporozoites** (the **infective stage for man**) with saliva.
2. Sporozoites reach the **liver** and multiply asexually (**pre-erythrocytic / exo-erythrocytic schizogony**). In *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* some stay dormant as **hypnozoites** → relapses months later.
3. Liver cells burst, releasing **merozoites**, which attack **red blood cells**.
4. In RBCs: **ring stage (signet ring) → trophozoite → schizont → merozoites** (erythrocytic schizogony, the "Golgi cycle").
5. RBCs rupture and release a toxin, **haemozoin**, responsible for the **chill and high fever** recurring every 3–4 days (tertian — every 48 h: *vivax*, *ovale*, *falciparum*; quartan — every 72 h: *malariae*).
6. Some merozoites become **gametocytes** (male and female) in the RBCs.

**In the mosquito (sexual — gamogony + sporogony):**
7. A female *Anopheles* bites an infected person and takes up **gametocytes** (infective stage for the mosquito).
8. Gametes form and **fertilisation occurs in the mosquito's stomach (gut)** → zygote → motile **ookinete**.
9. Ookinete bores into the stomach wall → **oocyst** → divides into thousands of **sporozoites** (sporogony).
10. Sporozoites move to the mosquito's **salivary glands**, ready to be injected.

**Treatment and control:** **quinine** from the bark of ***Cinchona*** (the first anti-malarial); chloroquine; primaquine (kills hypnozoites); **artemisinin** from *Artemisia annua* (Tu Youyou, Nobel 2015) used in ACT. Vaccines: **RTS,S/AS01** (WHO-recommended 2021) and **R21/Matrix-M** (2023, made by Serum Institute of India). Control: insecticide spray, long-lasting insecticidal nets, removing stagnant water, larvivorous fish **Gambusia**. India's National Framework for Malaria Elimination aims at elimination by **2030**.

## 9. Life cycle of *Entamoeba histolytica*

- **Monogenetic** (only man); lives in the **large intestine (colon)**.
- **Trophozoite** (active, feeding form) — the large invasive form eats into the intestinal wall and **ingests RBCs**, causing ulcers; a small form lives harmlessly in the lumen.
- In the lumen trophozoites round up → **precyst → cyst**; the mature cyst has **four nuclei** (quadrinucleate) and is the **infective stage**.
- Cysts pass out with faeces; they survive outside and reach food and water — **houseflies act as mechanical carriers**.
- Swallowed cysts **excyst** in the small intestine; each gives eight small amoebulae → trophozoites → colon.
- Complications: **amoebic liver abscess**. Drug: metronidazole.
- Related: *Entamoeba gingivalis* in the mouth (no cyst; gum disease), *Entamoeba coli* in the colon (harmless; 8-nucleate cyst).

## 10. Life cycle of *Ascaris lumbricoides*

- **Monogenetic** — **no intermediate host**; lives in the **small intestine** of man.
- **Sexual dimorphism** — female longer (20–40 cm) with a straight tail; male smaller with a **curved tail** and penial spicules.
- A female lays about **2 lakh (200,000) eggs per day**; eggs pass out with faeces.
- In moist soil each egg develops a larva in about 2–3 weeks — the **embryonated egg is the infective stage**.
- Eggs reach man through contaminated **water, vegetables and fruits**.
- In the small intestine larvae hatch → bore into the intestinal wall → **hepatic portal vein → liver → heart → lungs** (moult; may cause pneumonia-like symptoms, Loeffler's syndrome) → climb up the **trachea** → **pharynx** → swallowed again → mature in the small intestine. This migration is called the **hepato-tracheal** (or lung) migration.
- Drugs: **albendazole**, mebendazole. **National Deworming Day — 10 February** (and 10 August).

## 11. Life cycle of *Wuchereria bancrofti*

- **Digenetic** — **man is the definitive host**; **female *Culex*** (*C. quinquefasciatus*) is the **intermediate host and vector**.
- Adult worms (thread-like, female larger) live coiled in the **lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes**, especially of the legs and groin.
- Females give birth to sheathed larvae, **microfilariae** (viviparous), which enter the blood. They show **nocturnal periodicity** — they appear in peripheral blood at **night (about 10 pm–2 am)**, matching the night-biting habit of *Culex*. So blood for diagnosis is taken at night (**night blood smear**).
- *Culex* sucks microfilariae → they develop in the mosquito's **thoracic muscles** to the infective third-stage larva in about 10–14 days → move to the proboscis → enter human skin when the mosquito bites.
- Blocked lymph vessels cause chronic inflammation, **lymphoedema**, **elephantiasis** of legs and **hydrocele** (scrotal swelling).
- Treatment and control: **mass drug administration (MDA)** with **DEC (diethylcarbamazine) + albendazole**, or the triple drug **IDA (ivermectin + DEC + albendazole)**; mosquito control. Bihar is one of the most filaria-endemic states and runs regular MDA rounds; India aims to eliminate lymphatic filariasis by 2027.

## 12. Life cycle of *Schistosoma* (blood fluke)

- Trematode (flatworm), but unusual: **sexes are separate**; the female lives in a groove (**gynaecophoric canal**) of the male.
- Adults live in **veins** — *S. haematobium* around the **urinary bladder** (blood in urine; linked to bladder cancer), *S. mansoni* and *S. japonicum* in intestinal (mesenteric) veins.
- **Eggs** with a spine pass out in **urine or faeces** → hatch in fresh water → ciliated **miracidium** → enters a **snail** (intermediate host — *Bulinus*, *Biomphalaria*, *Oncomelania*) → **sporocysts** multiply → fork-tailed **cercariae** leave the snail.
- **Cercariae penetrate human skin directly** when people bathe, wash or work in water → schistosomula → blood → liver → adult worms in veins. (No redia, no metacercaria in this genus.)
- Named **bilharziasis** after Theodor Bilharz (1851). Drug: **praziquantel**. Rare in India (one known focus in Ratnagiri, Maharashtra); common in Africa.

## 13. *Leishmania donovani* (kala-azar)

- **Digenetic** — **man** (and other mammals) and the **female sandfly** *Phlebotomus argentipes*.
- In man: non-flagellated **amastigote** form (**Leishman–Donovan or LD bodies**) inside **macrophages** of the **spleen, liver and bone marrow**.
- In sandfly: flagellated **promastigote** form in the gut; multiplies and moves to the proboscis.
- Disease: long irregular fever, huge spleen (splenomegaly), liver enlargement, anaemia, weight loss, **greyish-black skin** ("kala-azar" = black fever). After treatment some develop **PKDL** (post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis — skin patches that keep the parasite reservoir alive).
- Diagnosis: **rK39 rapid test** (antibody), splenic/bone-marrow aspirate for LD bodies.
- Treatment: **single-dose liposomal amphotericin B**; miltefosine. Control: indoor residual spraying (synthetic pyrethroids replaced DDT), plastering mud walls. Bihar's elimination story is in [Drugs, Cancer and Public Health](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/drugs-cancer-and-public-health).

## 15. Diagnostic tests

| Test | For | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Widal test** | **Typhoid** | Agglutination of *Salmonella* O and H antigens by patient's antibodies |
| **ELISA** | **HIV** screening, dengue, hepatitis | Antigen–antibody reaction detected by enzyme colour change |
| Western blot | HIV confirmation | Antibodies against separated viral proteins |
| **PCR / RT-PCR** | COVID-19, HIV viral load, TB, early detection of any pathogen | **Amplifies** very small amounts of pathogen DNA/RNA — detects infection before symptoms |
| Peripheral blood smear (thick and thin, Leishman/Giemsa stain) | **Malaria** (gold standard) | See parasites in RBCs |
| Rapid diagnostic test (RDT) | Malaria | Detects parasite antigens (HRP-2, pLDH) |
| **rK39** | **Kala-azar** | Rapid antibody strip test |
| Night blood smear | **Filariasis** | Nocturnal microfilariae |
| **Mantoux (tuberculin) test** | TB exposure | Delayed hypersensitivity skin reaction |
| Sputum smear (Ziehl–Neelsen, acid-fast) / **CBNAAT (GeneXpert)**, TrueNat | TB | Acid-fast bacilli / molecular test (rifampicin resistance) |
| **VDRL** | Syphilis | Flocculation test |
| Schick test | Diphtheria susceptibility | Skin test with toxin |
| NS1 antigen / IgM ELISA | Dengue | Viral antigen / antibody |
| Stool microscopy | Amoebiasis cysts, Ascaris eggs, hookworm eggs | Microscope |
| KOH mount | Fungal infections | Dissolves keratin to show hyphae |

## 16. Prevention and control (NCERT)

- **Personal hygiene** — clean body, safe drinking water, clean food.
- **Public hygiene** — proper disposal of waste and excreta, periodic cleaning and disinfection of water reservoirs, pools, cesspools and tanks, standard practices of hygiene in public catering.
- For air-borne diseases — avoid close contact with infected persons and their belongings.
- For vector-borne diseases — control or eliminate vectors and their breeding places: **avoid stagnation of water** around homes, regular cleaning of household **coolers**, use of **mosquito nets**, introduce larvivorous fish like ***Gambusia*** in ponds, spray insecticides in ditches, drainage areas and swamps, fit **doors and windows with wire mesh**.
- **Vaccines and antibiotics** have helped eradicate smallpox and control polio, diphtheria, pneumonia and tetanus. Recombinant DNA technology has made new vaccines (hepatitis B) possible.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Only female *Anopheles*** transmits malaria; male mosquitoes do not suck blood.
- **Aedes** = dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever — a **day biter** in clean water. **Culex** = filariasis and JE.
- **Kala-azar → sandfly**, not mosquito. **Sleeping sickness → tsetse**, not sandfly.
- **Housefly** is a **mechanical** vector; Anopheles is a **biological** vector.
- Man is the **intermediate** host of *Plasmodium*; the mosquito is the **definitive** host.
- Infective stage of *Plasmodium* for man = **sporozoite**; for mosquito = **gametocyte**.
- Fever in malaria is due to **haemozoin** released when RBCs burst.
- *Entamoeba* lives in the **large** intestine; *Ascaris* in the **small** intestine.
- *Ascaris* and *Entamoeba* need **no intermediate host**; *Wuchereria*, *Taenia*, *Schistosoma* and *Plasmodium* do.
- *Schistosoma* is a fluke with **separate sexes**; its cercaria **enters through skin**.
- Common cold does **not** affect the lungs; pneumonia **does** (alveoli).
- *Treponema* → syphilis; *Salmonella* → typhoid; *Streptococcus pneumoniae* → pneumonia.
- **Chickenpox (varicella-zoster) and smallpox are DNA viruses**; polio, measles, rabies, dengue, HIV are RNA viruses.
- **Bed bugs** are pests, **not proven vectors** of any human disease.
- Ringworm is a **fungal** disease, not a worm.

## Quick revision

1. Typhoid — *Salmonella typhi*; Widal test.
2. Pneumonia — *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*; alveoli fill with fluid.
3. Common cold — rhinoviruses; nose and air passages, not lungs.
4. Malaria — *Plasmodium*; vector female *Anopheles*.
5. *P. falciparum* — malignant tertian, cerebral malaria.
6. Malaria fever — haemozoin; parasite seen by Laveran (1880), mosquito link by Ross (1897).
7. Amoebiasis — *Entamoeba histolytica*; houseflies carry cysts; mucus and blood in stool.
8. Ascariasis — *Ascaris*; eggs in contaminated food and water; no intermediate host.
9. Filariasis — *Wuchereria bancrofti*; female *Culex*; nocturnal microfilariae.
10. Ringworm — *Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton*.
11. Kala-azar — *Leishmania donovani*; sandfly *Phlebotomus*.
12. Sleeping sickness — *Trypanosoma*; tsetse fly.
13. Dengue, chikungunya — *Aedes aegypti*.
14. JE — *Culex*; pigs are amplifying hosts.
15. Plague — *Yersinia pestis*; rat flea.
16. Schistosomiasis — blood fluke; snail; cercaria through skin.
17. Hookworm — larvae through skin of bare feet; anaemia.
18. Neurocysticercosis — *Taenia solium* larvae in brain.
19. EBV, VZV, HSV — DNA (herpes) viruses.
20. Rabies — RNA virus; hydrophobia.
21. India polio-free — 2014; smallpox eradicated worldwide — 1980.
22. ELISA — HIV; PCR — detects tiny amounts of pathogen nucleic acid.
23. rK39 — kala-azar; night smear — filaria; Mantoux — TB.
24. *Gambusia* — larvivorous fish for mosquito control.

## Practice with answers

1. Which bacterium causes typhoid? — ***Salmonella typhi***.
2. Which test confirms typhoid? — **Widal test**.
3. Which part of the respiratory system does the common cold not affect? — **The lungs**.
4. What is the infective stage of *Plasmodium* for man? — **Sporozoite**.
5. Where does fertilisation of *Plasmodium* gametes occur? — **Stomach (gut) of the female *Anopheles***.
6. Which toxin causes malarial fever? — **Haemozoin**.
7. Which form of *Plasmodium vivax* causes relapse? — **Hypnozoite** (dormant liver stage).
8. In which part of the gut does *Entamoeba histolytica* live? — **Large intestine (colon)**.
9. How many nuclei does the infective cyst of *E. histolytica* have? — **Four**.
10. Name the vector of filariasis. — **Female *Culex*** mosquito.
11. When are microfilariae found in peripheral blood? — **At night** (nocturnal periodicity).
12. Which organ does the *Ascaris* larva pass through before being swallowed again? — **Lungs** (then trachea).
13. Which vector spreads kala-azar? — **Sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*).
14. Which fly spreads African sleeping sickness? — **Tsetse fly** (*Glossina*).
15. Which larval stage of *Schistosoma* enters human skin? — **Cercaria**.
16. Name a fungal genus causing ringworm. — ***Trichophyton*** (or *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*).
17. Which mosquito spreads dengue, and when does it bite? — ***Aedes aegypti*, in the daytime**.
18. Which insect transmits plague? — **Rat flea** (*Xenopsylla cheopis*).
19. Name a DNA virus that causes a childhood rash. — **Varicella-zoster virus** (chickenpox).
20. Which fish is used to control mosquito larvae? — ***Gambusia***.
