This chapter covers the organs that make gametes (sperms and eggs), the ducts and glands that carry and nourish them, and the process of gametogenesis. NCERT class 12 "Human Reproduction" is the base; the B.Sc. layer is ovary ligaments, fallopian tube regions, epididymal secretions, spermatogonia types and meiotic arrest points. TRE asks it as one-liners: "Bartholin glands are located on", "the ovary is suspended by", "100 secondary oocytes give how many ova".

> **In simple words:** A man's testes make sperms all through adult life; a woman's ovaries hold egg cells that started forming before she was born. Sperms travel through a long tube system and are mixed with fluids from three glands to make semen. An egg is released every month into the fallopian tube, where a sperm may meet it. One "mother cell" gives **four** sperms but only **one** egg (the other three cells are tiny polar bodies). Hormones from the brain (GnRH, FSH, LH) switch the whole system on at puberty.

## 1. Basic terms first

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Gamete** | Sex cell — sperm (male) or ovum/egg (female); haploid (n = 23 in humans) |
| **Gonad** | Organ that makes gametes and sex hormones — testis or ovary (primary sex organ) |
| **Accessory sex organs** | Ducts and glands that help (vas deferens, prostate, uterus, oviduct …) |
| **Secondary sex characters** | Body features that appear at puberty (beard, voice change, breasts, hip widening) |
| **Gametogenesis** | Formation of gametes — spermatogenesis (male), oogenesis (female) |
| **Haploid (n) / diploid (2n)** | One set / two sets of chromosomes; human 2n = 46 |
| **Puberty** | Age when gonads become active and secondary sex characters appear |
| **Homologous organs** | Organs of male and female that come from the same embryonic rudiment |

Humans are **unisexual** (sexes separate), **viviparous** (give birth to young ones) and show **internal fertilisation**. Reproductive events in sequence: gametogenesis → insemination → fertilisation → cleavage and blastocyst formation → implantation → placenta formation → gestation → parturition (birth) → lactation.

## 2. The male reproductive system

The male system lies in the pelvis and has four parts: a pair of **testes**, **accessory ducts**, **accessory glands** and **external genitalia** (penis).

### 2.1 Testes and scrotum

- The testes lie outside the abdomen in a pouch called the **scrotum**. The scrotum keeps the testes about **2–2.5 °C lower** than body temperature, which is necessary for spermatogenesis.
- Failure of the testes to descend into the scrotum is **cryptorchidism**; such testes cannot make sperms (sterility), though they still make testosterone.
- Each adult testis is oval, about **4–5 cm long and 2–3 cm wide**, covered by a dense capsule (**tunica albuginea**).
- Each testis has about **250 compartments** called **testicular lobules**. Each lobule contains **1–3 highly coiled seminiferous tubules** — the actual site of sperm formation.
- A seminiferous tubule is lined by two types of cells: **male germ cells (spermatogonia)**, which divide to form sperms, and **Sertoli cells**, which nourish them.
- Outside the tubules, in the **interstitial spaces**, lie blood vessels, immune cells and the **interstitial cells or Leydig cells**, which secrete **androgens** (mainly testosterone).

| Cell | Location | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Spermatogonia** | Wall of seminiferous tubule (on basement membrane) | Divide to form sperms |
| **Sertoli cells** (nurse cells) | Inside the tubule, from basement membrane to lumen | Nourish developing sperms; form the **blood–testis barrier**; secrete **androgen-binding protein (ABP)** and **inhibin**; eat leftover cytoplasm (residual bodies); in the foetus secrete anti-Müllerian hormone |
| **Leydig cells** (interstitial cells) | Between the tubules | Secrete **testosterone** under **LH** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q41 asked: "Which of the following is not a correct match? — (A) Sertoli cells : Nutritive (B) Corona radiata : Sperm (C) Leydig cells : Testosterone." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** The corona radiata is a layer of follicle cells around the **egg**, not the sperm. Sertoli = nutritive and Leydig = testosterone are both correct.

### 2.2 Male accessory ducts

The sperm path from tubule to outside (learn it in order):

<div class="formula">

**Seminiferous tubule → rete testis → vasa efferentia → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra → urethral meatus**

</div>

| Duct | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Rete testis** | Network of channels in the testis that collects sperms from the tubules |
| **Vasa efferentia** | 10–20 fine ducts that carry sperms out of the testis into the epididymis |
| **Epididymis** | Long coiled tube (about 6 m if uncoiled) on the back of each testis; parts — head (caput), body (corpus), tail (cauda). Sperms **mature and gain motility** here and are **stored in the cauda** |
| **Vas deferens** | Muscular tube that goes up into the abdomen, loops over the urinary bladder, receives the duct of the seminal vesicle and opens into the urethra as the **ejaculatory duct**. Cut and tied in **vasectomy** |
| **Ejaculatory duct** | Short duct formed by union of vas deferens and seminal vesicle duct; passes through the prostate |
| **Urethra** | Common passage for urine and semen; runs through the penis |

**B.Sc. point — secretions of the epididymis:** the epididymal epithelium secretes **L-carnitine, glycerophosphocholine and neutral α-glucosidase**. These are used as "markers" of epididymal function in semen analysis. **Acid phosphatase, citric acid, zinc and PSA (prostate-specific antigen)** are markers of the **prostate**; **fructose** is the marker of the **seminal vesicles**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q95 asked: "Which enzyme is produced by epididymis? — (A) Acid phosphatase (B) Neutral α-glucosidase (C) L-carnitine." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (D) More than one of the above.** Neutral α-glucosidase and L-carnitine are both epididymal products (strictly, L-carnitine is a small molecule, not an enzyme). Acid phosphatase is a prostate marker.

### 2.3 Male accessory glands

| Gland | Number | Secretion | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seminal vesicles** | Paired | Alkaline, thick fluid rich in **fructose**, prostaglandins, clotting proteins; about **60–70% of semen volume** | Fructose = energy for sperms; prostaglandins aid movement in the female tract |
| **Prostate gland** | Single, around the urethra below the bladder | Thin, milky, slightly acidic fluid with **citric acid, acid phosphatase, zinc, calcium, PSA**, clotting/liquefying enzymes; about 20–30% of semen | **Activates and stimulates sperm motility**; PSA later liquefies the semen clot |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** | Paired, pea-sized, below the prostate | Clear mucus (pre-ejaculate) | **Lubricates** the penis/urethra and **neutralises acidic urine** traces in the urethra |

**Seminal plasma** = secretions of epididymis + vas deferens + seminal vesicles + prostate + bulbourethral glands. **Semen** = seminal plasma + sperms. One ejaculation releases about **2–5 mL** of semen with **200–300 million sperms** (NCERT); for normal fertility at least **60% of sperms should have normal shape** and at least **40% should show vigorous motility**. Semen pH is slightly alkaline (about 7.2–8.0), which helps sperms survive the acidic vagina.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q111 asked: "Prostate gland produces secretion for — (A) attracting sperms (B) stimulating sperm activity (C) inhibiting sperm activity." **Official answer: (B) stimulating sperm activity.**

> **Memory trick:** "**F**ructose from **V**esicles, **C**itrate from **P**rostate, **M**ucus from **C**owper's" — FV-CP-MC.

### 2.4 Penis

The **penis** is the male external genital organ made of special erectile tissue (two corpora cavernosa and one corpus spongiosum around the urethra) that fills with blood to cause erection, which helps insemination. Its enlarged end is the **glans penis**, covered by a loose fold of skin, the **foreskin (prepuce)**.

## 3. The female reproductive system

It has a pair of **ovaries**, a pair of **oviducts (fallopian tubes)**, the **uterus**, the **cervix**, the **vagina** and the **external genitalia**, plus the **mammary glands** as accessory organs.

### 3.1 Ovaries and their ligaments

- The ovaries are the **primary female sex organs**: they make the **ovum** and the hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone** (and inhibin, relaxin).
- Each ovary is about **2–4 cm long**, lies on each side of the lower abdomen, and is covered by a thin epithelium (called "germinal epithelium" — a misnomer, because it does not make germ cells).
- Inside, the **ovarian stroma** has an outer **cortex** (with follicles) and an inner **medulla** (blood vessels, nerves).

**Ligaments that hold the ovary (B.Sc. level, asked in TRE 1.0):**

| Ligament | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Mesovarium** | Fold of peritoneum (part of the broad ligament) that **suspends the ovary** from the back of the broad ligament and carries its vessels into the hilum |
| **Mesosalpinx** | Part of the broad ligament that encloses the **fallopian tube** ("salpinx" = tube) |
| **Mesometrium** | Largest part of the broad ligament, beside the **uterus** ("metra" = uterus) |
| **Broad ligament** | Whole double fold of peritoneum from the sides of the uterus to the pelvic wall; = mesovarium + mesosalpinx + mesometrium |
| **Ovarian ligament** (ligament of the ovary) | Fibrous cord joining the ovary to the side of the **uterus** |
| **Suspensory ligament of the ovary** (infundibulopelvic) | Joins the ovary to the **lateral pelvic wall**; carries the ovarian artery and vein |
| **Round ligament of the uterus** | From the uterus through the inguinal canal to the labia majora |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q97 asked: "The ovary is suspended from the body wall by — (A) mesovarium (B) mesosalpinx (C) mesometrium." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) mesovarium.**

> **Memory trick:** "meso-" = hanging fold. **Ovarium** → ovary, **salpinx** → tube, **metrium** → uterus.

### 3.2 Oviduct (fallopian tube)

Each oviduct is about **10–12 cm long** and runs from near the ovary to the uterus. From the ovary end inward:

| Region | Features |
|---|---|
| **Infundibulum** | Funnel-shaped end close to the ovary; edges have finger-like **fimbriae** that **collect the ovum** after ovulation |
| **Ampulla** | Wider middle part; **site of fertilisation** |
| **Isthmus** | Last, narrow part with a narrow lumen; joins the uterus |
| Intramural (interstitial) part | B.Sc. detail: the short part inside the uterine wall |

The tube wall has cilia and smooth muscle; ciliary beating and peristalsis move the egg and later the embryo toward the uterus.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q96 asked: "In which region of the fallopian tube fertilization takes place? — (A) Infundibulum (B) Ampulla (C) Isthmus." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) Ampulla.** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q107 asked the simpler version: "Fertilization of human occurs in — (A) uterus (B) vagina (C) fallopian tube." **Official answer: (C) fallopian tube.**

### 3.3 Uterus, cervix and vagina

- The **uterus (womb)** is single, shaped like an inverted pear, and held to the pelvic wall by ligaments.
- It opens into the vagina through a narrow **cervix**. The cavity of the cervix is the **cervical canal**; the **cervical canal + vagina = birth canal**.
- The uterine wall has three layers:

| Layer | Nature | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Perimetrium** | Outer thin membrane | Covering |
| **Myometrium** | Thick layer of **smooth muscle** | Strong contractions during **delivery** (parturition) |
| **Endometrium** | Inner glandular layer | Lines the cavity; **changes cyclically** in the menstrual cycle; site of implantation |

### 3.4 External genitalia and Bartholin's glands

The female external genitalia (together, the **vulva**) are: **mons pubis** (fatty cushion with hair), **labia majora** (outer fleshy folds), **labia minora** (inner folds), **hymen** (membrane partly covering the vaginal opening) and **clitoris** (small finger-like erectile structure above the urethral opening).

- The **hymen** may be torn by a sudden fall, jolt, insertion of a tampon, sports such as cycling or horse riding, or not at all after intercourse; it is **not** a reliable sign of virginity (NCERT says this clearly).
- **Bartholin's glands (greater vestibular glands)** are a pair of small glands lying on **either side of the vaginal opening** (in the vestibule, posterolateral). They secrete **mucus that lubricates** the vagina. They are **homologous to the Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands** of the male.
- **Skene's glands (lesser vestibular / paraurethral glands)** lie beside the urethral opening and are homologous to the **prostate**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q54 asked: "The Bartholin glands of the female reproductive system are located on the — (A) both sides of the uterus (B) outside of the cervix (C) either side of the vagina." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) either side of the vagina.**

### 3.5 Mammary glands

- Paired glands in the breasts, containing glandular tissue and fat.
- Each gland has **15–20 mammary lobes**, each with clusters of **alveoli** whose cells secrete milk.
- Milk path: **alveoli → mammary tubules → mammary duct → mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct → nipple.**
- Functional mammary glands are a character of **all female mammals**.

### 3.6 Male–female homologous organs

| Male | Female |
|---|---|
| Testis | Ovary |
| Scrotum | Labia majora |
| Penis | Clitoris |
| Cowper's (bulbourethral) glands | **Bartholin's glands** |
| Prostate | Skene's (paraurethral) glands |
| Gubernaculum | Ovarian ligament + round ligament of uterus |
| Wolffian (mesonephric) duct → vas deferens, epididymis | Wolffian duct degenerates (vestigial remnants) |
| Müllerian duct degenerates (by AMH from Sertoli cells) | Müllerian duct → oviduct, uterus, upper vagina |

## 4. Gametogenesis

### 4.1 Spermatogenesis

Spermatogenesis is the formation of sperms in the **seminiferous tubules**. It **starts at puberty** and continues throughout life. In humans one full cycle takes about **64–74 days** (figures differ by source).

Steps:

1. **Multiplication phase:** **spermatogonia** (2n = 46) on the tubule wall divide repeatedly by **mitosis**. B.Sc. detail: **type A spermatogonia** are stem cells that keep the supply going (type A dark = reserve, type A pale = renewing); **type B spermatogonia** are committed — they divide by **mitosis** and grow into **primary spermatocytes**.
2. **Growth phase:** primary spermatocytes (2n) grow and replicate their DNA.
3. **Maturation phase (meiosis):** each primary spermatocyte undergoes **meiosis I** → **two secondary spermatocytes** (n = 23, each chromosome still with two chromatids) → **meiosis II** → **four spermatids** (n = 23).
4. **Spermiogenesis:** spermatids change shape into **spermatozoa** (no division) — nucleus condenses, acrosome forms from the Golgi body, tail forms from the distal centriole, mitochondria collect in the middle piece, extra cytoplasm is shed.
5. **Spermiation:** release of sperms from the Sertoli cells into the lumen of the tubule.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q99 asked: "During spermatogenesis the type B spermatogonia divides by — (A) amitosis (B) mitosis (C) meiosis." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) mitosis.** Meiosis begins only in the primary spermatocyte.

### 4.2 Oogenesis

Oogenesis is the formation of the ovum. Its peculiarity is that it **starts before birth**, stops for years, and finishes only after fertilisation.

1. **In the foetus:** a couple of million **oogonia** (egg mother cells) form in each foetal ovary; no new oogonia are formed after birth.
2. Oogonia enter **meiosis I** and **stop (arrest) in prophase I** (diplotene stage, also called dictyotene). These cells are **primary oocytes**.
3. Each primary oocyte gets surrounded by a layer of **granulosa cells** → **primary follicle**. Many follicles degenerate from birth to puberty; at puberty only **60,000–80,000 primary follicles remain in each ovary** (NCERT).
4. **Secondary follicle:** more layers of granulosa cells and a new outer layer, the **theca**.
5. **Tertiary follicle:** has a fluid-filled cavity called the **antrum**; theca becomes **theca interna** and **theca externa**.
6. Inside the tertiary follicle, the primary oocyte grows and **completes meiosis I** — an **unequal** division giving a large **secondary oocyte** (n) and a tiny **first polar body**. The secondary oocyte keeps almost all the nutrient-rich cytoplasm.
7. The tertiary follicle becomes the mature **Graafian follicle**. The secondary oocyte forms a new membrane, the **zona pellucida**, around itself.
8. **Ovulation:** the Graafian follicle ruptures and releases the **secondary oocyte**, which is arrested in **metaphase II**.
9. **Meiosis II is completed only when a sperm enters** the secondary oocyte → large **ovum (ootid)** + small **second polar body**. The first polar body may also divide.

So **one primary oocyte → one ovum + two (or three) polar bodies**. Polar bodies degenerate; they exist to throw away extra chromosome sets while keeping the cytoplasm in one cell.

### 4.4 Spermatogenesis vs oogenesis

| Point | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Seminiferous tubules of testis | Ovary (cortex) |
| When it starts | Puberty | Foetal life |
| Continues | Throughout adult life | Ends at **menopause** (about 45–55 years) |
| Products of one mother cell | **4 functional sperms** | **1 ovum + 2–3 polar bodies** |
| Cytoplasm division | Equal | **Unequal** |
| Meiosis | Continuous, no arrest | Two arrests — prophase I (till puberty) and metaphase II (till sperm entry) |
| Growth phase | Short | Long; oocyte stores yolk and nutrients |
| Final step | Spermiogenesis (shape change) | No such step |
| Number formed | Hundreds of millions daily | About **one per month**; roughly 400–500 in a lifetime |

### 4.5 Worked counting problems (TRE loves these)

Rule: **primary spermatocyte → 4 sperms; secondary spermatocyte → 2 sperms; spermatid → 1 sperm. Primary oocyte → 1 ovum; secondary oocyte → 1 ovum.**

| Starting cells | Product |
|---|---|
| 100 primary spermatocytes | 400 sperms |
| 100 secondary spermatocytes | **200 sperms** |
| 100 spermatids | 100 sperms |
| 100 primary oocytes | 100 ova (+ 200–300 polar bodies) |
| 100 secondary oocytes | **100 ova** |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q52 asked: "In humans, the numbers of ova and sperms that would be produced from 100 secondary oocytes and 100 secondary spermatocytes during gametogenesis are — (A) 50 ova and 100 sperms (B) 100 ova and 100 sperms (C) 100 ova and 200 sperms." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) 100 ova and 200 sperms.**

**Worked problem 1:** How many meiotic divisions are needed to form 100 sperms? Each meiosis (one primary spermatocyte going through meiosis I and II) gives 4 sperms, so 100 ÷ 4 = **25 meiotic divisions**.

**Worked problem 2:** How many meiotic divisions are needed to form 100 zygotes? 100 ova need **100** meioses (one each); 100 sperms need **25**. Total = **125**.

**Worked problem 3:** How many chromosomes and chromatids are in each cell?

| Cell | Chromosomes | Chromatids per chromosome |
|---|---|---|
| Spermatogonium / oogonium (G1) | 46 | 1 |
| Primary spermatocyte / oocyte (after S phase) | 46 | 2 (92 chromatids total) |
| Secondary spermatocyte / oocyte, first polar body | 23 | 2 |
| Spermatid, sperm, ovum, second polar body | 23 | 1 |

**Worked problem 4:** A human sperm carries 22 + X or 22 + Y. An ovum always carries 22 + X. So **the father decides the sex of the child** — a fact asked often.

## 5. Structure of the sperm

A human sperm is a microscopic cell (about 50–60 µm long) with four parts:

| Part | Contents | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Head** | Elongated **haploid nucleus**, capped by the **acrosome** | Carries the father's DNA |
| **Acrosome** | Cap-like vesicle formed from the **Golgi body**; contains enzymes **hyaluronidase** and **acrosin** (sperm lysins) | Dissolves the corona radiata and zona pellucida so the sperm can **penetrate the egg** |
| **Neck** | Proximal and distal **centrioles** | Proximal centriole enters the egg and helps the first cleavage; distal gives the tail axoneme |
| **Middle piece** | Numerous **mitochondria** coiled in a spiral | Produce **ATP** (energy) for tail movement |
| **Tail** | Axoneme with 9 + 2 microtubules | Swimming (motility) |

The whole sperm is covered by a plasma membrane. In the female tract the sperm undergoes **capacitation** (removal of coating proteins and cholesterol; takes a few hours) — only capacitated sperms can fertilise.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q69 asked: "Absence of acrosome will affect which activity in a normal sperm? — (A) Maturation of sperm (B) Energy utilization for movement (C) Penetration into the ovum." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Penetration into the ovum.** Energy for movement comes from the mitochondria of the middle piece.

## 6. Structure of the ovum and Graafian follicle

- The human ovum is **alecithal/microlecithal** (very little yolk) — the embryo gets food from the mother through the placenta.
- Layers around the ovulated secondary oocyte, from inside out: **plasma membrane (vitelline membrane) → zona pellucida → corona radiata** (radially arranged follicle cells).
- A mature **Graafian follicle** (B.Sc. detail, outer to inner): theca externa → theca interna (makes androgens that granulosa converts to oestrogen) → membrana granulosa → antrum with liquor folliculi → **cumulus oophorus** (discus proligerus, a mound of cells holding the oocyte) → corona radiata → zona pellucida → oocyte.
- After ovulation the remains of the follicle become the **corpus luteum** (yellow body) that secretes **progesterone**; if no pregnancy, it degenerates into a white scar, the **corpus albicans**.

## 7. Hormonal control of reproduction

### 7.1 The hypothalamus–pituitary–gonad axis

<div class="formula">

**Hypothalamus → GnRH → Anterior pituitary → FSH + LH → Gonads → sex steroids (testosterone / oestrogen, progesterone) → negative feedback back to hypothalamus and pituitary**

</div>

- **GnRH** (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) — a peptide from the hypothalamus; a rise in GnRH at puberty starts the whole process.
- **FSH** (follicle-stimulating hormone) and **LH** (luteinising hormone) are the **gonadotropins** made by the **anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)**. In males LH was earlier called **ICSH** (interstitial cell-stimulating hormone).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q103 asked: "The gonadotropic hormone is secreted by — (A) posterior thyroid (B) adrenal cortex (C) anterior pituitary." **Official answer: (C) anterior pituitary.**

### 7.2 In males

| Hormone | Acts on | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **LH (ICSH)** | **Leydig cells** | Secretion of **testosterone** (androgens) |
| **Testosterone** | Seminiferous tubules, body | Needed for spermatogenesis; growth of accessory organs; **male secondary sex characters** (beard, deep voice, muscle, sex drive) |
| **FSH** | **Sertoli cells** | Secretion of ABP (keeps testosterone high inside the tubule), inhibin and factors that help **spermiogenesis** |
| **Inhibin** (from Sertoli cells) | Anterior pituitary | **Inhibits FSH** (negative feedback) |
| Testosterone (high level) | Hypothalamus, pituitary | Inhibits GnRH and LH (negative feedback) |

So spermatogenesis needs **both FSH and LH** (LH acting through testosterone).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q109 asked: "Spermatogenesis and sperm differentiation occur under the control of — (A) FSH (B) LH (C) parahormone." **Official answer: (D) More than one of the above** — FSH and LH both.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q94 asked: "Which of the following hormones from adenohypophysis controls the function of the seminiferous tubules? — (A) LH (B) FSH (C) Prolactin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) LH.** Most textbooks say **FSH** acts directly on the Sertoli cells inside the tubules, while LH acts on Leydig cells outside them. The key's logic is that LH-driven testosterone is essential for spermatogenesis inside the tubules. For the exam, remember the key; for teaching, remember that both are needed.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q104 asked: "Secondary sexual characters in male develop under the influence of — (A) oestrogen (B) testosterone (C) FSH." **Official answer: (B) testosterone.**

### 7.3 In females

| Hormone | Source | Main effect |
|---|---|---|
| **FSH** | Anterior pituitary | Growth of ovarian follicles; follicles secrete oestrogen |
| **LH** | Anterior pituitary | **LH surge → ovulation**; formation of corpus luteum |
| **Oestrogen** (oestradiol) | Growing follicles (granulosa cells) | Repair and growth of endometrium; female secondary sex characters (breast growth, wide pelvis, voice) |
| **Progesterone** | **Corpus luteum**, later placenta | Maintains endometrium for implantation and pregnancy ("pro-gestation") |
| **Inhibin** | Granulosa cells | Inhibits FSH |
| **Relaxin** | Corpus luteum, placenta (late pregnancy) | Softens the pubic symphysis and cervix for birth |

Oestrogen is made mainly by the **ovaries**; small amounts come from the adrenal cortex and, in pregnancy, large amounts from the placenta.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q100 asked: "Oestrogen is produced by — (A) pituitary (B) adrenals (C) ovaries." **Official answer: (C) ovaries.**

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q98 asked: "The pubertal changes in secondary sex characteristics result from increasing levels of which of the following hormones? — (A) Gonadal steroids (B) Growth hormone (C) Prolactin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) Gonadal steroids** (testosterone in boys, oestrogen in girls).

## 8. Puberty and reproductive phases

| Phase | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| Juvenile | Gonads inactive | Gonads inactive |
| Puberty | About 12–15 years; spermatogenesis starts | About 10–14 years; **menarche** (first menstruation) |
| Reproductive | Lifelong sperm production, slowly falling with age | Monthly cycles from menarche to menopause |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Corona radiata belongs to the egg**, not the sperm (TRE 1.0 Q41).
- The scrotum keeps testes **cooler**, not warmer — about 2–2.5 °C below body temperature.
- **Leydig cells make testosterone; Sertoli cells nourish** — do not swap them. Inhibin comes from **Sertoli** cells (male) and granulosa cells (female).
- **Type B spermatogonia divide by mitosis**; meiosis starts in the primary spermatocyte.
- **Spermiogenesis** (spermatid → sperm, shape change) is different from **spermiation** (release of sperm into lumen).
- What is ovulated in humans is the **secondary oocyte**, not a mature ovum. The ovum (ootid) forms only after sperm entry.
- **Fertilisation site = ampulla**; implantation site = uterus (endometrium). Ectopic pregnancy most commonly occurs in the fallopian tube.
- **Mesovarium** suspends the ovary; mesosalpinx goes with the tube; mesometrium goes with the uterus.
- **Bartholin's ↔ Cowper's**, **Skene's ↔ prostate** (homologues).
- Prostate secretion is **slightly acidic** and rich in citrate; seminal vesicle fluid is **alkaline** and rich in fructose. Fructose is the sperm's energy source.
- The acrosome comes from the **Golgi body**; the tail axoneme from the **distal centriole**; mitochondria give energy.
- The hymen is **not** a reliable sign of virginity.
- 100 **secondary** spermatocytes give **200** sperms (not 400).

## Quick revision

1. Human 2n = 46; gametes n = 23.
2. Scrotal temperature — 2–2.5 °C below body temperature; undescended testis = cryptorchidism.
3. About 250 testicular lobules; 1–3 seminiferous tubules each.
4. Sertoli cells — nourish, blood–testis barrier, ABP, inhibin.
5. Leydig cells — testosterone under LH (ICSH).
6. Sperm path — tubule → rete testis → vasa efferentia → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra.
7. Sperms mature in epididymis, stored in cauda epididymis.
8. Seminal vesicle — fructose; prostate — citrate, acid phosphatase, PSA; Cowper's — mucus.
9. 200–300 million sperms per ejaculate; 60% normal shape, 40% vigorous motility.
10. Oviduct — infundibulum (fimbriae), ampulla (fertilisation), isthmus.
11. Ovary suspended by mesovarium; ovarian ligament joins it to uterus.
12. Uterine layers — perimetrium, myometrium (contractions), endometrium (cyclic).
13. Bartholin's glands — either side of vaginal opening; homologous to Cowper's.
14. Mammary milk path — alveoli → tubules → duct → ampulla → lactiferous duct.
15. Oogenesis starts in the foetus; primary oocyte arrested in prophase I.
16. 60,000–80,000 primary follicles per ovary at puberty.
17. Antrum appears in the tertiary follicle; zona pellucida formed around the secondary oocyte.
18. Ovulated cell — secondary oocyte at metaphase II.
19. 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperms; 1 primary oocyte → 1 ovum.
20. Acrosome — hyaluronidase, acrosin — penetration of egg.
21. Middle piece — mitochondria — energy for swimming.
22. FSH → Sertoli; LH → Leydig; inhibin blocks FSH.
23. Gonadotropins come from the anterior pituitary.
24. Father determines the sex of the child (X or Y sperm).

## Practice with answers

1. Which cells of the testis secrete testosterone? — **Leydig (interstitial) cells** (stimulated by LH).
2. Which cells form the blood–testis barrier? — **Sertoli cells** (tight junctions between them).
3. Where do sperms gain motility? — **Epididymis**.
4. Which accessory gland supplies fructose? — **Seminal vesicles**.
5. Which gland's secretion is rich in citric acid and acid phosphatase? — **Prostate**.
6. What is the female homologue of Cowper's glands? — **Bartholin's glands**.
7. Which fold of peritoneum suspends the ovary? — **Mesovarium**.
8. Which part of the fallopian tube has fimbriae? — **Infundibulum**.
9. Where does fertilisation occur? — **Ampulla of the fallopian tube**.
10. Which layer of the uterus contracts during childbirth? — **Myometrium**.
11. How many sperms come from 50 primary spermatocytes? — **200** (4 each).
12. How many ova come from 50 secondary oocytes? — **50** (1 each).
13. How many meiotic divisions are needed for 400 sperms? — **100** (each gives 4).
14. At which stage is the human oocyte released at ovulation? — **Secondary oocyte in metaphase II**.
15. When is meiosis II of the oocyte completed? — **After sperm entry** (fertilisation).
16. Which organelle forms the acrosome? — **Golgi body**.
17. Which hormone inhibits FSH secretion in males? — **Inhibin** (from Sertoli cells).
18. Which hormone is also called ICSH? — **LH** (in males).
19. What structure forms from the ruptured Graafian follicle? — **Corpus luteum** (secretes progesterone).
20. What is cryptorchidism? — **Failure of testes to descend into the scrotum** (leads to sterility).
