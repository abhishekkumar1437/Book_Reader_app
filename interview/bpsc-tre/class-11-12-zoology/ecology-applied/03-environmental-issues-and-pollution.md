This chapter covers the old NCERT class 12 chapter "Environmental Issues" (now removed from the rationalised book but still asked) and B.Sc. environmental biology: pollutants, BOD, eutrophication, biomagnification, acid rain, ozone, global warming, deforestation and India's environmental laws. TRE 2.0 asked five questions straight from this chapter — deforestation causes, ozone depletion, acid-rain weathering, the father of Indian ecology and eutrophication — and TRE 1.0 asked about pollution effects, DDT and radiation.

> **In simple words:** Pollution is any unwanted change in air, water or soil that harms living things. Smoke and gases dirty the air; sewage and fertilisers dirty rivers and lakes; pesticides like DDT build up in food chains. Some gases thin the ozone layer that protects us from UV rays, and others trap heat and warm the planet. Cutting forests washes away soil. India has laws (Water Act, Air Act, Environment Protection Act) and people's movements (Chipko, Bishnoi) to protect nature.

## 1. Pollution — basics

- **Pollution**: an **undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological characteristics** of air, land, water or soil (NCERT). Agents that cause it are **pollutants**.
- **Primary pollutants** are emitted directly (SO₂, CO, NO, particulates). **Secondary pollutants** form in the air by reactions (ozone, PAN, sulphuric acid).
- **Biodegradable** pollutants (sewage, food waste) are broken down by microbes; **non-biodegradable** pollutants (DDT, mercury, plastics, radioactive waste) persist and may biomagnify.
- The **harmful effect of a pollutant depends on its concentration, the duration of exposure and the organism** exposed.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q68 asked: "On what does the harmful effect of pollution depend? — (A) the concentration of pollutants and the organism (B) the concentration, duration of exposure to pollutants and the organism (C) the concentration of pollutants and duration of exposure." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** NCERT: "harmful effects depend on the concentration of pollutants, duration of exposure and the organism."

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q118 asked: "Which of the following activities of modern technological man is considered to cause indirect or unintentional impact on the natural environment? — (A) modifications in land (B) construction and excavation (C) use of DDT." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) use of DDT.** Land modification and construction change the environment **directly and deliberately**; DDT was meant to kill mosquitoes and pests but **unintentionally** poisoned birds and fish through **biomagnification**.

## 2. Air pollution

| Pollutant | Main source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Particulate matter** (PM10, **PM2.5**) | Vehicles, thermal plants, crop-residue burning, construction dust | PM2.5 (≤ 2.5 µm) enters deep into lungs — breathing problems, irritation, inflammation, damage to lungs and **premature death** (CPCB considers PM2.5 most harmful) |
| **Carbon monoxide (CO)** | Incomplete combustion (vehicles) | Binds haemoglobin **about 200–250 times** more strongly than O₂ → carboxyhaemoglobin → suffocation |
| **Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)** | Burning coal, smelting | Respiratory disease, **acid rain** |
| **Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ)** | Vehicles, power plants | Acid rain, photochemical smog |
| **Hydrocarbons + NOₓ + sunlight** | Vehicle exhaust | **Photochemical smog** — tropospheric **ozone, PAN** (peroxyacetyl nitrate); eye irritation; first described in Los Angeles |
| **Lead** | Old leaded petrol, batteries, paints | Nervous damage, anaemia; leaded petrol phased out in India by 2000 |
| **Methyl isocyanate (MIC)** | Pesticide plant | **Bhopal gas tragedy, 2–3 December 1984** (Union Carbide) |
| **Fluorides** | Aluminium and brick industries | Damage to plants and cattle |

**Smog types:** **classical (London / sulphurous / reducing) smog** — smoke + fog + SO₂ in cool, humid weather (London 1952, about 4,000 deaths in days); **photochemical (Los Angeles / oxidising) smog** — warm, sunny, dry weather.

### Control of air pollution

| Device | Removes | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrostatic precipitator** | Particulate matter from thermal-plant exhaust | Removes **over 99%** of particulates; electrodes at several thousand volts give dust a charge; collecting plates are grounded |
| **Scrubber** | Gases like **SO₂** | Spray of water or lime |
| **Catalytic converter** | CO, unburnt hydrocarbons, NO | Platinum–palladium and **rhodium**; converts to CO₂, H₂O and N₂; vehicles must use **unleaded petrol** because lead **inactivates the catalyst** |
| **Cyclone separator, bag filters** | Coarse dust | — |

- **Delhi case (NCERT):** Supreme Court-directed switch of public transport to **CNG** by end of 2002; Bharat Stage norms. **Bharat Stage IV** across the country from **April 2017**; jumped straight to **Bharat Stage VI** from **1 April 2020**.
- **National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)**, launched **10 January 2019** — cut PM levels in non-attainment cities (target revised to 40% reduction by 2025–26 over 2017 levels).
- **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981**, amended in **1987** to include **noise** as an air pollutant.

### Noise pollution

- Noise = **undesired high level of sound**; measured in **decibels (dB)**. Sound of **150 dB or more** (jet take-off, rocket) can **rupture eardrums** and cause permanent hearing loss. Chronic exposure to even lower levels (about 80 dB and above in industry) damages hearing, causes sleeplessness, increased heart rate, altered breathing and stress.
- **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000** — ambient limits (day / night): **industrial 75/70 dB**, **commercial 65/55**, **residential 55/45**, **silence zone 50/40** (100 m around hospitals, schools, courts). Day = 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.
- Control: silencers, sound-absorbing materials, green belts, horn-free zones, restricting loudspeakers at night.

## 3. Water pollution

### Sources

- **Domestic sewage** — mostly biodegradable organic matter; also detergents (phosphates), pathogens.
- **Industrial effluents** — heavy metals (**mercury, cadmium, lead, arsenic**), acids, dyes, oils.
- **Agricultural run-off** — fertilisers (**N and P**), pesticides.
- **Thermal effluents** from power plants; **oil spills** at sea.

### BOD and COD

<div class="formula">

**BOD (biochemical oxygen demand)** = amount of O₂ (mg/L) consumed by **micro-organisms** to break down the organic matter in a litre of water, usually measured over **5 days at 20 °C (BOD₅)**.

**High BOD = high organic pollution.** Clean water: BOD below about **5 mg/L** (pristine streams ~1–2). Untreated domestic sewage: about **200–400 mg/L**.

**COD (chemical oxygen demand)** = O₂ needed to oxidise **all** organic matter chemically (with potassium dichromate). **COD ≥ BOD** always.

**DO (dissolved oxygen)**: about 8–9 mg/L at 20 °C in clean water; fish suffer below ~4–5 mg/L.

</div>

- When sewage enters a river, micro-organisms decomposing it use up the dissolved oxygen → **sharp fall in DO**, rise in BOD → **death of fish** and other aquatic animals (NCERT figure: DO sag downstream of a sewage outfall, with recovery further downstream).
- Sewage also carries pathogens of **dysentery, typhoid, jaundice, cholera**.
- **Sewage treatment**: primary (physical — screening, sedimentation), secondary (biological — **aeration tanks with flocs; reduces BOD**; activated sludge; anaerobic sludge digesters produce **biogas**), tertiary (chemical — removes N, P, disinfection). Full detail in the class 9–10 chapter [Ecology, Environment and Biodiversity](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/ecology-environment-and-biodiversity).

### Eutrophication

- **Eutrophication** = **natural ageing of a lake by nutrient enrichment** of its water (NCERT). Young lakes are cold, clear, nutrient-poor (**oligotrophic**); with time, nutrients (especially **nitrogen and phosphorus**) arrive with streams, plant and animal life increases, organic remains settle, the lake becomes shallower and warmer (**eutrophic**) — and finally a marsh and then land. Naturally it takes **thousands of years**.
- **Cultural or accelerated eutrophication**: human activities — **sewage, detergents (phosphates), fertiliser run-off** (nitrates and phosphates) — speed this up to **decades**.
- Sequence: excess N and P → **algal bloom** (often toxic cyanobacteria like *Microcystis*) → water becomes green and turbid → algae die → decomposers use up O₂ → **anoxia** → **fish die** and the lake "chokes".
- **Water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*)** — the world's most problematic aquatic weed, the "**terror of Bengal**"; grows abundantly in eutrophic water bodies.
- Phosphorus is usually the **limiting nutrient** in freshwater, nitrogen in the sea.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q136 asked: "Eutrophication in the lake is due to the presence of — (A) DO and bacteria (B) nitrogen and phosphorus (C) BOD and COD." **Official answer: (B) nitrogen and phosphorus.** DO is dissolved oxygen (it actually falls during eutrophication); BOD and COD are **measures** of pollution, not causes.

### Biomagnification and heavy metals

**Biomagnification** = increase in the concentration of a toxicant at successive trophic levels. It happens because the toxin **cannot be metabolised or excreted** (non-biodegradable, fat-soluble) and is passed on to the next level. (**Bioaccumulation** = build-up within one organism over its lifetime.)

### Integrated wastewater treatment — Arcata (NCERT case)

The town of **Arcata, California** treats sewage in two stages: conventional sedimentation, filtering and chlorination, then a series of **six connected marshes** where bacteria, algae, fungi and plants absorb and neutralise pollutants. A citizens' group, **FOWL (Friends of the Arcata Marsh)**, looks after it. **Ecological sanitation (Ecosan) toilets** — composting dry toilets used in Kerala and Sri Lanka — turn human excreta into manure without water.

## 4. Soil, solid-waste, thermal and radioactive pollution

### Soil and solid waste

- **Sources**: pesticides, fertilisers, industrial waste, plastics, mining, **municipal solid waste** (paper, food waste, plastics, glass, metals).
- **Sanitary landfills** replaced open dumps; still risk leachate seeping into groundwater.
- **E-waste** (computers, phones) — mostly shipped to developing countries; recycling recovers **copper, iron, silicon, nickel and gold**; e-Waste (Management) Rules 2016, revised 2022.
- **Plastics**: **Ahmed Khan** of Bengaluru developed **polyblend** — fine powder of recycled plastic mixed with **bitumen** for road-laying (NCERT). India banned identified **single-use plastic items from 1 July 2022**.
- **Agrochemicals**: excess fertiliser causes soil acidity and eutrophication; pesticides kill soil organisms. **Organic farming** (example of **Ramesh Chandra Dagar**, Sonipat, Haryana — integrated organic farming; Haryana Kisan Welfare Club — NCERT) recycles waste as nutrients.

### Thermal pollution

- Hot water discharged from **thermal and nuclear power plants** and industries into rivers.
- Warm water holds **less dissolved oxygen** and raises metabolic rate of animals → fish deaths, loss of sensitive species, algal blooms, disturbed breeding.
- Control: **cooling towers**, cooling ponds, spray ponds before discharge.

### Radioactive (nuclear) pollution

- Sources: nuclear power plants, nuclear accidents (**Chernobyl, 26 April 1986**, Ukraine; **Fukushima, 11 March 2011**, Japan), weapons tests, mining of uranium and thorium, medical isotopes.
- **Ionising radiation** (α, β, γ, X-rays) causes **mutations** at a very high rate.
- **At high doses, nuclear radiation is lethal; at lower doses it creates various disorders, the most frequent being cancer** (NCERT).
- **Acute effects** of very high exposure: **skin burns (radiation burns) and radiation sickness** (nausea, vomiting, hair loss, bone-marrow failure); **chronic effects** of lower long-term exposure: **cancers** (leukaemia, thyroid cancer), genetic mutations, cataract.
- Nuclear waste should be stored after **pre-treatment** in suitably **shielded containers buried in rocks, about 500 m deep** below the Earth's surface (NCERT).
- Units: **becquerel** (activity), **gray** (absorbed dose), **sievert** (biological effect), older units curie, rad, rem.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q120 asked: "Which of the following is the acute health effect due to exposure to very high levels of radiation? — (A) skin burns and radiation sickness (B) cancer (C) cardiovascular disease." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A).** "Acute" means sudden, short-term; cancer is a **delayed (chronic)** effect.

## 5. Acid rain and chemical weathering

- Normal rain is slightly acidic, **pH about 5.6**, because CO₂ dissolves to form carbonic acid. **Acid rain** has **pH below 5.6** (often 4–4.5).
- Cause: **SO₂** (from coal) → sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄); **NOₓ** (from vehicles, power plants) → nitric acid (HNO₃). Term "acid rain" coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1872)**, Manchester.
- Effects: acidification of lakes (fish die — Scandinavia, Canada), leaching of nutrients and release of toxic **aluminium** in soil, damage to forests (needles and leaves), corrosion of metals, and damage to buildings.
- **Taj Mahal** — "**marble cancer**" (stone leprosy): acid rain and SO₂ from the **Mathura refinery** corrode the marble (CaCO₃). The **Taj Trapezium Zone** (Supreme Court, 1996, M.C. Mehta case) restricts polluting industries.

### Chemical weathering enhanced by acid rain

Weathering is the breakdown of rocks in place. **Chemical weathering** changes the minerals themselves:

| Process | What happens | Acid rain role |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrolysis** | Reaction of minerals (especially **silicates like feldspar**) with water and **H⁺ ions** → clay minerals and dissolved ions | Acid rain supplies extra **H⁺**, so hydrolysis speeds up |
| **Carbonation / dissolution (solution)** | Carbonic and other acids dissolve **limestone and marble** (CaCO₃ → soluble calcium salts) | Acid rain dissolves marble statues and limestone |
| **Oxidation** | Iron minerals react with O₂ → rust (red/yellow colour) | Not mainly driven by acidity |
| **Hydration** | Minerals absorb water and swell | — |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q128 asked: "What type of chemical weathering is enhanced by acid rain? — (A) Hydrolysis (B) Dissolution (C) Oxidation." **Official answer: (A) Hydrolysis.** The logic: acid rain adds hydrogen ions, and **hydrolysis is the reaction of minerals with H⁺/water**, so acid rain directly speeds it up. Many geography books say acid rain also speeds **dissolution/carbonation** of limestone and marble, so a case can be made for "(D) More than one of the above"; but the official final key is **(A)** — mark (A) if the same question returns.

## 6. Ozone depletion

- **Ozone (O₃)** — "**good ozone**" in the **stratosphere** (ozone layer, roughly 15–35 km, densest ~20–25 km) **absorbs UV radiation** (UV-B and UV-C); "**bad ozone**" in the troposphere is a pollutant and greenhouse gas.
- Measured in **Dobson units (DU)**; normal total ozone about 300 DU; the "**ozone hole**" is a region where total ozone falls below **220 DU**.
- **Ozone hole over Antarctica** — first reported in **1985** (Farman, Gardiner and Shanklin, British Antarctic Survey). It forms in the southern **spring (September–October)** after polar stratospheric clouds form in the dark winter.

### How CFCs destroy ozone

**Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** — used as **refrigerants** (freon in fridges and ACs), **aerosol propellants**, foam-blowing agents and cleaning solvents — are very stable and rise to the stratosphere. There, **UV rays break them and release chlorine atoms**:

<div class="formula">

**CFCl₃ + UV → CFCl₂ + Cl**

**Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂**

**ClO + O → Cl + O₂**

Chlorine is regenerated — it acts as a **catalyst**; **one Cl atom can destroy tens of thousands (often quoted ~100,000) of O₃ molecules**.

</div>

- Other ozone-depleting substances (ODS): **halons** (bromine — even more destructive per atom), **carbon tetrachloride**, **methyl chloroform**, **methyl bromide**, nitrous oxide.
- Science: **Molina and Rowland (1974)** warned that CFCs destroy ozone — **Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1995** with **Paul Crutzen**.
- **Effects of UV-B**: DNA damage and **mutations**; **skin cancer**, ageing of skin; **snow blindness** (inflamed cornea); **cataract**; reduced immunity; reduced phytoplankton productivity and crop yields.

| Agreement | Year | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Vienna Convention** | **1985** | Framework for protecting the ozone layer |
| **Montreal Protocol** | Signed **16 September 1987**; in force **1 January 1989** | Phase out ODS (CFCs, halons, CCl₄); India joined in **1992**; developing countries phased out CFC production by **2010** |
| **London (1990), Copenhagen (1992)** amendments | — | Tightened schedules |
| **Kigali Amendment** | **2016** (Rwanda) | Phase **down HFCs** (ozone-safe but powerful greenhouse gases); India's freeze year 2028 |

- **World Ozone Day — 16 September** (date of signing the Montreal Protocol). The Montreal Protocol is often called the **most successful environmental treaty**; the ozone layer is projected to recover to 1980 levels around **2040** (2066 over Antarctica) — UNEP/WMO 2023 assessment.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q127 asked: "Ozone depletion is caused due to increase in the level of — (A) water vapour (B) oxygen (C) chlorofluorocarbon." **Official answer: (C) chlorofluorocarbon.**

## 7. Greenhouse effect and global warming

- **Greenhouse effect**: short-wave solar radiation passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth; the Earth emits **long-wave infrared** radiation, part of which is **absorbed and re-emitted by greenhouse gases**, warming the lower atmosphere. Naturally it keeps Earth's mean temperature about **+15 °C**; without it, the Earth would be about **−18 °C**.
- History: **Joseph Fourier** (1824) proposed the idea; **John Tyndall** (1859) measured IR absorption by CO₂ and water vapour; **Svante Arrhenius** (1896) calculated warming from CO₂.
- **Greenhouse gases**: **CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs (and HFCs), water vapour, tropospheric ozone**. Shares in the enhanced effect (NCERT figure): **CO₂ ~60%, CH₄ ~20%, CFCs ~14%, N₂O ~6%**.
- **Not GHGs**: N₂, O₂, argon (TRE 2.0 asked this — see the class 9–10 chapter).
- Sources of methane: paddy fields, cattle (enteric fermentation), wetlands, landfills; of N₂O: fertilisers.
- **Effects** of warming: melting glaciers and polar ice caps, **sea-level rise**, odd climate changes (more frequent El Niño), extreme weather, coral bleaching, shift of species ranges. Global mean temperature has risen about **1.1–1.2 °C** above pre-industrial (IPCC AR6, 2021–23); NCERT's older figure: **0.6 °C rise in the last century**.
- **Control**: cut fossil-fuel use, improve efficiency, **reforestation**, slow population growth, renewable energy, carbon capture.

| Agreement | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **UNFCCC** | 1992 (Rio Earth Summit) | Framework convention on climate |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | **1997**; in force 2005 | Binding cuts for developed countries |
| **Paris Agreement** | **2015** (COP21) | Keep warming **well below 2 °C**, aim **1.5 °C**; national pledges (NDCs) |
| India's net-zero target | Announced at **COP26 Glasgow (2021)** | Net zero by **2070** |

## 8. Deforestation

- **Deforestation** = conversion of forested areas to non-forested ones.
- NCERT: about **40% of tropical forests** have been lost; India's forest cover fell from about **30% at the start of the 20th century to 19.4% at its end**. The **National Forest Policy (1988)** recommends **33% forest cover for the plains and 67% for the hills**. Current India forest cover about **21.8%** (forest + tree cover about 25%, ISFR 2023).
- **Causes**: conversion to agriculture (to feed a growing population), timber, firewood, cattle ranching, mining, dams, roads, urbanisation; **slash-and-burn agriculture (jhum cultivation)** in north-east India — farmers cut and burn the forest, farm the ash-fertilised land, then move on; the shortened fallow cycle no longer lets the forest recover.
- **Consequences** (NCERT): **enhanced CO₂** in the atmosphere (trees store carbon); **loss of biodiversity** (habitat destruction); **soil erosion** (no roots to hold soil, no canopy to break rain); **desertification**; disturbed hydrological cycle; floods and droughts; landslides.
- **Reforestation** = restoring a forest that once existed; it may happen naturally or by planting trees.
- **Joint Forest Management (JFM)** — introduced in the **1980s** (national resolution **1990**) to involve local communities in protecting and managing forests in return for forest produce.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q123 asked: "Deforestation causes — (A) thermal pollution (B) soil erosion (C) noise pollution." **Official answer: (B) soil erosion.** Thermal pollution comes from hot effluents; noise from machines. (Deforestation can indirectly raise local temperature, but that is not "thermal pollution" in the technical sense.)

## Traps and exceptions

- **Harmful effect** depends on **concentration + duration + organism** — all three (TRE 1.0 key B).
- **DDT** = **unintentional/indirect** impact (TRE 1.0 key C).
- **High BOD = more polluted**; **COD ≥ BOD**; DO **falls** in polluted water.
- **Eutrophication** is caused by **N and P** (TRE 2.0 key B), not by BOD/COD or DO.
- Biomagnification needs **non-biodegradable, fat-soluble** substances; DDT rises from 0.003 ppb to 25 ppm.
- **Minamata — mercury; itai-itai — cadmium; blue baby — nitrate; black-foot — arsenic.**
- **Electrostatic precipitator** removes particulates (> 99%); **scrubber** removes SO₂; **catalytic converter** needs unleaded petrol.
- Noise was added to the **Air Act in 1987**, not by a separate act (rules came in 2000).
- **Normal rain pH 5.6**; acid rain is below 5.6. Acid rain enhances **hydrolysis** (TRE 2.0 key A).
- **Ozone hole** — Antarctica, **spring** (Sept–Oct), below **220 DU**; caused by **CFCs** (chlorine is a catalyst).
- **Montreal (1987) = ozone; Kyoto (1997)/Paris (2015) = climate; Kigali (2016) = HFCs.**
- **HFCs** do not harm ozone but are strong greenhouse gases.
- **CO₂ ~60%** of enhanced greenhouse effect; **argon, N₂, O₂ are not GHGs**.
- Deforestation → **soil erosion** (TRE 2.0 key B).
- **Chipko — 1973, Chamoli; Bishnoi/Amrita Devi — 1731, Khejarli; Appiko — 1983, Karnataka.**
- **Father of Indian ecology — Ramdeo Misra** (TRE 2.0 key A), not Salim Ali (birds) or M.S. Swaminathan.
- **Environment Protection Act 1986** came after **Bhopal 1984**; **Article 48A and 51A(g)** came with the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.
- **Acute** radiation effect = **burns and radiation sickness**; cancer is a **late** effect (TRE 1.0 key A).

## Quick revision

1. Harmful effect of pollutants — concentration, duration, organism.
2. DDT use — unintentional environmental impact; *Silent Spring* (1962).
3. PM2.5 — most harmful particulate; ESP removes > 99%.
4. Catalytic converter — Pt, Pd, Rh; unleaded petrol.
5. Noise limits: residential 55/45 dB, silence zone 50/40; 150 dB ruptures eardrums.
6. BOD measured over 5 days at 20 °C; high BOD = organic pollution.
7. Eutrophication — **N and P**; algal bloom; fish deaths.
8. Water hyacinth — terror of Bengal.
9. DDT: 0.003 ppb → 25 ppm in birds; thin eggshells.
10. Minamata (Hg), itai-itai (Cd).
11. Arsenic — Ganga plains of Bihar; fluoride — south Bihar.
12. Thermal pollution lowers dissolved O₂.
13. Nuclear waste — shielded containers ~500 m deep in rocks.
14. Acute radiation — skin burns, radiation sickness.
15. Acid rain — pH < 5.6; SO₂ and NOₓ; enhances **hydrolysis**; marble cancer of Taj.
16. Ozone layer — stratosphere; < 220 DU = hole; Antarctica, spring.
17. One Cl atom destroys ~100,000 O₃ molecules.
18. Montreal Protocol **1987**; Ozone Day **16 Sept**; Kigali **2016**.
19. GHG shares — CO₂ 60, CH₄ 20, CFC 14, N₂O 6.
20. Without greenhouse effect Earth ≈ −18 °C.
21. Forest Policy 1988 — 33% plains, 67% hills.
22. Deforestation → **soil erosion**, CO₂ rise, biodiversity loss.
23. Ramdeo Misra — father of Indian ecology (BHU).
24. Amrita Devi Bishnoi — 1731, Khejarli; Chipko — 1973, Chamoli.
25. Wildlife Act 1972; Water Act 1974; FCA 1980; Air Act 1981; EPA 1986; BDA 2002; NGT 2010.

## Practice with answers

1. A river has BOD 300 mg/L at a sewage outfall. Is it clean or polluted? — **Heavily polluted** (clean water is below about 5 mg/L).
2. Which is always higher, BOD or COD? — **COD** (it oxidises all organic matter chemically).
3. What causes cultural eutrophication? — **Nitrates and phosphates from sewage, detergents and fertilisers**.
4. DDT concentration in fish-eating birds in NCERT's example? — **25 ppm**.
5. Which disease is caused by mercury poisoning? — **Minamata disease**.
6. Device that removes SO₂ from exhaust? — **Scrubber**.
7. Why must vehicles with catalytic converters use unleaded petrol? — **Lead inactivates the catalyst**.
8. The pH of normal rainwater is about? — **5.6**.
9. Which weathering process does acid rain enhance (official TRE 2.0 key)? — **Hydrolysis**.
10. Which atom from CFCs destroys ozone? — **Chlorine** (released by UV; acts as a catalyst).
11. When is the Antarctic ozone hole largest? — **September–October (southern spring)**.
12. Which protocol phases down HFCs? — **Kigali Amendment (2016)**.
13. Which greenhouse gas contributes most to global warming? — **CO₂ (~60%)**.
14. What forest cover does the National Forest Policy 1988 recommend for hills? — **67%**.
15. Name the shifting cultivation practised in north-east India. — **Jhum cultivation** (slash and burn).
16. Who led the Bishnoi sacrifice at Khejarli in 1731? — **Amrita Devi Bishnoi**.
17. Which act was passed as an umbrella law after the Bhopal gas tragedy? — **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
18. Who is called the father of Indian ecology? — **Ramdeo Misra** (TRE 2.0 official answer).
19. Which constitutional article makes protecting the environment a fundamental duty? — **Article 51A(g)**.
20. Where is the headquarters of UNEP? — **Nairobi, Kenya**.
