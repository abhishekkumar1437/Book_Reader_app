Economic zoology (applied zoology) studies animals that are useful or harmful to humans. The base is NCERT class 12 "Strategies for Enhancement in Food Production" (animal husbandry) and the old class 9 "Improvement in Food Resources"; the rest is the standard B.Sc. applied-zoology syllabus. TRE 1.0 asked four questions here — false statements about honeybees, the largest honeybee, polyculture of fish and the order of ingredients in poultry layer feed.

> **In simple words:** People keep animals for food, money and materials. Bees give honey and wax; silkworms give silk; the lac insect gives lac (shellac); oysters give pearls; fish, chicken, cows and buffaloes give protein-rich food. Earthworms turn waste into manure, and some insects and fish help control pests. India has run big "revolutions" to raise output — Blue (fish), White (milk) and Silver (eggs). Bihar is strong in freshwater fish, makhana, tasar silk (Bhagalpur), dairy (Sudha) and honey.

## 1. Apiculture (bee-keeping)

**Apiculture** = rearing of honeybees for honey, wax and pollination. Bees are **social insects** (order **Hymenoptera**, family Apidae) living in colonies with division of labour.

### Species of honeybees

| Species | Common name | Features | Honey per colony per year (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Apis dorsata*** | **Rock bee / giant bee** | **Largest** honeybee; builds a **single huge comb in the open** (cliffs, tall trees, buildings); ferocious; **migratory**; cannot be domesticated in boxes | **Highest per colony** — about 35–40 kg (up to 50 kg) |
| ***Apis florea*** | **Little bee** | **Smallest**; single small comb on bushes; gentle; poor yield | ~0.5–1 kg |
| ***Apis cerana indica*** (*Apis indica*) | **Indian hive bee** | Medium size; multiple parallel combs in dark cavities; **domesticated** in Newton's hives; gentle | ~3–10 kg |
| ***Apis mellifera*** | **European / Italian bee** | Domesticated worldwide; prolific queen, less swarming; introduced into India (Punjab, 1960s) — now the main commercial bee | ~25–40 kg |
| *Tetragonula* (*Trigona*), *Melipona* | **Stingless (dammar) bee** | Very small; little honey of medicinal value; useful pollinators | Very little |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q116 asked: "Which of the following is the largest honeybee? — (A) *Apis florea* (B) *Apis indica* (C) *Apis dorsata*." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) *Apis dorsata*** (rock bee). *Apis florea* is the smallest.

### Castes of a colony

| Caste | Sex and origin | Number | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Queen** | **Fertile female**, diploid (2n = 32), from a **fertilised egg**; larva fed **royal jelly** throughout | **One per colony** | Lays eggs (up to ~1,500–2,000 a day); mates once in a **nuptial flight** with several drones and stores sperm; lives 3–5 years |
| **Workers** | **Sterile females**, diploid, from **fertilised eggs**; larvae fed royal jelly for only the first 2–3 days, then **bee bread** (pollen + honey) | Thousands (20,000–80,000) | All the work: nursing larvae, secreting **wax** (from abdominal wax glands) and **royal jelly** (from hypopharyngeal glands), building comb, guarding, foraging; have a **sting** (modified ovipositor); live ~6 weeks in summer |
| **Drones** | **Males**, **haploid (n = 16)**, from **unfertilised eggs** by **parthenogenesis** (arrhenotoky) | A few hundred | Only to mate with a queen; **no sting**; die after mating; driven out in lean seasons |

- **Sex determination**: **haplodiploidy** — fertilised egg (2n) → female; unfertilised egg (n) → male.
- **Communication**: **round dance** (food within ~50–100 m) and **waggle (tail-wagging) dance** (direction and distance of distant food relative to the sun) — **Karl von Frisch**, Nobel Prize **1973**.
- **Swarming** — the old queen leaves with part of the colony when a new queen is about to emerge.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q63 asked: "Which of the following statements is false? — (A) Every honeybee colony comprises a single queen. (B) The drone bees are female and do not have a sting. (C) Both workers and drones develop from unfertilized eggs." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (D) More than one of the above.** (A) is true. (B) is false — drones are **males** (it is true they have no sting). (C) is false — **workers come from fertilised eggs**; only drones come from unfertilised eggs. Two statements are false, so (D).

### Bee-keeping and products

- **Modern movable-frame hive**: **Langstroth hive** (L.L. Langstroth, 1851, USA — the "bee space" of about 6–9 mm); **Newton's hive** is the small Indian version for *A. cerana*. Parts: bottom board, brood chamber (with frames), **queen excluder**, super (honey chamber), inner cover, top cover. A **honey extractor** spins combs (centrifugal) so combs can be reused.
- **Honey**: mainly **fructose (levulose, ~38%)** and **glucose (dextrose, ~31%)**, water about **17–18%**, small amounts of sucrose, minerals, enzymes (invertase); made from flower **nectar** by evaporation and enzyme action. Used as food and in **Ayurvedic and Unani** medicines.
- **Beeswax**: secreted by **wax glands on the abdomen of workers**; used in cosmetics, polishes, candles, ointments.
- Others: **royal jelly**, **propolis** (bee glue from plant resins), **bee venom** (apitoxin, for arthritis treatments), **pollen**.
- **Pollination**: bees are the major pollinators of crops like **sunflower, Brassica (mustard), apple and pear**; keeping beehives in crop fields during flowering **increases yield** (NCERT).
- NCERT: bee-keeping needs knowledge of the nature and habits of bees, selection of a suitable location, catching and hiving of swarms, management of hives during different seasons, and handling and collection of honey and beeswax.
- **"Sweet Revolution"** — honey production; **National Beekeeping and Honey Mission (NBHM)**, 2020. India is among the top honey exporters. **Bihar** is reported among the leading honey-producing states (Muzaffarpur — litchi honey; check the latest rank).
- Pests/diseases: **wax moth** (*Galleria mellonella*), **mite *Varroa destructor***, *Tropilaelaps*, Thai sac brood virus, American and European foulbrood (bacterial), **Nosema** disease (microsporidian), bee-eater birds.

## 2. Sericulture (silk farming)

**Sericulture** = rearing of silkworms for **silk**. Silkworms are moth larvae (order **Lepidoptera**). Silk is secreted by a pair of **silk glands**, which are **modified salivary (labial) glands** of the caterpillar.

### Types of silk and silkworms

| Silk | Silkworm | Host (food) plant | Main area |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mulberry silk** (~70–75% of India's silk) | ***Bombyx mori*** (fully **domesticated**; family Bombycidae) | **Mulberry** (*Morus alba*, *M. indica*) | **Karnataka** (largest producer), Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, J&K |
| **Tasar (tussar) silk** — tropical | ***Antheraea mylitta*** (wild/semi-domesticated; Saturniidae) | **Arjun (*Terminalia arjuna*)**, **asan (*Terminalia tomentosa*)**, **sal (*Shorea robusta*)**, ber | **Jharkhand** (largest), Chhattisgarh, Odisha, **Bihar (Bhagalpur, Banka)** |
| **Oak tasar** — temperate | ***Antheraea proylei*** (also *A. pernyi*, *A. roylei*) | **Oak (*Quercus*)** | Manipur, Himalayan states |
| **Muga silk** — golden | ***Antheraea assamensis*** (*A. assama*) | **Som (*Persea bombycina*)** and **soalu (*Litsea polyantha*)** | **Only Assam** (and NE); GI tag; **golden-yellow** natural colour |
| **Eri (endi) silk** — "ahimsa silk" | ***Samia ricini*** (*Philosamia ricini*) | **Castor (*Ricinus communis*)**, kesseru | Assam, Meghalaya, NE; the moth emerges before the cocoon is used, so it is spun like cotton |

- **India is the world's second-largest silk producer** (after **China**) and the **only country producing all five commercial silks** (mulberry, tropical tasar, oak tasar, eri, muga). India is also the largest consumer of silk.
- **Central Silk Board** — statutory body under the Ministry of Textiles, set up **1948**, HQ **Bengaluru**. **Central Tasar Research and Training Institute** — **Ranchi**.
- **Bhagalpur** — "**Silk City**" of Bihar; famous for **tasar (Bhagalpuri) silk**; Bhagalpur silk has a **GI tag** (verify year).

- Silk thread from one cocoon: about **1,000–1,500 m** of continuous filament (mulberry silk).
- **Silk proteins**: **fibroin** (inner core, ~70–80%, insoluble) and **sericin** (outer gum, ~20–25%, removed by boiling — **degumming**).
- **Stifling** — killing the pupa inside the cocoon (hot water, steam, sun or hot air) before the moth emerges and breaks the thread; **reeling** — unwinding the filament from several cocoons onto a reel to form raw silk.
- **Voltinism**: **univoltine** (one generation a year — temperate races, better silk), **bivoltine** (two), **multivoltine** (many — tropical Indian races, poorer silk). Bivoltine hybrids are now promoted in India.

### Diseases of silkworm

| Disease | Cause | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Pebrine** | ***Nosema bombycis*** (microsporidian, a fungus-like protozoan) | Black pepper-like spots; transmitted through eggs; **Louis Pasteur** studied it (1865–70) and saved the French silk industry by egg-screening |
| **Flacherie** | Bacteria and viruses (e.g. *Streptococcus*, infectious flacherie virus) | Larvae become flaccid (soft), diarrhoea |
| **Grasserie** | **Nuclear polyhedrosis virus (BmNPV)** | Swollen, shiny larvae with milky fluid |
| **Muscardine** | **Fungus *Beauveria bassiana*** (white muscardine) | Larvae become hard and covered with white powder |
| **Uzi fly** | ***Exorista bombycis*** (tachinid fly) | Parasitoid; lays eggs on larvae |

## 3. Lac culture

- **Lac** is a resinous secretion of the **lac insect *Kerria lacca*** (old name *Laccifer lacca*; *Tachardia lacca*), order **Hemiptera**, family Kerriidae (Tachardiidae). India is the **largest producer of lac** in the world; **Jharkhand** produces the most (over half), followed by Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, West Bengal and Odisha.
- The resin is secreted by **dermal (cutaneous) glands**, mainly by the **females**, which become sessile and form encrustations on twigs. The tiny newly hatched larvae (**crawlers**) settle on tender twigs, insert their mouthparts to suck sap, and begin secreting lac.
- **Host plants**: **kusum (*Schleichera oleosa*)** — best quality lac; **palas (*Butea monosperma*)**; **ber (*Ziziphus mauritiana*)**; also *Acacia* (khair, babul), *Ficus*, *Flemingia* (bush host).
- **Strains and crops**:

| Strain | Crops (named after Hindi months of harvest) | Main host |
|---|---|---|
| **Rangeeni** | **Katki (Kartiki)** — harvested Oct–Nov; **Baisakhi** — harvested Apr–May | Palas, ber |
| **Kusumi** | **Aghani** — Jan–Feb; **Jethwi** — Jun–Jul | Kusum (and ber) |

- **Brood lac** — twigs carrying gravid females about to release crawlers, tied on fresh host trees (**inoculation**). Harvested twigs = **stick lac** → crushed and washed = **seed lac** → melted and filtered = **shellac** (flakes) or button lac.
- **Uses**: varnishes and polishes, **sealing wax**, **bangles** and toys, printing inks, electrical insulation, gramophone records (historical), coating of fruits, tablets and confectionery (food glaze), leather and hat-making; **lac dye** (laccaic acid, red) and **lac wax**.
- **Enemies**: predator moths ***Eublemma amabilis*** and *Pseudohypatopa pulverea* (*Holcocera*), parasitoid wasps, squirrels and birds.
- **Indian Institute of Natural Resins and Gums (IINRG)**, **Namkum, Ranchi** (founded **1925** as Indian Lac Research Institute), now ICAR's National Institute of Secondary Agriculture (renamed 2022 — verify).

## 4. Pearl culture

- A **pearl** is a hard, lustrous object produced inside the shell of certain **molluscs (bivalves)** — oysters and mussels — when an **irritant** (sand grain, parasite or implanted nucleus) enters between the **mantle** and the shell. The **mantle epithelium** forms a **pearl sac** around it and deposits concentric layers of **nacre (mother-of-pearl)**.
- **Composition**: **calcium carbonate (~82–86%, as aragonite)**, **conchiolin** (organic protein, ~10–14%) and water (~2–4%).
- **Marine pearl oysters**: ***Pinctada fucata*** (*P. vulgaris*, Indian pearl oyster), ***P. margaritifera*** (black-lip), ***P. maxima*** (gold/silver-lip, South Sea pearls).
- **Freshwater pearl mussels**: ***Lamellidens marginalis***, *L. corrianus*, *Parreysia corrugata* (India); *Hyriopsis cumingii* (China — world's largest freshwater pearl producer).
- **Culture technique**: a small **nucleus** (bead of shell) along with a piece of **mantle tissue (graft)** from a donor oyster is implanted surgically into the gonad (marine) or mantle (freshwater) of a host; the host is kept in cages in the sea or pond for 1–3 years; pearls are harvested.
- **Kokichi Mikimoto (Japan)** — "**father of cultured pearls**" — produced semi-spherical cultured pearls (1893) and later round pearls (patented 1916; technique of Mise and Nishikawa).
- **India**: natural pearl beds in the **Gulf of Mannar (Tuticorin, Tamil Nadu)** and **Gulf of Kachchh (Gujarat)**. **CMFRI** produced India's **first cultured pearl at Tuticorin in 1973**. **CIFA, Bhubaneswar** developed freshwater pearl culture (designer pearls). Bihar has trial freshwater pearl farming in ponds (check schemes).

## 5. Fisheries

**Fisheries** = catching, culturing, processing and selling fish and other aquatic animals (prawns, crabs, lobsters, edible oysters). Branches: **capture fisheries** (natural waters) and **culture fisheries (aquaculture)**; **inland** (freshwater, brackish) and **marine**. **Pisciculture** = fish culture.

### India's fish production

- India is the **second-largest fish-producing country**, with about **8% of global production**, and among the top aquaculture producers (Department of Fisheries; verify the latest rank). Fish production about **175–185 lakh tonnes** (2023–24), of which **inland fisheries contribute about 75%** (check latest).
- **Andhra Pradesh** is the top fish-producing state (largely aquaculture shrimp and carps).
- Schemes: **Blue Revolution** (see section 8), **Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY, 2020)**, Fisheries and Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund. **National Fish Farmers Day — 10 July** (marks the first successful induced breeding of carps by **K.H. Alikunhi and H.L. Chaudhury**, 10 July 1957).

### Composite fish culture (polyculture)

- **Composite fish culture** = culturing **five or six compatible species together** in one pond so that they use **all feeding zones** (surface, column, bottom) and different foods **without competing** — catla + silver carp (surface), rohu + grass carp (column/weeds), mrigal + common carp (bottom). Yields are much higher than monoculture.
- **Monoculture** = only one species; **polyculture** = more than one species at a time; **integrated fish farming** = fish with paddy, ducks, poultry, pigs, cattle or **makhana** (wastes of one become food or manure for the other).
- Problem in composite culture: lack of good-quality seed (fingerlings) of all species at the same time, because many carps breed only in monsoon → solved by **induced breeding**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q66 asked: "If more than a single species of fish is cultured at a time, then it is called — (A) monoculture (B) aquaculture (C) polyculture." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) polyculture.** Aquaculture is the general term for farming any aquatic organism.

### Induced breeding (hypophysation)

- Carps do not breed in still pond water. **Pituitary gland extract** (containing **gonadotropins — FSH and LH**) from mature fish is injected into brood fish to induce spawning (**hypophysation**).
- **Hiralal Chaudhuri** (with K.H. Alikunhi) achieved the first successful induced breeding of Indian major carps at **Cuttack (CIFRI substation), 10 July 1957** — called the **father of induced breeding** in India.
- Now synthetic hormones such as **Ovaprim** (GnRH analogue + domperidone) and **Ovatide** are used. Eggs hatch in **hatcheries** (Chinese circular hatchery, hapa).
- Stages: egg → **spawn** (hatchling, few days) → **fry** (~2–3 cm) → **fingerling** (~10–15 cm, stocked in grow-out ponds) → table fish.

### Air-breathing fishes (useful in Bihar's swampy waters)

| Fish | Accessory respiratory organ |
|---|---|
| *Clarias batrachus* (magur) | **Arborescent (dendritic) organ** in suprabranchial chamber |
| *Heteropneustes fossilis* (singhi) | **Air sacs** extending into the body muscles |
| *Anabas testudineus* (koi) | **Labyrinthine organ** — can survive out of water, "climbing perch" |
| *Channa* spp. (murrel) | **Suprabranchial chamber** |
| Lungfish (*Protopterus*) | True **lungs** (swim bladder modified) |

### Fish products and by-products

- **Fish liver oil** — cod and **shark** liver oil, rich in **vitamins A and D**; **fish body oil** (sardine) for soaps, paints.
- **Fish meal** (cattle and poultry feed), **fish manure (guano)**.
- **Isinglass** — gelatin from the **air bladder (swim bladder)**, used to **clarify wine and beer** and in glues.
- **Shagreen** — dried shark skin used as sandpaper and leather.
- **Larvivorous fish** for mosquito control — ***Gambusia affinis*** (mosquito fish), ***Poecilia reticulata*** (guppy), and native *Aplocheilus*.
- Preservation: chilling, freezing, salting, sun-drying, smoking, canning.

### Bihar — fish and makhana

- Bihar has abundant inland water: rivers (Ganga, Kosi, Gandak), **chaurs** (low-lying waterlogged land), **mauns** (oxbow lakes), ponds and reservoirs.
- Fish production about **9.6 lakh tonnes** in 2024–25 (growth ~9.9%); **Madhubani** is the leading fish-producing district; Bihar has moved close to **self-sufficiency in fish** (Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26).
- Popular fish: **rohu, catla, mrigal**, **magur, singhi, koi**, **boari**, tengra, **hilsa** (declined after the **Farakka barrage** blocked its migration), garai, pothia.
- **Makhana (fox nut, *Euryale ferox*)** — an aquatic plant of ponds and wetlands; Bihar produces the bulk of India's makhana (about three-fourths or more); **Mithila Makhana GI tag (2022)**; **National Makhana Board** launched at **Purnia (September 2025)**; ICAR research centre for makhana at **Darbhanga**.
- **Makhana-cum-fish integrated farming**: ponds of about 1–1.5 m depth grow makhana while **air-breathing fishes (magur, singhi, koi)** and carps are stocked; the fish feed on insects and organic matter, their wastes fertilise the crop, and the farmer earns from both. Developed and promoted by the ICAR (Research Complex for Eastern Region / makhana centre, Darbhanga) and Bihar's agriculture department.
- **Chaur development scheme** (Mukhyamantri Samekit Chaur Vikas Yojana) converts waterlogged chaurs into fish-and-makhana farms. **Bihar Animal Sciences University** (Patna, 2017) includes fisheries education.

See [Agriculture, Minerals and Natural Resources of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/agriculture-minerals-and-natural-resources-of-bihar) for the latest Bihar numbers.

## 6. Poultry

**Poultry** = rearing of domesticated fowl (chicken), ducks, turkeys, geese and quails for eggs and meat. **Broilers** are reared for **meat** (ready in about **6 weeks**, ~2 kg); **layers** for **eggs** (start laying at about **18–22 weeks**).

### Breeds of chicken

| Type | Breed | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Indian (desi)** | **Aseel** (Asil) | **Fighting** cock; hardy, good meat; Andhra Pradesh, also reared in Bihar–UP |
| | **Kadaknath** | **Black meat** (melanin), high protein, low fat; **Jhabua (MP)**; GI tag (2018) |
| | **Chittagong** (Malay) | Tall, meaty |
| | **Busra, Ghagus**, Brahma (Bramah) | Brahma — heavy meat breed from the Brahmaputra region |
| **Exotic layers** | **White Leghorn** | **Best egg layer** (Italy); light body; ~250–300 eggs/year |
| | Minorca | Egg type |
| **Exotic dual / meat** | **Rhode Island Red** (USA), **Plymouth Rock**, New Hampshire, Australorp | Dual purpose |
| | **Cornish** | Broiler (meat) |

### Feed, housing and diseases

- **Layer feed** (approximate): **yellow maize** is the main energy source (40–50%), with **broken/polished rice**, **barley/wheat**, **oil cakes** (decorticated groundnut cake, soybean meal) for protein, fish meal, mineral mixture (calcium from shell grit for egg shells) and vitamins.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q115 asked: "Arrange the following ingredients of poultry feed for layers in decreasing percentage: 1. Decorticated groundnut cake 2. Barley 3. Polished rice 4. Yellow maize 5. Corn gluten." Codes: (A) 4, 3, 2, 1, 5 (B) 4, 3, 1, 2, 5 (C) 3, 4, 1, 2, 5. **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) 4, 3, 2, 1, 5** — yellow maize > polished rice > barley > decorticated groundnut cake > corn gluten. This follows a standard layer-ration table in Indian animal-husbandry textbooks; exact percentages differ between formulations, so learn the key's order.

- Housing: **deep-litter system** (birds on a thick layer of paddy husk/sawdust on the floor) and **cage (battery) system** for layers; good ventilation, lighting (16 h light promotes laying), temperature control.
- **Diseases**:

| Disease | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Ranikhet disease** (Newcastle disease) | **Virus** (paramyxovirus) — most serious viral disease of Indian poultry |
| **Bird flu (avian influenza)** | **Virus** — H5N1 (also infects humans) |
| **Fowl pox** | Virus |
| **Marek's disease** | Herpes virus (tumours, paralysis) |
| Infectious bursal disease (Gumboro) | Virus |
| **Fowl cholera** | Bacterium *Pasteurella multocida* |
| Salmonellosis (pullorum, fowl typhoid) | *Salmonella* |
| **Coccidiosis** | **Protozoan *Eimeria*** — bloody droppings |
| **Aspergillosis** | **Fungus *Aspergillus*** (brooder pneumonia) |

- Institutes: **Central Avian Research Institute (CARI), Izatnagar (Bareilly, UP)**; ICAR–Directorate of Poultry Research, **Hyderabad**.
- India is the **world's second-largest egg producer** (after China) and among the top broiler producers (verify latest). **Andhra Pradesh** leads in eggs.

- **Buffalo milk** has more fat (~6–8%) than cow milk (~3.5–4.5%). **Casein** is the main milk protein; **lactose** the milk sugar. India's milk comes roughly half from buffaloes.
- **Breeding methods** (NCERT): **inbreeding** (closely related, same breed — increases homozygosity, exposes harmful recessives → **inbreeding depression**), **out-breeding**: **out-crossing** (same breed, no common ancestor for 4–6 generations), **cross-breeding** (different breeds — **Hisardale** sheep from **Bikaneri ewes × Marino rams**, Punjab), **interspecific hybridisation** (**mule** = female horse × male donkey). **Artificial insemination** (frozen semen) and **MOET (multiple ovulation embryo transfer)** — cow given FSH-like hormones to produce 6–8 eggs per cycle (super-ovulation); mated or artificially inseminated; embryos at **8–32 cell stage** recovered non-surgically and transferred to surrogate mothers; used in cattle, sheep, rabbits, buffaloes, mares (NCERT).
- **Diseases**: **foot-and-mouth disease** (virus), **rinderpest** (virus — India declared free **2006**, world eradicated **2011**), **anthrax** (*Bacillus anthracis*), **mastitis** (udder infection), **brucellosis**, **haemorrhagic septicaemia** (*Pasteurella multocida*), **lumpy skin disease** (capripox virus; big outbreak **2022**), black quarter (*Clostridium chauvoei*).
- **Milk processing**: **pasteurisation** — **LTLT** 63 °C for 30 min or **HTST** 72 °C for 15 s (Louis Pasteur's method); **UHT** 135–150 °C for 2–4 s (long shelf life); homogenisation (fat globules broken so cream does not separate).
- **Bihar**: **COMFED** (Bihar State Milk Co-operative Federation, est. **1983**) sells under the **Sudha** brand; Bihar Animal Sciences University (2017), Bihar Veterinary College (Patna, 1927).
- **National Milk Day — 26 November** (birthday of **Verghese Kurien**); **World Milk Day — 1 June**. **Rashtriya Gokul Mission** (2014) — conservation of indigenous breeds.

## 9. Vermiculture (vermicomposting)

- **Vermiculture** = rearing of earthworms; **vermicomposting** = using them to convert organic wastes (crop residue, cow dung, kitchen waste) into nutrient-rich manure (**vermicompost** = worm castings).
- **Charles Darwin** studied earthworms' role in soil formation — *The Formation of Vegetable Mould through the Action of Worms* (**1881**); Aristotle called earthworms the "**intestines of the earth**".
- **Species used** (surface-dwelling, **epigeic**, fast-breeding): ***Eisenia fetida*** (red wiggler — most widely used), ***Eudrilus eugeniae*** (African night crawler), ***Perionyx excavatus*** (Indian blue worm), *Lampito mauritii* (Indian). The common **Indian earthworm *Pheretima posthuma*** is a burrowing species, not preferred for composting.
- Vermicompost contains more available **N, P, K**, micronutrients, enzymes, growth hormones and beneficial microbes than ordinary compost; improves soil aeration and water-holding capacity. **Vermiwash** — liquid collected from vermicompost beds, used as foliar spray.
- Conditions: shade, moisture ~40–60%, temperature 20–30 °C, no salt or oily waste.

## 10. Pest management

- **Pest** = any organism that damages crops, stored grain, animals or human health.

| Pest | Crop / damage |
|---|---|
| **Desert locust** (*Schistocerca gregaria*) | Polyphagous swarms (big outbreak 2019–20 in Rajasthan, Gujarat) |
| **Rice stem borer** (*Scirpophaga incertulas*) | "Dead heart" and "white ear" in rice |
| **Gundhi bug** (*Leptocorisa acuta*) | Sucks milky rice grains |
| Brown plant hopper (*Nilaparvata lugens*) | "Hopper burn" in rice |
| **Pink bollworm** (*Pectinophora gossypiella*) and **American bollworm** (*Helicoverpa armigera*) | Cotton (Helicoverpa also on gram, tomato) |
| **Aphids** (mustard aphid *Lipaphis erysimi*) | Mustard — sap sucking |
| Termites (white ants) | Wood, crops |
| **Stored-grain pests**: rice weevil (*Sitophilus oryzae*), **khapra beetle** (*Trogoderma granarium*), red flour beetle (*Tribolium castaneum*), **pulse beetle** (*Callosobruchus chinensis*), lesser grain borer (*Rhyzopertha dominica*), Angoumois grain moth | Stored grain |
| Rats (*Rattus rattus*, *Bandicota bengalensis*) | Field and storage |

- **Integrated pest management (IPM)** — combines **cultural** (crop rotation, resistant varieties, timely sowing, trap crops), **mechanical** (hand-picking, light traps, pheromone traps), **biological** and **chemical** (need-based, minimum use) methods to keep pests below the **economic threshold level (ETL)**.
- **Biological control** (NCERT): **ladybird beetle** (eats **aphids**), **dragonflies** (eat **mosquitoes**), ***Bacillus thuringiensis*** (Bt spores sprayed on plants — kills caterpillars), ***Trichoderma*** (fungus against plant pathogens), **Baculoviruses** (genus ***Nucleopolyhedrovirus***) — species-specific, narrow-spectrum, no harm to non-target insects — ideal in IPM; **Trichogramma** (tiny wasp — egg parasitoid of borers and bollworms); larvivorous fish **Gambusia** against mosquito larvae; **cactus moth** against prickly pear in Australia.
- **Pheromone traps** (sex attractants) to monitor or confuse insects; **sterile insect technique** (release of radiation-sterilised males — screwworm eradication, Knipling); **botanical pesticides** — **neem (azadirachtin)**, pyrethrum, nicotine.
- Problems of chemical pesticides: **resistance**, killing of natural enemies, **pest resurgence**, residues and **biomagnification** (DDT — see chapter 03).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Largest honeybee — *Apis dorsata*** (TRE 1.0 key C); **smallest — *A. florea***; most domesticated in India — *A. cerana indica* and *A. mellifera*.
- **Drones are haploid males** from **unfertilised** eggs; **workers are sterile females** from **fertilised** eggs; the **queen** is a fertile female (royal jelly). TRE 1.0 Q63 key **D** (two false statements).
- **Beeswax** comes from **abdominal wax glands of workers**; **royal jelly** from their hypopharyngeal (head) glands.
- **Waggle dance** — **Karl von Frisch** (Nobel 1973).
- **Silk glands are modified salivary glands**; silk = **fibroin + sericin**.
- **Mulberry — *Bombyx mori***; **tasar — *Antheraea mylitta*** (arjun, asan, sal); **muga — *A. assamensis*** (som, soalu; only Assam); **eri — *Samia ricini*** (castor).
- **Pebrine** — *Nosema bombycis* (Pasteur); **grasserie** — virus; **muscardine** — fungus *Beauveria*; **flacherie** — bacteria/virus.
- **Lac** is secreted mainly by the **female** *Kerria lacca*; best host **kusum**; India largest producer, **Jharkhand** top state; IINRG at **Ranchi**.
- **Pearl** = nacre (CaCO₃ as aragonite + conchiolin) from the **mantle**; Mikimoto — cultured pearls.
- **Catla — surface; rohu — column; mrigal — bottom**; silver carp — surface; grass carp — weeds; common carp — bottom.
- **Polyculture** = more than one species together (TRE 1.0 key C); aquaculture is the general term.
- **Induced breeding** uses **pituitary extract (gonadotropins)** — not thyroxine; first success **10 July 1957** (National Fish Farmers Day).
- **Isinglass** comes from the **swim bladder**; fish liver oil gives **vitamins A and D**.
- **White Leghorn** — best egg layer; **Aseel** — fighting breed; **Kadaknath** — black meat.
- **Ranikhet** — virus; **coccidiosis** — protozoan *Eimeria*; **aspergillosis** — fungus.
- **Sahiwal** — best Indian milch cow; **Murrah** — best buffalo; **Holstein-Friesian** — highest yield; **Jersey** — high fat; **Bachaur** — Bihar draught breed.
- **White = milk (Kurien, Operation Flood 1970)**; **Blue = fish**; **Silver = eggs**; **Yellow = oilseeds**; **Golden = horticulture/honey**.
- Composting worms are **epigeic *Eisenia fetida*, *Eudrilus*, *Perionyx*** — not the burrowing *Pheretima*.
