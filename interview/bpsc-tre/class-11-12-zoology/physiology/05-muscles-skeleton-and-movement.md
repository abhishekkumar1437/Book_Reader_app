This chapter covers NCERT class 11 "Locomotion and Movement" and climbs to B.Sc. points: the triad and T-tubules, troponin subunits, the cross-bridge cycle, twitch–tetanus, energy systems and the Cori cycle. TRE 2.0 asked **six** muscle questions in a row (Q90–95) and TRE 1.0 asked about the sarcoplasmic reticulum (Q81) and the make-up of the actin filament (Q90).

> **In simple words:** A skeletal muscle is a bundle of long cells. Each cell is packed with **myofibrils**, which are chains of tiny repeating units called **sarcomeres**. Inside a sarcomere, thin **actin** threads and thick **myosin** threads overlap. When a nerve signal arrives, calcium is released, myosin heads grab actin and pull it inwards, so the sarcomere shortens — the threads **slide**, they do not shrink. ATP provides the energy, and bones and joints turn this pull into movement.

Basic version: [Human Physiology — Control, Movement and Reproduction (Class 9–10)](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/human-physiology-control-movement-and-reproduction).

## 1. Types of movement

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Amoeboid** | **Pseudopodia** formed by streaming of protoplasm; cytoskeletal **microfilaments** (actin) | *Amoeba*, **macrophages and leucocytes** in blood |
| **Ciliary** | Coordinated beating of **cilia** | Lining of **trachea** (removes dust), **fallopian tube** (moves ovum), *Paramecium* |
| **Flagellar** | Flagella | Sperm, *Euglena*, choanocytes of sponges |
| **Muscular** | Contraction of muscle fibres | Limbs, jaws, tongue; locomotion in most animals |

**Locomotion** = movement that changes the location of the whole body (walking, swimming, flying). All locomotion is movement, but not all movement is locomotion.

## 2. Types of muscle

Muscle is a specialised **mesodermal** tissue. ~40–50% of adult body weight. Properties: excitability, **contractility**, extensibility, elasticity.

| Feature | **Skeletal** (striated, voluntary) | **Visceral** (smooth, unstriated, involuntary) | **Cardiac** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | Attached to bones by **tendons** | Walls of hollow organs — gut, blood vessels, uterus, bladder | Heart wall only |
| Striations | **Present** | Absent | **Present** |
| Control | **Voluntary** | **Involuntary** | **Involuntary** |
| Cell shape | Long, **cylindrical, unbranched** | **Spindle-shaped** | **Branched** |
| Nuclei | **Many** (syncytium), peripheral | One, central | One (sometimes two), central |
| Special feature | Triads, T-tubules | Gap junctions; slow, sustained contraction | **Intercalated discs** (gap junctions) → whole heart contracts as one unit |
| Fatigue | Fatigues quickly | Hardly fatigues | **Never fatigues** (rich in mitochondria and myoglobin) |

## 3. Structure of a skeletal muscle

**Muscle** → wrapped by **epimysium**; made of bundles called **fascicles** (held by **fascia / perimysium**) → each fascicle has many **muscle fibres** (= muscle cells; wrapped by endomysium) → each fibre contains many **myofibrils** → each myofibril is made of **myofilaments** (actin and myosin) arranged in sarcomeres.

- **Sarcolemma** — plasma membrane of the muscle fibre.
- **Sarcoplasm** — its cytoplasm; has many nuclei, mitochondria, glycogen and **myoglobin** (red O₂-storing pigment).
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)** — modified **smooth endoplasmic reticulum**; the **store of Ca²⁺** ions.
- **T-tubules (transverse tubules)** — inward folds of the sarcolemma that carry the action potential deep into the fibre. In skeletal muscle one T-tubule + two terminal cisternae of SR = a **triad** (at the A–I junction in mammals). Cardiac muscle has **dyads**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q81 asked: "A modified form of smooth endoplasmic reticulum found in striated muscles is known as — (A) myofibril (B) sarcoplasmic reticulum (C) ergastoplasm." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) sarcoplasmic reticulum.** Ergastoplasm is an old name for **rough** ER (in gland cells).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q90 asked: "Which of the following forms a muscle? — (A) Myofibril (B) Neurolemma (C) Sarcolemma." **Official answer: (A) myofibril.** Myofibrils are the contractile threads that make up the bulk of a muscle fibre; the sarcolemma is only its membrane and the neurolemma covers a nerve fibre.

| Part | Meaning | Contains | During contraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sarcomere** | Portion of myofibril **between two successive Z lines** — **functional unit of contraction** | — | **Shortens** |
| **Z line** (Krause's membrane) | Elastic fibre in the centre of each I band | Anchors **actin** | Z lines come closer |
| **I band** (isotropic, light) | Light band | **Only actin** | **Shortens** |
| **A band** (anisotropic, dark) | Dark band | **Myosin** (+ overlapping actin) | **Length unchanged** |
| **H zone** (Hensen's) | Central part of the A band **not overlapped by thin filaments** | Only myosin | **Shortens / disappears** |
| **M line** | Thin fibrous membrane in the middle of the A band | Holds **myosin** filaments together | Stays central |

- Resting sarcomere length ~**2–2.5 µm**; thick filaments ~1.6 µm, thin ~1 µm long.
- In cross-section at the overlap region, each thick filament is surrounded by **6 thin filaments**; each thin filament by 3 thick ones.
- The alternate light and dark bands of all myofibrils line up → the **striated** appearance.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q91 asked: "The functional unit of the contractile system in a striated muscle is — (A) sarcomere (B) A-band (C) myofibril." **Official answer: (A) sarcomere.**

## 5. Contractile and regulatory proteins

### Thin (actin) filament

- Made of **two "F" (filamentous) actins helically wound** around each other.
- Each F-actin is a **polymer of monomeric "G" (globular) actins**.
- **Two filaments of tropomyosin** run close to the F-actins along their whole length (in the grooves).
- **Troponin**, a complex protein, sits at regular intervals on tropomyosin. At rest, a subunit of troponin **masks the active binding sites for myosin** on actin.
- Troponin has three subunits (B.Sc.): **TnC** (binds **Ca²⁺**), **TnI** (inhibits actin–myosin binding), **TnT** (binds tropomyosin).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q90 asked: "Each actin (thin) filament is made up of — (A) two F actins (B) two filaments of tropomyosin (C) troponin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (D) More than one of the above.** NCERT describes the thin filament as two F-actins + two tropomyosin filaments + troponin — all three are parts of it.

### Thick (myosin) filament

- A **polymerised protein**; many monomers called **meromyosins**.
- Each meromyosin has a **globular head with a short arm** = **heavy meromyosin (HMM)**, and a **tail** = **light meromyosin (LMM)**.
- The HMM component (head + short arm) projects outwards at regular distance and angle from the surface of the filament = the **cross arm**.
- The globular head is an **active ATPase enzyme** and has **binding sites for ATP and active sites for actin**.
- Giant protein **titin** (largest known protein) anchors myosin to the Z line and gives elasticity; **nebulin** sets actin length (B.Sc.).

| Protein | Filament | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Actin** | Thin | **Contractile** — binds myosin |
| **Myosin** | Thick | **Contractile** — ATPase head, pulls actin |
| **Tropomyosin** | Thin | **Regulatory** — covers myosin-binding sites |
| **Troponin** | Thin | **Regulatory** — binds Ca²⁺, moves tropomyosin |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q92 asked: "Contractile protein in the muscles is/are — (A) actin and myosin (B) troponin (C) tropomyosin." **Official answer: (A) actin and myosin.** Troponin and tropomyosin are **regulatory** proteins, not contractile — that is why BPSC did not choose "More than one".

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q93 asked: "Myofibrils of muscle consist of — (A) actin and myosin (B) actin and troponin (C) actin and tropomyosin." **Official answer: (A) actin and myosin.** The two filament proteins that make up the bands.

## 6. Mechanism of muscle contraction — sliding filament theory

Proposed by **A. F. Huxley & R. Niedergerke** and **H. E. Huxley & Jean Hanson (1954)**. Thin filaments **slide over** thick filaments; neither filament changes its length.

1. A signal from the CNS travels down a **motor neuron**. A motor neuron with the muscle fibres it supplies = a **motor unit**.
2. At the **neuromuscular junction (motor end plate)**, the neuron releases **acetylcholine (ACh)**.
3. ACh produces an **action potential** in the sarcolemma, which spreads through the fibre and down the **T-tubules**.
4. This causes release of **Ca²⁺ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum** into the sarcoplasm.
5. Ca²⁺ binds **troponin (TnC)** on actin → tropomyosin shifts → **active sites on actin are unmasked**.
6. The **myosin head**, already energised by hydrolysing ATP (holding ADP + Pi), binds actin → **cross-bridge** forms.
7. **Power stroke**: the head tilts and releases ADP + Pi, pulling the actin filament **towards the centre of the A band (M line)**. The Z lines attached to actin are pulled inwards → **sarcomere shortens**.
8. A **new ATP** binds the myosin head → cross-bridge **breaks**.
9. ATP is hydrolysed → head returns to the "cocked" high-energy position → binds a new site further along. The cycle repeats while Ca²⁺ is present.
10. **Relaxation**: nerve stimulation stops → Ca²⁺ is pumped back into the SR (**Ca²⁺-ATPase / SERCA**, uses ATP) → troponin–tropomyosin mask actin again → Z lines return to their original position.

<div class="formula">

**During contraction:** I band **shortens** · H zone **shortens or disappears** · A band **unchanged** · sarcomere **shortens** · Z lines move closer.

**ATP is needed for:** (1) myosin head energising, (2) **breaking** the cross-bridge (detachment), (3) pumping Ca²⁺ back into the SR (relaxation).

</div>

- **Rigor mortis** — stiffness after death: no ATP → myosin cannot detach from actin.
- Muscle fibres obey the **all-or-none law**; a whole muscle grades force by recruiting more motor units.
- **Twitch** — single brief contraction to one stimulus (latent period → contraction → relaxation, ~0.1 s in frog gastrocnemius). **Summation** — stimuli in quick succession add up. **Tetanus** — smooth, sustained maximal contraction from rapid repeated stimuli. **Muscle tone (tonus)** — slight continuous contraction at rest.

### Neuromuscular junction — drugs and toxins (B.Sc.)

| Agent | Action at the neuromuscular junction | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Curare** (arrow poison, *Strychnos/Chondrodendron* plants) | **Blocks ACh receptors** | Flaccid paralysis (used as muscle relaxant in surgery) |
| **Botulinum toxin** (*Clostridium botulinum*) | **Blocks ACh release** | Flaccid paralysis (botulism); "Botox" in cosmetics |
| **Tetanus toxin** (*Clostridium tetani*) | Blocks inhibitory transmitters (glycine/GABA) in spinal cord | Spastic paralysis, **lockjaw** |
| **Organophosphates, nerve gases** (sarin) | **Inhibit acetylcholinesterase** → ACh accumulates | Continuous stimulation, spasms |
| Neostigmine | Reversible cholinesterase inhibitor | Treatment of **myasthenia gravis** |

### Types of contraction

- **Isotonic** — muscle shortens and moves a load; tension stays about the same (lifting a book).
- **Isometric** — tension rises but length does not change (pushing a wall, holding a heavy bag still).
- **Length–tension relation**: force is maximum at the resting sarcomere length (~2.0–2.2 µm), where overlap of actin and myosin is best; too stretched or too compressed → weaker.

## 7. Energy for contraction

| Source | How | Lasts |
|---|---|---|
| **Stored ATP** | Direct | ~2–3 seconds |
| **Creatine phosphate (phosphocreatine)** | **Creatine phosphate + ADP → creatine + ATP** (enzyme **creatine kinase**); fastest way to regenerate ATP | ~8–10 seconds (sprint start) |
| **Anaerobic glycolysis** | Glucose/glycogen → pyruvate → **lactic acid**; 2 ATP per glucose | 1–2 minutes |
| **Aerobic respiration** | Glucose and fatty acids fully oxidised in mitochondria | Long periods (marathon) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q95 asked: "Which of the following yields ATP required for muscle contraction? — (A) Myosin (B) Myoglobin (C) Creatine phosphate." **Official answer: (C) creatine phosphate.** Myosin **hydrolyses** (uses) ATP; myoglobin stores O₂.

- **Muscle fatigue** — repeated activation of muscles can lead to accumulation of **lactic acid** due to anaerobic breakdown of glycogen, causing fatigue (NCERT). Modern view: fatigue is also due to Pi accumulation, H⁺ and ion changes — but answer **lactic acid** in exams.
- **Oxygen debt** (excess post-exercise O₂ consumption) — extra O₂ needed after exercise to oxidise lactic acid, restore creatine phosphate and ATP, refill myoglobin.
- **Cori cycle** (Carl and Gerty Cori; Nobel 1947): lactic acid from muscle → blood → **liver** → converted back to glucose (**gluconeogenesis**) → blood → muscle.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q94 asked: "Substances that accumulate in a fatigued muscle are — (A) pyruvic acid (B) lactic acid (C) carbon dioxide." **Official answer: (B) lactic acid.**

### Red and white muscle fibres

| | **Red fibres** (slow-twitch, type I) | **White fibres** (fast-twitch, type II) |
|---|---|---|
| Myoglobin | **High** — red colour | Low — pale |
| Mitochondria | **Many** | Few |
| SR | Less | **Plenty** (fast Ca²⁺ release) |
| Respiration | **Aerobic** | Mainly **anaerobic** |
| Fatigue | Resistant | Fatigue fast |
| Examples | Back (postural) muscles; flight muscles of long-flying birds | Eye muscles; breast muscles of domestic fowl |

## 8. The skeletal system

Skeleton = **bones** (hard; matrix of calcium salts — mainly hydroxyapatite) + **cartilage** (slightly pliable; matrix of chondroitin salts). Adult human: **206 bones**; newborn ~270–300 (fuse later).

### Axial skeleton — 80 bones

| Region | Count | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Skull** | **22** | **Cranial 8** (protect brain — frontal 1, parietal 2, temporal 2, occipital 1, sphenoid 1, ethmoid 1) + **facial 14** |
| **Hyoid** | 1 | U-shaped, at the base of the buccal cavity; touches no other bone |
| **Ear ossicles** | 6 | Malleus, incus, stapes in each middle ear |
| **Vertebral column** | **26** | **Cervical 7**, **thoracic 12**, **lumbar 5**, **sacral 1** (5 fused), **coccygeal 1** (4 fused) |
| **Sternum** | 1 | Flat bone on the ventral midline of the thorax |
| **Ribs** | **24** (12 pairs) | **True ribs 1–7** (attach directly to sternum via hyaline cartilage); **false (vertebrochondral) ribs 8–10** (join the 7th rib's cartilage); **floating ribs 11–12** (no ventral attachment) |

- Skull + hyoid + ear ossicles = **29** (NCERT). The human skull is **dicondylic** (two occipital condyles articulate with the atlas).
- **Atlas** (1st cervical) — nodding ("yes"); **axis** (2nd) — rotation ("no", pivot joint).
- Almost all mammals including the **giraffe and whale** have **7 cervical vertebrae** (exceptions: sloths, manatee).
- Thoracic vertebrae + ribs + sternum = **rib cage**.

### Appendicular skeleton — 126 bones

| Part | Bones | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **Fore limb** (each) | **Humerus**, **radius and ulna**, **carpals 8**, **metacarpals 5**, **phalanges 14** | 30 × 2 = 60 |
| **Hind limb** (each) | **Femur** (longest bone), **tibia and fibula**, **patella** (knee cap, sesamoid), **tarsals 7**, **metatarsals 5**, **phalanges 14** | 30 × 2 = 60 |
| **Pectoral girdle** (each side) | **Clavicle** (collar bone) + **scapula** (shoulder blade; has the **glenoid cavity** and acromion) | 2 × 2 = 4 |
| **Pelvic girdle** | Two **coxal bones**, each formed by fusion of **ilium, ischium and pubis**; meet at the **acetabulum** (socket for femur); join ventrally at the **pubic symphysis** | 2 |

- **Longest and strongest bone — femur**; **smallest — stapes** (middle ear).
- Total: 80 + 126 = **206**.

### Bone and cartilage (B.Sc.)

- **Compact bone** has **Haversian systems (osteons)** — central Haversian canal with blood vessels, concentric lamellae, **osteocytes** in lacunae linked by canaliculi; Volkmann's canals connect them. **Spongy bone** has trabeculae and red marrow.
- **Osteoblasts** build bone; **osteoclasts** (multinucleate, macrophage lineage) dissolve bone; **osteocytes** maintain it.
- **Cartilage types**: **hyaline** (ends of long bones, nose, trachea, ribs' costal cartilage; most common), **elastic** (pinna, epiglottis), **fibrocartilage** (intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis — strongest).
- Bones store calcium and phosphate; red marrow makes blood cells.

### Bone formation (ossification) — B.Sc.

- **Intramembranous ossification** — bone forms directly in connective tissue membranes: **flat bones of the skull**, clavicle (partly). These are **membrane (dermal) bones**.
- **Endochondral ossification** — a hyaline cartilage model is replaced by bone: **long bones** (femur, humerus), vertebrae. These are **cartilage (replacing) bones**.
- Growth in length happens at the **epiphyseal (growth) plate** of cartilage; it closes after puberty — this is why excess GH before closure causes gigantism and after closure causes acromegaly.
- Bone remodelling needs **vitamin D, calcium, PTH, calcitonin, oestrogen/testosterone and GH**.

**Worked count — bones of one fore limb:** humerus 1 + radius 1 + ulna 1 + carpals 8 + metacarpals 5 + phalanges 14 (thumb 2, other fingers 3 each = 2 + 12) = **30**. Hind limb: femur 1 + tibia 1 + fibula 1 + patella 1 + tarsals 7 + metatarsals 5 + phalanges 14 = **30**. Four limbs = 120; add 4 pectoral + 2 pelvic girdle bones = **126 appendicular**.

## 9. Joints

Joints are points of contact between bones, or between bones and cartilage. Classified by structure:

| Type | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous joints** | **No movement** — dense fibrous tissue | **Sutures** between the flat skull bones |
| **Cartilaginous joints** | **Limited movement** — joined by cartilage | Between **adjacent vertebrae** (intervertebral discs), **pubic symphysis** |
| **Synovial joints** | **Considerable movement** — fluid-filled **synovial cavity** between articulating surfaces, with **synovial fluid** and articular cartilage | See below |

| Synovial joint | Example |
|---|---|
| **Ball and socket** | Between **humerus and pectoral girdle** (shoulder); femur and acetabulum (hip) |
| **Hinge** | **Knee**, elbow, interphalangeal joints |
| **Pivot** | Between **atlas and axis** |
| **Gliding** | Between the **carpals** (and tarsals) |
| **Saddle** | Between the **carpal and metacarpal of the thumb** |
| Condyloid (ellipsoid) | Wrist (radio-carpal), metacarpo-phalangeal |

- **Ligaments** join bone to bone (elastic, yellow fibres); **tendons** join muscle to bone (inelastic, white collagen fibres).
- Lever types in the body: head nodding (1st class), standing on toes (2nd class), biceps flexing forearm (3rd class — most common).

### Muscle facts often asked

| Fact | Answer |
|---|---|
| Largest muscle of the body | **Gluteus maximus** (buttock) |
| Smallest skeletal muscle | **Stapedius** (middle ear, attached to stapes) |
| Longest muscle | **Sartorius** (thigh) |
| Strongest muscle (by force for its size) | **Masseter** (jaw) |
| Main muscle of breathing | **Diaphragm** |
| Muscle that flexes the forearm / extends it | **Biceps / triceps** (antagonistic pair) |
| Number of skeletal muscles | ~**600–650** |

Muscles work in **antagonistic pairs**: a **flexor** bends a joint and an **extensor** straightens it; an **abductor** moves a limb away from the midline and an **adductor** brings it back.

## 10. Disorders of muscular and skeletal system

| Disorder | Cause / feature |
|---|---|
| **Myasthenia gravis** | **Autoimmune** disorder affecting the **neuromuscular junction** (antibodies against ACh receptors) → fatigue, weakening and **paralysis** of skeletal muscle |
| **Muscular dystrophy** | **Progressive degeneration** of skeletal muscle, mostly **genetic** (Duchenne — X-linked, missing protein **dystrophin**) |
| **Tetany** | Rapid spasms (wild contractions) in muscle due to **low Ca²⁺** in body fluid (often hypoparathyroidism) |
| **Arthritis** | **Inflammation of joints**; osteoarthritis (wear), **rheumatoid** (autoimmune) |
| **Osteoporosis** | **Age-related**; decreased bone mass, increased fractures; **decreased oestrogen** (post-menopause) is a common cause |
| **Gout** | Inflammation of joints due to **accumulation of uric acid crystals** |
| Cramps | Sudden painful contraction (salt loss, fatigue) |
| Slipped disc | Herniated intervertebral disc pressing on a nerve |
| Rickets / osteomalacia | Vitamin D deficiency |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Sarcomere** (Z to Z) is the functional unit; **myofibril** is a chain of sarcomeres; the **muscle fibre** is the cell.
- **A band stays the same length** during contraction; I band and H zone shorten.
- **A = anisotropic (dark), I = isotropic (light)** — not "A for actin". The A band is where **myosin** lies; the I band has only actin.
- Z lines bisect the **I band**; M line bisects the **A band** (and H zone).
- **Contractile proteins = actin and myosin**; troponin and tropomyosin are **regulatory**.
- **Ca²⁺ binds troponin**, not tropomyosin or myosin.
- ATP is needed for **detachment** of myosin from actin — hence rigor mortis.
- **Creatine phosphate yields ATP**; myosin uses ATP; myoglobin stores O₂.
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum** = modified **smooth** ER; ergastoplasm = rough ER.
- **Cardiac muscle is striated but involuntary**; smooth muscle is non-striated and involuntary.
- **Floating ribs are 11th and 12th**; false ribs 8–10.
- **Sacrum = 5 fused vertebrae but counted as 1**; coccyx = 4 fused counted as 1.
- **Hyoid** is part of the axial skeleton but does not articulate with any bone.
- Shoulder (humerus–pectoral girdle) = ball and socket; **knee = hinge**; atlas–axis = pivot; carpals = gliding; thumb = saddle.
- **Myasthenia gravis** = neuromuscular junction (autoimmune); **muscular dystrophy** = genetic muscle degeneration.
