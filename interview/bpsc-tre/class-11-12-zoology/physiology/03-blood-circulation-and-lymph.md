This chapter covers NCERT class 11 "Body Fluids and Circulation" and adds B.Sc./Guyton-level points: plasma protein functions, all thirteen clotting factors, Bombay blood group, Frank–Starling law, ECG intervals and pressure numbers. TRE 2.0 asked lymph (Q81), lymphocytes (Q82) and fibrinogen (Q83); TRE 1.0 asked the rabbit's brain artery (Q76) and Rh-incompatible marriage (Q89).

> **In simple words:** Blood is a fluid connective tissue: a liquid called **plasma** carrying cells — red cells for O₂, white cells for defence, platelets for clotting. The **heart** is a four-chambered pump. It sends blood twice round the body in each full circuit — once to the lungs and once to the rest of the body (**double circulation**). Fluid that leaks out of capillaries returns through a second set of tubes, the **lymphatic system**.

Basic version: [Human Physiology — Digestion to Excretion (Class 9–10)](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/human-physiology-digestion-to-excretion). Antibodies and immunity: [Immunology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/reproduction-health/immunology).

## 1. Blood — the basics

- Blood is a **fluid connective tissue** (mesodermal origin) with a fluid matrix (plasma) and formed elements.
- Volume in an adult ~**5–5.5 L** (about 7–8% of body weight); pH **7.35–7.45** (slightly alkaline).
- **Plasma ~55%**, **formed elements ~45%** (this percentage of RBCs is the **haematocrit / packed cell volume**, PCV ~40–45%).
- Blood cell formation = **haemopoiesis**; in adults in the **red bone marrow** (of flat bones, ends of long bones); in the embryo in the yolk sac, then liver and spleen.

### Plasma

| Component | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | **90–92%** | Solvent and heat carrier |
| **Plasma proteins** | **6–8%** (6–8 g/100 mL) | See table below |
| Minerals | ~1% | Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, HCO₃⁻, Cl⁻ |
| Others | small | Glucose, amino acids, lipids, hormones, urea, CO₂ |

| Plasma protein | Made in | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** (most abundant, ~3.5–5 g/dL) | Liver | Maintains **colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure**; carries bilirubin, fatty acids, drugs |
| **Globulins** (α, β, γ) | Liver (α, β); **plasma cells** (γ) | **Defence** — γ-globulins are **antibodies**; α and β carry hormones, iron (transferrin), copper (caeruloplasmin) |
| **Fibrinogen** | Liver | **Coagulation** — becomes fibrin |
| Prothrombin | Liver (needs vitamin K) | Coagulation |

- **Serum** = plasma **minus clotting factors** (fibrinogen etc.). Serum is what remains after blood clots.
- Normal albumin : globulin (A:G) ratio ≈ **1.5–2 : 1**.

## 2. Formed elements

| Feature | **RBC (erythrocyte)** | **WBC (leucocyte)** | **Platelet (thrombocyte)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Count | **5–5.5 million/mm³** (men); ~4.5–5 million (women) | **6000–8000/mm³** | **1.5–3.5 lakh/mm³** |
| Shape | **Biconcave, non-nucleated** in mammals | Nucleated, colourless | Cell fragments (no nucleus) |
| Life span | **~120 days** | Hours to years | ~7–10 days |
| Origin | Red bone marrow | Bone marrow (lymphocytes mature in thymus/lymph organs) | **Megakaryocytes** of bone marrow |
| Function | Carry O₂ (haemoglobin, **12–16 g/100 mL**) and some CO₂ | Defence | **Clotting** |
| Destroyed in | **Spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs") and liver | — | Spleen |

- RBCs lack a nucleus, mitochondria and ER in mammals — more space for Hb, and they use **anaerobic glycolysis**. Camel and llama RBCs are **oval** but still non-nucleated; frog and bird RBCs are **nucleated**.
- RBC production (**erythropoiesis**) is driven by **erythropoietin** from the **kidney**; needs iron, vitamin B₁₂, folic acid.
- Abnormal counts: **polycythaemia** (excess RBCs), **anaemia** (low Hb), **leucocytosis/leukaemia** (excess WBCs; leukaemia is blood cancer), **leucopenia**, **thrombocytopenia** (low platelets — common in **dengue**).

### White blood cells

| Type | % of WBCs | Nucleus | Main role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** (granulocyte) | **60–65%** (most abundant) | 3–5 lobes | **Phagocytosis** of bacteria; first to arrive; pus cells |
| **Eosinophils** (acidophils) | 2–3% | Bilobed | **Allergy**, parasitic worm infections |
| **Basophils** | **0.5–1%** (least) | S-shaped | Secrete **histamine, serotonin, heparin**; inflammation |
| **Lymphocytes** (agranulocyte) | 20–25% | Large round | **Immunity** — B cells (antibodies) and T cells (cell-mediated) |
| **Monocytes** (agranulocyte) | 6–8% | Kidney-shaped | **Largest WBCs**; become **macrophages**; phagocytosis |

Memory for order of abundance: "**Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas**" — Neutrophil > Lymphocyte > Monocyte > Eosinophil > Basophil.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q82 asked: "Component of blood responsible for producing antibodies is — (A) lymphocyte (B) thrombocyte (C) RBC." **Official answer: (A) lymphocyte.** Strictly, **B lymphocytes** turn into **plasma cells**, which secrete antibodies.

## 3. Coagulation (clotting) of blood

When a vessel is injured, three things happen: **vasoconstriction**, a **platelet plug**, and a **fibrin clot**.

NCERT summary: injured tissue and platelets release factors → an enzyme complex **thrombokinase (prothrombinase)** forms → it converts **prothrombin → thrombin** (needs **Ca²⁺**) → thrombin converts soluble **fibrinogen → insoluble fibrin** threads → threads trap RBCs and platelets → **clot (coagulum)**.

<div class="formula">

**Extrinsic pathway** (fast, seconds): tissue damage → **tissue factor (III)** + **VII** → activates **X**.

**Intrinsic pathway** (slower, minutes): contact with collagen/glass → **XII → XI → IX (with VIII)** → activates **X**.

**Common pathway:** **Xa + V + Ca²⁺ + platelet phospholipid = prothrombinase** → prothrombin (II) → **thrombin** → fibrinogen (I) → **fibrin** → **XIII** cross-links fibrin into a stable mesh.

</div>

| Factor | Name | Factor | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Fibrinogen** | VIII | **Antihaemophilic factor (A)** |
| II | **Prothrombin** | IX | **Christmas factor** (antihaemophilic factor B) |
| III | **Tissue factor / thromboplastin** | X | Stuart–Prower factor |
| IV | **Calcium ions (Ca²⁺)** | XI | Plasma thromboplastin antecedent (PTA) |
| V | Proaccelerin (labile factor) | XII | Hageman factor (contact factor) |
| VII | Proconvertin (stable factor) | XIII | Fibrin-stabilising factor |

- There is **no factor VI** (it was found to be activated factor V).
- **Vitamin K-dependent factors: II, VII, IX, X** (memory "**1972**" — 10, 9, 7, 2).
- **Haemophilia A** — factor **VIII** deficiency; **haemophilia B (Christmas disease)** — factor **IX**; both **X-linked recessive** ("royal disease" — Queen Victoria's descendants).
- Normal bleeding time ~**1–5 min**; clotting time ~**4–10 min** (method-dependent).
- **Anticoagulants**: **heparin** (made by mast cells and basophils, liver; prevents thrombin formation), **hirudin** (from leech saliva), **dicoumarol/warfarin** (block vitamin K), **sodium citrate, sodium oxalate, EDTA** (bind Ca²⁺ — used to store blood in blood banks).
- A clot inside a vessel = **thrombus**; a travelling clot = **embolus**. Plasmin (from plasminogen) dissolves clots (**fibrinolysis**); streptokinase is a clot-dissolving drug.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q83 asked: "The plasma content that forms the fibrous skeleton of a clot is — (A) platelet (B) fibrinogen (C) thromboplastin." **Official answer: (B) fibrinogen.** Fibrinogen becomes **fibrin**, whose threads are the clot's framework. Platelets are cells, not plasma contents.

## 4. Blood groups

### ABO system

Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner (1900)** (A, B, O); group **AB** by **von Decastello and Sturli (1902)**. Landsteiner got the Nobel Prize in 1930. Based on **antigens (agglutinogens)** A and B on the RBC surface and **antibodies (agglutinins)** in plasma. Controlled by gene **I** with three alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i — an example of **multiple allelism** and **codominance** (Iᴬ Iᴮ) — see [Mendelian Genetics and its Extensions](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/mendelian-genetics-and-its-extensions).

<div class="formula">

| Blood group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Genotype | Can donate to | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | A | anti-B | IᴬIᴬ, Iᴬi | A, AB | A, O |
| **B** | B | anti-A | IᴮIᴮ, Iᴮi | B, AB | B, O |
| **AB** | A and B | **None** | IᴬIᴮ | AB only | **All** (universal recipient) |
| **O** | **None** | anti-A and anti-B | ii | **All** (universal donor) | O only |

</div>

- Mismatched transfusion → **agglutination** (clumping) of donor RBCs → haemolysis, kidney failure.
- **Bombay blood group (hh)** — discovered by **Y. M. Bhende (1952)** in Mumbai: no H antigen, so no A or B antigen can form even with Iᴬ/Iᴮ genes; has anti-A, anti-B and anti-H; can receive only Bombay blood.
- TRE 2.0 Q147 asked "ABO blood group system exhibits" — official answer **(B) multiple allelism**.

### Rh system

- Discovered by **Landsteiner and Wiener (1940)** in **rhesus monkeys**. The main antigen is **D**.
- Nearly **80%** of humans are **Rh⁺** (have the antigen); Rh⁻ people lack it (higher share in Europeans, lower in Indians — about 5–7%).
- Unlike ABO, anti-Rh antibodies are **not naturally present**; they form only after an Rh⁻ person is exposed to Rh⁺ blood.

### Erythroblastosis foetalis (haemolytic disease of the newborn)

1. **Rh⁻ mother** carries an **Rh⁺ foetus** (father Rh⁺).
2. The first pregnancy is usually safe — the blood of mother and foetus is separated by the placenta.
3. At **delivery** (or abortion, bleeding), some foetal Rh⁺ RBCs enter the mother's blood → she makes **anti-Rh (anti-D) antibodies**.
4. In a **second Rh⁺ pregnancy**, these IgG antibodies **cross the placenta** and destroy foetal RBCs → anaemia, jaundice, **erythroblasts** in foetal blood, possible death (hydrops foetalis).
5. **Prevention**: give the mother **anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** within **72 hours** after the first delivery (also at ~28 weeks) — it destroys foetal Rh⁺ cells before her immune system notices them.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q89 asked: "Which marriage should be prohibited from biological point of view? — (A) Rh⁺ boy and Rh⁺ girl (B) Rh⁺ boy and Rh⁻ girl (C) Rh⁻ boy and Rh⁺ girl." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) Rh⁺ boy and Rh⁻ girl.** Only an Rh⁻ **mother** with an Rh⁺ child is at risk. ("Prohibited" is too strong today — anti-D injection prevents the disease — but this pairing is the risky one.)

## 5. Lymph and the lymphatic system

- As blood passes through capillaries, some water and small solutes leak into spaces between cells = **interstitial (tissue) fluid**. It has the same mineral distribution as plasma but **fewer proteins** and **no RBCs**.
- Tissue fluid enters thin **lymph capillaries** → it is now **lymph** → larger lymph vessels (with valves) → **lymph nodes** → **thoracic duct** (largest; drains the left side and lower body) into the **left subclavian vein**; the **right lymphatic duct** drains the right upper body into the right subclavian vein.
- Lymph: colourless fluid with **lymphocytes** (specialised WBCs); no RBCs; few or no platelets; less protein; after a fatty meal, lacteal lymph is milky (**chyle**).
- Functions: returns tissue fluid and proteins to blood; absorbs and carries **fats** (lacteals); **immunity** (lymphocytes, lymph nodes filter microbes).
- Lymphoid organs: **primary** — bone marrow and **thymus**; **secondary** — lymph nodes, **spleen** (largest lymphoid organ), tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix.
- **Oedema** — swelling when tissue fluid accumulates (low plasma protein, blocked lymph vessels). **Elephantiasis (lymphatic filariasis)** — *Wuchereria bancrofti* blocks lymph vessels; vector *Culex* mosquito.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q81 asked: "Lymph differs from blood in possessing — (A) only WBC (B) more RBC and WBC (C) more RBC and few WBC." **Official answer: (A) only WBC.** Lymph has lymphocytes but no RBCs.

## 6. Circulatory patterns in animals

- **Open circulation** — blood flows through open spaces (**haemocoel / sinuses**): arthropods (cockroach, 13-chambered tubular heart) and most molluscs.
- **Closed circulation** — blood always inside vessels: annelids (earthworm), **cephalopod molluscs** (octopus, squid) and all vertebrates. Closed is more efficient and precisely regulated.

| Group | Heart chambers | Circulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Fishes** | **2** (1 atrium + 1 ventricle) — "**venous heart**" (only deoxygenated blood) | **Single** circulation |
| **Amphibians** | **3** (2 atria + 1 ventricle) | Incomplete double circulation (mixed blood) |
| **Reptiles** | 3 (ventricle **partly** divided) — **crocodiles have 4** | Incomplete double circulation |
| **Birds and mammals** | **4** (2 atria + 2 ventricles) | **Complete double circulation** |

- Birds retain the **right** systemic aortic arch; mammals the **left** systemic arch.

## 7. The human heart

- **Mesodermal** in origin; size of a closed fist; ~250–350 g; lies in the thoracic cavity between the lungs, slightly tilted to the **left**.
- Protected by a double-walled **pericardium** with **pericardial fluid** between the layers.
- Wall: epicardium, thick **myocardium** (cardiac muscle), endocardium.
- **Four chambers**: two thin-walled upper **atria** (receiving) and two thick-walled lower **ventricles** (pumping). The **left ventricle has the thickest wall** — it pumps to the whole body.
- Septa: **inter-atrial septum** (thin, muscular; has the **fossa ovalis** — remnant of the foetal **foramen ovale**), **inter-ventricular septum** (thick), **atrio-ventricular septum** (fibrous).

| Valve | Location | Flaps |
|---|---|---|
| **Tricuspid** | Right atrium → right ventricle | 3 cusps |
| **Bicuspid (mitral)** | Left atrium → left ventricle | 2 cusps |
| **Pulmonary semilunar** | Right ventricle → pulmonary artery | 3 half-moon cusps |
| **Aortic semilunar** | Left ventricle → aorta | 3 half-moon cusps |

- AV valve cusps are held by **chordae tendineae** attached to **papillary muscles** — they stop the valves turning inside out.
- Valves allow flow in **one direction only**: atria → ventricles → arteries.
- Heart muscle gets its own blood from the **coronary arteries** (first branches of the aorta); venous blood returns via the **coronary sinus** into the right atrium.
- Foetal shunts: **foramen ovale** (RA → LA) and **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary artery → aorta; becomes the ligamentum arteriosum). A foetal **ductus venosus** bypasses the liver.

- **Sino-atrial node (SAN)** — in the **upper right corner of the right atrium**; generates the most action potentials (**70–75 per minute**) → **pacemaker**.
- **Atrio-ventricular node (AVN)** — lower left corner of the right atrium, near the AV septum; **delays** the impulse (~0.1 s) so atria finish emptying first.
- **AV bundle (bundle of His)** → right and left **bundle branches** in the interventricular septum → **Purkinje fibres** throughout the ventricular wall.
- The human heart is **myogenic** — the beat starts in its own muscle (nodal tissue). The heart of many arthropods (e.g. *Limulus*, crustaceans) and annelids is **neurogenic** (beat started by a nerve ganglion).
- A damaged SAN or block → an **artificial pacemaker** is implanted.

## 8. The cardiac cycle

One **cardiac cycle** = one complete sequence of contraction (**systole**) and relaxation (**diastole**) of atria and ventricles. At **72 beats/min**, one cycle lasts **60/72 ≈ 0.8 s**.

<div class="formula">

| Phase | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Joint (complete) diastole** | ~0.4 s | All chambers relaxed; AV valves open, semilunar valves closed; blood flows from veins into atria and ventricles (~70% of ventricular filling) |
| **Atrial systole** | **0.1 s** | Atria contract (SAN impulse) → push the last ~**30%** of blood into ventricles |
| **Ventricular systole** | **0.3 s** | Impulse via AVN–His–Purkinje; ventricular pressure rises → **AV valves close (1st sound "lub")** → isovolumetric contraction → **semilunar valves open** → blood ejected into aorta and pulmonary artery |
| Ventricular diastole | 0.5 s (overlaps joint diastole) | Ventricles relax; pressure falls → **semilunar valves close (2nd sound "dub")** → isovolumetric relaxation → AV valves open |

</div>

- Atrial systole 0.1 s + atrial diastole 0.7 s = 0.8 s. Ventricular systole 0.3 s + ventricular diastole 0.5 s = 0.8 s.
- **Lub (S1)** — longer, low-pitched; closure of **tricuspid and bicuspid** valves; start of ventricular systole.
- **Dub (S2)** — shorter, sharper; closure of **semilunar** valves; start of ventricular diastole. Heard with a **stethoscope** (invented by **René Laennec, 1816**).
- **End-diastolic volume (EDV)** ~120–130 mL; **end-systolic volume (ESV)** ~50–60 mL; **stroke volume (SV)** = EDV − ESV ≈ **70 mL**; **ejection fraction** = SV/EDV ≈ 55–65%.

### Electrocardiogram (ECG)

Graph of the electrical activity of the heart; invented by **Willem Einthoven** (Nobel 1924). A standard ECG uses leads on the wrists and the left ankle (plus chest leads).

| Wave | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P wave** | **Depolarisation of the atria** → atrial contraction |
| **QRS complex** | **Depolarisation of the ventricles** → ventricular contraction begins (atrial repolarisation is hidden here) |
| **T wave** | **Repolarisation of the ventricles** → end of ventricular systole |
| PR interval (0.12–0.20 s) | Time from atrial to ventricular depolarisation (AV delay); lengthened in heart block |
| ST segment | Raised or depressed in myocardial infarction (heart attack) |

- **Heart rate from ECG**: count QRS complexes in a known time. Example: 18 QRS in 15 s → 18 × 4 = **72 beats/min**.

## 9. Cardiac output and its regulation

<div class="formula">

**Cardiac output (CO) = stroke volume (SV) × heart rate (HR)**

Normal: 70 mL × 72 beats/min = **5040 mL/min ≈ 5 L/min**. Athletes can raise CO to 25–35 L/min.

</div>

**Worked numericals**

1. HR 75/min, SV 80 mL → CO = 75 × 80 = **6000 mL/min (6 L/min)**.
2. CO 4.9 L/min, HR 70/min → SV = 4900 / 70 = **70 mL**.
3. EDV 130 mL, ESV 60 mL → SV = **70 mL**; ejection fraction = 70/130 = **54%**.
4. A whole blood volume of 5 L at CO 5 L/min passes through the heart **once every minute** (each side).
5. **Cardiac index** = CO / body surface area; 5 L/min ÷ 1.7 m² ≈ **3 L/min/m²**.

**Regulation**

- The heart is **auto-regulated** by nodal tissue but can be changed by the **cardiovascular centre in the medulla oblongata** through the autonomic nerves.
- **Sympathetic** nerves and **adrenaline/noradrenaline** (adrenal medulla) → **↑ heart rate, ↑ force of contraction → ↑ cardiac output**.
- **Parasympathetic** (**vagus nerve**, acetylcholine) → ↓ heart rate, slows AV conduction → ↓ cardiac output.
- **Frank–Starling law of the heart**: the more the ventricle is filled (stretched) in diastole, the stronger it contracts — so output matches venous return.
- **Baroreceptors** in the carotid sinus and aortic arch sense BP and adjust heart rate (baroreceptor reflex).

## 10. Double circulation and portal systems

<div class="formula">

**Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** (deoxygenated) → lungs → **pulmonary veins** (oxygenated) → left atrium.

**Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → **aorta** → arteries → arterioles → capillaries (tissues) → venules → veins → **superior and inferior vena cava** → right atrium.

</div>

- **Pulmonary artery** is the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood (after birth); **pulmonary vein** the only vein carrying oxygenated blood. (In the foetus, the umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood.)
- Discovery: **William Harvey (1628)**, *De Motu Cordis*; capillaries seen by **Marcello Malpighi (1661)**.
- **Hepatic portal system**: blood from the intestine (stomach, spleen, pancreas) → **hepatic portal vein** → liver → hepatic vein → inferior vena cava. A portal vein starts and ends in capillaries.
- **Hypophyseal portal system**: hypothalamus → anterior pituitary (carries releasing hormones).
- **Renal portal system** — present in fishes, amphibians and reptiles (and reduced in birds); **absent in mammals**.
- Coronary circulation supplies the heart muscle itself.

### Arteries and veins

| | Artery | Vein |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Away from heart | Towards heart |
| Wall | Thick, elastic, muscular | Thin, less muscle |
| Lumen | Narrow | Wide |
| Valves | Absent (except at origin of aorta/pulmonary trunk) | Present (stop backflow) |
| Pressure | High, pulsatile | Low |

Three layers in both: **tunica interna (intima)**, **tunica media** (smooth muscle, elastic fibres), **tunica externa (adventitia)**. Capillaries have only endothelium.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q76 asked: "Which artery supplies oxygenated blood to the rabbit brain? — (A) Pulmonary artery (B) External carotid (C) Internal carotid." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Internal carotid.** In rabbit (and human), each **common carotid** splits into an **internal carotid** (to the **brain** and eye) and an **external carotid** (to the face, jaws, tongue). The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

## 11. Blood pressure

- **Blood pressure** — the pressure blood exerts on the walls of arteries. Measured with a **sphygmomanometer** (Riva-Rocci cuff; Korotkoff sounds) in the **brachial artery**.
- Normal: **120/80 mmHg** — **systolic** (during ventricular systole) / **diastolic** (during diastole).
- **Pulse pressure** = systolic − diastolic = 120 − 80 = **40 mmHg**.
- **Mean arterial pressure (MAP)** ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure = 80 + 40/3 ≈ **93 mmHg**.
- **Hypertension**: repeated readings of **140/90 mmHg or more** (NCERT). It damages the heart, brain and kidneys.
- **Hypotension**: below ~90/60.
- Factors: cardiac output, peripheral resistance (arteriole diameter), blood volume, viscosity, elasticity of vessels. BP falls along the circuit: aorta ~100 → capillaries ~30 → venae cavae ~0 mmHg.
- **Pulse** — rhythmic expansion of an artery with each heartbeat; felt at the **radial artery** (wrist); normal **72/min**.

## 12. Disorders of the circulatory system

| Disorder | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension** | BP ≥ 140/90; leads to heart disease, stroke, kidney damage |
| **Coronary artery disease (CAD) / atherosclerosis** | Deposits of **calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue** narrow the coronary arteries |
| **Angina pectoris** | Acute chest pain when not enough O₂ reaches heart muscle; common in middle-aged and elderly |
| **Heart failure (congestive)** | Heart cannot pump enough blood; congestion of lungs is a main symptom (not the same as cardiac arrest) |
| **Cardiac arrest** | Heart **stops** beating |
| **Heart attack (myocardial infarction)** | Heart muscle **suddenly damaged** by inadequate blood supply |
| **Arteriosclerosis** | Hardening of arteries (loss of elasticity) |
| **Rheumatic heart disease** | Valve damage after *Streptococcus* throat infection |
| **Blue baby** | Congenital defect (e.g. Tetralogy of Fallot) or open foramen ovale → mixed blood |

Treatments: **angioplasty** with a stent; **coronary artery bypass graft**; drugs (aspirin, statins); first heart transplant — **Christiaan Barnard (1967)**, South Africa.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Serum = plasma − clotting factors**, not "plasma minus cells".
- **Albumin** keeps osmotic pressure; **globulins** are for defence; **fibrinogen** for clotting.
- **Factor IV is Ca²⁺**, factor III is thromboplastin; there is no factor VI.
- **Thrombocytes** (platelets) come from **megakaryocytes**, and are fragments, not true cells.
- Mammalian RBCs have **no nucleus**, but camel RBCs are still non-nucleated — only their shape (oval) differs.
- **O = universal donor; AB = universal recipient** (for ABO). O⁻ is the safest universal donor including Rh.
- **Anti-Rh antibodies are not natural** — they need sensitisation; so the **first** baby is usually safe.
- Erythroblastosis needs an **Rh⁻ mother** — an Rh⁻ father with Rh⁺ mother is safe.
- **Lub = closure of AV valves; dub = closure of semilunar valves** — never "opening".
- **P = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation; T = ventricular repolarisation**; atrial repolarisation has no separate wave.
- **SAN is in the right atrium**, not the left.
- **Fish heart pumps only deoxygenated blood** (venous heart).
- **Crocodile** (a reptile) has a **4-chambered** heart.
- **Renal portal system is absent in mammals**.
- **Lymph has lymphocytes but no RBCs**; thoracic duct opens into the **left subclavian vein**.
- **Spleen** is the largest lymphoid organ and the RBC graveyard; **thymus** shrinks after puberty.

## Practice with answers

1. Which plasma protein maintains osmotic pressure? — **Albumin**.
2. Which WBCs increase in allergy and worm infection? — **Eosinophils**.
3. Which cells release histamine and heparin? — **Basophils** (and mast cells).
4. Which ion is clotting factor IV? — **Calcium (Ca²⁺)**.
5. Which enzyme converts fibrinogen into fibrin? — **Thrombin**.
6. What does sodium citrate do in a blood bag? — **Binds Ca²⁺**, so blood does not clot.
7. A person of blood group A can receive blood from? — **A and O**.
8. What is the genotype of a person with group O? — **ii** (homozygous recessive).
9. Why is the first Rh⁺ child of an Rh⁻ mother usually safe? — **Mother has no anti-Rh antibodies yet** (sensitised only at delivery).
10. Into which vein does the thoracic duct open? — **Left subclavian vein**.
11. Which part of the heart is the pacemaker? — **SA node** (right atrium wall).
12. Which valve lies between the left atrium and left ventricle? — **Bicuspid (mitral) valve**.
13. Which ECG wave shows ventricular repolarisation? — **T wave**.
14. HR 80/min, SV 75 mL — cardiac output? — **6 L/min** (80 × 75 = 6000 mL).
15. CO 5.6 L/min at HR 80/min — stroke volume? — **70 mL**.
16. BP 130/85 — pulse pressure? — **45 mmHg**.
17. Which nerve slows the heart? — **Vagus nerve** (parasympathetic, acetylcholine).
18. Which vessel carries blood from the intestine to the liver? — **Hepatic portal vein**.
19. Which foetal opening between the atria closes after birth? — **Foramen ovale** (becomes fossa ovalis).
20. Which disorder is chest pain from lack of O₂ to heart muscle? — **Angina pectoris**.
