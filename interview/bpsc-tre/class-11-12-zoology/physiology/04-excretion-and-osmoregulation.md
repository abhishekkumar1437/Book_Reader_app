This chapter covers NCERT class 11 "Excretory Products and their Elimination" and adds the B.Sc. points: the enzymes of the ornithine cycle, transport in each nephron segment, osmolarity numbers of the medulla, renal clearance and osmoregulators versus osmoconformers. TRE 2.0 asked **six** excretion questions in a row (Q84–89): ultrafiltration, urea synthesis, ADH, urine volume hormones, diuresis and site of reabsorption.

> **In simple words:** Breaking down proteins and nucleic acids leaves **nitrogen** waste (ammonia, urea or uric acid). Kidneys filter blood like a sieve (**filtration**), take back everything useful (**reabsorption**), add some extra wastes (**secretion**), and concentrate what is left into **urine**. Hormones decide how much water and salt to keep. **Osmoregulation** means keeping the body's water and salt balance steady.

Basic version: [Human Physiology — Digestion to Excretion (Class 9–10)](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/human-physiology-digestion-to-excretion).

## 1. Nitrogenous wastes

| Mode | Main waste | Toxicity / water needed | Animals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonotelism** | **Ammonia** (NH₃) — very soluble | **Most toxic**; needs the most water (~500 mL per g N) | Most **bony fishes**, aquatic amphibians (tadpoles), aquatic insects, most aquatic invertebrates (Hydra, sponges) |
| **Ureotelism** | **Urea** — made in the liver | Less toxic; moderate water (~50 mL per g N) | **Mammals**, many terrestrial amphibians (adult frog), **marine (cartilaginous) fishes** like sharks |
| **Uricotelism** | **Uric acid** — paste/pellet, nearly insoluble | **Least toxic**; least water (~1 mL per g N) | **Reptiles, birds**, land snails, **insects** |

- Ammonia is excreted by **diffusion across body surfaces or gill surfaces** as NH₄⁺ ions.
- Sharks and rays keep **urea (and TMAO)** in their blood to stay osmotically equal to sea water.
- Tadpole is **ammonotelic**; it becomes **ureotelic** after metamorphosis into a frog.
- Uric acid is also made in humans from **purine** breakdown; excess → **gout**.
- Other wastes: **guanine** (spiders), **trimethylamine oxide** (marine bony fishes), **creatinine**, **hippuric acid**, allantoin.

## 3. The human excretory system

**Kidneys** (a pair) → **ureters** → **urinary bladder** → **urethra**.

- Kidneys are reddish-brown, **bean-shaped**, lying between the last thoracic and 3rd lumbar vertebrae close to the dorsal inner wall of the abdomen; **retroperitoneal**; the **right kidney is slightly lower** (liver above it).
- Size: **10–12 cm long, 5–7 cm wide, 2–3 cm thick**; weight **120–170 g** each.
- **Hilum** — notch on the inner concave side where the ureter, blood vessels and nerves enter or leave. Inside, the **renal pelvis** (funnel) with projections called **calyces**.
- Outer **cortex** and inner **medulla**; medulla has conical **medullary pyramids** projecting into calyces. Cortex extends between pyramids as **renal columns (columns of Bertini)**.
- Each kidney has nearly **one million nephrons** — the functional units.
- Blood supply: ~**1100–1200 mL/min** (about 20–25% of cardiac output) through the renal artery.

Each nephron has two parts:

1. **Malpighian body (renal corpuscle)** = **glomerulus** (a tuft of capillaries formed by the **afferent arteriole**, drained by the narrower **efferent arteriole**) + **Bowman's capsule** (double-walled cup around it).
2. **Renal tubule**: **proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** → **Henle's loop** (descending limb, hairpin, ascending limb — thin and thick parts) → **distal convoluted tubule (DCT)** → opens into a **collecting duct** (several nephrons share one) → ducts converge into the renal pelvis via medullary pyramids.

- Malpighian corpuscle, PCT and DCT lie in the **cortex**; the loop dips into the **medulla**.
- **Cortical nephrons** (~85%) — short loops; **juxtamedullary nephrons** (~15%) — **very long loops** deep into the medulla.
- The efferent arteriole forms the **peritubular capillaries** around the tubule; a minute vessel from it runs parallel to Henle's loop forming the U-shaped **vasa recta** — prominent in juxtamedullary nephrons, absent or reduced in cortical ones.
- The inner (visceral) layer of Bowman's capsule is made of **podocytes**, arranged to leave minute spaces called **filtration slits (slit pores)**.
- **Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** — where the DCT touches the afferent arteriole: **JG cells** (modified smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole, secrete **renin**) + **macula densa** (DCT cells that sense NaCl) + lacis (extraglomerular mesangial) cells.
- Glomerular blood pressure is high (~**55–60 mmHg**) because the efferent arteriole is narrower than the afferent.

### Cortical and juxtamedullary nephrons compared

| Feature | **Cortical nephron** | **Juxtamedullary nephron** |
|---|---|---|
| Share | ~85% | ~15% |
| Glomerulus position | Outer cortex | Inner cortex, near the medulla |
| Loop of Henle | **Short**, dips only slightly into the medulla | **Very long**, runs deep into the medulla |
| Vasa recta | **Absent or highly reduced** (peritubular capillaries only) | **Prominent** |
| Main job | Filtration and ordinary reabsorption | **Concentrating urine** (counter-current) |

### Histology of the tubule (B.Sc.)

| Segment | Epithelium | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bowman's capsule — outer layer | Simple squamous | Only a covering |
| Bowman's capsule — inner layer | **Podocytes** | Filtration slits |
| PCT | Simple **cuboidal with brush border** (microvilli), many mitochondria | Large surface, active transport |
| Thin limbs of Henle | Simple squamous | Passive water/salt movement |
| Thick ascending limb | Cuboidal, many mitochondria | Active NaCl pumping |
| DCT | Cuboidal, few microvilli | Hormone-controlled fine-tuning |
| Collecting duct | Cuboidal to columnar (principal and intercalated cells) | ADH-controlled water; H⁺/K⁺ secretion |

## 5. Urine formation — three processes

### (a) Glomerular filtration (ultrafiltration)

- Blood is filtered so finely through three layers — **endothelium of glomerular blood vessels** (with pores), **basement membrane**, and the **epithelium of Bowman's capsule (podocytes with filtration slits)** — that almost all plasma constituents **except proteins** pass into the capsule lumen. Hence it is called **ultrafiltration**.
- Filtrate = protein-free plasma (water, glucose, amino acids, salts, urea, uric acid, creatinine, vitamins).
- **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** ≈ **125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day** in a healthy person.
- Kidneys filter ~1100–1200 mL of blood per minute, about 1/5th of the blood pumped by each ventricle.

<div class="formula">

**Net filtration pressure** = glomerular hydrostatic pressure − (colloid osmotic pressure of plasma + capsular hydrostatic pressure)

Typical: 60 − (32 + 18) = **10 mmHg** (Guyton values; older books 75 − (30 + 20) = 25 mmHg).

</div>

- GFR is kept constant by **autoregulation** (myogenic response of the afferent arteriole and **tubuloglomerular feedback** via the macula densa → JG cells → renin).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q84 asked: "Ultrafiltration takes place in — (A) Bowman's capsule (B) glomerulus (C) collecting tubule." **Official answer: (B) glomerulus.** The filtering happens across the glomerular capillary wall (and the inner capsule layer), and the filtrate collects in Bowman's capsule. Since both form the Malpighian body, "(D) More than one" was arguable, but BPSC chose **glomerulus** (NCERT's "glomerular filtration").

### (b) Tubular reabsorption

About **99%** of the filtrate is reabsorbed; only ~**1.5 L/day** of urine is formed. Reabsorption is **active** (glucose, amino acids, Na⁺) or **passive** (water by osmosis, urea, Cl⁻ partly).

| Segment | What happens (NCERT + B.Sc.) |
|---|---|
| **PCT** | Lined by **simple cuboidal brush-border epithelium** (more surface). Reabsorbs **nearly all essential nutrients** (100% glucose and amino acids), **70–80% of electrolytes and water**. Secretes H⁺, NH₃ and absorbs HCO₃⁻ → keeps **pH and ionic balance**. Also secretes drugs, creatinine, penicillin |
| **Descending limb of Henle's loop** | **Permeable to water**, almost impermeable to electrolytes → filtrate becomes **concentrated** (hypertonic) as it goes down |
| **Ascending limb** | **Impermeable to water**, permits transport of electrolytes (passive in thin part, **active Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ in thick part**) → filtrate becomes **dilute** (hypotonic) as it goes up |
| **DCT** | **Conditional** reabsorption of Na⁺ and water (**aldosterone**, **ADH**); reabsorbs HCO₃⁻; selective secretion of H⁺, K⁺ and NH₃ → pH and Na–K balance |
| **Collecting duct** | Reabsorbs large amounts of **water** (under **ADH**, via **aquaporin-2** channels) → concentrated urine; allows passage of small amounts of **urea** into the medullary interstitium (keeps osmolarity); secretes H⁺ and K⁺ |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q89 asked: "Reabsorption of useful substances back into the blood from the glomerular filtrate in the nephron occurs in — (A) PCT (B) DCT (C) loop of Henle." **Official answer: (A) proximal convoluted tubule.** All three reabsorb something, but the PCT reabsorbs nearly all the **useful** substances (glucose, amino acids, most salts and water) — so BPSC chose (A), not "More than one".

### (c) Tubular secretion

Tubular cells **add** substances from blood to the filtrate: **H⁺, K⁺, NH₃/NH₄⁺**, creatinine, drugs (penicillin), dyes. This keeps the **ionic and acid–base balance** of body fluids.

### Renal threshold and clearance (B.Sc.)

- **Renal threshold for glucose** ≈ **180 mg/dL** blood glucose; above this, glucose appears in urine (**glycosuria** — diabetes mellitus). Tubular maximum (Tm) for glucose ≈ 375 mg/min.
- **Renal clearance** = (urine concentration × urine flow) / plasma concentration. **Inulin** clearance = GFR (inulin is filtered but neither reabsorbed nor secreted). **PAH** clearance ≈ renal plasma flow.

**Worked numericals**

1. GFR 125 mL/min → per day = 125 × 60 × 24 = 180,000 mL = **180 L/day**.
2. Urine 1.5 L/day from 180 L filtrate → reabsorbed = 178.5 L → **99.2%**.
3. Inulin: urine conc. 30 mg/mL, urine flow 1 mL/min, plasma conc. 0.25 mg/mL → clearance = 30 × 1 / 0.25 = **120 mL/min** = GFR.
4. Plasma glucose 100 mg/dL, GFR 125 mL/min → glucose filtered = 1 mg/mL × 125 = **125 mg/min**; all reabsorbed (below Tm), so urine glucose = 0.

## 6. The counter-current mechanism

The mammalian kidney can make urine **about four times more concentrated** than the filtrate (up to ~1200 mOsm/L vs 300 mOsm/L). Two structures with **counter-current** (opposite-direction) flow do this: **Henle's loop** (counter-current **multiplier**) and **vasa recta** (counter-current **exchanger**).

1. The thick ascending limb **pumps NaCl out** into the medullary interstitium but does not let water follow.
2. The descending limb loses **water** to this salty interstitium → fluid inside becomes concentrated; at the bend it is most concentrated.
3. Repeated along the loop, this builds a gradient: **300 mOsm/L in the cortex → about 1200 mOsm/L in the inner medulla**.
4. **Urea** leaking from the collecting duct into the inner medulla adds to the gradient (urea recycling: back into the thin ascending limb).
5. **Vasa recta** blood flows in the opposite direction and **does not wash away** the gradient — NaCl entering the descending vasa recta leaves the ascending one.
6. As collecting-duct fluid passes down through this hypertonic medulla, water leaves it (if **ADH** is present) → **concentrated (hypertonic) urine**.

> **Exam angle:** NCERT's two key lines — "the gradient is mainly caused by **NaCl and urea**" and "the proximity between Henle's loop and vasa recta, as well as the counter current in them, help in maintaining an increasing osmolarity towards the inner medullary interstitium, i.e., from **300 mOsmolL⁻¹ in the cortex to about 1200 mOsmolL⁻¹** in the inner medulla."

- Desert animals (kangaroo rat) have **very long loops of Henle** and can produce extremely concentrated urine; they may never drink water (use metabolic water).
- Birds and mammals are the only vertebrates with loops of Henle and can make hypertonic urine.

## 7. The urea (ornithine) cycle

Urea is made in the **liver** (hepatocytes) from **ammonia** (from deamination of amino acids) and **CO₂**. Discovered by **Hans Krebs and Kurt Henseleit (1932)** — the first metabolic cycle discovered (before the citric acid cycle, 1937). So it is also called the **Krebs–Henseleit cycle**.

<div class="formula">

| Step | Reaction | Enzyme | Site |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NH₃ + CO₂ + 2 ATP → **carbamoyl phosphate** | **Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I** | Mitochondria |
| 2 | Carbamoyl phosphate + **ornithine** → **citrulline** | Ornithine transcarbamoylase | Mitochondria |
| 3 | Citrulline + **aspartate** + ATP → **argininosuccinate** (ATP → AMP + PPi) | Argininosuccinate synthetase | Cytosol |
| 4 | Argininosuccinate → **arginine + fumarate** | Argininosuccinase (lyase) | Cytosol |
| 5 | Arginine + H₂O → **urea + ornithine** | **Arginase** | Cytosol |

Overall: NH₃ + CO₂ + aspartate + 3 ATP → urea + fumarate (4 high-energy bonds used). One N of urea comes from free **NH₃**, the other from **aspartate**; the carbon from **CO₂**.

</div>

- Ornithine is regenerated each turn — so it is a **cycle**. Fumarate links it to the Krebs cycle ("Krebs bicycle").
- Urea goes by blood to the kidneys; ~**25–30 g** urea is excreted per day.
- Defects in cycle enzymes → **hyperammonaemia** (brain damage).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q85 asked: "The synthesis of urea in liver takes place by — (A) glycolysis (B) Krebs cycle (C) ornithine cycle." **Official answer: (C) ornithine cycle.** Do not confuse with the Krebs (citric acid) cycle; the urea cycle is the Krebs–**Henseleit** cycle.

## 8. Regulation of kidney function

<div class="formula">

**ADH (antidiuretic hormone = vasopressin)** — made in the **hypothalamus**, released from the **posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)**. Trigger: ↑ blood osmolarity (sensed by **osmoreceptors** in the hypothalamus), ↓ blood volume. Action: **water reabsorption from the DCT and collecting duct** (inserts aquaporin-2) → less, concentrated urine; also constricts blood vessels → ↑ BP.

**RAAS (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system)** — ↓ GFR / ↓ BP / ↓ Na⁺ → **JG cells** release **renin** (an enzyme) → angiotensinogen (from liver) → **angiotensin I** → (**ACE**, angiotensin converting enzyme, mainly in **lungs**) → **angiotensin II** → (i) powerful **vasoconstrictor** → ↑ BP and ↑ GFR; (ii) stimulates the **adrenal cortex** to release **aldosterone**; (iii) stimulates thirst and ADH.

**Aldosterone** (mineralocorticoid, zona glomerulosa) — **Na⁺ (and water) reabsorption** from the DCT and collecting duct, K⁺ secretion → ↑ blood volume and BP.

**ANF (atrial natriuretic factor/peptide)** — from the **atria of the heart** when they are stretched by ↑ blood volume/BP → **vasodilation**, ↑ Na⁺ and water excretion (natriuresis) → ↓ BP. It is the **check on RAAS**.

</div>

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q86 asked: "Reabsorption of water in distal convoluted tubule is controlled by — (A) ACTH (B) oxytocin (C) vasopressin." **Official answer: (C) vasopressin** (ADH).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q87 asked: "Volume of urine is regulated by — (A) aldosterone (B) aldosterone, ADH and testosterone (C) aldosterone and ADH." **Official answer: (C) aldosterone and ADH.** Testosterone has no role; ANF also acts, but it was not in the options.

### Diuresis

- **Diuresis** = **increased production of urine** (dilute urine). **Antidiuresis** = less urine.
- **Diuretics** — substances that increase urine: **alcohol** and **caffeine** (inhibit ADH release / action), furosemide (blocks Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ in the loop), thiazides (DCT), mannitol (osmotic). Drinking lots of water suppresses ADH → **water diuresis**.
- **Diabetes insipidus** — lack of ADH (or kidney not responding) → large volumes (up to 20 L/day) of dilute urine, intense thirst.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q88 asked: "Diuresis is characterised by — (A) increase in urine volume (B) decrease in urine volume (C) decrease in electrolyte balance." **Official answer: (A) increase in urine volume.**

### Hormone summary for the kidney

| Hormone | Source | Trigger | Effect on urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | Hypothalamus → posterior pituitary | ↑ osmolarity, ↓ volume | **Less, concentrated** urine |
| **Aldosterone** | Adrenal cortex (zona glomerulosa) | Angiotensin II, ↑ K⁺ | Less urine; **Na⁺ kept, K⁺ lost** |
| **Angiotensin II** | Blood (from angiotensin I by ACE) | Renin release | Vasoconstriction, aldosterone, thirst |
| **ANF** | Heart atria | ↑ blood volume (stretch) | **More urine, more Na⁺ lost** |
| PTH | Parathyroid | ↓ blood Ca²⁺ | ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption, ↑ phosphate excretion |

## 9. Micturition and urine

- **Micturition** — the process of releasing urine. Stretch receptors in the bladder wall send signals to the **CNS** (spinal cord) → motor messages contract the **detrusor** (smooth muscle of the bladder) and relax the **urethral sphincter** → **micturition reflex**. Voluntary control develops in childhood.
- Normal adult urine: **1–1.5 L/day**; light yellow (urochrome/urobilin); slightly **acidic, pH ~6.0**; characteristic odour; ~**25–30 g urea/day**.
- Abnormal constituents: **glucose (glycosuria)** and **ketone bodies (ketonuria)** — diabetes mellitus; proteins (albuminuria) — kidney damage; blood (haematuria); bile pigments — jaundice.

## 10. Role of other organs in excretion

| Organ | Role |
|---|---|
| **Lungs** | Remove **CO₂** (~200 mL/min at rest) and water vapour |
| **Liver** | Makes **urea**; excretes **bilirubin, biliverdin**, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins and drugs via bile |
| **Skin** | **Sweat glands** — NaCl, small amounts of urea, lactic acid (mainly for cooling); **sebaceous glands** — sterols, hydrocarbons, waxes (sebum) |
| **Saliva** | Small amounts of nitrogenous wastes |

## 11. Disorders and treatment

| Disorder | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Uraemia** | Accumulation of **urea** in blood from kidney malfunction — very harmful |
| **Renal calculi (kidney stones)** | Insoluble masses of crystallised salts (**calcium oxalate**, phosphate, uric acid) in the kidney |
| **Glomerulonephritis** | Inflammation of the **glomeruli** |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | Heavy protein loss in urine, oedema |
| **Gout** | Uric acid crystals deposit in joints |
| **Diabetes insipidus** | ADH deficiency → polyuria |

### Haemodialysis (artificial kidney)

- Blood from a convenient **artery** is drawn, mixed with an anticoagulant (**heparin**), pumped into a **dialysing unit** (artificial kidney) — a long coiled **cellophane tube** (semi-permeable) surrounded by **dialysing fluid** having **the same composition as plasma except nitrogenous wastes**.
- Wastes move from blood into the fluid by **diffusion** (concentration gradient) → purified blood is mixed with **anti-heparin** and returned via a **vein**.
- Also **peritoneal dialysis** (the peritoneum acts as the membrane).
- First practical artificial kidney: **Willem Kolff (1943)**.

### Kidney transplantation

The ultimate treatment for **acute renal failure**: a functioning kidney from a donor, preferably a **close relative** to reduce rejection by the immune system; immunosuppressants given afterwards.

## 12. Osmoregulation in animals

- **Osmoconformers** — body fluid osmolarity changes with the surroundings: most marine invertebrates, hagfish.
- **Osmoregulators** — keep internal osmolarity constant.

| Animal | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Freshwater fish** | Body hypertonic → water enters, salts lost | Drinks little; **large volumes of dilute urine**; gills **actively absorb salts** |
| **Marine bony fish** | Body hypotonic → loses water | **Drinks sea water**; little concentrated urine; gills (chloride cells) **secrete salts**; few or no glomeruli (aglomerular kidney in some) |
| **Marine cartilaginous fish** (shark) | — | Retains **urea and TMAO** → nearly isotonic to sea water; **rectal gland** secretes NaCl |
| **Marine birds and reptiles** | Salt intake | **Salt glands** (nasal / orbital) excrete NaCl |
| **Desert mammals** (kangaroo rat, camel) | Water scarcity | Long loops of Henle; metabolic water; dry faeces |
| **Protozoa** (Amoeba, Paramecium) | Water enters by osmosis | **Contractile vacuole** expels water (absent in most marine forms) |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Renin** is an **enzyme** from **JG cells**; **rennin** is a gastric enzyme. Renin is not a hormone in the strict sense.
- **Vasopressin = ADH**; it is made in the **hypothalamus** and only stored/released by the posterior pituitary.
- ADH and aldosterone both **reduce** urine; **ANF increases** urine (opposes RAAS).
- **Descending limb: water out, salt stays. Ascending limb: salt out, water stays.**
- **Urea cycle ≠ Krebs cycle**: urea cycle is in the **liver** (part mitochondria, part cytosol); urea is finally released by **arginase**.
- **Uricotelic animals need the least water**; ammonia is the most toxic and most soluble.
- **Glomerular filtrate has no proteins** (or almost none); it does contain glucose — glucose in **urine** is abnormal.
- **PCT** reabsorbs most (65–80%); people often wrongly answer the loop of Henle.
- **Efferent arteriole is narrower** than the afferent — that keeps glomerular pressure high.
- **Vasa recta** are absent or reduced in **cortical** nephrons.
- The dialysing fluid has **no nitrogenous wastes** but otherwise matches plasma — otherwise useful salts would be lost.
- Freshwater fish produce **dilute, abundant** urine; marine fish **scanty** urine.
- **Tadpole ammonotelic → frog ureotelic**.

## Quick revision

1. Ammonotelic — bony fishes; ureotelic — mammals, sharks; uricotelic — birds, reptiles, insects.
2. Flame cells — **Platyhelminthes**; nephridia — **annelids**; Malpighian tubules — **insects**; green glands — **prawn**.
3. Each kidney ~**1 million nephrons**; 10–12 cm × 5–7 cm × 2–3 cm.
4. Malpighian body = **glomerulus + Bowman's capsule**.
5. Podocytes leave **filtration slits**.
6. GFR **125 mL/min = 180 L/day**; ~**99%** reabsorbed; urine 1–1.5 L/day.
7. Ultrafiltration in the **glomerulus** (official answer).
8. **PCT** reabsorbs all glucose, amino acids and 70–80% water and salts.
9. Descending limb permeable to **water**; ascending limb to **electrolytes**.
10. Medullary gradient **300 → 1200 mOsm/L**, by **NaCl and urea**.
11. **Henle's loop** = counter-current multiplier; **vasa recta** = exchanger.
12. Urea made by **ornithine (Krebs–Henseleit) cycle**, 1932, in **liver**.
13. Last enzyme of urea cycle — **arginase**.
14. **ADH (vasopressin)** → water reabsorption in DCT and collecting duct.
15. JG cells → **renin** → angiotensin II → **aldosterone**.
16. **ACE** mainly in the **lungs**.
17. **ANF** from heart atria → ↓ BP, ↑ urine.
18. Diuresis — **increase in urine volume**; alcohol and caffeine are diuretics.
