This chapter covers NCERT class 11 "Digestion and Absorption" and adds the B.Sc. points: histology of the gut wall, the cell types of each gland, zymogen activation, GI hormones, the carriers used in absorption and the fate of bile pigments. TRE 2.0 asked **seven** questions in a row from this chapter (Q71–77) and TRE 1.0 asked about Kupffer cells, so this is a high-yield chapter.

> **In simple words:** Food is made of big molecules (starch, proteins, fats). The body can absorb only small ones (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids). Digestion is the breaking of big into small, done by **enzymes** (biological catalysts) in the mouth, stomach and small intestine. The small molecules then pass through finger-like **villi** into blood or lymph. Learn three things for each part of the gut: which cells, which secretion, and which product.

Basic class 10 version: [Human Physiology — Digestion to Excretion (Class 9–10)](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-9-10-science/biology/human-physiology-digestion-to-excretion). Enzyme chemistry: [Biomolecules and Enzymes](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/biomolecules-and-enzymes).

## 1. Parts of the digestive system

The digestive system = **alimentary canal** (the tube from mouth to anus) + **digestive glands** (salivary glands, liver, pancreas, and glands in the gut wall).

**Path of food:** mouth → buccal (oral) cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum → jejunum → ileum) → large intestine (caecum → colon → rectum) → anal canal → anus.

| Part | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Buccal cavity** | Teeth, tongue; saliva added; food becomes a **bolus** (soft ball) |
| **Pharynx** | Common passage for food and air; **epiglottis** (cartilage flap) closes the glottis during swallowing |
| **Oesophagus** | ~25 cm tube; no digestion; food pushed by **peristalsis** (waves of muscle contraction); upper third has skeletal muscle |
| **Stomach** | J-shaped; parts: **cardiac**, **fundic**, **body**, **pyloric**; opens into duodenum through the **pyloric sphincter** |
| **Duodenum** | C-shaped, ~25 cm; receives the common **hepato-pancreatic duct** guarded by the **sphincter of Oddi** |
| **Jejunum** | Middle coiled part; most digestion and absorption |
| **Ileum** | Longest part (~3.5 m); absorbs vitamin B₁₂ and bile salts; has **Peyer's patches** (lymphoid tissue) |
| **Caecum** | Blind sac with the **vermiform appendix** (vestigial in humans; hosts symbiotic bacteria in herbivores) |
| **Colon** | Ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid; absorbs **water** and salts; pouches called **haustra** |
| **Rectum → anus** | Stores faeces; anus has internal (smooth) and external (skeletal) sphincters |

Small intestine total ~**6 m** (longest part of the canal); large intestine ~1.5 m.

### Teeth

- Humans are **thecodont** (each tooth in a socket of the jaw bone), **diphyodont** (two sets in life — 20 milk/deciduous teeth, 32 permanent) and **heterodont** (four types: incisor, canine, premolar, molar).
- Adult dental formula (one half of upper / lower jaw): **I 2/2, C 1/1, PM 2/2, M 3/3 = 2123/2123 × 2 = 32**.
- Milk teeth: 2102/2102 = **20** (no premolars; no third molar).
- **Enamel** (made by ameloblasts, ectodermal) is the **hardest substance in the body**; **dentine** (odontoblasts, mesodermal) forms most of the tooth; **cement** fixes the root.
- Third molars are the **wisdom teeth** — the last to erupt (17–25 years).

### Tongue

Muscular, attached to the floor of the mouth by the **frenulum**. Its upper surface has **papillae** (small projections), many carrying **taste buds**. Circumvallate papillae (largest) lie in a V-row at the back.

## 2. Histology: the four layers of the gut wall

From the oesophagus to the rectum, the wall has the same four layers (from outside inward):

| Layer | Made of | Special features |
|---|---|---|
| **Serosa** (outermost) | Thin mesothelium (squamous epithelium) + connective tissue | Part of the visceral peritoneum; in the oesophagus it is replaced by **adventitia** |
| **Muscularis** | Smooth muscle: **outer longitudinal + inner circular** | Stomach has a third, **inner oblique** layer; **Auerbach's (myenteric) plexus** lies between the two layers and controls peristalsis |
| **Submucosa** | Loose connective tissue with blood vessels, lymph vessels and nerves | **Meissner's (submucosal) plexus**; in the duodenum it holds **Brunner's glands** |
| **Mucosa** (innermost) | Epithelium + lamina propria + muscularis mucosae | Folds called **rugae** in the stomach; **villi** in the small intestine; **goblet cells** secrete mucus |

> **Remember:** Brunner's glands are **submucosal** and found only in the **duodenum**. Crypts of Lieberkühn are **mucosal** pits between the villi, all along the small intestine (and in the colon, where they make only mucus).

## 3. Digestive glands and their cells

### Salivary glands (three pairs)

| Gland | Location | Duct |
|---|---|---|
| **Parotid** (largest) | Below and in front of the ear (cheek) | **Stensen's duct** |
| **Submandibular** (sub-maxillary) | Below the lower jaw | **Wharton's duct** |
| **Sublingual** (smallest) | Below the tongue | Ducts of Rivinus / Bartholin's duct |

Saliva: ~1–1.5 L per day, pH about **6.8**; contains water, electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻), **salivary amylase (ptyalin)**, **lysozyme** (kills bacteria) and mucus. Mumps is a viral infection of the **parotid** gland.

### Gastric glands — four cell types

| Cell | Also called | Secretes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mucous neck cells** | — | **Mucus** (with HCO₃⁻, protects the lining from acid) |
| **Chief cells** | **Peptic** or **zymogen** cells | **Pepsinogen** (inactive pepsin) and **prorennin** (in infants), small gastric lipase |
| **Parietal cells** | **Oxyntic** cells | **HCl** and **Castle's intrinsic factor** (needed to absorb vitamin B₁₂) |
| **G cells** (enteroendocrine) | — | Hormone **gastrin** (mainly in the pyloric region) |

How parietal cells make HCl (B.Sc.): inside the cell, **carbonic anhydrase** makes CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. H⁺ is pumped into the stomach lumen by the **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump)**; HCO₃⁻ leaves into blood in exchange for Cl⁻ (the "**alkaline tide**" after meals); Cl⁻ follows H⁺ into the lumen. Drugs like omeprazole block this proton pump.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q71 asked: "HCl secreting cells of the gastric gland are called — (A) zymogen cells (B) goblet cells (C) oxyntic cells." **Official answer: (C) oxyntic cells.** Zymogen (chief) cells make pepsinogen; goblet cells make mucus.

Gastric juice: about 2–3 L per day, pH **1.5–2.0** (strongly acidic). HCl kills bacteria, gives the acid pH for pepsin, and converts pepsinogen → pepsin. The food in the stomach becomes an acidic semi-liquid called **chyme**. Food stays in the stomach about **4–5 hours**.

### Liver — the largest gland

- Weight ~**1.2–1.5 kg** in an adult; lies below the diaphragm on the right; has two main lobes.
- **Hepatic lobules** are its structural and functional units; each lobule is covered by a connective tissue sheath called **Glisson's capsule**. Liver cells (**hepatocytes**) are arranged as cords around a **central vein**; between the cords run **sinusoids** (wide capillaries).
- **Kupffer cells** — macrophages lining the sinusoids; they **phagocytose** (engulf) bacteria, worn-out RBCs and debris. They belong to the reticulo-endothelial (mononuclear phagocyte) system.
- Bile is made by hepatocytes, flows through bile canaliculi → hepatic duct → stored and concentrated in the **gall bladder** → **cystic duct** joins the hepatic duct to form the **common bile duct**, which joins the pancreatic duct as the **hepato-pancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater)**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q88 asked: "Which of the following liver cells phagocytizes dead and inactive RBCs? — (A) Hepatocytes (B) Kupffer's cells (C) Sinusoids." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) Kupffer's cells.** Sinusoids are blood channels, not cells.

**Functions of the liver (one-liners):** makes bile; stores glycogen (glycogenesis) and releases glucose (glycogenolysis); makes glucose from non-carbohydrates (gluconeogenesis); deamination of amino acids and **urea synthesis (ornithine cycle)**; makes plasma proteins (albumin, fibrinogen, prothrombin) and **heparin**; stores vitamins A, D, B₁₂ and iron; detoxifies drugs and alcohol; destroys old RBCs (with the spleen); produces RBCs in the **foetus**; regenerates after injury.

### Pancreas — the mixed gland

- Lies in the loop of the duodenum. **Exocrine** part (acini, ~98–99%) makes pancreatic juice; **endocrine** part (**islets of Langerhans**, ~1–2%) makes insulin and glucagon.
- Pancreatic juice: ~1.2–1.5 L per day, pH about **7.8–8.0** (alkaline because of **HCO₃⁻**), carries **trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, procarboxypeptidase, pancreatic amylase, lipase, nucleases**.
- Duct of Wirsung (main pancreatic duct); duct of Santorini (accessory).

### Intestinal glands

- **Crypts of Lieberkühn** — tubular glands at the base of villi; make **succus entericus** (intestinal juice) and hold **Paneth cells** (make lysozyme, defensins) and stem cells that renew the lining every few days.
- **Brunner's glands** (duodenum only, submucosal) — alkaline mucus that protects the duodenum from acid chyme.
- **Goblet cells** — single-celled mucus glands scattered in the epithelium of the intestine.
- The **brush border** (microvilli) of intestinal cells carries the final enzymes (disaccharidases, dipeptidases, enterokinase).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q75 asked: "Which enzyme digests protein at pH 1·6 (acidic pH, as printed in the booklet)? — (A) Trypsin (B) Pepsin (C) Peptidase." **Official answer: (B) Pepsin.** Only pepsin works in the acid of the stomach; trypsin and peptidases work at alkaline pH ~8.

### Zymogen activation — the cascade

**Zymogens (proenzymes)** are inactive forms released so that the gland does not digest itself.

- Pepsinogen —(HCl, and then pepsin itself = **autocatalysis**)→ pepsin.
- Trypsinogen —(**enterokinase / enteropeptidase** of the brush border)→ trypsin.
- Trypsin then activates chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin and procarboxypeptidase → carboxypeptidase (and more trypsinogen).
- If trypsin is activated inside the pancreas → **acute pancreatitis**. The pancreas also makes a **trypsin inhibitor** to prevent this.

### Summary by food type

| Food | Mouth | Stomach | Small intestine | Final product absorbed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Starch → maltose | (amylase stops at acid pH) | Pancreatic amylase; maltase, lactase, sucrase | **Monosaccharides** (glucose, galactose, fructose) |
| Protein | — | Pepsin → peptones | Trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase, dipeptidases | **Amino acids** (and some di/tripeptides) |
| Fat | (lingual lipase) | Gastric lipase (little) | Bile emulsifies; pancreatic lipase | **Fatty acids, monoglycerides, glycerol** |
| Nucleic acid | — | — | Nucleases → nucleotidases → nucleosidases | Pentose sugars, nitrogenous bases, phosphate |

## 5. Gastro-intestinal (GI) hormones

| Hormone | Made by | Stimulus | Main action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | **G cells** of the pyloric stomach (and duodenum) | Peptides, stomach stretch, vagus nerve | Stimulates **HCl and pepsinogen** secretion; gastric motility |
| **Secretin** | **S cells** of the duodenum | **Acid chyme** entering duodenum | Pancreas releases **water and HCO₃⁻**; increases bile flow; inhibits gastric secretion |
| **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** (= pancreozymin) | **I cells** of duodenum/jejunum | Fats and peptides in duodenum | **Gall bladder contracts** (bile released) and pancreas releases **enzymes**; relaxes sphincter of Oddi |
| **GIP** (gastric inhibitory peptide / glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide) | **K cells** of duodenum | Glucose, fat | Inhibits gastric secretion and motility; stimulates **insulin** release |
| Motilin | Duodenum | Fasting | Gut motility between meals |
| **Ghrelin** | Stomach (fundus) | Empty stomach | **Hunger** hormone; stimulates GH release |
| Villikinin | Small intestine | — | Movement of villi (older textbook name) |
| Enterocrinin | Small intestine | — | Secretion of intestinal juice (older name) |

- **Secretin** was the **first hormone discovered** — by **W. M. Bayliss and E. H. Starling (1902)**. Starling coined the word "**hormone**" (1905).
- Secretin and CCK were older names: CCK and "pancreozymin" were found to be the same hormone.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q72 asked: "Secretin stimulates the functioning of — (A) liver (B) pancreas (C) intestinal gland." **Official answer: (B) pancreas.** Secretin also increases bile secretion from the liver, so "More than one" could be argued, but BPSC chose the main textbook action: **pancreatic bicarbonate secretion**.

### Nervous control

Digestion is controlled by the **enteric nervous system** (Auerbach's and Meissner's plexuses — "the gut's own brain") plus the autonomic system: **parasympathetic (vagus nerve) increases** secretion and motility; sympathetic decreases them. Gastric secretion has three phases: **cephalic** (sight, smell, thought — via vagus), **gastric** (food in stomach — gastrin), **intestinal** (chyme in duodenum — mostly inhibitory, by secretin, GIP, CCK).

## 6. Absorption

**Absorption** = passage of digested end-products through the intestinal lining into blood or lymph. It uses **simple diffusion, facilitated transport, active transport and passive water movement (osmosis)**.

- **Folds of Kerckring (plicae circulares)** → **villi** (finger-like, ~1 mm) → **microvilli** (brush border on each cell). Together they raise the surface area to ~200–250 m² (older books quote ~600-fold increase).
- Each villus has a network of **blood capillaries** and a central lymph vessel, the **lacteal**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q73 asked: "The functional unit involved in absorption of digested food is — (A) Brunner's gland (B) crypts of Lieberkühn (C) villus." **Official answer: (C) villus.** Brunner's glands and crypts are secretory, not absorptive.

### How each nutrient is absorbed

| Nutrient | Mechanism (B.Sc. detail) | Goes into |
|---|---|---|
| **Glucose, galactose** | **Secondary active transport** with Na⁺ through **SGLT-1** (Na⁺–glucose co-transporter); Na⁺ gradient kept by the Na⁺/K⁺ pump; exit to blood by **GLUT-2** | Blood capillaries → hepatic portal vein |
| **Fructose** | **Facilitated diffusion** through **GLUT-5** | Blood |
| **Amino acids** | Na⁺-coupled active transport; di/tripeptides by H⁺-coupled PepT1 | Blood |
| **Fatty acids, monoglycerides** | Bile salts form **micelles** → contents diffuse into cells → **re-synthesised into triglycerides** in smooth ER → coated with protein = **chylomicrons** → exocytosed | **Lacteals** (lymph) → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein |
| Short/medium-chain fatty acids, glycerol | Simple diffusion | Blood |
| **Water** | Osmosis (follows absorbed solutes); ~80% in small intestine | Blood |
| Vitamin B₁₂ | Bound to **intrinsic factor**, taken up in the **ileum** | Blood |
| Iron, calcium | Active; mainly **duodenum** | Blood |
| Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K | With micelles and chylomicrons | Lymph |

- Absorption in the stomach: **water, simple sugars, alcohol and some drugs** (aspirin).
- Absorption in the large intestine: **water, some minerals and drugs**; bacteria there make **vitamin K** and some B vitamins.
- **Assimilation** — use of absorbed substances by cells; **egestion (defaecation)** — removal of faeces (voluntary, via the anal sphincter).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q76 asked: "Carbohydrates are absorbed in the form of — (A) polysaccharides (B) disaccharides (C) monosaccharides." **Official answer: (C) monosaccharides.**

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q77 asked: "Lacteals are connected with — (A) absorption of fats (B) production of milk (C) secretion of lactic acid." **Official answer: (A) absorption of fats.** The name comes from Latin *lac* = milk, because lymph full of chylomicrons looks milky (**chyle**) — that is the trap.

## 7. Bile: salts and pigments

**Bile** = water + **bile salts** + **bile pigments** + cholesterol + phospholipids + HCO₃⁻. No enzymes. About 0.5–1 L per day.

- **Bile salts** (sodium glycocholate and sodium taurocholate) are made from **cholesterol**. They **emulsify** fat (lower surface tension, break big drops into tiny ones), form micelles, activate lipase. About 95% are reabsorbed in the ileum and return to the liver — the **enterohepatic circulation**.
- **Bile pigments** are waste products of **haemoglobin** breakdown:

<div class="formula">

Old RBCs (spleen, Kupffer cells) → haemoglobin → globin (to amino acids) + haem → iron saved (as ferritin) + **biliverdin (green)** —(biliverdin reductase)→ **bilirubin (yellow-orange)** → carried in blood on **albumin** → liver **conjugates** it with glucuronic acid → secreted in bile → in gut, bacteria convert it to **urobilinogen** → **stercobilin** (brown colour of **faeces**) and some reabsorbed → **urobilin** (yellow colour of **urine**).

</div>

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q74 asked: "Green colour of bile is due to — (A) biliverdin (B) bilirubin (C) bile salts." **Official answer: (A) biliverdin.** Note: human bile is mostly golden-yellow because bilirubin is the main pigment; bile turns green when bilirubin is oxidised back to biliverdin (and bird bile is green). For "green" pick **biliverdin**; for "yellow/jaundice" pick **bilirubin**.

**Jaundice** = bilirubin in blood above ~2 mg/dL (normal 0.3–1.2 mg/dL); skin and eye whites turn yellow. Types: **pre-hepatic (haemolytic)** — too much RBC breakdown; **hepatic** — liver damage (hepatitis); **post-hepatic (obstructive)** — bile duct blocked (gallstones); newborn (physiological) jaundice is common and treated with blue-light **phototherapy**.

## 8. Movements and control of the gut

- **Mastication** (chewing) and **deglutition** (swallowing — voluntary in the mouth, reflex after the pharynx; centre in the medulla).
- **Peristalsis** — wave of circular-muscle contraction behind the food and relaxation in front.
- **Segmentation** — mixing contractions in the small intestine.
- **Vomiting** — reverse expulsion of stomach contents; reflex controlled by the vomit centre in the **medulla**.
- Hunger and satiety centres are in the **hypothalamus** (lateral = feeding centre; ventromedial = satiety centre).

## 9. Nutrition and calorific values

| Nutrient | Gross (bomb calorimeter) value | Physiological value |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 4.1 kcal/g | **4.0 kcal/g** |
| Protein | 5.65 kcal/g | **4.0 kcal/g** (nitrogen not burnt) |
| Fat | 9.45 kcal/g | **9.0 kcal/g** |

(NCERT: 1 kcal ≈ 4.18 kJ. A balanced adult diet ~2000–2500 kcal/day; heavy workers more.)

- **Essential amino acids** (body cannot make them): 9 in adults — histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine (arginine is semi-essential in children). Memory: "**PVT TIM HaLL**".
- **Essential fatty acids**: linoleic and α-linolenic acid (arachidonic acid can be made from linoleic acid).
- **BMR** (basal metabolic rate) — energy used at complete rest; regulated mainly by **thyroid hormones**.

## 10. Disorders of the digestive system

- **Indigestion** (dyspepsia) — inadequate enzymes, anxiety, food poisoning, over-eating.
- **Constipation** — irregular bowel movement; faeces retained.
- **Diarrhoea** — frequent watery stools, less absorption; treat with **ORS** (oral rehydration solution).
- **Vomiting** — reflex controlled by the medulla.
- **Peptic ulcer** — sore in stomach/duodenum; main cause bacterium ***Helicobacter pylori*** (Marshall and Warren, Nobel 2005) plus NSAIDs, excess acid.
- **Gallstones (cholelithiasis)** — mostly cholesterol stones.
- **Lactose intolerance** — low **lactase** in adults; milk causes gas and diarrhoea.
- **Liver cirrhosis** — fibrosis, often from alcohol or hepatitis B/C.
- **Appendicitis** — inflamed appendix.

## Worked numbers

1. **Teeth count.** Dental formula 2123/2123: per half jaw upper = 8, lower = 8 → one side = 16 → both sides = **32**. Milk teeth 2102/2102 → 5 × 4 = **20**.
2. **Daily energy.** A diet with 300 g carbohydrate, 70 g protein, 60 g fat: 300 × 4 + 70 × 4 + 60 × 9 = 1200 + 280 + 540 = **2020 kcal**.
3. **Fluid handled.** Saliva 1.5 L + gastric 2 L + bile 0.7 L + pancreatic 1.3 L + intestinal 1.5 L ≈ 7 L of secretions + ~2 L intake ≈ 9 L reach the gut each day; only ~0.1–0.2 L leaves in faeces — about **98% is reabsorbed**.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Rennin** (gastric, milk-curdling, infants) ≠ **renin** (kidney JG cells, raises BP). TRE has used this spelling trap.
- **Oxyntic = parietal = HCl + intrinsic factor**; **chief = peptic = zymogen = pepsinogen**. Do not swap them.
- **Bile has no enzyme**, but it is a digestive juice. Bile is made in the **liver**, only stored in the gall bladder.
- **Enterokinase is an enzyme**, not a hormone, even though its name sounds like a hormone. It activates **trypsinogen**, not pepsinogen.
- **Brunner's glands** = duodenum + submucosa; **crypts of Lieberkühn** = mucosa, whole intestine.
- **Lacteals absorb fat**, not milk; the milky lymph is called chyle.
- **Fats go to lymph first** (lacteals); sugars and amino acids go to blood (hepatic portal vein). So absorbed fat bypasses the liver at first.
- **Fructose** is absorbed by facilitated diffusion (no energy); glucose and galactose by Na⁺-linked secondary active transport.
- **Largest gland — liver**; largest salivary gland — parotid; **largest endocrine gland — thyroid**.
- **Secretin acts on the pancreas for bicarbonate**, **CCK for enzymes and the gall bladder**.
- **Stomach absorbs alcohol and water**, not proteins.
- Human bile is yellow-brown (bilirubin) — but "green colour" answer is **biliverdin**.
- **Vitamin B₁₂ is absorbed in the ileum**, iron in the duodenum.
- Kupffer cells are in the **liver**; similar macrophages: microglia (brain), alveolar macrophages (lung), osteoclasts (bone, same lineage), Langerhans cells (skin).

## Quick revision

1. Human dentition — thecodont, diphyodont, heterodont; formula **2123/2123**.
2. Hardest body substance — **enamel** (ectodermal).
3. Gut wall layers (out → in) — **serosa, muscularis, submucosa, mucosa**.
4. Stomach has an extra **oblique muscle** layer.
5. Largest salivary gland — **parotid** (Stensen's duct); mumps affects it.
6. Saliva pH ~**6.8**; salivary amylase → **maltose**.
7. **Oxyntic (parietal) cells** → HCl + intrinsic factor.
8. **Chief (zymogen) cells** → pepsinogen + prorennin.
9. Pepsin works at pH **1.5–2**; products are **proteoses and peptones**.
10. **Enterokinase** activates trypsinogen → trypsin.
11. **Bile salts** emulsify fat; bile has **no enzyme**.
12. Green bile pigment — **biliverdin**; yellow — **bilirubin**; faeces brown — **stercobilin**; urine yellow — **urobilin(ogen)**.
13. **Kupffer cells** — liver macrophages that destroy old RBCs.
14. **Brunner's glands** — duodenum, submucosa, alkaline mucus.
15. **Crypts of Lieberkühn** — intestinal juice; Paneth cells.
16. First hormone discovered — **secretin** (Bayliss and Starling, 1902).
17. **CCK** — gall bladder contraction + pancreatic enzymes.
18. **Gastrin** — from G cells, stimulates HCl.
19. Functional unit of absorption — **villus**.
20. Carbohydrates absorbed as **monosaccharides**; glucose via **SGLT-1** with Na⁺.
21. Fats → micelles → **chylomicrons** → **lacteals**.
22. Fat — **9 kcal/g**; carbohydrate and protein — **4 kcal/g**.
23. **Kwashiorkor** — protein lack (oedema); **marasmus** — protein + calorie lack.
24. **Peptic ulcer** — *Helicobacter pylori*.
25. Hunger/satiety centres — **hypothalamus**; vomiting centre — **medulla**.

## Practice with answers

1. Which cells secrete pepsinogen? — **Chief (peptic/zymogen) cells** (of the gastric glands).
2. What activates pepsinogen? — **HCl** (and then pepsin itself — autocatalysis).
3. Which enzyme curdles milk in infants? — **Rennin** (casein → paracasein, with Ca²⁺).
4. Which duct carries saliva from the parotid gland? — **Stensen's duct**.
5. Where are Brunner's glands located? — **Submucosa of the duodenum**.
6. Which hormone makes the gall bladder contract? — **Cholecystokinin (CCK)**.
7. Which hormone is called the hunger hormone? — **Ghrelin** (from the stomach).
8. Which enzyme converts sucrose to glucose and fructose? — **Sucrase (invertase)**.
9. In which form are fats transported from intestinal cells into lacteals? — **Chylomicrons**.
10. Which factor is needed for absorption of vitamin B₁₂? — **Castle's intrinsic factor** (parietal cells).
11. Which substance gives faeces its brown colour? — **Stercobilin**.
12. Which plexus in the gut wall controls peristalsis? — **Auerbach's (myenteric) plexus**.
13. Which pancreatic enzyme digests emulsified fats? — **Steapsin (pancreatic lipase)**.
14. Where in the gut are Peyer's patches found? — **Ileum** (lymphoid tissue).
15. A child of 3 years has oedema and a swollen belly after weaning. Disorder? — **Kwashiorkor**.
16. Deficiency of which vitamin causes pellagra? — **Niacin (B₃)**.
17. Calorie value of 50 g fat? — **450 kcal** (50 × 9).
18. Which enzyme in parietal cells forms H⁺ and HCO₃⁻? — **Carbonic anhydrase**.
19. How is fructose absorbed? — **Facilitated diffusion via GLUT-5** (no ATP).
20. Name the liver's structural and functional unit. — **Hepatic lobule** (covered by Glisson's capsule).
