This chapter covers NCERT class 11 "Chemical Coordination and Integration" and climbs to B.Sc. points: hormone chemistry, receptor types, second messengers, axis-wise feedback, zones of the adrenal cortex, islet cell types and the full disorder list. TRE 2.0 asked **seven** endocrine questions in a row (Q98–104), and TRE 1.0 asked Cushing's syndrome (Q91), FSH and seminiferous tubules (Q94) and pubertal hormones (Q98). This is the most-asked physiology topic.

> **In simple words:** Hormones are chemical messengers made in small amounts by **endocrine (ductless) glands** and carried by blood to distant **target cells**, which have matching **receptors**. Nerves work fast and briefly; hormones work slowly but for longer. The **hypothalamus** controls the **pituitary**, and the pituitary controls many other glands. When a hormone level rises too high, it switches off its own controller — this is **negative feedback**.

## 1. Hormones — definitions and history

- **Endocrine glands** — **ductless**; secretions (hormones) go directly into blood. **Exocrine glands** — have ducts (salivary, sweat). **Heterocrine (mixed) glands** — both: **pancreas** (and gonads, which release gametes plus hormones).
- NCERT's modern definition: **hormones are non-nutrient chemicals which act as intercellular messengers and are produced in trace amounts.** This includes hormones made by tissues that are not organised glands (gut, heart, kidney).
- Invertebrates have simple endocrine systems with few hormones (e.g. **ecdysone** — moulting in insects; **juvenile hormone**).

## 2. Chemical nature of hormones

NCERT divides hormones into four groups:

<div class="formula">

| Group | Examples | Solubility / receptor |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) Peptide, polypeptide, protein hormones** | **Insulin, glucagon**, all **pituitary** hormones (GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, MSH, oxytocin, vasopressin), **hypothalamic** hormones, PTH, calcitonin, thymosins, ANF, gut hormones | Water-soluble → **membrane receptors** |
| **(ii) Steroids** (from **cholesterol**) | **Cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, oestradiol, progesterone**; calcitriol (vitamin D hormone); ecdysone | Lipid-soluble → **intracellular (nuclear/cytoplasmic) receptors** |
| **(iii) Iodothyronines** | **Thyroid hormones — T₄ (thyroxine) and T₃** | Enter the cell → **nuclear receptors** |
| **(iv) Amino-acid derivatives (amines)** | **Adrenaline (epinephrine), noradrenaline** (from tyrosine), **melatonin** (from tryptophan) | Catecholamines → **membrane receptors** |

</div>

- FSH, LH, TSH and hCG are **glycoproteins**.
- Thyroid hormones are made from tyrosine too, so some books group them with amino-acid derivatives — but they act like steroids (nuclear receptors).

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q98 asked: "By chemical nature, hormones may be — (A) steroids (B) peptides (C) amino acid derivatives." **Official answer: (D) More than one of the above.** All three are correct categories.

## 3. Mechanism of hormone action

Hormones act by binding **specific receptors** present only in target tissues. The hormone–receptor complex causes biochemical changes in the target.

### (a) Membrane-bound receptors — second messengers (peptide hormones and catecholamines)

1. Hormone (**first messenger**) binds a receptor on the cell surface; it **does not enter** the cell.
2. Receptor activates a **G-protein**, which activates **adenylate cyclase**.
3. ATP → **cyclic AMP (cAMP)** — the **second messenger**.
4. cAMP activates **protein kinase A** → phosphorylates enzymes → physiological response (e.g. glycogenolysis by adrenaline and glucagon in liver).
5. **Phosphodiesterase** breaks cAMP down, ending the signal.

- Other second messengers: **IP₃ (inositol trisphosphate)**, **DAG**, **Ca²⁺** (e.g. oxytocin, vasopressin V1 receptors, GnRH), cGMP (ANF, nitric oxide).
- **Insulin** works through a **receptor tyrosine kinase** (and GLUT-4 insertion), not cAMP.
- Advantage: **amplification** — one hormone molecule → thousands of cAMP → millions of product molecules.

### (b) Intracellular receptors (steroids and iodothyronines)

1. Lipid-soluble hormone **diffuses through the plasma membrane**.
2. Binds a receptor in the **cytoplasm or nucleus** → **hormone–receptor complex**.
3. Complex binds specific DNA sequences (hormone response elements) → **regulates gene expression / chromosome function** → new mRNA → new proteins → response.
4. Slower in onset, longer lasting.

| Feature | Peptide/amine hormones | Steroid/thyroid hormones |
|---|---|---|
| Receptor | Cell membrane | Cytoplasm / nucleus |
| Second messenger | Yes (cAMP, IP₃, Ca²⁺) | No |
| Carried in blood | Mostly free | Bound to carrier proteins |
| Action | Fast, modifies existing enzymes | Slow, new protein synthesis |
| Stored? | Stored in vesicles | Steroids made on demand (not stored); thyroid stored as thyroglobulin |

- **Negative feedback** (most common): e.g. high thyroxine → ↓ TRH and ↓ TSH → ↓ thyroxine. High blood glucose → insulin → glucose falls → insulin release stops.
- **Positive feedback** (rare): **oxytocin during childbirth** (contractions → more oxytocin → stronger contractions); **LH surge** before ovulation (rising oestrogen → LH surge).
- Main axes: **hypothalamo–pituitary–thyroid (HPT)**, **–adrenal (HPA)**, **–gonadal (HPG)** axes.

## 5. Hypothalamus

- Basal part of the diencephalon (forebrain); contains groups of **neurosecretory cells** called **nuclei** that make hormones.
- **Releasing hormones** (stimulate pituitary) and **inhibiting hormones** (stop pituitary secretion). They reach the anterior pituitary through the **hypophyseal portal system**.
- **Posterior pituitary is under direct neural regulation** of the hypothalamus — axons run down the pituitary stalk.

| Hypothalamic hormone | Effect on anterior pituitary |
|---|---|
| **GnRH** (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) | ↑ FSH and LH |
| **TRH** (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) | ↑ TSH (and prolactin) |
| **CRH** (corticotropin-releasing hormone) | ↑ ACTH |
| **GHRH** (growth hormone-releasing hormone) | ↑ GH |
| **Somatostatin (GHIH)** | ↓ GH (and TSH) |
| **PIH = dopamine** | ↓ Prolactin |
| (Made in hypothalamus, stored in posterior pituitary) | **Oxytocin, vasopressin (ADH)** — from supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei |

## 6. Pituitary gland (hypophysis)

- Located in a bony cavity of the sphenoid called the **sella turcica**; attached to the hypothalamus by a stalk (infundibulum). Pea-sized (~0.5–1 g). Once called the "master gland" — but it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus.
- Two parts: **adenohypophysis** (pars distalis + pars intermedia; from the roof of the mouth — **Rathke's pouch**, ectoderm) and **neurohypophysis** (pars nervosa; from the brain floor). In humans the pars intermedia is almost merged with the pars distalis.

<div class="formula">

| Part | Hormone | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| **Pars distalis** (anterior pituitary) | **Growth hormone (GH, somatotropin)** | Growth of body (bones, muscles); protein synthesis, lipolysis; acts via IGF-1 (somatomedins) from liver |
| Pars distalis | **Prolactin (PRL)** | Growth of **mammary glands** and **milk formation** |
| Pars distalis | **TSH** (thyroid-stimulating hormone, thyrotropin) | Synthesis and secretion of **thyroid hormones** |
| Pars distalis | **ACTH** (adrenocorticotrophic hormone) | Synthesis and secretion of **glucocorticoids** from the adrenal cortex |
| Pars distalis | **LH** (luteinising hormone) — in males also called **ICSH** | **Males**: androgen (testosterone) synthesis by **Leydig cells**. **Females**: induces **ovulation** of fully mature (Graafian) follicles and maintains the **corpus luteum** |
| Pars distalis | **FSH** (follicle-stimulating hormone) | **Males**: with androgens regulates **spermatogenesis** (acts on **Sertoli cells** of seminiferous tubules). **Females**: growth and development of **ovarian follicles** |
| **Pars intermedia** | **MSH** (melanocyte-stimulating hormone) | Acts on **melanocytes** — skin pigmentation |
| **Pars nervosa** (posterior pituitary) — stores and releases | **Oxytocin** | Contraction of **smooth muscles**: vigorous uterine contraction at **childbirth**, **milk ejection** from mammary glands |
| Pars nervosa | **Vasopressin (ADH)** | **Water and electrolyte reabsorption** by DCT and collecting duct → ↓ diuresis; vasoconstriction |

</div>

- **FSH and LH** together are the **gonadotropins**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q103 asked: "The gonadotropic hormone is secreted by — (A) posterior thyroid (B) adrenal cortex (C) anterior pituitary." **Official answer: (C) anterior pituitary.**

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q94 asked: "Which of the following hormones from adenohypophysis controls the function of the seminiferous tubules? — (A) LH (B) FSH (C) Prolactin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B) FSH.** FSH acts on **Sertoli cells** inside the seminiferous tubules; LH acts on Leydig cells **between** the tubules.

> **PYQ (related, reproduction):** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q109: "Spermatogenesis and sperm differentiation occur under the control of — (A) FSH (B) LH (C) parahormone." **Official answer: (D) More than one** (FSH and LH, via testosterone). Q110: "Ovulation occurs under the influence of — (A) LH (B) FSH (C) oestrogen." **Official answer: (A) LH** (the mid-cycle LH surge).

### Pituitary disorders

| Disorder | Cause | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Gigantism** | **Excess GH in childhood** (before epiphyses close) | Abnormal height |
| **Acromegaly** | **Excess GH in adults** (especially middle age) | Severe disfigurement, especially of the **face** (enlarged jaw, nose, hands, feet); hard to diagnose early; can shorten life |
| **Pituitary dwarfism** | **Low GH in childhood** | Stunted growth but **normal intelligence** and proportions |
| **Diabetes insipidus** | **Low ADH** (impaired synthesis/release) | Excessive dilute urine (diuresis), dehydration, thirst |
| Simmond's disease | Destruction of anterior pituitary (adults) | Premature ageing, wasting |
| Hyperprolactinaemia | Excess PRL | Infertility, galactorrhoea |

## 7. Pineal gland

- On the **dorsal side of the forebrain**; part of the epithalamus.
- Secretes **melatonin** (amine, from tryptophan via serotonin) — regulates the **24-hour (diurnal / circadian) rhythm**: sleep–wake cycle, body temperature; also influences metabolism, pigmentation, the menstrual cycle and defence capability. Secreted more in **darkness**.
- Calcifies with age ("brain sand"). It is called the "third eye" in some reptiles (*Sphenodon*).

## 8. Thyroid gland

- Two lobes on either side of the **trachea**, joined by a thin flap of connective tissue, the **isthmus**. **Largest endocrine gland** (~20–25 g).
- Made of **follicles** (lined by **follicular cells**, filled with colloid **thyroglobulin**) and stromal tissues.
- **Follicular cells** → **tetraiodothyronine (T₄, thyroxine)** and **triiodothyronine (T₃)**. T₃ is ~4× more active; most T₄ is converted to T₃ in tissues. **Iodine** is essential for their normal rate of synthesis.
- **Parafollicular (C) cells** → **thyrocalcitonin (TCT, calcitonin)** — a peptide that **lowers blood Ca²⁺** (deposits Ca in bone).

**Actions of thyroid hormones:** regulate **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**; support RBC formation; control metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats; maintain water and electrolyte balance; needed for normal growth and development of the brain and body; **metamorphosis in frog tadpoles** (thyroxine — tadpoles fail to metamorphose without it).

| Disorder | Cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple (endemic) goitre** | **Iodine deficiency** → hypothyroidism → TSH ↑ → gland enlarges | Swollen neck; common in hilly areas (Himalayan belt); prevented by **iodised salt** |
| **Cretinism** | **Hypothyroidism in children / during pregnancy** | **Stunted growth**, **mental retardation**, low IQ, abnormal skin, **deaf-mutism**; pot belly, protruding tongue |
| **Myxoedema** | **Hypothyroidism in adults** | Puffy face and skin (mucoid oedema), low BMR, lethargy, weight gain, cold intolerance; in women irregular menstrual cycles |
| **Hashimoto's thyroiditis** | Autoimmune destruction of thyroid | Hypothyroidism |
| **Graves' disease (exophthalmic goitre)** | **Hyperthyroidism**; **autoimmune** — antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor | Enlarged thyroid, **protruding eyeballs (exophthalmos)**, ↑ BMR, **weight loss**, nervousness, tremors, heat intolerance, fast heart rate |
| Thyroid cancer / nodules | — | Can cause hyperthyroidism |

## 9. Parathyroid glands

- **Four** small glands on the **back side of the thyroid**, one pair in each lobe.
- Secrete **parathyroid hormone (PTH, parathormone)** — a **peptide**; secretion regulated by circulating **Ca²⁺** levels (not by the pituitary).
- PTH **raises blood Ca²⁺** (**hypercalcaemic** hormone): (1) stimulates **bone resorption** (dissolution/demineralisation by osteoclasts); (2) stimulates **Ca²⁺ reabsorption by renal tubules**; (3) increases **Ca²⁺ absorption from digested food** (by activating vitamin D → calcitriol in the kidney). Also lowers blood phosphate.
- **PTH and TCT are antagonists** — together with vitamin D they keep Ca²⁺ balance.
- **Hypoparathyroidism** → low blood Ca²⁺ → **tetany** (painful muscle spasms, carpopedal spasm, laryngeal spasm). Accidental removal during thyroid surgery is a classic cause.
- **Hyperparathyroidism** → high Ca²⁺, weak brittle bones (osteitis fibrosa), **kidney stones**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q102 asked: "The gland responsible for Ca metabolism is — (A) thymus (B) thyroid (C) parathyroid." **Official answer: (C) parathyroid.** The thyroid's calcitonin also lowers Ca²⁺, so "More than one" could be argued — but PTH is the **main** regulator; follow the key.

## 10. Thymus

- Lobular gland between the **lungs behind the sternum**, on the ventral side of the **aorta**.
- Secretes peptide hormones **thymosins** — major role in **differentiation of T-lymphocytes** (cell-mediated immunity); also promote production of **antibodies** (humoral immunity).
- Large in children; **degenerates in old age** → lower thymosin → weaker immune responses in the elderly.

## 11. Adrenal glands

One pair, one at the **anterior part (top) of each kidney**. Two tissues of different origin: central **adrenal medulla** (ectoderm — neural crest) and outer **adrenal cortex** (mesoderm).

### Adrenal medulla — "emergency hormones"

- Secretes **adrenaline (epinephrine)** and **noradrenaline (norepinephrine)** — together the **catecholamines** (amines from tyrosine).
- Released rapidly in response to any stress or emergency — "**fight, flight or fright**" (the **3F** hormones).
- Effects: ↑ alertness, **pupil dilation**, **piloerection** (hair raising), sweating, ↑ **heart rate and force of contraction**, ↑ **breathing rate**, **glycogenolysis** → ↑ blood glucose, lipolysis, proteolysis.
- Tumour of the medulla — **phaeochromocytoma** (severe hypertension).

### Adrenal cortex — three zones (outside → inside)

| Zone | Hormone group | Main hormone | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zona glomerulosa** (outer) | **Mineralocorticoids** | **Aldosterone** | **Na⁺ and water reabsorption** from renal tubules, **K⁺ and phosphate excretion** → electrolyte balance, body fluid volume, osmotic pressure and blood pressure |
| **Zona fasciculata** (middle; thickest) | **Glucocorticoids** | **Cortisol** | **Gluconeogenesis**, lipolysis, proteolysis; inhibit cellular uptake and use of amino acids; maintain cardiovascular system and kidney function; **anti-inflammatory**, **suppress the immune response**; stimulate RBC production |
| **Zona reticularis** (inner) | **Sex corticoids (androgens)** | DHEA, small androgens | Growth of **axial, pubic and facial hair** during puberty |

> **Memory trick:** "**GFR → Salt, Sugar, Sex**" — Glomerulosa = salt (aldosterone), Fasciculata = sugar (cortisol), Reticularis = sex (androgens). Deeper you go, the sweeter (the better) it gets.

| Disorder | Cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Addison's disease** | **Under-production of adrenal cortex hormones** (often autoimmune; TB earlier) | Altered carbohydrate metabolism, **acute weakness and fatigue**, low BP, weight loss, **bronze (hyper)pigmentation of skin** (ACTH/MSH high), salt craving |
| **Cushing's syndrome** | **Excess cortisol** (adrenal tumour, pituitary ACTH tumour = Cushing's disease, or steroid drugs) | **Moon face**, **buffalo hump**, central (trunk) obesity with thin limbs, purple striae, **hyperglycaemia**, hypertension, osteoporosis, weak immunity |
| **Conn's syndrome** (primary aldosteronism) | Excess **aldosterone** | Hypertension, low K⁺ (hypokalaemia), muscle weakness |
| **Adrenogenital syndrome** / congenital adrenal hyperplasia | Excess adrenal androgens | Masculinisation (virilism) in females, precocious puberty in boys |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q91 asked: "Cushing's syndrome occurs due to — (A) maximum secretion of thyroxin (B) no secretion of growth hormone (C) maximum secretion of cortisol." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) maximum secretion of cortisol.**

## 12. Pancreas — endocrine part

- **Composite (heterocrine) gland** — both exocrine (digestive enzymes) and endocrine.
- Endocrine part = **islets of Langerhans** (Paul Langerhans, 1869): about **1–2 million** islets, only **1–2%** of pancreatic tissue.

| Cell | Share (approx.) | Hormone | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **α (alpha) cells** | ~20–25% | **Glucagon** (peptide, 29 amino acids) | **Hyperglycaemic** — glycogenolysis and **gluconeogenesis** in liver; ↓ cellular glucose uptake |
| **β (beta) cells** | ~60–70% (most numerous) | **Insulin** (peptide, 51 amino acids) | **Hypoglycaemic** — ↑ glucose uptake by **hepatocytes and adipocytes** (and muscle via GLUT-4); **glycogenesis**; fat and protein synthesis |
| **δ (delta) cells** | ~5–10% | **Somatostatin** | Inhibits insulin and glucagon release; slows digestion |
| **PP (F) cells** | few | **Pancreatic polypeptide** | Inhibits pancreatic exocrine secretion |

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q99 asked: "Which type of cells in the islets of Langerhans secrete glucagon? — (A) α-cells (B) β-cells (C) γ-cells." **Official answer: (A) α-cells.**

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q101 asked: "The gland which is both exocrine and endocrine is — (A) adrenal (B) pituitary (C) pancreas." **Official answer: (C) pancreas.**

### Diabetes mellitus

- Prolonged **hyperglycaemia** from **lack of insulin or insulin resistance** → **glycosuria** (glucose in urine, above the renal threshold ~180 mg/dL), **ketone bodies** in blood and urine (fat breakdown) → ketoacidosis; also polyuria, polydipsia (thirst), polyphagia (hunger).
- **Type 1** (insulin-dependent, juvenile) — **autoimmune destruction of β cells**; needs insulin injections.
- **Type 2** (non-insulin-dependent, adult-onset) — **insulin resistance**; ~90% of cases; linked to obesity and inactivity.
- Treated with **insulin therapy** (Type 1), diet, exercise and oral drugs (Type 2). Human insulin is now made by **recombinant DNA technology** (A and B chains made separately in *E. coli* and joined by disulphide bonds).
- **Hypoglycaemia** — low blood sugar (excess insulin, insulinoma) → sweating, confusion, coma.

| Blood glucose (mg/dL) | Fasting value (WHO/ADA criteria) |
|---|---|
| Normal | 70–99 |
| Pre-diabetes (impaired fasting glucose) | 100–125 |
| Diabetes | **≥ 126** (or ≥ 200 two hours after glucose; HbA1c ≥ 6.5%) |

## 13. Gonads

### Testis

- Pair in the **scrotal sac** (outside the abdomen). Each has **seminiferous tubules** and stromal/interstitial tissue.
- **Leydig cells (interstitial cells)** between the tubules secrete **androgens**, mainly **testosterone** (steroid).
- **Testosterone**: development, maturation and functions of the male **accessory sex organs** (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra); **male secondary sex characters** — muscular growth, facial and axillary hair, **aggressiveness**, **low-pitched voice**; stimulates **spermatogenesis**; male sexual behaviour (**libido**); **anabolic** (synthetic) effects on protein and carbohydrate metabolism.
- **Sertoli cells** secrete **inhibin** (↓ FSH) and androgen-binding protein.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q104 asked: "Secondary sexual characters in male develop under the influence of — (A) oestrogen (B) testosterone (C) FSH." **Official answer: (B) testosterone.**

### Ovary

- Pair in the abdomen; each ovary has ovarian **follicles** and stromal tissue.
- **Oestrogen** (mainly oestradiol; steroid) — synthesised and secreted mainly by **growing ovarian follicles**: stimulates growth and activities of **female secondary sex organs**, development of growing follicles, **female secondary sex characters** (e.g. high-pitched voice), **mammary gland development**, female sexual behaviour.
- After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**, which secretes mainly **progesterone** — **supports pregnancy** (maintains the endometrium); acts on mammary glands — stimulates formation of **alveoli** (sac-like structures storing milk) and **milk secretion**.
- **Relaxin** (corpus luteum, placenta — softens pubic symphysis and cervix near birth) and **inhibin**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q100 asked: "Oestrogen is produced by — (A) pituitary (B) adrenals (C) ovaries." **Official answer: (C) ovaries.** (The placenta also makes oestrogen in pregnancy, and the adrenal cortex makes small amounts of sex steroids — but the textbook source is the ovary.)

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q98 asked: "The pubertal changes in secondary sex characteristics result from increasing levels of which of the following hormones? — (A) Gonadal steroids (B) Growth hormone (C) Prolactin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) Gonadal steroids** (testosterone in boys, oestrogen in girls), driven by rising GnRH → FSH/LH.

## 14. Hormones of heart, kidney, gut and placenta

| Source | Hormone | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Atrial wall of heart** | **Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF)** (peptide) | When BP rises — **vasodilation**, ↓ BP; natriuresis |
| **Kidney** (JG cells per NCERT; peritubular interstitial cells per newer texts) | **Erythropoietin** (glycoprotein) | Stimulates **erythropoiesis** (RBC formation) |
| Kidney | Calcitriol (active vitamin D₃) | ↑ Ca²⁺ absorption from gut |
| Kidney (JG cells) | Renin (enzyme) | Starts RAAS |
| **Gastro-intestinal tract** | **Gastrin** | Gastric glands → HCl and pepsinogen |
| GI tract | **Secretin** | Exocrine pancreas → water and **bicarbonate** |
| GI tract | **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** | Pancreatic enzymes + **bile** (gall bladder) |
| GI tract | **GIP** (gastric inhibitory peptide) | Inhibits gastric secretion and motility |
| **Placenta** | **hCG**, **hPL** (human placental lactogen), oestrogens, progestogens, relaxin | Maintain pregnancy; hCG is detected in **pregnancy tests** |
| **Adipose tissue** | **Leptin** | Satiety (reduces appetite) |
| Various tissues | **Growth factors** (e.g. EGF, NGF) | Normal growth, repair and regeneration |

## 15. Summary — which hormone, which disorder

| Hormone | Hypo- (deficiency) | Hyper- (excess) |
|---|---|---|
| GH | Dwarfism | Gigantism (child), **acromegaly** (adult) |
| ADH | **Diabetes insipidus** | SIADH (water retention, low Na⁺) |
| Thyroxine | Cretinism (child), **myxoedema** (adult), goitre | **Graves' disease** (exophthalmic goitre) |
| PTH | **Tetany** | Osteoporosis-like bone loss, kidney stones |
| Cortisol | **Addison's disease** (with aldosterone) | **Cushing's syndrome** |

| Insulin | **Diabetes mellitus** | Hypoglycaemia (insulin shock) |
| Testosterone | Eunuchoidism, delayed puberty | Precocious puberty |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Oxytocin and vasopressin are made in the hypothalamus**, only stored and released by the posterior pituitary.
- **Pituitary is not the true "master"** — the hypothalamus controls it.
- **Largest endocrine gland — thyroid**; **largest gland overall — liver**; smallest endocrine — pineal.
- **Calcitonin is from the thyroid's C cells**, not the parathyroid; it **lowers** Ca²⁺. PTH **raises** Ca²⁺.
- **Tetany = low Ca²⁺ (hypoparathyroidism)** — do not confuse with tetanus (bacterial disease) or tetanus (sustained muscle contraction).
- **Graves' disease = hyperthyroid (autoimmune)**; **Hashimoto's = hypothyroid (autoimmune)**; **simple goitre = iodine deficiency**.
- **Cretinism (child) vs myxoedema (adult)** — both hypothyroid.
- **Gigantism (child) vs acromegaly (adult)** — both excess GH.
- **Pituitary dwarf has normal intelligence**; a cretin has mental retardation.
- **Diabetes insipidus** (ADH lack, no sugar in urine) vs **diabetes mellitus** (insulin, sugar in urine).
- **Addison's = cortex under-active; Cushing's = cortisol excess**.
- **Adrenaline is from the medulla**; **aldosterone and cortisol from the cortex**.
- **Zona glomerulosa (outer) → aldosterone**; zona fasciculata → cortisol; zona reticularis → androgens.
- **α cells → glucagon; β cells → insulin** — the most frequent swap in exams.
- **FSH → Sertoli cells (spermatogenesis); LH (ICSH) → Leydig cells (testosterone)**.
- **LH surge causes ovulation** (not FSH or oestrogen directly).
- **Steroid hormones act on intracellular receptors**; **thyroid hormones too** (despite being amino-acid derived).
- **Insulin uses tyrosine kinase receptors**, not cAMP.
- **Melatonin** is from the **pineal**, not from melanocytes; MSH acts on melanocytes.
- **Erythropoietin** is from the **kidney**, not the bone marrow.
- **Renin is an enzyme**, not a hormone in the strict sense.

## Quick revision

1. First hormone discovered — **secretin** (Bayliss and Starling, 1902); word "hormone" — Starling 1905.
2. Hormone groups — peptide, steroid, iodothyronine, amino-acid derivative.
3. Steroids and thyroid hormones — **intracellular receptors**, regulate genes.
4. Peptide hormones and catecholamines — membrane receptors, **cAMP** second messenger (Sutherland).
5. Hypothalamic hormones reach the anterior pituitary via the **hypophyseal portal system**.
6. Pituitary sits in the **sella turcica** of the sphenoid.
7. GH excess — **gigantism** (child), **acromegaly** (adult).
8. Gonadotropins — **FSH and LH** (anterior pituitary).
9. Oxytocin — uterine contraction, milk ejection; positive feedback in labour.
10. ADH deficiency — **diabetes insipidus**.
11. Pineal — **melatonin**, circadian rhythm.
12. Thyroid — T₄, T₃ (follicular cells), **calcitonin** (C cells).
13. Iodine deficiency — **goitre**; hypothyroid child — **cretinism**; adult — **myxoedema**.
14. Graves' disease — hyperthyroidism, **exophthalmos**.
15. Parathyroid — **PTH**, raises Ca²⁺; deficiency — **tetany**.
16. Thymus — **thymosins**, T-cell differentiation; shrinks with age.
17. Adrenal medulla — adrenaline, noradrenaline (3F hormones).
18. Cortex zones — glomerulosa (**aldosterone**), fasciculata (**cortisol**), reticularis (**androgens**).
19. **Addison's** — cortex deficiency; **Cushing's** — cortisol excess.
20. Islets — **α → glucagon, β → insulin, δ → somatostatin**.
21. Pancreas — **both exocrine and endocrine**.
22. Leydig cells — **testosterone**; Sertoli — inhibin.
23. Oestrogen — growing follicles; progesterone — **corpus luteum**.
24. ANF — heart atria; erythropoietin — kidney.
25. Insulin — 51 amino acids; first protein sequenced (Sanger).
