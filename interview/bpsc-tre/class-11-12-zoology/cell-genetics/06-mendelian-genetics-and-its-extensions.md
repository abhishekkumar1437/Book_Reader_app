Mendelian genetics is the most numerical part of the syllabus and TRE loves it: "9:7 represents", "1:2:1 for both genotype and phenotype shows", "phenotypic ratio of independent assortment", "what is incomplete dominance", "ABO shows", "Mendel chose peas because". NCERT class 12 "Principles of Inheritance and Variation" is the base; the B.Sc. layer is gene interaction (all epistatic ratios), probability, chi-square and history. Every ratio in this chapter has been worked from the Punnett square.

> **In simple words:** Each trait is controlled by a pair of "factors" (genes), one from each parent. The two members of the pair separate when gametes form, so each gamete carries only one. When two different forms (alleles) meet, often one hides the other (dominant over recessive). Different gene pairs usually sort independently. Many traits do not follow this simple pattern — alleles can blend (incomplete dominance), both can show (codominance), there can be more than two alleles, or one gene can mask another (epistasis) — but each of these still follows Mendel's rule of segregation.

## 1. Gregor Johann Mendel — the man and the paper

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | **22 July 1822**, Heinzendorf, Silesia (now Hynčice, Czech Republic) |
| Work place | Augustinian **St Thomas monastery, Brünn** (now **Brno**, Czech Republic); became its abbot in 1868 |
| Experiments | **1856–1863** on the garden pea ***Pisum sativum*** — about 28,000–29,000 plants in total |
| Presented | Two lectures to the **Natural History Society of Brünn** on **8 February and 8 March 1865** |
| Published | **1866**, "***Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden***" ("**Experiments on Plant Hybridization**"), in the *Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn* (Proceedings of the Natural History Society of Brünn), vol. IV (for 1865) |
| Later work | Hawkweed (*Hieracium*) — results did not fit, because *Hieracium* reproduces by apomixis |
| Died | **6 January 1884** — his work was ignored in his lifetime |
| Rediscovered | **1900**, independently by **Hugo de Vries** (Holland), **Carl Correns** (Germany) and **Erich von Tschermak** (Austria) |
| Why ignored | Poor communication, mathematical approach new to biology, "factors" not seen physically, contradicted the popular idea of blending inheritance |

**Why Mendel's work succeeded (and why he chose the pea):**
1. Pea is naturally **self-pollinating** (flowers pollinate before opening), so **pure (true-breeding) lines** were easy to keep; yet it can be **artificially cross-pollinated** after removing anthers (**emasculation**).
2. It has many **clear contrasting characters** (tall/dwarf etc.) with no intermediates.
3. **Short life cycle** (annual), many seeds per generation, easy to grow.
4. He studied **one or two characters at a time**, kept careful **counts** of large numbers and used **statistics**; he confirmed results by **test crosses** and **reciprocal crosses**.
5. He was lucky: the seven traits are on different chromosomes or far apart (no strong linkage complicated his dihybrid results).

| Character | Dominant | Recessive | Mendel's F2 count | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Flower colour | **Violet** | White | 705 : 224 | 3.15 : 1 |
| Flower position | **Axial** | Terminal | 651 : 207 | 3.14 : 1 |
| Pod shape | **Inflated (full)** | Constricted | 882 : 299 | 2.95 : 1 |

Mendel also scored **seed-coat colour** (grey/brown versus white), but he noticed it always went with flower colour: **violet flowers ↔ grey seed coat** and coloured leaf axils; **white flowers ↔ white seed coat**. These are effects of **one gene** (now known as gene *A*, a transcription factor for anthocyanin) — an early example of **pleiotropy**.

## 3. Monohybrid cross and Mendel's first two laws

Cross pure tall (TT) × pure dwarf (tt):
- **P:** TT × tt → gametes T and t.
- **F1:** all **Tt** — all tall (dwarf trait hidden, not lost).
- **F1 selfed:** Tt × Tt.

<div class="formula">

**Monohybrid F2:** phenotypic ratio **3 : 1**; genotypic ratio **1 : 2 : 1** (TT : Tt : tt).

- **Test cross** — F1 (or any dominant-phenotype individual) × **homozygous recessive** parent. Tt × tt → **1 tall : 1 dwarf**; TT × tt → all tall. Used to find whether a dominant-looking plant is TT or Tt.
- **Back cross** — F1 × **either** parent. (Every test cross is a back cross if the recessive parent is used; not every back cross is a test cross.)
- **Reciprocal cross** — same cross with sexes reversed (♀ tall × ♂ dwarf, and ♀ dwarf × ♂ tall). Same result → genes are **nuclear and autosomal**; different results → sex-linked or cytoplasmic inheritance.

</div>

**Mendel's laws:**
1. **Law of dominance** — in a heterozygote one allele (dominant) expresses itself and the other (recessive) is masked. (Some books do not count it as a law because it has many exceptions.)
2. **Law of segregation (law of purity of gametes)** — the two alleles of a gene **separate** during gamete formation, so each gamete gets **only one** allele; alleles do not blend. **Universal** — no exception in normal meiosis. Cytological basis: separation of **homologous chromosomes in anaphase I**.
3. **Law of independent assortment** — when two pairs of traits are combined, the segregation of one pair is **independent** of the other. Holds only for genes on **different chromosomes** (or very far apart on one). Cytological basis: **random orientation of bivalents in metaphase I**.

## 4. Dihybrid cross

Pure round-yellow (RRYY) × pure wrinkled-green (rryy) → F1 **RrYy** (round yellow). F1 forms **four kinds of gametes in equal numbers**: RY, Ry, rY, ry.

<div class="formula">

**F2 Punnett square (RrYy × RrYy):**

| ♀ \ ♂ | **RY** | **Ry** | **rY** | **ry** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RY** | RRYY | RRYy | RrYY | RrYy |
| **Ry** | RRYy | RRyy | RrYy | Rryy |
| **rY** | RrYY | RrYy | rrYY | rrYy |
| **ry** | RrYy | Rryy | rrYy | rryy |

Count from the square:

| Genotype | Number | Phenotype |
|---|---|---|
| RRYY | 1 | Round yellow |
| RRYy | 2 | Round yellow |
| RrYY | 2 | Round yellow |
| RrYy | 4 | Round yellow |
| RRyy | 1 | Round green |
| Rryy | 2 | Round green |
| rrYY | 1 | Wrinkled yellow |
| rrYy | 2 | Wrinkled yellow |
| rryy | 1 | Wrinkled green |

- **Phenotypic ratio = 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green** (1+2+2+4 = 9; 1+2 = 3; 1+2 = 3; 1).
- **Genotypic ratio = 1 : 2 : 2 : 4 : 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1** — **9 genotypes**.
- Each single trait still gives 3 : 1 (12 round : 4 wrinkled; 12 yellow : 4 green).
- Parental combinations (round yellow, wrinkled green) = 10/16; **recombinant** (new) combinations (round green, wrinkled yellow) = **6/16**.
- **Dihybrid test cross:** RrYy × rryy → **1 : 1 : 1 : 1**.

</div>

Mendel's actual dihybrid F2 (556 seeds): **315 round yellow, 108 round green, 101 wrinkled yellow, 32 wrinkled green** — very close to 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (expected 312.75 : 104.25 : 104.25 : 34.75).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q106 asked: "What was the phenotype ratio in Mendel's law of independent assortment in F2 generation? (A) 3 : 1 (B) 1 : 2 : 2 : 4 : 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1 (C) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C).** Option (B) is the dihybrid **genotypic** ratio — a deliberate trap.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Homozygous recessive fraction | (1/4)ⁿ | 1/4 | 1/16 | 1/64 |

## 5. Probability — the quick way to solve crosses

- **Product rule:** probability of two independent events both happening = product of their probabilities.
- **Sum rule:** probability of either of two mutually exclusive events = sum.
- **Branch (forked-line) method:** treat each gene separately, then multiply.

**Worked problems:**
1. AaBbCc × AaBbCc — probability of an **aabbcc** offspring? = 1/4 × 1/4 × 1/4 = **1/64**.
2. Same cross — probability of **AaBbCc**? = 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = **1/8**.
3. Same cross — probability of the **dominant phenotype for all three** (A_B_C_)? = 3/4 × 3/4 × 3/4 = **27/64**.
4. AaBb × aaBb — fraction **aabb**? = (1/2) × (1/4) = **1/8**. Phenotypic ratio = (1 A_ : 1 aa) × (3 B_ : 1 bb) = **3 : 1 : 3 : 1**.
5. Two carriers (Aa × Aa) have **3 children**. Probability **all three normal** = (3/4)³ = **27/64**. Probability **exactly one affected** = 3 × (3/4)² × (1/4) = **27/64**. Probability **at least one affected** = 1 − 27/64 = **37/64**.
6. A normal child of two carrier parents — probability that the child is a carrier? Among normal children (AA, Aa, Aa) = **2/3** (not 1/2 — a frequent trap).
7. AaBb × AaBb — how many of 160 offspring will be aabb? 160 × 1/16 = **10**.

### Chi-square test (goodness of fit) — B.Sc.

χ² = Σ (observed − expected)² / expected. For Mendel's dihybrid data: (315 − 312.75)²/312.75 + (108 − 104.25)²/104.25 + (101 − 104.25)²/104.25 + (32 − 34.75)²/34.75 = 0.016 + 0.135 + 0.101 + 0.218 ≈ **0.47**. Degrees of freedom = classes − 1 = **3**; table value at 5% = **7.815**. 0.47 < 7.815 → the deviation is due to chance; data **fit 9 : 3 : 3 : 1**.

## 6. Incomplete dominance and codominance

### Incomplete dominance (partial dominance, blending)

**Neither allele is fully dominant**; the heterozygote shows an **intermediate** phenotype. **F2 phenotypic ratio = genotypic ratio = 1 : 2 : 1.**
- **Snapdragon / dog flower (*Antirrhinum majus*)** — red (RR) × white (rr) → F1 **pink** (Rr) → F2 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white (NCERT example).
- **Four o'clock plant (*Mirabilis jalapa*)** — same pattern; studied by **Correns**.
- **Andalusian fowl** — black × splashed white → **blue** F1; F2 1 black : 2 blue : 1 white (often listed as incomplete dominance).
- **Starch grain size in pea** (NCERT): BB → large grains (round seed), bb → small grains (wrinkled), Bb → **intermediate grain size** but **round seed**. So for seed shape B is dominant, for grain size it is incompletely dominant — **dominance depends on which phenotype we look at**.
- Familial hypercholesterolaemia in humans (heterozygotes have intermediate LDL levels).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q55 asked: "If both genotype and phenotype show the same ratio of 1 : 2 : 1 in the F2 generation, it shows — (A) incomplete dominance in a monohybrid cross (B) complete dominance in a monohybrid cross (C) codominance." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A).** Note: codominance **also** gives 1 : 2 : 1 for both (e.g. roan cattle, MN blood group), so (D) could be argued; the key chose (A), the textbook phrase.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q107 asked: "What is incomplete dominance? (A) No allele can block or mask the expression of the other allele (B) Neither of the two alleles is fully dominant over the other (C) The alleles do not show any dominant and recessive allele relationship." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** (A) and (C) better describe **codominance**, where both alleles express fully.

### Codominance

**Both alleles express fully and separately** in the heterozygote (no blending — both products are present).
- **ABO blood group**: I^A I^B → **AB** blood (both A and B antigens on RBCs) — NCERT example.
- **MN blood group**: L^M L^N → MN (both antigens).
- **Roan cattle** (Shorthorn): red (RR) × white (WW) → **roan** (red and white hairs mixed — each hair is either red or white).
- **Sickle-cell trait** at the molecular level (HbA and HbS both made); at the level of the whole person, normal is dominant for health.
- F2 ratio again **1 : 2 : 1** for both genotype and phenotype.

| Point | Complete dominance | Incomplete dominance | Codominance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heterozygote looks like | Dominant parent | **In between** (pink) | **Both** parents (red and white patches; AB) |
| F2 phenotype ratio | 3 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| F2 genotype ratio | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Example | Tall pea | Snapdragon, *Mirabilis* | AB blood group, roan cattle |

## 7. Multiple alleles — ABO and Rh

**Multiple alleles** — **more than two alleles** of a gene exist in the **population**, though any diploid individual carries only **two**.

<div class="formula">

**ABO blood group** (discovered by **Karl Landsteiner, 1900–01** — groups A, B, O; AB by Decastello and Sturli, 1902; Landsteiner got the Nobel Prize in 1930). Gene **I** on chromosome 9 with three alleles: **I^A**, **I^B**, **i**.
- I^A and I^B are **codominant** with each other; both are **dominant over i**.

| Genotype | Blood group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma |
|---|---|---|---|
| I^A I^A, I^A i | **A** | A | anti-B |
| I^B I^B, I^B i | **B** | B | anti-A |
| I^A I^B | **AB** | A and B | none (**universal recipient**) |
| ii | **O** | none | anti-A and anti-B (**universal donor**) |

**6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes.** Number of genotypes for n alleles = **n(n + 1)/2** → for 3 alleles = 3 × 4 / 2 = 6; for 4 alleles = 10.

</div>

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Q147 asked: "ABO blood group system exhibits — (A) epistasis (B) multiple allelism (C) incomplete dominance." **Official answer: (B).** (Codominance of I^A and I^B is also true, but it was not offered.)

**ABO worked problems:**
- Father A (I^A i) × mother B (I^B i) → children **A, B, AB and O in 1 : 1 : 1 : 1** — all four groups possible.
- Father AB × mother O → children only **A (I^A i) or B (I^B i)**, 1 : 1 — never AB or O.
- Child O and mother A → father must carry **i** → father can be A (I^A i), B (I^B i) or O — **not AB**. (Used in disputed-parentage cases.)

**Other multiple-allele series:**
- **Coat colour in rabbits** — C (full colour, agouti) > c^ch (chinchilla) > c^h (Himalayan) > c (albino) — four alleles, dominance in that order.
- **Eye colour in *Drosophila*** — the white series (w⁺ red, many intermediate alleles, w white).
- **Self-incompatibility alleles** in *Nicotiana* (S1, S2, S3 …).
- **HLA** genes — hundreds of alleles.

### Rh factor

- Discovered by **Landsteiner and Wiener (1940)** in **rhesus monkey** blood. About 80% or more of people are **Rh⁺** (D antigen present); **Rh⁺ (D) is dominant** over Rh⁻ (d).
- **Erythroblastosis fetalis (haemolytic disease of the newborn):** an **Rh⁻ mother** carrying an **Rh⁺ foetus** (Rh⁺ father) — at the **first delivery** foetal Rh⁺ cells enter her blood and she makes **anti-Rh (anti-D) antibodies**; in a **second Rh⁺ pregnancy** these IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy foetal RBCs → anaemia and jaundice of the baby.
- Prevented by giving the mother **anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** within **72 hours** of the first delivery (and often at 28 weeks).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q89 asked: "Which marriage should be prohibited from biological point of view? (A) Rh⁺ boy and Rh⁺ girl (B) Rh⁺ boy and Rh⁻ girl (C) Rh⁻ boy and Rh⁺ girl." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** The risk is an **Rh⁻ woman** with an **Rh⁺ man**. ("Prohibited" is too strong in real medicine — anti-D prophylaxis solves it — but this is the expected answer.)

## 8. Lethal genes, pleiotropy, penetrance

- **Lethal genes** — kill the individual carrying them (usually when homozygous). **Yellow coat in mice** (Lucien **Cuénot, 1905**): A^Y is dominant for yellow colour but **recessive lethal**. A^Y a × A^Y a → 1 A^Y A^Y (dies in embryo) : 2 A^Y a (yellow) : 1 aa (agouti) → **2 yellow : 1 agouti** among the living. Other examples: Creeper fowl, Manx cat (tailless), brachydactyly, thalassaemia major, sickle-cell anaemia (sub-lethal).
- **Pleiotropy** — **one gene, many effects** (NCERT): **phenylketonuria** (mental retardation **and** reduced hair and skin pigmentation), **sickle-cell anaemia** (anaemia, pain crises, organ damage, malaria resistance in heterozygotes), Mendel's flower/seed-coat gene, Marfan syndrome (long limbs, eye lens dislocation, heart defects), white-eye gene of *Drosophila* (eye colour and sperm-storage organ shape).
- **Penetrance** — percentage of individuals with a genotype who actually show the trait (e.g. polydactyly is incompletely penetrant). **Expressivity** — degree to which the trait is shown (mild or severe).
- **Phenocopy** — an environmentally caused copy of a genetic phenotype (e.g. thalidomide limb defects resembling a genetic disorder).

## 9. Polygenic (quantitative) inheritance

Traits controlled by **several genes with additive (cumulative) effect**, each dominant allele adding a small equal amount; the environment also influences them. Phenotypes form a **continuous** range (bell-shaped curve). Examples: **human skin colour**, height, intelligence, weight; **kernel colour in wheat**; ear length in maize.

<div class="formula">

**Wheat kernel colour — H. Nilsson-Ehle (1909), two genes:** R₁r₁R₂r₂ × R₁r₁R₂r₂. Count dominant (colour) alleles in each of 16 F2 combinations:

| Dominant alleles | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phenotype | Dark red | Medium-dark red | Medium red | Light red | White |
| Number out of 16 | 1 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 1 |

Ratio **1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1** (5 phenotypes).

**Human skin colour — C. B. Davenport (1913); NCERT uses three genes A, B, C:** AaBbCc × AaBbCc gives **7 phenotypes** in the ratio **1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1** (out of 64). Very dark = AABBCC (6 dominant), very light = aabbcc.

</div>

- Number of phenotypes = **2n + 1** (n = number of gene pairs). Fraction showing either extreme = **(1/4)ⁿ** each.
- The coefficients are those of the binomial expansion (a + b)²ⁿ.

## 10. Gene interaction and epistasis

**Epistasis** (term by **William Bateson, 1909**) — one gene (**epistatic**) masks or modifies the expression of another, **non-allelic** gene (**hypostatic**). Compare dominance, which is between **alleles of the same gene**. All the classic ratios come from **modifying 9 : 3 : 3 : 1** of a dihybrid F2 (AaBb × AaBb).

<div class="formula">

| Ratio | Name | Gene action | Classic example | Grouping of 9 A_B_ : 3 A_bb : 3 aaB_ : 1 aabb | Test cross (AaBb × aabb) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** | No modification (interaction without change of ratio) | Two genes give four new phenotypes | **Comb shape in fowl** (Bateson and Punnett): R_P_ walnut, R_pp rose, rrP_ pea, rrpp single | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 |
| **9 : 7** | **Complementary genes** (duplicate recessive epistasis) | Both dominant genes needed for the trait | **Flower colour in sweet pea (*Lathyrus odoratus*)** — Bateson and Punnett: two white varieties → F1 purple → F2 9 purple : 7 white | 9 : (3 + 3 + 1) | **1 : 3** |
| **9 : 3 : 4** | **Recessive epistasis** (supplementary genes) | aa masks B/b | **Coat colour in mice**: C_A_ agouti, C_aa black, cc__ albino; also Labrador coat (E_B_ black, E_bb chocolate, ee__ yellow) | 9 : 3 : (3 + 1) | **1 : 1 : 2** |
| **12 : 3 : 1** | **Dominant epistasis** | A_ masks B/b | **Fruit colour in summer squash**: W___ white, wwY_ yellow, wwyy green; dog coat colour | (9 + 3) : 3 : 1 | **2 : 1 : 1** |
| **15 : 1** | **Duplicate (dominant) genes** | Either dominant allele gives the trait | **Capsule shape in shepherd's purse (*Capsella bursa-pastoris*)** — G. H. Shull: triangular 15 : ovoid (top-shaped) 1; seed colour in wheat (Nilsson-Ehle) | (9 + 3 + 3) : 1 | **3 : 1** |
| **13 : 3** | **Dominant suppression / inhibitory gene** (dominant and recessive epistasis) | Dominant I suppresses colour gene C | **Feather colour in fowl**: White Leghorn (IICC) × White Wyandotte (iicc) → F1 white → F2 13 white : 3 coloured (iiC_) | (9 + 3 + 1) : 3 | **3 : 1** |
| **9 : 6 : 1** | **Duplicate genes with cumulative effect** | One dominant gives intermediate, both give full | **Fruit shape in summer squash**: A_B_ disc, A_bb or aaB_ spherical, aabb elongated | 9 : (3 + 3) : 1 | **1 : 2 : 1** |

</div>

**Working the 9 : 7 square** (CcPp × CcPp; purple needs at least one C and one P):
- C_P_ = 9/16 → **purple**.
- C_pp = 3/16, ccP_ = 3/16, ccpp = 1/16 → all **white** = 7/16.
- **9 purple : 7 white.** Test cross CcPp × ccpp → CcPp (purple) : Ccpp : ccPp : ccpp (white) = **1 purple : 3 white**.

**Working the 13 : 3 square** (IiCc × IiCc; colour needs C and absence of I):
- I_C_ (9) white, I_cc (3) white, iicc (1) white → **13 white**.
- iiC_ (3) → **coloured**. Ratio **13 : 3**.

**Working the 9 : 3 : 4 square** (CcAa × CcAa in mice):
- C_A_ (9) agouti; C_aa (3) black; ccA_ (3) + ccaa (1) albino (cc blocks all pigment) → **9 : 3 : 4**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q44 asked: "In the F2 generation, 9 : 7 ratio represents — (A) incomplete dominance (B) codominance (C) epistasis." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) epistasis** — specifically complementary gene interaction (duplicate recessive epistasis).

> **Memory trick:** Add up to 16 and ask "which boxes merged?" — **9 : 7** = everything except 9 merged; **15 : 1** = everything except 1 merged; **12 : 3 : 1** = first two merged; **9 : 3 : 4** = last two merged; **13 : 3** = all but one "3" merged; **9 : 6 : 1** = middle two merged.

### How to recognise the interaction from data

1. Add up the F2 classes and divide by 16 to see the ratio (e.g. 180 purple : 140 white → 320/16 = 20 → 9 : 7).
2. If the total of a dihybrid F2 phenotypes is in **sixteenths**, two genes are involved; if in **64ths**, three.
3. Two classes only: 9 : 7, 15 : 1, 13 : 3 (or 3 : 1 if monohybrid). Three classes: 9 : 3 : 4, 12 : 3 : 1, 9 : 6 : 1, (1 : 2 : 1 if monohybrid incomplete dominance).

**Worked example:** An F2 of fowl gives 195 white and 45 coloured birds. Total 240; 240/16 = 15; 195/15 = 13, 45/15 = 3 → **13 : 3** → dominant suppression (inhibitor gene).

**Worked example:** Summer squash F2: 180 disc, 120 spherical, 20 long. Total 320; /20 → **9 : 6 : 1** → duplicate genes with cumulative effect.

## 11. Exceptions to Mendelism — a summary

| Deviation | Ratio change | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete dominance | 1 : 2 : 1 | Snapdragon |
| Codominance | 1 : 2 : 1 | AB blood, roan cattle |
| Multiple alleles | More genotypes | ABO, rabbit coat |
| Lethal genes | 2 : 1 | Yellow mouse |
| Epistasis | 9 : 7, 9 : 3 : 4 etc. | Sweet pea, mouse coat |
| Polygenic inheritance | 1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1 etc. | Wheat colour, skin colour |
| Pleiotropy | One gene, many traits | PKU, sickle cell |
| **Linkage** | Excess of parental types | Morgan's *Drosophila* — see [next chapter](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/chromosomes-linkage-sex-determination-and-disorders) |
| Sex linkage | Criss-cross inheritance | Haemophilia, colour blindness |
| Cytoplasmic inheritance | Maternal only; reciprocal crosses differ | *Mirabilis* plastids, mitochondrial DNA |

## Traps and exceptions

- Mendel **presented in 1865 and published in 1866**; rediscovery in **1900** by de Vries, Correns, Tschermak. "Mendel's book" was really a **paper** in a society journal.
- The **law of segregation** has no exception; the **law of independent assortment** fails for **linked** genes.
- **9 : 3 : 3 : 1** is the dihybrid **phenotypic** ratio; **1 : 2 : 2 : 4 : 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1** is the **genotypic** ratio.
- **1 : 2 : 1 for both** phenotype and genotype → **incomplete dominance or codominance** — never complete dominance.
- **9 : 7 = complementary genes; 15 : 1 = duplicate genes; 13 : 3 = inhibitory gene; 12 : 3 : 1 = dominant epistasis; 9 : 3 : 4 = recessive epistasis; 9 : 6 : 1 = cumulative duplicate genes.**
- Epistasis is between **non-alleles**; dominance is between **alleles**.
- **AB blood group = codominance**; ABO system as a whole = **multiple alleles**.
- **Rh⁻ mother + Rh⁺ father** is the risky combination, not the reverse.
- Among **normal** children of two carriers, the chance of being a carrier is **2/3**.
- **Test cross** always uses the **homozygous recessive**; back cross uses either parent.
- In polygenic inheritance the number of phenotypes is **2n + 1**, not 2ⁿ.
- Lethal yellow mouse gives **2 : 1**, not 3 : 1.
- Mendel's **seed-coat colour and flower colour** are one gene; **seed colour (cotyledon) and seed coat colour** are different genes.

## Quick revision

1. Mendel — Brno monastery; peas 1856–63; lectures 1865; paper 1866 "Experiments on Plant Hybridization".
2. Rediscovery 1900 — de Vries, Correns, Tschermak.
3. Pea chosen for clear contrasting characters, self-pollination, easy crossing, short life.
4. Seven characters; dominant: tall, violet, axial, inflated, green pod, round, yellow.
5. Bateson — genetics, allelomorph, epistasis; Johannsen — gene, genotype, phenotype.
6. Monohybrid F2 — 3 : 1 phenotype, 1 : 2 : 1 genotype; test cross 1 : 1.
7. Dihybrid F2 — 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotype; 1 : 2 : 2 : 4 : 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1 genotype; test cross 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.
8. Trihybrid F2 — 27 : 9 : 9 : 9 : 3 : 3 : 3 : 1; 8 gametes, 27 genotypes, 64 squares.
9. Law of segregation is universal; independent assortment needs unlinked genes.
10. Incomplete dominance — snapdragon, Mirabilis — 1 : 2 : 1.
11. Codominance — AB blood, MN blood, roan cattle — 1 : 2 : 1.
12. ABO — Landsteiner; 3 alleles, 6 genotypes, 4 phenotypes; n(n+1)/2 genotypes.
13. Rh — Landsteiner and Wiener 1940; Rh⁻ mother with Rh⁺ foetus → erythroblastosis fetalis; anti-D within 72 h.
14. Yellow mouse lethal (Cuénot) → 2 : 1.
15. Pleiotropy — PKU, sickle cell.
16. Polygenic — wheat 1 : 4 : 6 : 4 : 1 (Nilsson-Ehle); skin colour 1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1.
17. 9 : 7 — complementary (sweet pea flower colour); test cross 1 : 3.
18. 9 : 3 : 4 — recessive epistasis (mouse coat); test cross 1 : 1 : 2.
19. 12 : 3 : 1 — dominant epistasis (summer squash colour); test cross 2 : 1 : 1.
20. 15 : 1 — duplicate genes (Capsella capsule); test cross 3 : 1.
21. 13 : 3 — inhibitory gene (White Leghorn × White Wyandotte); test cross 3 : 1.
22. 9 : 6 : 1 — cumulative (squash fruit shape); test cross 1 : 2 : 1.
23. Fowl comb (walnut, rose, pea, single) — 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 by interaction.
24. Chi-square for Mendel's dihybrid data ≈ 0.47 (df 3) — good fit.
