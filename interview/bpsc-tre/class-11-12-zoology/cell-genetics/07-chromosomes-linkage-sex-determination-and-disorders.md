This is the chapter TRE 1.0 drew most heavily from: chromosome theory (Q108), metacentric chromosomes (Q47, Q109), polytene chromosomes (Q43), sex-linked dominant inheritance (Q45), XX males (Q58), honeybee sex (Q63) and cytoplasmic inheritance (Q57, repeated as TRE 2.0 Q140). NCERT class 12 "Principles of Inheritance and Variation" gives the base; the B.Sc. layer adds chromosome classification, mapping with interference, sex-determination variants and maternal inheritance.

> **In simple words:** Genes sit on chromosomes like beads on a string. Genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together (**linkage**), unless crossing-over separates them; the more often they separate, the farther apart they are — that is how gene maps are made. In humans a **Y chromosome** (its SRY gene) makes a baby male. Genes on the **X** show special patterns because males have only one X. Wrong numbers of chromosomes cause syndromes like Down, Turner and Klinefelter. A few genes live outside the nucleus (in mitochondria and plastids) and come only from the mother.

## 1. Chromosome theory of inheritance

- **Walter Sutton** (USA, 1902–03, grasshopper *Brachystola magna* meiosis) and **Theodor Boveri** (Germany, 1902, sea-urchin embryos) independently noticed that chromosomes behave exactly like Mendel's factors. This is the **Sutton–Boveri chromosome theory of inheritance**.
- Parallels (NCERT): both occur in **pairs**; both **segregate** at gamete formation so that only one of each pair goes to a gamete; **independent pairs segregate independently** of each other.
- **Experimental proof: Thomas Hunt Morgan (1910–11)** with the fruit fly ***Drosophila melanogaster*** (white-eye mutation linked to the X chromosome); Nobel Prize **1933**. **Calvin Bridges (1916)** gave decisive proof through **non-disjunction** of X chromosomes.
- Why *Drosophila* is ideal (NCERT): grows on simple synthetic medium, **life cycle about two weeks**, a single mating gives many progeny, **clear sexual dimorphism** (male smaller, with dark abdomen tip and sex combs), many easily seen hereditary variations, only **4 pairs of chromosomes** (2n = 8).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q108 asked: "Who proposed the chromosome theory of inheritance? (A) Watson and Crick (B) Walter Sutton and Theodor Boveri (C) T. H. Morgan." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** Morgan **proved** it experimentally; he did not propose it.

## 2. Structure of a chromosome

- The word **chromosome** ("coloured body") — **Waldeyer (1888)**. Chromosomes are best seen at **metaphase**.
- Each metaphase chromosome has **two sister chromatids** joined at the **centromere** (**primary constriction**), on which sits the **kinetochore** (disc-shaped protein structure where spindle fibres attach).
- **Secondary constriction** — a non-staining narrow region on some chromosomes; often the **nucleolar organiser region (NOR)** containing rRNA genes. A small knob beyond it is the **satellite** — such chromosomes are **SAT chromosomes** (in humans the acrocentrics 13, 14, 15, 21, 22).
- **Telomeres** — protective ends with repeats (**TTAGGG** in humans); term by H. J. Muller (1938); maintained by **telomerase**.
- **Chromomeres** — bead-like regions on the chromonema; **matrix** and **pellicle** are old terms.

### Types by number of centromeres

| Type | Centromeres | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Acentric** | None | Lost in division (cannot attach to spindle) |
| **Monocentric** | One | Normal |
| **Dicentric** | Two | Breaks at anaphase ("bridge") |
| **Polycentric** | Many | Rare |
| **Holocentric (diffuse centromere)** | Spindle attaches along whole length | ***Luzula***, some insects (Hemiptera, Lepidoptera), nematodes (*C. elegans*, *Ascaris*) |

### Human karyotype

- Correct human number **2n = 46** — **Tjio and Levan (1956)** (Painter, 1923, had counted 48).
- **22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes** (XX female, XY male). Karyotype formula: 46,XX and 46,XY.
- **Denver classification (1960)** groups chromosomes by size and centromere: A (1–3), B (4–5), C (6–12 + X), D (13–15, acrocentric), E (16–18), F (19–20), G (21–22 + Y, small acrocentric). Chromosome **1** is the largest; **21** is the smallest autosome.
- Banding: **G-banding (Giemsa)**, Q-banding (quinacrine — first, Caspersson 1970), C-banding (centromeric heterochromatin), R-banding.

## 4. Linkage and crossing-over

**Linkage** — the tendency of genes on the **same chromosome** to be **inherited together**. **Recombination** — appearance of new (non-parental) gene combinations.

- **Bateson and Punnett (1905–06)** in sweet pea (flower colour and pollen shape) found far more parental combinations than 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 predicts; they called it **coupling** (dominant alleles together, AB/ab — **cis**) and **repulsion** (Ab/aB — **trans**), but could not explain it.
- **Morgan (1910–11)** explained it as genes being on the same chromosome and coined **"linkage"**; he also coined **"crossing-over"** for exchange between homologous chromosomes.
- **Morgan's dihybrid crosses in *Drosophila*** (NCERT): genes on the X chromosome —
  - **yellow body (y) and white eye (w)**: only **1.3%** recombinants — **tightly linked**;
  - **white eye (w) and miniature wing (m)**: **37.2%** recombinants — **loosely linked**.
- **Alfred Sturtevant (1913)**, Morgan's student, used **recombination frequency as a measure of distance** and made the **first genetic (linkage) map** of the *Drosophila* X chromosome.

<div class="formula">

- **Recombination frequency (RF)** = (number of recombinant progeny ÷ total progeny) × 100.
- **1% recombination = 1 map unit = 1 centimorgan (cM)**.
- RF can never exceed **50%** (two crossovers can cancel each other); genes far apart on a chromosome behave as if unlinked.
- **Number of linkage groups = haploid chromosome number**: *Drosophila* 4, pea 7, maize 10, human 23 (24 if X and Y are counted separately in males), *E. coli* 1.
- **Complete linkage** (no crossing-over): **male *Drosophila*** and **female silkworm (*Bombyx mori*)** — the heterogametic sex.

</div>

**Crossing-over facts:**
- Occurs at the **four-strand (tetrad) stage** in **pachytene**, between **non-sister chromatids** of homologous chromosomes; the enzyme **recombinase**; the visible result is the **chiasma** (diplotene).
- Mechanism (Janssens' chiasmatype theory, 1909; molecular — **Holliday model, 1964**: breakage and reunion with a Holliday junction).
- Cytological proof: **Creighton and McClintock (1931)** in maize; **Curt Stern (1931)** in *Drosophila*.
- Frequency of crossing-over **increases** with distance between genes, temperature (within limits), X-rays and age (in *Drosophila* females); it is **reduced** near centromeres and by inversions (which suppress recovery of crossovers).
- Somatic (mitotic) crossing-over — rare (Stern, 1936, twin spots in *Drosophila*).

### Worked problem 1 — two-point test cross

A dihybrid AaBb (A and B came from one parent — coupling, AB/ab) is test crossed with ab/ab. Progeny: AB 420, ab 430, Ab 75, aB 75 (total 1000).
- Parental types (AB, ab) = 850; recombinants (Ab, aB) = 150.
- RF = 150/1000 × 100 = **15%** → genes are **15 cM** apart.
- If the genes were unlinked, all four classes would be about 250 each (1 : 1 : 1 : 1).

### Worked problem 2 — three-point test cross with interference (B.Sc.)

Heterozygote +++/abc test crossed with abc/abc; 1000 progeny:

| Class | Phenotype | Number | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | + + + | 405 | Parental |
| 2 | a b c | 395 | Parental |
| 3 | a + + | 62 | Single crossover, region a–b |
| 4 | + b c | 48 | Single crossover, region a–b |
| 5 | a b + | 42 | Single crossover, region b–c |
| 6 | + + c | 38 | Single crossover, region b–c |
| 7 | a + c | 6 | Double crossover |
| 8 | + b + | 4 | Double crossover |

Steps:
1. **Parental** classes are the most frequent (1, 2); **double crossovers** the least (7, 8).
2. **Gene order:** compare a DCO class with a parental class — a + c differs from a b c only at **b**, so **b is in the middle**: order **a – b – c**.
3. **Distance a–b** = (SCO in region I + DCO) / total = (62 + 48 + 6 + 4)/1000 = 120/1000 = **12 cM**.
4. **Distance b–c** = (42 + 38 + 6 + 4)/1000 = 90/1000 = **9 cM**.
5. **Distance a–c** = 12 + 9 = **21 cM** (the direct a–c recombinant count, 110 + 80 = 190, i.e. 19%, under-estimates because double crossovers restore the parental a–c combination).
6. **Expected DCO** = 0.12 × 0.09 × 1000 = **10.8**; observed = **10**.
7. **Coefficient of coincidence** = observed ÷ expected = 10/10.8 = **0.926**; **interference** = 1 − 0.926 = **0.074** (a crossover slightly reduces the chance of another nearby — H. J. Muller named it).

## 5. Sex determination

**History:** **Henking (1891)** saw a special nuclear structure in about half the sperm of the bug *Pyrrhocoris* and named it the **"X body"**; **McClung (1902)** called it the "accessory chromosome"; **Nettie Stevens** and **E. B. Wilson (1905)** described XX–XY and XX–XO systems in insects (Stevens in the mealworm *Tenebrio*).

<div class="formula">

| System | Female | Male | Heterogametic sex | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XX–XY** | XX | **XY** | **Male** | **Humans and most mammals**, *Drosophila*, some plants (*Melandrium*/*Silene*, *Coccinia*) |
| **XX–XO** | XX | **XO** (one X only; one chromosome fewer) | **Male** | **Grasshopper**, cockroach, many bugs (Hemiptera, Orthoptera) — NCERT |
| **ZW–ZZ** | **ZW** | ZZ | **Female** | **Birds** (fowl), some reptiles (snakes), some fishes, **butterflies and moths** (silkworm) |
| **ZO–ZZ** | **ZO** | ZZ | Female | A few moths and butterflies (older textbook example) |
| **Haplodiploidy** | **Diploid** (2n) — from **fertilised** eggs | **Haploid** (n) — from **unfertilised** eggs (**parthenogenesis**) | — | **Honeybee**, ants, wasps (Hymenoptera) |
| **Genic balance (X : A ratio)** | X : A = 1.0 | X : A = 0.5 | — | ***Drosophila*** (Calvin Bridges, 1921) |
| **Environmental / temperature-dependent (TSD)** | Decided by incubation temperature | — | — | **Turtles** (warm → females), **crocodiles and alligators**, some lizards |

| **Social / sequential hermaphroditism** | — | — | — | **Clownfish** (male → female, protandry), **wrasse** (female → male, protogyny) |

### Humans — Y decides maleness

- The Y chromosome carries the **SRY gene** (**Sex-determining Region Y**, on the short arm, Yp11.2; Sinclair and colleagues, 1990), which codes for the **testis-determining factor (TDF)**. SRY switches the bipotential gonad into a **testis** (about 6–7 weeks of embryonic life); testosterone and AMH then make the male body.
- **XXY** (Klinefelter) is **male** and **XO** (Turner) is **female** — proof that in humans the **Y** (not the number of X) decides sex.
- Males produce two kinds of sperm (**22 + X** and **22 + Y**) in equal numbers; ova are all **22 + X**. So **the father's sperm decides the sex of the child**; each pregnancy has a **50%** chance of either sex.
- **Pseudoautosomal regions (PAR1, PAR2)** at the tips of X and Y pair and cross over in male meiosis.

### *Drosophila* — genic balance (Bridges)

X chromosomes carry female-determining genes and autosomes carry male-determining genes; sex depends on the **ratio of X chromosomes to sets of autosomes (X : A)**. The Y is needed only for **male fertility**, not for maleness.

| Chromosomes | X : A | Sex |
|---|---|---|
| 3X : 2A | 1.5 | **Metafemale (superfemale)** — weak, sterile |
| 2X : 2A (XX or XXY) | 1.0 | **Female** |
| 3X : 4A | 0.75 | **Intersex** |
| 1X : 2A (XY or XO) | 0.5 | **Male** (XO is a sterile male) |
| 1X : 3A | 0.33 | **Metamale (supermale)** — sterile |

### Honeybee — haplodiploidy

- **Queen** (fertile female, 2n = 32) and **workers** (sterile females, 2n = 32) develop from **fertilised** eggs; the difference is nutrition (larvae fed **royal jelly** become queens).
- **Drones** (males, n = 16) develop from **unfertilised** eggs by **parthenogenesis** (arrhenotoky); they produce sperm by **mitosis**. A drone has a mother and a grandfather but **no father** and **no sons**.
- **Workers have a sting** (a modified ovipositor); **drones have no sting**.
- NCERT sex-determination diagram of honeybee: female (32) × male (16) → female offspring from fertilised eggs (32), male offspring from unfertilised eggs (16).

### Sex reversal — XX males and XY females

| Condition | Karyotype | Cause | Phenotype |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XX male (de la Chapelle syndrome)** | 46,XX | **SRY moved onto an X chromosome** by abnormal X–Y exchange (crossing-over outside the pseudoautosomal region / **segment transfer**) during the father's meiosis | Male, small testes, sterile |
| **XY female (Swyer syndrome)** | 46,XY | **SRY missing or mutated** on the Y (the reciprocal of the above) | Female, streak gonads |
| **Androgen insensitivity (testicular feminisation)** | 46,XY | Androgen receptor (X-linked) defective | Female external body, internal testes |
| **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia** | 46,XX | Excess adrenal androgens | Masculinised external genitalia (ovaries normal) |
| **Hen-to-cock reversal (fowl)** | ZW | The functional left ovary is destroyed by disease → the rudimentary right gonad develops as a testis (Crew, 1923) | Crowing, fertile "cock" (still ZW) |
| **Freemartin (cattle)** | XX twin of a male | Male hormones from the twin through shared placental vessels | Sterile, masculinised female |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q58 asked: "Male XX and female XY are sometimes seen as a result of — (A) hormonal discord (B) segment transfer in the X and Y chromosomes (C) the presence of aneuploidy." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** Transfer of the SRY segment from Y to X gives XX males; its loss from Y gives XY females. Aneuploidy gives XXY (male) or XO (female) — different karyotypes, not "XX male / XY female".

### Dosage compensation and the Barr body

- Females have two X chromosomes, males one; to equalise X-gene products, **one X in each female somatic cell is inactivated** and condenses into the **Barr body (sex chromatin)** — discovered by **Murray Barr and Ewart Bertram (1949)** in nerve cells of female cats.
- **Lyon hypothesis (Mary Lyon, 1961):** inactivation is **random** (maternal or paternal X), happens early in embryo (about the blastocyst stage), and is then fixed in all descendants of that cell → females are **mosaics** — e.g. **tortoiseshell/calico cats** (almost always female), patchy sweat glands in carriers of anhidrotic ectodermal dysplasia. Gene **XIST** produces the RNA that coats the inactive X.
- **Number of Barr bodies = number of X chromosomes − 1**: normal female 1, normal male 0, **Turner (XO) 0**, **Klinefelter (XXY) 1**, XXX 2.
- **Drumstick** appendage on the nucleus of about 3% of neutrophils in females (Davidson and Smith, 1954).
- *Drosophila* does the opposite — the male's single X is **hyperactivated** (twice the transcription).

## 6. Sex-linked inheritance

Genes on the **X chromosome** (non-homologous part) are **X-linked**; on the Y, **Y-linked (holandric)**. Because a male has only one X, he is **hemizygous** — one recessive allele shows.

### X-linked recessive

Examples: **haemophilia A** (factor VIII) and **B** (factor IX, "Christmas disease"), **red–green colour blindness** (about 8% of males and 0.4% of females — NCERT), **Duchenne muscular dystrophy**, **G6PD deficiency**, X-linked agammaglobulinaemia. Haemophilia spread through the royal families of Europe from **Queen Victoria** (a carrier) — called the "royal disease".

Features:
- **Much more common in males**.
- **Criss-cross inheritance** — affected father → carrier daughter → affected grandson (Morgan). Passes from the maternal grandfather through his daughter.
- **Affected father never passes it to his son** (sons get his Y).
- A female is affected only if her father is affected **and** her mother is a carrier (or affected).

| Normal woman (XX) × colour-blind man (XᶜY) | **All carriers** (XXᶜ), normal vision | **All normal** (XY) |
| **Carrier woman (XXᶜ) × normal man (XY)** | 1/2 normal (XX), 1/2 carriers (XXᶜ) — all see normally | **1/2 normal, 1/2 colour-blind** |
| Carrier woman (XXᶜ) × colour-blind man (XᶜY) | **1/2 carriers, 1/2 colour-blind** | 1/2 normal, 1/2 colour-blind |
| **Colour-blind woman (XᶜXᶜ) × normal man (XY)** | All carriers | **All colour-blind** (criss-cross) |

In the carrier × normal cross, the chance that **any child** is colour-blind = 1/4; the chance that a **son** is colour-blind = 1/2.

</div>

### X-linked dominant

Examples: **hypophosphataemic (vitamin-D-resistant) rickets**, **Rett syndrome**, incontinentia pigmenti, faulty tooth enamel (amelogenesis imperfecta, one form), fragile-X (with complications).

Features:
- An **affected father passes it to ALL his daughters and NONE of his sons** (XᴬY × XX → daughters XᴬX affected; sons XY normal).
- An affected heterozygous mother passes it to **half** her sons and **half** her daughters.
- More females than males are affected (about twice), but males are more severely affected.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q45 asked: "If the father in a family has a disease while the mother is normal, the daughters only inherited this disease and not the sons, name of this type of inheritance is — (A) sex-linked dominant (B) sex-linked recessive (C) autosomal recessive." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) sex-linked dominant.** Father's X goes to every daughter; if one copy is enough to cause disease (dominant), all daughters are affected and no son is.

### Y-linked, sex-limited and sex-influenced traits

- **Y-linked (holandric) genes** pass from **father to all sons** only: **SRY/TDF**, genes for spermatogenesis (AZF); the classic textbook example **hypertrichosis of the ear pinna (hairy ears)** — its Y-linkage is now doubtful, but exams still use it.
- **Sex-limited traits** — genes on autosomes, but expressed **in one sex only**: beard and moustache (men), milk production and breast development (women), plumage in some birds.
- **Sex-influenced traits** — autosomal genes **dominant in one sex and recessive in the other**: **pattern baldness** (B' dominant in males: Bb' male bald; female bald only if b'b'), horns in some sheep breeds.

### Pedigree analysis

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Square / circle | Male / female |
| Filled | Affected |
| Half-filled or dot inside | Heterozygous carrier |
| Horizontal line between two | Mating |
| **Double line** | **Consanguineous mating** (between relatives) |
| Vertical line down to sibship line | Offspring |
| Diamond | Sex unspecified |
| Diagonal line through | Dead |
| Arrow | Proband (the person through whom the family was studied) |

**Reading a pedigree:**
- **Autosomal dominant** — appears in **every generation**; affected child has an affected parent; males and females equally; two affected parents may have a normal child.
- **Autosomal recessive** — **skips generations**; two normal (carrier) parents can have an affected child; more common with consanguinity; both sexes equally.
- **X-linked recessive** — mostly **males**; through carrier mothers; **no father-to-son** transmission.
- **X-linked dominant** — affected father → **all daughters**, no sons.
- **Y-linked** — father → **all sons** only.
- **Mitochondrial** — affected **mother → all children**; affected father → none.

### Chromosomal disorders

Caused mostly by **non-disjunction** (failure of chromosomes or chromatids to separate in anaphase) → **aneuploidy** (gain or loss of one or a few chromosomes). Failure of cytokinesis after telophase → **polyploidy** (common in plants; lethal in humans).

|---|---|---|
| Monosomy | 2n − 1 | **Turner (45,X)** |
| Nullisomy | 2n − 2 | Lethal in animals; seen in wheat |
| Trisomy | 2n + 1 | **Down (47,+21)**, Klinefelter (47,XXY) |
| Tetrasomy | 2n + 2 | 48,XXXX |
| Double trisomy | 2n + 1 + 1 | 48,XXY,+21 |
| Triploidy | 3n | 69 chromosomes — miscarriage; seedless banana and watermelon in plants |

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Deletion (deficiency)** | A segment is lost (terminal or intercalary) | **Cri-du-chat** (5p−) |
| **Duplication** | A segment is repeated | **Bar eye in *Drosophila*** (duplication of region 16A of X; Bridges and Muller, 1936) |

## 8. Cytoplasmic (extra-nuclear) inheritance

Some genes are carried in **mitochondria** (all eukaryotes) and **chloroplasts/plastids** (plants). At fertilisation the **egg provides almost all the cytoplasm** of the zygote; the sperm contributes essentially only a nucleus (its few mitochondria are destroyed). So these traits are inherited from the **mother only** — **maternal (uniparental) inheritance**.

**Features:** reciprocal crosses give **different** results; **no Mendelian segregation ratios**; the trait persists even after repeated back-crossing to replace the nuclear genes; not mapped to any chromosome.

<div class="formula">

| Example | Discoverer | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Plastid inheritance in four o'clock plant (*Mirabilis jalapa*)** | **Carl Correns (1908–09)** | Seeds from a **green** branch give green plants, from a **white** branch white seedlings, from a **variegated** branch all three — **regardless of the pollen** |
| Plastid inheritance in maize (iojap), *Oenothera* | Rhoades; Renner | — |
| **Kappa particles in *Paramecium*** ("killer" trait) | **Tracy Sonneborn (1938)** | Killer strains carry kappa particles (endosymbiotic bacteria) in the cytoplasm; maintained only with nuclear gene K |
| **Petite mutants of yeast** | **Boris Ephrussi (1949)** | Defective mitochondria → small colonies |
| **Cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) in maize** | Rhoades (1933) | Mitochondrial gene → pollen sterile; used to produce **hybrid seed** |
| **Mitochondrial diseases in humans** | Wallace (1988) | **Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON)**, MELAS — passed by mothers to all children |
| Sigma factor in *Drosophila* (CO₂ sensitivity) | L'Héritier | Virus-like particle passed through egg cytoplasm |

</div>

**Maternal effect** is different: the **mother's nuclear genotype** decides the offspring's phenotype — e.g. **shell coiling in the snail *Limnaea peregra*** (Boycott and Diver, 1923; explained by Sturtevant, 1923): dextral (D) is dominant; a dd mother produces sinistral offspring even if they are Dd. It is nuclear inheritance delayed by one generation, not cytoplasmic inheritance.

A repeated question: learn it.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Sutton and Boveri proposed** the chromosome theory; **Morgan proved** it; **Bridges** confirmed it by non-disjunction.
- **Metacentric = V at anaphase**; telocentric = I/rod; sub-metacentric = L/J. Humans have **no telocentric** chromosomes. The BPSC key for "centromere near the middle" was **sub-metacentric** — see the PYQ note.
- **Polytene** — salivary glands of dipteran larvae (interphase, endoreduplication, puffs). **Lampbrush** — amphibian oocytes (diplotene, loops). Do not swap.
- Map distance: **1% recombination = 1 cM**; maximum RF = **50%**.
- **No crossing-over in male *Drosophila*** and female silkworm.
- **Grasshopper males are XO** (have one chromosome fewer than females). **Birds and butterflies: female heterogametic (ZW)**.
- **Honeybee drones** are haploid **males** from **unfertilised** eggs; **workers** are diploid sterile **females** from fertilised eggs; drones have **no sting**.
- In *Drosophila*, **XO is male** (sterile); in humans **XO is female** (Turner). The Y decides sex in humans but not in *Drosophila*.
- **XX male = SRY translocation**, not aneuploidy.
- Barr bodies = X − 1: Turner 0, Klinefelter 1.
- **Affected father → all daughters affected** = **X-linked dominant**; **affected father → no affected children but carrier daughters** = X-linked recessive.
- Haemophilia and colour blindness are **X-linked recessive**; **myotonic dystrophy, Huntington and Marfan are autosomal dominant**; sickle-cell, PKU, thalassaemia, albinism, cystic fibrosis are **autosomal recessive**.
- Sickle-cell: **glutamic acid → valine at position 6** of β-chain; **GAG → GUG** (on mRNA; on DNA coding strand GAG → GTG).
- **Down syndrome is trisomy 21**, not 23; Klinefelter is **47** (XXY), Turner **45** (X0).
- **Cytoplasmic inheritance** = genes in organelle DNA, reciprocal crosses differ. **Maternal effect** = mother's nuclear genotype.

## Quick revision

1. Sutton (grasshopper) and Boveri (sea urchin), 1902 — chromosome theory.
2. Morgan (Drosophila, 1910) — proof, linkage, crossing-over; Nobel 1933.
3. Waldeyer (1888) — "chromosome"; Tjio and Levan (1956) — human 2n = 46.
4. Metacentric (middle, V), sub-metacentric (L/J), acrocentric (near end, J), telocentric (end, I).
5. Human acrocentrics — 13, 14, 15, 21, 22 (and Y) — carry NORs/satellites.
6. Polytene — Balbiani 1881, salivary glands of Chironomus/Drosophila larvae, Balbiani rings.
7. Lampbrush — Flemming 1882, named by Rückert 1892, amphibian oocytes at diplotene.
8. Linkage groups = haploid number (Drosophila 4, human 23).
9. Morgan: y–w 1.3% (tight), w–m 37.2% (loose).
10. Sturtevant (1913) — first genetic map; 1% RF = 1 cM.
11. Interference = 1 − coefficient of coincidence.
12. Henking (1891) — X body in Pyrrhocoris.
13. XX–XY (humans), XX–XO (grasshopper), ZW–ZZ (birds, butterflies), haplodiploidy (honeybee).
14. Drosophila sex by X : A ratio (Bridges): 1.0 female, 0.5 male, 0.75 intersex.
15. SRY on Y (Sinclair 1990) → testis-determining factor.
16. Turtles — temperature-dependent sex; Bonellia — location-dependent.
17. XX male — SRY transferred to X; XY female — SRY lost (Swyer).
18. Barr body — Barr and Bertram 1949; Lyon hypothesis 1961; number = X − 1.
19. X-linked recessive — criss-cross; affected father → carrier daughters.
20. X-linked dominant — affected father → all daughters affected, no sons.
21. Sickle-cell: Glu → Val at β6 (GAG → GUG).
22. Down 47,+21 (Langdon Down 1866; Lejeune 1959); Klinefelter 47,XXY; Turner 45,X0.
23. Cri-du-chat — 5p deletion; Philadelphia chromosome — t(9;22), CML.
24. Correns (1909) — plastid inheritance in Mirabilis jalapa.
25. Cytoplasmic inheritance is maternal because the ovum gives most of the zygote's cytoplasm.
