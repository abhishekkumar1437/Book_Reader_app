Organelle questions are the single biggest block of cell-biology MCQs in TRE 1.0: Golgi cisternae number, sarcoplasmic reticulum, the enzyme of the mitochondrial membrane, amyloplasts, types of secondary lysosomes, actin filaments. NCERT class 11 gives the outline; this chapter adds the B.Sc. details (discoverers, marker enzymes, sub-types, sizes) that those questions came from.

> **In simple words:** Think of the cell as a factory. The **nucleus** is the head office with the plans (DNA). **Ribosomes** are machines that build proteins. The **rough ER** is the assembly line, the **Golgi** is the packing and dispatch department, **lysosomes** are the waste-disposal and recycling unit, **peroxisomes** handle dangerous chemicals, **mitochondria** are the power house, and the **cytoskeleton** is the steel frame and the road network.

## 1. Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)

- Seen first by **Porter, Claude and Fullam (1945)** under the EM; named "endoplasmic reticulum" by **Porter (1953)**. Garnier (1897) had earlier called the basophilic (RER-rich) cytoplasm **"ergastoplasm"**.
- A network of membrane-bound tubules, **cisternae** (flattened sacs) and **vesicles**, continuous with the **outer nuclear membrane**. It divides the cytoplasm into the **luminal** (inside ER) and **extra-luminal** (cytosol) compartments.

| Point | **Rough ER (RER)** | **Smooth ER (SER)** |
|---|---|---|
| Ribosomes | Attached on outer surface (by **ribophorins**) | Absent |
| Shape | Mostly flattened cisternae | Mostly tubules and vesicles |
| Function | **Protein synthesis** for secretion, membranes and lysosomes; first glycosylation (N-linked) | **Lipid and steroid synthesis**, glycogen metabolism, **detoxification** of drugs (cytochrome P450 in liver), Ca²⁺ storage |
| Abundant in | Cells making secretory proteins — pancreatic acinar cells, plasma cells, goblet cells, liver | **Steroid-making cells** (adrenal cortex, Leydig cells, corpus luteum), liver, muscle |

**Special forms of ER:**
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum** — a modified **smooth ER** in **striated (skeletal and cardiac) muscle**; stores **Ca²⁺** and releases it when an impulse arrives, starting contraction; the **Ca²⁺-ATPase pump** takes it back to relax the muscle.
- **Nissl granules (Nissl bodies)** — clumps of **RER** with free ribosomes in the cell body of **neurons**.
- **Myeloid bodies** — SER in retinal pigment epithelium.
- **Annulate lamellae** — stacks of membranes with pores in oocytes.
- **Microsomes** — fragments of ER (with ribosomes) obtained by centrifugation; not present in living cells.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q81 asked: "A modified form of smooth endoplasmic reticulum found in striated muscles is known as — (A) myofibril (B) sarcoplasmic reticulum (C) ergastoplasm." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (B).** Ergastoplasm is the old name for **rough** ER; a myofibril is a bundle of contractile filaments, not a membrane system.

## 2. Golgi complex (Golgi apparatus, dictyosome)

- Discovered by **Camillo Golgi (1898)** in nerve cells of the barn owl and cat using his **silver nitrate–osmium "black reaction"**; he called it the "**internal reticular apparatus**". Golgi shared the **1906 Nobel Prize** with Ramón y Cajal.
- In plants the separate stacks are called **dictyosomes**.
- Structure (NCERT): **flat, disc-shaped sacs (cisternae) of 0.5–1.0 µm diameter, stacked parallel to each other**. Each stack is associated with tubules and vesicles.
- **Number of cisternae per stack: usually 3 to 8** in most animal and plant cells (some protists have 20 or more; "4–8" is also commonly printed).
- **Polarity:** the **cis face (forming face)** is convex and faces the ER/nucleus; the **trans face (maturing face)** is concave and faces the plasma membrane. Materials enter at cis, pass through medial cisternae, and leave from the trans-Golgi network (TGN).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q80 asked: "The numbers of cisternae in a stack of Golgi complex found in most animals and plants are — (A) 1–5 (B) 2–6 (C) 3–8." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) 3–8.**

**Functions of the Golgi complex:**
1. **Packaging and sorting** of proteins coming from RER — sent to the plasma membrane, secreted outside, or sent to lysosomes (proteins for lysosomes are tagged with **mannose-6-phosphate**).
2. **Glycosylation** — the Golgi is the **main site for formation of glycoproteins and glycolipids** (completes N-linked sugars; adds O-linked sugars).
3. **Formation of lysosomes** (primary lysosomes bud from the trans face).
4. **Acrosome** formation in spermatids (spermiogenesis).
5. **Secretion** — zymogen granules, mucus, hormones; **cell plate** and wall polysaccharides (hemicellulose, pectin) in plants.
6. Formation of **yolk** (vitellogenesis) and **melanin granules**; contractile vacuole-like role in some protists.

Golgi is well developed in **secretory cells** (pancreas, goblet cells, plasma cells) and is the "**traffic police**" or "**post office**" of the cell.

## 3. Lysosomes — "suicide bags"

- Discovered by **Christian de Duve (1955)** by cell fractionation (he noticed acid phosphatase activity rising when the lysosome membrane broke); Nobel 1974. Novikoff later confirmed with EM.
- Single-membrane vesicles formed from the **Golgi**; contain about **40–60 acid hydrolases** — proteases (cathepsins), lipases, nucleases, carbohydrases, phosphatases — all active at **acidic pH about 4.5–5.0** (kept acidic by an **H⁺-ATPase pump**). **Marker enzyme: acid phosphatase.**
- Absent in mammalian RBCs; abundant in macrophages, neutrophils, kidney and liver cells.

### Polymorphism — types of lysosomes

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Primary lysosome** (also called **storage granule**, protolysosome, virgin lysosome) | Newly budded from Golgi; enzymes not yet used |
| **Secondary lysosome** (digestive vacuole / **phagolysosome**) | Primary lysosome **fused** with a vacuole containing material to be digested |
| — **Heterophagosome** (heterolysosome) | Contains material from **outside** the cell (after phagocytosis/pinocytosis) |
| — **Autophagosome** (autophagic vacuole, cytolysosome) | Contains the cell's **own** worn-out organelles |
| **Residual body** (tertiary lysosome, telolysosome) | Undigested remains after digestion; may be thrown out by exocytosis or stay as **lipofuscin "age pigment"** granules |

Note: a **phagosome** by itself is just the vacuole formed by phagocytosis — it is **not** a lysosome until a primary lysosome fuses with it (then it becomes a phagolysosome = secondary lysosome).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q84 asked: "Which of the following is not the type of secondary lysosomes? (A) Phagosomes (B) Residual bodies (C) Granules." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (D) More than one of the above.** "Granules" (storage granules) are **primary** lysosomes, and a plain phagosome has no lysosomal enzymes yet. Residual bodies are counted as the end stage of secondary lysosomes (many books call them tertiary), so the key treats (A) and (C) as "not secondary".

**Functions:** intracellular digestion (heterophagy), **autophagy** (turnover of old organelles; Yoshinori Ohsumi, Nobel 2016), **autolysis** (self-destruction of the cell — e.g. **tail of tadpole regresses** during metamorphosis), extracellular digestion (osteoclasts dissolving bone; **acrosome of sperm** is a modified lysosome with hyaluronidase), and defence (macrophages).

**Lysosomal storage diseases** (a missing enzyme leaves material undigested): **Tay–Sachs disease** (hexosaminidase A — GM2 ganglioside in neurons), **Pompe disease** (acid maltase — glycogen; type II glycogen storage disease), **Gaucher disease** (glucocerebrosidase), Hurler syndrome (mucopolysaccharides), I-cell disease (mannose-6-phosphate tagging fails).

## 4. Microbodies — peroxisomes and glyoxysomes

- **Microbodies** were described by **Rhodin (1954)** in mouse kidney cells; de Duve named **peroxisomes (1965)**. Single membrane, about 0.5–1.0 µm, often with a crystalline core (urate oxidase in many animals).
- **Peroxisomes** contain **oxidases** that produce **hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)** and **catalase** that breaks it (2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂). **Marker enzyme: catalase.**
- Functions: **β-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids**, detoxifying alcohol (in liver), making **plasmalogens** (myelin lipids) and bile acids; in plants, **photorespiration** (glycolate → glyoxylate, with chloroplast and mitochondrion).
- **Zellweger syndrome** — peroxisomes fail to form (very-long-chain fatty acids accumulate).
- Peroxisomes are **not** formed from the Golgi; they grow by taking up proteins from the cytosol and divide by fission (they have **no DNA**).
- **Glyoxysomes** (Beevers and Breidenbach, 1967) — special microbodies in **germinating fatty seeds** (castor, groundnut); run the **glyoxylate cycle** (enzymes **isocitrate lyase** and **malate synthase**) to convert stored fat into sugar. Animals have no glyoxysomes, so animals cannot make glucose from fatty acids.

## 5. Mitochondria — the power house

- First seen by **Kölliker (1857)** in insect flight muscle ("sarcosomes"); **Altmann (1890)** called them "bioblasts"; **Benda (1898)** named them **mitochondria** (*mitos* = thread, *chondrion* = granule). **Michaelis (1900)** stained them with **Janus green B**. Kennedy and Lehninger (1948–49) showed they are the site of Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.
- Size (NCERT): sausage- or cylinder-shaped, **0.2–1.0 µm (average 0.5 µm) diameter and 1.0–4.1 µm long**. Number varies: a few in some algae, about 1000–2000 in a liver cell, up to 500,000 in some giant amoebae; **absent in mature mammalian RBCs** and in prokaryotes.

**Structure:**
- **Two membranes**: the **outer membrane** is smooth and permeable (has **porins**); the **inner membrane** is folded into **cristae** (shelf-like in animals, tubular in protists and steroid-making cells) and is impermeable to most ions (contains **cardiolipin**).
- **Outer compartment** (inter-membrane space, about 6–10 nm) and **inner compartment** (**matrix**).
- **F₀–F₁ particles (oxysomes, elementary particles)** on the matrix side of cristae — discovered by **Fernández-Morán (1962)**; this is **ATP synthase** (F₁ head = the catalytic ATPase; F₀ = proton channel in the membrane).
- **Matrix** contains **circular, naked DNA**, **70S-type ribosomes** (55S in mammals), RNA, and enzymes of the **Krebs cycle** and fatty-acid β-oxidation.

### Marker enzymes of mitochondrial compartments (B.Sc. favourite)

| Compartment | Marker enzyme / content |
|---|---|
| **Outer membrane** | **Monoamine oxidase (MAO)**; also porins, acyl-CoA synthetase |
| Inter-membrane space | **Adenylate kinase**; cytochrome c (loosely on inner membrane) |
| **Inner membrane (cristae)** | **Cytochrome oxidase** (complex IV), **succinate dehydrogenase** (the only Krebs enzyme bound to membrane), **ATP synthase (F₀F₁-ATPase)**, NADH dehydrogenase, **choline dehydrogenase**, carnitine acyltransferase |
| **Matrix** | Krebs-cycle enzymes (citrate synthase, malate dehydrogenase etc.), pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, β-oxidation enzymes |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q82 asked: "Which of the following enzymes is found on the outer membrane of mitochondrial cristae? (A) ATPase (B) Choline dehydrogenase (C) Cytochrome oxidase." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (E) None of the above.** Cristae are folds of the **inner** membrane, and all three listed enzymes belong to the inner membrane. The marker of the **outer** mitochondrial membrane is **monoamine oxidase**, which was not offered. Read such stems word by word.

**Functions:** aerobic respiration (Krebs cycle in matrix, electron transport and oxidative phosphorylation on inner membrane), ATP production (**38 ATP** per glucose by old textbook count; **about 30–32** by modern count), part of urea cycle (liver), haem synthesis, Ca²⁺ storage, heat production in **brown fat** (thermogenin/UCP1 uncouples), **apoptosis** (releases cytochrome c).

**Semi-autonomous:** own DNA, ribosomes and protein-making system; divide by **fission**; but most of their proteins are coded by nuclear genes. **Endosymbiotic theory** — **Lynn Margulis (1967, 1970)**: mitochondria came from aerobic bacteria (α-proteobacteria) and chloroplasts from cyanobacteria engulfed by an ancestral cell. Evidence: double membrane, circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, fission, antibiotic sensitivity. **Mitochondrial DNA is inherited from the mother** (see [cytoplasmic inheritance](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/chromosomes-linkage-sex-determination-and-disorders)).

## 6. Plastids (for completeness — asked in TRE 1.0)

Plastids occur in plant cells and some protists (*Euglena*). They are classified by pigment:

| Plastid | Pigment / store | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Chloroplast** | Chlorophyll + carotenoids — photosynthesis | Green leaves |
| **Chromoplast** | Carotene, xanthophyll (yellow, orange, red) | Petals, ripe tomato (lycopene), carrot |
| **Leucoplast** (colourless) — storage | | |
| — **Amyloplast** | **Starch** | Potato tuber, rice and wheat endosperm |
| — **Elaioplast** (oleoplast) | **Oils and fats** | Oil seeds |
| — **Aleuroplast** (proteinoplast) | **Proteins** | Aleurone layer of maize grain |

Chloroplast: double membrane, **thylakoids** stacked as **grana** (light reaction), **stroma** (dark reaction/Calvin cycle; contains circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, starch grains, RuBisCO). About 5–10 µm long, 2–4 µm wide.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q83 asked: "The leucoplasts which function in the storage of starch are called — (A) amyloplasts (B) elaioplasts (C) aleuroneplasts." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A).**

> **Memory trick:** **Amylo**-plast = **amylum** (Latin for starch); **Elaio** = olive oil (Greek *elaion*); **Aleuro** = flour (protein-rich aleurone).

## 7. Ribosomes

- First seen as dense particles by **George Palade (1953)** — "Palade granules"; term "ribosome" by Richard Roberts (1958). Palade shared the 1974 Nobel with de Duve and Claude.
- **Non-membranous**, made of **rRNA + protein** (roughly 60:40 in prokaryotes, 50:50 in eukaryotes). **Mg²⁺** (about 1 mM or more) holds the two subunits together; at low Mg²⁺ they separate, at very high Mg²⁺ two 70S ribosomes join as a 100S dimer.

<div class="formula">

| Type | Whole | Large subunit (rRNAs) | Small subunit (rRNA) | Found in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **70S** | 70S | **50S** (23S + 5S rRNA, ~34 proteins) | **30S** (16S rRNA, ~21 proteins) | Prokaryotes, **mitochondria** (55S in mammals), **chloroplasts** |
| **80S** | 80S | **60S** (28S + 5.8S + 5S rRNA; 25S in yeast/plants) | **40S** (18S rRNA) | Eukaryotic cytoplasm (free or on RER) |

</div>

- **Free ribosomes** make proteins that stay in the cytosol; **ER-bound ribosomes** make secretory, membrane and lysosomal proteins (signal hypothesis — **Günter Blobel**, Nobel 1999).
- **Polysome (polyribosome)** — several ribosomes reading one mRNA at the same time (Rich, Warner and Knopf, 1963).
- **16S rRNA** of the small subunit pairs with the **Shine–Dalgarno sequence** of bacterial mRNA; **23S rRNA** is the **peptidyl transferase** — a **ribozyme**.
- Antibiotics act on 70S ribosomes: streptomycin and tetracycline (30S), chloramphenicol and erythromycin (50S). Cycloheximide inhibits 80S ribosomes.
- Eukaryotic rRNA (except 5S) is made in the **nucleolus**.

## 8. Centrosome, centrioles, cilia and flagella

- **Centrosome** — region near the nucleus containing **two centrioles** at right angles to each other, surrounded by amorphous **pericentriolar material**. Named by **Boveri (1888)**; centrioles described by Van Beneden (1876–87) and Boveri.
- **Centriole structure:** a cylinder of **nine peripheral triplet microtubules** (A, B, C tubules) and **no central tubule** — the **9 + 0 pattern**; a central proteinaceous **hub** connected to the triplets by radial **spokes** gives a **cartwheel** appearance (NCERT). About 0.15–0.25 µm wide and 0.3–0.5 µm long.
- Functions: organise the **spindle (aster)** in animal cell division; form **basal bodies** of cilia and flagella; form the **tail (axial filament)** of the sperm. Centrioles **duplicate in S phase** (some books: G1–S).
- **Absent in higher plants** (they form an anastral spindle) and in most fungi.

**Cilia and flagella (eukaryotic):** hair-like outgrowths covered by plasma membrane. Core = **axoneme** with **nine doublets of peripheral microtubules and a central pair** — **9 + 2 arrangement**. Central tubules are enclosed by a central sheath connected to the doublets by **radial spokes**; peripheral doublets are linked by **nexin** links; each doublet carries **dynein arms** (ATPase motor protein — sliding of doublets causes bending). They arise from a **basal body** (9 + 0 triplets, like a centriole).
- **Cilia** — short, many, beat like oars (trachea, fallopian tube, *Paramecium*). **Flagella** — long, few, whip-like (sperm, *Euglena*).
- **Kartagener syndrome** (primary ciliary dyskinesia) — dynein arms missing: chronic respiratory infections, male sterility (immotile sperm), situs inversus.
- Bacterial flagella are **structurally different** — made of **flagellin**, no microtubules, rotate.

## 9. Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments giving shape, mechanical support, movement of the cell and of organelles, and cell division. Term coined by Koltzoff (1928); Paul Wintrebert and later Porter studied it.

<div class="formula">

| Feature | **Microtubules** | **Microfilaments (actin filaments)** | **Intermediate filaments** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diameter | **about 25 nm** (hollow tube) | **about 7 nm** (thinnest; 5–7 nm) | **about 10 nm** (8–12 nm) |
| Protein | **α- and β-tubulin** dimers; **13 protofilaments** in a ring | **G-actin** monomers → **F-actin** (two strands twisted as a helix) | Various: **keratin** (epithelia, hair), **vimentin** (fibroblasts), **desmin** (muscle), **neurofilaments** (neurons), **lamins** (nuclear lamina), GFAP (glial cells) |
| Polarity | Yes (+ and − ends); grow from centrosome (MTOC) | Yes (+ "barbed", − "pointed") | No |
| Motor proteins | **Kinesin** (towards + end), **dynein** (towards − end) | **Myosin** | None |
| Functions | Spindle fibres, cilia, flagella, centrioles, axonal transport, cell shape | Muscle contraction (thin filament), microvilli core, cytoplasmic streaming, **amoeboid movement**, **cleavage furrow** (contractile ring), cell shape | Mechanical strength, anchor desmosomes, nuclear lamina |
| Drugs | **Colchicine** (stops polymerisation — arrests mitosis at metaphase), vinblastine, vincristine; **taxol** stabilises them | **Cytochalasin B** (blocks polymerisation), phalloidin (stabilises) | — |

</div>

**The thin (actin) filament of muscle** (NCERT class 11, Locomotion and Movement): "Each actin (thin) filament is made of **two 'F' (filamentous) actins helically wound** to each other. Each 'F' actin is a polymer of monomeric 'G' (globular) actins. **Two filaments of another protein, tropomyosin**, also run close to the F actins throughout their length. A complex protein **troponin** is distributed at regular intervals on the tropomyosin." The thick filament is a polymer of **meromyosin** (heavy meromyosin head + light meromyosin tail).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Q90 asked: "Each actin (thin) filament is made up of — (A) two F actins (B) two filaments of tropomyosin (C) troponin." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (D) More than one of the above** (A and B). The key follows the NCERT wording: two F-actin strands with two tropomyosin filaments running along them. Troponin is a complex attached at intervals on tropomyosin, which is probably why the key does not count it as one of the filaments "making up" the thin filament. See [Muscles, Skeleton and Movement](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/muscles-skeleton-and-movement) for the sliding-filament theory.

## 10. Vacuoles and other inclusions

- **Vacuole** — membrane-bound space with water, sap, excretory products; membrane = **tonoplast**. In plants the vacuole may occupy **up to 90%** of the cell volume; ions are pumped in **against** the gradient, so concentration in the vacuole is higher than in the cytoplasm.
- **Contractile vacuole** — osmoregulation and excretion in freshwater protists (*Amoeba*, *Paramecium*).
- **Food vacuole** — formed by phagocytosis in protists.
- **Sap vacuoles**, gas vacuoles (prokaryotes).

## 11. The nucleus

- Discovered by **Robert Brown (1831)**. Usually one per cell; absent in mature mammalian RBCs and sieve tubes; two in *Paramecium*; many in skeletal muscle.
- **Nuclear envelope** — two membranes with a **perinuclear space** of about **10–50 nm**; the outer membrane is continuous with the ER and carries ribosomes. **Nuclear pores** (about 70–100 nm, built of a **nuclear pore complex** of about 30 nucleoporins) control movement of RNA out and proteins in. The inner surface is lined by the **nuclear lamina** (lamins — intermediate filaments).
- **Nucleoplasm (karyolymph)** — contains **chromatin** and **nucleolus**.
- **Nucleolus** — not bounded by membrane; seen by **Fontana (1781)**; site of **rRNA synthesis** (by RNA polymerase I) and ribosome subunit assembly; formed around the **nucleolar organiser region (NOR)** — the **secondary constriction** of satellite chromosomes; larger in cells making much protein. It disappears in late prophase and reappears in telophase.
- **Chromatin** (Flemming 1879) — DNA + histone proteins + non-histone proteins + RNA. **Euchromatin** — loosely packed, lightly stained, **transcriptionally active**. **Heterochromatin** — densely packed, darkly stained, **inactive** (constitutive — e.g. centromeres, telomeres; facultative — e.g. the inactive X / Barr body). Terms by **Heitz (1928)**.

### Nucleosome — packing of DNA

<div class="formula">

- **Nucleosome** (Kornberg 1974 proposed; Oudet and colleagues named it 1975) = a core of **8 histone molecules (octamer: two each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4)** with DNA wrapped around it about **1.75 turns (about 146–147 bp)**. NCERT gives "**about 200 bp**" per nucleosome including linker DNA.
- **H1 histone** binds the **linker DNA** between nucleosomes.
- Under EM chromatin looks like **"beads-on-string"** (10 nm fibre) → coils into the **30 nm fibre (solenoid)** → loops on a scaffold → condensed chromosome at metaphase.
- **Histones are rich in basic amino acids lysine and arginine** — positively charged, they bind negatively charged DNA (phosphates).
- Prokaryotes lack histones; their DNA is held in loops by **NAPs/HU proteins** and **polyamines**.

</div>

Worked number (NCERT): Human diploid DNA has about **6.6 × 10⁹ bp**; at 0.34 nm per bp, length = 6.6 × 10⁹ × 0.34 × 10⁻⁹ m ≈ **2.2 m** — packed into a nucleus about 10 µm across.

### Cilia versus flagella (eukaryotic)

| Point | Cilia | Flagella |
|---|---|---|
| Length | Short (5–10 µm) | Long (up to 150 µm) |
| Number per cell | Many (hundreds) | One or few |
| Beat | Oar-like (effective + recovery stroke), coordinated (metachronal rhythm) | Undulating, whip-like |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum is smooth ER**, not rough ER. Ergastoplasm = rough ER.
- **Nissl granules are RER** in neurons — they are absent from the axon (and axon hillock).
- Golgi cisternae per stack: **3–8** (NCERT diameter 0.5–1.0 µm). Cis face toward ER; trans face toward plasma membrane.
- **Lysosomes come from the Golgi; peroxisomes do not** (they come from pre-existing peroxisomes/ER and import proteins).
- Primary lysosome = **storage granule**; residual body = remnant after digestion; a **phagosome without enzymes is not a lysosome**.
- Marker enzymes: lysosome — **acid phosphatase**; peroxisome — **catalase**; mitochondrial outer membrane — **monoamine oxidase**; inner membrane — **cytochrome oxidase**/succinate dehydrogenase; matrix — Krebs enzymes; Golgi — **glycosyl transferases** (thiamine pyrophosphatase); ER — **glucose-6-phosphatase**.
- **Cristae are folds of the inner membrane**, so "outer membrane of cristae" still means inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Succinate dehydrogenase** is the only Krebs-cycle enzyme on the inner membrane; all others are in the matrix.
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts have **70S** ribosomes even in eukaryotic cells.
- **Centrioles = 9 + 0 triplets**; **axoneme of cilia = 9 + 2 doublets**; **basal body = 9 + 0 triplets**.
- Microtubules are **thickest (25 nm)**, microfilaments **thinnest (7 nm)**, intermediate filaments in between (10 nm).
- **Colchicine** acts on **microtubules** (spindle), not on actin. **Cytochalasin** acts on **actin**.
- **Nucleolus has no membrane.** It is the site of **rRNA** (not mRNA) synthesis.
- Histone octamer has **8** proteins (not 9 — H1 is outside the core).
- Amyloplast stores **starch**, elaioplast **oil**, aleuroplast **protein**.

## Quick revision

1. ER — Porter, Claude, Fullam (1945); RER makes proteins, SER makes lipids and steroids and detoxifies.
2. Sarcoplasmic reticulum — modified SER in striated muscle, stores Ca²⁺.
3. Nissl bodies — RER in neuron cell body.
4. Golgi — Camillo Golgi 1898, silver staining; cisternae 0.5–1.0 µm, 3–8 per stack.
5. Golgi is the main site of glycoprotein and glycolipid formation; forms lysosomes and acrosome.
6. Lysosomes — de Duve 1955; acid hydrolases at pH ~5; marker acid phosphatase.
7. Primary lysosome → secondary (heterophagosome/autophagosome) → residual body.
8. Autolysis — tadpole tail regression.
9. Peroxisome — catalase, H₂O₂ metabolism; glyoxysome — glyoxylate cycle in fatty seeds.
10. Mitochondria — Benda 1898 named; 0.2–1.0 µm wide, 1.0–4.1 µm long.
11. Outer mitochondrial membrane marker — monoamine oxidase; inner — cytochrome oxidase.
12. F₀–F₁ particles (oxysomes) — ATP synthase on cristae (Fernández-Morán).
13. Endosymbiont theory — Lynn Margulis.
14. Amyloplast — starch; elaioplast — oil; aleuroplast — protein.
15. Ribosomes — Palade 1953; 70S = 50S + 30S; 80S = 60S + 40S; Mg²⁺ binds subunits.
16. 23S rRNA (peptidyl transferase) is a ribozyme.
17. Centriole — 9 + 0 triplets, cartwheel; cilia/flagella — 9 + 2.
18. Dynein arms move cilia; missing dynein → Kartagener syndrome.
19. Microtubule 25 nm (tubulin); microfilament 7 nm (actin); intermediate filament 10 nm.
20. Colchicine blocks microtubules; cytochalasin blocks actin.
21. Thin filament — two F-actins + two tropomyosin filaments + troponin.
22. Nucleolus — rRNA synthesis, at NOR (secondary constriction).
23. Nucleosome — histone octamer + ~146 bp DNA; ~200 bp with linker; H1 on linker.
24. Human DNA ≈ 2.2 m long (6.6 × 10⁹ bp × 0.34 nm).

## Practice with answers

1. Old name for rough ER? — **Ergastoplasm** (Garnier, 1897).
2. Which ER is abundant in cells of the adrenal cortex? — **Smooth ER** (steroid synthesis).
3. The forming face of the Golgi faces which organelle? — **ER/nucleus** (cis face).
4. Which organelle forms the acrosome of sperm? — **Golgi complex**.
5. Which tag sends proteins from the Golgi to lysosomes? — **Mannose-6-phosphate**.
6. Why are lysosomes called suicide bags? — **Their hydrolases can digest the cell itself when released (autolysis)**.
7. Lysosomal storage disease of neurons with GM2 ganglioside? — **Tay–Sachs disease**.
8. Marker enzyme of peroxisomes? — **Catalase**.
9. Which organelle runs the glyoxylate cycle? — **Glyoxysome** (in germinating fatty seeds).
10. Which enzyme marks the outer mitochondrial membrane? — **Monoamine oxidase**.
11. Which Krebs-cycle enzyme is bound to the inner mitochondrial membrane? — **Succinate dehydrogenase**.
12. What are oxysomes? — **F₀–F₁ ATP synthase particles on the cristae**.
13. Ribosomes of mitochondria are of which type? — **70S type** (55S in mammals).
14. rRNA of the 40S subunit? — **18S rRNA**.
15. Arrangement of microtubules in a centriole? — **9 + 0 triplets (cartwheel)**.
16. Motor protein that walks toward the plus end of microtubules? — **Kinesin**.
17. Intermediate filament of the nuclear lamina? — **Lamins**.
18. Drug that arrests cells at metaphase by blocking spindle formation? — **Colchicine**.
19. Composition of a histone octamer? — **Two each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4**.
20. Where is rRNA made in eukaryotes? — **Nucleolus** (5S rRNA is made outside it by RNA polymerase III).
