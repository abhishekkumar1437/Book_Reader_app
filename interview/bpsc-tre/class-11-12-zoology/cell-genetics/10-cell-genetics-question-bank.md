## 4. Attempt strategy under 1/3 negative marking

TRE 4.0 (as reported from Advt. 15/2026 — verify with the official notice) gives **+1 for a correct answer and −1/3 for a wrong one**, with option **E = "question not attempted"** that must be darkened to skip (leaving all five circles blank is also penalised by 1/3). That leaves **four** real answer choices (A–D).

<div class="formula">

**Expected score of guessing among the remaining choices:**

| Options you can confidently eliminate | Choices left | Expected marks per guess |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | 1/4 − (3/4 × 1/3) = **0** (no gain, high risk) |
| 1 | 3 | 1/3 − (2/3 × 1/3) = **+1/9 ≈ +0.11** |
| 2 | 2 | 1/2 − (1/2 × 1/3) = **+1/3 ≈ +0.33** |

</div>

**Rules for this book's questions:**
1. **Answer immediately** if it is a direct fact you know (names, stage names, ratios, enzyme roles) — these are 70–80% of cell/genetics questions.
2. **Guess only if you can eliminate at least one option**; otherwise darken **E**.
3. For "**More than one of the above**" options, test each of A, B, C separately as true/false before choosing D. In TRE papers D was correct surprisingly often (Q63, Q79, Q84, Q90, Q131).
4. For **numerical genetics**, write the Punnett square or branch method in the margin — 30 seconds of working avoids the classic traps (genotypic vs phenotypic ratio; primary vs secondary spermatocytes; 2/3 carrier probability).
5. Watch the **exact words**: "inner/outer membrane", "in/near the middle", "synapsis/crossing-over/chiasmata", "renin/rennin", "DNA fingerprinting / autoantibody fingerprinting".
6. When the key and NCERT may disagree (Q46, Q47 type), go with the **most textbook (NCERT) wording** unless you have seen BPSC's previous key on the same question.

More on the paper pattern: [Exam Pattern and Marking](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/exam-pattern-and-marking) and [How Questions Are Asked](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/exam-guide/how-questions-are-asked).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Genotypic 1 : 2 : 2 : 4 : 1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1** is offered as a decoy for the phenotypic 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
- **Chiasmata (diplotene)** is a decoy for crossing-over (pachytene).
- **Gyrase** is a decoy for helicase; **Pol III** is the real main replicase even though BPSC accepted "polymerase I" in Q46.
- **Morgan** is a decoy for Sutton–Boveri; **Watson–Crick** for anything about chromosomes.
- **Ergastoplasm** (RER) is a decoy for sarcoplasmic reticulum (SER).
- **Haemoglobin** looks "simple" but is conjugated.
- **Satellite DNA** is a decoy next to "repetitive DNA" — the official answer was repetitive DNA.
- **Codominance** also gives 1 : 2 : 1 — but the key for "both ratios 1 : 2 : 1" was incomplete dominance.
