This chapter covers the simplest animals and animal-like protists: single-celled Protozoa, the cellular-level sponges and the tissue-level Cnidaria and Ctenophora. NCERT class 11 gives one paragraph each; B.Sc. type studies (*Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Plasmodium*, *Sycon*, *Hydra*, *Obelia*, *Aurelia*) give the details TRE likes. TRE asks "which protozoan causes disease in man", "malaria is transmitted by", "larva of *Aurelia*", "canal system of *Spongilla*".

> **In simple words:** Protozoa are one-celled organisms that behave like animals — they move and eat food. Some cause disease (malaria, kala-azar, amoebic dysentery). Sponges are the simplest many-celled animals: water enters through tiny pores, flows through canals, and food is filtered out by collar cells. Cnidarians (*Hydra*, jellyfish, corals) have stinging cells and a sac-like gut with one opening; many have two body forms, the fixed **polyp** and the swimming **medusa**.

## 1. Protozoa — animal-like protists

**Protozoa** (Greek *protos* = first, *zoon* = animal) are unicellular eukaryotes, usually heterotrophic. NCERT places them in **Kingdom Protista** (Whittaker). The word protozoa was coined by **Goldfuss (1818)**; **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** first saw them (1670s, he called them "animalcules").

General features: single cell doing all life functions (acellular), naked or with a **pellicle**, locomotion by **pseudopodia, flagella or cilia**, nutrition holozoic/saprozoic/parasitic (some, like *Euglena*, also photosynthetic), osmoregulation by **contractile vacuoles** (in freshwater forms), asexual reproduction by **binary or multiple fission**, sexual by **syngamy or conjugation**, **encystment** to survive bad conditions.

### Classification (classical four groups — still used in exams)

| Group | Locomotion | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sarcodina (Rhizopoda)** — amoeboid | **Pseudopodia** | *Amoeba proteus*, *Entamoeba histolytica*, Foraminifera (*Elphidium*, *Globigerina*), Radiolaria | Foraminifera shells form chalk and "*Globigerina* ooze" on the sea floor |
| **Mastigophora (Flagellata)** — flagellated | **Flagella** | *Euglena*, *Trypanosoma*, *Leishmania*, *Giardia*, *Trichomonas*, *Trichonympha*, *Noctiluca* | *Trichonympha* lives in termite gut and digests wood (mutualism); *Noctiluca* makes the sea glow |
| **Sporozoa (Apicomplexa)** — spore-formers | **None** in adult (gliding) | *Plasmodium*, *Monocystis*, *Eimeria*, *Toxoplasma* | **All are parasites**; apical complex |
| **Ciliata (Ciliophora)** — ciliated | **Cilia** | *Paramecium*, *Vorticella*, *Balantidium coli*, *Stentor* | Two kinds of nuclei (macro- and micronucleus) |

### 1.1 *Amoeba proteus*

- Freshwater; shape always changing ("proteus" = Greek god who changed form).
- Cytoplasm: outer clear **ectoplasm** (gel, *plasmagel*) and inner granular **endoplasm** (sol, *plasmasol*).
- **Pseudopodia** (false feet; blunt **lobopodia**) form by **sol–gel change** of cytoplasm (sol–gel theory, **Mast 1926**). Used for movement and catching food.
- Feeding: **holozoic**, by **phagocytosis** (engulfing — "cell eating"); liquid drops by **pinocytosis**. Food is digested in the **food vacuole**; undigested matter leaves from any point.
- **Contractile vacuole** pumps out excess water — **osmoregulation** (absent in most marine and parasitic amoebae).
- Reproduction: **binary fission** (favourable times); **multiple fission** inside a cyst (sporulation) in bad times. *Amoeba* has no natural death — called "**immortal**".

**Parasitic amoebae:**

| Species | Lives in | Disease | Infective stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Entamoeba histolytica*** | Large intestine of man | **Amoebic dysentery (amoebiasis)**; liver abscess | **Four-nucleate cyst**, swallowed with food/water; **housefly** is a mechanical carrier |
| *Entamoeba gingivalis* | Mouth (gums) | Non-pathogenic, found in pyorrhoea | No cyst; spread by kissing |
| *Entamoeba coli* | Colon | Harmless commensal | Eight-nucleate cyst |

### 1.2 *Paramecium caudatum* — the "slipper animalcule"

- Freshwater ciliate, shaped like a slipper; body covered by a firm **pellicle** with many **cilia** (locomotion and feeding).
- **Oral groove → vestibule → cytostome (mouth) → cytopharynx → food vacuole**. Waste leaves at a fixed point, the **cytopyge** (anal spot).
- **Two contractile vacuoles** (anterior and posterior), each with radiating canals.
- **Trichocysts** — spindle-shaped sacs under the pellicle that shoot out threads for **defence and anchoring**.
- **Nuclear dimorphism:** a large **macronucleus** (polyploid, controls daily metabolism — "vegetative nucleus") and a small **micronucleus** (diploid, controls reproduction). *P. aurelia* has **two** micronuclei.
- **Avoiding reaction** — backs off and turns when it hits an obstacle (trial and error).
- Asexual: **transverse binary fission**. Sexual processes: **conjugation** (two individuals join temporarily at the oral groove and **exchange micronuclei**; no new individuals are made at once, but genes are mixed), **autogamy** (self-fertilisation within one individual), **endomixis**, **cytogamy**.
- *P. bursaria* is green because of symbiotic algae (zoochlorellae). "**Killer**" strains of *P. aurelia* carry **kappa particles** — a classic example of **cytoplasmic inheritance** (T. M. Sonneborn).

> **Exam angle:** "Which organelle of *Paramecium* is for defence?" — **Trichocyst**. "Exchange of micronuclei between two *Paramecia*" — **conjugation**. "Which nucleus controls metabolism?" — **macronucleus**.

### 1.3 *Euglena viridis* — the plant–animal link

- Freshwater flagellate, spindle-shaped, covered by a flexible protein **pellicle** (no cellulose wall) — allows "**euglenoid movement**" (metaboly).
- One long emergent **flagellum** from a flask-shaped **reservoir** (a second short flagellum stays inside).
- Red **eyespot (stigma)** shades the **paraflagellar body** (the true photoreceptor) — helps it swim towards light (**positive phototaxis**).
- Many **chloroplasts** with chlorophyll a and b; stores food as **paramylon** (a β-1,3 glucan, not starch).
- **Mixotrophic**: photosynthetic (holophytic) in light, **saprozoic** in darkness.
- **Longitudinal binary fission** (unlike *Paramecium*'s transverse fission).
- Because it has **chlorophyll (plant character)** and a **pellicle, flagellum and no wall (animal characters)**, *Euglena* is the classic **connecting link between plants and animals**. Botanists place it in Euglenophyta.

## 2. *Plasmodium* — the malaria parasite (most asked type study)

### Species of human malaria

| Species | Malaria type | Fever cycle | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***P. vivax*** | Benign tertian | Every **48 h** | Commonest in India; **relapses** from dormant liver stages (**hypnozoites**); Schüffner's dots in RBC |
| ***P. falciparum*** | **Malignant (pernicious) tertian** | 36–48 h | **Most dangerous**; cerebral malaria; infected RBCs stick to vessel walls |
| *P. malariae* | Quartan | Every **72 h** | Mild, long-lasting |
| *P. ovale* | Mild (ovale) tertian | 48 h | Relapses; mainly Africa |
| *P. knowlesi* | Quotidian | **24 h** | From macaque monkeys (zoonotic), South-East Asia |

### Hosts

- **Man = intermediate (secondary) host** — only **asexual** multiplication (**schizogony**) occurs here.
- **Female *Anopheles* mosquito = definitive (primary) host** — **sexual** phase (gamete formation and fertilisation) and **sporogony** occur here. The mosquito is also the **vector**.
- Male mosquitoes feed on plant juices and **cannot** transmit malaria.

### Life cycle in man

1. **Infective stage for man = sporozoite**, injected with the saliva of a biting **female *Anopheles***.
2. Sporozoites reach the **liver** within about 30 minutes → **pre-erythrocytic schizogony** in liver cells (discovered by **Shortt and Garnham, 1948**) → thousands of **merozoites (cryptozoites)**. In *P. vivax* and *P. ovale*, some stay dormant as **hypnozoites** → relapses months later (exo-erythrocytic cycle).
3. Merozoites enter **RBCs** → **erythrocytic schizogony** (also called the **Golgi cycle**): trophozoite → **signet-ring stage** → amoeboid trophozoite → **schizont** → 12–24 merozoites.
4. RBCs burst together, releasing merozoites and the toxin **haemozoin** (from digested haemoglobin) → **chill, high fever and sweating** — the typical malaria attack, repeating every 48 h (vivax).
5. After some cycles, some merozoites become **gametocytes** — **microgametocytes (male)** and **macrogametocytes (female)**. They do not develop further in man. **Gametocytes are the infective stage for the mosquito.**

### Life cycle in mosquito

1. Female *Anopheles* sucks blood with gametocytes; in its **stomach (midgut)** the microgametocyte forms about **8 flagellated microgametes** (**exflagellation**); the macrogametocyte forms one macrogamete.
2. **Fertilisation** → **zygote** (the only **diploid** stage; all other stages are haploid) → motile **ookinete**.
3. Ookinete bores through the stomach wall → **oocyst** on the **outer surface of the stomach wall**.
4. **Sporogony** inside the oocyst (the first division of the zygote nucleus is meiotic, so sporozoites are haploid) → thousands of **sporozoites**; the oocyst bursts and sporozoites move through the haemocoel to the **salivary glands**. This takes about **10–14 days**.
5. Next bite → sporozoites enter a new human.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Zoology Q122 asked: "Malaria is transmitted by (A) female *Culex* (B) male *Anopheles* (C) female *Anopheles*." **Official answer: (C) female *Anopheles*.** Female *Culex* transmits filaria (*Wuchereria*) and Japanese encephalitis; male mosquitoes do not suck blood.

> **Common mistake:** The mosquito is the **definitive** host (sexual phase), and man is the **intermediate** host — the reverse of what most students guess. The infective stage *for man* is the **sporozoite**; *for the mosquito* it is the **gametocyte**.

Other sporozoans: ***Monocystis*** (in **seminal vesicles of earthworm**), ***Eimeria*** (**coccidiosis** of poultry and rabbits), ***Toxoplasma gondii*** (toxoplasmosis; cat is definitive host; dangerous in pregnancy).

## 3. Phylum Porifera — sponges

**Porifera** = "pore bearers" (term by **Robert Grant**, 1836). Their animal nature was confirmed by **Ellis (1765)**, who saw water currents through them.

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Mostly **marine**, a few **freshwater** (*Spongilla*); **sessile**, attached to rocks.
- **Cellular level** of organisation; mostly **asymmetrical**.
- **Water canal system**: water enters through tiny pores, the **ostia**, into a central cavity, the **spongocoel**, and leaves through the **osculum**. This current brings food and oxygen and removes waste.
- **Choanocytes (collar cells)** — flagellated cells with a collar of microvilli; they line the spongocoel or the canals, beat water and trap food particles. **Digestion is intracellular.**
- Outer layer of flat **pinacocytes**; middle jelly (**mesohyl**) with amoeboid cells — **archaeocytes** (totipotent; form gametes and gemmules), **scleroblasts** (secrete spicules), **spongioblasts** (secrete spongin).
- **Skeleton** of **spicules** (calcareous or siliceous) and/or **spongin fibres** (a protein, like collagen).
- **Hermaphrodite** (eggs and sperm in the same individual); **internal fertilisation**; **indirect development** with a ciliated larva.
- Asexual reproduction by **fragmentation**, **budding** and **gemmules**; very high power of **regeneration**.
- No nerve cells, no muscles, no organs.

### Classes of Porifera

| Class | Skeleton | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcarea (Calcispongiae)** | **Calcareous** (CaCO₃) spicules | *Sycon*, *Leucosolenia* |
| **Hexactinellida (Hyalospongiae)** — glass sponges | **Siliceous six-rayed** spicules | ***Euplectella*** (**Venus's flower basket**), *Hyalonema* (glass-rope sponge) |
| **Demospongiae** | Siliceous spicules and/or **spongin** fibres | ***Spongilla*** (freshwater), ***Euspongia*** (**bath sponge**), *Cliona* (boring sponge), *Chalina* (dead man's fingers) |

- **Gemmules** — internal asexual buds of freshwater sponges (*Spongilla*): a mass of archaeocytes in a hard coat with spicules; survive winter or drought and hatch when conditions improve.
- **Larvae**: **amphiblastula** (*Sycon*, calcareous sponges) and **parenchymula** (most demosponges).
- *Euplectella* is a traditional wedding gift in Japan — a pair of shrimps lives trapped inside for life ("till death do us part").

## 4. Phylum Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

Name from **cnidoblasts (cnidocytes)** — stinging cells. Older name Coelenterata (hollow gut).

**Salient features:**
- Aquatic, **mostly marine**; sessile or free-swimming; **radially symmetrical**.
- **Tissue level**; **diploblastic** (ectoderm + endoderm with **mesoglea** between).
- **Cnidoblasts** on tentacles and body contain **nematocysts** (stinging capsules) — for **anchorage, defence and capture of prey**. A trigger hair, the **cnidocil**, fires the coiled thread.
- Central **gastrovascular cavity (coelenteron)** with a single opening, the mouth on the **hypostome**. Digestion **extracellular and intracellular**.
- **Nerve net** (diffuse), no brain.
- Some (corals) have a skeleton of **calcium carbonate**.
- Two body forms: **polyp** (cylindrical, sessile, mouth upward — *Hydra*, *Adamsia*) and **medusa** (umbrella-shaped, free-swimming, mouth downward — *Aurelia*). Polyps reproduce **asexually** (budding) and medusae **sexually**.

### Classes

| Class | Dominant form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrozoa** | Polyp usually dominant; medusa small, **with velum** (craspedote) | *Hydra*, ***Obelia***, ***Physalia*** (Portuguese man-of-war), *Velella* |
| **Scyphozoa** — true jellyfish | **Medusa** dominant; medusa **without velum** (acraspedote); polyp tiny | ***Aurelia*** (moon jelly), *Rhizostoma*, *Cassiopea* |
| **Anthozoa** — "flower animals" | **Only polyps, no medusa** | ***Adamsia*** (sea anemone), ***Pennatula*** (sea pen), ***Gorgonia*** (sea fan), ***Meandrina*** (brain coral), *Fungia*, *Corallium* (red coral) |
| Cubozoa | Box jellyfish | *Chironex* (sea wasp, very toxic) |

### 4.1 *Hydra*

- **Freshwater**, solitary **polyp**, about 1–2 cm; **no medusa stage**.
- Body: basal disc (attachment), column, **hypostome** with mouth and **6–10 hollow tentacles**.
- Movement: **looping and somersaulting**; feeds on small crustaceans (*Daphnia*, *Cyclops*).
- **Interstitial cells** — undifferentiated, can form any cell type → extraordinary **regeneration** (first studied by **Abraham Trembley, 1744**).
- Asexual: **budding** (favourable season). Sexual: in autumn; **testes near the hypostome, ovaries near the base**; fertilisation internal (in ovary); embryo forms a hard **cyst** and hatches directly into a young hydra — **no larva**.
- Green *Hydra* (*Chlorohydra viridissima*) carries algae (zoochlorellae) — mutualism.

### 4.2 *Obelia* — polymorphism and metagenesis

*Obelia* (sea fur) is a **marine, colonial hydrozoan** growing as a branched, plant-like colony on rocks and seaweeds. A chitinous **perisarc** covers the living **coenosarc**.

Zooids of the colony:
- **Gastrozooids (hydranths)** — feeding polyps with tentacles, inside a cup, the **hydrotheca**.
- **Gonozooids (blastostyles)** — reproductive polyps inside a **gonotheca**; they **bud off medusae** — no mouth, no tentacles.
- **Medusae** — free-swimming, sexual, with four radial canals, four gonads and statocysts.

**Polymorphism** = the presence of **more than one structural and functional type of individual (zooid)** in the same species. *Obelia* is dimorphic (polyp and medusa; and two kinds of polyps). Extreme polymorphism: ***Physalia*** — float (**pneumatophore**), feeding gastrozooids, stinging **dactylozooids**, reproductive gonozooids. Polymorphism means **division of labour**.

- Both polyp and medusa are **diploid** — so metagenesis is **not** the same as alternation of generations in plants (haploid gametophyte ↔ diploid sporophyte).
- *Hydra* has **no** metagenesis (no medusa); Anthozoa have **no** metagenesis (no medusa).

### 4.3 *Aurelia* (moon jellyfish) — life cycle

Medusa (sexes separate) → gametes → zygote → **planula** (ciliated larva) → settles → **scyphistoma** (small polyp) → **strobila** (polyp divides by transverse fission = **strobilation**) → **ephyra** (tiny eight-lobed young medusa) → adult medusa.

- Medusa has eight marginal sense organs, the **rhopalia (tentaculocysts)**, with statocysts for balance.
- Four **horseshoe-shaped gonads** — easily seen through the transparent bell.

### 4.4 Corals and coral reefs

- Corals are **anthozoan polyps** that secrete a **calcium carbonate exoskeleton**; colonies over thousands of years build **coral reefs**.
- Reef-building corals carry symbiotic dinoflagellate algae, **zooxanthellae**, in their tissues (food and colour). They need **warm (about 20–30 °C), clear, shallow, salty water** with light.
- Reef types: **fringing** (close to shore), **barrier** (separated by a lagoon — **Great Barrier Reef**, Australia, about 2,300 km, the largest), **atoll** (ring-shaped round a lagoon — **Lakshadweep**). Darwin explained atolls by **subsidence** (1842).
- Indian reefs: **Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh, Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep**.
- **Coral bleaching** — high sea temperature makes corals expel zooxanthellae; they turn white and may die.
- Examples: *Madrepora/Acropora* (stag-horn), *Fungia* (mushroom), *Meandrina* (brain), *Tubipora* (organ-pipe), *Corallium rubrum* (precious red coral).
- **Sea anemone *Adamsia* on the shell of hermit crab** (*Eupagurus*) — classic **mutualism** (anemone gets transport and food scraps; crab gets protection from the stinging cells).

## 5. Phylum Ctenophora — comb jellies

- Called **comb jellies** or **sea walnuts**; **exclusively marine**; radially (biradially) symmetrical, **diploblastic**, tissue level.
- Body bears **eight external rows of ciliated comb plates** — used for **locomotion** (Greek *ktenos* = comb).
- Digestion both extracellular and intracellular.
- **Bioluminescence** (the property of a living organism to emit light) is well marked.
- **No cnidoblasts**; instead sticky **colloblasts (lasso cells)** catch prey.
- Sexes **not separate** (hermaphrodite); reproduction **only sexual**; **external fertilisation**; **indirect development** (cydippid larva).
- Examples: ***Pleurobrachia***, ***Ctenoplana***, *Beroe*.

| Feature | Cnidaria | Ctenophora |
|---|---|---|
| Stinging cells | Cnidoblasts present | Absent (colloblasts instead) |
| Locomotion | Muscle contraction (medusa) | **Comb plates** (cilia) |
| Polymorphism | Present in many | Absent |
| Asexual reproduction | Present (budding) | Absent |
| Habitat | Marine + freshwater (*Hydra*) | **Only marine** |

## Traps and exceptions

- *Hydra* is a **freshwater** cnidarian with **no medusa** and **no larva**; most cnidarians are marine.
- *Euglena* stores **paramylon**, not starch.
- *Paramecium* divides **transversely**, *Euglena* **longitudinally**.
- In malaria, man is the **intermediate host**; mosquito the **definitive host**; zygote is the only diploid stage.
- Haemozoin (not haemoglobin) causes the malaria fever.
- *Trichonympha* is a **helpful** termite symbiont; *Leishmania* causes kala-azar.
- *Balantidium coli* is the only ciliate parasite of man.
- Freshwater sponge (*Spongilla*) has a **leucon** canal system and **gemmules**.
- Sponges are **hermaphrodite** but **cross-fertilisation** is usual.
- Metagenesis polyp and medusa are both **diploid** — unlike plant alternation of generations.
- Anthozoa (corals, sea anemones) have **no medusa**; Scyphozoa have a dominant medusa.
- Ctenophores are **hermaphrodite and have no asexual reproduction**.
