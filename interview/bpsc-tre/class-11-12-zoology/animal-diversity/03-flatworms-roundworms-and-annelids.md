"Worm" is not a scientific group. Three different phyla look worm-like: the flat, acoelomate **Platyhelminthes**, the round, pseudocoelomate **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)**, and the segmented, coelomate **Annelida**. NCERT gives their salient features; B.Sc. type studies give life cycles, hosts and segment numbers — exactly the material of one-line TRE questions ("intermediate host of *Fasciola*", "infective stage of *Wuchereria*", "clitellum of earthworm lies in segments").

> **In simple words:** Flatworms are flat like a ribbon, have no body cavity and are mostly parasites (liver fluke, tapeworm). Roundworms are round in section, have a "false" body cavity and separate sexes (*Ascaris*, filarial worm, hookworm). Annelids are made of ring-like segments, have a true body cavity and a closed blood system (earthworm, leech, *Nereis*). For parasites, remember three things: **where it lives, its intermediate host, and how it enters man**.

## 1. Phylum Platyhelminthes — flatworms

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Body **dorso-ventrally flattened** (flattened from top to bottom), hence "flatworms".
- Mostly **endoparasites** of animals including man; some free-living (*Planaria*).
- **Bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, acoelomate**, **organ level** of organisation.
- Parasitic forms have **hooks and suckers**; some absorb food directly through the body surface (tapeworms have no gut).
- Specialised cells called **flame cells** (protonephridia) help in **osmoregulation and excretion**.
- **Sexes not separate** (hermaphrodite); **fertilisation internal**; development through **many larval stages**.
- ***Planaria*** shows very high **regeneration** capacity.
- Digestive system incomplete (single opening) or absent; no circulatory or respiratory system.

### Classes

| Class | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Turbellaria** | Free-living, **ciliated epidermis**, rhabdites | ***Dugesia* (*Planaria*)** |
| **Trematoda** — flukes | Parasitic, leaf-like, **suckers** (oral and ventral), gut forked | ***Fasciola hepatica*** (liver fluke), ***Schistosoma*** (blood fluke), *Paragonimus* (lung fluke), *Clonorchis* (Chinese liver fluke) |
| **Cestoda** — tapeworms | Parasitic, ribbon-like, **no mouth or gut**, body of **proglottids** | ***Taenia solium***, ***T. saginata***, *Echinococcus granulosus*, *Hymenolepis nana*, *Diphyllobothrium latum* |
| Monogenea | Mostly ectoparasites, one host | *Polystoma* (urinary bladder of frog) |

**Body surface of parasitic flatworms** = a living syncytial **tegument** (not a dead cuticle); in tapeworms it bears **microtriches** that increase absorption. Parasites respire mostly **anaerobically** (very little oxygen in the gut).

### 1.1 *Fasciola hepatica* — sheep liver fluke

- Lives in the **bile ducts of the liver of sheep** (also goat, cattle; occasionally man). Causes **liver rot (fascioliasis)**.
- Leaf-shaped, 2–3 cm; **oral sucker** round the mouth and **ventral sucker (acetabulum)** for attachment.
- **Hermaphrodite**; both self- and cross-fertilisation occur.
- **Digenetic** — needs **two hosts**: **sheep (definitive/primary host)** and the snail ***Lymnaea*** (intermediate host; *L. truncatula* in Europe, *L. auricularia* in India for the Indian cattle fluke *F. gigantica*).

**Life cycle and larval stages:**

Egg (passes with bile into the sheep's faeces) → **miracidium** (ciliated, free-swimming; must find a snail within hours) → in snail: **sporocyst** → **redia** → **cercaria** (tailed; leaves the snail and swims) → **metacercaria** (cercaria loses its tail and **encysts on grass** — **infective stage**) → sheep eats grass → young fluke bores through the intestinal wall, reaches the liver and settles in the bile ducts.

- One miracidium gives rise to hundreds of cercariae because **sporocysts and rediae multiply asexually** — called **larval multiplication (polyembryony)**.
- Control: kill the snails (molluscicides, ducks), drain wet pastures, treat sheep.

### 1.2 *Taenia solium* — pork tapeworm

- Lives in the **small intestine of man** (definitive host); **pig is the intermediate host**.
- Length about **2–3 m** (can reach more); body: **scolex** (head) → **neck** (budding zone) → **strobila** of about 800–900 **proglottids**.
- **Scolex** has **four suckers** and a **rostellum** with a **double crown of hooks** (22–32) — an "**armed**" scolex.
- Proglottids: **immature** (near neck) → **mature** (both male and female organs — hermaphrodite) → **gravid** (at the end; only a **branched uterus** full of eggs). Gravid proglottids break off (**apolysis**) and pass out with faeces.
- No mouth, no gut — food is **absorbed through the tegument**. Excretion by flame cells.

**Life cycle:** Pig eats eggs with human faeces → **onchosphere (hexacanth embryo**, six hooks) hatches → bores into blood → settles in **muscles** → **cysticercus ("bladder worm")** → pork with cysticerci is "**measly pork**" → man eats **undercooked pork** → scolex evaginates and attaches → adult.

**Cysticercosis:** if a person swallows *T. solium* **eggs** (dirty food, or autoinfection), the human becomes the intermediate host and cysticerci form in the **brain** (**neurocysticercosis** → epilepsy), eyes and muscles. This is why *T. solium* is more dangerous than *T. saginata*.

| Feature | *Taenia solium* (pork) | *Taenia saginata* (beef) |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediate host | **Pig** | **Cattle** |
| Scolex | **Armed** (rostellum with hooks) | **Unarmed** (no hooks, no rostellum) |
| Length | 2–3 m | Longer, 5–10 m |
| Proglottids | ~800–900 | ~1000–2000 |
| Uterine branches in gravid proglottid | 7–13 on each side | 15–20 on each side |
| Human cysticercosis | **Yes** (dangerous) | Practically no |

Other tapeworms: ***Echinococcus granulosus*** — tiny dog tapeworm; man becomes the intermediate host and develops **hydatid cysts** in liver and lungs. ***Hymenolepis nana*** — dwarf tapeworm; **no intermediate host needed**. ***Diphyllobothrium latum*** — broad fish tapeworm, the largest human tapeworm; causes **vitamin B₁₂ deficiency anaemia**.

### 1.3 *Schistosoma* — blood fluke

- Lives in the **veins** (mesenteric or urinary-bladder veins) of man. Disease: **schistosomiasis (bilharziasis)** — named after **Theodor Bilharz (1851)**.
- **Sexes separate** — the big **exception** among flatworms. The slender female lives permanently in a groove of the male body, the **gynaecophoric canal**.
- Intermediate host: **freshwater snails** (*Bulinus*, *Biomphalaria*, *Oncomelania*).
- Larvae: miracidium → sporocyst → daughter sporocysts (no redia) → **fork-tailed cercaria**, which **penetrates the skin directly** when people bathe or work in water (no metacercaria).
- *S. haematobium* — urinary schistosomiasis (blood in urine, linked to bladder cancer); *S. mansoni* — intestinal; *S. japonicum* — oriental.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) class 9–10 Science paper asked: "Which of the following is a human 'blood fluke'? — (A) *Enterobius vermicularis* (B) *Schistosoma mansoni* (C) *Wuchereria bancrofti*." **(B) *Schistosoma mansoni*.** *Enterobius* (pinworm) and *Wuchereria* (filarial worm) are **nematodes**, not flukes. (No key for that paper in our files; the answer is standard.)

| Feature | *Fasciola* | *Taenia solium* | *Schistosoma* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class | Trematoda | Cestoda | Trematoda |
| Site | Bile ducts (sheep) | Small intestine (man) | Blood veins (man) |
| Sexes | Hermaphrodite | Hermaphrodite | **Separate** |
| Gut | Forked | **Absent** | Present |
| Intermediate host | Snail *Lymnaea* | Pig | Snail |
| Infective stage to main host | **Metacercaria** on grass | **Cysticercus** in pork | **Cercaria** through skin |

## 2. Phylum Aschelminthes (Nematoda) — roundworms

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Body **circular in cross-section** — "roundworms".
- Free-living (aquatic, terrestrial) or **parasitic in plants and animals**.
- **Organ-system level**; bilaterally symmetrical, **triploblastic, pseudocoelomate**.
- Alimentary canal **complete** with a well-developed **muscular pharynx**.
- An **excretory tube** removes body waste through the **excretory pore**.
- **Sexes separate (dioecious)**; **sexual dimorphism** — females longer than males (male has a **curved tail**).
- **Fertilisation internal**; development **direct** (young resemble adults) or **indirect**.

**B.Sc. points:**
- Body covered by a tough collagen **cuticle**; growth needs **four moults**.
- Body-wall muscles are **only longitudinal** (no circular muscles) → typical **whip-like (thrashing)** movement.
- **No cilia or flagella anywhere** — even sperm are **amoeboid**.
- Excretory system **H-shaped** (canal type) or of gland cells (**renette cells**) in free-living forms; no flame cells.
- **Eutely (cell constancy)** — adults have a fixed number of cells. ***Caenorhabditis elegans*** (959 somatic cells in the adult hermaphrodite) is a model animal: cell lineage and apoptosis (**Sydney Brenner and colleagues, Nobel 2002**) and **RNA interference** (**Fire and Mello, Nobel 2006**).
- Plant parasites: root-knot nematode ***Meloidogyne incognita*** (NCERT biotech — RNAi-based resistant tobacco). Most plant-eating nematodes are **root parasites**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) class 9–10 Science paper asked: "The majority of phytophagous nematodes are — (A) root parasites (B) stem parasites (C) tissue parasites." Phytophagous = plant-eating. Most attack **roots** (root-knot, cyst and lesion nematodes) — expected answer **(A)**. (No key in our files.)

### 2.1 *Ascaris lumbricoides*

- **Largest intestinal nematode of man**: female about 20–35 cm, male 15–30 cm with a **curved tail** and two **penial spicules**.
- Mouth with **three lips** (one dorsal, two ventro-lateral).
- A female lays about **2 lakh eggs per day**; eggs have a thick, **mammillated** (bumpy) protein coat and need about 2–3 weeks in soil to become infective.
- **Life cycle (single host):** swallowed embryonated egg → larva hatches in the small intestine → bores into the wall → blood → **liver → heart → lungs** (moults twice; can cause pneumonia-like symptoms, **Loeffler's syndrome**) → climbs the trachea → swallowed again → becomes adult in the small intestine.
- Effects: abdominal pain, malnutrition, **intestinal obstruction**. Its body makes **anti-enzymes** so it is not digested by the host.

### 2.2 *Wuchereria bancrofti* — filarial worm

- Adults live in the **lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes**; they block lymph flow → **chronic inflammation and swelling** of legs, scrotum (hydrocele), arms, breasts → **elephantiasis**.
- Females give birth to tiny larvae, the **microfilariae**, which circulate in the peripheral blood **at night** (**nocturnal periodicity**, roughly 10 pm–2 am) — blood is sampled at night for diagnosis.
- **Vector: female *Culex*** (*C. quinquefasciatus*). In the mosquito, microfilariae develop into infective **L3 larvae** that enter man at the next bite. Man = definitive host; mosquito = intermediate host.
- *Brugia malayi* also causes lymphatic filariasis (vector *Mansonia*).
- Control: **mass drug administration (MDA)** with **DEC (diethylcarbamazine)**, albendazole and ivermectin. **Bihar is one of the most filaria-endemic states.**

### 2.3 *Enterobius* and *Ancylostoma*

- *Enterobius*: the gravid female crawls out of the anus **at night** to lay eggs → itching (**pruritus ani**) → eggs reach the mouth through fingers → **autoinfection**. Diagnosis by **sticky (cellophane/NIH) tape swab** of the perianal skin. Commonest worm of children worldwide.
- *Ancylostoma*: attaches to the intestinal wall with a **buccal capsule** bearing **teeth** (two pairs of ventral teeth), secretes an **anticoagulant** and sucks blood → **anaemia**, protein loss. Male has a **copulatory bursa**. Eggs hatch in soil → **rhabditiform** larva → **filariform larva** (infective) → enters through skin of bare feet. **Wearing footwear** prevents it.

> **Memory trick:** "**A**scaris — **A**te eggs; **W**uchereria — **W**inged insect (*Culex*); **A**ncylostoma — **A**rrives through feet; **E**nterobius — **E**ggs at the **E**xit (anus) at night."

## 3. Phylum Annelida — segmented worms

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Aquatic (marine and freshwater) or terrestrial; free-living, sometimes parasitic.
- **Organ-system level**; bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, **metamerically segmented** and **coelomate** (Latin *annulus* = little ring).
- **Longitudinal and circular muscles** help in locomotion. Aquatic forms like ***Nereis*** have lateral appendages, **parapodia**, for swimming.
- **Closed circulatory system**.
- **Nephridia** (singular nephridium) for **osmoregulation and excretion**.
- Nervous system: paired **ganglia** connected by lateral nerves to a **double ventral nerve cord**.
- *Nereis* is **dioecious**; earthworms and leeches are **monoecious** (hermaphrodite). Reproduction sexual.
- Examples: ***Nereis***, ***Pheretima*** (earthworm), ***Hirudinaria*** (blood-sucking leech).

B.Sc. additions: coelom is a **schizocoel**; **chitinous setae (chaetae)** for locomotion; blood with **haemoglobin dissolved in plasma** (or erythrocruorin/chlorocruorin in some); typical marine larva **trochophore**.

### Classes

| Class | Setae / parapodia | Head | Sexes | Development | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Polychaeta** | **Many setae on parapodia** | Distinct (eyes, tentacles, palps) | **Separate** | Indirect, **trochophore** larva | ***Nereis*** (clam worm/ragworm), *Aphrodite* (sea mouse), *Arenicola* (lugworm), *Chaetopterus* (glows), *Eunice* (palolo worm) |
| **Oligochaeta** | **Few setae, no parapodia** | Not distinct | **Hermaphrodite**; clitellum permanent | **Direct**, in cocoon | ***Pheretima***, *Lumbricus*, *Tubifex* (sludge worm), *Eisenia fetida* (vermicompost) |
| **Hirudinea** | **No setae** (mostly), no parapodia | Not distinct; **two suckers** | Hermaphrodite; clitellum in breeding season | Direct, in cocoon | ***Hirudinaria granulosa*** (Indian cattle leech), *Hirudo medicinalis* (medicinal leech) |

### 3.1 *Nereis* (clam worm)

- Marine, burrows in sand and mud near the shore; body of 80–120 segments.
- Head: **prostomium** (two pairs of eyes, a pair of tentacles, a pair of palps) + **peristomium** (four pairs of tentacular cirri).
- **Eversible pharynx** with a pair of chitinous jaws — predatory.
- **Parapodia** are **biramous** (two lobes: dorsal **notopodium** and ventral **neuropodium**), each with bundles of setae; they help in swimming, crawling and **respiration**.
- **Dioecious**; in the breeding season the posterior body changes into a swimming sexual form, the **heteronereis** (**epitoky**); gametes are shed in water (**external fertilisation**) → **trochophore** larva.

### 3.2 Earthworm — *Pheretima posthuma* (Indian earthworm)

**External features:**
- Long, cylindrical, reddish-brown; about **100–120 segments (metameres)**.
- **Peristomium** — 1st segment, bears the mouth; **prostomium** — a lobe over the mouth that works as a wedge to open cracks in soil.
- **Clitellum** — a dark glandular band over segments **14, 15 and 16**; it divides the body into **pre-clitellar, clitellar and post-clitellar** regions and secretes the **cocoon**.
- **Setae** — S-shaped chitinous bristles in a ring round the middle of each segment, **except the first, the last and the clitellar segments**; used in locomotion.
- Ventral openings: **four pairs of spermathecal apertures** in the intersegmental grooves **5/6, 6/7, 7/8 and 8/9**; a **single female genital pore** on segment **14**; **one pair of male genital pores** on segment **18**; genital papillae on segments 17 and 19.
- Numerous minute **nephridiopores**; **dorsal pores** from groove 12/13 onwards (coelomic fluid keeps the skin moist).

**Internal organisation:**

| System | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Digestive** | Mouth → buccal cavity (segments 1–3) → **muscular pharynx** (4) → oesophagus (5–7) → **muscular gizzard (8–9)** grinds soil and leaves → stomach (9–14, with **calciferous glands** that neutralise humic acid) → intestine (from 15). A pair of **intestinal caeca** on segment **26**; after segment 26 (except the last 23–25 segments) the intestine has an internal dorsal fold, the **typhlosole**, which increases absorption |
| **Circulatory** | **Closed**; dorsal vessel pumps forward; **four pairs of hearts** in segments **7, 9, 12 and 13**; **blood glands** on segments 4, 5 and 6 make blood cells and **haemoglobin, which is dissolved in plasma** |
| **Respiratory** | No special organ — gas exchange through the **moist skin** |
| **Excretory** | **Nephridia** of three types: **septal** (on septa from segment 15 to the end, open into the intestine), **integumentary** (on body wall from segment 3 to the end, open on the skin), **pharyngeal** (three paired tufts in segments 4, 5, 6). Mainly **ureotelic** (also some ammonia) |
| **Nervous** | **Ganglia** arranged segment-wise on the **double ventral nerve cord**; brain (cerebral ganglia) in segment 3 with a nerve ring round the pharynx |
| **Sense** | **No eyes**; light- and touch-sensitive cells in the skin, chemoreceptors in the buccal cavity |
| **Reproductive** | **Hermaphrodite**; **two pairs of testes** in segments **10 and 11**; **one pair of ovaries** at the septum between segments **12 and 13**; **protandrous** (male organs mature first) → **cross-fertilisation** |

**Reproduction:** two worms exchange sperm during mating (stored in each other's spermathecae). The clitellum secretes a **cocoon** into which eggs and the partner's sperm are passed; fertilisation and development occur inside the cocoon in the soil. **No larva — direct development.** After about 3 weeks each cocoon releases **2 to 20 young worms (average 4)**.

**Economic importance:** "**friends of farmers**" — they make burrows that **aerate** soil and help roots, mix soil layers, and their excreta (**worm castings**) enrich soil — used in **vermicomposting** (*Eisenia fetida*, *Eudrilus eugeniae*). Also used as **fishing bait**. Charles Darwin wrote a whole book on earthworms (**1881**).

### 3.3 Leech — *Hirudinaria granulosa*

- Indian **cattle leech**, in freshwater ponds; **sanguivorous** (blood-sucking) **ectoparasite** of cattle, fish and man.
- Body has a **fixed number of 33 segments** (some texts count 34), each externally divided into rings (**annuli**) — the annuli are **not** true segments.
- **Anterior sucker** (round the mouth) and **posterior sucker**; locomotion by **looping** with suckers and by swimming.
- **Three jaws** with teeth make a **Y-shaped (triradiate) wound**; salivary glands secrete **hirudin**, an **anticoagulant**. The large **crop** with side chambers stores blood, so a leech can live for months after one meal.
- Coelom reduced, filled with **botryoidal tissue**; **17 pairs of nephridia**.
- Hermaphrodite and protandrous; clitellum (segments 10–12 region) appears only in the breeding season; cocoon formation; direct development.
- Medicinal leech ***Hirudo medicinalis*** is still used in **microsurgery** (to drain blood from re-attached fingers/flaps).

| Feature | *Nereis* | Earthworm | Leech |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class | Polychaeta | Oligochaeta | Hirudinea |
| Habitat | Marine | Moist soil | Fresh water |
| Setae / parapodia | Many setae on **parapodia** | Few setae, no parapodia | **None** |
| Segments | Variable (80–120) | 100–120 | **Fixed 33** |
| Sexes | **Separate** | Hermaphrodite | Hermaphrodite |
| Clitellum | Absent | Permanent (14–16) | Temporary |
| Larva | **Trochophore** | None | None |
| Special | Heteronereis | Typhlosole, gizzard | **Hirudin**, suckers |

## 4. The three worm phyla side by side

| Feature | Platyhelminthes | Aschelminthes | Annelida |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape in section | **Flat** (dorso-ventral) | **Round** | Round, **ringed** |
| Body cavity | **None** (acoelomate) | **Pseudocoel** | **True coelom** (schizocoel) |
| Gut | Incomplete (one opening) or absent | **Complete**, muscular pharynx | Complete, with gizzard etc. |
| Segmentation | None (pseudo in tapeworm) | None | **True metamerism** |
| Excretion | **Flame cells** | Excretory tube / renette cells | **Nephridia** |
| Circulation | None | None | **Closed** |
| Sexes | Mostly **hermaphrodite** | **Separate** (dimorphic) | Separate (*Nereis*) or hermaphrodite (earthworm, leech) |
| Muscles | Circular + longitudinal | **Longitudinal only** | Circular + longitudinal |
| Typical example | *Fasciola*, *Taenia* | *Ascaris* | *Pheretima* |

### Adaptations of endoparasites (flukes, tapeworms, nematodes)

- **Attachment organs** — suckers, hooks, rostellum, buccal capsule with teeth.
- **Protective covering** — tegument or tough cuticle; **anti-enzymes** so the host cannot digest them.
- **Reduced organs** — no locomotory organs; gut reduced or absent (tapeworm absorbs food through its surface).
- **Anaerobic respiration** — little oxygen in the host gut.
- **Huge reproductive output** — hermaphroditism, self-fertilisation possible, lakhs of eggs a day, **larval multiplication**.
- **Intermediate hosts and resistant stages** (cysts, eggs with thick shells) for transfer to new hosts.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Schistosoma** is a flatworm with **separate sexes** — every other example in this phylum is hermaphrodite.
- ***Taenia*** has **no digestive system**; *Fasciola* has a forked gut.
- **Man is the intermediate host** in **cysticercosis** (eggs of *T. solium* swallowed) and in **hydatid disease** (*Echinococcus*).
- *Hymenolepis nana* completes its cycle **without** an intermediate host.
- **Infective stage of *Fasciola* = metacercaria**, of *Taenia* = cysticercus, of *Ascaris* = **embryonated egg**, of *Ancylostoma* = **filariform larva**, of *Wuchereria* (for man) = **L3 larva** from the mosquito.
- *Wuchereria* is spread by **female *Culex***, not *Anopheles* (in India).
- **Nematode sperm are amoeboid** — no flagellum.
- **Annuli of the leech** are external rings, not segments; the leech has 33 true segments.
- **Earthworm** has no special respiratory organ and **no eyes**; its haemoglobin is **dissolved in plasma**, not in RBCs.
- **Earthworm is hermaphrodite but cross-fertilises** (protandry).
- *Nereis* is **dioecious**; earthworm and leech are monoecious.
- "Pinworm", "threadworm" and "seat worm" are all names of ***Enterobius***.

## Practice with answers

1. Why are flatworms called acoelomate? — **The space between body wall and gut is filled with parenchyma; there is no body cavity**.
2. Which stage of *Fasciola* leaves the snail? — **Cercaria**.
3. What is polyembryony in *Fasciola*? — **Asexual multiplication of larvae (sporocyst, redia) inside the snail**.
4. How does man get *Taenia solium*? — **By eating undercooked pork containing cysticerci**.
5. Which tapeworm causes vitamin B₁₂ deficiency? — ***Diphyllobothrium latum*** (fish tapeworm).
6. Which tapeworm forms hydatid cysts in man? — ***Echinococcus granulosus***.
7. How does the cercaria of *Schistosoma* enter man? — **By penetrating the skin in water**.
8. Name two features found in no nematode. — **Circular muscles and cilia/flagella**.
9. What is nocturnal periodicity? — **Microfilariae appear in peripheral blood at night**.
10. Which nematode passes through the lungs during its life cycle? — ***Ascaris*** (also hookworm).
11. Which worm causes iron-deficiency anaemia by sucking blood? — ***Ancylostoma duodenale***.
12. Which nematode was eradicated from India in 2000? — **Guinea worm (*Dracunculus*)**.
13. In which segments are the testes of *Pheretima*? — **10 and 11**.
14. Function of calciferous glands? — **Neutralise humic acid of the food (soil)**.
15. Which nephridia of earthworm open into the intestine? — **Septal (and pharyngeal) nephridia**.
16. Earthworm respires through? — **Moist skin**.
17. How many young emerge from an earthworm cocoon on average? — **About four** (range 2–20).
18. Which annelid class has no setae and two suckers? — **Hirudinea** (leeches).
19. What is heteronereis? — **Sexually mature swimming form of *Nereis*** (epitoky).
20. Which annelid is used in vermicomposting? — ***Eisenia fetida*** (red wiggler).
