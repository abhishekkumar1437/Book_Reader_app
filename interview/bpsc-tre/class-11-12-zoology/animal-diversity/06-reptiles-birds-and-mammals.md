Reptiles, birds and mammals are the **amniotes** — vertebrates whose embryo develops inside an **amnion**, which freed them from water for reproduction. NCERT gives the salient features and examples; the B.Sc. syllabus adds orders, skull types, flight adaptations, migration and the three mammalian subclasses. TRE asks "egg-laying mammal", "four-chambered heart in reptiles", "connecting link between reptiles and birds", "national aquatic animal".

> **In simple words:** **Reptiles** have dry scaly skin and lay shelled eggs on land; they are cold-blooded. **Birds** are feathered, warm-blooded flyers with hollow bones and a beak. **Mammals** have hair and feed their young on milk; most give birth to live young. Only two kinds of mammals (platypus and echidna) still lay eggs; kangaroos and koalas raise their tiny young in a pouch; all others have a placenta.

## 1. Class Reptilia — creeping vertebrates

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Name from their **creeping or crawling** mode of movement (Latin *repere* = to creep); mostly **terrestrial**.
- Body covered by **dry, cornified skin** with **epidermal scales or scutes**.
- **No external ear opening**; a **tympanum** represents the ear.
- Limbs, when present, are **two pairs**.
- **Heart usually three-chambered, but four-chambered in crocodiles**.
- **Poikilotherms** (cold-blooded).
- Snakes and lizards **shed their scales as a skin cast** (moulting/ecdysis).
- Sexes separate; **internal fertilisation**; **oviparous**; **direct development**.
- Examples: ***Chelone*** (turtle), ***Testudo*** (tortoise), ***Chameleon*** (tree lizard), ***Calotes*** (garden lizard), ***Crocodilus*** (crocodile), ***Alligator***, ***Hemidactylus*** (wall lizard); poisonous snakes — ***Naja*** (cobra), ***Bungarus*** (krait), ***Vipera*** (viper).

**B.Sc. points:** lungs only (no gills at any stage); **cleidoic** (closed, shelled) **amniotic egg**; **uricotelic** (land forms); **metanephric** kidneys; skull **monocondylic** (one occipital condyle); **12 pairs of cranial nerves**; **Jacobson's (vomeronasal) organ** for smell in lizards and snakes (the forked tongue carries scent to it); in many turtles and all crocodiles the **sex of the young is fixed by nest temperature** (temperature-dependent sex determination).

### Orders of living reptiles

| Order | Key features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Chelonia (Testudines)** | Body in a bony **shell** (carapace above, plastron below); **no teeth** (horny beak); anapsid skull | ***Chelone*** (green turtle), ***Testudo*** (land tortoise), olive ridley *Lepidochelys*, Ganga soft-shell turtles |
| **Rhynchocephalia** | Primitive; **parietal "third eye"** on top of head | ***Sphenodon*** (tuatara, New Zealand) — **living fossil**, only surviving member |
| **Squamata — lizards (Lacertilia)** | Movable eyelids, external ear opening usually, limbs usually present, **tail autotomy** (can break off and regrow) | ***Hemidactylus***, ***Calotes***, ***Chamaeleo***, ***Draco*** (flying lizard), *Varanus* (monitor), ***Heloderma*** (Gila monster — **only venomous lizards**) |
| **Squamata — snakes (Ophidia/Serpentes)** | **Limbless**, **no eyelids** (transparent spectacle), **no external ear**, **no urinary bladder**, one functional lung, jaws loosely joined (swallow big prey) | ***Naja***, ***Bungarus***, ***Vipera/Daboia***, ***Python***, *Ptyas* (rat snake), *Hydrophis* (sea snake) |
| **Crocodilia** | **Four-chambered heart** (with the foramen of Panizza linking the two systemic arches), thecodont teeth, secondary palate | ***Crocodylus*** (mugger, saltwater croc), ***Alligator***, ***Gavialis gangeticus*** (gharial) |

### Venomous snakes of India

- **"Big Four"** responsible for most snakebite deaths in India: **spectacled cobra (*Naja naja*)**, **common krait (*Bungarus caeruleus*)**, **Russell's viper (*Daboia russelii*)**, **saw-scaled viper (*Echis carinatus*)**.
- Venom glands are **modified salivary (parotid) glands**; fangs are modified teeth.
- **Neurotoxic venom** (paralyses nerves and breathing) — **cobra, krait** (family Elapidae). **Haemotoxic/vasculotoxic venom** (destroys blood vessels, bleeding) — **vipers** (Viperidae).
- **Polyvalent anti-venom** (against the Big Four) is made from **horse serum** — passive immunity.
- **King cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*)** — longest venomous snake; eats other snakes; the female builds a **nest**.
- Non-venomous: **python** (has **vestigial hind limbs** seen as small spurs and a pelvic girdle), rat snake, sand boa, wolf snake.

> **Common mistake:** "All lizards are harmless" — true for India, but the **Gila monster and the Mexican beaded lizard (*Heloderma*)** of North America are venomous. The Indian **garden lizard (*Calotes*)** called "blood-sucker" sucks no blood; the male's throat simply turns red in the breeding season.

### Crocodilians and turtles of India

- **Gharial (*Gavialis gangeticus*)** — long thin snout, eats fish, **critically endangered**; in the **Chambal**, Girwa and the **Gandak (Bihar)** rivers. The male has a bulb ("ghara") on its snout tip.
- **Mugger (*Crocodylus palustris*)** — marsh crocodile of fresh water.
- **Saltwater crocodile (*Crocodylus porosus*)** — **largest living reptile**; Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Sundarbans, Andamans.
- **Olive ridley turtle** — mass nesting (*arribada*) at **Gahirmatha and Rushikulya, Odisha**.

### The age of reptiles

The **Mesozoic era** (about 252–66 million years ago) is the **"Age of Reptiles"**. Dinosaurs ruled the land (*Tyrannosaurus*, *Brachiosaurus*, *Triceratops*), **pterosaurs** (*Pteranodon*) flew and **ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs** swam in the sea. Most disappeared in the **end-Cretaceous mass extinction (about 66 million years ago)**, linked to the **asteroid impact at Chicxulub (Mexico)** and Deccan volcanism. Birds are the living descendants of one group of dinosaurs.

## 2. Class Aves — birds

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- **Feathers**; most can fly except **flightless birds** (e.g. ostrich).
- **Beak**; **forelimbs modified into wings**.
- Hind limbs generally have **scales** and are modified for walking, swimming or clasping branches.
- **Skin dry, without glands except the oil gland at the base of the tail** (uropygial/preen gland).
- Endoskeleton fully ossified; **long bones hollow with air cavities (pneumatic bones)**.
- Digestive tract has additional chambers — **crop and gizzard**.
- **Heart completely four-chambered**.
- **Warm-blooded (homoiothermous)**.
- Respiration by **lungs**; **air sacs** connected to the lungs supplement respiration.
- Sexes separate; internal fertilisation; **oviparous**; direct development.
- Examples: ***Corvus*** (crow), ***Columba*** (pigeon), ***Psittacula*** (parrot), ***Struthio*** (ostrich), ***Pavo*** (peacock), ***Aptenodytes*** (penguin), ***Neophron*** (vulture).

### Flight adaptations

| System | Adaptation | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Body shape | **Streamlined (spindle-shaped)**, compact | Less air resistance |
| Exoskeleton | **Feathers** (contour, down, filoplume); vane of barbs with hooked **barbules** | Light, flight surface, insulation |
| Bones | **Pneumatic** (hollow, air-filled), fused bones | Light yet strong |
| Sternum | Large **keel (carina)** | Attachment of huge flight muscles |
| Flight muscles | **Pectoralis major** (downstroke) and **supracoracoideus / pectoralis minor** (upstroke, via a tendon "pulley") | Powerful wing beats |
| Girdles | Clavicles fused into **furcula (wishbone)**; **synsacrum** (fused hip vertebrae + pelvis) | Rigid frame |
| Tail | Fused caudal vertebrae = **pygostyle** | Supports tail feathers for steering |
| Jaws | **Toothless beak** | Weight saved |
| Respiration | **Air sacs** (usually 9) → **one-way air flow** through lungs; air passes the lungs on **both inspiration and expiration** ("double respiration") | Very efficient O₂ supply |
| Circulation | **Four chambers**, **right aortic arch**, fast heart rate | High metabolism, body temperature ~40–42 °C |
| Excretion | **Uricotelic** (semi-solid uric acid), **no urinary bladder** (most) | Less water weight |
| Reproduction | Only the **left ovary and oviduct** work | Less weight |
| Senses | Large eyes, sharp vision; large **cerebellum** (balance) and optic lobes | Control in air |
| Voice | **Syrinx** at the base of the trachea (larynx has no vocal cords) | Song |

- **Crop milk** — pigeons (both sexes) feed young on a secretion of the crop, stimulated by **prolactin**.
- **Precocial** chicks (hen, duck) hatch with down and open eyes and walk at once; **altricial** chicks (pigeon, crow, parrot) hatch naked, blind and helpless.
- **Brood parasitism** — the **koel (cuckoo)** lays its eggs in the **crow's** nest.

### Flightless birds (ratites)

**Ratites** have a **flat sternum without a keel** (Latin *ratis* = raft): **ostrich *Struthio*** (Africa — **largest living bird, largest egg, fastest runner, only two toes**), **emu *Dromaius*** (Australia), **cassowary *Casuarius***, **rhea *Rhea*** (South America), **kiwi *Apteryx*** (New Zealand — nostrils at the tip of the beak; very large egg for its size).
**Penguins** cannot fly but are **carinates** (keeled) — their wings are flippers for "flying" under water.
Extinct flightless birds: **dodo (*Raphus cucullatus*)**, Mauritius (last seen about 1681); moa (New Zealand); elephant bird (Madagascar).

Bird records: smallest — **bee hummingbird** (Cuba); only bird that can fly backwards — **hummingbird**; largest wingspan — **wandering albatross** (~3.5 m); fastest (diving) — **peregrine falcon**.

### *Archaeopteryx* — connecting link between reptiles and birds

Fossil from the **Jurassic (about 150 million years ago)**, found in the Solnhofen limestone of **Germany in 1861**.

| Reptilian features | Bird features |
|---|---|
| **Teeth** in both jaws | **Feathers** |
| **Long bony tail** of about 20 free vertebrae | Forelimbs as **wings** |
| **Three clawed fingers** on each wing | **Furcula** (wishbone) |
| Weak sternum **without a keel** | Beak-like jaws |
| Abdominal ribs | Bird-like hind limbs with a backward toe |

### Bird migration

**Migration** is the **regular, seasonal, two-way** journey of birds between breeding and wintering areas along fixed routes (**flyways**). India lies on the **Central Asian Flyway**.

- **Why:** escape winter cold and food shortage; long summer days in the north for breeding.
- **Trigger:** change in **day length (photoperiod)** acting through the hypothalamus–pituitary and gonads; birds lay down fat (hyperphagia) and become restless (**Zugunruhe**).
- **Navigation:** **sun compass** (day migrants), **star compass** (night migrants), **Earth's magnetic field**, landmarks, smell.
- **Records:** **Arctic tern** — longest migration (Arctic ↔ Antarctic, about 70,000 km a year); **bar-tailed godwit** — longest non-stop flight (Alaska → New Zealand, over 11,000 km); **bar-headed goose** — flies over the Himalaya; **Amur falcon** — Siberia/China → Nagaland → southern Africa; **Siberian crane** — once wintered at **Keoladeo (Bharatpur)**, Rajasthan.
- **Bihar:** **Kanwar (Kabar) Taal, Begusarai** — Bihar's first **Ramsar site (2020)** and a major winter home of migratory ducks; **Nagi and Nakti bird sanctuaries, Jamui** — Ramsar sites (2024). Bihar's state bird is the **house sparrow** (*gauraiya*).
- **Salim Ali** — "**Birdman of India**" (*The Book of Indian Birds*). National bird — **peacock, *Pavo cristatus*** (1963).

## 3. Class Mammalia — mammals

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Found in every habitat — polar ice, deserts, mountains, forests, grasslands, dark caves; some fly or live in water.
- **Most unique feature: mammary glands** (milk-producing) to nourish the young.
- **Two pairs of limbs**, adapted for walking, running, climbing, burrowing, swimming or flying.
- **Skin has hair**; **external ears (pinnae)** present.
- **Different types of teeth** in the jaw (heterodont).
- **Heart four-chambered**; **homoiothermous**; respiration by lungs.
- Sexes separate; internal fertilisation; **viviparous with a few exceptions**; direct development.
- Examples: **oviparous — *Ornithorhynchus*** (platypus); viviparous — ***Macropus*** (kangaroo), ***Pteropus*** (flying fox), ***Camelus***, ***Macaca*** (monkey), ***Rattus***, ***Canis***, ***Felis***, ***Elephas***, ***Equus***, ***Delphinus*** (common dolphin), ***Balaenoptera*** (blue whale), ***Panthera tigris***, ***Panthera leo***.

**B.Sc. characters:**
- Muscular **diaphragm** separating thorax and abdomen.
- **Seven cervical vertebrae** in almost all (giraffe too!) — exceptions: **sloths** (6–10) and **manatees** (6).
- **Three ear ossicles** — malleus, incus, stapes (malleus and incus come from reptilian jaw bones).
- Lower jaw of a single bone (**dentary**); **dicondylic** skull; **secondary palate**.
- Teeth **heterodont, thecodont** (in sockets) and **diphyodont** (milk and permanent sets).
- **RBCs without nucleus** (biconcave; oval but still non-nucleated in **camels and llamas**).
- **Left aortic arch** only; **metanephric** kidneys; **ureotelic**.
- Skin glands: **sweat, sebaceous, mammary, scent glands**.
- **Corpus callosum** joins the cerebral hemispheres (eutherians only).
- Testes usually in a **scrotum** outside the body (cooler for sperm).

### The three subclasses

| Feature | **Prototheria (Monotremata)** | **Metatheria (Marsupialia)** | **Eutheria (Placentalia)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproduction | **Lay eggs** (leathery shell) | Live birth of a **tiny, embryo-like young** | Live birth of well-developed young |
| Placenta | None | **Yolk-sac (choriovitelline)**, short-lived | **Chorio-allantoic** (true placenta) |
| Pouch (marsupium) | Temporary in echidna | **Present** — young completes growth on the nipple | Absent |
| Nipples | **Absent** — milk oozes from skin patches, young licks it | Present, inside pouch | Present |
| Cloaca | **Present** (single opening — "monotreme") | Absent (separate openings) | Absent |
| Pinna | Absent | Present | Present |
| Corpus callosum | Absent | Absent | **Present** |
| Body temperature | Low (~30–32 °C) | Moderate | ~37 °C |
| Examples | ***Ornithorhynchus*** (duck-billed platypus), ***Tachyglossus*** (echidna, spiny anteater), *Zaglossus* | ***Macropus*** (kangaroo), *Phascolarctos* (koala), ***Didelphis*** (opossum), Tasmanian devil, *Thylacinus* (extinct Tasmanian wolf) | All other mammals |
| Distribution | Australia, Tasmania, New Guinea | **Australia** + the **Americas** (opossum) | Worldwide |

- Monotremes are the **connecting link between reptiles and mammals** — reptile-like eggs, cloaca and shoulder girdle, but hair and milk.
- **Platypus**: adult has no teeth (horny plates), duck-like bill with **electroreceptors**, webbed feet; the **male has a venomous spur** on the hind leg.
- **Kangaroo**: gestation only about **33 days**; the newborn (about 2 cm) crawls into the pouch and fixes onto a nipple. Marsupials have **epipubic bones** and the female a **double (bifid) uterus and vagina**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) class 9–10 Science paper asked: "Which of the following mammals lays eggs? — (A) Echidna (B) Kangaroo (C) Loris." **Official answer: (A) Echidna.** Echidna and platypus are monotremes (Prototheria). Kangaroo is a marsupial and loris a placental primate. The same idea fits the Zoology paper.

### Orders of Eutheria (with examples)

| Order | Key feature | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Insectivora (Eulipotyphla)** | Small, pointed snout, insect-eaters | Shrew (*Suncus*), mole, hedgehog; **Etruscan shrew — smallest mammal by mass (~2 g)** |
| **Chiroptera** | **Only flying mammals**; wing = skin (patagium) on long fingers; **echolocation** (microbats) | ***Pteropus*** (flying fox — fruit bat), *Megaderma*, *Rhinolophus*; **second-largest order** |
| **Primates** | Large brain, grasping hands with nails, forward eyes | *Macaca*, ***Homo***, *Loris*, langur, **hoolock gibbon** (India's **only ape**, North-East) |
| **Xenarthra (Edentata)** | Teeth few or none | Sloth, armadillo, anteater |
| **Pholidota** | Body covered with horny **scales** | **Pangolin *Manis*** (scaly anteater; most trafficked mammal) |
| **Lagomorpha** | **Two pairs of upper incisors** (a small peg pair behind) | **Rabbit *Oryctolagus***, hare *Lepus* |
| **Rodentia** | **One pair** of chisel-like, **ever-growing incisors** in each jaw; no canines (gap = **diastema**) | ***Rattus***, *Mus*, squirrel *Funambulus*, porcupine *Hystrix*; **largest mammalian order** (~40% of species) |
| **Cetacea** | Aquatic, fish-shaped, forelimbs → flippers, no hind limbs, blowhole, blubber | ***Balaenoptera musculus*** (**blue whale — largest animal ever**), ***Delphinus***, **Ganges river dolphin *Platanista gangetica*** |
| **Carnivora** | Flesh-eaters with large canines and **carnassial teeth** | ***Panthera***, ***Felis***, ***Canis***, bear, mongoose; seals and walrus |
| **Proboscidea** | **Trunk** (nose + upper lip); **tusks = modified upper incisors** | ***Elephas maximus*** (Asian), *Loxodonta* (African — largest land animal); gestation ~22 months (longest) |
| **Sirenia** | Herbivorous sea cows | ***Dugong*** (Gulf of Mannar), manatee |
| **Perissodactyla** | **Odd-toed** hoofed | **Horse *Equus***, **one-horned rhinoceros *Rhinoceros unicornis***, tapir |
| **Artiodactyla** | **Even-toed** hoofed; many are **ruminants** | Cow *Bos*, buffalo, **camel**, deer, giraffe, pig, hippopotamus |

- **Ruminant stomach** has four chambers — **rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum** (the abomasum is the "true stomach" with gastric glands). The **camel has no distinct omasum** (three chambers).
- Modern molecular classification puts whales inside the even-toed group (**Cetartiodactyla**) — the hippopotamus is the whale's closest living relative.

### Ganges river dolphin — Bihar's pride

- ***Platanista gangetica*** (local name **susu**) — **National Aquatic Animal of India (2009)**; almost **blind** (eyes without a lens), finds prey by **echolocation**; must surface to breathe every 30–120 seconds.
- **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary**, **Bhagalpur, Bihar (1991)** — the only protected area in India set up specially for this dolphin (about 60 km of the Ganga from Sultanganj to Kahalgaon). The **National Dolphin Research Centre** is at **Patna**. **National Dolphin Day — 5 October**.
- Bihar's other wildlife: **Valmiki Tiger Reserve** (West Champaran) — Bihar's only tiger reserve, with tigers, gharials in the Gandak and occasional rhinoceroses.

(The Vikramshila sanctuary PYQ — TRE 1.0 Q74 — is treated in [Biodiversity conservation and wildlife of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/biodiversity-conservation-and-wildlife-of-bihar).)

### Dental formulae (half upper jaw / half lower jaw: I C P M)

<div class="formula">

| Animal | Formula | Total |
|---|---|---|
| **Human (adult)** | 2 1 2 3 / 2 1 2 3 | **32** |
| Human (milk teeth) | 2 1 0 2 / 2 1 0 2 | **20** |
| **Rabbit** | 2 0 3 3 / 1 0 2 3 | **28** |
| Rat | 1 0 0 3 / 1 0 0 3 | **16** |
| Dog | 3 1 4 2 / 3 1 4 3 | **42** |
| Cat | 3 1 3 1 / 3 1 2 1 | **30** |
| Cow | 0 0 3 3 / 3 1 3 3 | **32** (no upper incisors — a hard dental pad) |
| Elephant | 1 0 3 3 / 0 0 3 3 | **26** |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Crocodiles** have a **four-chambered heart** — the only reptiles with one.
- **Snakes**: no limbs (python has vestigial hind limbs), no eyelids, no external ear, no urinary bladder.
- **Venomous lizards** exist only in North America (*Heloderma*).
- **Sphenodon** is the only living rhynchocephalian (living fossil, "third eye").
- **Ratites** (ostrich, emu, kiwi) have no keel; **penguins** are flightless but keeled.
- Birds have a **right** aortic arch; mammals a **left** one.
- Birds usually have **no urinary bladder** and only a **left ovary**.
- **Archaeopteryx** had **teeth and a long bony tail**.
- **Seven neck vertebrae** even in the giraffe; sloths and manatees are the exceptions.
- **Camel RBCs are oval** but **non-nucleated** — all mammalian RBCs lack a nucleus.
- **Monotremes have no nipples**; milk oozes from skin patches.
- **Opossum** is a marsupial of the **Americas**, not Australia.
- Tusk of elephant = **incisor**; tusk of walrus = **canine**.
- **Rabbits are lagomorphs, not rodents** (two pairs of upper incisors).
- Blue whale is the largest animal **ever** — bigger than any dinosaur.

## Quick revision

1. Reptiles with four-chambered heart — **crocodiles**.
2. Living fossil reptile with third eye — ***Sphenodon***.
3. Only venomous lizards — ***Heloderma***.
4. Big Four snakes — **cobra, krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper**.
5. Neurotoxic venom — **cobra, krait**; haemotoxic — **vipers**.
6. Gharial in Bihar — **Gandak river**.
7. Largest living reptile — **saltwater crocodile**.
8. Mammals evolved from — **synapsid** reptiles.
9. Age of reptiles — **Mesozoic era**.
10. Oil gland of birds — **uropygial gland** at tail base.
11. Wishbone — **furcula** (fused clavicles).
12. Bird voice box — **syrinx**.
13. Largest living bird — **ostrich**; smallest — **bee hummingbird**.
14. Connecting link reptiles–birds — ***Archaeopteryx*** (Jurassic, Germany 1861).
15. Longest migration — **Arctic tern**.
16. Bihar's first Ramsar site — **Kanwar Taal (Begusarai)**.
17. Most unique mammalian feature — **mammary glands**.
18. Egg-laying mammals — **platypus, echidna** (Prototheria).
19. Pouched mammals — **Metatheria** (kangaroo, koala, opossum).
20. Only flying mammals — **bats** (Chiroptera).
21. Largest mammalian order — **Rodentia**.
22. Rabbit dental formula — **2033/1023 = 28**.
23. National Aquatic Animal — **Ganges river dolphin** (2009).
24. Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary — **Bhagalpur, Bihar**.

## Practice with answers

1. What covers the reptile body? — **Dry cornified skin with epidermal scales or scutes**.
2. Which reptile order has a shell and no teeth? — **Chelonia**.
3. Which organ detects scent carried by a snake's tongue? — **Jacobson's organ**.
4. Which snake builds a nest? — **King cobra**.
5. Which skull type do mammals have? — **Synapsid**.
6. Which flight muscle produces the downstroke? — **Pectoralis major**.
7. How many air sacs does a typical bird have? — **Nine**.
8. What are pneumatic bones? — **Hollow bones filled with air** (birds).
9. Why is the kiwi called a ratite? — **Its sternum has no keel**.
10. Name two reptilian features of *Archaeopteryx*. — **Teeth and a long bony tail** (also clawed fingers).
11. What triggers migration in birds? — **Change in day length (photoperiod)**.
12. Which bird lays its eggs in a crow's nest? — **Koel (cuckoo)** — brood parasitism.
13. Which mammals have a cloaca? — **Monotremes** (platypus, echidna).
14. What type of placenta do marsupials have? — **Yolk-sac (choriovitelline) placenta**.
15. Which mammal order has ever-growing incisors and no canines? — **Rodentia**.
16. Which part of the ruminant stomach is the true stomach? — **Abomasum**.
17. Elephant tusks are modified? — **Upper incisors**.
18. How does the Ganges river dolphin find food? — **Echolocation** (it is nearly blind).
19. Dog dental formula total? — **42** (3142/3143).
20. Which is the only ape found in India? — **Hoolock gibbon**.
