This chapter puts the whole animal kingdom side by side. NCERT touches it in "Evolution" (homologous and analogous organs), "Body Fluids and Circulation" (two-, three- and four-chambered hearts) and "Human Reproduction" (placenta, extra-embryonic membranes); B.Sc. comparative anatomy adds aortic arches, kidney types and larval forms. TRE asks "which artery supplies the rabbit brain", "embryonic respiratory organ of chick", "larva of *Unio*", "neoteny is seen in".

> **In simple words:** When we compare the same organ in different animals, we see a slow change from simple to complex — a two-room heart in fish, three rooms in frogs, four in birds and mammals. Organs with the same origin but different jobs (human arm, bat wing) are **homologous**; organs with different origins but the same job (bird wing, butterfly wing) are **analogous**. Many animals hatch as **larvae** that look nothing like the adult — each phylum has its own typical larva, and these names are a favourite exam topic.

## 1. Homologous, analogous and vestigial organs

| Type | Definition | Examples | Evolution type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homologous** | **Same basic structure and embryonic origin**, may do **different jobs** | Forelimbs of **man, cheetah, whale, bat** (pentadactyl limb: humerus, radius–ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); hearts and brains of vertebrates; **swim bladder and lungs**; **endostyle and thyroid**; in plants, **thorns of *Bougainvillea* and tendrils of *Cucurbita*** | **Divergent evolution** (common ancestor, different paths) |
| **Analogous** | **Different structure and origin**, **same job** | **Wings of butterfly and bird**; **eye of octopus and mammal**; **flippers of penguin and dolphin**; **sweet potato (root) and potato (stem)**; gills of fish and prawn | **Convergent evolution** (similar habitat, similar solution) |
| **Vestigial** | Reduced, functionless remains of organs that worked in ancestors | Human **vermiform appendix, coccyx, nictitating membrane (plica semilunaris), ear (auricular) muscles, wisdom teeth, body hair**; hind-limb bones of **python** and whale; wings of **kiwi**; splint bones of horse | Evidence of descent |

- **Wiedersheim** listed about 180 vestigial structures in man (often quoted: "about 90").
- **Atavism (reversion)** — sudden reappearance of an ancestral feature: a **tail** in a newborn baby, **extra nipples**, thick body hair, long canines.
- **Serial homology** — similar structures repeated along one body, e.g. the **appendages of prawn** (all biramous, modified for different jobs).

> **Common mistake:** The **wing of a bird and the wing of a bat** are **homologous as forelimbs** (same bones) but **analogous as wings** (flight evolved separately). Read the question: "as organs of flight" → analogous; "as forelimbs" → homologous.

Fishes have single circulation; amphibians and reptiles incomplete double circulation; birds and mammals complete double circulation.

| Arch | Fishes | Frog | Reptiles | Birds | Mammals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I, II | Lost (II kept in sharks as gill arch) | Lost | Lost | Lost | Lost (small parts in face arteries) |
| **III** | Gill arch | **Carotid arch** | **Carotid** | **Carotid** | **Common carotid + base of internal carotid** |
| **IV** | Gill arch | **Systemic arch** (both sides) | **Both systemic arches** | **Right systemic arch only** | **Left systemic arch = arch of aorta**; right IV → part of right subclavian |
| V | Gill arch | Lost (kept in urodeles) | Lost | Lost | Lost |
| **VI** | Gill arch | **Pulmocutaneous arch** | **Pulmonary arch** | **Pulmonary** | **Pulmonary arteries**; left VI dorsal part = **ductus arteriosus (ductus Botalli)** |

- Bony fishes keep **four pairs (III–VI)** as branchial arches.
- The **ductus arteriosus** carries blood from the pulmonary trunk to the aorta in the foetus (by-passing the non-working lungs). It closes after birth and becomes the **ligamentum arteriosum**. If it stays open → **patent ductus arteriosus**.

### Blood supply to the brain of the rabbit (and man)

The **common carotid artery** (from arch III) divides into:
- **Internal carotid** → enters the skull → supplies the **brain** (and eyes); joins the vertebral arteries in the **circle of Willis** at the base of the brain.
- **External carotid** → face, tongue, jaws, scalp, neck muscles — **outside** the skull.

The **pulmonary artery** carries **deoxygenated** blood from the right ventricle to the lungs — it never supplies the brain.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q76 asked: "Which artery supplies oxygenated blood to rabbit brain? (A) Pulmonary artery (B) External carotid (C) Internal carotid." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Internal carotid.** External carotid serves the face and neck; the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. (The vertebral arteries also feed the brain, but they were not among the options.)

## 4. Kidneys — three generations

| Kidney | Position in embryo | Functional in | Duct |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pronephros** ("first kidney") | Most anterior; tubules open into the coelom by ciliated **nephrostomes** | **Larvae of fishes and amphibians**; adult **hagfish** and a few bony fishes | Pronephric (archinephric) duct |
| **Mesonephros** ("middle kidney") | Middle region | **Adult fishes and amphibians** (often called **opisthonephros**); **embryonic** in reptiles, birds, mammals | **Wolffian (mesonephric) duct** — becomes the **vas deferens** in male amniotes |
| **Metanephros** ("hind kidney") | Posterior; compact, many nephrons, no nephrostomes | **Adult reptiles, birds and mammals** | **Ureter** (from the ureteric bud) |

The female reproductive duct (oviduct, uterus) comes from a separate **Müllerian duct**.

**Nitrogenous waste and habitat (link):** **ammonotelic** — bony fishes, tadpoles, aquatic invertebrates (ammonia needs much water); **ureotelic** — mammals, adult frogs, sharks (store urea), turtles in water; **uricotelic** — birds, lizards, snakes, insects, land snails (uric acid saves water; also safe inside a closed egg). Details: [Excretion and osmoregulation](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/excretion-and-osmoregulation).

### Neoteny and paedogenesis

- **Neoteny** — the animal becomes **sexually mature while keeping larval features** (gills, tail fin) and does not complete metamorphosis. Classic example: the **axolotl** — the larva of the Mexican salamander ***Ambystoma mexicanum*** (also *A. tigrinum* in some lakes). Cause: low thyroxine activity, iodine-poor or cold water. **Feeding thyroxine or iodine makes the axolotl metamorphose** into an adult *Ambystoma* (lungs, no gills) — showing that neoteny is environmental/hormonal.
- **Permanent larvae** — *Necturus* (mudpuppy) and *Proteus* (olm) keep gills for life and **do not metamorphose even with thyroxine** (tissues unresponsive) — called perennibranchiate urodeles.
- Neoteny in invertebrates: ***Oikopleura*** (urochordate keeps its tail).
- **Paedogenesis** — **larvae reproduce**, often by parthenogenesis: larvae of the gall midge ***Miastor***; rediae of *Fasciola* produce more larvae.
- **Garstang's theory**: vertebrates may have arisen from a **neotenic ascidian-type tadpole larva** that never settled — neoteny as a route to new groups.

|---|---|---|---|

**Placenta** = an intimate connection between foetal membranes and the mother's uterine wall for nutrition, gas exchange, excretion and hormone production.

## 8. Skin and its derivatives compared

| Group | Skin | Main derivatives |
|---|---|---|
| Fishes | Many mucous glands | **Dermal** scales (placoid, ganoid, cycloid, ctenoid) |
| Amphibians | Thin, moist, glandular (mucous and poison glands); used in breathing | Usually no scales (small dermal scales in some caecilians) |
| Reptiles | Dry, few glands | **Epidermal** horny scales and scutes; shed as a whole (snakes) |
| Birds | Dry; only the **oil (uropygial) gland** | **Feathers**, beak, claws, leg scales (all epidermal, keratin) |
| Mammals | Glandular — **sweat, sebaceous, mammary**, scent glands | **Hair**, nails, claws, hooves, horns (keratin); antlers are bone |

- Fish scales are **dermal** (from the dermis); reptile scales and bird feathers are **epidermal** — so the scales of fish and reptiles are not strictly homologous.
- **Mammary glands are modified sweat glands** (homologous).
- **Horns** of cattle (bony core + keratin sheath, permanent, unbranched) differ from **antlers** of deer (pure bone, branched, shed every year, only in males except reindeer).

## 9. Metamorphosis compared

| Animal | Type | Controlling hormone |
|---|---|---|
| Insects (butterfly, housefly) | Complete (egg–larva–pupa–adult) | **Ecdysone** (moulting) and **juvenile hormone** (keeps larval form) |
| Cockroach, grasshopper | Incomplete (egg–nymph–adult) | Ecdysone, juvenile hormone |
| Frog | Progressive (tadpole → frog) | **Thyroxine** |
| *Herdmania* | **Retrogressive** (active larva → sessile adult) | — |
| Axolotl | Neoteny (metamorphosis suppressed) | Low thyroxine; induced by thyroxine/iodine |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Bird wing vs bat wing** — homologous as forelimbs, analogous as wings.
- **Sweet potato and potato** are **analogous** (root vs stem).
- Fish heart is **venous** (deoxygenated blood only).
- **Crocodile** heart is four-chambered — but a reptile.
- Birds keep the **right** fourth arch; mammals the **left**.
- **Internal carotid → brain**; external carotid → face and neck.
- **Mesonephros** is the adult kidney of fishes and amphibians, but only embryonic in amniotes.
- **Wolffian duct → vas deferens**; Müllerian duct → oviduct and uterus.
- **Glochidium** (bivalve) is parasitic; **veliger** is free.
- **Zoea and megalopa** belong to crabs; **mysis** to *Penaeus*; **nauplius** to most crustaceans.
- **Tornaria** (hemichordate) looks like **bipinnaria** (starfish).
- **Axolotl metamorphoses with thyroxine**; *Necturus* and *Proteus* do not.
- Amnion and chorion are **somatopleuric**; allantois and yolk sac are **splanchnopleuric**.
- **Chick embryo breathes through the allantois**, not the amnion.

## Quick revision

1. Same origin, different function — **homologous** (divergent evolution).
2. Different origin, same function — **analogous** (convergent evolution).
3. Vestigial organ examples in man — **appendix, coccyx, nictitating membrane, wisdom teeth**.
4. Two-chambered venous heart — **fishes**.
5. Foramen of Panizza — **crocodile heart**.
6. Embryonic aortic arches — **six pairs**.
7. Arch III → **carotid**; arch IV → **systemic**; arch VI → **pulmonary**.
8. Ductus arteriosus after birth → **ligamentum arteriosum**.
9. Artery to rabbit brain (TRE key) — **internal carotid**.
10. Adult kidney of mammals — **metanephros**; of frog — **mesonephros**.
11. Cranial nerves: anamniotes **10 pairs**, amniotes **12 pairs**.
12. Larva of *Unio* — **glochidium**.
13. Larva of *Nereis* — **trochophore**.
14. Larva of crab — **zoea**; of *Penaeus* — **mysis**.
15. Larva of starfish — **bipinnaria**; of *Balanoglossus* — **tornaria**.
16. Larva of lamprey — **ammocoete**; of eel — **leptocephalus**.
17. Infective larva of *Fasciola* — **metacercaria**.
18. Neotenic larva — **axolotl** (*Ambystoma*).
19. Larvae reproducing — **paedogenesis** (*Miastor*).
20. Chick embryonic respiratory organ — **allantois**.
21. First blood cells form in — **yolk sac** wall.
22. Human placenta — **discoidal, haemochorial**.
23. Cotyledonary placenta — **cow, sheep**.

## Practice with answers

1. Why are a whale's flipper and a bat's wing homologous? — **Same pentadactyl bone plan and embryonic origin**.
2. Which type of evolution produces analogous organs? — **Convergent evolution**.
3. What is atavism? — **Reappearance of an ancestral character** (e.g. tail in a newborn).
4. Which vertebrates have incomplete double circulation? — **Amphibians and most reptiles**.
5. What does the sinus venosus become in mammals? — **Part of the right atrium containing the SA node**.
6. Which aortic arch forms the arch of aorta in man? — **Left fourth arch**.
7. What is a patent ductus arteriosus? — **Failure of the foetal ductus arteriosus to close after birth**.
8. Which artery supplies the face of the rabbit? — **External carotid**.
9. Which kidney has nephrostomes opening into the coelom? — **Pronephros**.
10. Which duct becomes the vas deferens? — **Wolffian (mesonephric) duct**.
11. Name the larva with a velum. — **Veliger** (molluscs).
12. Which crustacean larva has three pairs of appendages and one eye? — **Nauplius**.
13. Larva of the sea urchin? — **Echinopluteus**.
14. Which larva shows retrogressive metamorphosis? — **Ascidian tadpole** (*Herdmania*).
15. How can an axolotl be made to metamorphose? — **By giving thyroxine or iodine**.
16. Which extra-embryonic membrane stores uric acid in the bird egg? — **Allantois**.
17. From which layer does the amnion arise? — **Somatopleure** (ectoderm + somatic mesoderm).
18. Which placenta type has villi in a girdle? — **Zonary** (dog, cat).
19. Which placenta type is found in marsupials? — **Yolk-sac (choriovitelline)**.
