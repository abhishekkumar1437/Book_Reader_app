Evolution is the most asked theory topic in TRE: both TRE 1.0 and TRE 2.0 asked the title and year of Darwin's book, and TRE 1.0 asked where Darwin's theory fails. NCERT class 12 "Evolution" is the base; the B.Sc. syllabus adds Hardy–Weinberg numericals, speciation, isolating mechanisms and the geological time scale. Questions are short: "*Origin of Species* was published in", "Miller's experiment used", "industrial melanism is an example of", "founder effect".

> **In simple words:** Life began on the early Earth from simple chemicals (methane, ammonia, hydrogen, water) that, with energy from lightning and UV light, joined into bigger molecules and then into the first cells. Since then living things have slowly changed over millions of years — this is **evolution**. Darwin explained *how*: more young are born than can survive; they differ slightly; those with useful differences survive and leave more young (**natural selection**). Genetics later explained *where the differences come from* — mutations and recombination.

## 1. Origin of the universe and Earth

| Event | Time ago | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Bang** — origin of the universe | about **13.8 billion years** | NCERT gives an older estimate of "about 20 billion years" |
| Formation of Earth | about **4.5–4.6 billion years** | Early atmosphere of water vapour, CH₄, CO₂, NH₃ released from the molten mass |
| First life | about **4 billion years** (NCERT: "about 500 million years after the formation of Earth") | Oldest fossils (stromatolites of cyanobacteria) about **3.5 billion years** |

## 2. Theories of the origin of life

| Theory | Idea | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Special creation** | God created all species in their present form; diversity was always the same; Earth about 4,000 years old | Rejected by evidence |
| **Spontaneous generation (abiogenesis)** | Life arises from non-living matter (decaying meat → maggots, mud → frogs) — Aristotle's view | **Disproved** (below) |
| **Panspermia (cosmozoic)** | "Spores" of life came from outer space (Arrhenius) | Still favoured by some astronomers (NCERT) |
| **Biogenesis** | Life comes only from pre-existing life | Accepted for present-day life |
| **Chemical evolution (Oparin–Haldane)** | First life formed from inorganic molecules through organic molecules under early-Earth conditions | **Accepted model** |

**Disproof of spontaneous generation:**
- **Francesco Redi (1668)** — meat in covered jars developed no maggots; open jars did.
- **Lazzaro Spallanzani (1767)** — boiled broth in sealed flasks stayed sterile.
- **Louis Pasteur (1859–1862)** — **swan-neck flask** experiment: boiled broth in a flask with a long curved neck stayed free of microbes (dust trapped in the bend); when the neck was broken, microbes grew. Proved "**life comes only from pre-existing life**". Phrase *omne vivum ex vivo* (all life from life) is associated with this idea.

> **Remember:** Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation **for life today**. But the first life on early Earth **did** arise from non-living molecules — by slow chemical evolution, under conditions that no longer exist (no free oxygen, very high energy).

### Oparin–Haldane theory (chemical evolution)

- **A. I. Oparin** (Russia, **1924**) and **J. B. S. Haldane** (England, **1929**) independently proposed it.
- The **primitive atmosphere was reducing**: **methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen (H₂) and water vapour**; **no free oxygen**.
- Energy from **lightning, UV radiation (no ozone layer), volcanic heat** and radioactivity drove reactions.
- Simple molecules → **organic monomers** (amino acids, sugars, nitrogen bases) collected in warm seas — Haldane's "**hot dilute soup**" (prebiotic soup) → **polymers** (proteins, nucleic acids) → membrane-bound droplets: **coacervates** (Oparin) / **proteinoid microspheres** (Sidney Fox) → **protocells (protobionts)** → first true cells.
- First cells were **anaerobic, heterotrophic prokaryotes**; later came chemoautotrophs, then **photosynthetic cyanobacteria** that released oxygen (Great Oxidation Event, about 2.4 billion years ago), the **ozone layer**, aerobic life and **eukaryotes** (by **endosymbiosis** — Lynn Margulis).
- **RNA world hypothesis**: RNA was the first genetic material, because RNA can both store information and act as an enzyme (**ribozyme**); DNA came later as a more stable store.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) class 9–10 Science paper asked: "According to Oparin, which of the following was not present in the primitive atmosphere of the Earth? (A) Oxygen (B) Hydrogen (C) Water vapour …". Answer **(A) Oxygen** — the early atmosphere was **reducing**; free O₂ came later from photosynthesis. (No key for that paper in our files.)

- A **closed glass apparatus** with **methane, ammonia, hydrogen and water vapour**; **electric sparks** (imitating lightning) passed through the gases; water boiled in one flask and vapour condensed in another (imitating rain).
- After about **one week**, the water contained **amino acids** (glycine, alanine, aspartic acid and others).
- In similar later experiments others obtained **sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats**.
- NCERT states the gases were kept "at 800 °C"; in the actual set-up the water was simply boiled and the hot gases sparked — just remember **CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O + electric discharge → amino acids**.
- Support from space: the **Murchison meteorite (1969)** contained amino acids, showing such molecules form naturally.

## 3. Evidences of evolution

### 3.1 Palaeontological (fossil) evidence

- **Fossils** are remains or traces of past organisms preserved in **sedimentary rocks**; deeper layers are older. Fossils show simple forms in old rocks and complex forms in newer rocks, and many extinct forms (dinosaurs).
- **Dating**: **radioactive (radiometric) dating** — **carbon-14** (half-life **5,730 years**; for remains up to about 50,000 years; **W. F. Libby**, Nobel 1960), **potassium–argon** and **uranium–lead** for old rocks.
- **Evolution of the horse** (a complete fossil series from North America):

| Stage | Epoch | Toes (fore-limb) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Eohippus*** (*Hyracotherium*) | **Eocene** (~55 mya) | 4 | Fox-sized, browser |
| ***Mesohippus*** | Oligocene | 3 | Larger |
| ***Merychippus*** | Miocene | 3 (middle one large) | Grazing teeth |
| ***Pliohippus*** | Pliocene | **1** (first one-toed) | Long legs |
| ***Equus*** | Pleistocene → now | 1 (side toes as **splint bones**) | Modern horse |

Trends: larger size, longer face, **fewer toes**, grinding (grazing) teeth.

- **Connecting links** (show features of two groups):

| Link | Between |
|---|---|
| ***Archaeopteryx*** (fossil) | Reptiles and birds |
| ***Peripatus*** | Annelids and arthropods |
| ***Neopilina*** | Annelids and molluscs |
| ***Balanoglossus*** | Non-chordates and chordates |
| **Lungfishes** (*Protopterus*) | Fishes and amphibians |
| ***Seymouria*** (fossil) | Amphibians and reptiles |
| **Duck-billed platypus, echidna** | Reptiles and mammals |
| ***Euglena*** | Plants and animals |
| *Proterospongia* | Protozoa and sponges |

- **Living fossils** (unchanged for millions of years): ***Limulus*** (king crab), ***Sphenodon*** (tuatara), ***Latimeria*** (coelacanth), ***Peripatus***, ***Neopilina***, ***Nautilus***, *Ginkgo* (plant).

### 3.2 Morphological and anatomical evidence

**Homologous organs** (divergent evolution), **analogous organs** (convergent evolution), **vestigial organs** and **atavism** — full tables in [Comparative anatomy](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/comparative-anatomy-and-larval-forms). Homology shows **common ancestry**.

### 3.3 Embryological evidence

- Embryos of all vertebrates look alike in early stages — all have **gill slits (pharyngeal pouches)**, a notochord and a tail, even in man.
- **Karl Ernst von Baer (1828)** — general characters appear in the embryo before special ones; embryos of higher animals resemble **embryos** (not adults) of lower animals.
- **Ernst Haeckel — biogenetic law / recapitulation theory (1866)**: "**ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny**" (an individual's development repeats its evolutionary history). NCERT notes that this proposal was **disapproved** on careful study by von Baer — embryos never pass through the *adult* stages of other animals.

### 3.4 Molecular, biogeographical and direct evidence

- **Biochemical**: all life uses DNA, the same genetic code, ATP; similarity of **cytochrome c** and **haemoglobin** sequences tracks relationships (man and chimpanzee differ very little). **Precipitin (serological) test** (Nuttall, 1904) showed man is closest to the great apes.
- **Biogeographical**: island species resemble those of the nearest mainland (**Galápagos** finches resemble South American finches); Australia's unique **marsupials**; the **Wallace line** separating Asian and Australian faunas.
- **Evolution seen directly**:
  - **Industrial melanism** in the **peppered moth *Biston betularia*** (England): before industrialisation (before ~1850) **light-coloured** moths were common because lichen-covered pale tree trunks hid them; after industrialisation soot killed lichens and darkened trunks, so **dark (melanic) moths** escaped birds and became common (counts around 1920). **Bernard Kettlewell** confirmed this by release–recapture in the 1950s; after clean-air laws the light form returned. This is **directional natural selection** by predators. (Lichens are also **indicators of air pollution** — they do not grow in polluted air.)
  - **Antibiotic resistance** in bacteria, **DDT resistance** in mosquitoes, **herbicide resistance** in weeds — **anthropogenic** (human-caused) evolution, seen within years.

## 4. Lamarckism

**Jean-Baptiste Lamarck** (France, 1744–1829) — book ***Philosophie Zoologique* (1809)**; he also used the word "biology" (1802, with Treviranus).

1. **Internal vital force** tends to increase size.
2. **Effect of environment and new needs** create new organs.
3. **Use and disuse of organs** — used organs grow; unused ones shrink.
4. **Inheritance of acquired characters** — changes gained in a lifetime pass to offspring.

Examples he gave: the **giraffe** stretched its neck to reach leaves and passed the longer neck on; snakes lost legs by not using them while crawling through grass; webbed feet of ducks; long legs of wading birds.

**Criticism:**
- **August Weismann** cut the tails of mice for about **20–22 successive generations** (books quote both numbers) — tailless parents still produced tailed young.
- Weismann's **germplasm theory (1892)**: only changes in the **germ cells** (gametes) are inherited; changes in body (somatic) cells are not.
- Pierced ears and noses, a wrestler's muscles, or circumcision over many generations are never inherited.
- **Neo-Lamarckism** tried to revive it; today's **epigenetics** shows a few acquired chemical marks can pass for a generation or two, but Lamarck's mechanism is not accepted.

## 5. Darwinism — natural selection

**Charles Darwin** (England, 1809–1882) sailed round the world on **HMS *Beagle* (1831–1836)**; his observations, especially on the **Galápagos Islands** (finches, tortoises), led him to natural selection.

**Influences:** **Thomas Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (1798) — populations grow geometrically, food arithmetically → struggle; **Charles Lyell**, *Principles of Geology* — the Earth is very old and changes slowly.

**Alfred Russel Wallace**, working in the **Malay Archipelago**, reached the same idea. Their joint papers were read at the **Linnean Society, London, on 1 July 1858**. Darwin published ***On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*** on **24 November 1859**.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q48 asked: "Which is the book in which Darwin published the Theory of Evolution? (A) The Evolution of Species (B) On the Evolution of Species by Means of Natural Selection (C) On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C).** Look for the word "**Origin**", not "Evolution".

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Zoology Q150 asked: "*Origin of Species* by Darwin was published in (A) 1849 (B) 1859 (C) 1869." **Official answer: (B) 1859.**

**Main points of Darwin's theory:**

| Point | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Overproduction (prodigality of reproduction)** | Every species produces far more young than can survive; populations would rise geometrically | Even the slow-breeding **elephant** — one pair could leave about 19 million descendants in 750 years (Darwin's estimate) |
| **Limited resources, constant population** | Food and space are limited, so numbers stay roughly constant | — |
| **Struggle for existence** | Competition for food, space, mates — **intraspecific** (most intense, same species), **interspecific**, and against the **environment** | — |
| **Variation** | No two individuals are alike; small, continuous, heritable variations | — |
| **Survival of the fittest / natural selection** | Individuals with useful variations survive and **reproduce more** ("fitness" = **reproductive fitness**, NCERT). Phrase "survival of the fittest" by **Herbert Spencer** | Giraffes with longer necks got more food |
| **Inheritance and origin of species** | Useful variations accumulate over generations → new species, slowly (**gradualism**) | Darwin's finches |

Darwin's other books: *The Variation of Animals and Plants under Domestication* (1868), ***The Descent of Man* (1871)**, *The Expression of the Emotions* (1872), *The Formation of Vegetable Mould through the Action of Worms* (1881).

**Weaknesses of Darwinism** (what it could not explain):
- **Origin and inheritance of variations** — Darwin knew nothing of genes or mutations (he proposed a wrong "pangenesis" with "gemmules").
- **"Arrival of the fittest"** — it explains the *survival* of fit forms but not how new features first *arise*.
- **Vestigial and over-specialised organs** (huge antlers of the Irish elk).
- **Discontinuous (sudden, large) variations** and neutral variations.

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q50 asked: "In which aspects, Darwin's theory is wrong? (A) High efficiency of reproduction (B) Origin of species (C) Survival of the fittest." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (E) None of the above.** All three listed points — overproduction, origin of species by selection and survival of the fittest — are **accepted parts** of evolutionary theory even today. Darwin's real gap was the **source and inheritance of variation**, which was not offered as an option.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Zoology Q149 asked: "Which one is linked to evolution? (A) Variation (B) Competition (C) Reproduction." **Official answer: (A) Variation.** Variation is the **raw material** of evolution — without differences there is nothing to select. One could argue that competition and over-reproduction are also parts of Darwin's theory (pointing to "D — more than one"), but BPSC's final key gives only **(A)**; follow the key's logic that variation is *the* basis.

> **PYQ:** TRE 2.0 (2023) Zoology Q148 asked: "Heredity or inheritance of specific traits becomes clearer due to (A) Lamarck's theory (B) Mendel's work on green peas (C) Darwinism." **Official answer: (B) Mendel's work on green peas.** Lamarck and Darwin explained *change*; Mendel explained *inheritance*. (Mendel is taught in [Mendelian genetics](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/cell-genetics/mendelian-genetics-and-its-extensions).)

## 6. Mutation theory — Hugo de Vries

- **Hugo de Vries** (Netherlands) — *Die Mutationstheorie* (**1901–1903**); coined the word **mutation**. He was also one of the three **rediscoverers of Mendel's laws (1900)**, with Correns and Tschermak.
- Worked on the evening primrose ***Oenothera lamarckiana*** and found sudden new forms.
- **Mutations** are **sudden, large, discontinuous, heritable** changes; new species can arise in a **single step** — **saltation**.
- NCERT contrast: **Darwinian variations are small and directional; de Vries's mutations are random and directionless.**
- Later work showed most *Oenothera* "mutants" were due to **chromosomal changes** (e.g. the tetraploid *O. gigas*), not gene mutations — but the idea that **mutation is the source of new variation** survived.

## 7. Modern synthetic theory (neo-Darwinism)

Built in the 1930s–40s by **R. A. Fisher, J. B. S. Haldane, Sewall Wright, Theodosius Dobzhansky** (*Genetics and the Origin of Species*, 1937), **Ernst Mayr, G. G. Simpson, Julian Huxley** (*Evolution: The Modern Synthesis*, 1942). It joins Darwin's selection with Mendelian and population genetics.

**Factors of evolution** (G. L. Stebbins listed five basic ones):
1. **Gene mutation** — new alleles.
2. **Chromosomal aberrations** (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation, polyploidy).
3. **Genetic recombination** (crossing-over, independent assortment, fertilisation).
4. **Natural selection**.
5. **Reproductive isolation**.
Plus **gene flow (migration)** and **genetic drift**.

**Evolution** in this view = a **change in allele (gene) frequencies in a population** over generations. The unit of evolution is the **population**, not the individual.

## 8. Hardy–Weinberg principle

**G. H. Hardy** (English mathematician) and **Wilhelm Weinberg** (German physician), **1908**: *allele frequencies in a population remain constant from generation to generation* if no evolutionary force acts. This stable state is **genetic equilibrium**.

<div class="formula">

For one gene with two alleles A (frequency **p**) and a (frequency **q**):

**p + q = 1**

**p² + 2pq + q² = 1**

p² = frequency of **AA**, 2pq = frequency of **Aa** (carriers), q² = frequency of **aa**.

**Conditions:** large population, **random mating**, **no mutation**, **no gene flow (migration)**, **no natural selection**, (and so no genetic drift).

**Factors that disturb it (NCERT):** gene migration / gene flow, genetic drift, mutation, genetic recombination, natural selection.

</div>

### Worked numericals

**Problem 1.** In a population, 16% of people show a recessive trait (aa). Find allele and genotype frequencies.
- q² = 0.16 → **q = 0.4**; p = 1 − 0.4 = **0.6**.
- AA = p² = **0.36 (36%)**; Aa = 2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = **0.48 (48%)**; aa = **0.16**. Check: 0.36 + 0.48 + 0.16 = 1 (correct).

**Problem 2.** Albinism (recessive) occurs in 1 in 10,000 people. What fraction are carriers?
- q² = 1/10,000 = 0.0001 → **q = 0.01**; p = 0.99.
- Carriers 2pq = 2 × 0.99 × 0.01 = **0.0198 ≈ 2%** (about 1 in 50). Note: carriers are about **200 times** commoner than albinos.

**Problem 3.** In 1000 individuals: AA = 490, Aa = 420, aa = 90. Find p and q and test equilibrium.
- Total alleles = 2000. A alleles = 2 × 490 + 420 = 1400 → **p = 0.7**; **q = 0.3**.
- Expected: p² = 0.49 (490), 2pq = 0.42 (420), q² = 0.09 (90) — exactly as observed → **population is in equilibrium**.

**Problem 4.** 91% of a population shows the dominant phenotype. Find the frequency of heterozygotes.
- Recessive phenotype = 9% → q² = 0.09 → q = 0.3, p = 0.7 → 2pq = **0.42 (42%)**.

**Problem 5 (X-linked).** 8% of men in a population are red–green colour-blind. Find the frequency of colour-blind women and carrier women.
- In males (one X), frequency of affected males = q → **q = 0.08**, p = 0.92.
- Colour-blind women = q² = **0.0064 (0.64%)**; carrier women = 2pq = 2 × 0.92 × 0.08 = **0.147 (about 14.7%)**.

**Problem 6.** If p = 0.5 for one allele of a gene with two alleles, what proportion of the population is heterozygous? — 2pq = 2 × 0.5 × 0.5 = **0.5 (50%)** — the **maximum possible** heterozygosity for two alleles.

> **Exam angle:** Number of possible genotypes for *n* alleles of a gene = **n(n + 1)/2**. For the ABO gene (3 alleles: Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i) → 3 × 4 / 2 = **6 genotypes** (but only 4 phenotypes).

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Stabilising (normalising)** | Individuals near the **mean** are favoured; extremes removed; variation decreases | **Human birth weight** — very light and very heavy babies have lower survival |
| **Directional** | One **extreme** is favoured; the mean shifts | **Industrial melanism**, antibiotic and DDT resistance, longer giraffe necks |
| **Disruptive (diversifying)** | **Both extremes** favoured over the mean → two peaks; can split a species | African seed-cracker finches with small and large beaks (medium beaks poor at both seed types) |

Other forms: **sexual selection** (peacock's tail — choice by females), **kin selection** (helping relatives — worker bees), **artificial selection** (breeds of dogs, cattle, wheat — selection by man).

### Genetic drift, founder effect and bottleneck

- **Genetic drift** (**Sewall Wright effect**) — **random change in allele frequency by chance**, strongest in **small populations**; some alleles may be lost or **fixed** without any selection.
- **Founder effect** — a few individuals start a new isolated population; it carries only a sample of the original alleles (e.g. high frequency of a rare polydactyly syndrome among the **Amish** of Pennsylvania). NCERT: the original drifted population becomes the **founder**.
- **Bottleneck effect** — a population crashes to a few survivors, losing variation (**cheetah**, northern elephant seal, Asiatic lions of Gir).

**Gene flow (migration)** — movement of alleles between populations by migration of individuals or gametes; makes populations similar and can add new alleles.

## 10. Species and speciation

**Biological species concept (Ernst Mayr, 1942):** a species is a group of **actually or potentially interbreeding natural populations reproductively isolated** from other such groups.

### Modes of speciation

| Mode | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Allopatric** ("other country") | Population split by a **geographical barrier** (mountain, river, sea); each part changes separately; later cannot interbreed | **Darwin's finches** on separate islands; Kaibab and Abert squirrels on two sides of the Grand Canyon |
| **Sympatric** ("same country") | New species arises **within the same area**, without a physical barrier — by **polyploidy** (mainly plants), disruptive selection, host shifts | Bread wheat (hexaploid), ***Raphanobrassica*** (Karpechenko, 1927); apple maggot fly host races |
| Parapatric | Neighbouring populations with a narrow contact zone diverge along a gradient | Grasses on mine tailings |
| Peripatric | A small peripheral (founder) population diverges | Island colonists |

### Isolating mechanisms

| Group | Mechanism | Meaning / example |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-zygotic** (prevent mating or fertilisation) | **Habitat (ecological)** | Live in different habitats in the same area |
| | **Temporal (seasonal)** | Breed at different times or seasons |
| | **Behavioural (ethological)** | Different courtship songs, dances, **firefly flash patterns** |
| | **Mechanical** | Genitalia (or flower parts) do not fit |
| | **Gametic** | Sperm cannot fertilise the egg (sea urchin species) |
| **Post-zygotic** (after hybrid forms) | **Hybrid inviability** | Hybrid embryo dies |
| | **Hybrid sterility** | **Mule** (female horse × male donkey) is sterile — horse 2n = 64, donkey 2n = 62, mule **63** (odd, so meiosis fails). **Hinny** = male horse × female donkey |
| | **Hybrid breakdown** | F₁ fertile, but F₂ weak or sterile |

Big-cat hybrids: **liger** (male lion × tigress), **tigon** (male tiger × lioness) — usually sterile males.

## 11. Patterns of evolution

- **Adaptive radiation** (term by **H. F. Osborn, 1902**) — evolution of **many different forms from one ancestral stock**, radiating into different habitats/niches. NCERT examples: **Darwin's finches** (from a seed-eating ancestor came insect-eating, cactus-eating and other forms with different **beaks**) and **Australian marsupials** (from one ancestral stock on the island continent). Placental mammals also radiated (mole, bat, whale, horse …). It is **divergent evolution**.
- **Convergent evolution** — unrelated forms in similar habitats become alike. NCERT pairs (placental — Australian marsupial): **wolf — Tasmanian wolf**, **anteater — numbat (banded anteater)**, **mouse — marsupial mouse**, **mole — marsupial mole**, **flying squirrel — flying phalanger (sugar glider)**, **lemur — spotted cuscus**, **bobcat — tiger cat**.
- **Parallel evolution** — related groups evolving similar features independently.
- **Co-evolution** — two species evolve in response to each other: **fig and fig wasp**, flowers and pollinators, **Calvaria tree and the dodo** (below).
- **Microevolution** (small changes within a species — allele frequencies) vs **macroevolution** (origin of new groups over long times).
- **Gradualism** (Darwin — slow steady change) vs **punctuated equilibrium** (**Eldredge and Gould, 1972** — long stable periods broken by short bursts of rapid change).

### Extinction

- **Five mass extinctions**: end-Ordovician, late Devonian, **end-Permian (the largest, ~252 mya, "Great Dying")**, end-Triassic, **end-Cretaceous (~66 mya — dinosaurs)**. Many scientists call today's human-driven loss the **sixth extinction**.
- **Co-extinction** (NCERT, biodiversity) — when a species dies out, species tied to it in an obligatory way also die out (a host fish and its unique parasites; a plant and its only pollinator).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q119 asked: "Which of the following trees got nearly extinct with the extinction of dodo animal? (A) *Calvaria* (B) Palm tree (C) *Cinchona*." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) *Calvaria*.** *Calvaria major* (tambalacoque) of **Mauritius** was said to need its hard seeds to pass through the dodo's gut before they could germinate; after the dodo became extinct (late 17th century) few new trees appeared (Stanley Temple's 1977 hypothesis — later doubted, but it is the standard exam example of co-extinction/mutualism). *Cinchona* gives quinine and is unrelated.

## 12. Human evolution

| Stage (NCERT order) | Time | Brain (cc) | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Dryopithecus*** and ***Ramapithecus*** | about **15 mya** | — | Hairy, walked like gorillas and chimpanzees; ***Ramapithecus* more man-like**, *Dryopithecus* more **ape-like**. *Ramapithecus* fossils from the **Siwalik hills** (India) — now grouped with *Sivapithecus* (orang-utan line) |

| ***Homo habilis*** ("handy man") | about **2 mya** | **650–800** | **First human-like being**; made stone tools; **probably did not eat meat** (NCERT); Olduvai Gorge (Leakeys) |
| ***Homo erectus*** | about **1.5 mya** | about **900** | **Probably ate meat**; used fire; **Java man (1891, Dubois)**, Peking man |
| **Neanderthal man** (*H. neanderthalensis*) | **1,00,000–40,000 years ago** | about **1400** | Lived in the **Near East and Central Asia** (and Europe); used **hides** to protect the body; **buried their dead** |

Newer findings beyond NCERT: oldest *H. sapiens* fossils about **3,00,000 years** (Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, 2017); **Cro-Magnon** (France, 1868) is an early modern European; **Svante Pääbo** (Nobel 2022) sequenced the **Neanderthal genome** — non-Africans carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA; **Narmada man** (Hathnora, Madhya Pradesh, 1982, Arun Sonakia) is India's oldest hominin skull fossil; **Bhimbetka** rock shelters (MP) show prehistoric cave paintings.

**Trends in human evolution:** **bipedal** upright walking (S-shaped spine, broad pelvis, arched foot), **increase in brain size**, smaller jaws and teeth, appearance of a **chin**, flatter face, **opposable thumb** with precision grip, less body hair, tool use, language and culture.

## Traps and exceptions

- The title is ***On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*** — not "Evolution of Species"; year **1859** (Wallace–Darwin papers **1858**).
- "**Survival of the fittest**" was coined by **Herbert Spencer**, not Darwin.
- **Lamarck — use and disuse; inheritance of acquired characters**; **Weismann** disproved it (mouse tails; germplasm).
- **de Vries — mutation, saltation, *Oenothera***; Darwin — small continuous variations.
- Early atmosphere was **reducing** — **no free oxygen**.
- Miller–Urey gases: **CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O** (no O₂, no N₂ in the classic set-up).
- **Industrial melanism** is **directional** selection; human birth weight is **stabilising**.
- **Founder effect and bottleneck are forms of genetic drift**, not selection.
- **Mule is sterile** (63 chromosomes) — post-zygotic isolation.
- **Adaptive radiation = divergent**; Australian marsupial vs placental look-alikes = **convergent**.
- *Ramapithecus* more man-like; *Dryopithecus* more ape-like.
- **Homo habilis did not eat meat; Homo erectus probably did** (NCERT).
- **Neanderthals buried their dead** (1400 cc).
- Haeckel's recapitulation is **not accepted** in its original form.
- TRE key: Darwin's theory is "wrong" in **none** of overproduction, origin of species, survival of the fittest.

## Practice with answers

1. Who showed that boiled broth in a swan-neck flask stays sterile? — **Louis Pasteur**.
2. Which gas was absent from the primitive atmosphere? — **Free oxygen**.
3. What energy source did Miller use? — **Electric discharge (sparks)**.
4. What are coacervates? — **Colloidal droplets of organic molecules surrounded by a water layer (Oparin's pre-cells)**.
5. Name two evidences that show evolution is happening now. — **Industrial melanism and antibiotic/pesticide resistance**.
6. What did Weismann's mouse-tail experiment disprove? — **Inheritance of acquired characters (Lamarckism)**.
7. Whose essay on population influenced Darwin? — **Thomas Malthus**.
8. Which struggle is most intense according to Darwin? — **Intraspecific** (same species).
9. What is "fitness" in Darwinism (NCERT)? — **Reproductive fitness** — leaving more offspring.
10. What did de Vries call sudden large changes? — **Mutations** (saltation).
11. If q² = 0.25, what is the frequency of heterozygotes? — **0.5** (q = 0.5, p = 0.5, 2pq = 0.5).
12. If 1 in 400 people has a recessive disease, what is q? — **0.05** (√(1/400)).
13. Which type of selection shifts the mean of a trait? — **Directional selection**.
14. Which type of selection keeps human birth weight near the average? — **Stabilising selection**.
15. What is the founder effect? — **Genetic drift when a few individuals start a new population**.
16. Two frog species breed in different months. Which isolation? — **Temporal (seasonal) isolation**.
17. Why is a mule sterile? — **Odd chromosome number (63); homologues cannot pair in meiosis**.
18. Which fossil hominid was found in Java in 1891? — ***Homo erectus*** (Java man).
19. Which period is called the Age of Fishes? — **Devonian**.
20. In which period did *Archaeopteryx* live? — **Jurassic**.
