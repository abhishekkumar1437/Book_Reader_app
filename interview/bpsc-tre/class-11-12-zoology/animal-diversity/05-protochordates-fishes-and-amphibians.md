This chapter starts the chordates. NCERT class 11 lists the features of Chordata, the three subphyla and the classes Cyclostomata, Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes and Amphibia; the B.Sc. syllabus adds the type studies *Balanoglossus*, *Herdmania* and *Amphioxus*, fish scales and migration, and frog metamorphosis. No TRE 1.0/2.0 Zoology question came from here, which makes it a likely area for TRE 4.0 — expect "retrogressive metamorphosis is seen in", "notochord from head to tail throughout life", "air bladder absent in", "larva of lamprey".

> **In simple words:** Chordates are animals that, at least as embryos, have a stiff rod (notochord) along the back, a hollow nerve cord above it, slits in the throat (gill slits) and a tail behind the anus. The simplest chordates (sea squirts, *Amphioxus*) never get a backbone. Vertebrates replace the notochord with a backbone. Fishes live in water and breathe by gills; amphibians start life in water (tadpole) and change into land-living adults.

## 1. Phylum Chordata — the four fundamental characters

1. **Notochord** — a rod-like, **mesodermal** support on the dorsal side, between gut and nerve cord.
2. **Dorsal, hollow, single nerve cord** (formed from ectoderm by infolding).
3. **Paired pharyngeal gill slits** — openings in the throat wall.
4. **Post-anal tail** — the body continues beyond the anus.

Also: bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, coelomate, organ-system level, **closed circulatory system**, **ventral heart**, an **endostyle** (or its descendant, the thyroid gland).

### Chordates vs non-chordates (NCERT table)

| Character | Chordates | Non-chordates |
|---|---|---|
| Notochord | **Present** | Absent |
| Central nervous system | **Dorsal, hollow, single** | **Ventral, solid, double** |
| Pharynx | **Perforated by gill slits** | No gill slits |
| Heart | **Ventral** | **Dorsal** (if present) |
| Post-anal tail | **Present** | Absent |

### Subphyla

| Subphylum | Notochord | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Urochordata (Tunicata)** | **Only in the larval tail** | *Ascidia*, *Salpa*, *Doliolum*, ***Herdmania*** |
| **Cephalochordata** | **From head to tail, throughout life** | ***Branchiostoma* (*Amphioxus*, lancelet)** |
| **Vertebrata** | **Only in the embryo**, replaced by the vertebral column | Fishes to mammals |

**Protochordates** = Urochordata + Cephalochordata — marine chordates without a vertebral column (older books also add Hemichordata).

> **Remember:** "**All vertebrates are chordates but all chordates are not vertebrates.**"

## 2. Hemichordata — *Balanoglossus*

NCERT now treats Hemichordata as a **separate non-chordate phylum** (earlier a subphylum of Chordata).

- **Worm-like**, marine, burrows in sand (U-shaped burrows); called **acorn worm** or tongue worm.
- Body in three parts: **proboscis**, **collar** and long **trunk** (hence enterocoelous coelom in three parts — protocoel, mesocoel, metacoel).
- A short hollow outgrowth of the gut into the proboscis, the **stomochord** (buccal diverticulum), was once taken as a notochord — now considered **not homologous** to a notochord.
- **Pharyngeal gill slits** present (many pairs) — the chordate-like feature.
- **Open circulatory system**; respiration through **gills**; excretion by the **proboscis gland** (glomerulus).
- **Sexes separate**, external fertilisation, indirect development with the **tornaria** larva (very like the **bipinnaria** of starfish).
- Examples: ***Balanoglossus***, ***Saccoglossus***, *Ptychodera*.

> **Exam angle:** *Balanoglossus* is a **connecting link between non-chordates (echinoderms) and chordates** — because of its tornaria larva (echinoderm-like) and gill slits (chordate-like).

## 3. Urochordata — *Herdmania* and retrogressive metamorphosis

**Urochordates (tunicates)** are marine. Adults are mostly **sessile sea squirts (ascidians)**; *Salpa* and *Doliolum* float; *Oikopleura* keeps its larval tail all life (neoteny).

**Adult *Herdmania pallida*** (Indian sea squirt, attached to rocks):
- Body enclosed in a tough covering, the **tunic (test)**, made of **tunicin** — a cellulose-like substance (**cellulose is otherwise very rare in animals**).
- Two openings: **branchial (incurrent) siphon** and **atrial (excurrent) siphon**.
- Huge **pharynx** perforated by numerous gill slits (**stigmata**), with an **endostyle** (makes mucus to trap food) — **filter feeder**.
- Nervous system reduced to a single **nerve ganglion** with a **neural gland**; no notochord, no nerve cord.
- Heart shows **periodic reversal** of beat (blood flows one way, then the other).
- **Hermaphrodite**; cross-fertilisation.

**The larva — ascidian tadpole:** free-swimming, non-feeding, lives only a few hours. It has **all chordate characters**: a **notochord in the tail** (hence "uro" = tail chordate), a **dorsal hollow nerve cord**, **gill slits**, sense organs (an eye-spot or ocellus and a statocyst) and three **adhesive papillae** in front.

**Retrogressive metamorphosis** = the change from an **advanced, active larva into a simple, degenerate, sessile adult**:
1. Larva attaches to a rock by its **adhesive papillae**.
2. **Tail with notochord, nerve cord and muscles is absorbed**.
3. Nerve cord shrinks into a **single ganglion**; sense organs disappear.
4. Pharynx enlarges and gill slits multiply; the body **rotates about 180°** so that the siphons face upward.
5. Tunic forms → sessile adult.

> **Common mistake:** Frog metamorphosis is **progressive** (the adult is more complex than the tadpole). *Herdmania* metamorphosis is **retrogressive** (the adult loses chordate features). Do not mix the two "tadpoles".

Some ascidians store the rare metal **vanadium** in blood cells (vanadocytes).

## 4. Cephalochordata — *Amphioxus* (*Branchiostoma*)

- Small (about 5–8 cm), translucent, fish-like, marine; lives buried in sand with only the front end out; called **lancelet**.
- **Notochord extends from the tip of the snout to the tail** and persists **throughout life** — it even goes in front of the nerve cord (hence "cephalo" = head chordate).
- **No head, no brain, no skull** (acraniate); no paired fins; no jaws; no scales.
- Body muscles in **V-shaped (arrow-head shaped) segments, the myotomes** — metamerism.
- **Oral hood** with **buccal cirri**; **wheel organ** (ciliary); **velum** with tentacles.
- Large pharynx with very many gill slits, an **endostyle** (homologous to the **thyroid** of vertebrates), opening into an **atrium** that leads out by the **atriopore** — **ciliary filter feeding**.
- **Closed circulation but no heart** (contractile vessels pump blood); blood **colourless, without respiratory pigment**.
- Excretion by protonephridia with **solenocytes** (flame-cell-like) — unusual for a chordate.
- **Sexes separate**; gonads repeated metamerically (about 26 pairs); external fertilisation; ciliated larva.
- Seen as the "**blueprint of the vertebrate body plan**".

| Feature | *Herdmania* (Urochordata) | *Amphioxus* (Cephalochordata) |
|---|---|---|
| Notochord | Larval tail only | Snout to tail, lifelong |
| Nerve cord in adult | Reduced to a ganglion | Present, dorsal, hollow |
| Habit | Sessile adult | Free, burrowing |
| Covering | **Tunic (tunicin)** | Thin epidermis |
| Metamerism | Absent | **Myotomes**, gonads |
| Sexes | Hermaphrodite | Separate |
| Metamorphosis | **Retrogressive** | Simple, progressive |

## 5. Subphylum Vertebrata — the plan

**Vertebrates** have a notochord in the embryo that is **replaced by a cartilaginous or bony vertebral column** in the adult. Also: a **cranium** (skull) protecting the brain (so also called Craniata); **ventral muscular heart with two, three or four chambers**; **kidneys** for excretion and osmoregulation; **paired appendages** (fins or limbs).

Other groupings: **Anamniota** (no amnion — cyclostomes, fishes, amphibians) vs **Amniota** (reptiles, birds, mammals); **poikilotherms/cold-blooded** (fishes, amphibians, reptiles) vs **homeotherms/warm-blooded** (birds, mammals).

## 6. Class Cyclostomata — jawless vertebrates

- All living members are **ectoparasites on fishes** (lampreys) or scavengers (hagfishes).
- Elongated eel-like body; **6–15 pairs of gill slits**.
- **Sucking, circular mouth without jaws** (cyclostome = round mouth), with horny teeth and a rasping tongue.
- Body **without scales and without paired fins**; a single median nostril.
- Cranium and vertebral column **cartilaginous**; notochord persists.
- Closed circulation; two-chambered heart.
- Lampreys are **marine but migrate to fresh water for spawning**; **after spawning, within a few days, they die**. Their larvae, the **ammocoete**, live in fresh-water mud for several years, then metamorphose and return to the sea.
- Examples: ***Petromyzon*** (lamprey), ***Myxine*** (hagfish — wholly marine, makes huge amounts of slime, direct development).

> **Exam angle:** The **ammocoete** larva of the lamprey looks so much like *Amphioxus* (endostyle, filter feeding) that it was once described as a separate animal. Its **endostyle becomes the thyroid gland** — strong evidence that the thyroid evolved from the endostyle.

## 7. Fishes — Pisces

### Chondrichthyes vs Osteichthyes

| Feature | **Chondrichthyes** (cartilaginous fishes) | **Osteichthyes** (bony fishes) |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | **Marine** | Marine and fresh water |
| Endoskeleton | **Cartilage**; notochord persists throughout life | **Bone** |
| Mouth | **Ventral** | Mostly **terminal** |
| Gill slits | **5–7 pairs, separate, no operculum** | **Four pairs of gills covered by an operculum** on each side |
| Scales | **Placoid** (minute, tooth-like) | **Cycloid or ctenoid** (ganoid in some) |
| Teeth | **Modified placoid scales, backwardly directed** | Fixed to jaws |
| Air (swim) bladder | **Absent** → must **swim constantly** to avoid sinking | **Present** → regulates buoyancy |
| Tail | **Heterocercal** (upper lobe bigger) | **Homocercal** (lobes equal) |
| Fertilisation | **Internal**; males have **claspers** on pelvic fins | Usually **external** |
| Development | Many **viviparous** | Mostly **oviparous** |
| Intestine | **Spiral valve** | No spiral valve |
| Examples | ***Scoliodon*** (dogfish), ***Pristis*** (sawfish), ***Carcharodon*** (great white shark), ***Trygon*** (stingray), ***Torpedo*** (electric ray) | Marine: ***Exocoetus*** (flying fish), ***Hippocampus*** (sea horse); freshwater: ***Labeo*** (rohu), ***Catla***, ***Clarias*** (magur); aquarium: ***Betta*** (fighting fish), ***Pterophyllum*** (angel fish) |

Common to both: **two-chambered heart** (one auricle, one ventricle — a "venous heart" carrying only deoxygenated blood), **cold-blooded**, sexes separate, gill respiration, **lateral line** sense organs.

Extra points: *Torpedo* has **electric organs** (modified muscles); *Trygon* has a **poison sting**; sharks keep **urea** in blood for osmotic balance in sea water; electroreceptors called **ampullae of Lorenzini**. Largest fish — **whale shark *Rhincodon typus***.

### Fish scales

| Scale | Origin / structure | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| **Placoid** | Dermal + epidermal; a basal plate with a backward spine of **dentine covered by enamel-like vitrodentine** — like a tooth ("dermal denticle") | **Sharks, rays** |
| **Ganoid** | Rhomboid, thick, covered by shiny **ganoine** | *Lepisosteus* (gar), *Polypterus*, *Acipenser* (sturgeon — bony plates) |
| **Cycloid** | Thin, round, **smooth hind edge**, growth rings | **Carps (*Labeo*, *Catla*)**, lungfishes |
| **Ctenoid** | Thin, round, hind edge with **comb-like teeth** | **Perches**, *Anabas*, advanced bony fishes |
| Cosmoid | Thick, with cosmine | Extinct lobe-fins, coelacanth (modified) |

> **Memory trick:** **C**ycloid = **C**ircle smooth; **Ct**enoid = **C**omb **t**eeth; **P**lacoid = **P**ointed tooth-like (sharks); **G**anoid = **G**lossy (gars).

Rings on cycloid/ctenoid scales are used to **find the age of a fish**.

### Fins and tails

- **Paired fins**: pectoral and pelvic (homologous to tetrapod fore- and hindlimbs). **Unpaired**: dorsal, anal, caudal.
- **Tail types**: **protocercal** (equal, notochord straight — cyclostomes, *Amphioxus*), **heterocercal** (sharks, sturgeon), **homocercal** (most bony fishes), **diphycercal** (lungfishes, coelacanth).

### Swim bladder (air bladder)

- An outgrowth of the **gut** (oesophagus) filled with gases; controls **buoyancy** so the fish can stay at any depth without swimming. It is **homologous to the lungs** of tetrapods.
- **Physostomous** — open duct to the gut (carps, catfishes, eels); **physoclistous** — duct closed, gas exchanged through a gas gland and an "oval" (perches and most advanced teleosts).
- In carps and catfishes a chain of small bones, the **Weberian ossicles**, links the swim bladder to the inner ear — better hearing.
- **Absent** in sharks and rays and in some bottom-living bony fishes (flatfishes). In **lungfishes** it works as a **lung**.

### Air-breathing (accessory respiratory) fishes — common in Bihar's ponds

| Fish | Local name | Accessory organ |
|---|---|---|
| *Anabas testudineus* | Koi, **climbing perch** | **Labyrinthine organ** |
| *Clarias batrachus* | **Magur** | **Arborescent (tree-like) organ** |
| *Heteropneustes fossilis* | **Singhi** | Long **air sacs** |
| *Channa* spp. | Murrel, saur | Suprabranchial chamber |
| *Amphipnous cuchia* | Cuchia (eel-like) | Air sacs |

These fishes survive in muddy, oxygen-poor water and are sold live.

### Fish migration

| Type | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Anadromous** | Live in the **sea**, go up **rivers to spawn** | **Salmon**, **Hilsa (*Tenualosa ilisha*)**, lamprey, sturgeon |
| **Catadromous** | Live in **fresh water**, go to the **sea to spawn** | **Eel (*Anguilla*)** — spawns in the **Sargasso Sea**; its leaf-like larva, the **leptocephalus**, drifts back |
| Potamodromous | Wholly within fresh water | Mahseer, many carps |
| Oceanodromous | Wholly within the sea | Tuna, herring |

- Salmon return to the stream of their birth (**homing**) by **smell** (olfactory imprinting).
- The **Farakka barrage (1975)** on the Ganga blocked the upstream breeding migration of **Hilsa**, and hilsa catches in Bihar fell sharply.

### Living fossils and remarkable fishes

- ***Latimeria chalumnae*** — **coelacanth**, a lobe-finned fish thought extinct for ~66 million years, found alive off **South Africa in 1938**. Lobe-fins (Crossopterygii) are the group from which **tetrapods** arose.
- **Lungfishes (Dipnoi)**: ***Protopterus*** (Africa), ***Lepidosiren*** (South America), ***Neoceratodus*** (Australia). *Protopterus* aestivates in a mucus cocoon in dry mud — a **fish–amphibian connecting link**.
- ***Hippocampus*** (sea horse) — the **male** carries the eggs in a brood pouch.
- ***Gambusia affinis*** (mosquito fish) — **larvivorous**, released to control mosquito larvae (malaria).
- ***Echeneis*** (remora / sucker fish) — attaches to sharks (**commensalism**).
- ***Electrophorus*** (electric "eel", actually a bony knife-fish) — up to about 600 V.
- Indian major carps: **rohu (*Labeo rohita*), catla (*Catla catla*), mrigal (*Cirrhinus mrigala*)** — see [Economic zoology](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/ecology-applied/economic-zoology) for composite fish culture.

## 8. Class Amphibia — two lives

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Can live in **aquatic as well as terrestrial** habitats (*amphi* = dual, *bios* = life).
- Most have **two pairs of limbs**; body = **head and trunk**; tail in some.
- **Skin moist, without scales** (glandular, used in breathing).
- **Eyes have eyelids**; a **tympanum** represents the ear.
- Alimentary canal, urinary and reproductive tracts open into a common chamber, the **cloaca**.
- Respiration by **gills, lungs and skin**.
- **Heart three-chambered** (two auricles, one ventricle).
- **Cold-blooded**; sexes separate; **external fertilisation**; **oviparous**; **indirect development** (larva = tadpole).
- Examples: ***Bufo*** (toad), ***Rana*** (frog), ***Hyla*** (tree frog), ***Salamandra*** (salamander), ***Ichthyophis*** (limbless amphibian).

### Orders

| Order | Meaning | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apoda (Gymnophiona)** | Without legs | **Limbless**, worm-like, burrowing; some have tiny **dermal scales** | ***Ichthyophis*** (caecilian) |
| **Urodela (Caudata)** | Tailed | Tail all life, two pairs of similar limbs | ***Salamandra***, *Triturus* (newt), ***Ambystoma*** (axolotl), *Necturus* (mudpuppy), *Proteus* (olm) |
| **Anura (Salientia)** | Without tail | **Tailless adult**, long jumping hind legs | ***Rana***, ***Bufo***, ***Hyla***, *Rhacophorus* (flying frog) |

### Frog — *Rana tigrina* (Indian bullfrog; now *Hoplobatrachus tigerinus*)

| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Body | Head and trunk; **no neck, no tail** |
| Skin | Smooth, slippery (mucus); can change colour (**camouflage**); absorbs water — frogs **never drink** water |
| Eyes | Bulging, with a **nictitating membrane** that protects the eye in water |
| Ear | **Tympanum**; no external ear |
| Limbs | Forelimbs **4 digits**, hindlimbs **5 digits** (webbed) |
| Sexual dimorphism | Male has **vocal sacs** and a **copulatory (nuptial) pad** on the first finger of the forelimb |
| Respiration | **Skin** in water (and during **aestivation and hibernation**); **lungs** and buccal cavity on land |
| Heart | **Three chambers** + **sinus venosus** (dorsal) + **conus arteriosus** (ventral); **hepatic portal** and **renal portal** systems |
| Blood | **RBCs nucleated**, with haemoglobin |
| Excretion | Pair of kidneys; adult is **ureotelic** (tadpole **ammonotelic**); in males the ureter carries both urine and sperm (**urinogenital duct**) |
| Nervous | Brain in three parts; **10 pairs of cranial nerves** |
| Skeleton | 9 vertebrae + rod-like **urostyle**; no ribs |
| Reproduction | Female lays **2500–3000 ova** at a time; **external fertilisation in water** |
| Seasonal sleep | **Hibernation** (winter sleep) and **aestivation** (summer sleep) |

### Metamorphosis — tadpole to frog

Controlled by the thyroid hormone **thyroxine** (needs **iodine**). **Gudernatsch (1912)** fed tadpoles thyroid tissue and they metamorphosed early into tiny frogs; tadpoles without a thyroid (or in iodine-free water) stay as **giant tadpoles**.

| Tadpole | Frog |
|---|---|
| Tail and fins, **lateral line** | Tail absorbed (by **lysosomal enzymes** — programmed cell death); lateral line lost |
| **Gills** (external → internal) | **Lungs** |
| **Herbivorous**, long coiled gut, horny jaws | **Carnivorous** (insects), short gut, true jaws, long sticky tongue |
| **Ammonotelic** | **Ureotelic** |
| Two-chambered (fish-like) heart and circulation | Three-chambered heart |
| No limbs — **hind limbs appear first**, then forelimbs | Two pairs of limbs |

**Neoteny** (larva becomes sexually mature without metamorphosis) — the **axolotl** of *Ambystoma*; details in [Comparative anatomy and larval forms](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/comparative-anatomy-and-larval-forms).

### Parental care in amphibians

| Animal | Care |
|---|---|
| ***Alytes*** (midwife toad) | **Male** carries egg strings wound round its hind legs |
| ***Rhinoderma*** (Darwin's frog) | Male keeps the eggs and tadpoles in his **vocal sac** |
| ***Pipa*** (Surinam toad) | Eggs develop in pits in the **skin of the female's back** |
| *Hyla* (some tree frogs) | Mud nests / foam nests |
| ***Ichthyophis*** | Female coils round her eggs |

Frogs are useful: they eat insects (crop protection) and keep the food chain balanced; frog legs are eaten in some countries (export banned in India since 1987).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Hemichordata** is no longer a chordate subphylum — the stomochord is not a notochord.
- In **urochordates the notochord is only in the larval tail**; in cephalochordates it is lifelong and reaches the snout.
- **Herdmania metamorphosis is retrogressive**; frog metamorphosis is progressive.
- **Tunicin** (cellulose-like) occurs in the tunic of urochordates — a rare animal cellulose.
- ***Amphioxus* has no heart** (but a closed circulation) and excretes by **solenocytes**.
- **Lampreys die soon after spawning**; their larva is the **ammocoete**.
- **Sharks have no swim bladder** and must keep swimming; their teeth are modified placoid scales.
- Notochord **persists throughout life in Chondrichthyes** (NCERT) and cyclostomes.
- *Electrophorus* (electric eel) is a bony fish; *Torpedo* (electric ray) is cartilaginous.
- **Hilsa is anadromous**, **eel is catadromous**.
- **Apoda (*Ichthyophis*)** may have small dermal scales — an exception to "amphibians have no scales".
- Fish heart is **two-chambered and carries only deoxygenated blood**.
- Male frog — **vocal sacs and nuptial pads**; frog does not drink water.

## Quick revision

1. Four chordate characters — **notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail**.
2. Notochord only in larval tail — **Urochordata**.
3. Notochord from head to tail throughout life — **Cephalochordata (*Amphioxus*)**.
4. Retrogressive metamorphosis — ***Herdmania*** (ascidians).
5. Tunic made of — **tunicin**.
6. *Amphioxus* excretory cells — **solenocytes**.
7. Endostyle evolves into — **thyroid gland**.
8. Larva of *Balanoglossus* — **tornaria**.
9. Jawless vertebrates — **Cyclostomata** (*Petromyzon*, *Myxine*).
10. Larva of lamprey — **ammocoete**.
11. Placoid scales — **cartilaginous fishes**.
12. Cycloid scales — **carps (*Labeo*)**; ctenoid — **perches**.
13. Air bladder absent — **Chondrichthyes**.
14. Claspers — **male cartilaginous fishes** (pelvic fins).
15. Weberian ossicles — **carps and catfishes**.
16. Climbing perch — ***Anabas***; labyrinthine organ.
17. Anadromous — **Hilsa, salmon**; catadromous — **eel**.
18. Living fossil fish — ***Latimeria*** (1938).
19. Male broods eggs — ***Hippocampus***.
20. Limbless amphibian — ***Ichthyophis***.
21. Frog heart — **three-chambered** (2 auricles + 1 ventricle).
22. Metamorphosis hormone — **thyroxine** (iodine).
23. Cranial nerves of frog — **10 pairs**.
24. Larvivorous fish — ***Gambusia***.

## Practice with answers

1. Where does the heart of a chordate lie? — **Ventral** (below the gut).
2. Which chordate subphylum lacks a notochord in the adult? — **Urochordata**.
3. What attaches the ascidian tadpole to a rock? — **Adhesive papillae**.
4. Name the Indian sea squirt studied as a type. — ***Herdmania pallida***.
5. Why is *Amphioxus* called acraniate? — **It has no skull (cranium) or brain**.
6. What are myotomes? — **V-shaped segmental muscle blocks** (*Amphioxus*, fishes).
7. Give the two parts of the *Balanoglossus* body besides the trunk. — **Proboscis and collar**.
8. How many gill slits do cyclostomes have? — **6 to 15 pairs**.
9. Why must a shark keep swimming? — **It has no swim bladder**.
10. Which tail type do sharks have? — **Heterocercal**.
11. Which scale is covered by ganoine? — **Ganoid** (gars, *Polypterus*).
12. What is a physostomous swim bladder? — **One with an open duct to the gut** (carps).
13. Which fish breathes air with an arborescent organ? — ***Clarias* (magur)**.
14. Where does the eel spawn? — **Sargasso Sea** (catadromous migration).
15. Which barrage affected hilsa migration in Bihar? — **Farakka barrage** (1975).
16. Name the three lungfish genera. — ***Protopterus*, *Lepidosiren*, *Neoceratodus***.
17. Which amphibian order has a tail throughout life? — **Urodela** (salamanders, newts).
18. What protects a frog's eyes under water? — **Nictitating membrane**.
19. Tadpole excretes which nitrogenous waste? — **Ammonia** (ammonotelic).
20. Which toad male carries eggs on his legs? — ***Alytes*** (midwife toad).
