This chapter is the foundation of the whole book. It covers NCERT class 11 "The Living World" and the first half of "Animal Kingdom", then adds the B.Sc. points (protostome vs deuterostome, cleavage types, coelom formation, ICZN rules). TRE asks it as one-liners: "father of biological classification", "which phylum is exclusively marine", "serial repetition of organs is called".

> **In simple words:** There are more than a million known kinds of animals. To study them we put similar animals in one group, give every animal one fixed two-word scientific name, and arrange groups one inside another (species inside genus, genus inside family, and so on). To decide the big groups (phyla) we look at a few body features: how well cells are organised, whether the body can be cut into equal halves, how many embryonic layers form the body, whether there is a body cavity, whether the body is made of repeated segments, and whether a notochord is present.

## 1. Why classify, and the key words

| Term | Meaning (simple) | Coined / associated with |
|---|---|---|
| **Classification** | Placing organisms into groups on the basis of similarities and differences | Aristotle began it |
| **Taxonomy** | The science of naming, describing and classifying organisms | Word coined by **A. P. de Candolle (1813)** |
| **Systematics** | Classification **plus evolutionary relationships** among organisms | From Linnaeus's *Systema Naturae* |
| **Nomenclature** | Giving a correct scientific name | Linnaeus (binomial system) |
| **Identification** | Finding the correct place of an organism in an existing system | Uses keys, museums, monographs |
| **Taxon** (plural taxa) | Any group at any rank — e.g. "Mammalia", "Felidae", "*Panthera*" | — |

**Taxonomic aids** (places/tools that help identification): herbarium (plants), **museum** (preserved animals in formalin, stuffed skins, skeletons), **zoological parks**, botanical gardens, **keys** (paired, contrasting statements called *couplets* — each half is a *lead*), monographs, manuals, catalogues and flora/fauna books. The **Zoological Survey of India (ZSI)**, Kolkata (founded 1916), keeps India's national animal collections.

## 2. History of classification — who did what

| Scientist | Contribution | Year |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristotle** (Greek) | First scientific classification of animals: **Enaima** (with red blood) and **Anaima** (without red blood); called **father of zoology / father of biology** | 4th century BCE |
| **John Ray** (England) | Gave the first biological meaning of the word **species** (a group of individuals that breed true) | 1686 (*Historia Plantarum*) |
| **Carolus Linnaeus** (Sweden) | **Binomial nomenclature**, the hierarchy of categories, two-kingdom system (Plantae, Animalia); **father of taxonomy** | *Systema Naturae* 1735; **10th edition 1758** |
| **Ernst Haeckel** | Third kingdom **Protista** for single-celled forms | 1866 |
| **Herbert Copeland** | Four kingdoms (separate **Monera** for bacteria) | 1956 |
| **R. H. Whittaker** | **Five kingdoms**: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia | **1969** |
| **Carl Woese** | **Three domains**: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya (from rRNA sequences) | 1990 |
| **Ernst Mayr** | **Biological species concept**: a species is a group of actually or potentially interbreeding natural populations reproductively isolated from other such groups | 1942 |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q71 asked: "Who among the following is known as the father of biological classification? (A) Carolus Linnaeus (B) Aristotle (C) John Ray." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (A) Carolus Linnaeus.** Note: many Indian books call **Aristotle** the "father of zoology" and even the "father of classification" because he made the first classification. BPSC chose Linnaeus because the *system* of classification we still use (hierarchy + binomial names) is his. In the exam, "father of taxonomy / biological classification / binomial nomenclature" → **Linnaeus**; "father of zoology / biology" → **Aristotle**.

> **Remember:** For zoological names the starting date is **1 January 1758** — the 10th edition of *Systema Naturae*. For plant names it is **1753** (*Species Plantarum*). Any animal name published before 1758 is not valid.

## 3. Binomial nomenclature and the ICZN rules

Every animal gets **two Latin (or Latinised) words**: the first is the **genus** (generic name), the second is the **specific epithet**. Example: *Homo sapiens*, *Panthera tigris*, *Musca domestica*.

Rules of the **International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN)**:

1. Names are in **Latin** or Latinised form, whatever their origin.
2. **Genus begins with a capital letter**; the specific epithet with a **small** letter — *Rana tigrina*, never *Rana Tigrina*.
3. Printed in **italics**; when **hand-written**, each word is **underlined separately**.
4. The name of the author who first described it, and the year, may follow in normal type: *Homo sapiens* Linnaeus, 1758. If the species was later moved to another genus, the author's name is put in brackets: *Panthera tigris* (Linnaeus, 1758).
5. **Law of priority** — the oldest validly published name is the correct one; later names for the same animal are *synonyms*.
6. **Tautonyms are allowed in zoology** (same word for genus and species): *Rattus rattus* (house rat), *Naja naja* (Indian cobra), *Gorilla gorilla*, *Bison bison*, *Axis axis* (chital). Tautonyms are **not allowed in botany**.
7. A third word gives a **subspecies** (trinomial nomenclature): *Homo sapiens sapiens*, *Corvus splendens splendens*.
8. Every name is tied to a **type specimen**: **holotype** (the single specimen chosen by the author), **paratypes** (other specimens of the original series), **lectotype** (chosen later when no holotype was named), **neotype** (new type when all originals are lost).

> **Common mistake:** "*homo Sapiens*" or "*Homo Sapiens*" is wrong. Only the genus is capitalised. Family names in zoology end in **-idae** (Felidae, Hominidae) and subfamilies in **-inae**; in botany families end in **-aceae**. Orders and classes have no fixed ending in zoology.

## 4. The taxonomic hierarchy

Linnaeus arranged categories in a **ladder** (hierarchy). Going **up** the ladder, the number of shared characters **decreases** and the number of organisms **increases**. Going down, organisms become more similar.

**Seven obligate categories:** Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.
Memory sentence: **"King Philip Came Over For Good Soup."**

Intermediate categories are made by adding sub-, super- or infra-: subphylum (Vertebrata), superclass (Pisces, Tetrapoda), subclass (Eutheria), suborder, superfamily. In botany "Division" is used in place of phylum.

<div class="formula">

| Category | Human | Housefly | Tiger | Frog (Indian bullfrog) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kingdom | Animalia | Animalia | Animalia | Animalia |
| Phylum | Chordata | Arthropoda | Chordata | Chordata |
| Class | Mammalia | Insecta | Mammalia | Amphibia |
| Order | Primates | Diptera | Carnivora | Anura |
| Family | Hominidae | Muscidae | Felidae | Dicroglossidae (older books: Ranidae) |
| Genus | *Homo* | *Musca* | *Panthera* | *Hoplobatrachus* (older books: *Rana*) |
| Species | *sapiens* | *domestica* | *tigris* | *tigerinus* (older books: *Rana tigrina*) |

</div>

- **Species** is the basic unit of classification — the only category that is a real, natural, interbreeding unit.
- Lion (*Panthera leo*), tiger (*P. tigris*), leopard (*P. pardus*) and jaguar (*P. onca*) are different species of **one genus**, *Panthera*. Cat (*Felis catus*) is in the **same family** (Felidae) but a different genus.
- Cats (Felidae) and dogs (Canidae) are in the **same order** Carnivora but different families.

> **Exam angle:** "Which category contains the largest number of organisms with the fewest common characters?" — **Kingdom**. "Which category contains organisms with maximum similarity?" — **Species**.

**Worked example — a simple dichotomous key** (each step has two contrasting leads):

```
1a. Notochord absent at all stages ................ go to 2
1b. Notochord present at some stage ............... Chordata
2a. Body with jointed legs and chitin exoskeleton .. Arthropoda
2b. No jointed legs ............................... go to 3
3a. Body segmented, with setae ..................... Annelida
3b. Body not segmented, soft, with shell/mantle .... Mollusca
```

A key moves from **general to particular** characters; a *bracketed* key keeps the two leads together (as above), an *indented* key places them far apart under headings.

**Taxonomy types (B.Sc.):** alpha taxonomy (describing and naming species from morphology), beta (arranging into a natural system), gamma (evolutionary and population studies). Modern branches — **numerical taxonomy** (computer, many equally weighted characters), **cytotaxonomy** (chromosome number and shape), **chemotaxonomy** (chemical constituents), **molecular taxonomy/cladistics** (DNA sequences, shared derived characters).

## 5. Basis of classification — the seven key features

NCERT says that, in spite of differences in structure, animals share some fundamental features. These features are used to make the phyla.

### 5.1 Levels of organisation

| Level | Meaning | Phylum |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellular level** | Cells are loose aggregates; division of labour among cells, but no true tissues | **Porifera** (sponges) |
| **Tissue level** | Cells doing the same job form tissues | **Cnidaria (Coelenterata), Ctenophora** |
| **Organ level** | Tissues grouped into organs, each organ doing one job | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Organ-system level** | Organs work together as systems (digestive, circulatory …) | Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

Within organ-system level:
- **Digestive system** — **incomplete** (one opening that works as mouth and anus): Cnidaria, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes. **Complete** (separate mouth and anus): Aschelminthes onwards.
- **Circulatory system** — **open** (blood flows into open spaces, the haemocoel, and bathes organs): Arthropoda, most Mollusca, Hemichordata (largely). **Closed** (blood always inside vessels): Annelida, **cephalopod molluscs** (*Sepia*, *Octopus*), Chordata.

### 5.2 Symmetry

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Asymmetrical** | No plane divides the body into equal halves | Most **sponges**; adult gastropods (snails, due to torsion) |
| **Radial** | Any plane through the central axis gives two identical halves | **Cnidaria, Ctenophora (biradial), adult Echinodermata (pentaradial)** |
| **Bilateral** | Only **one** plane (the median sagittal plane) divides the body into left and right halves | Platyhelminthes to Chordata (larvae of echinoderms too) |

- **Biradial** symmetry (two planes) — **Ctenophora** and sea anemones.
- **Pentaradial** symmetry (body in five parts round a central axis) — **adult echinoderms**. Their **larvae are bilateral**, so echinoderms are bilateral animals that became radial secondarily.
- Radial symmetry suits **sessile or slow, drifting** animals that meet food from all sides; bilateral symmetry goes with active, forward movement and a head end (**cephalisation**).

### 5.3 Germ layers

During embryo development, cells form layers called **germ layers**:

- **Diploblastic** — two layers: outer **ectoderm** and inner **endoderm**, with a non-cellular jelly, the **mesoglea**, between them. **Cnidaria, Ctenophora.** (Sponges are usually described as having no true germ layers; NCERT simply calls them cellular level.)
- **Triploblastic** — three layers: ectoderm, **mesoderm** (middle) and endoderm. **Platyhelminthes to Chordata.**

| Germ layer | Main adult derivatives (vertebrates) |
|---|---|
| Ectoderm | Epidermis, hair, nails, nervous system, lens and retina, enamel, adrenal medulla |
| Mesoderm | Muscles, bones, cartilage, blood, heart, kidneys, gonads, dermis, adrenal cortex |
| Endoderm | Lining of gut, liver, pancreas, lungs (lining), thyroid, thymus, urinary bladder lining |

### 5.4 Coelom (body cavity)

The **coelom** is a body cavity **lined by mesoderm** on both sides — the outer *parietal* (somatic) layer against the body wall and the inner *visceral* (splanchnic) layer around the gut.

| Type | Feature | Phyla |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoelomate** | No cavity; space between gut and body wall filled with mesodermal cells (**parenchyma**) | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Pseudocoelomate** | Cavity present, but mesoderm only as **scattered pouches / only on the body-wall side**; the cavity is the remnant of the blastocoel | **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)** |
| **Coelomate (eucoelomate)** | True coelom fully lined by mesoderm | Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

**How the true coelom forms (B.Sc.):**
- **Schizocoel** — mesoderm band *splits* to form the cavity: **Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca**.
- **Enterocoel** — cavity arises from *pouches of the embryonic gut (archenteron)*: **Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata**.
- **Haemocoel** — in arthropods and molluscs the true coelom is reduced (round gonads and excretory organs) and the main cavity is a **blood-filled haemocoel** — not a true coelom.

### 5.5 Segmentation (metamerism)

In some animals the body is divided **externally and internally** into a series of similar segments, with **serial repetition of at least some organs** (nephridia, ganglia, blood vessels, muscles). This is **metamerism** (NCERT: *metameric segmentation*). Each segment is a **metamere** (somite).

- **True metamerism** — **Annelida** (earthworm: ~100–120 segments), **Arthropoda**, and **Chordata** (internally: myotomes, vertebrae, spinal nerves).
- **Pseudometamerism** — **tapeworm** (*Taenia*): the proglottids are **budded off from the neck**, the youngest is nearest the head and each proglottid is an almost independent reproductive unit. True metameres form from the posterior end with the oldest near the head — so tapeworm segmentation is **not true metamerism**.
- **Homonomous** metamerism (segments alike — earthworm) vs **heteronomous** (segments differ and group into regions, *tagmata* — insects: head, thorax, abdomen).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) class 9–10 Science paper asked: "In some animal groups, the body is found divided into compartments with at least some organs serially repeated. This feature is named as — (A) segmentation (B) metamerism (C) metagenesis." The textbook term is **metamerism** (NCERT: metameric segmentation). **Metagenesis** = alternation of polyp and medusa generations (*Obelia*) — a trap. (No key for this paper in our files.)

### 5.6 Notochord

The **notochord** is a **mesodermal**, rod-like supporting structure on the **dorsal side**, formed during embryonic development.
- Animals with a notochord at least at some stage → **chordates**; all others → **non-chordates** (Porifera to Echinodermata, plus Hemichordata).
- In **vertebrates** the notochord is replaced by the **vertebral column** (backbone) in the adult; remnants remain as the **nucleus pulposus** of intervertebral discs in mammals.
- **"All vertebrates are chordates, but not all chordates are vertebrates"** — urochordates and cephalochordates have a notochord but no vertebral column.

### 5.7 Pattern of development (B.Sc.)

| Feature | **Protostomes** | **Deuterostomes** |
|---|---|---|
| Fate of blastopore | Becomes **mouth** | Becomes **anus** (mouth forms later) |
| Cleavage | **Spiral, determinate** (fate of each cell fixed early) | **Radial, indeterminate** (early cells can form whole embryo — identical twins possible) |
| Coelom | **Schizocoel** | **Enterocoel** |
| Typical larva | **Trochophore** (annelids, molluscs) | Dipleurula-type (bipinnaria, tornaria) |
| Nerve cord | Ventral, solid | Dorsal, hollow (chordates) |
| Phyla | Platyhelminthes, Nematoda (these two loosely), **Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca** | **Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata** |

> **Memory trick:** "Deutero" = second. In **deuterostomes** the mouth forms **second**; the first opening (blastopore) becomes the anus. Echinoderms are therefore our closest invertebrate relatives — that is why the starfish larva looks like the *Balanoglossus* larva.

## 7. Master table of the phyla (NCERT order)

| Phylum | Common name / meaning | Level | Symmetry | Germ layers | Coelom | Special feature | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Porifera** | Pore bearers, sponges | Cellular | Mostly asymmetrical | — | — | **Canal system, choanocytes**, spicules | *Sycon*, *Spongilla*, *Euspongia* |
| **Cnidaria (Coelenterata)** | Stinging cells | Tissue | Radial | Diploblastic | — | **Cnidoblasts**, polyp and medusa | *Hydra*, *Obelia*, *Aurelia*, *Physalia* |
| **Ctenophora** | Comb jellies, sea walnuts | Tissue | Radial (biradial) | Diploblastic | — | **8 comb plates**, bioluminescence | *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana* |
| **Platyhelminthes** | Flatworms | Organ | Bilateral | Triploblastic | Acoelomate | **Flame cells**, dorso-ventrally flat | *Taenia*, *Fasciola*, *Planaria* |
| **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)** | Roundworms | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triploblastic | **Pseudocoelomate** | Round in section, sexes separate | *Ascaris*, *Wuchereria*, *Ancylostoma* |
| **Annelida** | Ringed worms | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triploblastic | Coelomate (schizo) | **Metamerism**, nephridia, closed circulation | *Nereis*, *Pheretima*, *Hirudinaria* |
| **Arthropoda** | Jointed legs | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triploblastic | Coelomate (haemocoel) | **Chitin exoskeleton, jointed appendages**; largest phylum | Cockroach, prawn, spider, *Limulus* |
| **Mollusca** | Soft-bodied | Organ-system | Bilateral (gastropods asymmetrical) | Triploblastic | Coelomate (reduced) | **Mantle, shell, radula, muscular foot** | *Pila*, *Unio*, *Sepia*, *Octopus* |
| **Echinodermata** | Spiny skin | Organ-system | **Pentaradial adult**, bilateral larva | Triploblastic | Coelomate (entero) | **Water vascular system**, all marine | *Asterias*, *Echinus*, *Antedon* |
| **Hemichordata** | "Half chordates" | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triploblastic | Coelomate (entero) | Proboscis–collar–trunk; **stomochord** | *Balanoglossus*, *Saccoglossus* |
| **Chordata** | Notochord bearers | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triploblastic | Coelomate (entero) | **Notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits** | *Herdmania*, *Amphioxus*, vertebrates |

**Protozoa** (*Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Plasmodium*) are placed by NCERT in **Kingdom Protista**, not Animalia, but every zoology syllabus still teaches them as "animal-like protists" — see [Chapter 2](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/animal-diversity/protozoa-porifera-and-cnidaria).

### Habitat facts that are asked

| Statement | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Exclusively marine** phyla | **Echinodermata, Ctenophora, Hemichordata** (and among chordates the Urochordata and Cephalochordata) |

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q72 asked: "The animals from which of the following phyla are exclusively marine? (A) Porifera (B) Mollusca (C) Echinodermata." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Echinodermata.** Porifera has the freshwater family Spongillidae (*Spongilla*), and Mollusca has freshwater and land snails, so only Echinodermata is 100% marine. Note that option (D) "More than one" is wrong because only one option is exclusively marine.

## 8. Number of animal species

- About **1.5 million** animal species have been described (NCERT's biodiversity chapter: animals are more than **70%** of all recorded species; **insects** are the richest group — roughly **7 out of every 10 animals**).
- India has about **6.5–7%** of the world's recorded animal species (ZSI: over 1 lakh species); India is one of the **17 megadiverse countries**. These numbers change every year — use them only as approximate values.

## 9. Ways to group phyla (B.Sc. view)

- **Parazoa** (no true tissues — Porifera) vs **Eumetazoa** (all others).
- **Radiata** (Cnidaria, Ctenophora) vs **Bilateria** (rest).
- **Invertebrates** — no vertebral column (all non-chordates + protochordates). **Vertebrates** — vertebral column.
- **Protochordates** = Hemichordata (older books), Urochordata and Cephalochordata — "first chordates", no vertebral column.

> **Common mistake:** Hemichordata was earlier a **subphylum of Chordata**. NCERT now treats it as a **separate non-chordate phylum** because its "notochord" (the **stomochord**, a buccal diverticulum) is not homologous to a true notochord. If a question says "Hemichordata is a subphylum of Chordata", the modern answer is **false**.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Sponges** are asymmetrical "mostly" — some (*Sycon*) are radially symmetrical.
- **Echinoderm adults are radial but larvae are bilateral** — echinoderms are counted with the Bilateria.
- **Gastropods** (snails) are bilaterally symmetrical as larvae but become **asymmetrical** after **torsion**.
- **Tapeworm** segmentation is **pseudometamerism**, not true metamerism.
- **Arthropods and molluscs** have a reduced true coelom; their large cavity is a **haemocoel**.
- **Cephalopods** (*Sepia*, *Octopus*) have a **closed** circulatory system although other molluscs have an open one.
- **Mesoglea** in cnidarians is a jelly layer, **not** a third germ layer.
- **Tautonymy** (*Naja naja*) is allowed only in zoological names.
- Zoological nomenclature starts in **1758**, botanical in **1753**.
- "Father of biological classification" in the TRE key = **Linnaeus**; "father of zoology" = **Aristotle**.
- **Protozoa are not in Kingdom Animalia** in NCERT (Whittaker) — they are in Protista.

## Quick revision

1. Taxonomy word coined by — **A. P. de Candolle (1813)**.
2. Father of taxonomy / binomial nomenclature — **Carolus Linnaeus**; book **Systema Naturae** (10th edition **1758**).
3. Father of zoology — **Aristotle** (Enaima and Anaima).
4. Biological species concept — **Ernst Mayr (1942)**.
5. Five-kingdom classification — **R. H. Whittaker (1969)**; three domains — **Carl Woese (1990)**.
6. Basic unit of classification — **species**.
7. Seven obligate categories — kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species.
8. Animal family names end in **-idae**.
9. Tautonym example — ***Rattus rattus***, ***Naja naja***.
10. Cellular level of organisation — **Porifera**; tissue level — **Cnidaria, Ctenophora**; organ level — **Platyhelminthes**.
11. Diploblastic phyla — **Cnidaria and Ctenophora**.
12. Acoelomate — **Platyhelminthes**; pseudocoelomate — **Aschelminthes**.
13. Schizocoelous — **Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca**; enterocoelous — **Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata**.
14. True metamerism first appears in — **Annelida**.
15. Notochord is **mesodermal** in origin and **dorsal** in position.
16. Blastopore becomes anus in — **deuterostomes**.
17. Exclusively marine phylum (TRE key) — **Echinodermata**.
18. Largest phylum — **Arthropoda**; second — **Mollusca**.
19. Open circulation — **arthropods and most molluscs**; closed — **annelids, cephalopods, chordates**.
20. Hemichordata is now a **separate non-chordate phylum**.
21. Spiral determinate cleavage — **protostomes**; radial indeterminate — **deuterostomes**.
22. Pentaradial symmetry — **adult echinoderms**.

## Practice with answers

1. Who gave the two-word system of naming? — **Carolus Linnaeus** (binomial nomenclature).
2. Which edition of *Systema Naturae* is the starting point of zoological nomenclature? — **The 10th edition (1758)**.
3. In *Panthera leo*, which word is the specific epithet? — ***leo*** (the second word).
4. Which category is always written with a capital first letter in a binomial? — **Genus**.
5. Name one tautonym. — ***Gorilla gorilla*** (genus and species words identical).
6. Which phylum shows the cellular level of organisation? — **Porifera**.
7. Mesoglea is found between which layers? — **Ectoderm and endoderm** of diploblastic animals.
8. The body cavity of roundworms is called? — **Pseudocoelom** (not fully lined by mesoderm).
9. Coelom formed by splitting of mesoderm is called? — **Schizocoel**.
10. Which group shows pentaradial symmetry as adults? — **Echinodermata**.
11. Is tapeworm truly metameric? — **No** — pseudometamerism (proglottids budded from the neck).
12. In which group does the blastopore become the mouth? — **Protostomes** (annelids, arthropods, molluscs).
13. Which non-chordate phylum has a closed circulatory system? — **Annelida** (also cephalopod molluscs).
14. Who classified animals into Enaima and Anaima? — **Aristotle**.
15. Which category contains the most organisms? — **Kingdom**.
16. Why is Porifera not called "exclusively marine"? — **Freshwater sponge *Spongilla*** exists.
17. What is the main body cavity of arthropods? — **Haemocoel** (blood-filled).
18. The stomochord of *Balanoglossus* is now considered? — **A buccal diverticulum, not a true notochord**.
