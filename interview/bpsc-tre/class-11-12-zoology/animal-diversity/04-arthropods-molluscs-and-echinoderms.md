These three phyla hold most of the animal species on Earth. NCERT class 11 gives their salient features with examples; the B.Sc. type studies (cockroach, prawn, *Pila*, *Unio*, *Sepia*, starfish) give the counts and names that TRE asks: "number of chambers in the cockroach heart", "larva of *Unio*", "exclusively marine phylum", "madreporite is part of".

> **In simple words:** **Arthropods** have jointed legs and a hard outer skeleton of chitin — insects, prawns, spiders, centipedes. **Molluscs** are soft-bodied animals usually inside a shell, with a fleshy foot and a "mantle" — snails, mussels, octopus. **Echinoderms** are spiny-skinned sea animals (starfish, sea urchin) that move with hundreds of tiny water-filled tube feet. Learn the classes, one or two examples each, the type studies, and the larvae.

## 1. Phylum Arthropoda — the largest phylum

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- **Largest phylum** — over **two-thirds of all named species on Earth** are arthropods (insects alone over a million).
- **Organ-system level**; bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, **segmented, coelomate** (the cavity is mainly a blood-filled **haemocoel**).
- Body covered by a **chitinous exoskeleton**; body divided into **head, thorax and abdomen** (or cephalothorax and abdomen).
- **Jointed appendages** (*arthros* = joint, *podos* = foot).
- **Respiratory organs**: **gills, book gills, book lungs or tracheal system**.
- **Circulatory system open**.
- Sensory organs: **antennae, eyes (compound and simple), statocysts** (balance organs).
- **Excretion through Malpighian tubules** (insects, arachnids); green (antennal) glands in crustaceans; coxal glands in arachnids.
- Mostly **dioecious**, fertilisation usually **internal**, mostly **oviparous**; development **direct or indirect**.
- Examples: economically important — ***Apis*** (honeybee), ***Bombyx*** (silkworm), ***Laccifer*** (lac insect); vectors — ***Anopheles***, ***Culex***, ***Aedes***; gregarious pest — ***Locusta*** (locust); living fossil — ***Limulus*** (king crab).

**B.Sc. points:** growth only by **moulting (ecdysis)**, controlled by the hormone **ecdysone** (from the prothoracic gland); **juvenile hormone** (from the corpora allata) keeps the larva young. Muscles are striated even in the gut. No cilia anywhere.

### Classes of Arthropoda

| Class | Body regions | Legs | Antennae | Respiration | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Crustacea** | Cephalothorax + abdomen | Many (biramous) | **Two pairs** | **Gills** | Prawn (*Palaemon*, *Macrobrachium*), crab, lobster, *Daphnia* (water flea), *Cyclops*, barnacle (*Balanus*) |
| **Insecta (Hexapoda)** | **Head, thorax, abdomen** | **Three pairs** | **One pair** | **Tracheae** | Cockroach, housefly, mosquito, honeybee, butterfly, beetle |
| **Arachnida** | Cephalothorax (prosoma) + abdomen | **Four pairs** | **None** (chelicerae and pedipalps instead) | **Book lungs** and/or tracheae | Spider, scorpion (*Heterometrus*/*Palamnaeus*), tick, mite (*Sarcoptes* — scabies) |
| **Chilopoda** — centipedes | Head + many trunk segments | **One pair per segment** | One pair | Tracheae | *Scolopendra* (poison claws, fast predator) |
| **Diplopoda** — millipedes | Head + many segments | **Two pairs per segment** | One pair | Tracheae | *Julus* (slow herbivore) |
| **Merostomata** | Cephalothorax + abdomen | Five pairs | None | **Book gills** | ***Limulus*** (king crab — **living fossil**; blue haemocyanin blood) |

> **Memory trick:** **"Centi = one, Milli = two"** — centipedes one pair of legs per segment, millipedes two pairs (each millipede "segment" is really two fused segments, a **diplosegment**).

- ***Peripatus*** (Onychophora) has annelid features (nephridia, soft worm-like segmented body) and arthropod features (tracheae, claws, haemocoel) — the **connecting link between Annelida and Arthropoda**.
- Spider silk comes from abdominal **spinnerets**; scorpions are **viviparous** and sting with the last abdominal segment.
- Ticks carry disease: ***Haemaphysalis*** — Kyasanur Forest Disease (KFD, Karnataka); *Ixodes* — Lyme disease.

### 1.1 Cockroach — *Periplaneta americana*

**External features:**
- Reddish-brown, about **34–53 mm** long; nocturnal and omnivorous; body in **head, thorax and abdomen**.
- **Head**: triangular, formed by **fusion of six segments**, hypognathous (mouth downwards); a pair of **compound eyes**, a pair of long thread-like **antennae**.
- **Mouth parts — biting and chewing type**: **labrum** (upper lip), a pair of **mandibles**, a pair of **first maxillae**, **labium** (lower lip; fused second maxillae) and a median **hypopharynx** (tongue).
- **Thorax** of three segments — **prothorax, mesothorax, metathorax** — each with **one pair of walking legs** (coxa, trochanter, femur, tibia, tarsus).
- **Two pairs of wings**: forewings (**mesothoracic**) are thick, dark, leathery — **tegmina** — and cover the hind wings; hind wings (**metathoracic**) are membranous and used in flight.
- **Abdomen** of **10 segments**. **Anal cerci** (jointed, on the 10th segment) in **both sexes**; **anal styles** (unjointed, on the 9th sternum) **only in males**. In females the 7th sternum is boat-shaped and with the 8th and 9th sterna forms a **brood (genital) pouch**.

**Internal organisation:**

| System | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Digestive** | Foregut: pharynx → oesophagus → **crop** (storage) → **gizzard (proventriculus)** with **six chitinous teeth** for grinding. At the junction of fore- and midgut a ring of **6–8 hepatic (gastric) caeca** secrete digestive juice. Midgut (mesenteron) → hindgut (ileum, colon, rectum) |
| **Excretory** | **Malpighian tubules (100–150)** at the junction of midgut and hindgut; also fat body, nephrocytes, uricose glands. **Uricotelic** (uric acid) |
| **Circulatory** | **Open**; colourless **haemolymph**; heart an elongated tube along the mid-dorsal line with **13 funnel-shaped chambers**, each with ostia on both sides |
| **Respiratory** | **Tracheae** opening by **10 pairs of spiracles** (2 thoracic + 8 abdominal) on the sides; spiracles regulated by sphincters. Gas reaches cells directly; haemolymph carries no O₂ |
| **Nervous** | Ventral chain of fused ganglia: **3 thoracic and 6 abdominal**; brain = **supra-oesophageal ganglion** in the head. Because ganglia are spread in the body, a beheaded cockroach can live for up to a week |
| **Sense** | Each compound eye has about **2000 hexagonal ommatidia** → **mosaic vision** (more sensitivity, less resolution — useful at night) |
| **Male reproductive** | A pair of **testes** in abdominal segments 4–6, vasa deferentia, ejaculatory duct, **mushroom-shaped gland** (accessory gland, segments 6–7), phallic gland; sperm stored in **spermatophores** |
| **Female reproductive** | Two large **ovaries** in abdominal segments 2–6, each of **eight ovarioles**; **spermathecae** in segment 6 |

**Development:** fertilised eggs are packed in capsules called **oothecae** (dark reddish-brown, about 8 mm). A female produces on average **9–10 oothecae, each with 14–16 eggs**. Development is **paurometabolous** (incomplete metamorphosis) — the young **nymph** looks like the adult; it **moults about 13 times**, and wing pads appear only in the last nymphal stage. Cockroaches carry germs of cholera, typhoid and dysentery on their bodies (mechanical carriers).

### 1.2 Prawn — *Palaemon* / *Macrobrachium* (freshwater)

- Body: **cephalothorax** (5 head + 8 thoracic segments = 13) covered by a **carapace** with a toothed **rostrum**, and **abdomen** of 6 segments + **telson**.
- **19 pairs of appendages**, typically **biramous** (base = protopodite; two branches = exopodite and endopodite): **head 5** (antennules, antennae, mandibles, maxillulae, maxillae), **thorax 8** (3 pairs **maxillipeds** + 5 pairs **walking legs**), **abdomen 6** (5 pairs **pleopods / swimmerets** + 1 pair **uropods**, which with the telson form the tail fan).
- Respiration by **gills** in branchial chambers under the carapace; the **scaphognathite** ("baler", part of the maxilla) beats water over the gills.
- Excretion by **antennal (green) glands**; blood with **haemocyanin** (copper, bluish); open circulation.
- **Statocysts** in the base of the antennules (balance); **stalked compound eyes**.
- Freshwater prawns hatch as a **zoea**; marine *Penaeus* passes through **nauplius → protozoea → mysis** larvae.

### 1.3 Insects — orders, metamorphosis and vectors

Insect body: head (one pair of antennae, compound eyes), thorax (three pairs of legs, usually two pairs of wings), abdomen (no legs in adults). **Insecta is the largest class**; **Coleoptera (beetles) the largest order**.

| Order | Wings / key feature | Metamorphosis | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thysanura | **Wingless** | **Ametabolous** (none) | *Lepisma* (silverfish) |
| Orthoptera | Straight forewings; jumping hind legs | Incomplete | Grasshopper, **locust** (*Schistocerca gregaria*, *Locusta*), cricket |
| Dictyoptera / Blattodea | Tegmina | Incomplete | **Cockroach**, praying mantis; **termites** (old order Isoptera) |
| Odonata | Two pairs of net-veined wings | Incomplete (aquatic naiad) | Dragonfly, damselfly |
| Hemiptera | Piercing–sucking mouth | Incomplete | **Bed bug *Cimex***, **lac insect *Laccifer (Kerria) lacca***, aphids, cicada |
| Lepidoptera | **Scaly wings** | **Complete** (caterpillar, pupa) | Butterflies, moths, **silkworm *Bombyx mori*** |
| Diptera | **One pair of wings**; hind wings → **halteres** (balancers) | Complete (maggot, wriggler) | **Housefly *Musca***, **mosquitoes**, sandfly, tsetse fly |
| Hymenoptera | Membranous wings, "waist" | Complete | **Honeybee *Apis***, wasps, **ants** |
| Coleoptera | Forewings hard **elytra** | Complete (grub) | Beetles, weevils, ladybird |
| Siphonaptera | **Wingless**, jumping | Complete | **Rat flea *Xenopsylla cheopis*** (plague) |
| Phthiraptera | Wingless | Incomplete | **Louse *Pediculus humanus*** (epidemic typhus, relapsing fever) |

**Types of development:**
- **Ametabolous** — young look like adults, no wings ever (silverfish).
- **Hemimetabolous / paurometabolous (incomplete)** — egg → **nymph** → adult (cockroach, grasshopper, bugs). Aquatic nymphs of dragonflies are **naiads**.
- **Holometabolous (complete)** — egg → **larva** → **pupa** → adult (butterfly, housefly, mosquito, honeybee, beetle, silkworm). Larvae: caterpillar (butterfly), **maggot** (housefly), **wriggler** (mosquito), **grub** (beetle); pupa of mosquito = **tumbler**; silkworm pupa is inside a **cocoon**.

## 2. Phylum Mollusca — soft-bodied animals

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- **Second-largest** animal phylum; terrestrial or aquatic (marine or freshwater).
- **Organ-system level**; bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, **coelomate** (coelom reduced to pericardium and gonad cavities).
- Body usually covered by a **calcareous shell**; **unsegmented**, with a distinct **head, muscular foot and visceral hump**.
- A soft, spongy layer of skin, the **mantle**, covers the visceral hump; the space between hump and mantle is the **mantle cavity**, with feather-like **gills (ctenidia)** for respiration and excretion.
- Head bears sensory **tentacles**; the mouth contains a file-like rasping organ, the **radula** (absent in bivalves).
- Usually **dioecious and oviparous** with **indirect development**.
- Examples: ***Pila*** (apple snail), ***Pinctada*** (pearl oyster), ***Sepia*** (cuttlefish), ***Loligo*** (squid), ***Octopus*** (devil fish), ***Aplysia*** (sea hare), ***Dentalium*** (tusk shell), ***Chaetopleura*** (chiton).

**B.Sc. points:** blood with **haemocyanin** (copper, blue); circulation **open** except in cephalopods (**closed**); excretion by paired **kidneys (organs of Bojanus** in bivalves); a chemoreceptor, the **osphradium**, tests incoming water; typical larvae **trochophore → veliger**.

### Classes of Mollusca

| Class | Key feature | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Monoplacophora** | Single cap-like shell; some organs serially repeated | ***Neopilina*** (living fossil, found 1952; annelid–mollusc link) |
| **Polyplacophora (Amphineura)** | Shell of **eight plates** | *Chiton*, *Chaetopleura* |
| **Gastropoda** | Spiral shell (or none), **torsion**, distinct head | ***Pila***, *Helix* (land snail), *Limax* (slug, no shell), ***Aplysia*** (sea hare), *Doris* (sea lemon), *Cypraea* (cowry) |
| **Scaphopoda** | Tusk-shaped shell open at both ends | ***Dentalium*** |
| **Bivalvia (Pelecypoda / Lamellibranchia)** | **Two valves**, no head, **no radula**, filter feeders | ***Unio***, *Lamellidens* (Indian mussel), ***Pinctada*** (pearl oyster), *Ostrea* (edible oyster), *Mytilus*, *Teredo* (shipworm) |
| **Cephalopoda** | Foot changed into **arms/tentacles** round the head, **closed circulation**, ink gland | ***Sepia***, ***Loligo***, ***Octopus***, ***Nautilus*** (external chambered shell — living fossil) |

### 2.1 *Pila globosa* — Indian apple snail

- Freshwater ponds and paddy fields; **amphibious** — breathes in water by a **ctenidium (gill)** and in air by a **pulmonary sac (lung)**.
- Shell **spirally coiled and dextral** (opening on the right); the foot carries a horny lid, the **operculum**, that closes the shell mouth.
- **Torsion**: during larval development the visceral mass twists **180°** anticlockwise, so the mantle cavity and anus come to lie **in front, over the head** and the nerve cords cross into a **figure of 8** (streptoneury). Torsion makes the adult **asymmetrical**.
- Feeds on plants with the **radula**; **osphradium** tests water.
- **Dioecious**; eggs laid on land near water; **direct development**. **Aestivates** (summer sleep) buried in mud.

### 2.2 *Unio* (and *Lamellidens*) — freshwater mussel

- Body enclosed in **two valves** joined dorsally by a hinge **ligament**; closed by **anterior and posterior adductor muscles**.
- **No head, no radula, no tentacles**; hatchet-shaped foot for burrowing (*Pelecypoda* = hatchet foot).
- Large plate-like (**lamellibranch**) gills trap food particles with cilia — **filter (ciliary) feeding**.
- **Dioecious**; eggs develop in the gills of the female (brood pouch) into the **glochidium** larva — a tiny bivalved larva with hooks that attaches to the **gills or fins of fish** as a temporary **parasite** (forms a cyst), then drops off as a young mussel.
- **Pearl formation**: when a foreign particle lodges between mantle and shell, the mantle covers it in layers of **nacre (mother-of-pearl)** — mainly **calcium carbonate (aragonite)** with the protein **conchiolin**. Marine **pearl oyster *Pinctada*** gives the best pearls; cultured pearls by **Kokichi Mikimoto** (Japan).

### 2.3 Cephalopods — *Sepia*, *Loligo*, *Octopus*

| Feature | *Sepia* (cuttlefish) | *Loligo* (squid) | *Octopus* (devil fish) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arms | **10** (8 arms + 2 long tentacles) | **10** | **8** |
| Shell | **Internal calcareous "cuttlebone"** | Internal horny **pen (gladius)** | **None** |
| Fins | Along whole body edge | Triangular at the end | None |

Common features: marine, fast **jet propulsion** by squirting water from the **siphon (funnel)**; **ink gland** (ink = melanin; "sepia" colour) for escape; **chromatophores** for rapid colour change; **camera-type eye** (analogous to the vertebrate eye); **closed circulation** with **three hearts** (one systemic + two branchial); large brain — *Octopus* is the **most intelligent invertebrate**; male has one modified arm (**hectocotylus**) to transfer sperm; **direct development** (no larva).

- **Largest invertebrate** — giant squid ***Architeuthis*** (colossal squid *Mesonychoteuthis* is the heaviest).
- The **giant axon of the squid** was used by **Hodgkin and Huxley** to work out the nerve impulse (Nobel 1963).
- Cuttlebone is given to cage birds as a calcium supplement.

> **Common mistake:** "Cuttlefish", "jellyfish", "starfish", "silverfish" and "crayfish" are **not fish**. Cuttlefish is a mollusc, jellyfish a cnidarian, starfish an echinoderm, silverfish an insect, crayfish a crustacean.

## 3. Phylum Echinodermata — spiny-skinned animals

**Salient features (NCERT):**
- Endoskeleton of **calcareous ossicles** (hence "spiny-bodied").
- **All are marine** (exclusively marine phylum).
- **Organ-system level**; **adults radially (pentaradially) symmetrical but larvae bilaterally symmetrical**; triploblastic, **coelomate** (enterocoel).
- Digestive system complete, **mouth on the lower (ventral/oral) side**, **anus on the upper (dorsal/aboral) side**.
- Most distinctive feature: the **water vascular system** — helps in **locomotion, capture and transport of food, and respiration**.
- **Excretory system absent**.
- **Sexes separate**; sexual reproduction; fertilisation usually **external**; development **indirect** with free-swimming larva.
- Examples: ***Asterias*** (starfish), ***Echinus*** (sea urchin), ***Antedon*** (sea lily / feather star), ***Cucumaria*** (sea cucumber), ***Ophiura*** (brittle star).

> **PYQ:** TRE 1.0 (2023) Zoology Q72 asked: "The animals from which of the following phyla are exclusively marine? (A) Porifera (B) Mollusca (C) Echinodermata." **BPSC key (2nd provisional): (C) Echinodermata.** No echinoderm lives in fresh water or on land. Porifera (*Spongilla*) and Mollusca (*Pila*, *Unio*, land snails) have non-marine members.

### 3.1 Water vascular (ambulacral) system of the starfish

Sea water enters and passes as follows:

**Madreporite** (sieve plate on the **aboral** surface, between two arms) → **stone canal** (with calcareous rings) → **ring canal** round the mouth (bears **Tiedemann's bodies** — make coelomocytes; **Polian vesicles** in many echinoderms but absent in *Asterias*) → **five radial canals**, one in each arm along the **ambulacral groove** → **lateral canals** (with valves) → **tube feet (podia)**, each with a bulb-like **ampulla** above and a **sucker** below.

- **Working:** the ampulla contracts → water is pushed into the tube foot → it **extends** and its sucker grips the surface; muscles in the tube foot then contract, water returns to the ampulla and the animal is pulled forward. It is a **hydraulic** system.
- Feeding of starfish: it pulls open a bivalve with its tube feet, **everts its cardiac stomach** into the shell and digests the prey **outside its body**.
- Other starfish structures: **pedicellariae** (tiny pincers that keep the surface clean), **dermal branchiae (papulae)** for respiration and excretion, **eyespots** at arm tips.
- Starfish has great **regeneration** — a single arm with part of the central disc can grow a whole animal (*Linckia*). Starfish are pests of oyster beds.

### 3.2 Classes and larvae

| Class | Common name | Examples | Larva | Special point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Asteroidea** | Starfish (sea star) | ***Asterias***, *Pentaceros* | **Bipinnaria → brachiolaria** | Arms not sharply set off from disc; open ambulacral grooves |
| **Ophiuroidea** | Brittle star | ***Ophiura***, *Ophiothrix* | **Ophiopluteus** | Thin, snake-like arms sharply set off; no anus |
| **Echinoidea** | Sea urchin, sand dollar, heart urchin | ***Echinus***, *Clypeaster*, *Echinocardium* | **Echinopluteus** | No arms; movable spines; chewing apparatus "**Aristotle's lantern**" |
| **Holothuroidea** | Sea cucumber | ***Cucumaria***, *Holothuria* | **Auricularia** | Soft cucumber body, skeleton reduced; **respiratory tree**; throws out viscera (**evisceration**) and regenerates; dried form "bêche-de-mer" eaten in East Asia |
| **Crinoidea** | Sea lily, feather star | ***Antedon*** | **Doliolaria** | Oldest class; mouth and anus both on the upper (oral) surface |

> **Memory trick:** Larvae of echinoderms in class order "**Bi**g **O**ld **E**chinoderms **A**re **D**ull" — **Bi**pinnaria (Asteroidea), **O**phiopluteus (Ophiuroidea), **E**chinopluteus (Echinoidea), **A**uricularia (Holothuroidea), **D**oliolaria (Crinoidea).

- Echinoderms are **deuterostomes** like chordates; their bilateral larvae resemble the **tornaria** larva of *Balanoglossus* — evidence that echinoderms and chordates share an ancestor.

### Respiratory and excretory organs compared

| Group | Respiration | Excretion |
|---|---|---|
| Insects | Tracheae | Malpighian tubules |
| Crustaceans | Gills | Green (antennal) glands |
| Arachnids | Book lungs, tracheae | Malpighian tubules, coxal glands |
| *Limulus* | Book gills | Coxal glands |
| Molluscs | Ctenidia (pulmonary sac in land snails, *Pila*) | Kidneys (organ of Bojanus in bivalves) |
| Echinoderms | Tube feet, dermal branchiae (papulae), respiratory tree (sea cucumber) | **No excretory organ** — wastes diffuse out |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Cephalopods** have a **closed** circulatory system; all other molluscs have an open one.
- **Bivalves have no radula and no head**.
- ***Limulus* (king crab) is not a crab** — it is closer to spiders (Merostomata) and is a living fossil.
- **Spiders and scorpions have no antennae** and four pairs of legs.
- **Insects respire by tracheae**; their blood (haemolymph) does not carry O₂.
- **Anal styles only in male cockroaches**; anal cerci in both.
- Cockroach heart has **13 chambers**; 10 pairs of spiracles.
- **Halteres** (balancers) are modified **hind wings** of Diptera.
- Echinoderm **adults radial, larvae bilateral**; **no excretory organs**.
- *Asterias* has **no Polian vesicles** (other echinoderms do).
- **Torsion** (180° twist) makes gastropods asymmetrical — don't confuse with spiral coiling of the shell.
- *Nautilus* has an **external** shell; *Sepia* internal; *Octopus* none.

## Quick revision

1. Largest phylum — **Arthropoda**; largest class — **Insecta**; largest order — **Coleoptera**.
2. Living fossil arthropod — ***Limulus*** (king crab).
3. Annelid–arthropod connecting link — ***Peripatus***.
4. Excretory organs of insects — **Malpighian tubules**; of prawn — **green (antennal) glands**.
5. Respiratory organs of spiders — **book lungs**.
6. Moulting hormone — **ecdysone**; juvenile hormone from **corpora allata**.
7. Cockroach heart — **13 chambers**; spiracles — **10 pairs**.
8. Cockroach forewings — **tegmina**.
9. Cockroach eye — about **2000 ommatidia**, **mosaic vision**.
10. Cockroach development — **paurometabolous**, nymph moults about **13 times**.
11. Prawn appendages — **19 pairs**.
12. Crustacean larva — **nauplius**; crab larva — **zoea**.
13. Plague vector — **rat flea *Xenopsylla***.
14. Dengue vector — ***Aedes aegypti***.
15. Second-largest phylum — **Mollusca**.
16. Rasping tongue — **radula** (absent in bivalves).
17. Larva of *Unio* — **glochidium** (parasite on fish).
18. Larvae of molluscs — **trochophore and veliger**.
19. Most intelligent invertebrate — ***Octopus***.
20. Living fossil mollusc — ***Neopilina*** (also *Nautilus*).
21. Exclusively marine phylum (TRE key) — **Echinodermata**.
22. Entry point of water vascular system — **madreporite**.
23. Starfish larva — **bipinnaria**; sea urchin — **echinopluteus**.
24. Aristotle's lantern — **sea urchin** (Echinoidea).

## Practice with answers

1. What is the exoskeleton of arthropods made of? — **Chitin** (a nitrogen-containing polysaccharide).
2. How many pairs of legs do millipedes have per segment? — **Two** (diplosegments).
3. Which arthropods breathe by book gills? — **King crab (*Limulus*)**.
4. Which mouth part of the cockroach acts as the lower lip? — **Labium**.
5. Where are hepatic caeca found in the cockroach? — **At the junction of foregut and midgut**.
6. How many eggs does a cockroach ootheca contain? — **About 14–16**.
7. What drives water over the prawn's gills? — **Scaphognathite (baler) of the maxilla**.
8. Which insect order has one pair of wings and halteres? — **Diptera**.
9. Name the larva of the housefly. — **Maggot**.
10. Which insect transmits kala-azar? — **Sandfly (*Phlebotomus*)**.
11. What is torsion? — **180° twisting of the visceral mass in gastropod larvae, bringing the anus over the head**.
12. Why is *Pila* called amphibious? — **It has both a gill (ctenidium) and a lung (pulmonary sac)**.
13. What is a pearl chemically? — **Calcium carbonate (aragonite) with conchiolin** (nacre).
14. How many arms does *Sepia* have? — **Ten** (8 arms + 2 tentacles).
15. How many hearts does a cephalopod have? — **Three** (one systemic, two branchial).
16. What does the osphradium do? — **Tests the incoming water (chemoreceptor)**.
17. Where is the madreporite of the starfish? — **On the aboral surface, between two arms**.
18. How does a starfish eat a mussel? — **Everts its cardiac stomach into the shell and digests outside**.
19. Larva of sea cucumber? — **Auricularia**.
20. Which echinoderm class has both mouth and anus on the upper surface? — **Crinoidea** (*Antedon*).
