Eight type studies are named in the "Non-Chordates" point of the syllabus; this chapter teaches the first three — the unicellular *Amoeba*, the cellular-level *Sycon* and the tissue-level *Hydra* — in the order morphology, anatomy, nutrition, respiration and reproduction. UP PGT 2026 asked an experiment-based question on the food vacuole of *Paramecium* and the medusa–polyp difference — that is, depth up to the B.Sc. practical manual.

> **In simple words:** *Amoeba* is one cell that does the work of the whole animal — it moves and catches food with "false feet" (pseudopodia). *Sycon* is a small vase-like sponge whose wall has countless pores; water comes in through them, collar cells filter out the food, and the water leaves through the large opening at the top (osculum). *Hydra* is a tiny freshwater polyp — a tube-like body with a ring of tentacles at the top, whose stinging cells paralyse the prey.

## Part A — *Amoeba proteus*

**Classification:** Phylum Protozoa → Subphylum Sarcomastigophora → Class Sarcodina (Rhizopoda) → Order Amoebida (Lobosa) → *Amoeba proteus*. Discovery: **Rösel von Rosenhof (1755)** called it the "little Proteus"; name *Amoeba* (Greek *amoibe* = change).

### Morphology and structure

- Habitat: at the bottom of clean, slow-flowing fresh water, near decaying vegetation. Size ~0.25–0.6 mm (a white speck to the naked eye).
- **No definite shape** — the shape keeps changing (polymorphic).
- **Plasmalemma** — thin outer membrane (with glycocalyx).
- **Cytoplasm** in two parts: **ectoplasm** — clear, non-granular, thick (**plasmagel**); **endoplasm** — granular, fluid (**plasmasol**); a clear **hyaline cap** at the tip of the pseudopodium.
- **Nucleus** — one, **disc-shaped/flattened biconcave**; folded in older animals.
- **Contractile vacuole** — one, osmoregulation; **food vacuoles**, water vacuoles, crystals (urinary salts/excretory), mitochondria, Golgi etc.
- **Pseudopodia** — in *A. proteus* broad, blunt finger-like **lobopodia** (made of both ecto- and endoplasm). Other types: **filopodia** (thin, ectoplasm only — *Euglypha*), **reticulopodia/rhizopodia** (net-like — foraminifera, *Elphidium*), **axopodia** (with an axial filament, straight — *Actinophrys*, Heliozoa).

### Locomotion — amoeboid movement

| Theory | Proposed by | Essence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sol–gel transformation theory** (accepted classical) | **Hyman (1917)**, **Pantin (1923)**, **Mast (1926)** | At the front end **gel → sol** (endoplasm flows into the pseudopodium); at the rear end **sol → gel**, and **contraction** of the plasmagel pushes the endoplasm forward |
| Fountain-zone (front-contraction) theory | **Allen (1961)** | Contraction at the front tip of the pseudopodium pulls the endoplasm |
| Surface-tension theory | Berthold, Bütschli | Surface tension decreases at the front |
| Rolling movement | Jennings | The upper surface rolls forward |
| **Modern view** | — | Polymerisation/depolymerisation of **actin** filaments and contraction by **myosin**; Ca²⁺ and ATP needed |

- Speed ~2–3 μm/s (about 1 cm per hour); attachment to a substratum is necessary.
- Amoeboid movement in humans: **white blood cells (macrophages, neutrophils)** — diapedesis.

### Nutrition — holozoic

Food: diatoms, bacteria, small protozoa (even *Paramecium*), algae.

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| **Ingestion** — from any part of the body (no permanent mouth) | **Modes:** (1) **circumvallation** — pseudopodia surround and close around motile prey, some water also enters; (2) **circumfluence** — immobile food is surrounded in direct contact, no water; (3) **import** — on contact the food sinks in passively; (4) **invagination** — the surface sinks in to form a tube |
| **Phagocytosis** ("cell eating") and **pinocytosis** ("cell drinking") | Pinocytosis — tiny droplets of fluid/dissolved protein through **pinocytic channels** (Mast and Doyle, 1934) |
| **Digestion (intracellular)** | Lysosomes fuse with the food vacuole; the vacuole is first **acidic** (pH down to ~5.6 — prey dies, proteins are digested), then **alkaline** (pH ~7.6 — further digestion); proteinases, amylase, lipase |
| **Absorption and assimilation** | Digested food diffuses into the cytoplasm; excess food stored as glycogen/fat |
| **Egestion** | Undigested food leaves from **any point** (usually the posterior end — uroid); there is no "cell anus" |

**Experiment on pH change in the food vacuole (B.Sc. practical):** Yeast cells stained with **Congo red** are fed to *Paramecium*. Congo red is a **pH indicator** — **red in neutral/alkaline, blue** (green–blue) **in acid**. The vacuole first turns from red to **blue/green** (acid phase) and later red again (alkaline phase). The same experiment is done with **neutral red** (cherry-red in acid).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "To study intracellular digestion in *Paramecium*, yeast cells were stained with the pH-sensitive dye Congo red. After ingestion the colour of the food vacuole changes from red to green. What does this observation most accurately show? (A) The vacuole becomes alkaline during digestion (B) Yeast cells carry out photosynthesis (C) The dye is chemically broken down inside the vacuole (D) The environment of the vacuole becomes acidic due to enzyme action" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**.

### Respiration, excretion and osmoregulation

- **Respiration** — **diffusion** of O₂ and CO₂ through the whole surface; aerobic (mitochondria).
- **Excretion** — the main excretory product is **ammonia** (**ammonotelic**) — diffuses out through the surface; some through the contractile vacuole.
- **Osmoregulation** — in fresh water the cell is **hypertonic**, so water keeps entering. The **contractile vacuole** collects water from tiny vesicles, swells (**diastole**) and bursts at the surface throwing the water out (**systole**) — this is an energy (ATP)-requiring process. Marine and parasitic amoebae (*Entamoeba*) have **no** contractile vacuole because the surroundings are isotonic.

### Reproduction

| Mode | When | Process |
|---|---|---|
| **Binary fission** — most common | Favourable conditions; completed in ~30–35 minutes | The animal rounds off, pseudopodia shorten, the nucleus divides by **mitosis (intranuclear, nuclear membrane persists — promitosis/eumitosis differs between texts)**, then the cytoplasm constricts into two — **transversely/in any plane**; the contractile vacuole goes to one daughter, a new one forms in the other |
| **Multiple fission / encystment** | Unfavourable conditions (drought, cold, lack of food) | The animal rounds off and forms a **three-layered chitinous cyst**; inside, the nucleus divides repeatedly; cytoplasm gathers around each nucleus — **amoebulae / pseudopodiospores**; in favourable conditions the cyst bursts releasing **hundreds** of small amoebae |
| **Sporulation** | Even without a cyst | The nucleus breaks into many parts, each surrounded by a spore membrane — up to about 200 "spores" (according to some authors) |
| **Regeneration** | Only from the fragment that has the **nucleus** | A fragment without a nucleus moves for a while and then dies — proof of nuclear control |
| **Sexual reproduction** | — | **Not proven** in *Amoeba proteus* (some old reports disputed) |

> **Common mistake:** "*Amoeba* is immortal" — in the sense that in binary fission **no corpse is left** (the parent cell is shared between the two daughters), so there is no natural death; death from accident/adverse conditions does occur.

## Part B — *Sycon* (*Scypha*)

**Classification:** Phylum Porifera → Class **Calcarea** → Order Heterocoela → *Sycon gelatinosum* / *S. ciliatum* (synonym *Scypha*). Marine, attached to rocks in shallow coastal water, often in **groups (colonies)**; branched by budding from the base.

### Morphology

- Small (~1–3 cm), **vase/urn-shaped**, cylindrical; colour grey/light brown.
- At the upper end a large opening, the **osculum** — surrounded by a **fringe** of long, straight **monaxon spicules**.
- On the surface countless tiny pores, the **ostia** (incurrent pores), and the raised **ends of the radial canals** in a hexagonal pattern — projecting spicules give a "bristly" look.
- Inside, a central cavity, the **spongocoel (paragaster)** — **not** lined by choanocytes (in sycon it is lined by **flat pinacocytes**).

| Canal-system type | Where the choanocytes are | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ascon** — simplest | Lining the **spongocoel** | *Leucosolenia* |
| **Sycon** | Lining the **radial canals** (incurrent canals — pinacocytes) | *Sycon*, *Grantia* |
| **Leucon** — most complex, most efficient | In small **flagellated chambers** | *Spongilla*, *Euspongia* |
| Rhagon | A stage of leucon (young of some demosponges) | — |

**Functions of the canal system:** bringing food (micro-organisms, organic particles), **respiration (O₂)**, **excretion** (ammonia out), reproduction (sperm out and in) — the water current is the "life" of the sponge.

### Cell types

| Cell | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Pinacocytes** | Outer surface (**pinacoderm**) and incurrent canals, spongocoel | Flat covering cells; contractile — control the size of the pores |
| **Porocytes** | Body wall (form the ostia) | Tubular, perforated cells — **prosopyles** (ostia in ascon) |
| **Choanocytes (collar cells)** | Radial canals (**choanoderm**) | **Collar** (ring of microvilli) + one **flagellum**; produce the water current, filter and capture food; carry sperm to the egg |
| **Amoebocytes of the mesenchyme/mesohyl** | Middle gelatinous layer (**mesohyl/mesenchyme**) | Food distribution, storage |
| **Archaeocytes** | Mesohyl | **Totipotent** — can form any cell, gametes, buds/gemmules |
| **Scleroblasts / calcoblasts** | Mesohyl | Secrete the **spicules** (CaCO₃ in sycon) |
| **Collencytes** | Mesohyl | Secrete collagen/jelly |
| **Myocytes** | Around the osculum/pores | Contractile — close the pores |

### Skeleton — calcareous spicules (CaCO₃)

- **Monaxon** — long ones: the oscular fringe; short ones: at the outer ends of the radial canals.
- **Triradiate (triaxon)** — in the wall of the canals (basal part).
- **Tetraxon (tetraradiate)** — projecting inward from the wall of the spongocoel.

### Nutrition, respiration, excretion

- **Nutrition** — **filter feeder / suspension feeder**; food particles stick to the collar of the choanocyte and are taken in by **phagocytosis**; digestion **intracellular** (in choanocytes and amoebocytes); amoebocytes carry food to other cells. **No mouth/alimentary canal.**
- **Respiration and excretion** — direct **diffusion** by each cell from the water current; ammonia (ammonotelic).
- **No nerve cells**; conduction of stimuli is cell-to-cell, slow.

### Reproduction

**Asexual —**
- **Budding** — external buds near the base; if they stay attached, a colony.
- **Regeneration** — a whole sponge from a small piece (because of archaeocytes).
- (**Gemmules** — internal buds, in the freshwater *Spongilla*; not in *Sycon*.)

**Sexual —**
- *Sycon* is **hermaphrodite** but **cross-fertilised**; usually **protogynous** (eggs mature first) — textbooks give "protogyny".
- Gametes are formed from **archaeocytes (or choanocytes)**; eggs lie in the mesohyl.
- Sperm reach another sponge with the water current; a **choanocyte** captures a sperm, becomes a **carrier (nurse) cell** and takes it to the egg — **fertilisation is internal (inside the sponge)**.
- Cleavage **holoblastic**; first a **stomoblastula** forms (flagella facing inward, a mouth-opening at one end through which it feeds on maternal cells) → **inversion** — flagella turn outward → **amphiblastula larva** (one half small flagellated cells, the other half large non-flagellated macromeres).
- The amphiblastula leaves the sponge and swims → the flagellated half sinks inward (**invagination**) → the flagellated cells become **choanocytes** and the macromeres **pinacocytes** — **"inversion of layers"** — the outer cells of the larva move inside in the adult.
- It attaches and forms the young **olynthus** stage (asconoid) → syconoid adult.

> **Exam angle:** "Inversion of layers in sponges" and "sponges = a blind branch of evolution (Parazoa)" — both are favourite B.Sc. statements.

## Part C — *Hydra*

**Classification:** Phylum Cnidaria → Class **Hydrozoa** → Order Hydroida → *Hydra vulgaris* (brown), *H. oligactis* (*Pelmatohydra*, long tentacles), **green hydra** *Chlorohydra viridissima* (*H. viridissima*) — in **symbiosis** with **zoochlorellae** (the alga *Chlorella*). Discovery: **Leeuwenhoek (1702)**; regeneration experiments **Abraham Trembley (1744)**; Linnaeus named it after "Hydra", the many-headed serpent of Greek legend.

### Morphology

- Freshwater, **solitary**, **polyp** (**no** medusa stage). Can extend up to ~10–30 mm in length.
- Cylindrical body: at the top the **hypostome** (conical) — with the **mouth** at its apex; below it **6–10 hollow tentacles** (usually 6–8); at the lower end the **basal disc (pedal disc)** — sticky secretion for attachment to the substratum.
- Body wall: **epidermis** — **mesoglea** — **gastrodermis**; inside, the **gastrovascular cavity (coelenteron)**, which extends into the tentacles too.
- **Radial symmetry**, **diploblastic**, **tissue level**.

### Cell types

| Epidermis | Function |
|---|---|
| **Epitheliomuscular cells** — most numerous | **Longitudinal** muscle fibres (**myonemes**) at the base — contraction **shortens** the body |
| **Interstitial cells (I-cells)** | Small, **totipotent/multipotent** — form cnidoblasts, gametes, buds, other cells; basis of regeneration |
| **Cnidoblasts (nematocytes)** | Most numerous in **battery cells** on the tentacles; also on the hypostome; **not on the basal disc** |
| **Sensory cells** | Receive stimuli (more on tentacles, hypostome) |
| **Nerve cells** | A **nerve net** near the mesoglea — no brain; **synapses and non-polarised conduction** (some synapses bidirectional) |
| **Gland cells** | Basal disc (sticky secretion + gas bubble), hypostome |
| **Germ cells** | In the breeding season (from interstitial cells) |

| Gastrodermis | Function |
|---|---|
| **Nutritive-muscular cells** | **Circular** muscle fibres at the base — contraction makes the body **thin and long**; flagella and pseudopodia — phagocytosis; in green hydra the zoochlorellae lie in these |
| **Gland cells** | Digestive enzymes (near the hypostome — mucus) |
| Interstitial, sensory, nerve cells | Few in number |

### Cnidoblast and nematocyst

- Inside the cnidoblast is a capsule, the **nematocyst** — a coiled hollow **thread tube**; outside, the **cnidocil** — the trigger; a lid (**operculum**); the capsule contains the poisonous fluid **hypnotoxin**.
- On touch + chemical stimulus, **rapid entry of water** into the capsule (osmotic pressure, Ca²⁺) → the thread turns inside out and shoots out — **one of the fastest cellular processes in the living world**.
- **Used only once**; a new cnidoblast forms from interstitial cells.

| Nematocyst (4 types in hydra) | Thread | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Penetrant (stenotele)** — largest | **3 large barbs (stylets)** at the base, and spiny; tip open | **Pierces** the prey's skin and injects **hypnotoxin** (paralysis) |
| **Volvent (desmoneme)** — smallest | Short, thick, forms a **coil**, tip closed | **Wraps** around bristles/appendages of the prey |
| **Streptoline glutinant (holotrichous isorhiza)** | Long, spiny, tip open, sticky | **Adhesion** (in locomotion) |
| **Stereoline glutinant (atrichous isorhiza)** | Straight, without spines | Adhesion, defence |

### Nutrition — carnivorous, holozoic

- Food: *Daphnia*, *Cyclops*, insect larvae, small worms.
- Touch of prey → cnidoblasts (penetrant, volvent) → tentacles carry the prey to the mouth. **Glutathione** (released from the prey's wound) is the chemical stimulus for the **feeding response** (opening of the mouth) — **Lenhoff's (1955)** experiment.
- **Digestion in two phases** — (1) **extracellular** in the gastrovascular cavity — proteases (trypsin-like) of the gland cells; (2) **intracellular** — nutritive cells take in small particles by phagocytosis and digest fats and carbohydrates in food vacuoles. **Extracellular digestion appears for the first time** in evolution in Cnidaria.
- Undigested food leaves **through the mouth itself** (mouth = anus; incomplete alimentary canal).
- **Storage** — glycogen and fat; green hydra gets sugar from the alga, the alga gets CO₂ and nitrogenous waste.

### Respiration, excretion, osmoregulation

- **No respiratory/excretory organs** — each cell takes O₂ and gives out CO₂ and **ammonia** by diffusion through water (outer cells directly, inner cells through the water of the cavity).
- Excess water is mainly expelled from the gastrovascular cavity through the mouth (osmoregulation).

### Locomotion

| Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Looping / inchworm-like** | Bending the body, attaching the tentacles to the surface, then bringing the basal disc near the tentacles — leech-like movement |
| **Somersaulting** — fastest | Tentacles on the surface, the basal disc rises and flips over to the other side |
| **Gliding** | Slow sliding by pseudopodia/secretion of the basal disc |
| **Walking on tentacles** | Upside down, using the tentacles as legs |
| **Floating** | Forming a **gas bubble** at the basal disc and floating upside down at the surface |
| **Expansion–contraction** | Staying in place and catching prey |

### Reproduction

**Asexual —**
- **Budding** — most common in favourable conditions (summer, plenty of food); a bulge of interstitial cells in the **middle to lower part** of the body (budding zone) → all three layers (epidermis, mesoglea, gastrodermis) included → tentacles and mouth form → separates by constriction at the base. The cavity of the bud remains connected with the parent's cavity until it separates. **Exogenous budding**.
- **Fission** — longitudinal/transverse (occasionally, possibly due to injury).
- **Regeneration** — Trembley (1744): a whole hydra from small pieces (down to 1/200 of the body); **polarity** is retained — the hypostome forms only at the oral end. **Hydra is regarded as an example of "biological immortality"** (signs of ageing negligible — continuous renewal by interstitial/stem cells).

**Sexual —**
- Usually in **autumn** (falling temperature, less food); gonads **temporary**, formed from interstitial cells in the epidermis.
- Most species are **monoecious (hermaphrodite)** — e.g. *H. vulgaris*; some **dioecious** — e.g. *H. oligactis*.
- **Testes** — conical, in the **upper (oral) part**, many; **ovary** — one/few, in the **lower part**, each with **one egg**.
- Hermaphrodite species are usually **protandrous** — testes mature first; hence **cross-fertilisation**.
- Fertilisation **on the epidermis, in the ovary itself (in situ)** — the sperm enters the egg attached to the body.
- Zygote → holoblastic cleavage → blastula → **solid gastrula (stereogastrula)** → a **chitinous spiny cyst (embryotheca)** forms around it → the embryo separates from the parent and spends several weeks (winter) at the bottom → at a favourable time a **young hydra** hatches.
- **Development direct — no larva** (the **planula** larva of most marine cnidarians is absent in hydra).

### Comparison with *Obelia* — polymorphism and alternation of generations

| Character | *Hydra* | *Obelia* |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Fresh water | Sea |
| Form | **Solitary polyp** | **Colony**: feeding polyp (**hydranth**/gastrozooid) + reproductive polyp (**blastostyle/gonangium**) + free **medusa** |
| Outer covering | Absent | Chitinous **perisarc** (hydrotheca, gonotheca) — calyptoblastic |
| Alternation of generations | Absent | **Metagenesis** — asexual polyp ⇌ sexual medusa (both diploid; this is not the "alternation of generations" of plants) |
| Larva | Absent | **Planula** |

### Polyp vs medusa

| Character | Polyp | Medusa |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Cylindrical, tube-like | **Umbrella/bell-like** |
| Condition | Usually **sessile**, attached | **Free-swimming** |
| Mouth | Facing **upward** (oral surface up) | Facing **downward** (oral surface down; manubrium hangs) |
| Mesoglea | Thin | **Thick**, jelly-like |
| Function | Feeding, asexual reproduction (budding) | **Sexual reproduction** (gonads), dispersal |
| Sense organs | Absent | **Statocysts**, eye-spots (ocelli) |
| Examples | *Hydra*, sea anemone, coral polyp | *Aurelia*, medusa of *Obelia* |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Which statement correctly distinguishes a medusa from a polyp? (A) The medusa is free-swimming with its oral surface facing downward (B) The medusa's mouth faces upward, the polyp's downward (C) Polyps lack tentacles whereas medusae have them (D) The medusa is sessile and the polyp motile" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)**.

## Traps and exceptions

- *Amoeba proteus* has **one** contractile vacuole; *Paramecium* has **two** (anterior and posterior, with star-shaped feeding canals).
- The food vacuole is **first acidic, then alkaline** — the order is asked reversed.
- *Entamoeba* has no contractile vacuole — no osmotic problem.
- In sycon the choanocytes are in the **radial canals**, **not** in the spongocoel (that is a feature of ascon).
- Spicules of sycon are **calcareous**; of *Euplectella* **siliceous**; *Euspongia* has **spongin**.
- Gemmules occur in **freshwater sponges** (*Spongilla*) — not in *Sycon* (marine).
- **No** cnidoblasts on the basal disc of hydra; muscle fibres of epitheliomuscular cells are **longitudinal**, those of nutritive-muscular cells **circular**.
- Hydra is a **cnidarian** but forms **no medusa** and **no larva** — both are exceptions.
- Green hydra's symbiosis is with **zoochlorellae** (green alga); corals' with **zooxanthellae** (dinoflagellates) — these are expelled in coral bleaching.
- In the cnidarian "alternation of generations" (*Obelia*) both generations are **diploid**.

## Quick revision

- *Amoeba proteus* — lobopodia; ectoplasm = plasmagel, endoplasm = plasmasol.
- Sol–gel theory — Mast (1926)/Hyman/Pantin; modern — actin–myosin.
- Food capture — circumvallation, circumfluence, import, invagination.
- Pinocytosis — Mast and Doyle (1934).
- Food vacuole: acidic → alkaline; Congo red: red (neutral) → blue/green (acid).
- Contractile vacuole = osmoregulation; in freshwater protozoa.
- Amoeba in unfavourable conditions — encystment and multiple fission (pseudopodiospores).
- *Sycon* — Calcarea; syconoid canal system.
- Water path: ostia → incurrent canal → prosopyle → radial canal → apopyle → spongocoel → osculum.
- Archaeocytes totipotent; scleroblasts form spicules.
- *Sycon* hermaphrodite, protogynous, internal fertilisation (carrier choanocyte).
- Stomoblastula → inversion → amphiblastula → olynthus.
- *Hydra* — polyp only, no medusa and no larva.
- Interstitial cells — source of regeneration, budding, cnidoblasts.
- Four nematocysts: penetrant (largest), volvent (smallest), two glutinants.
- Stimulus of the feeding response — glutathione.
- Digestion extracellular + intracellular.
- Somersaulting — fastest locomotion.
- Testes above, ovary below; protandrous; encysted embryo; direct development.
- Regeneration experiments — Trembley (1744).

2. The sol–gel theory of amoeboid movement is associated with? — **Mast** (1926; Hyman, Pantin).
3. The mode of surrounding immobile food without water? — **Circumfluence**.
4. The main nitrogenous waste of *Amoeba*? — **Ammonia**.
5. Why do marine amoebae lack a contractile vacuole? — **Surroundings isotonic, no water entry**.
6. Small amoebae formed inside the cyst? — **Pseudopodiospores (amoebulae)**.
7. *Sycon* belongs to which class? — **Calcarea** (calcareous spicules).

9. The most efficient canal system of sponges? — **Leucon** (flagellated chambers).
10. Which cells of sponges are totipotent? — **Archaeocytes**.
11. Larva of *Sycon*? — **Amphiblastula**.
12. Who carries the sperm to the egg in a sponge? — **Choanocyte (carrier/nurse cell)**.
13. Symbiont of green hydra? — **Zoochlorellae** (*Chlorella*).
14. The largest nematocyst of hydra? — **Penetrant (stenotele)** — hypnotoxin.
15. Which chemical triggers the feeding response in hydra? — **Glutathione** (Lenhoff).
16. The fibres that shorten the body of hydra are in which cell? — **Epitheliomuscular** (longitudinal myonemes).
17. The fastest locomotion of hydra? — **Somersaulting**.
18. Larva in hydra? — **None** — direct development (encysted embryo).
19. What is alternation of generations in *Obelia* called? — **Metagenesis** (polyp ⇌ medusa).
20. Which way does the mouth face in a medusa? — **Downward** (upward in the polyp).
