The syllabus point "Animal Histology — tissues" looks small, but its facts (which tissue is where) keep coming up in questions on physiology, embryology and cell biology too. NCERT Class 11 ("Structural Organisation in Animals") is the base; at B.Sc. level modes of secretion, the fine structure of bone, neuroglia and junctions are added. UP PGT usually asks "matching" (tissue ↔ location) and "choose the incorrect statement" type questions from here.

> **In simple words:** A tissue = a group of cells of similar origin and function, together with their intercellular material. Animals have only four basic tissues — **epithelial** (covering/lining), **connective** (joining and supporting), **muscular** (contracting) and **nervous** (carrying messages). Every organ is made of a combination of these four.

## 1. Tissues — introduction

- The term "tissue" — **Bichat (1801)** — "father of histology"; the term "Histology" — **Mayer (1819)**; **Marcello Malpighi** — "founder of microscopic anatomy/histology" (in some books).
- **Embryonic origin** of the four tissues:

| Tissue | Origin |
|---|---|
| Epithelial | **All three** germ layers (epidermis of skin — ectoderm; intestinal lining — endoderm; kidney tubules, endothelium of blood vessels — mesoderm) |
| Connective | **Mesoderm** (embryonic **mesenchyme**) — some parts of the head from the **neural crest** |
| Muscular | **Mesoderm** (exceptions: the muscles of the **iris** and the **myoepithelial** cells of sweat/mammary glands — ectoderm) |
| Nervous | **Ectoderm** (neural tube and neural crest); **microglia — mesoderm** (monocyte lineage) |

## 2. Epithelial tissue

**General characters:** cells **closely packed**, **very little** intercellular material; a free (apical) surface and a surface resting on a **basement membrane**; **avascular** — no blood vessels, nourished by diffusion from the underlying connective tissue; **may have nerves**; **high regenerative capacity**.
- **Basement membrane** = **basal lamina** (secreted by the epithelial cells — **type IV collagen, laminin**) + **reticular lamina** (from the underlying connective tissue — type III collagen).
- **Mesothelium** — the simple squamous epithelium lining the body cavities (peritoneum, pleura, pericardium); **endothelium** — lining of blood and lymph vessels and the heart.

### (a) Simple epithelium — one layer of cells

| Type | Cell shape | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple squamous (pavement)** | Flat, polygonal, irregular margins, flat nucleus | **Alveoli of lungs**, outer wall of **Bowman's capsule**, **blood capillaries** (endothelium), **thin segment of the loop of Henle**, lining of body cavities (mesothelium), inner part of the tympanic membrane | **Diffusion, filtration** — a thin barrier |
| **Simple cuboidal** | Cube-shaped, round nucleus in the centre | **Kidney tubules** (**PCT** — **microvilli / brush border** at the apex), **thyroid follicles**, **ducts** of glands (salivary, pancreas), **germinal epithelium of the ovary** (named "germinal" but actually a covering), pigment epithelium of the retina, secretory part of sweat glands | **Secretion, absorption** |
| **Simple columnar** | Tall, nucleus towards the base | Lining of the **stomach** (mucus-secreting, no microvilli), **intestine** (**microvilli — striated border** + **goblet cells**), **gall bladder**, large intestine | **Absorption, secretion** |
| **Ciliated columnar** | Columnar + cilia at the apex | **Oviduct (Fallopian tube)**, **bronchioles (small)**, **ventricles of the brain and central canal of the spinal cord** (**ependyma** — ciliated cuboidal/columnar) | Moves particles/mucus/ovum **in one direction** |
| **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar** | **A single layer** — all cells rest on the basement membrane but differ in height → nuclei at different levels → **appears stratified**; goblet cells | **Trachea**, **bronchi**, most of the nasal cavity (**respiratory epithelium**) | The **"mucociliary escalator"** — moves dust particles upward |
| **Pseudostratified with stereocilia** | — | **Epididymis**, vas deferens (**stereocilia** = long microvilli, not true cilia) | Absorption |

### (b) Compound / stratified epithelium — many layers

**Named after the shape of the cells of the outermost (top) layer**; main function — **protection** (from chemical and mechanical stress); limited role in secretion/absorption.

| Type | Location |
|---|---|
| **Keratinised stratified squamous** | **Epidermis of skin** (dry surface) — the upper dead cells are filled with **keratin** |
| **Non-keratinised stratified squamous** | **Buccal cavity, pharynx, oesophagus**, **vagina**, lower part of the anal canal, **cornea** (outer), tongue (partly keratinised) — moist surfaces |
| **Stratified cuboidal** | **Ducts of sweat glands**, large ducts of mammary/salivary glands (a rare type) |
| **Stratified columnar** | Part of the **conjunctiva**, parts of the **male urethra**, large ducts (rare) |
| **Transitional / urothelium** | **Urinary bladder, ureters**, renal pelvis, upper part of the urethra — upper cells large, rounded-domed (**umbrella cells**, sometimes binucleate); **flatten on stretching** → the organ can expand; **impermeable** to urine |

> **Memory trick:** "**Breath — Pseudo, Bladder — Transitional, Bowel — Columnar, Air sac — Squamous, Skin — Keratinised**" — trachea = pseudostratified, urinary bladder = transitional, intestine = columnar, alveolus = squamous, skin = keratinised stratified squamous.

### (c) Glandular epithelium

| Basis | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Number of cells | **Unicellular** | **Goblet cells** — intestine, trachea (**mucin/mucus**) — **the only unicellular exocrine gland** |
| | **Multicellular** | Salivary, sweat, gastric glands |
| Route of secretion | **Exocrine** — through a **duct** onto a surface/into a cavity | Mucus, saliva, earwax (cerumen), oil, milk, digestive enzymes |
| | **Endocrine** — **ductless**, directly into blood (**hormones**) | Thyroid, pituitary, adrenal |
| | **Mixed (heterocrine)** | **Pancreas** (digestive juice + insulin/glucagon), gonads, liver (bile + IGF etc.) |
| Shape (multicellular) | **Simple tubular** (intestinal glands — **crypts of Lieberkühn**), **simple coiled tubular** (**sweat gland**), **simple branched tubular** (gastric glands, Brunner's glands), **simple alveolar/acinar** (**sebaceous gland** — branched acinar), **compound tubulo-alveolar** (**salivary glands**, pancreas, **mammary gland**) | — |

**Modes of secretion — exocrine:**

| Mode | What happens | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Merocrine / eccrine** | Secretion by **exocytosis**; **no damage to the cell** — the commonest | **Salivary glands**, **pancreas**, **goblet cells**, most **sweat glands (eccrine)** |
| **Apocrine** | The **apical part** of the cell breaks off with the secretion, then is rebuilt | **Mammary gland** (fat droplets), **sweat glands of the armpit/genital region** (apocrine — odour), ceruminous (earwax) glands, glands of Moll |
| **Holocrine** | The **whole cell** disintegrates to become the secretion; new cells from the basal layer | **Sebaceous gland** — **sebum**; **Meibomian glands** of the eyelids |

### (d) Cell junctions

| Junction | Structure | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tight junction (zonula occludens)** | Adjacent membranes "stitched" by the proteins **claudin, occludin** | Prevents leakage of substances **between** cells | Intestinal epithelium, **blood–brain barrier**, kidney tubules |
| **Adherens junction (zonula adherens)** | **Cadherin** + actin | Holds cells together | Epithelia |
| **Desmosome (macula adherens)** | A "spot weld" — cadherin + **intermediate filaments (keratin)** | Mechanical **strength** — resists stretching | **Skin**, **cardiac muscle** (intercalated discs), cervix |
| **Hemidesmosome** | Joins the cell to the **basement membrane** (integrin) | Attachment to the base | Basal cells of the skin |
| **Gap junction (nexus)** | **Connexons** (6 connexins) — channels joining the cytoplasm of two cells | Direct communication of **ions, small molecules, electrical signals** | **Cardiac muscle**, smooth muscle, electrical synapses |

## 3. Connective tissue

**General characters:** the **most widespread and abundant** tissue in the body; cells few and far apart, **intercellular material (matrix) abundant** — **ground substance** (proteoglycans, glycosaminoglycans — **hyaluronic acid, chondroitin sulphate**) + **fibres**; usually **vascular** (cartilage is the exception). In all connective tissues **except blood**, the cells secrete structural proteins (**collagen, elastin**) (NCERT).

### Fibres and cells of connective tissue

| Fibre | Protein | Character | Main locations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **White / collagen fibres** | **Collagen** (mainly type I) — **the most abundant protein of the body** | Thick, **unbranched**, in bundles, **very high tensile strength**, little elasticity; yield **gelatin** on boiling | **Tendons**, bone, dermis |
| **Yellow / elastic fibres** | **Elastin** (+ fibrillin) | Thin, **branched**, single, **elastic** — stretch and recoil | **Ligaments**, walls of large arteries, lungs, pinna |
| **Reticular fibres** | **Type III collagen** | Very thin, branched, a **network**; black with silver salts (argyrophilic) | **Lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow, liver** — framework (stroma) of soft organs |

| Cell | Function |
|---|---|
| **Fibroblast** (most common) | **Secretes** fibres and ground substance; wound healing |
| **Macrophage (histiocyte)** | **Phagocytosis**; antigen presentation; from monocytes |
| **Mast cell** | **Histamine** (vasodilator, allergy), **heparin** (anticoagulant), **serotonin**; IgE receptors |
| **Plasma cell** | **Antibodies** — differentiated from B-lymphocytes; "cartwheel" nucleus |
| **Fat cell (adipocyte)** | **Fat storage** — nucleus and cytoplasm pushed to the edge ("signet ring") |
| **Pigment cells (melanocytes)** | Melanin (skin, choroid of the eye) |

### (a) Connective tissue proper

| Type | Structure | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loose — areolar** | **The commonest**; all three fibres and all cell types loosely in a semi-fluid matrix | **Beneath the skin (hypodermis)**, around blood vessels/nerves/organs, beneath mucosa (**submucosa**), **joining organs** | Supporting framework, joining, route for nourishment, **immunity**, wound healing |
| **Loose — adipose** | Groups of fat cells, a little areolar tissue | **Hypodermis**, around the kidneys, mesentery, behind the eyeball, bone marrow (yellow) | **Energy storage, insulation, cushioning (shock absorption)** |
| **White fat** | One large fat droplet (**unilocular**) | Most fat in adults | Storage; hormone **leptin** |
| **Brown fat** | Many small droplets (**multilocular**), **very many mitochondria** (cytochromes — brown colour) — **thermogenin (UCP-1)** | **Newborn babies** (between the shoulders, neck), **hibernating animals** | **Non-shivering thermogenesis** — heat instead of ATP |
| **Dense regular** | Collagen/elastic fibres in **parallel rows**, with rows of fibroblasts between | **Tendon** — joins **muscle to bone** (white, collagen); **ligament** — joins **bone to bone** (some elastic, e.g. **ligamentum flavum**, **ligamentum nuchae** of the neck) | Withstands great pull in one direction |
| **Dense irregular** | Collagen fibres interwoven in **various directions** | **Dermis of the skin**, capsules of organs, periosteum, perichondrium | Withstands tension from many directions |
| **Reticular tissue** | Reticular fibres + reticular cells | Lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow | Framework of soft organs |

> **Common mistake:** "**Tendon = muscle to bone**, **ligament = bone to bone**" — asked reversed. Tendons have mainly **white collagen** fibres, ligaments also **yellow elastic** fibres (traditional textbook statement).

### (b) Specialised connective tissue — skeletal: cartilage

- Matrix solid but **flexible, semi-rigid** — **chondrin** (chondroitin sulphate + collagen); cells are **chondrocytes** — in small spaces, the **lacunae**, secreted by the matrix (1–4 cells in one lacuna — **cell nests**); outside, the **perichondrium** — a firm connective sheath (growth and nourishment).
- **Avascular** — nourished by diffusion → **slow repair**.

| Type | Matrix | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyaline** — **the commonest** | Transparent, bluish, fine collagen (II) fibres not visible | **Tip of the nose**, **rings of the trachea and bronchi**, larynx (thyroid, cricoid), **sternal ends of the ribs (costal cartilage)**, **articular surfaces of bones at joints**, **embryonic skeleton**, **growth plate of long bones (epiphyseal plate)** |
| **Elastic** | A network of **yellow elastic fibres** — yellow, flexible | **Pinna**, **external auditory canal**, **epiglottis**, **Eustachian tube**, some laryngeal cartilages (corniculate, cuneiform) |
| **White fibrous (fibrocartilage)** | **Thick collagen (I) bundles** — **the strongest**; **no perichondrium** | **Intervertebral discs** (outer **annulus fibrosus**; inner **nucleus pulposus** = notochordal remnant), **pubic symphysis**, **menisci** of the knee, attachment of some tendons to bone |
| **Calcified** | **Calcium salts** in the matrix | **Suprascapula** of the frog's scapula, skeleton of sharks, larynx in old age |

### (c) Bone

- **The hardest connective tissue**; matrix — **organic ~1/3** (mainly **ossein** — type I collagen) + **inorganic ~2/3** (**hydroxyapatite — Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂**; calcium phosphate ~85%, CaCO₃ ~10%). Soaked in acid (inorganic removed) bone becomes **flexible, rubber-like**; burnt (organic removed) it becomes **brittle**.
- **Cells:** **osteoblasts** (bone-**forming** — secrete matrix), **osteocytes** (mature, enclosed in **lacunae**, in contact with each other by **canaliculi**), **osteoclasts** (bone-**dissolving/resorbing** — **multinucleate giant cells**, monocyte–macrophage lineage; **parathormone** activates them, **calcitonin** inhibits them).
- **Periosteum** — outer covering (growth, repair, tendon attachment — **Sharpey's fibres**); **endosteum** — lining of the marrow cavity.

| | **Compact bone** | **Spongy / cancellous bone** |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **Haversian systems (osteons)** — **concentric lamellae** around a central **Haversian canal** (blood vessel, nerve, lymphatic), **lacunae** between them and **canaliculi** joining them; **Volkmann's canals** connect the Haversian canals transversely; **interstitial lamellae** between osteons | **Trabeculae** — a network of thin bony plates; **no Haversian systems**; spaces between filled with **red marrow** |
| Location | **Shaft (diaphysis)** of long bones, outer layer of all bones | **Ends (epiphyses)** of long bones, **flat bones** (skull — **diploë**), vertebral bodies, sternum, pelvis |
| Function | Weight-bearing, strength | Lightness, **blood formation (red marrow)** |

- **Bone marrow** — **red** (blood-cell formation — **haemopoiesis**; in adults in flat bones, vertebrae, ends of long bones) and **yellow** (fat; in the shaft cavity of long bones).
- **Ossification** — **intramembranous** — bone forms directly from mesenchyme — **flat bones of the skull, clavicle** → **dermal / membrane bones**; **endochondral** — first a hyaline-cartilage model, then replaced by bone — **long bones, vertebrae** → **cartilage / replacing bones**.
- **Pneumatic bones of birds** — air cavities inside; **Haversian systems** are characteristic of **mammalian bones** (usually absent/scanty in small vertebrates like the frog).

### (d) Fluid connective tissue — blood and lymph

- **Blood** = **plasma** (~55% — 90–92% water, proteins — **albumin, globulin, fibrinogen**) + **formed elements** (~45% — **RBCs, WBCs, platelets**); the matrix (plasma) has **no fibres** (fibrin forms only when a clot forms) and its cells do not secrete the matrix — yet it is a connective tissue because of its **mesodermal origin** and **fluid matrix**.
- **WBCs** — **granulocytes**: **neutrophils** (60–65% — most numerous; phagocytic; multilobed nucleus), **eosinophils** (2–3% — allergy, **worm infections**; bilobed), **basophils** (0.5–1% — fewest; histamine, heparin, serotonin); **agranulocytes**: **lymphocytes** (20–25% — B and T; immunity), **monocytes** (6–8% — the largest WBC; macrophages in tissues; kidney-shaped nucleus).
- **Memory trick (decreasing order):** "**Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas**" — neutrophils > lymphocytes > monocytes > eosinophils > basophils.
- **Lymph** — colourless, from tissue fluid; **no RBCs or platelets**, **lymphocytes abundant**, less protein; absorption of fat (**chylomicrons**) by the **lacteals** of the intestine.
- (Details: [Heart and circulation](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/hriday-aur-parisancharan), [Immune system](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/pratiraksha-tantra-aur-enzyme).)

## 4. Muscular tissue

**General characters:** long, **excitable and contractile** cells — **muscle fibres**; cytoplasm = **sarcoplasm**, membrane = **sarcolemma**, endoplasmic reticulum = **sarcoplasmic reticulum** (Ca²⁺ store); contractile proteins — **actin (thin) and myosin (thick)** filaments (**myofilaments**) → **myofibrils**.

| Character | **Skeletal / striated** | **Cardiac** | **Smooth / unstriated / visceral** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control** | **Voluntary** — somatic nervous system | **Involuntary** — autonomic + **self-excitatory (myogenic — SA node)** | **Involuntary** — autonomic nerves, hormones |
| **Cell shape** | **Long, cylindrical, unbranched** (up to several cm) | Cylindrical, **branched** — form a network | **Spindle-shaped**, both ends pointed |
| **Nucleus** | **Multinucleate** (**syncytium**, by fusion of myoblasts), **peripheral** (beneath the sarcolemma) | **One (sometimes two), central** | **One, central** (elongated) |
| **Striations** | **Distinct** — dark **A-band** (anisotropic) and light **I-band** (isotropic); **Z-line**, **H-zone**, **M-line**; **sarcomere** = the unit between two Z-lines | **Present** (less distinct) | **Absent** (actin–myosin irregular; **dense bodies**) |
| **Intercalated discs** | Absent | **Present** — **gap junctions + desmosomes** → the whole heart contracts together like **one functional syncytium** | Absent (but **gap junctions** in single-unit smooth muscle) |
| **T-tubules** | Present (at the A–I junction — triad) | Present (at the Z-line — dyad) | Absent (**caveolae**) |
| **Contraction** | **Rapid, powerful, fatigues quickly** | **Rhythmic, continuous, never tiring**; **long refractory period** → **no tetanus** | **Slow, sustained, rhythmic** (peristalsis), very little fatigue |
| **Location** | **Attached to the skeleton** (by tendons) — legs, arms, tongue, pharynx, **upper 1/3 of the oesophagus**, diaphragm, extrinsic muscles of the eyeball | **Only the wall of the heart (myocardium)** (and a little at the bases of the venae cavae/pulmonary veins) | **Walls of viscera** — alimentary canal (lower oesophagus to rectum), **blood vessels**, urinary bladder, uterus, bronchi, **iris and ciliary muscle (eye)**, **arrector pili muscle**, vas deferens |
| **Regeneration** | Limited (satellite cells) | **Almost none** (scar tissue after a heart attack) | **Good** |

- **Red muscle fibres** (slow, more **myoglobin**, more mitochondria, aerobic — sustained work; flight muscles of birds, muscles of runners) vs **white muscle fibres** (fast, anaerobic, fatigue quickly — **lactic acid** accumulation; eye muscles).
- (The **sliding filament theory** of contraction — Huxley and Hanson: [Movement and locomotion](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/gati-aur-chalan).)

## 5. Nervous tissue

- **Nerve cell (neuron)** — the **structural and functional unit**; highest **excitability and conductivity**; **does not divide** (most in adults — **G₀** stage; exception — limited neurogenesis in the hippocampus, olfactory bulb); **the longest cell** (~1 m from spinal cord to foot).
- **Cell body (cyton / soma / perikaryon)** — nucleus, **Nissl granules** — groups of **rough ER + ribosomes** (protein synthesis; **absent from the axon and axon hillock**), **neurofibrils**; **centriole usually absent** → no division.
- **Dendrites** — short, branched, many; carry **impulses towards the cell body (afferent)**; Nissl granules present.
- **Axon** — **one**, long; carries **impulses away from the cell body**; **axon hillock** (site of initiation of the action potential); branches at the end → **synaptic knobs** — vesicles containing **neurotransmitters**.
- **Myelin sheath** — coiled layers of lipoprotein (sphingomyelin); in the **peripheral nervous system (PNS)** formed by **Schwann cells** (one cell — one segment of one axon), in the **central (CNS)** by **oligodendrocytes** (one cell — segments of many axons); the gaps between are the **nodes of Ranvier** → **saltatory conduction** — fast (up to ~100 m/s) and energy-saving. The outer layer of the Schwann cell is the **neurilemma** (helps regeneration in the PNS).
- **Myelinated** — cranial and spinal nerves (PNS), **white matter** of the CNS; **non-myelinated** — postganglionic fibres of the **autonomic nervous system**, some somatic fibres, grey matter of the CNS.

### Types of neurons

| Basis | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Number of processes | **Multipolar** — one axon + many dendrites — **the commonest** | **Cerebral cortex**, **motor neurons** of the spinal cord |
| | **Bipolar** — one axon + one dendrite | **Retina**, **olfactory epithelium**, inner ear (spiral/vestibular ganglia) |
| | **Unipolar** — axon only | In the **embryonic stage**; invertebrates |
| | **Pseudounipolar** — a single process that divides T-wise into two branches | **Sensory** neurons of the **dorsal root ganglion** of spinal nerves |
| Function | **Sensory (afferent)**, **motor (efferent)**, **interneuron (connector)** — in the CNS, the most numerous | — |

### Neuroglia (glia)

- **~10 times the number of neurons** (newer estimates ~1:1) and **more than half the volume of nervous tissue**; **can divide** (brain tumours — gliomas usually arise from them).

| Glia | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Astrocyte** — the most numerous, star-shaped | CNS | Helps form the **blood–brain barrier** (end-feet on capillaries), balance of ions/neurotransmitters, nourishment, scar formation at wounds (gliosis) |
| **Oligodendrocyte** | CNS | **Myelin in the CNS** |
| **Microglia** — the smallest | CNS | **Phagocytic** — "macrophages of the brain"; **mesodermal** (monocyte lineage) |
| **Ependymal cells** | Lining of the **ventricles of the brain and central canal of the spinal cord** — ciliated | Formation (choroid plexus) and circulation of **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)** |
| **Schwann cells** | PNS | **Myelin in the PNS**, regeneration |
| **Satellite cells** | PNS — around neuron cell bodies in ganglia | Nourishment, protection |

- **Nerve** = a bundle of axons — coverings: **epineurium** (whole nerve), **perineurium** (bundle/fascicle), **endoneurium** (each fibre).
- **Synapse** — **chemical** (neurotransmitters — acetylcholine, norepinephrine, dopamine, GABA; one-way; **synaptic delay** ~0.5 ms) and **electrical** (gap junctions; two-way, fast).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Pseudostratified** epithelium is actually **single-layered**.
- The **"germinal epithelium" of the ovary** does not form gametes — it is only a **simple cuboidal covering**.
- **Goblet cell** = a unicellular **exocrine** gland (not endocrine).
- **Mammary gland** — apocrine (traditional; modern studies also describe merocrine secretion of proteins); **sebaceous gland — holocrine**.
- **Epithelium and cartilage** — both are **avascular**.
- **Fibrocartilage has no perichondrium**.
- **Blood** is a connective tissue, but its cells do **not** form fibres.
- **Tendon** — muscle to bone; **ligament** — bone to bone.
- **Cardiac muscle** is striated but **involuntary**; the **upper part of the oesophagus is striated (voluntary)**, the lower part smooth.
- **Muscles of the iris** — smooth, but of **ectodermal** origin.
- **Microglia arise from mesoderm** — the other glia from ectoderm.
- **No Nissl granules in the axon**.
- **Myelin in the CNS — oligodendrocyte**, **in the PNS — Schwann cell**.
- **Brown fat** — thermogenin (UCP-1) — heat, not ATP.

## Quick revision

- Four tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous; the term "tissue" — Bichat.
- Epithelium avascular; basement membrane = basal lamina + reticular lamina.
- Alveolus — squamous; PCT — cuboidal; intestine — columnar; trachea — pseudostratified; urinary bladder — transitional; skin — keratinised stratified squamous.
- Modes of secretion: merocrine (salivary), apocrine (mammary), holocrine (sebaceous).
- Junctions: tight (leak-proof), desmosome (strength), gap (communication).
- Collagen — the most abundant protein; reticular fibres — type III collagen.
- Mast cell — histamine + heparin; plasma cell — antibodies.
- Areolar — the commonest connective tissue; brown fat — newborn, thermogenesis.
- Tendon: muscle → bone; ligament: bone → bone.
- Hyaline — the commonest cartilage (nose, trachea, articular surfaces); elastic — pinna, epiglottis; fibrous — intervertebral disc.
- Bone: hydroxyapatite + ossein; Haversian canal, Volkmann's canal, lacunae, canaliculi.
- Osteoblasts build, osteoclasts dissolve (PTH ↑), osteocytes mature.
- Skeletal muscle — multinucleate, peripheral nuclei, voluntary; cardiac — branched, intercalated discs; smooth — spindle, one nucleus.
- Neuron — Nissl granules (RER), axon hillock; does not divide.
- Myelin: CNS — oligodendrocyte; PNS — Schwann; node of Ranvier — saltatory conduction.
- Bipolar — retina, olfactory; pseudounipolar — dorsal root ganglion; multipolar — motor.
- Astrocyte — BBB; microglia — phagocytic; ependyma — CSF.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Who coined the term "tissue"? — **Bichat**.
2. What type of epithelium is in the PCT of the kidney? — **Simple cuboidal** (with brush border).
3. Epithelium of the trachea? — **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar** (with goblet cells).
4. Why does the epithelium of the urinary bladder flatten on stretching? — **Transitional epithelium** — an adaptation for expansion.
5. Mode of secretion of the sebaceous gland? — **Holocrine**.
6. A unicellular exocrine gland? — **Goblet cell**.
7. The junction that makes the heart muscles contract together? — **Gap junction** (in the intercalated discs).
8. The most abundant protein of the body? — **Collagen**.
9. Heparin comes from which cell? — **Mast cell** (and basophil).
10. The structure joining muscle to bone? — **Tendon**.
11. Cartilage of the intervertebral disc? — **White fibrocartilage**.
12. Cartilage of the pinna? — **Elastic cartilage**.
13. Structural unit of compact bone? — **Haversian system (osteon)**.
