This chapter takes the type studies of Arthropoda (cockroach, prawn), Mollusca (*Pila*) and Echinodermata (starfish) — morphology, anatomy, nutrition, respiration and reproduction — up to B.Sc. level. NCERT 11's "Structural Organisation in Animals" is the base for the cockroach. UP PGT 2026 asked about larva–metamorphosis in starfish, matching of crustacean larvae and growth (moulting) in incomplete metamorphosis.

> **In simple words:** Both cockroach and prawn have a hard shell (exoskeleton) over the body and jointed legs; to grow they shed the old shell. The cockroach breathes air through tubes (tracheae), the prawn breathes in water through gills. *Pila* (apple snail) has both a gill and a lung — so it can breathe both in water and on land. The starfish is not a fish — it is a five-armed marine animal that moves using water-filled pipes (the water vascular system).

## Part A — Cockroach (*Periplaneta americana*)

**Classification:** Phylum Arthropoda → Class Insecta → Order **Dictyoptera** (old: Orthoptera; modern: **Blattodea**) → Family Blattidae → *Periplaneta americana* (American cockroach). *Blatta orientalis* (Oriental), *Blattella germanica* (German, small). Nocturnal, omnivorous, warm-moist places.

### External morphology

- Length **34–53 mm**; colour shiny brown/black; body dorsoventrally flattened, in three parts — **head, thorax, abdomen**.
- **Exoskeleton** — hard chitinous plates, the **sclerites**: **tergites** dorsally, **sternites** ventrally, **pleurites** laterally; flexible **arthrodial membrane** in between.
- **Head** — triangular, at **right angles** to the body (**hypognathous** — mouthparts directed downward); formed by fusion of **6 segments**; joined to the thorax by a flexible **neck**; one pair of long **antennae** — sensory (touch, smell); one pair of **compound eyes**; two **fenestrae** (sites of simple eyes).
- **Thorax** — 3 segments: **prothorax**, **mesothorax**, **metathorax**; **one pair of walking legs** on each; segments of the leg — **coxa, trochanter, femur, tibia, tarsus** (5 tarsomeres, ending in claws and an **arolium/pulvillus** pad).
- **Wings** — **forewings (tegmina / elytra)** — from the **mesothorax**, dark, hard, leathery, **not used in flight — protect the hind wings**; **hind wings** — from the **metathorax**, thin, membranous, **used in flight**. (Wings vestigial in the female of *Blatta orientalis*.)
- **Abdomen** — **10 segments**; **one pair of anal cerci** — on the **10th segment**, in **both sexes**; **anal styles** — on the **9th sternum**, **only in males**. In the female the **7th sternum is boat-shaped** and together with the 8th and 9th sterna forms the **genital pouch**.

### Mouthparts — biting and chewing (orthopterous)

| Mouthpart | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Labrum (upper lip)** | Single, unpaired plate | Holds food from the front; taste |
| **Mandibles (1 pair)** | Hard, toothed, chitinous | **Cutting and chewing** food |
| **Maxillae (1 pair, first maxillae)** | Cardo, stipes, galea, lacinia, **maxillary palp (5-segmented)** | Holding food, touch/taste |
| **Labium (lower lip)** | Formed by **fusion of the second pair of maxillae**; submentum, mentum, prementum, **labial palp (3-segmented)** | Prevents food from falling back |
| **Hypopharynx (tongue)** | In the middle of the cavity enclosed by the mouthparts, unpaired | The **salivary duct** opens at its base |

### Alimentary canal (nutrition)

**Mouth → pre-oral cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → crop → gizzard (proventriculus) → midgut (mesenteron) → ileum → colon → rectum → anus**

| Part | Origin / lining | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Foregut (stomodaeum)** — pharynx, oesophagus, **crop**, **gizzard** | From **ectoderm**, **lined by cuticle** | Crop — **food storage**; gizzard — **6 chitinous teeth** — **grinding** food, sieve |
| **Midgut (mesenteron)** | Endoderm; **no cuticle**, but a **peritrophic membrane** | **Digestion and absorption**; at the foregut–midgut junction **6–8 blind hepatic / gastric caeca** — digestive juice |
| **Hindgut (proctodaeum)** — ileum, colon, rectum | **Ectoderm**, lined by cuticle | **Reabsorption of water** (**6 rectal papillae** in the rectum); **100–150 Malpighian tubules** at the midgut–hindgut junction |

- **Salivary glands** — one pair, near the crop; with a **salivary reservoir**; saliva contains **amylase**.
- The cockroach is **omnivorous** — dead organic matter, paper, cloth; **symbiotic bacteria** in the gut (bacteriocytes — *Blattabacterium*) — some vitamin/nitrogen metabolism.

### Circulation — open circulatory system

- Blood (**haemolymph**) **colourless** — no respiratory pigment (O₂ is carried by the tracheae); plasma + **haemocytes**.
- **Heart** — dorsal, a long tube, **13 chambers** (3 thoracic + 10 abdominal); **ostia** (valved openings) on both sides of each chamber; lies in the **pericardial sinus**.
- **Haemocoel** — three **sinuses**: **dorsal (pericardial)**, **middle (perivisceral)**, **ventral (perineural)** — separated by septa bearing **alary muscles** (triangular, 12 pairs).
- Flow: pericardial sinus → ostia → heart → forward through the **anterior aorta** → sinuses of the head → perivisceral → perineural → back to pericardial.

### Respiration — tracheal system

- **10 pairs of spiracles (stigmata)** — **2 pairs on the thorax** (mesothorax and metathorax), **8 pairs on the abdomen** (first 8 segments); valved (**sphincter**) — water loss controlled.
- Spiracles → **tracheae** — kept open by chitinous **spiral thickenings (taenidia)** → **tracheoles** — reach the cells; their tips contain **fluid** in which O₂ dissolves and goes **directly to the tissues**.
- **Blood plays no direct role in respiration.**
- Ventilation: by rhythmic contraction–relaxation of abdominal muscles.

### Excretion

- **Malpighian tubules** — **100–150**, thin, yellow, at the midgut–hindgut junction; take nitrogenous waste from the haemolymph and pass it into the hindgut as **uric acid**; water reabsorbed in the rectum — **uricotelic**.
- Accessory excretory structures: **fat body** — urate cells; **nephrocytes**; **cuticle** (during moulting); **uricose glands** (in males, peripheral tubules of the mushroom gland — store uric acid).

### Nervous system and sense organs

- **Brain = supra-oesophageal ganglion** — formed by fusion of **3 pairs of ganglia** (proto-, deuto-, tritocerebrum); joined to the **sub-oesophageal ganglion** by **circum-oesophageal connectives**.
- **Ventral nerve cord** — double, solid; **3 thoracic** + **6 abdominal** ganglia.
- The nervous system is **spread over the whole body** — **a cockroach can live for ~1 week even after its head is cut off** (the brain is only one part of the control).
- **Compound eyes** — on the dorso-lateral part of the head; each with **~2000 hexagonal ommatidia**; **mosaic vision** — more sensitivity but less resolution; **apposition image** in the cockroach (in daylight).
- Other sense organs — antennae, maxillary palps, labial palps, **anal cerci** (sound/air vibrations), hairs on the legs.

### Reproduction

| Male | Female |
|---|---|
| **Testes** — one pair, lateral, in **abdominal segments 4–6** | **Ovaries** — one pair, in **abdominal segments 2–6**; each with **8 ovarioles** — chains of developing eggs |
| **Vasa deferentia** → **ejaculatory duct**; at the junction the **seminal vesicles** and **mushroom gland** — in **segments 6–7** (accessory gland) | **Oviducts** → **common oviduct (vagina)** → **genital chamber** |
| **Phallic gland (conglobate gland)** | **Spermathecae** — one pair, in the **6th segment**, open into the genital chamber |
| **External genitalia** — **phallomeres (gonapophyses)** — chitinous, asymmetrical | **Collaterial glands** — one pair, branched — secrete the material of the **ootheca**; **gonapophyses** (3 pairs) |
| Sperm bundled in a **spermatophore** | — |

- Fertilisation **internal**; the fertilised eggs are enclosed in an **ootheca** — a dark reddish-brown capsule of ~8 mm; each with **14–16 eggs** (in two rows) (NCERT); a female produces on average **9–10 oothecae**; the ootheca hangs from the female's genital pouch for a few days and is then dropped in a safe crevice.
- **Development — paurometabolous / incomplete metamorphosis** — egg → **nymph** (like the adult, but smaller and wingless) → **~13 moults** → adult; the last nymph has **wing pads**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "In simple (incomplete) metamorphosis, growth takes place by? (A) Cell division only (B) Pupa formation (C) Moulting (ecdysis) (D) Fertilisation" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C) Moulting**. In incomplete metamorphosis there is **no pupa** — egg → nymph → adult; size increases after every moult (hormone ecdysone). In complete metamorphosis (Holometabola — butterfly, fly, beetle, honeybee) egg → larva → **pupa** → adult.

| Type of metamorphosis | Stages | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Ametabola** (no metamorphosis) | Egg → young (adult-like) → adult | *Lepisma* (silverfish) |
| **Hemimetabola / incomplete** | Egg → **nymph** (terrestrial) / **naiad** (aquatic — dragonfly, mayfly) → adult | Cockroach, grasshopper, bed bug, termite |
| **Holometabola / complete** | Egg → larva → **pupa** → imago | Butterfly (caterpillar, chrysalis), mosquito (wriggler, tumbler), housefly (maggot), silkworm |

**Hormonal control:** prothoracicotropic hormone (brain) → **ecdysone** (prothoracic gland) — moulting; **juvenile hormone** (**corpora allata**) — maintains larval/nymphal characters; when JH falls, metamorphosis into the adult.

## Part B — Prawn (*Palaemon*)

**Classification:** Phylum Arthropoda → Subphylum/Class **Crustacea** → Subclass Malacostraca → Order **Decapoda** (10 legs) → Family Palaemonidae → *Palaemon malcolmsonii* — Indian freshwater prawn (modern name ***Macrobrachium malcolmsonii***); *Macrobrachium rosenbergii* — giant freshwater prawn (aquaculture). Marine prawns: *Penaeus* (*Penaeus monodon* — tiger prawn). A "prawn" is not a crab and not a fish.

### External morphology

- Length up to ~25 cm (large species); slightly compressed laterally; two parts — **cephalothorax** and **abdomen**.
- **Cephalothorax** — **13 segments** (5 head + 8 thorax) — covered above by a **carapace**; in front a toothed **rostrum** — ~**7 (sometimes 11–14)** teeth above and ~**4–6** below (differs with species); the lateral parts of the carapace, the **branchiostegites**, cover the gill chambers.
- **Abdomen** — **6 segments** + **telson** (not counted as a segment); each segment has a tergum, sternum, pleuron; the pleuron of the second segment overlaps both the first and third — **identifying feature of Palaemonidae**.
- **Stalked compound eyes** — movable.

### Appendages — 19 pairs in all

Typical **biramous** appendage: basal **protopodite** (coxa + basis) + inner **endopodite** + outer **exopodite**; many have an **epipodite**.

| Region | Appendage (pair) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Head (5)** | 1. **Antennule (first antenna)** — three-segmented peduncle + **3 flagella**; **statocyst** in the base | Sensory, **balance** |
| | 2. **Antenna (second antenna)** — long flagellum (endopodite) + flat **squama** (exopodite); **excretory (renal) pore** on the coxa | Touch, balance (squama) |
| | 3. **Mandible** — hard, toothed; **3-segmented palp** | Chewing |
| | 4. **Maxillule (first maxilla)** | Holding food |
| | 5. **Maxilla (second)** — a large plate formed by the exopodite + epipodite, the **scaphognathite (baler)** | Creates the **water current in the gill chamber** (respiration) |
| **Thorax (8)** | 6–8. **3 pairs of maxillipeds** — **epipodite** on the coxa (respiration); podobranch on the second, 2 arthrobranchs on the third | Holding food/bringing it to the mouth, respiration |
| | 9–13. **5 pairs of walking legs (pereiopods)** — **first two chelate (pincer-like)**; **the second chelate leg is very large, especially in the male**; the other three simple; these are **uniramous** — exopodite lost | Walking, catching food, defence |
| **Abdomen (6)** | 14–18. **5 pairs of pleopods (swimmerets)** — biramous, flat; **"appendix masculina" on the second pleopod** of the male; **appendix interna** on all | Swimming; **carrying eggs** in the female |
| | 19. **Uropods** — broad; with the telson form the **tail fan** | **Quick backward jump (escape reaction)**, rudder |

- All appendages follow one basic plan — an excellent example of **serial homology**.

### Nutrition and alimentary canal

- **Omnivorous scavenger** — algae, aquatic plants, small animals, decaying matter; mainly **nocturnal**.
- **Mouth → short oesophagus → stomach → midgut → hindgut → anus** (ventral, at the base of the telson).
- **Stomach** — two chambers: **cardiac stomach** — large, with chitinous ossicles and teeth inside — the **gastric mill** — grinds food; **pyloric stomach** — small, a **filter** of hairs/bristles — only fine particles pass on.
- **Hepatopancreas (digestive gland)** — a large, orange, bilobed gland; digestive enzymes (carbohydrate, protein, fat), absorption, storage of glycogen–fat, storage of Ca²⁺ (for moulting).
- Foregut and hindgut **lined by cuticle** (this is also shed during moulting).

### Respiration

- **Respiratory organs** (on each side):
  - **Inner lining of the branchiostegite** — thin, rich in blood vessels.
  - **Epipodites of the maxillipeds** — thin, vascular plates (textbooks differ on the number; many B.Sc. books write "3 pairs").
  - **8 gills** — **phyllobranchiate** — rows of leaf-like/plate-like lamellae on an axis. **Gill formula (per side): 1 podobranch** (on the coxa of the second maxilliped) + **2 arthrobranchs** (on the joint membrane of the third maxilliped) + **5 pleurobranchs** (on the body wall above the five walking legs) = **8**.
- **Water current** — by the rhythmic movement of the **scaphognathite** (maxilla) water enters the gill chamber from behind and leaves in front.
- Respiratory pigment of the blood **haemocyanin** — containing **copper (Cu)**, dissolved in plasma; **blue** when oxygenated.

### Circulation — open

- **Heart** — dorsal, in the cephalothorax, in the **pericardial sinus**; muscular, **3 pairs of ostia** (valved).
- **Arteries** from the heart — anterior: ophthalmic artery (median), 2 antennary, 2 hepatic; posterior: **dorsal abdominal artery**, **sternal artery** → ventral artery.
- The arteries open into tissue spaces (**sinuses**) → **ventral (sternal) sinus** → **afferent vessels** to the gills → oxygenated → **efferent** → **branchio-pericardial vessels** → pericardial sinus → ostia → heart. That is, the heart contains **oxygenated** blood (**arterial heart**).

### Excretion and osmoregulation

- **Antennary glands / green glands** — one pair, in the base of the antennae; parts: **end sac**, **labyrinth**, **bladder** → excretory pore (on the coxa of the antenna).
- A large **renal sac** connected with both bladders — dorsally in the cephalothorax.
- Waste mainly **ammonia** (**ammonotelic**) — much of it also diffuses out through the gills.
- In fresh water, removing excess water by forming **dilute urine** and active uptake of salts by the gills — **osmoregulation**.

### Nervous system and sense organs

- **Brain (supra-oesophageal ganglion)** → **circum-oesophageal connectives** → **thoracic ganglionic mass** — formed by fusion of 11 pairs of ganglia (head and thorax) → **6 abdominal ganglia** on the **ventral nerve cord**.
- **Giant fibres** — rapid escape reaction.
- **Compound eyes** — on stalks; **apposition (mosaic) image in daylight**, **superposition image at night/in dim light** — by migration of pigment cells.
- **Statocyst** — in the base of the antennule; sand grains (**statoliths**) — **balance**; replaced at moulting.
- **X-organ–sinus gland complex in the eyestalk** — **moult-inhibiting hormone (MIH)** — its removal causes early moulting (**eyestalk ablation**, used in aquaculture to speed up breeding); **Y-organ** — ecdysone.

### Reproduction

- **Dioecious**, **sexual dimorphism** — male larger, **second chelate leg larger and longer**; **appendix masculina** on the second pleopod of the male; female abdomen broader (brood chamber).
- **Male** — **testes** one pair (dorsal, in the cephalothorax, joined at the base) → **vasa deferentia** → **male genital pore — on the coxa of the 5th walking leg** (fifth leg = eighth thoracic appendage); sperm **non-motile**, in **spermatophores**.
- **Female** — **ovaries** one pair → **oviducts** → **female genital pore — on the coxa of the 3rd walking leg** (sixth thoracic appendage).
- Fertilisation **external** (on the female's body, as the eggs are laid). The eggs remain stuck to the hairs of the pleopods — "**berried female**".
- **Development** — in *Macrobrachium* a **zoaea larva** hatches from the egg (the nauplius stage is passed inside the egg), which needs **brackish water** for development — so in the monsoon females migrate towards river estuaries; after several zoaeal stages a **post-larva** → young prawn. (Some older textbooks describe development in *Palaemon* as nearly "direct" — in such a question "zoaea/indirect" is safer.)

### Crustacean larvae

| Larva | Characters | Found in |
|---|---|---|
| **Nauplius** | **Most primitive**; unsegmented, **3 pairs of appendages** (antennule, antenna, mandible), **one median eye (naupliar eye)** | *Penaeus*, *Cyclops*, *Balanus*, *Sacculina* |
| Metanauplius | A little advanced over the nauplius, more appendage buds | *Penaeus* |
| Protozoaea | Short thorax, compound eyes begin to form | *Penaeus* |
| **Zoaea** | **Spiny carapace** (dorsal and rostral spines), **segmented abdomen**, planktonic life; thoracic appendages incomplete | Crab, *Macrobrachium*, lobster |
| **Mysis** | **Developed thoracic appendages (biramous)** — like the adult shrimp (like *Mysis*) | *Penaeus*, *Lucifer* |
| **Megalopa** | Large eyes, abdomen straight — the last larva of crabs | Crab (*Cancer*, *Carcinus*) |
| Phyllosoma | Leaf-like, transparent | Spiny lobster (*Palinurus*) |
| Cypris | Bivalved shell — attaches and becomes the adult | *Balanus*, *Sacculina* |
| Alima | — | *Squilla* |

- ***Sacculina*** — a **parasitic barnacle** (Cirripedia) on crabs; the adult is only a root-like network (**interna**) and a sac outside (**externa**); causes **parasitic castration** of the host — even female characters in the male crab.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Match List-I (a) Nauplius (b) Zoaea (c) Mysis (d) *Sacculina* with List-II (I) Parasitic barnacle that alters the physiology and reproduction of the host (II) Free-swimming larva with three pairs of appendages and a median eye (III) Larva with developed thoracic appendages like the adult shrimp (IV) Larva with a spiny carapace and segmented abdomen, adapted for planktonic life. Reasoned answer (official key not available): **a-II, b-IV, c-III, d-I** (option **(C)** in the original booklet).

**Sequence in the marine *Penaeus*:** egg → **nauplius → protozoaea → mysis → post-larva** → juvenile → adult (some texts also call the protozoaea a zoaea).

## Part C — *Pila globosa* (apple snail)

**Classification:** Phylum Mollusca → Class **Gastropoda** → Subclass **Prosobranchia** (gills in front of the heart; modern: Caenogastropoda) → Order Mesogastropoda → Family **Ampullariidae (Pilidae)** → *Pila globosa* — ponds, paddy fields and lakes of India and Myanmar. *Pila virens* — South India.

### Shell

- **Globular, apple-shaped, spiral**, **dextral** — when the shell is held with the apex up and the mouth (aperture) facing you, the aperture is on the **right**.
- Parts: **apex** — the oldest part; **whorls** — 6–7; the largest, last whorl is the **body whorl**; the upper whorls together form the **spire**; between whorls the **suture**; the central axis, the **columella** — the **columellar muscle** is attached to it; the **aperture** — closed by the **operculum** (attached to the dorso-posterior part of the foot, calcareous/horny, with concentric rings); **umbilicus** — a small depression at the base.
- Layers of the shell — outer **periostracum** (horny, conchiolin), middle **prismatic (CaCO₃ — calcite)**, inner **nacreous / mother-of-pearl layer (aragonite)** — secreted by the mantle.
- The shell is **anti-predator** and **anti-desiccation**; by closing the operculum it undergoes **aestivation** — buried in mud during drought for months (sometimes years).

### Body and torsion

- Body — **head**, **foot** (broad, muscular, for creeping; wave-like movement), **visceral mass** (coiled in the whorls of the shell) and **mantle**.
- **Head** — **snout** — two **labial palps (first tentacles)**; two long **tentacles (second, cephalic tentacles)**; **eyes** on short stalks, the **ommatophores**, near the bases of the tentacles.
- **Nuchal lobes** — outgrowths of the mantle edge on both sides of the head; the **left nuchal lobe** folds to form a **respiratory siphon** — for taking **air** from the surface (its length can be increased); the right one helps in water outflow.
- **Torsion** — during the larval period the visceral mass and mantle cavity undergo a **180° anticlockwise rotation** relative to the foot → the mantle cavity, anus, gills and renal opening come to lie **in front, above the head**; results: **twisted/crossed nerve connectives — "8" shape (streptoneury)**, loss/reduction of the gill, auricle and kidney of one side, body **asymmetrical**. **Torsion ≠ coiling** — coiling is the spiralling of the shell, a separate process.
- **Detorsion** — partial reversal in opisthobranchs (*Aplysia*) and pulmonates (euthyneury).

### Respiration — amphibious

- **Mantle cavity** — divided into two chambers by a long ridge, the **epitaenia**:
  - **Right — branchial chamber** — **one ctenidium** — **monopectinate** — only one row of triangular gill lamellae; respiration in water.
  - **Left — pulmonary sac (lung)** — inner wall of the mantle highly vascular; **air breathing** — comes to the surface and fills air through the **siphon of the left nuchal lobe**.
- **Osphradium** — on the left, near the entrance of the pulmonary chamber, **yellowish-green, feather-like** — **chemoreceptor — tests the quality of incoming water** (silt, O₂).
- If the water has enough O₂ it uses the gill; if O₂ is low or it is on land, the lung — "**amphibious respiration**".

### Nutrition and alimentary canal

- **Herbivorous** — aquatic plants (*Vallisneria*, *Pistia*, paddy), algae.
- **Mouth → buccal cavity / buccal mass** — two **jaws** and the **radula** — a chitinous ribbon bearing transverse rows of teeth; in *Pila* **taenioglossan** — **7 teeth per row** — formula **2 – 1 – 1 – 1 – 2** (two marginals, one lateral, one median/rachidian, one lateral, two marginals); on the **odontophore** cartilage, it **rasps** food by moving back and forth; new rows are continuously formed behind in the **radular sac**.
- **Salivary glands** (one pair) → **oesophagus** → **crop** → **stomach** → **digestive gland (hepatopancreas)** — large, brownish-green, in the whorls of the visceral mass; secretes enzymes, also **intracellular digestion**, absorption, storage → **intestine** (long, coiled) → **rectum** → **anus** — on the right side in the mantle cavity (anterior because of torsion).
- The digestive gland also has **cellulase** (plant food).

### Circulation — open

- **Heart** — in the **pericardial cavity** (this is a remnant of the true coelom), **one auricle** + **one ventricle** — the second auricle lost due to torsion; auricle **in front**, ventricle behind (**prosobranch** character — gills in front of the heart).
- From the ventricle the **aorta** → **anterior (cephalic) and posterior (visceral) aortae** → tissue spaces (**haemocoel**).
- Blood contains **haemocyanin** (Cu, blue) — in plasma; amoebocytes.
- **Anterior (pallial) venous sinus** → kidney → gill/lung → auricle.

### Excretion

- **Kidney (renal organ)** — **one** (one lost due to torsion), near the pericardium; **two chambers — anterior (dorsal) and posterior (ventral) renal chambers**; connected to the pericardium by the **reno-pericardial aperture**, the **renal aperture** opens into the mantle cavity.
- In water **ammonia**; during **aestivation**, instead of toxic ammonia it forms and stores **uric acid** — **shift towards uricotely** (water conservation).

### Nervous system and sense organs

- **Ganglia** — **cerebral** (pair, above the buccal mass, joined by the cerebral commissure), **pleuro-pedal ganglionic mass** (fusion of pleural + pedal), **buccal**, **supra-intestinal**, **sub-intestinal**, **visceral**.
- The **visceral loop** is **"8"-shaped** because of torsion — **streptoneury**; the supra-intestinal ganglion on the left, the sub-intestinal on the right (crossing over).
- **Sense organs** — tentacles (touch), **eyes** (on ommatophores), **statocysts** (in the foot near the pedal ganglia — balance; statoconia), **osphradium** (chemical).

### Reproduction

- **Dioecious**; slight sexual dimorphism — the male's shell slightly smaller and the body whorl more swollen; the male has a **penis sheath** and **penis** (copulatory organ) on the mantle edge.
- **Male** — **testis** (in the upper whorls of the visceral mass, adjoining the digestive gland) → vas deferens → **seminal vesicle** → **penis**.
- **Female** — **ovary** → oviduct → **albumen gland / uterus** → **vagina** (in the mantle cavity); also a **receptaculum seminis**.
- **Internal fertilisation** (copulation lasts several hours).
- Eggs laid **out of water** — **clusters of pink/white, calcareous-shelled eggs** (200–800) on moist soil at the edge, leaves or stones; **development direct** — the **veliger stage is passed inside the egg**, a small snail hatches from the egg.

## Part D — Starfish (*Asterias*)

**Classification:** Phylum Echinodermata → Subphylum Eleutherozoa → Class **Asteroidea** → Order Forcipulata → *Asterias rubens* / *A. forbesi*. Indian coast: *Pentaceros* (*Oreaster*), *Astropecten*, *Linckia*. Marine, bottom-dwelling, **carnivorous** (enemy of oysters).

### External morphology

- **Pentamerous radial symmetry** — a central **disc** + **5 arms (rays)** (more in some species — up to 40 in *Heliaster*).
- **Oral surface** — below; the **mouth** in the centre (surrounded by the **peristome** membrane, **oral spines**); in each arm an **ambulacral groove** from the mouth to the tip — with **tube feet (podia)** in **2 or 4 rows** (**4 rows** in *Asterias*, with **suckers** at the tips); movable **ambulacral spines** along the edges of the groove. At the tip of each arm a **terminal tentacle** with a red **eye-spot (optic cushion)** at its base.
- **Aboral surface** — above; **spines** formed of calcareous **ossicles**; between two arms (**interradial**) a sieve-like plate, the **madreporite**; a small **anus** near the centre.
- **Pedicellariae** — tiny pincer-like structures — **straight (forcipulate)** and **crossed**, two/three valves; **cleaning** (so that silt and larvae do not settle), defence, sometimes catching small food.
- **Dermal branchiae / papulae** — **thin-walled outgrowths** on the aboral surface — **extensions of the coelom**; **respiration and excretion**.

- **Madreporite** (perforated plate, on the aboral surface) → **stone canal** — stiffened by calcareous rings (S-shaped) → **ring canal** — around the mouth; on it **9 Tiedemann's bodies** (interradial, in pairs; the 5th position is taken by the stone canal — hence 9) — produce amoebocytes; (**Polian vesicles absent** in *Asterias* — present in *Astropecten* etc.) → **5 radial canals** → alternate **lateral canals** on both sides — valved → **ampulla** (a muscular sac inside) + **tube foot (podium)**.
- Fluid — like sea water, but containing some protein, K⁺ and **coelomocytes**.
- **Functions:** **locomotion** (slow, ~15 cm/minute), attachment to surfaces, **catching food** (pulling apart the valves of oysters), helps in respiration and excretion, sensory.
- The system develops from the **enterocoelic coelom (hydrocoel)**.

### Nutrition and alimentary canal

- Food — **bivalves (oysters, mussels), snails**, small crustaceans, dead matter.
- Alimentary canal short, straight (mouth below, anus above): **mouth → short oesophagus → cardiac stomach** (large, folded, **can be everted**) → **pyloric stomach** (small, pentagonal) → **10 pyloric caeca / hepatic caeca** — **one pair in each arm**; digestive enzymes, absorption, storage → **short intestine** → **2–3 rectal caeca** → **anus**.
- **External (extra-oral) digestion** — the starfish wraps its arms around an oyster and **pulls continuously** on the valves with its tube feet; the oyster's adductor muscle tires, and as soon as a gap of ~0.1–0.2 mm appears the starfish **everts its cardiac stomach** through that crack into the oyster → the oyster's flesh is **partly digested** there → the semi-fluid food and the stomach are drawn back in → complete digestion–absorption in the pyloric caeca.
- **Economic importance** — **destructive to oyster beds**; earlier fishermen used to cut them up and throw them back — because of regeneration their numbers increased further.

### Respiration, circulation, excretion

- **Respiration** — **papulae (dermal gills)** mainly; **tube feet** too; the coelomic fluid is circulated by cilia.
- **Circulation** — **blood-lacunar system / haemal system** — highly reduced (oral and aboral haemal rings, radial haemal canals, **axial gland / axial organ** — not a "heart"); surrounded by the **perihaemal system**; the actual distribution is done by the **coelomic fluid**.
- **Excretion** — **no specialised excretory organs**; **ammonia** (ammonotelic) diffuses out through the papulae and tube feet; **coelomocytes (amoebocytes)** collect wastes and leave through the papulae.
- Echinoderms are **osmoconformers** — no osmoregulation, hence **exclusively marine** (no echinoderm in fresh water).

### Nervous system and sense organs

- **No brain**; three interconnected systems:
  - **Ectoneural (oral)** — main, sensory; **nerve ring** (around the mouth) + **5 radial nerves** (in the ambulacral grooves, V-shaped).
  - **Hyponeural** — motor, deep.
  - **Entoneural (aboral)** — on the aboral surface, motor.
- **Sense organs** — **eye-spot (optic cushion)** at the arm tips — **80–200 ocelli**, light-sensitive; **terminal tentacle** — touch/chemical; the whole skin has sensory cells.

### Reproduction and development

- **Dioecious**, male and female alike externally.
- **10 gonads** — **one pair in each arm** (at the base of the arm); each gonad opens by a **genital pore** in the interradial region of the aboral surface.
- **External fertilisation** (in sea water); one female releases **millions of eggs** in a season.
- Cleavage **holoblastic, radial, indeterminate** (deuterostome) → **coeloblastula** → **gastrula** by invagination → **enterocoelous coelom**.
- Larva: the **bilateral** **bipinnaria** — ciliated bands, free-swimming, planktonic feeder → **brachiolaria** — three anterior **brachiolar arms** and an **adhesive disc/sucker** — **attaches to the substratum** → **metamorphosis** — much of the larval body is absorbed, a new aboral surface forms **from the left side**; extensive **reorganisation** of internal structures → **pentamerous radial** juvenile starfish.

### Regeneration and autotomy

- **Autotomy** — breaking off an arm itself when caught by an enemy; a new arm grows.
- One arm + part of the disc → a whole starfish. In ***Linckia*** a whole animal forms even from **a single arm (without the disc)** — the "**comet form**".
- Some species also reproduce asexually by **fission (fissiparity)**.

## Traps and exceptions

- The cockroach's **forewings (tegmina) arise from the mesothorax** and are **not used in flight**; the hind wings arise from the metathorax — used in flight.
- **Anal cerci in both sexes**, **anal styles only in the male**.
- The cockroach's blood is **colourless** — does not carry O₂; the blood of prawn and *Pila* has **haemocyanin** (blue).
- The cockroach heart has **13 chambers**; the prawn heart is one muscular sac (3 pairs of ostia).
- Cockroach **uricotelic** (Malpighian tubules), prawn **ammonotelic** (green gland).
- Prawn has **19 pairs** of appendages (the telson has no appendage); cockroach has **3 pairs** of legs.
- In the prawn the **male pore is on the 5th** and the **female pore on the 3rd** walking leg — the order is asked reversed.
- The prawn heart pumps **oxygenated** blood (coming from the gills); the fish heart pumps **deoxygenated** blood ("venous heart").
- *Pila* is **amphibious** but not an amphibian (Amphibia); its "lung" is part of the mantle cavity.
- **Torsion** (180° rotation) and **coiling** (spiral shell) are different processes.
- *Pila* is **dioecious**; *Helix*/*Limax* (pulmonates) are **hermaphrodite**.
- In *Pila* the **veliger is inside the egg** — direct development.
- The starfish is **not a fish**; its madreporite is on the **aboral surface** and in an **interradial** position.
- *Asterias* has **no Polian vesicles**; Tiedemann's bodies **9**.
- Echinoderm **larva bilateral**, adult radial — "secondary radial symmetry".

## Quick revision

- Cockroach: head 6 segments, thorax 3, abdomen 10; hypognathous.
- Mouthparts biting-chewing; labium = fused second maxillae; hypopharynx = tongue.
- 6 teeth in the gizzard; hepatic caeca 6–8; Malpighian tubules 100–150.
- Heart 13 chambers; 3 sinuses of the haemocoel; blood colourless.
- Spiracles 10 pairs (2 thoracic + 8 abdominal).
- Brain = supra-oesophageal ganglion; 3 thoracic + 6 abdominal ganglia.
- Compound eye ~2000 ommatidia; mosaic vision.
- Testes segments 4–6, mushroom gland 6–7, ovaries 2–6 (8 ovarioles), spermatheca 6th segment.
- Ootheca 14–16 eggs; nymph 13 moults; paurometabolous.
- Prawn: cephalothorax 13 + abdomen 6 segments; 19 pairs of appendages.
- Scaphognathite (maxilla) — water current; statocyst — in the antennule.
- Gill formula per side: 1 podo + 2 arthro + 5 pleuro = 8; phyllobranchiate.
- Green gland — in the base of the antenna; haemocyanin.
- X-organ–sinus gland — MIH; eyestalk ablation.
- *Pila*: dextral shell, operculum, monopectinate ctenidium + lung, left nuchal lobe = siphon.
- Radula taenioglossan — 2-1-1-1-2.
- Torsion 180° → streptoneury; one auricle, one kidney.
- Starfish: madreporite → stone canal → ring canal → radial → lateral → ampulla–tube foot.
- Papulae — respiration + excretion; pedicellariae — cleaning.
- External digestion by everting the cardiac stomach; 2 pyloric caeca and 2 gonads in each arm.
- Bipinnaria → brachiolaria (attaches) → metamorphosis.
