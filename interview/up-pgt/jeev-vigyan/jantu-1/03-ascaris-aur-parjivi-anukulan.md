This part of the syllabus is two lines — "*Ascaris* (type study)" and "parasitic adaptations in helminthes". NCERT 11–12 introduces roundworm, tapeworm, filaria and them as "human diseases"; at B.Sc. the anatomy of *Ascaris* and the life cycles of *Fasciola*/*Taenia* are covered in detail. UP PGT 2026 asked an assertion–reason question on tapeworm and a case-based question on filaria.

> **In simple words:** Helminths = parasitic worms — flat (flukes, tapeworms) and round (roundworm, hookworm, filaria). To live in a harsh place like the gut they have "lost" a lot (locomotory organs, sense organs, sometimes the whole alimentary canal) and "gained" a lot (hooks, suckers, a thick covering, anaerobic respiration, millions of eggs). These are the "parasitic adaptations".

## Part A — *Ascaris lumbricoides* (human roundworm)

**Classification:** Phylum **Nematoda (Aschelminthes)** → Class **Phasmidia (Secernentea)** → Order Ascaridida → Family Ascarididae → *Ascaris lumbricoides* (Linnaeus, 1758). *A. suum* of the pig is almost identical in morphology.

- **Habitat:** **small intestine (mainly jejunum)** of man — **endoparasite**; about 800 million–1 billion people in the world are infected (the commonest helminth), children more often.
- **Monogenetic** — the life cycle is completed in **a single host**; **no intermediate host**.

### Morphology — sexual dimorphism

| Character | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **Smaller** — 15–30 cm | **Larger** — 20–40 cm (sometimes 49 cm) |
| Thickness | Slender (2–4 mm) | Stout (3–6 mm) |
| Posterior end | **Curved** — hook-like towards the ventral side | **Straight**, conical |
| Special structure | **Two penial setae / spicules** — project from the cloaca, used in copulation to open the female genital pore; pre-anal and post-anal **papillae** | **Genital pore (vulva)** — mid-ventral, at the end of the **anterior one-third** of the body |
| Posterior opening | **Cloacal aperture** — both alimentary and genital | **Anus** — alimentary canal only |

- Body long, cylindrical, pointed at both ends; colour light pink/cream; **unsegmented**.
- **Four longitudinal lines** — **two lateral** (broad; the excretory canals run in them), **one dorsal** and **one ventral** (thin; the nerve cords run in them).
- At the anterior end the **mouth is surrounded by three lips** — **one mid-dorsal** (with **2 double papillae**) and **two ventro-lateral** (each with one double papilla and one **amphid**).
- **Excretory pore** — mid-ventral, about **2 mm behind** the anterior end.

### Body wall

From outside to inside —
1. **Cuticle** — thick, multilayered, **collagen-like protein** (not chitin), **impermeable, resistant to digestive enzymes**; **moulted 4 times** in life.
2. **Epidermis/hypodermis** — **syncytial** — no cell boundaries, nuclei scattered; thickened and projecting inward in the four longitudinal lines.
3. **Muscle layer** — **longitudinal muscles only** (**no** circular) — so the body bends/undulates in an **"S" only in the dorso-ventral direction** (not the snake-like lateral wave). The muscle cells are peculiar — each has a contractile fibrillar part (outside) and a **cytoplasmic balloon-like part** (inside, with glycogen), from which a **muscle arm goes to the nerve cord** (the reverse of other animals, where the nerve comes to the muscle). The muscle arrangement is **polymyarian** (many cells in each quadrant) and **coelomyarian** (fibres in three sides of the cell) — *Ascaris*.
4. **Pseudocoel** — fluid-filled cavity between the body wall and the gut; acts as a **hydrostatic skeleton** — the high internal pressure (up to ~70 mm Hg) keeps the body turgid. A few giant **pseudocoelomic cells (coelomocytes / fixed cells)** in the pseudocoel.

### Alimentary canal and nutrition

**Mouth → buccal cavity (short) → pharynx — muscular, sucking, triradiate lumen → intestine — long, flattened, a single layer of columnar cells bearing microvilli; no muscle around it → rectum → anus (female) / cloaca (male).**

- **Complete alimentary canal** (separate mouth and anus) — first appears in evolution in Nematoda.
- Food: the host's **semi-digested food (chyme)** (and sometimes intestinal mucosa, blood) — by **sucking** action of the pharynx.
- Digestion mainly **extracellular** (in the lumen of the intestine), absorption by intestinal cells; food stored as **glycogen** (up to ~20–25% of dry weight).
- Protection from the host's digestive enzymes: thick cuticle + **anti-enzymes** such as **trypsin/pepsin inhibitors**.

### Respiration — mainly anaerobic

- O₂ negligible in the gut; **no respiratory organs**.
- Energy from **anaerobic glycolysis**; the end products are **not lactate** but **CO₂ and volatile fatty acids** (2-methyl butyric, 2-methyl valeric, succinic) — "**fermentation respiration**"; the **fumarate reductase** pathway (fumarate → succinate).
- What little O₂ is available is held by the **haemoglobin** of the body fluid (extremely high affinity for O₂ — thousands of times that of human Hb; it hardly releases O₂ at all, so its role is disputed).
- Eggs and larvae **need O₂** for development — hence the eggs embryonate in the soil.

### Excretion — H-shaped system

- **No flame cells** (unlike Platyhelminthes).
- **Two lateral excretory canals** — in both lateral lines, along the whole length; in the anterior part they are joined by a **transverse canal** forming an "**H**"; open through a short **terminal canal** at the **excretory pore**.
- The whole system is believed to be formed by a **single giant cell (renette cell / H-cell)** — **nucleus near the transverse canal**.
- Wastes: **ammonia** (main, ammonotelic) and **urea**; also diffusion through the body wall.
- The **phagocytic cells** (4) of the pseudocoel also collect wastes.

### Nervous system and sense organs

- A **circum-pharyngeal nerve ring** around the pharynx, with attached **ganglia** — lateral, dorsal, ventral.
- From the ring, **6 anterior nerves** (to lips and papillae) and **6 posterior nerve cords** — **one thick ventral** (main, **ganglionated**, motor + sensory), **one dorsal** (motor), two lateral pairs.
- **Amphids** — at the anterior end (on the ventro-lateral lips), **chemoreceptors** (olfactory).
- **Phasmids** — at the **posterior end**, a pair of glandulo-sensory pits near the tail — chemoreceptors; the **class Phasmidia** is named after them.
- **Papillae** — tactile (on the lips, on the male's tail — help in copulation).

### Reproductive system

| Male (monorchic) | Female (didelphic) |
|---|---|
| **One** long, thread-like coiled **testis** (anterior part) → **vas deferens** → broad **seminal vesicle** → short muscular **ejaculatory duct** → **cloaca** | **Two** thread-like **ovaries** → **oviducts** → broad **uteri** (two) → join into a short muscular **vagina** → **vulva** — at the anterior 1/3 of the body |
| **Sperm amoeboid (no flagellum)** — a special feature of Nematoda; move by pseudopodia | Upper part of the oviduct acts as the **seminal receptacle** |
| Spicule sacs in the dorsal part of the cloaca | One female lays **~2,00,000 eggs per day** (up to ~26 million in a lifetime) |

- Fertilisation **internal** (in the oviduct/upper part of the uterus of the female).
- **Fertilised egg** — oval, ~60 × 45 μm; outside, an **uneven, bumpy protein coat secreted by the uterus — the "mammillated coat"** (stained **yellow-brown by bile** in faeces); inside it a thick **chitinous shell** and innermost a **lipid layer / vitelline membrane** (impermeable; ascarosides). The egg is **extremely resistant** — survives chemicals (even formalin) and drought for years.
- Unfertilised eggs are elongated, with a thin coat.

1. **Embryonation in soil** — eggs passed in faeces (still undeveloped) form the **first rhabditiform larva (L1)** in **2–3 weeks** in moist, shady, oxygenated soil (~25–30 °C); the **first moult** occurs inside the egg itself → **second-stage larva (L2)** — **this is the infective stage** (embryonated egg). (Older texts say "second rhabditiform larva"; newer studies consider the infective larva to be L3 — texts differ.)
2. **Infection** — embryonated eggs are swallowed with **contaminated food, water, raw vegetables, dirty hands** (**faeco-oral route**).
3. **Hatching** — in the small intestine (by intestinal juice, CO₂ and suitable pH) the egg shell dissolves and the larva comes out.
4. **Migration within the body** — the larva bores through the gut wall → **hepatic portal vein** → **liver** → **posterior vena cava** → **right heart** → **pulmonary artery** → **lungs**. In the alveoli of the lungs **~10 days**, **second and third moults**.
5. Then **trachea → pharynx (throat)** → **swallowed again** into the intestine → **fourth (final) moult** → **adult**. From infection to egg-laying ~**2–3 months**; lifespan of the adult ~**1–2 years**.

> **Exam angle:** Questions often ask the "order of the route" — **intestine → liver → heart → lungs → trachea → pharynx → intestine**; and that the larval **migration to the lungs** is believed to be linked to the need for O₂. **4 moults in all** (1 in soil/egg, 2 in lungs, 1 in intestine).

### Ascariasis — disease, diagnosis, treatment

- **Lung phase** — **Löffler's pneumonitis / syndrome** (cough, fever, eosinophilia), from large numbers of larvae.
- **Intestinal phase** — abdominal pain, indigestion, **malnutrition** (especially stunted growth and vitamin A deficiency in children), in heavy infection a bolus of worms → **intestinal obstruction**; wandering worms (**erratic migration**) — bile duct, pancreatic duct, appendix, sometimes out through nose/mouth.
- Diagnosis — **mammillated eggs** in stool.
- Treatment — **albendazole**, **mebendazole**, pyrantel pamoate, **piperazine** (old, paralyses the worm), ivermectin. In India, **National Deworming Day (10 February, 10 August)** — albendazole to children aged 1–19 years.
- Prevention — use of toilets (no open defecation), hand-washing, washing vegetables, clean water; not using raw human faeces as manure.

### *Ascaris*, *Fasciola* and earthworm — comparison

| Character | *Fasciola* (fluke) | *Ascaris* (roundworm) | *Pheretima* (earthworm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phylum | Platyhelminthes | Nematoda | Annelida |
| Shape | Leaf-like, dorsoventrally flattened | Cylindrical, unsegmented | Cylindrical, **segmented** (~100–120 segments) |
| Coelom | **Acoelomate** (parenchyma) | **Pseudocoelomate** | **True (schizocoelous)** |
| Covering | Tegument (spiny) | Thick cuticle (collagen-like) | Thin cuticle; setae |
| Muscles | Circular, longitudinal, oblique | **Longitudinal only** | Circular + longitudinal |
| Alimentary canal | **Incomplete** (no anus), bifurcated intestine | **Complete** | Complete; gizzard, **typhlosole** |
| Circulation | Absent | Absent (pseudocoelomic fluid) | **Closed**, haemoglobin in plasma; 4 pairs of "hearts" |
| Respiration | Anaerobic | Anaerobic | Skin (aerobic) |
| Excretion | **Flame cells** | **H-shaped canals (renette)** | **Nephridia** |
| Nervous | Ganglionic ring + longitudinal nerve cords | Pharyngeal nerve ring + dorsal/ventral cords | Suprapharyngeal ganglia + **ventral ganglionated nerve cord** |
| Sex | **Hermaphrodite** | **Dioecious**, dimorphism | **Hermaphrodite** (protandrous), cross-fertilisation, **cocoon** |
| Life cycle | Digenetic (snail), many larvae | Monogenetic, larval migration | Direct development |

### General characters of Aschelminthes (Nematoda) — summary

- Triploblastic, bilateral, **pseudocoelomate**, unsegmented, cylindrical ("roundworms").
- **Cuticle** and moulting (**Ecdysozoa** — molecular grouping with Arthropoda).
- **Complete absence of cilia and flagella** (sperm amoeboid) — unique in the animal kingdom.
- **Constant cell number (eutely)** — *Caenorhabditis elegans* (free-living soil nematode; **959 somatic cells** in the adult hermaphrodite) — model for developmental biology and studies of **RNAi** and **programmed cell death (apoptosis)** (Nobel 2002, 2006).
- Many free-living (soil, water) and plant-parasitic — **root-knot nematode *Meloidogyne incognita*** (resistance in tobacco by RNAi — NCERT 12).

### Life cycle of *Fasciola hepatica* (in brief)

**Adult (bile duct of sheep; hermaphrodite, self/cross-fertilisation) → eggs into water with faeces → miracidium (ciliated, swims, seeks the snail *Lymnaea*) → sporocyst inside the snail → redia (two generations — daughter rediae) → cercaria (tailed, leaves the snail) → metacercaria encysted on grass (infective) → sheep eats the grass → cyst bursts in the intestine → enters the liver → adult in the bile duct.**

- **Hundreds of cercariae** from one miracidium — **polyembryony/larval multiplication** in the larval stages — a parasitic adaptation.
- Excretion in *Fasciola* by **flame cells (protonephridia)**.

### *Taenia solium* — main points of structure

- **Scolex** — **4 suckers + ~22–32 hooks in two rings on the rostellum** (*armed* — hence "armed tapeworm").
- **Neck** — **budding zone**, new proglottids form here (the oldest are at the rear).
- **Strobila** — 800–1000 **proglottids** (2–3 m, sometimes more): immature → mature (**hermaphrodite**, male and female organs; **self-fertilisation** possible — within the same proglottid or between different proglottids) → **gravid** — only a branched uterus full of eggs (30,000–50,000), passed out with faeces.
- **No mouth, no alimentary canal**; the whole body absorbs food through the **tegument** — **microtriches** (microvilli-like) on the tegument increase the surface area.
- Excretion — flame cells + lateral excretory canals; nervous — ganglionic ring in the scolex, lateral nerve cords.
- Inside the egg the **hexacanth embryo (onchosphere)** → hatches in the pig's intestine and reaches the muscles via blood → **Cysticercus cellulosae** (bladderworm, with an inverted scolex) → man eats undercooked meat → scolex turns out (evagination) → attaches to the intestine, becomes adult.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): Tapeworms have no mouth or digestive cavity. Reason (R): They absorb nutrients from the host directly through their body surface. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **Both are true and R is the correct explanation of A** (option **(B)** in the original question booklet). The host's already digested food can be absorbed through the tegument, so the alimentary canal has been completely lost — this is **parasitic degeneration**.

## Part C — Parasitic adaptations in helminths

### 1. Morphological adaptations

| Adaptation | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Organs of attachment** — hooks, suckers, bothria, teeth of the buccal capsule | *Taenia* (4 suckers + hooks), *Fasciola* (oral and ventral suckers), *Diphyllobothrium* (bothria), *Ancylostoma* (teeth/cutting plates) |
| **Protective covering** — thick, enzyme-resistant **cuticle** (nematodes) or a living **tegument** (flukes, tapeworms) that is continuously renewed | *Ascaris*, *Taenia* |
| **Loss of locomotory organs** — cilia (present in free-living Turbellaria) absent in adult parasites | *Fasciola*, *Taenia* |
| **Degeneration of sense organs** — no eyes (the miracidium larva has eye-spots) | All intestinal parasites |
| **Simplification or loss of the alimentary canal** | *Fasciola* — bifurcated blind intestine, no anus; *Taenia* — **alimentary canal completely absent** |
| **Increase in body surface** — flat, long body; microtriches | Tapeworms |
| **Proglottids** — detachment of egg-filled segments | *Taenia* |

### 2. Physiological adaptations

- **Anaerobic respiration** — glycolysis, fumarate reductase; fatty acids and CO₂ as end products; huge glycogen stores.
- **Anti-enzymes** — protection from the host's trypsin, pepsin.
- **High osmotic tolerance** — survive the changing osmotic pressure of the host's gut.
- **pH tolerance** — acidic/alkaline environment.
- **Anticoagulants** — *Ancylostoma* (hookworm anticoagulant) — keeps the blood flowing.
- **Chemical/thermal sensitivity of larvae to find the site of infection** — cercariae and filariform larvae are attracted towards the skin.
- **Periodicity** — microfilariae appear in peripheral blood at night — synchronised with the biting time of the mosquito.

### 3. Reproductive adaptations

- **Hermaphroditism** and **self-fertilisation** — reproduction possible even if there is only one worm (flukes, tapeworms).
- **Enormous fecundity** — *Ascaris* ~2 lakh eggs per day; *Taenia* ~40,000 per proglottid; out of millions, only a few reach a new host.
- **Polyembryony / larval multiplication** — sporocyst–redia in *Fasciola*; thousands of **protoscolices** (brood capsules) in the hydatid cyst of *Echinococcus*.
- **Complex life cycle and intermediate hosts** — increase the chance of transmission, use the host's food chain (intermediate host = prey, definitive host = predator).
- **Resistant eggs/cysts** — hard covering, survive for years.
- **Dioecious exception** — in *Schistosoma* male and female form a permanent pair (female in the male's canal) — mating is ensured.

### 4. Immune evasion

- **Molecular mimicry** and a **coat of host antigens** — *Schistosoma* covers its surface with the host's blood-group antigens and MHC molecules ("**antigenic disguise**").
- **Continuous renewal of the tegument** — sheds attached antibodies.
- **Immunosuppressive secretions** — divert the host's T-cell/cytokine response towards Th2; a rise in **eosinophils** and **IgE** indicates helminth infection.
- **Encystment** — cysticercus, hydatid cyst and the muscle cyst of *Trichinella* are protected from the host's immune attack.

> **Memory trick:** Parasitic adaptations = "**4 C + 3 R**" — **C**ling (attachment), **C**uticle (covering), **C**ut-down (loss of organs), **C**omplex life cycle; **R**espiration anaerobic, **R**eproduction prolific, **R**esistance to enzymes/immunity.

## Traps and exceptions

- *Ascaris* has **longitudinal muscles only** — the option "both circular and longitudinal" belongs to the earthworm.
- The sperm of *Ascaris* are **amoeboid**, not flagellated.
- *Ascaris* has **no flame cells**; flame cells are found in Platyhelminthes (and rotifers).
- *Ascaris* has **no intermediate host** — yet it migrates within the body up to the lungs.
- Male *Ascaris* has a **cloaca**, the female an **anus** — "cloaca in both" is wrong.
- In *Taenia solium* man is the **definitive host**, but in cysticercosis man plays the role of the **intermediate host** (by swallowing eggs) — this is the dangerous "dead-end host level".
- The scolex of *T. saginata* is **unarmed**; that of *T. solium* is **armed**.
- In *Echinococcus* man is the **intermediate** host (dog is definitive).
- The vector of *Wuchereria* is mainly ***Culex*** (in India *C. quinquefasciatus*) — not *Anopheles* (in some regions *Anopheles* also spreads filaria, but the textbook answer is *Culex*).
- *Schistosoma*, though a fluke, is **dioecious**.
- The intermediate host of guinea worm is *Cyclops* — not a mosquito.

## Quick revision

- *Ascaris* — Nematoda, pseudocoelomate, monogenetic, small intestine (jejunum).
- Male smaller, tail curved, 2 penial spicules, cloaca; female larger, straight, genital pore at anterior 1/3.
- Mouth with three lips — 1 dorsal, 2 ventro-lateral.
- Cuticle (4 moults) → syncytial epidermis → longitudinal muscles only → pseudocoel.
- Four lines: excretory canals in the lateral, nerve cords in the dorsal/ventral.
- H-shaped excretory system — one cell (renette), no flame cells.
- Anaerobic respiration — CO₂ + fatty acids; glycogen storage.
- Amphids anterior, phasmids posterior (chemoreceptors).
- Male monorchic, female didelphic; sperm amoeboid.
- Egg mammillated; ~2 lakh/day.
- Infection — embryonated egg by mouth; route: intestine → liver → heart → lungs → trachea → pharynx → intestine.
- 4 moults; Löffler's syndrome; albendazole.
- *Fasciola* — snail *Lymnaea*; metacercaria infective.
- *T. solium* — pig, armed scolex, cysticercus; *T. saginata* — cow, unarmed.
- *Echinococcus* — dog definitive, hydatid cyst.
- Hookworm — skin penetration, anaemia.
- *Wuchereria* — *Culex*, lymphatics, nocturnal periodicity.
- Guinea worm — *Cyclops*; India free in 2000.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. *Ascaris* belongs to which phylum? — **Nematoda (Aschelminthes)** — pseudocoelomate.
2. Infective form of *Ascaris*? — **Embryonated egg** (second-stage larva inside).
3. Intermediate host of *Ascaris*? — **None** (monogenetic).
4. How many moults do *Ascaris* larvae undergo in the lungs? — **Two** (second and third).
5. Which muscles are in the body wall of *Ascaris*? — **Longitudinal only**.
6. Shape of the excretory system of *Ascaris*? — **H-shaped** (single renette cell).
7. What forms the outer bumpy coat of the *Ascaris* egg? — **Protein secretion of the uterine wall** (mammillated coat).
8. Posterior chemoreceptors of nematodes? — **Phasmids**.
9. What are the lung symptoms of *Ascaris* infection called? — **Löffler's pneumonitis**.
10. Intermediate host of *Fasciola*? — **Snail *Lymnaea***.
11. Which larval stage of *Fasciola* infects sheep? — **Metacercaria**.
12. Whose is the armed scolex? — ***Taenia solium*** (hooks on the rostellum).
13. How does neurocysticercosis occur in man? — **By swallowing the eggs of *T. solium***.
14. Cause of hydatid disease? — ***Echinococcus granulosus*** (dog the definitive host).
15. Two helminths that enter by penetrating the skin? — **Hookworm (filariform larva) and *Schistosoma* (cercaria)**.
16. Practical importance of the nocturnal periodicity of microfilariae? — **The blood sample is taken at night** (10 pm–2 am).
17. Vitamin B₁₂ deficiency is caused by which tapeworm? — ***Diphyllobothrium latum***.
18. A dioecious fluke? — ***Schistosoma***.
19. What does *Schistosoma* achieve by coating itself with host antigens? — **Immune evasion (molecular disguise)**.
20. What is the increase in number through sporocyst–redia in *Fasciola* called? — **Polyembryony / larval multiplication** (reproductive adaptation).
