This chapter covers point 1 of Section B of the syllabus (Animal Diversity — taxonomy with characteristic features) and the first part of point 2 (Non-Chordates — classification of phyla). The NCERT Class 11 chapter "Animal Kingdom" is its base; at B.Sc. level the classes of each phylum and their examples are added. In UP PGT 2026 both direct questions ("what is a taxon", "canal system is found in which phylum") and statement-combination questions (artificial vs natural classification) came from here.

> **In simple words:** It is impossible to remember more than about 1.5 million known animal species, so we divide them into groups on the basis of shared characters. The largest animal group is the **phylum** and the smallest is the **species**. Which phylum an animal is placed in is decided by a few basic questions: do the cells form tissues or not? Is the body symmetrical or not? Are there two germ layers or three? Is there a coelom or not? Is there a notochord or not?

## 1. Basic concepts of taxonomy

| Term | Meaning | Fact to remember |
|---|---|---|
| **Taxonomy** | The science of identification, nomenclature and classification of organisms | Term coined by **A. P. de Candolle** (1813) |
| **Systematics** | Study of the diversity of organisms and their **evolutionary relationships** | Term from **Linnaeus**'s *Systema Naturae*; taxonomy is a part of it |
| **Taxon** | A group of organisms at **any level** of classification | "Insects", "mammals", "*Panthera*" — all are taxa |
| **Category / Rank** | A level of the taxonomic hierarchy | Kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species |
| **Species** | The basic unit of classification | Biological species concept — **Ernst Mayr** (reproductive isolation) |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "What is a group of organisms at a particular level of classification called? (A) Species (B) Genus (C) Taxon (D) Phylum" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C) Taxon**. Species, genus and phylum are themselves particular categories; "a group at any level" = taxon.

### Taxonomic hierarchy

Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species

| Category | Human | Housefly | Frog (Indian) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kingdom | Animalia | Animalia | Animalia |
| Phylum | Chordata | Arthropoda | Chordata |
| Class | Mammalia | Insecta | Amphibia |
| Order | Primata | Diptera | Anura |
| Family | Hominidae | Muscidae | Dicroglossidae (earlier Ranidae) |
| Genus | *Homo* | *Musca* | *Hoplobatrachus* (earlier *Rana*) |
| Species | *sapiens* | *domestica* | *tigerinus* (earlier *tigrina*) |

- In plants **Division** is used in place of "phylum".
- Going upward, the **number of shared characters decreases** and the number of members increases.

### Binomial nomenclature

- Founder: **Carolus Linnaeus** (Sweden) — the **10th edition of *Systema Naturae* (1758)** is the starting point of zoological nomenclature (for plants *Species Plantarum*, 1753).
- Rules: the first word is the **genus** (capital initial), the second the **specific epithet** (small initial); *italic* when printed, underlined separately when handwritten; names in Latin/Latinised form; the abbreviated name of the author at the end (*Mangifera indica* Linn.).
- Animal names are governed by the **ICZN** (International Code of Zoological Nomenclature), plants by the **ICN** (earlier ICBN), bacteria by the ICNB.
- **Tautonym** — genus and species names identical: *Rattus rattus*, *Naja naja*, *Gorilla gorilla*. **Valid under ICZN, not valid under ICN (plants).**
- **Trinomial nomenclature** — a third word for the subspecies: *Homo sapiens sapiens*, *Corvus splendens splendens*.
- **Law of priority** — if an organism has several names, the earliest validly published name is accepted.

### Taxonomic aids

| Aid | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Museum** | Preserved specimens (preservative solution — formalin; insects — dried and pinned) |
| **Zoo** | Living wild animals (ex-situ conservation) |
| **Key** | Identification tool based on paired contrasting characters (**couplet**, lead) — dichotomous key |
| **Monograph / Flora / Fauna / Manual** | Description of the organisms of a taxon / region |
| **Type specimen** | Holotype (original specimen), paratype, lectotype (chosen later if there is no holotype), neotype (if all original material is destroyed) |

## 2. Systems of classification

| System | Basis | Example / founder | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Artificial** | **One or a few easily seen** characters (habitat, ability to fly, size) | **Aristotle** — animals with blood (Enaima) and without blood (Anaima); Linnaeus's sexual system (plants) | Unrelated organisms grouped together (bat, bird, insect — all "fliers") |
| **Natural** | **Many characters** — morphology, anatomy, embryology, cytology; overall similarity | Bentham–Hooker (plants) | Does not clearly show evolutionary relationships |
| **Phylogenetic** | **Evolutionary relationships** (common ancestor) | Engler–Prantl, Hutchinson; cladistics (**Willi Hennig**) | Fossil record incomplete |
| **Numerical (Phenetics)** | All characters given **equal weight**, analysed by computer | Sokal and Sneath | — |
| **Cytotaxonomy** | Chromosome number, structure, behaviour | — | — |
| **Chemotaxonomy** | Chemical constituents, proteins, DNA sequences | Molecular taxonomy (16S rRNA) | — |

- The term **New systematics** — **Julian Huxley** (1940); **alpha taxonomy** = identification and naming based on morphology; **beta** = arrangement into natural groups; **gamma** = evolution at population level, intraspecific variation.
- **Clade** = an ancestor and **all** its descendants (monophyletic). **Paraphyletic** = an ancestor and some descendants (traditional "Reptilia" — because birds are left out). **Polyphyletic** = a group derived from different ancestors (traditional "Protozoa").
- **Character-state terms of cladistics:** **plesiomorphy** = ancestral (primitive) character; **apomorphy** = derived (new) character; **synapomorphy** = shared derived character — **only this** defines a clade (e.g. feathers — birds; mammary glands — mammals); **symplesiomorphy** = shared primitive character (e.g. notochord — all chordates; it does not make fish + amphibians a separate clade); **homoplasy** = similarity due to convergent evolution (wings of bat and bird).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Early taxonomists grouped organisms on the basis of visible similarities. What is the limitation of this method?" Option (B): "Ignoring derived and ancestral character states, leading to artificial groups" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**. (This question was in the Botany section, but the principle applies equally to animal taxonomy.)

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Statements on artificial and natural classification — (I) Artificial classification is based on a few easily seen characters and may group unrelated organisms together; (II) Natural classification includes many characters and tries to show overall similarity and evolutionary relationships; (III) Artificial classification, because of its simplicity, is better at showing phylogenetic relationships; (IV) Natural classification is more stable because it considers a wide range of morphological, anatomical and genetic characters. Options: (A) I, II, III, IV (B) II, III, IV only (C) I, III only (D) I, II, IV only — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**. Statement III is wrong — simplicity does not give the ability to show phylogeny.

### History of kingdom classification

| Year | Scientist | Kingdoms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1758 | Linnaeus | 2 — Plantae, Animalia | — |
| 1866 | **Ernst Haeckel** | 3 — + **Protista** | — |
| 1938/1956 | Copeland | 4 — + **Monera** | — |
| **1969** | **R. H. Whittaker** | **5** — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia | Basis: cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction, phylogeny — NCERT follows this |
| **1990** | **Carl Woese** | **3 Domains** — Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya | Based on 16S rRNA sequences |
| 1998/2015 | Cavalier-Smith / Ruggiero et al. | 6 or 7 kingdoms | Chromista, Protozoa separate |

- According to Whittaker, **protozoa** are in "Kingdom Protista", not in the animal kingdom — but the B.Sc. syllabus (and the "Non-chordates" part of UP PGT) traditionally teaches them as "Phylum Protozoa".

## 3. Bases of animal classification

### (a) Level of organisation

| Level | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cellular** | Cells in loose aggregates; division of labour at cell level | **Porifera** (sponges) |
| **Tissue** | Cells with the same function form tissues | **Cnidaria, Ctenophora** |
| **Organ** | Tissues together form organs | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Organ-system** | Organs together form systems | Aschelminthes to Chordata |

- **Incomplete alimentary canal** (a single opening — the mouth serves as anus): Cnidaria, Ctenophora, Platyhelminthes. **Complete alimentary canal** (separate mouth and anus): from Aschelminthes onward.
- **Open circulation** (blood in cavities/sinuses): Arthropoda, most Mollusca. **Closed circulation**: Annelida, Cephalopoda (Mollusca), Chordata.

### (b) Symmetry

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Asymmetrical** | No plane gives two equal halves | Most sponges, *Amoeba*, (adult) *Pila* — due to torsion |
| **Radial** | **Any** plane passing through the central axis gives two equal halves | Cnidaria, Ctenophora, **adult** echinoderms (pentamerous) |
| **Biradial** | Only **two** planes | Ctenophora, sea anemone |
| **Bilateral** | Only **one** plane (mid-sagittal) gives two equal halves | Platyhelminthes to Chordata; **larvae** of echinoderms |

### (c) Germ layers

- **Diploblastic** — ectoderm and endoderm; in between an acellular **mesoglea**: **Cnidaria, Ctenophora**.
- **Triploblastic** — a third layer, the **mesoderm**: Platyhelminthes to Chordata.

### (d) Coelom

Coelom = the cavity between the body wall and the alimentary canal **that is lined by mesoderm**.

| Type | Character | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoelomate** | No cavity; filled with **parenchyma** | **Platyhelminthes** |
| **Pseudocoelomate** | Cavity present but mesoderm as **scattered pouches**, not completely lined (remnant of the embryonic **blastocoel**) | **Aschelminthes** (*Ascaris*), Rotifera |
| **Eucoelomate (true coelom)** | Cavity lined all round by mesoderm | Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

**Mode of formation of the true coelom (B.Sc.):**
- **Schizocoelous** — solid masses of mesoderm **split** to form the cavity: Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca — **protostomes**.
- **Enterocoelous** — **pouches bud off** from the wall of the archenteron to form the cavity: Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata — **deuterostomes**.
- In Arthropoda and Mollusca the true coelom is **very small** (around gonads, excretory organs, pericardium); the main body cavity is the blood-filled **haemocoel**.

| Character | Protostomia | Deuterostomia |
|---|---|---|
| Blastopore becomes | **Mouth** | **Anus** |
| Cleavage | **Spiral, determinate** | **Radial, indeterminate** |
| Coelom | Schizocoelous | Enterocoelous |
| Typical larva | **Trochophore** (Annelida, Mollusca) | **Dipleurula** type (bipinnaria, tornaria) |
| Nerve cord | Ventral, solid | Dorsal, hollow (Chordata) |
| Examples | Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca | Echinodermata, Hemichordata, Chordata |

### (e) Segmentation, notochord and other bases

- **Metamerism** — body divided externally and internally into serial segments: **Annelida** (earthworm), Arthropoda, Chordata (myotomes, vertebrae). The "segmentation" of tapeworm (proglottids) is **pseudometamerism**.
- **Notochord** — a dorsal rod-like structure formed from mesoderm in the embryo: present = **Chordata**, absent = **non-chordates**.
- **Teloblastic mesoderm**, cuticle, exoskeleton, jointed appendages etc. are phylum-level characters.

## 4. Summary of non-chordate phyla (NCERT 11)

| Phylum | Organisation | Symmetry | Layers | Coelom | Distinctive features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Porifera** | Cellular | Mostly asymmetrical | — (cellular) | Absent | **Canal system**, ostia, osculum, **choanocytes (collar cells)**, spicules / spongin fibres | *Sycon* (Scypha), *Spongilla* (freshwater), *Euspongia* (bath sponge) |
| **Cnidaria (Coelenterata)** | Tissue | Radial | Diplo | Absent | **Cnidoblasts**, gastrovascular cavity, **polyp and medusa**, alternation of generations (metagenesis) | *Physalia* (Portuguese man-of-war), *Adamsia* (sea anemone), *Pennatula* (sea pen), *Gorgonia* (sea fan), *Meandrina* (brain coral), *Hydra*, *Obelia*, *Aurelia* |
| **Ctenophora** | Tissue | Biradial | Diplo | Absent | **8 comb plates**, **colloblasts** (no cnidoblasts), bioluminescence, only sexual reproduction, indirect development | *Pleurobrachia*, *Ctenoplana* (sea walnut / comb jelly) |
| **Platyhelminthes** | Organ | Bilateral | Triplo | Acoelomate | Dorsoventrally flattened, **flame cells**, hooks/suckers, mostly parasitic, hermaphrodite | *Taenia* (tapeworm), *Fasciola* (liver fluke), *Planaria* (free-living, regeneration) |
| **Aschelminthes (Nematoda)** | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triplo | **Pseudocoelomate** | Circular in cross-section, cuticle, **dioecious, sexual dimorphism** (male smaller), complete alimentary canal | *Ascaris* (roundworm), *Wuchereria* (filaria), *Ancylostoma* (hookworm) |
| **Annelida** | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triplo | True (schizocoelous) | **Metamerism**, **nephridia**, setae/parapodia, closed circulation, ventral nerve cord | *Nereis* (parapodia, dioecious), *Pheretima* (earthworm), *Hirudinaria* (blood-sucking leech) |
| **Arthropoda** | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triplo | True (haemocoel) | **Jointed appendages, chitinous exoskeleton**, open circulation, moulting; **largest phylum** (~2/3 of known species) | *Apis*, *Bombyx*, *Laccifer*, *Anopheles*, *Culex*, *Aedes*, *Locusta*, *Limulus* (king crab, living fossil) |
| **Mollusca** | Organ-system | Bilateral (asymmetrical in Gastropoda due to torsion) | Triplo | True (reduced) | **Mantle, shell, muscular foot, radula**, gills (ctenidia); second largest phylum | *Pila* (apple snail), *Pinctada* (pearl oyster), *Sepia* (cuttlefish), *Loligo* (squid), *Octopus*, *Aplysia* (sea hare), *Dentalium* (tusk shell), *Chaetopleura* (chiton) |
| **Echinodermata** | Organ-system | Adult radial (pentamerous), larva bilateral | Triplo | True (enterocoelous) | **Water vascular system**, tube feet, calcareous ossicles (endoskeleton), exclusively marine, excretory system absent | *Asterias* (starfish), *Echinus* (sea urchin), *Antedon* (sea lily), *Cucumaria* (sea cucumber), *Ophiura* (brittle star) |
| **Hemichordata** | Organ-system | Bilateral | Triplo | True | Body in three parts — **proboscis, collar, trunk**; **stomochord**; earlier a subphylum of Chordata, now a separate non-chordate phylum | *Balanoglossus*, *Saccoglossus* |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Which of the following phyla is identified by a porous body with a canal system for water circulation? (A) Platyhelminthes (B) Coelenterata (C) Porifera (D) Annelida" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C) Porifera**.

> **Remember:** In both the **water vascular system** of Echinodermata and the **canal system** of Porifera "water" flows — but the first is for locomotion (tube feet) and capturing food, the second for food, respiration and excretion.

## 5. B.Sc. level — classes of the phyla and examples

### Phylum Protozoa (traditional, based on Hyman/Kudo)

| Class | Locomotory organ | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Sarcodina (Rhizopoda)** | **Pseudopodia** | *Amoeba*, *Entamoeba*, Foraminifera (*Elphidium*, *Globigerina*), Radiolaria |
| **Mastigophora (Flagellata)** | **Flagella** | *Euglena*, *Trypanosoma*, *Leishmania*, *Giardia*, *Trichomonas*, *Noctiluca* (bioluminescence of the sea) |
| **Sporozoa** | **No locomotory organ** (all parasitic) | *Plasmodium*, *Monocystis* (seminal vesicles of earthworm), *Eimeria*, *Toxoplasma*, *Babesia* |
| **Ciliata (Ciliophora)** | **Cilia**, two kinds of nuclei (macro and micro) | *Paramecium*, *Vorticella*, *Balantidium* (the only parasitic ciliate of man), *Opalina* (intestine of frog) |

- Levine et al. (1980) divided protozoa into **7 phyla** (Sarcomastigophora, Apicomplexa, Ciliophora, Microspora etc.) — *Plasmodium* = **Apicomplexa** (with an apical complex).

### Phylum Porifera

| Class | Skeleton | Canal system | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcarea (Calcispongiae)** | Spicules of **calcium carbonate** (monaxon, triradiate, tetraxon) | Ascon, sycon, leucon | *Leucosolenia*, *Sycon*, *Grantia* |
| **Hexactinellida (Hyalospongiae)** | **Six-rayed (triaxon)** spicules of **silica** — "glass sponges" | Syconoid-like | *Euplectella* (**Venus's flower basket**), *Hyalonema* |
| **Demospongiae** | Silica (monaxon/tetraxon) and/or **spongin** fibres | Leucon | *Spongilla* (freshwater), *Euspongia* (bath sponge), *Cliona* (boring sponge), *Chalina* (mermaid's gloves) |

### Phylum Cnidaria

| Class | Dominant form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrozoa** | Polyp and/or medusa (medusa with a **velum** — craspedote) | *Hydra* (polyp only), *Obelia* (alternation of generations), *Physalia*, *Velella*, *Millepora* (fire coral) |
| **Scyphozoa** | **Medusa dominant** (no velum — acraspedote); "true jellyfish" | *Aurelia* (moon jelly), *Rhizostoma* |
| **Cubozoa** | Cube-shaped medusa | *Chironex* (sea wasp, extremely venomous) |
| **Anthozoa** | **Polyp only** (no medusa) | *Metridium*, *Adamsia* (sea anemone), corals (*Madrepora*, *Fungia*), *Gorgonia*, *Pennatula* |

### Phylum Platyhelminthes and Nematoda

| Class | Characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Turbellaria** | Free-living, ciliated epidermis | *Planaria (Dugesia)* |
| **Trematoda** | Parasitic flukes, suckers | *Fasciola hepatica*, *Schistosoma* (blood fluke, dioecious) |
| **Cestoda** | Parasitic tapeworms, no alimentary canal | *Taenia solium*, *T. saginata*, *Echinococcus* |
| **Nematoda** (classes Phasmidia / Aphasmidia) | Phasmids (posterior sense organs) present or absent | Phasmidia: *Ascaris*, *Enterobius*, *Wuchereria*, *Ancylostoma*; Aphasmidia: *Trichinella*, *Trichuris* |

### Phylum Annelida

| Class | Characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Polychaeta** | Many setae on **parapodia**, distinct head, dioecious, **trochophore** larva, marine | *Nereis* (ragworm), *Aphrodite* (sea mouse), *Arenicola* (lugworm), *Chaetopterus* |
| **Oligochaeta** | Few setae, no parapodia, **clitellum**, hermaphrodite, direct development | *Pheretima*, *Lumbricus*, *Tubifex* |
| **Hirudinea** | No setae, suckers, fixed number of segments (usually **33**), hermaphrodite, **hirudin** (anticoagulant) | *Hirudinaria*, *Hirudo* |
| **Archiannelida** | Simple, marine | *Polygordius* |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Which structure in polychaetes mainly enhances both locomotion and respiration? (A) Parapodia (B) Clitellum (C) Prostomium (D) Nephridia" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A) Parapodia** — lateral muscular flaps that act as paddles in swimming/crawling and, because of their thin, richly vascular wall, also as gills.

### Phylum Arthropoda

| Subphylum / Class | Body | Appendages / respiration | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trilobita** (extinct) | Three lobes | — | *Triarthrus* |
| **Crustacea** | Cephalothorax + abdomen | **2 pairs of antennae**, biramous appendages, gills; **green glands** | *Palaemon*, *Cancer* (crab), *Daphnia*, *Cyclops*, *Balanus*, *Sacculina* |
| **Myriapoda** — Chilopoda / Diplopoda | Head + long trunk | Chilopoda: 1 pair of legs per segment, poison claws; Diplopoda: 2 pairs per (double) segment | *Scolopendra* (centipede), *Julus* (millipede) |
| **Insecta (Hexapoda)** | Head, thorax, abdomen | **3 pairs of legs**, 1 pair of antennae, usually 2 pairs of wings; tracheae; **Malpighian tubules** | *Periplaneta*, *Apis*, *Musca* |
| **Arachnida** (subphylum Chelicerata) | Cephalothorax + abdomen | **No** antennae, **4 pairs of legs**, chelicerae; **book lungs** / tracheae; **coxal glands** | *Palamnaeus* (scorpion), spider, *Sarcoptes* (itch mite), tick |
| **Merostomata** | Cephalothorax + abdomen | **Book gills** | *Limulus* (king crab) |
| **Onychophora** (considered a separate phylum) | — | — | *Peripatus* — **connecting link between Annelida and Arthropoda** |

### Phylum Mollusca

| Class | Characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Monoplacophora** | Single cap-like shell, partial metamerism | *Neopilina* (**living fossil**; Annelida–Mollusca relationship) |
| **Polyplacophora (Amphineura)** | **8 shell plates** | *Chiton*, *Chaetopleura* |
| **Aplacophora** | No shell, worm-like | *Neomenia* |
| **Scaphopoda** | Hollow tubular shell **like an elephant's tusk**, open at both ends | *Dentalium* |
| **Gastropoda** | Spiral shell, **torsion**, distinct head | *Pila*, *Limax* (slug, no shell), *Aplysia*, *Doris*, *Achatina* (giant land snail) |
| **Pelecypoda (Bivalvia / Lamellibranchia)** | **Two valves**, no head, no radula, filter feeders | *Unio*, *Lamellidens* (freshwater mussel), *Pinctada* (pearl oyster), *Teredo* (**shipworm** — wood borer), *Mytilus* |
| **Cephalopoda** | Foot modified into **arms/tentacles**, **closed circulation**, well-developed eyes, ink gland | *Sepia*, *Loligo*, *Octopus*, *Nautilus* (the only living one with an external shell), *Architeuthis* (**largest invertebrate** — giant squid) |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Why are molluscan shells considered an effective anti-predator adaptation? (A) They increase metabolic rate (B) They increase reproduction (C) They reduce the handling efficiency of predators (D) They increase mobility" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)** — a hard shell increases the time and energy a predator needs to break/open it; the shell actually reduces mobility.

### Phylum Echinodermata

| Class | Characters | Examples | Larva |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asteroidea** | Star-shaped, arms not sharply marked off from the central disc, ambulacral groove open | *Asterias*, *Pentaceros*, *Astropecten* | **Bipinnaria → Brachiolaria** |
| **Ophiuroidea** | Arms slender, clearly separate, groove closed; no anus | *Ophiura*, *Ophiothrix* (brittle star) | **Ophiopluteus** |
| **Echinoidea** | No arms, rounded **test**, movable spines; **Aristotle's lantern** (masticatory apparatus) | *Echinus* (sea urchin), *Echinocardium* | **Echinopluteus** |
| **Holothuroidea** | Cucumber-shaped, ossicles microscopic; **respiratory tree**; Cuvierian tubules | *Cucumaria*, *Holothuria* (sea cucumber) | **Auricularia** |
| **Crinoidea** | Attached by a stalk or free, mouth facing upward | *Antedon* (feather star / sea lily) | **Doliolaria** |

## 7. Diversity figures (useful for the exam)

- Known species ~1.7–1.8 million (described); of these **more than 70% are animals**; among animals **insects ~70%** (the most).
- Largest phylum: **Arthropoda**; second: **Mollusca**; largest class: **Insecta**; largest insect order: **Coleoptera** (beetles).
- India: ~2.4% of the world's area, ~6.5–7% of known animal species; the **ZSI (Zoological Survey of India, 1916, Kolkata)** keeps the record of Indian fauna (more than about 1 lakh animal species recorded).
- **Largest living animal** — blue whale (*Balaenoptera musculus*); **largest invertebrate** — giant/colossal squid; **largest protozoan (living)** — some foraminifera/xenophyophores; the textbook answer is usually *Pelomyxa* / *Porospora gigantea* (largest gregarine).

## Traps and exceptions

- **Ctenophores have no cnidoblasts** (exception: *Haeckelia*, which uses the cnidoblasts of swallowed cnidarians); their sticky cells are **colloblasts**.
- **Sponges have no tissues** — yet they are multicellular animals (**Parazoa**); all the others are **Eumetazoa**.
- **Adult echinoderms are radial but the larva is bilateral** — so they are considered to have evolved from a bilateral ancestor (secondary radial symmetry).
- **Hemichordata are no longer chordates** — their "stomochord" is not considered homologous to the notochord; NCERT teaches them as a separate non-chordate phylum.
- *Limulus* (king crab) is not a true crab (Crustacea) — it is a chelicerate; silverfish (*Lepisma*) is not a fish — it is a wingless insect; "jellyfish", "starfish", "cuttlefish", "silverfish" — none is a fish.
- *Nautilus* has an external shell, *Sepia* an internal one (cuttlebone), *Loligo* a **pen (gladius)**, *Octopus* no shell.
- **Pseudometamerism** — tapeworm; **true metamerism** — earthworm.
- Some annelids are hermaphrodite (earthworm, leech) but *Nereis* is **dioecious**.
- In Arthropoda the blood is often colourless (insects) but in Crustacea it has **haemocyanin** (blue).

## Quick revision

- Taxon = a group at any level; category = level (rank).
- Binomial nomenclature — Linnaeus; zoological nomenclature starts from *Systema Naturae* (10th ed., 1758).
- Tautonyms valid under ICZN (*Naja naja*), not valid in plants.
- Five kingdoms — Whittaker (1969); three domains — Carl Woese (1990).
- Artificial = a few characters (Aristotle); natural = many characters; phylogenetic = evolutionary relationships.
- Cellular level — Porifera; tissue — Cnidaria, Ctenophora; organ — Platyhelminthes.
- Diploblastic — Cnidaria, Ctenophora; mesoglea in between.
- Acoelomate — flatworms; pseudocoelomate — roundworms; schizocoelous — Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca; enterocoelous — echinoderms to chordates.
- In deuterostomes blastopore → anus; radial indeterminate cleavage.
- Canal system — Porifera; water vascular system — Echinodermata.
- Cnidoblasts — Cnidaria; comb plates and colloblasts — Ctenophora.
- Flame cells — Platyhelminthes; nephridia — Annelida; Malpighian tubules — insects; green gland — Crustacea.
- Parapodia — Polychaeta (locomotion + respiration).
- Largest phylum Arthropoda, second Mollusca.
- *Peripatus* — Annelida–Arthropoda link; *Neopilina* — Annelida–Mollusca link.
- *Euplectella* — Venus's flower basket (glass sponge).
- Glochidium — *Unio*; amphiblastula — *Sycon*; tornaria — *Balanoglossus*.
- *Balantidium coli* — the only parasitic ciliate of man.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Who coined the term "Taxonomy"? — **A. P. de Candolle** (1813).
2. Which edition of *Systema Naturae* is the starting point of zoological nomenclature? — **10th (1758)** (ICZN accepts this date).
3. What kind of name is *Rattus rattus*? — **Tautonym** (genus = species name; valid in animals).
4. What is the basis of the three-domain system? — **16S rRNA sequences** (Carl Woese).
5. What type was Aristotle's classification? — **Artificial** (with blood/without blood).
6. Level of organisation of Porifera? — **Cellular** (no tissues).
7. The pseudocoel is a remnant of what? — **Blastocoel** (mesoderm on one side only).
8. What does the blastopore become in deuterostomes? — **Anus**.
9. Biradial symmetry is found in? — **Ctenophora** (and sea anemone).
10. How many comb plates are there? — **8** (Ctenophora).
11. The bath sponge belongs to which class? — **Demospongiae** (*Euspongia*, spongin fibres).
12. The cnidarian class with only the polyp form? — **Anthozoa**.
13. Fixed number of segments in a leech? — **33** (usually).
14. Book lungs are found in? — **Arachnida** (scorpion, spider).
15. What is the shipworm? — ***Teredo*** (a bivalve mollusc, not a worm).
16. Aristotle's lantern is found in? — **Sea urchin** (Echinoidea).
17. Auricularia larva belongs to? — **Sea cucumber** (Holothuroidea).
18. The living cephalopod with an external shell? — ***Nautilus***.
19. *Peripatus* is a link between which phyla? — **Annelida and Arthropoda**.
20. In which class is *Euglena* placed (traditionally)? — **Mastigophora** (flagella; both plant and animal characters).
