The syllabus has a whole point — "Anatomy of Frog, *Uromastyx*, Pigeon and Rabbit". These four animals represent four classes (amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals); so examiners often ask **comparisons** — "in which is the sinus venosus a separate chamber", "which has no urinary bladder", "whose pelvic girdle is joined to the synsacrum". UP PGT 2026 asked about the pelvic girdle of the pigeon, the food–water adaptations of *Uromastyx* and a comparative matching of classes. The level of the B.Sc. practical dissection manual is expected.

> **In simple words:** Going from frog to rabbit we see that life moved from water to land — breathing through the skin decreased, lungs became complex; the heart went from 3 to 4 chambers; kidneys changed from mesonephros to metanephros; ammonia/urea gave way to uric acid (lizard, pigeon) or back to urea (rabbit); the cerebrum kept enlarging in the brain.

## 1. Introduction — the four types

| | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scientific name | *Hoplobatrachus tigerinus* (*Rana tigrina*) — Indian bullfrog; *Euphlyctis hexadactylus* | ***Uromastyx hardwickii*** (modern: ***Saara hardwickii***) — **Indian spiny-tailed lizard / sanda** | ***Columba livia*** — wild/blue rock pigeon | ***Oryctolagus cuniculus*** — European/domestic rabbit |
| Class / order | Amphibia / Anura | Reptilia / Squamata (Lacertilia), family **Agamidae** | Aves / Columbiformes | Mammalia / **Lagomorpha** (not a rodent) |
| Habitat | Water–land (amphibious) | **Dry desert** — Rajasthan (Thar), Gujarat, Haryana — in **burrows** | Air–land; human settlements | Land — burrows (warren) |
| Temperature regulation | Poikilothermous — hibernation, aestivation | Poikilothermous — **basking**; darker colour in cold (more heat absorption) | **Homeothermous** (~41 °C) | **Homeothermous** (~38–39 °C); **large ears — heat loss** |
| Food | **Insectivorous** (adult); tadpole herbivorous | **Herbivorous** (adult — grass, leaves, flowers, seeds; young also eat insects) | **Granivorous** | **Herbivorous** |

### *Uromastyx* — special external features

- Body **dorsoventrally flattened**, heavy; small **granular scales** on the skin; **tail short, thick, flattened, with whorls of spiny scales** — **defence** (closes the burrow entrance with the tail, lashes the enemy with the tail) and **fat storage**.
- **Tympanum** on the side of the head (in a depression); movable **eyelids** and a nictitating membrane on the eyes.
- Digits **5–5**, **clawed** (for digging burrows).
- Hunted because of a local superstition ("sande ka tel" — sanda oil) — threatened; protected under the **Wildlife (Protection) Act**.

## 2. Alimentary canal and dentition

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Teeth** | **Only on the upper jaw** — **maxillary** (on the margin) and **vomerine** (on the palate, two groups); **lower jaw toothless**; **homodont, acrodont, polyphyodont** — teeth only for **holding** prey, not for chewing | **Acrodont** (fused to the crest of the bone), usually **homodont** but the front teeth **chisel-like, incisor-like** (in adults the front teeth fuse into a cutting edge) — **nibbling leaves**; **replacement very limited** (agamid) | **No teeth** — **horny beak** — rhamphotheca | **Heterodont, thecodont, diphyodont**; **dental formula i 2/1, c 0/0, pm 3/2, m 3/3 = 28**; **no canines** → a large gap between incisors and premolars, the **diastema**; a second small pair behind the upper incisors — **peg teeth** (identifying feature of lagomorphs); incisors **rootless, ever-growing**, enamel only in front — chisel edge |
| **Tongue** | **Bilobed**, **attached in front, free behind** — flicked out to catch insects; sticky | Thick, fleshy, slightly forked at the tip; protrudes very little | Pointed, horny | Thick, fleshy, taste buds |
| **Salivary glands** | **Absent** (only mucous glands — intermaxillary gland on the palate) | Buccal glands (labial, sublingual) | Small | **4 pairs** — **parotid, submandibular, sublingual, infraorbital** |
| **Oesophagus / special** | Short | Normal | **Crop** — a bilobed sac; in the breeding season **pigeon milk (crop milk)** — induced by **prolactin**, in **both male and female**, for the young | Normal |
| **Stomach** | Simple, J-shaped; pyloric sphincter | Simple (cardiac–pyloric) | Two parts — **proventriculus (glandular stomach)** — digestive juice; **gizzard** — thick muscular wall, horny lining (koilin), **pebbles/grit** — **grinding seeds** | Simple, large — cardiac, fundic, pyloric parts |
| **Intestine** | Duodenum (pancreas in the U-bend), ileum (coiled), broad **rectum** → **cloaca** | Duodenum, ileum, **well-developed caecum in the large intestine** and **transverse folds/valves in the colon** — **digestion of cellulose by microbial fermentation**; **intestine long** (herbivore) → **cloaca** (coprodaeum, urodaeum, proctodaeum) | Duodenum (pancreas in the U-loop), ileum; **two small vestigial caeca** at the ileo-rectal junction; short rectum → **cloaca** (coprodaeum, urodaeum, proctodaeum; **bursa of Fabricius** on the dorsal wall of the proctodaeum — B-lymphocytes, in young birds) | Duodenum, jejunum, ileum → **sacculus rotundus** (round sac, lymphoid) → **very large caecum** (spiral folds, cellulose fermentation) + **vermiform appendix** at its tip (lymphoid tissue) → colon → rectum → **anus** (no cloaca); **coprophagy / caecotrophy** — eating the soft faeces formed at night (caecotropes) directly to obtain vitamin B and protein |
| **Liver** | 3 lobes; gall bladder present | Present, gall bladder present | **2 lobes**; **no gall bladder** (in *Columba*) — two bile ducts open directly into the duodenum | **5 lobes**; gall bladder present |
| **Pancreas and duct** | In the U of the duodenum; **the bile duct passes through the pancreas** and the pancreatic ducts open into it → **hepato-pancreatic duct** | Normal | In the duodenal loop; 3 ducts | Spread diffusely in the duodenal loop; **pancreatic duct separate from the bile duct**, opens into the duodenum **much further along (posteriorly)** |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "*Uromastix* is a herbivorous reptile adapted to a dry environment. Which combination best explains how its structural features help both in diet and in water conservation?" Option (D): "A long intestine with a well-developed caecum for cellulose digestion, together with efficient kidneys producing concentrated urine to reduce water loss" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**. Fine point: the reptilian kidney (without loops of Henle) cannot by itself make urine **more concentrated than blood**; water conservation is mainly through excretion of **uric acid**, **reabsorption of water in the cloaca/urinary bladder** and **nasal salt glands** — still, among the options D points in the right direction.

## 3. Respiratory system

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory surfaces** | (1) **Cutaneous** — the only one in water and during hibernation; (2) **bucco-pharyngeal**; (3) **pulmonary** (during activity on land); **gills** in the tadpole | **Lungs only** (scaly skin — no cutaneous respiration) | **Lungs + air sacs** | **Lungs** |
| **Lungs** | Thin-walled **sacs** with shallow folds inside (primary/secondary/tertiary septa — "alveoli") | Large sacs with **honeycomb**-like **faveoli** inside, posterior part less vascular | Small, spongy, **attached to the ribs, non-expansile**; **parabronchi** (air capillaries) | Spongy, elastic, **alveoli** — millions; right lung 4 lobes, left 2 (rabbit) |
| **Air passage** | External nares → internal nares → bucco-pharynx → **glottis** → short **laryngo-tracheal chamber** → lungs (no separate trachea); **vocal cords** (in the larynx); **vocal sacs** in the male | Nostrils → mouth → glottis → larynx → **trachea** (cartilaginous rings) → 2 bronchi → lungs | **Trachea** (complete bony rings) → at the bifurcation of the trachea the **syrinx** — **sound organ** (no vocal cords in the larynx) → mesobronchi → parabronchi | Larynx (**epiglottis**, thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid cartilages; **vocal cords**) → **trachea** (**C-shaped** cartilaginous rings) → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli |
| **Mechanism of breathing** | **Positive pressure — buccal force pump**: nostrils open, floor of the mouth lowered → air into the mouth; nostrils closed (by the projection of the **mentomeckelian** bone of the lower jaw), floor of the mouth raised → air is **pushed** into the lungs; **no diaphragm or ribs** | **Negative pressure — costal breathing**: **intercostal muscles** move the ribs forward and outward → the thoraco-abdominal cavity enlarges → air is **drawn** in; **no diaphragm** | Movements of the ribs and **sternum** (sternum moves down and forward) expand/compress the **air sacs** — not the lungs; wing muscles during flight; **one-way flow in two cycles**; **9 air sacs**: interclavicular (1, unpaired), cervical (2), anterior thoracic (2), posterior thoracic (2), abdominal (2) | **Negative pressure** — **muscular diaphragm** (flattens on contraction) + external intercostal muscles; **thoracic cavity closed** (pleura) |
| **Respiratory pigment / RBC** | Haemoglobin; **oval, nucleated** RBCs (large) | **Oval, nucleated** | **Oval, nucleated** | **Round, biconcave, non-nucleated** |

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chambers** | **3** — 2 auricles + 1 ventricle | **3** — 2 auricles + 1 ventricle with an **incomplete septum** (cavum arteriosum, cavum venosum, cavum pulmonale) | **4** | **4** |
| **Sinus venosus** | **Well developed, a separate triangular chamber** (dorsal) — 2 precavals + 1 postcaval → into the right auricle | **Small**, joined to the right auricle | **Absent** (merged into the right auricle) | **Absent** — remnant is the **SA node** (pacemaker) |
| **Arterial trunk** | **Truncus/conus arteriosus** — **spiral valve** — sends oxygenated and deoxygenated blood partly into separate arches | **Absent** — three arterial trunks arise directly from the ventricle: **pulmonary aorta**, **left systemic arch**, **right systemic arch** (carotids from it) | Absent — pulmonary artery (right ventricle) and aorta (left ventricle) | Absent — pulmonary trunk and aorta |
| **Aortic arches** | **3 arches** on each side of the truncus: **carotid (III)**, **systemic (IV)**, **pulmocutaneous (VI)**; **both systemic arches** join to form the dorsal aorta; **carotid labyrinth** on the carotid | **Both systemic arches** (left and right) — form the dorsal aorta; pulmonary arch | **Only the right systemic (IV) arch** | **Only the left systemic (IV) arch** = aortic arch |
| **Circulation** | **Incomplete double** (mixing in the ventricle — but the spongy wall and spiral valve reduce mixing) | **Incomplete double** (little mixing; right-to-left shunt useful during diving/breath-holding) | **Complete double** | **Complete double** |
| **Precavals** | 2 | 2 | 2 | **2** (right and left — in the rabbit; only one superior vena cava in man) |
| **Renal portal system** | **Well developed** | **Present** | **Reduced** (can be bypassed by a venous valve) | **Absent** |
| **Hepatic portal system** | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| **Heart rate** | Slow (~30–60/min, depends on temperature) | Depends on temperature | **Fast** (~180–250/min at rest) | ~130–300/min |

### Fate of the embryonic aortic arches (6 pairs)

| Arch | **Frog** | **Reptile (*Uromastyx*)** | **Bird (pigeon)** | **Mammal (rabbit)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I, II | Lost | Lost | Lost | Lost |
| **III** | **Carotid** | **Carotid** | **Carotid** | **Common carotid** + base of the internal carotid |
| **IV** | **Both systemic arches** | **Both systemic arches** | **Right only** | **Left only** (right → part of the right subclavian artery) |
| V | Lost (persists in Urodela) | Lost | Lost | Lost |
| **VI** | **Pulmocutaneous** (pulmonary + cutaneous branch) | **Pulmonary** | **Pulmonary** | **Pulmonary**; dorsal part of the left VI = **ductus arteriosus (Botalli)** → after birth the **ligamentum arteriosum** |

- **Blood supply to the rabbit's brain** — common carotid → **internal carotid** (brain) and **external carotid** (face, neck); the **arterial circle of Willis** at the base of the brain. ## 5. Excretory and reproductive (urinogenital) systems

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type of kidney** | **Mesonephros / opisthonephros** — long, dark red, in the dorsal part of the body cavity; a yellow **adrenal** strip on the ventral surface | **Metanephros** — small, **posterior in the pelvic region**, lobed | **Metanephros** — **3-lobed**, sunk in depressions of the pelvic girdle (synsacrum) | **Metanephros** — **bean-shaped**, in the lumbar region, **cortex–medulla** and a **single pyramid/papilla** (rabbit — unipapillary kidney) |
| **Nephrons** | Small; **nephrostomes** (from the coelom in embryo/young), no loop of Henle | **No** loop of Henle | **Short loop of Henle** in some nephrons (mammalian type) | **Loop of Henle** well developed — **hypertonic (concentrated) urine** |
| **Nitrogenous waste** | **Ureotelic** (adult); tadpole **ammonotelic** | **Uricotelic** | **Uricotelic** — white semi-solid, with the faeces | **Ureotelic** |
| **Urinary bladder** | **Bilobed, thin** — from the **ventral wall of the cloaca** (cloacal bladder — not homologous to the mammalian bladder); ureters open directly into the cloaca, urine fills the bladder from the cloaca; a water reserve (reabsorption in drought) | **Present** (in lizards) — joined to the cloaca; reabsorption of water | **Absent** | **Present** — the ureters open into it; **urethra** |
| **Extra water conservation** | Absorption of water through the skin | **Nasal salt glands** (secrete K⁺, Na⁺ — compensate for the limited ability to concentrate urine), reabsorption in the cloaca, humid micro-environment of the burrow, dry scaly skin | Reabsorption in the cloaca/rectum | Long loops of Henle |
| **Male reproductive** | **Testes** — oval, yellow, on the ventral surface of the kidneys (attached by the **mesorchium**), **fat bodies** above; **vasa efferentia** pass through the kidney into **Bidder's canal** → **ureter** — so in the male the ureter = **urinogenital duct**; **seminal vesicle** in some species; **no copulatory organ**; in the breeding season a **nuptial pad** on the first digit — clasping (**amplexus**) | **Testes** (paired, in the coelom) → **epididymis** → **vas deferens** → cloaca; **paired hemipenes** — in sacs at the base of the tail, everted during copulation | **Testes** — paired, bean-shaped, **near the anterior part of the kidneys (in the abdomen)**, much enlarged in the breeding season; **vas deferens** (coiled) → **seminal vesicle** → cloaca (urodaeum); **no penis** (pigeon) — sperm transfer by the **"cloacal kiss"** | **Testes** — in **scrotal sacs** (descend in the breeding season, the **inguinal canal remains open**); **epididymis** (caput, corpus, cauda), **vas deferens**, **urethra**; accessory glands — **prostate**, **Cowper's (bulbourethral)**, **perineal** glands; **penis** |
| **Female reproductive** | **Ovaries** — paired, multilobed; **oviducts** long, coiled, the anterior end (**ostium**) opens into the coelom; the posterior part a broad **"uterus" (ovisac)** → cloaca; ureters **separate**; **external fertilisation** | Paired ovaries, oviducts (with shell glands) → cloaca; **internal fertilisation**; **oviparous** — cleidoic, leathery-shelled eggs in burrows/sand | **Only the left ovary and left oviduct** functional (right ones reduced); parts of the oviduct — **infundibulum** (fertilisation here), **magnum** (**albumen** — egg white), **isthmus** (**shell membranes**), **uterus / shell gland** (**CaCO₃ shell**, ~20 hours), **vagina** → cloaca; **2 eggs** per clutch | Paired ovaries; **Fallopian tubes** (fimbriated ostium); **duplex uterus** — **two completely separate uteri opening by two cervices into one vagina**; **vestibule**, **clitoris**; **induced (reflex) ovulation** — ~10 hours after copulation; gestation ~**31 days**; **discoidal placenta** |

## 6. Nervous system — brain

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Olfactory lobes** | In front, **fused in the middle** | Well developed, long olfactory stalks | **Very small** (reduced) | **Large** (keen smell — macrosmatic) |
| **Cerebral hemispheres** | Small, smooth, elongated | **Larger than in the frog** | **Large, smooth**, a large **corpus striatum** inside (behaviour, instinctive acts) | **Largest**, a few grooves on the surface (almost smooth — **lissencephalic**); outer grey **cerebral cortex (neopallium)**; the two hemispheres joined by the **corpus callosum** |
| **Diencephalon** | Pineal, pituitary, infundibulum | **Parietal/pineal eye** (in many lizards — a "third eye" on the head), pituitary | Pineal, pituitary | Pineal, pituitary, thalamus, hypothalamus |
| **Optic lobes (midbrain)** | **2 large, oval** (**corpora bigemina**) | 2, well developed | **2 very large**, pushed to the sides — sharp vision | **4 small — corpora quadrigemina** (2 superior — vision, 2 inferior — hearing) |
| **Cerebellum** | **Very small** — a thin transverse band (limited movement) | Small, crescent-shaped | **Large**, a median **vermis** with transverse folds — **balance in flight** | **Large, folded** — median **vermis**, lateral lobes and **flocculus** |
| **Pons (pons Varolii)** | Absent | Absent | Absent | **Present** — only in mammals |
| **Medulla** | Present | Present, bent (ventral flexure) | Present | Present |
| **Cranial nerves** | **10 pairs** | **12 pairs** | **12 pairs** | **12 pairs** |
| **Spinal nerves** | **10 pairs** | Many | Many | ~37 pairs |

- **Comparison of sense organs:** frog — **tympanum** (no external ear), one ossicle in the middle ear (**columella**); *Uromastyx* — tympanum in a depression, columella, **Jacobson's organ (vomeronasal)**; pigeon — external ear opening covered by feathers, columella, **pecten** in the eye; rabbit — **pinna**, **3 ear ossicles**, coiled cochlea (2.5 turns), **tapetum** (night vision).

## 7. Skeletal system — main points

| Character | **Frog** | ***Uromastyx*** | **Pigeon** | **Rabbit** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Skull** | Flat, broad, partly cartilaginous; **dicondylic** (2 occipital condyles); **platybasic** | **Monocondylic**; **modified diapsid** (lower temporal bar lost); **movable quadrate — streptostyly** (kinetic skull); **parietal foramen** | **Monocondylic**; bones thin, **sutures completely fused**; large orbits; movable quadrate; **tropibasic** | **Dicondylic**; **secondary palate**; **lower jaw a single bone — the dentary**; dentary–squamosal joint; **3 ear ossicles**; **synapsid** (one temporal fossa — zygomatic arch) |
| **Vertebral column** | **Only 9 vertebrae + urostyle** (fused caudal vertebrae); first — **atlas** (no transverse processes, 2 concave facets); **2–7 procoelous**; **8th amphicoelous**; **9th** (sacral) — convex in front, **2 condyles** behind (biconvex/acoelous) | **Procoelous**; regions — cervical, dorsal (thoraco-lumbar), **2 sacral**, many caudal; caudal vertebrae have **no autotomy planes** (agamid — *Uromastyx* does not shed its tail) | **Heterocoelous / saddle-shaped** cervical vertebrae — neck highly flexible (**cervical vertebrae ~14**); **thoracic vertebrae** partly fused (**notarium**); **synsacrum** — last thoracic, lumbar, sacral, anterior caudal (~10–14 vertebrae) fused; a few free caudals + **pygostyle** | **Acoelous** — centra with flat ends, **intervertebral discs** between; formula **cervical 7, thoracic 12, lumbar 7, sacral 4 (fused into the sacrum), caudal ~15–16**; atlas–axis (odontoid process) |
| **Ribs** | **Absent** (only transverse processes) | On **almost all dorsal vertebrae**; some joined to the sternum | With **uncinate processes**; each rib in two parts (vertebral and sternal) — joint between them | 12 pairs — **7 true** (directly to the sternum), **5 false/floating** |
| **Sternum** | Omosternum, episternum, mesosternum, xiphisternum (cartilaginous tip) | Broad, cartilaginous-bony plate, ribs attached; **interclavicle (T-shaped)** | **Large, broad, boat-shaped**, with a ventral **keel (carina)** — flight muscles | Segmented — **manubrium**, **mesosternum** (4 sternebrae), **xiphisternum** (cartilaginous **xiphoid**) |
| **Pectoral girdle** | **Firmisternal** (*Rana* — the two halves fused in the middle); **scapula + suprascapula**, **coracoid**, **clavicle**, cartilaginous **epicoracoid**; **glenoid cavity** | Scapula, suprascapula, **coracoid** (with fenestrae), clavicle, **interclavicle** | **Sword-like scapula**, **strong pillar-like coracoid** (joined to the sternum), **furcula (wishbone)** — both clavicles + interclavicle; the **triosseal canal** between the three | **Scapula** — triangular, with a **spine**, **acromion** and **metacromion** processes; **coracoid** reduced to a **process** of the scapula; **clavicle** small, vestigial (rabbit) |
| **Pelvic girdle** | **V-shaped** — long **ilium**, solid **ischium**, cartilaginous/calcified **pubis**; the three together form the **acetabulum** | Ilium, ischium, pubis; ventrally **pubic and ischial symphyses**; joined to 2 sacral vertebrae | **Large, strong, fused with the synsacrum**; ilium–ischium–pubis fused into the **innominate**; **no ventral symphysis (open pelvis)** — for the passage of **large shelled eggs**; **ilio-ischiatic foramen**; pubis thin, directed backward | Two **innominate (coxal) bones** — ilium, ischium, pubis fused; **pubic symphysis**; large **obturator foramen**; joined to the sacrum |
| **Forelimb** | Humerus, **radio-ulna (fused)**, 6 carpals, **4 digits** (+ vestigial thumb) | Humerus, radius, ulna (separate), carpals, 5 digits, claws | Humerus (pneumatic), radius, ulna, **2 free carpals**, **carpometacarpus**, **3 digits** | Humerus, radius, ulna, 9 carpals, **5 digits** |
| **Hindlimb** | Femur, **tibio-fibula (fused)**, **astragalus and calcaneum elongated** (extra lever for jumping), 5 digits + **prehallux (calcar)**, web | Femur, tibia, fibula, **intertarsal (mesotarsal) joint**, 5 digits | Femur, **tibiotarsus** (tibia + proximal tarsals), **fibula reduced**, **tarsometatarsus**, **intertarsal joint**; **4 toes** — 3 in front, 1 behind (**anisodactyl**); **phalangeal formula of toes 2-3-4-5** | Femur, **tibio-fibula** (fused in the lower part), **patella**, tarsals (**calcaneum — heel**), **4 toes** (**hallux absent**) |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "In *Columba* the pelvic girdle is: (A) Cartilaginous (B) Large, strong and fused with the synsacrum (C) Absent (D) Small and heavy" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

## 8. Combined comparative summary of the classes

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Match List-I (a) Well-developed sinus venosus as a separate chamber (b) Metanephros as the functional adult excretory organ (c) Double circulation with complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood (d) Cloaca receiving the digestive, excretory and reproductive ducts — with List-II (I) Mammalia (II) Pisces (III) Reptilia (IV) Amphibia. Options: (A) a-II, b-IV, c-I, d-III (B) a-IV, b-III, c-I, d-II (C) a-IV, b-II, c-I, d-III (D) a-II, b-III, c-I, d-IV — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**. Reasoning: (b) **metanephros** only in amniotes — among the given classes, Reptilia; (c) complete separation — Mammalia; now Pisces and Amphibia remain for (a) and (d) — the **cloaca** is a character of all amphibians whereas among fishes only cartilaginous fishes have it; and the **sinus venosus** is the distinct (first of the four serially arranged) chamber of the fish heart. Hence a-II, d-IV. (Option B's a-IV is also partly true — the frog too has the sinus venosus as a separate chamber — but then d-II, "cloaca in fishes", is weak as a generalisation.)

## 9. "Identification points" of each type (spotting / practical exam style)

**Frog**
- The **nasal cavity** opens into the roof of the mouth by the **internal nares (choanae)** — the air passage goes through the bucco-pharynx (no secondary palate).
- The **tympanum** is visible on the skin itself; the **Eustachian tube** connects the middle ear with the bucco-pharynx.
- **Fat bodies** — yellow finger-like structures — energy source during **hibernation**.
- In the male, **nuptial pads** and **vocal sacs** in the breeding season; in the female the ovaries fill the body cavity in the breeding season.
- **Bidder's canal** — a longitudinal canal inside the kidney into which the vasa efferentia open.
- Frog **blood** — RBCs large, oval, **nucleated** (larger than in mammals).

***Uromastyx***
- **Short flat tail with spiny whorls** — the most obvious identification; the tail is not autotomised.
- **Acrodont** teeth; front teeth incisor-like.
- **Burrow-dweller** — basks in the sun by day, stays in the burrow at night/in the midday heat; closes the burrow mouth with its tail.
- **Colour change with temperature** — dark on a cold morning (heat absorption), light in the heat (reflection).
- **Hemipenes** (male), **parietal foramen**, **three chambers of the cloaca** (coprodaeum, urodaeum, proctodaeum).
- Food–water adaptations: long intestine + **caecum**, **uric acid**, **nasal salt gland**, reabsorption of water in the cloaca/bladder; water obtained from the food (plants) itself.

**Pigeon**
- **Crop** (pigeon milk), **gizzard** (grit), **no gall bladder**, **bursa of Fabricius** (young).
- **Syrinx** — at the bifurcation of the trachea; **9 air sacs**; lungs non-expansile.
- **Right aortic arch**; RBCs nucleated; kidneys **3-lobed**; **no urinary bladder**.
- **Only the left ovary**; **no penis**.
- Skeleton: **pneumatic bones**, **synsacrum**, **pygostyle**, **keel**, **furcula**, **uncinate processes**, **carpometacarpus**, **tibiotarsus**, **tarsometatarsus**, **open pelvis**, **heterocoelous cervical vertebrae**.
- Sensory: large eyes, **pecten**, nictitating membrane; large cerebellum and optic lobes; small olfactory lobes.
- Identifiable feather types: **quill (contour)**, **down**, **filoplume**.

**Rabbit**
- **Peg teeth** (lagomorph), **diastema**, **no canines**, rootless ever-growing incisors.
- **Sacculus rotundus**, **huge caecum + vermiform appendix**, **coprophagy**.
- **Diaphragm**, **epiglottis**, C-shaped tracheal rings, alveoli.
- **Left aortic arch**, **two precavals**, RBCs non-nucleated, **no renal portal system**.
- **Scrotal sacs** (inguinal canal open), **duplex uterus**, **induced ovulation**, discoidal placenta.
- Brain: **corpus callosum**, **corpora quadrigemina**, **pons**, **flocculus**; large olfactory lobes.
- Skeleton: **acoelous vertebrae**, formula 7-12-7-4-16, **acromion + metacromion on the scapula**, **pubic symphysis**, 4 toes in the hindlimb.
- **Large pinnae** — hearing and **heat loss** (network of blood vessels).

## Traps and exceptions

- The frog has **no teeth on the lower jaw**; the teeth are **not for chewing**.
- The frog has **no salivary glands**; the rabbit has **4 pairs**.
- The rabbit is **not a rodent** — it is a **lagomorph** (peg teeth, 4 incisors above); dental formula **28**, no canines.
- The pigeon has **no gall bladder**; it is present in the frog, *Uromastyx* and rabbit.
- Pigeon milk is produced by **both male and female** (prolactin), not by a mammary gland — by the crop.
- The frog's **urinary bladder is cloacal** — not homologous to the mammalian bladder; the ureters do not open directly into it.
- In the male frog **ureter = urinogenital duct**; in the female the ureter and oviduct are separate.
- The pigeon has **only the left ovary**; **no urinary bladder**; **no penis**.
- **Right** aortic arch in birds, **left** in mammals — the fact most often asked reversed.
- Reptiles (*Uromastyx*) have **both** systemic arches; the carotids arise from the **right** arch.
- The frog has **10** cranial nerves, the other three **12**.
- **Pons and corpus callosum** only in (eutherian) mammals; **corpora quadrigemina** only in mammals (bigemina in the others).
- **The rabbit's uterus is duplex** — two cervices; man's is **simplex**.
- The pigeon's pelvis is **open ventrally** (no symphysis) — for the egg; the rabbit has a **pubic symphysis**.
- Agamids like *Uromastyx* **do not shed the tail** (autotomy — geckos, *Hemidactylus*).

- Pigeon: beak, crop (pigeon milk), proventriculus + gizzard, no gall bladder, bursa of Fabricius.
- Pigeon: parabronchi, 9 air sacs, syrinx; 4-chambered heart, right arch; 3-lobed metanephros, no bladder; only the left ovary.
