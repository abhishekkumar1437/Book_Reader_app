The "Chordates" point of the syllabus has five parts — classification (classes, characters, examples), aquatic adaptations of fishes, origin and evolution of chordates, flight adaptations of birds, and the phylogeny of Prototheria–Metatheria–Eutheria. NCERT 11's "Animal Kingdom" (Chordata part) is the base. UP PGT 2026 asked the largest number of questions from here — *Herdmania* vs *Amphioxus*, matching of chordate classes, assertion–reason on *Scoliodon*, Darwin's frog, parental care in amphibians, Eutheria vs marsupials, the hood of the cobra and economically important mammals.

> **In simple words:** Chordates are animals whose embryo, at least at some stage, has a flexible rod along the back (notochord), a hollow nerve tube above it, gill slits in the throat and a tail behind the anus. The simplest among them are the marine "protochordates"; then come those with a backbone (vertebral column) — fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. Each class has developed special "adaptations" suited to its environment (water, land, air).

## 1. Basic characters of Chordata

| Character | Description | Condition in the adult |
|---|---|---|
| **Notochord** | A dorsal, flexible rod of vacuolated cells formed from mesoderm, with a sheath | Lifelong in Cephalochordata; replaced by the **vertebral column** in vertebrates (remnant — **nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral disc**) |
| **Dorsal, hollow, single nerve cord** | From ectoderm, **above** the alimentary canal | Brain and spinal cord |
| **Pharyngeal gill slits** | Paired openings in the wall of the pharynx | Gills in fishes; in terrestrial forms only in the embryo (Eustachian tube etc.) |
| **Post-anal tail** | Muscular tail behind the anus | In man only in the embryo (remnant — **coccyx**) |
| **Endostyle / thyroid gland** | A groove in the ventral part of the pharynx — binds mucus and iodine | Homologous to the **thyroid** of vertebrates |

| Character | Chordata | Non-chordates |
|---|---|---|
| Notochord | Present | Absent |
| Nerve cord | Dorsal, hollow, single | Ventral, solid, double (ganglionated) |
| Gill slits | Present | Absent |
| Heart | **Ventral** | Dorsal (if present) |
| Post-anal tail | Present | Absent |

## 2. Classification of Chordata — outline

```
Phylum Chordata
├── Subphylum Urochordata (Tunicata)      ┐ Protochordata
├── Subphylum Cephalochordata             ┘ (Acrania)
└── Subphylum Vertebrata (Craniata)
    ├── Division Agnatha (no jaws) — Class Cyclostomata
    └── Division Gnathostomata (with jaws)
        ├── Superclass Pisces — Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes
        └── Superclass Tetrapoda — Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia
```

- **Anamniota** — no embryonic membranes: cyclostomes, fishes, amphibians. **Amniota** — amnion, chorion, allantois, yolk sac: reptiles, birds, mammals.
- **Poikilotherms (ectotherms)** — cyclostomes to reptiles; **homeotherms (endotherms)** — birds, mammals.

## 3. Protochordata

### Subphylum Urochordata (Tunicata) — *Herdmania*

- Marine, adults usually **sessile**; body covered by a **tunic (test)** — **tunicin** (cellulose-like — rare in animals).
- **Notochord only in the tail of the larva** (hence "uro" = tail); in the adult the nerve cord is reduced to a **ganglion**.
- **Tadpole larva** — free-swimming, with all four chordate characters → attaches and undergoes **retrogressive metamorphosis** — tail, notochord, nerve cord, sense organs are lost; the adult is **degenerate, sac-like, a filter feeder**.
- Huge pharynx, countless gill slits (**stigmata**), endostyle; **the direction of the heartbeat reverses repeatedly** (reversal of heartbeat).
- **Vanadium** in the blood (vanadocytes — in some tunicates).
- Examples: ***Herdmania*** (Indian coast — "sea squirt"), ***Ascidia***, ***Salpa*** (free-swimming, alternation of generations), ***Doliolum***, ***Oikopleura*** (Larvacea — **tail and notochord lifelong** — an example of paedogenesis), *Pyrosoma* (bioluminescence).

### Subphylum Cephalochordata — *Branchiostoma* (*Amphioxus*)

- Small (~5–8 cm), fish-like, transparent, burrowed in marine sand — "**lancelet**".
- **Notochord lifelong**, from head to tail — extends **in front of the head (up to the rostrum)** (hence "cephalo").
- All four chordate characters **lifelong** — the "**ideal/basic chordate (archetype)**".
- **Myotomes** (V/< shaped), **atrium** and **atriopore**, **buccal cirri** around the mouth, **wheel organ**, **Hatschek's pit** (homologous to the pituitary), endostyle; **no heart** (contractile vessels), blood colourless; excretion — **protonephridia with solenocytes** (like invertebrates); gonads segmental; **no cranium, vertebrae, paired fins or jaws**.
- Examples: *Branchiostoma lanceolatum*, *Asymmetron*.

### Hemichordata — now a separate phylum

*Balanoglossus* (acorn worm) — proboscis, collar, trunk; **stomochord** — earlier considered a "notochord", now an **anterior extension of the buccal cavity** (not homologous) → NCERT treats it as a **separate non-chordate phylum**; **tornaria larva** (like echinoderm larvae) — evidence of the Chordata–Echinodermata relationship; **gill slits and a dorsal hollow collar nerve cord** are present.

## 4. Subphylum Vertebrata — general characters

- Notochord in the embryo; in the adult a **cartilaginous/bony vertebral column**; brain enclosed in a **cranium**; **ventral muscular heart** (2, 3 or 4 chambers); **paired kidneys** (excretion + osmoregulation); **paired appendages** (fins or limbs); **red blood cells** with haemoglobin; **neural crest** cells — a unique character of vertebrates.
- "**All vertebrates are chordates, but all chordates are not vertebrates**" (NCERT).

### Class Cyclostomata (Division Agnatha)

- **No jaws** — mouth **circular, suctorial** ("cyclo-stome" = circular mouth); body long, eel-like; **no scales or paired fins**; **6–15 pairs of gill slits**; **one nostril** (median); skeleton cartilaginous; heart 2-chambered; **ectoparasites** — on fishes.
- **Live in the sea but migrate to fresh water to spawn** (anadromous) and die a few days after spawning; the larva of the lamprey is the **ammocoete** — **once thought to be a separate animal**; like *Amphioxus*.
- Examples: ***Petromyzon*** (lamprey), ***Myxine*** (hagfish — wholly marine; **osmoconformer** — the only vertebrate).
- **Ostracoderms** — extinct, armoured, jawless — **the oldest vertebrates** (Cambrian–Ordovician).

### Superclass Pisces — fishes

| Character | **Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fishes)** | **Osteichthyes (bony fishes)** |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Almost all **marine** | Marine and freshwater |
| Endoskeleton | **Cartilaginous** (calcified) | **Bony** |
| Scales | **Placoid** (tooth-like — skin like "sandpaper") | **Cycloid / ctenoid** (ganoid — primitive) |
| Mouth | **Ventral** (below) | **Anterior/terminal** |
| Gill slits | **5–7 pairs, opening separately, no cover** | **4 pairs, covered by an operculum (gill cover)** |
| Swim bladder | **Absent** — must **swim continuously** to avoid sinking; oil in the liver (squalene) gives buoyancy | **Usually present** (buoyancy) |
| Caudal fin | **Heterocercal** | Usually **homocercal** |
| Teeth | Modified placoid scales, backwardly directed, **continuously replaced** | — |
| Fertilisation | **Internal** — **claspers** on the pelvic fins of the male; many **viviparous** | Usually **external**; most oviparous |
| Cloaca | **Present** | Usually absent (separate openings) |
| Excretion | **Retain urea** (~2–2.5% in blood) + **TMAO**; **rectal gland** (salt) | Ammonotelic |
| Examples | ***Scoliodon*** (dogfish / shark), ***Pristis*** (sawfish), ***Carcharodon*** (great white shark), ***Trygon*** (**sting ray** — poisonous spine), ***Torpedo*** (**electric ray** — electric organs, up to ~200 V), *Rhincodon* (**whale shark — the largest fish**) | ***Labeo rohita*** (rohu), ***Catla*** (catla), *Cirrhinus mrigala* (mrigal), ***Clarias*** (magur), *Heteropneustes* (singhi), ***Exocoetus*** (**flying fish**), ***Hippocampus*** (**sea horse** — brood pouch in the male), *Betta* (fighting fish), *Pterophyllum* (angel fish), *Anabas* (climbing perch), *Tenualosa ilisha* (hilsa), *Latimeria* (coelacanth) |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): In *Scoliodon*, propulsion during swimming is produced mainly by lateral undulation of the body, not by active flapping of the paired fins. Reason (R): Myotomal muscles arranged in segmental blocks, by alternate contraction on the two sides of the body, generate a wave travelling from head to tail. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **Both true, and R is the correct explanation of A** (option **(A)** in the original booklet). The paired (pectoral and pelvic) fins of the shark mainly provide **balance, steering and lift**; the heterocercal caudal fin and the muscular wave provide propulsion.

**Subclasses of Osteichthyes (B.Sc.):**
- **Actinopterygii (ray-finned)** — most modern fishes (teleosts — rohu, catla).
- **Sarcopterygii (lobe-finned)** — (a) **Crossopterygii** — *Latimeria chalumnae* (**coelacanth**, 1938, South Africa — **living fossil**; *L. menadoensis* from Indonesia in 1998) — **close to the ancestral group of tetrapods**; (b) **Dipnoi (lungfishes)** — ***Protopterus*** (Africa), ***Lepidosiren*** (South America), ***Neoceratodus*** (Australia) — **swim bladder works as a lung**, internal nostrils, aestivation (*Protopterus* in a cocoon in mud).

### Class Amphibia — amphibians

- Life **both in water and on land** (usually water for breeding); **the first tetrapods**.
- Skin **moist, glandular, without scales** (exception: tiny scales hidden in the skin of *Ichthyophis*); **cutaneous respiration** important.
- **2 pairs of limbs** (forelimbs usually with 4 digits, hindlimbs with 5); **no claws**.
- **Heart 3-chambered** (2 auricles + 1 ventricle); **sinus venosus** and **conus arteriosus**; **cloaca** present.
- **Tympanum** = represents the external ear; **eyelids** and **nictitating membrane** on the eyes.
- **Two occipital condyles (dicondylic skull)**; **10 pairs of cranial nerves**.
- **Poikilothermous**, **external fertilisation** (usually), **indirect development** — **tadpole larva → metamorphosis** (controlled by **thyroxine**).
- **Orders:** **Apoda / Gymnophiona** — limbless (**caecilians**) — ***Ichthyophis*** (India — Western Ghats, North-East); **Urodela / Caudata** — tailed — *Salamandra* (salamander), *Triturus*/*Triton* (newt), ***Ambystoma*** (**axolotl larva — paedogenesis/neoteny**), *Necturus*, *Tylototriton verrucosus* (Himalayan salamander — the only salamander genus found in India); **Anura / Salientia** — tailless — ***Rana*** (frog), ***Bufo*** (toad), ***Hyla*** (**tree frog** — adhesive pads on the digits), *Rhacophorus* (**flying frog** — webbed feet), *Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis* (purple frog — Western Ghats).

#### Parental care in amphibians

| Animal | Mode of care |
|---|---|
| ***Alytes obstetricans*** (**midwife toad**) | The **male** carries the string of eggs **wound around its hind legs** |
| ***Pipa pipa*** (**Surinam toad**) | Eggs embedded in pits in the **skin of the female's back**; young toads emerge from there |
| ***Rhinoderma darwinii*** (**Darwin's frog**) | The **male** swallows the eggs/tadpoles and keeps them in its **vocal sac**; after metamorphosis the young frogs come out of the mouth |
| ***Gastrotheca*** (**marsupial frog**) | **Brood pouch** on the **female's** back |
| *Rheobatrachus silus* (extinct) | The female swallowed the eggs and reared them in the **stomach** (stomach acid switched off) |
| *Hyla faber* | The male builds **mud enclosures/pools** |
| *Rhacophorus*, *Polypedates* | **Foam nests** — on leaves above water |
| ***Ichthyophis*** | The **female coils around the eggs** and guards them |
| *Nectophrynoides* | **Viviparous** — development in the uterus |
| *Hylambates*, *Arthroleptis* | Eggs in the mouth |

(A) Reducing reproduction (B) Increasing competition (C) Reducing predation risk (D) Increasing mutation" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)** — eggs/tadpoles in water are most vulnerable to predators and drying; species that provide care lay fewer but larger eggs.

### Class Reptilia — reptiles

- **The first truly terrestrial vertebrates**; skin **dry, with scales/scutes** (epidermal scales, keratin-containing) — **almost no glands**; **moulting of the skin (ecdysis / slough)** — in one piece in snakes.
- Respiration **only by lungs**; **heart usually 3-chambered** (ventricle **incompletely divided**) — **4-chambered in crocodiles (Crocodilia)**.
- **One occipital condyle (monocondylic)**; **12 pairs of cranial nerves**; **metanephric kidneys**, **uricotelic**; cloaca.
- **Internal fertilisation**, males with **hemipenes** (lizards, snakes — paired) or a single penis (turtles, crocodiles); **oviparous**, **cleidoic eggs with calcareous/leathery shells**; **direct development** — embryonic membranes (amnion etc.).
- **Temperature-dependent sex determination (TSD)** — crocodiles, turtles, some lizards.
- Examples: ***Chelone*** (sea turtle), ***Testudo*** (land tortoise), ***Chamaeleon*** (chameleon — colour change, independently moving eyes, long tongue), ***Calotes*** (garden lizard), ***Crocodylus*** (crocodile), ***Alligator***, *Gavialis gangeticus* (**gharial** — Ganga, Chambal), ***Hemidactylus*** (house lizard/gecko — **autotomy**), ***Uromastyx*** (spiny-tailed lizard), *Draco* (**flying lizard**), *Varanus* (monitor lizard), ***Sphenodon*** (**tuatara** — New Zealand, **living fossil**, distinct parietal eye).
- **Venomous snakes:** ***Naja*** (**cobra** — neurotoxin), ***Bungarus*** (**krait** — neurotoxin), ***Vipera russelli* / *Daboia russelii*** (**Russell's viper** — **haemotoxin**), *Echis carinatus* (**saw-scaled viper**) — India's "**Big Four**"; *Ophiophagus hannah* (**king cobra** — the longest venomous snake, eats snakes). **Non-venomous:** *Python*, *Ptyas* (rat snake); **vestiges of hind limbs** in the python.
- **Antivenom** — **polyvalent**, raised in horses (against the Big Four).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): The cobra can spread its neck to form a hood. Reason (R): The hood is supported by elongated ribs. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **Both true, R is the correct explanation of A** (option **(B)** in the original booklet). The **cervical ribs of the neck are long** and are raised forward and outward by special muscles, so the loose skin between them is stretched into a hood.

**Subclasses based on temporal fossae (B.Sc.):**

| Subclass | Temporal openings | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Anapsida** | **None** | Cotylosaurs (stem reptiles, extinct), **turtles** (traditionally; molecular evidence places them among diapsids) |
| **Synapsida** | **One lower** | Pelycosaurs, **therapsids** — **ancestors of mammals** (mammals are also synapsids) |
| **Euryapsida (Parapsida)** | **One upper** | Ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs (extinct marine) |
| **Diapsida** | **Two** | *Sphenodon*, lizards, snakes (modified), crocodiles, **dinosaurs**, pterosaurs, **birds** (descendants) |

### Class Aves — birds

- **Feathers** — only in birds (epidermal, β-keratin); **forelimbs modified into wings**; **scales** on the hind limbs (reptilian heritage), claws.
- **Beak** — **no teeth**; **skin dry, without glands** — only an **oil gland (preen / uropygial gland)** at the base of the tail.
- **Pneumatic bones**, **air sacs**, **4-chambered heart**, **homeothermous**, **right aortic arch**.
- **Crop** and **gizzard** in the alimentary canal; **no urinary bladder**; **only the left ovary** is functional; **oviparous**, large yolky (**polylecithal / telolecithal**) shelled eggs; **monocondylic** skull; 12 pairs of cranial nerves.
- **Classification (B.Sc.):** Subclass **Archaeornithes** — ***Archaeopteryx*** (Jurassic, ~150 million years, Germany — teeth, long bony tail, 3 clawed fingers on the wings + feathers and furcula → **reptile–bird connecting link**); Subclass **Neornithes** — (a) **Palaeognathae (Ratitae)** — **flightless**, **no keel** on the sternum, no hooks in the feathers — ***Struthio*** (**ostrich** — the largest living bird, 2 toes), *Rhea* (South America), *Dromaius* (**emu** — Australia), *Casuarius* (cassowary), ***Apteryx*** (**kiwi** — New Zealand; the largest egg relative to body size, nostrils at the tip of the beak); (b) **Neognathae (Carinatae)** — flying birds, **keel (carina) on the sternum** — ***Corvus*** (crow), ***Columba*** (pigeon), ***Psittacula*** (parrot), ***Pavo*** (peacock), *Neophron* (vulture), ***Aptenodytes*** (**penguin** — flightless but has a keel — wings are flippers for swimming), *Bubo* (owl), *Ardea* (heron).

### Class Mammalia — mammals

- **Mammary glands** — feeding the young with milk; **hair** — on the skin; **sweat and sebaceous (oil) glands** in the skin.
- **External ear / pinna**; **3 ossicles in the middle ear** (malleus, incus, stapes) — lower jaw of only **one bone (dentary)**; **dicondylic skull**; **secondary palate**.
- **Heterodont**, **thecodont**, **diphyodont** teeth.
- **Muscular diaphragm** — separates thorax and abdomen; **4-chambered heart**, **left aortic arch**; **RBCs without nucleus** (exception: **oval** but still non-nucleated in camel and llama); **metanephric kidneys**, **ureotelic**; 12 pairs of cranial nerves; **large cerebrum**, (in Eutheria) **corpus callosum**.
- **Cervical vertebrae usually 7** (exceptions: manatee 6, sloth 6–10).
- **Viviparous** (Prototheria are the exception), homeothermous.
- Examples: oviparous — ***Ornithorhynchus*** (**duck-billed platypus**); viviparous — ***Macropus*** (**kangaroo**), ***Pteropus*** (**flying fox** — fruit bat), ***Camelus*** (camel), ***Macaca*** (monkey), ***Rattus*** (rat), ***Canis*** (dog), ***Felis*** (cat), ***Elephas*** (elephant), ***Equus*** (horse), ***Delphinus*** (**dolphin**), ***Balaenoptera*** (**blue whale** — the largest animal), ***Panthera tigris*** (tiger), ***Panthera leo*** (lion).

## 5. Aquatic adaptations in fishes

| Adaptation | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Streamlined body** | Spindle-shaped — pointed head, broad trunk, tapering tail; no neck — less water resistance |
| **Scales and mucus** | Scales give protection; the **mucous glands** of the skin — reduce friction, protect from bacteria/fungi, osmotic barrier |
| **Fins** | **Paired** — pectoral and pelvic — balance, steering, braking; **unpaired** — dorsal, anal — stability (prevent rolling); **caudal** — **propulsion** and rudder |
| **Myotome muscles** | Segmental W-shaped muscle blocks — alternate contraction produces a lateral wave — the main propulsive force |
| **Gills and countercurrent exchange** | In the gill lamellae blood and water flow in **opposite directions** (**countercurrent**) → up to ~80% of the O₂ in water is extracted; one-way water flow by pumping of the operculum and mouth; **ram ventilation** in fast swimmers (tuna) |
| **Swim / air bladder** | A dorsal outgrowth of the pharynx/oesophagus; **buoyancy** — the fish can stay at any depth without spending energy; **physostomous** (swim bladder connected to the pharynx by a duct — carp, catfish, eel) and **physoclistous** (no duct — gas secreted from blood by the **gas gland** and **rete mirabile**, absorbed by the **oval** — perch); in some it aids **hearing** (**Weberian ossicles** — carp, catfish), **sound production**, respiration (lungfish) |
| **Lateral line system** | Canals under the skin containing **neuromasts** (hair cell + cupula) — sense **water vibrations, pressure and currents** ("distant touch"); detect prey/enemy even in darkness; also in amphibian tadpoles, not in terrestrial adults |
| **Ampullae of Lorenzini** | In sharks/rays — **electroreceptors** — sense the weak electric fields of prey |
| **Osmoregulation** | **Freshwater teleosts** — body hypertonic → water enters → **large volume of very dilute urine** (large glomeruli), do not drink water, **chloride cells** of the gills **absorb salts**; **marine teleosts** — body hypotonic → water is lost → **drink sea water**, chloride cells of the gills **excrete salts**, **small amount of concentrated urine** (glomeruli small/absent — **aglomerular kidney**, e.g. *Hippocampus*, toadfish); **marine elasmobranchs** — retain **urea + TMAO** in the blood and remain almost **isotonic** with the sea (slightly hypertonic), salts excreted by the **rectal gland** |
| **Eyes** | **Spherical lens**, no eyelids (nictitating membrane in sharks), the lens moves back and forth for distant vision |
| **Colour and camouflage** | **Countershading** — dark back, silvery belly; pigment cells (chromatophores) |
| **Special organs** | **Electric organs** (*Torpedo*, *Electrophorus* — electric eel ~600 V — modified muscles), **bioluminescence** (deep-sea fishes — *Photoblepharon*), **venomous spines** (*Trygon*, stonefish) |
| **Reproductive adaptations** | Enormous numbers of eggs (external fertilisation); **parental care** — **brood pouch of the male *Hippocampus***, mouth-brooding (*Tilapia*), nests (*Gasterosteus* — stickleback); **migration** — **anadromous** (sea → river to spawn: **salmon, hilsa**, lamprey) and **catadromous** (river → sea: **eel *Anguilla*** — Sargasso Sea) |

### Accessory respiratory organs — air-breathing fishes

Fishes of swampy/warm water low in O₂ also breathe air; some **drown** if they are not allowed to come to the surface.

| Fish | Accessory organ |
|---|---|
| ***Clarias batrachus*** (magur) | **Arborescent / dendritic (tree-like) organ** — from the second and fourth gill arches, in the **suprabranchial chamber** |
| ***Heteropneustes fossilis*** (singhi) | **Air sacs** — long tubular sacs, from the gill chamber through the muscles to the tail |
| ***Anabas testudineus*** (**climbing perch / koi**) | **Labyrinthine organ** — folded plates from the first gill arch in the suprabranchial chamber; can even walk on land |
| ***Channa*** (*Ophiocephalus* — **saul/murrel**) | Folded, vascular wall of the **suprabranchial chamber** |
| ***Amphipnous cuchia*** (*Monopterus cuchia* — cuchia) | **Lateral pharyngeal air sacs** — gills almost reduced |
| *Periophthalmus* (**mudskipper**) | Bucco-pharyngeal lining and skin; dips the tail in water — "walks" on tidal mud |
| *Saccobranchus*, *Boleophthalmus* | Air sacs, skin |
| Lungfishes (*Protopterus*, *Lepidosiren*, *Neoceratodus*) | **Swim bladder = lung** (*Neoceratodus* single-lobed, the others bilobed) |

## 6. Origin of chordates

The ancestors of chordates were soft-bodied, so fossils are rare; theories of origin are based on **comparative anatomy, embryology and biochemistry**.

| Theory | Proposed by | Essence | Objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Annelid theory** | **Dohrn (1875)**, **Semper (1875)** | Turn an annelid upside down and the ventral nerve cord becomes dorsal, the heart ventral — both have metamerism, a true coelom | Annelid nerve cord solid, double; position of the mouth; no notochord |
| **Arachnid theory** | **Gaskell (1890)**, **Patten** | From *Limulus*/scorpion-like forms; similarity to the armour of ostracoderms | No embryological basis |
| **Nemertean theory** | **Hubrecht (1883)** | The **proboscis sheath** of nemerteans = notochord | Inconsistent |
| **Hemichordate theory** | Bateson | From *Balanoglossus* | Stomochord not homologous |
| **Echinoderm theory / dipleurula theory** | **Garstang (1894, 1928)**, Bury, Berrill | The larvae of echinoderms (**auricularia**), hemichordates (**tornaria**) and chordates are very similar — a common **dipleurula** ancestor; the **ciliated bands** of the auricularia folded dorsally to form the nerve tube | — (**generally accepted**) |
| **Larval / paedomorphosis theory** | **Garstang (1928)** | The **ascidian tadpole larva** gave up becoming a sessile adult and began **reproducing in the larval stage itself** (**neoteny / paedomorphosis**) → the free-swimming chordate line | — (widely accepted; recent molecular phylogenies consider tunicates the **closest relatives** of vertebrates) |
| Calcichordate theory | **Jefferies (1986)** | From extinct calcichordates (carpoid echinoderms) | Disputed |

**Evidence for the echinoderm–chordate relationship:** both are **deuterostomes** — **radial indeterminate cleavage**, **blastopore → anus**, **enterocoelous coelom**, **endoskeleton from mesoderm**; **similarity of larvae**; biochemical — **phosphagen** — **creatine phosphate** in (some) echinoderms and chordates, arginine phosphate in most invertebrates; similarity of **serum proteins**.

### Sequence of vertebrate evolution (geological)

| Period | Event |
|---|---|
| **Cambrian** (~540–485 million years) | First chordate fossils — *Pikaia* (Burgess Shale), *Haikouichthys*, *Myllokunmingia* (China — early vertebrates) |
| **Ordovician–Silurian** | **Ostracoderms** (jawless, armoured) — the first vertebrate group; in the Silurian the **first jawed vertebrates — acanthodians, placoderms** |
| **Devonian** (~419–359 million) | "**Age of fishes**"; placoderms, cartilaginous and bony fishes; **lobe-finned fishes** (*Eusthenopteron*) → ***Tiktaalik*** (~375 million; "**fishapod**" — neck, wrist-like bones) → ***Acanthostega***, ***Ichthyostega*** (first tetrapods) |
| **Carboniferous** | "**Age of amphibians**" — **labyrinthodonts**; in the late Carboniferous the **first amniotes — cotylosaurs (stem reptiles)** — *Hylonomus*; ***Seymouria*** — amphibian–reptile connecting link (Permian) |
| **Permian** | Synapsids (pelycosaurs) → **therapsids**; at the end of the Permian the **largest mass extinction** (~252 million) |
| **Triassic** | **Dinosaurs** and the **first mammals** (~225–200 million — *Morganucodon*, *Eozostrodon*) — from **cynodont therapsids** |
| **Jurassic** | "**Age of reptiles**" (Mesozoic); ***Archaeopteryx*** (~150 million); **split of Theria** (*Juramaia* ~160 million — early eutherian) |
| **Cretaceous** | Flowering plants; at the end (~66 million) **extinction of dinosaurs** (K-Pg; meteorite — Chicxulub) |
| **Cenozoic (Tertiary–Quaternary)** | "**Age of mammals**"; **adaptive radiation** of birds and mammals; man (Quaternary) |

## 7. Flight adaptations in birds

| Category | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Body shape** | **Streamlined, spindle-shaped** body; smooth surface due to feathers; small head, flexible neck (feeding and preening with the beak) |
| **Feathers** | **Light, strong, insulating**; **contour feather** — **quill/calamus** + **rachis** + **vane** (**barbs** → **barbules** bearing **hooklets (barbicels)** — interlock to form an air-tight surface); **remiges** (flight feathers — on the wing: **primaries** — on the hand, **secondaries** — on the forearm), **rectrices** (tail feathers — rudder, brake); **down feathers** (insulation), **filoplumes** (hair feathers — sensory); **moulting** — gradual, so that flight is maintained |
| **Forelimbs → wings** | Digits reduced to 3 (II, III, IV — embryological evidence; I, II, III in traditional texts); **carpometacarpus** (fusion of wrist–hand bones); the wing has an **aerofoil** section — convex above → **lift**; **alula (bastard wing)** — on the thumb — control at low speed |
| **Skeleton — lightness** | **Pneumatic bones** — hollow, with **extensions of air sacs** inside and cross **struts (trabeculae)** — light but strong; skull bones thin, sutures fused; **no teeth or heavy jaws** — a light **beak**; grinding done by the gizzard (near the centre of gravity) |
| **Skeleton — rigidity (fusion)** | **Synsacrum** — fusion of the last thoracic, lumbar, sacral and first caudal vertebrae, **joined to the pelvic girdle** — a firm base during flight/landing; **pygostyle (ploughshare bone)** — the last caudal vertebrae — base for the tail feathers; **uncinate processes on the ribs** — strengthen the thorax; **furcula (wishbone)** — fusion of both clavicles (+ interclavicle) — **spring-like**, keeps the pectoral girdle apart; **tibiotarsus**, **tarsometatarsus** |
| **Sternum and flight muscles** | **Large keel (carina) on the sternum** — for **attachment of the flight muscles**; **pectoralis major** — **downstroke** — up to ~15–25% of body weight; **supracoracoideus (pectoralis minor)** — **upstroke** — arises from the keel and its tendon passes through the **triosseal canal** — between the coracoid, scapula and furcula — like a **pulley** to the upper part of the humerus; both muscles lie **low** (low centre of gravity → stability) |
| **Respiration** | **Air sacs** — **9** (7–12 depending on the bird) — **thin-walled extensions of the lungs**, reaching into the bones; lungs small, spongy, **non-expansile**, with **parabronchi** (no alveoli) — **one-way flow** of air — fresh air in the lungs **during both inspiration and expiration** ("**double respiration**"); heat loss, lighter body; ventilation by wing movement during flight |
| **Circulation and metabolism** | **4-chambered heart**, **complete double circulation**, fast heart rate (sparrow ~460/min at rest; hummingbird up to ~1200); more RBCs; **high metabolic rate**, **homeothermous** (body temperature ~40–42 °C) — continuous energy |
| **Digestion** | **High-energy** food (seeds, insects, fruits); **rapid digestion** (a few hours); **crop** (storage — no need to keep pecking) and **gizzard**; **short rectum** — faeces passed out at once (less weight) |
| **Excretion** | **Uricotelic** — semi-solid **uric acid** (less water, less weight); **no urinary bladder** (ostrich is an exception — stores urine) |
| **Reproduction** | **Only the left ovary and oviduct** — less weight; gonads enlarge only in the breeding season; eggs laid outside (no embryo weight in the body) |
| **Nervous system and sense organs** | **Cerebellum** — large — **balance and muscular coordination**; **optic lobes** large; **eyes large**, **pecten** (nourishes the retina), **nictitating membrane**, double accommodation (both lens and cornea), sharp vision (vulture, hawk); **olfactory lobes reduced** (smell is of little use to a flying animal) |
| **Hind limbs** | **Perching mechanism** — as soon as the bird sits on a branch, body weight pulls the **flexor tendons of the toes** and the toes close automatically — it does not fall even when asleep; shock absorption on landing |

> **Memory trick:** Flight = "**Light–Strong–Warm–Fast**": **light** (pneumatic bones, beak, one ovary, no bladder), **strong** (synsacrum, keel, furcula, uncinate processes), **warm** (homeothermy, feathers), **fast** (air sacs, 4-chambered heart, high metabolism).

## 8. Phylogeny of Prototheria, Metatheria and Eutheria

### Origin of mammals

- Mammals arose from the **synapsid** line — Permian **pelycosaurs** → **therapsids** → **cynodonts** (Triassic; *Cynognathus*, *Thrinaxodon* — heterodont, secondary palate, enlargement of the dentary) → **first mammals** — late Triassic (~200–220 million years) — small, insectivorous, nocturnal (*Morganucodon*, *Megazostrodon*).
- **Change of the jaw joint** — in reptiles an **articular–quadrate** joint; in mammals **dentary–squamosal**; articular → **malleus**, quadrate → **incus** (in the middle ear) — **Reichert–Gaupp theory**; stapes = the **columella** of reptiles/amphibians.
- In the Jurassic mammals split into two branches — **Prototheria** (oviparous, primitive) and **Theria**; Theria then into **Metatheria** and **Eutheria** (~160 million years, late Jurassic — *Juramaia sinensis* early eutherian; *Sinodelphys* ~125 million early metatherian).

### Explanation of phylogeny and geographical distribution

- **Prototheria** — a mixture of reptilian characters (eggs, cloaca, bones of the pectoral girdle, low body temperature) and mammalian characters (hair, milk, diaphragm, 3 ear ossicles, dentary jaw) → **a living reptile–mammal link**; but they are **not** the direct ancestors of modern mammals — rather **a distinct side branch that separated early**.
- **Metatheria** — probably originated in **North America/Asia** (Cretaceous) → South America → **Antarctica** (then connected and warm) → **Australia** (before the break-up of **Gondwana**). When Australia became **isolated**, eutherians did not reach it (bats and rodents later, the dingo with man) → **adaptive radiation** of marsupials — **NCERT's example: Australian marsupials**.
- **Convergent evolution** — Australian marsupials and eutherians of other continents have similar forms in similar habitats: **Tasmanian wolf ↔ wolf**, **marsupial mole ↔ mole**, **bandicoot ↔ rabbit**, **sugar glider ↔ flying squirrel**, **numbat (anteater) ↔ anteater**, **marsupial mouse ↔ mouse**, **wombat ↔ marmot**.
- **Eutheria** — long gestation through the placenta → larger brain, better-developed young → superior in competition; wherever eutherians arrived (e.g. South America — the **Great American Biotic Interchange**, ~3 million years ago via the Panama land bridge), most marsupials became extinct.

## Traps and exceptions

- In *Herdmania* (urochordate) the notochord is **only in the larval tail**; in *Amphioxus* it is **lifelong, up to the head**.
- In *Oikopleura* (larvacean) the tail and notochord are **lifelong** — an exception among tunicates.
- *Balanoglossus* is **no longer a chordate** (stomochord ≠ notochord).
- **Hagfish** — the only vertebrate that is an **osmoconformer**.
- **Sharks have no swim bladder**; in **lungfishes the swim bladder has become a lung**.
- The paired fins of sharks **do not propel** — the tail and myotomes do.
- **Marine teleosts drink water**, freshwater ones **do not**.
- **Penguins are flightless but carinate** (keel present); **ratites** have no keel.
- **Ostrich has a urinary bladder** — an exception among birds.
- **The crocodile heart is 4-chambered** — an exception among reptiles (**foramen of Panizza** — between the two aortic arches).
- **Epipubic bones are also present in monotremes** — not only "marsupial".
- **Prototheria have no teats** and **no pinna**; **both Metatheria and Prototheria lack a corpus callosum**.
- **Camel RBCs are oval** but **non-nucleated**.
- **Turtles are traditionally anapsid** — molecular phylogeny shows them to be diapsids.

## Quick revision

- 4 chordate characters: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail (+ endostyle).
- Urochordata — notochord in the larval tail; retrogressive metamorphosis; tunicin.
- Cephalochordata — *Amphioxus*, lifelong notochord up to the head.
- Cyclostomes — no jaws, round suctorial mouth, one nostril; lamprey larva ammocoete.
- Chondrichthyes — placoid scales, ventral mouth, 5–7 open gill slits, no swim bladder, claspers, urea retention.
- Osteichthyes — operculum, swim bladder, cycloid/ctenoid scales.
- Coelacanth (1938) — living fossil; lungfishes — *Protopterus*, *Lepidosiren*, *Neoceratodus*.
- Amphibians — 3-chambered heart, 10 cranial nerves, dicondylic skull; *Ichthyophis* — limbless.
- Darwin's frog — male's vocal sac; *Alytes* — on the male's legs; *Pipa* — female's back.
- Reptiles — scaly skin, monocondylic, 12 cranial nerves, cleidoic eggs; crocodile 4-chambered.
- Big Four — cobra, krait (neurotoxin), Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper (haemotoxin).
- Temporal fossae: anapsid (0), synapsid (1 lower — mammal ancestors), euryapsid (1 upper), diapsid (2).
- *Archaeopteryx* — Jurassic, reptile–bird link.
- Mammals — mammary glands, hair, 3 ear ossicles, diaphragm, non-nucleated RBCs, left aortic arch.
- Accessory respiration: *Clarias* — arborescent; *Heteropneustes* — air sacs; *Anabas* — labyrinthine; *Channa* — suprabranchial chamber; cuchia — pharyngeal sacs.
- Physoclistous swim bladder — gas gland + rete mirabile; oval.
- Garstang — dipleurula/auricularia and the paedomorphosis theory of the ascidian larva.
- Flight: pneumatic bones, synsacrum, pygostyle, keel, furcula, uncinate processes, 9 air sacs, triosseal canal.
- Prototheria — oviparous, cloaca, no teats, no pinna; Metatheria — pouch, yolk-sac placenta; Eutheria — chorio-allantoic placenta, corpus callosum.
- Australian marsupials — adaptive radiation + convergent evolution.
