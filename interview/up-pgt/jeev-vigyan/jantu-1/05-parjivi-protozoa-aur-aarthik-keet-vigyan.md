In the "Non-Chordates" point of the syllabus, "parasitic protozoa" and "economic importance of insects" are two separate lines; both are directly linked to disease, agriculture and the economy, so they are exam favourites. From NCERT 12 ("Human Health and Disease", "Animal Husbandry") up to B.Sc. economic zoology. UP PGT 2026 asked about transmission of *Entamoeba*, a *Plasmodium*–*Wuchereria* case question, matching of the silk–honey–lac industries and a question on classes of insecticides.

> **In simple words:** Some unicellular organisms live inside us and cause disease — malaria (*Plasmodium*), dysentery (*Entamoeba*), sleeping sickness (*Trypanosoma*), kala-azar (*Leishmania*). Many of them are carried from one person to another by insects (mosquito, sandfly, tsetse fly). But insects are not only enemies — they also give honey, silk, lac and pollination.

## Part A — Parasitic protozoa

### Basic terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Definitive / primary host** | The host in which the **sexual** stage or adult form of the parasite occurs — for *Plasmodium* the **female *Anopheles*** |
| **Intermediate / secondary host** | The host in which the **asexual** stage occurs — for *Plasmodium* **man** |
| **Vector** | One that carries the pathogen from one host to another — **biological vector** (the parasite develops/multiplies in it — mosquito) and **mechanical vector** (only carries it — housefly) |
| **Reservoir host** | One in which the parasite lives and which is a source for man — wild antelopes/cattle for *T. rhodesiense*; dogs for *Leishmania* (in some regions) |
| **Monogenetic / digenetic** | Whole cycle in one host (*Entamoeba*, *Giardia*) / two hosts (*Plasmodium*, *Trypanosoma*, *Leishmania*) |

### 1. *Plasmodium* — the malaria parasite

**Classification:** Phylum **Apicomplexa** (traditional: Protozoa, class Sporozoa) → Class **Sporozoa (Aconoidasida)** → Order Haemosporida → *Plasmodium*.

| Species | Malaria | Fever cycle (erythrocytic cycle) | Special |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***P. vivax*** | **Benign tertian** | **48 hours** | Most common in India (earlier); **hypnozoites → relapse**; RBC enlarged, **Schüffner's dots**; prefers young RBCs (reticulocytes) |
| ***P. falciparum*** | **Malignant tertian**, **cerebral malaria** | **36–48 hours** | **Most deadly**; RBCs of any age; several rings in one RBC; **crescent/banana-shaped gametocytes**; **Maurer's dots**; infected RBCs stick to the walls of capillaries (**cytoadherence**, "knobs") → blockage in the brain; **blackwater fever**; **no** hypnozoites |
| ***P. malariae*** | **Quartan** | **72 hours** | Old RBCs; **band-form** trophozoite; **Ziemann's dots**; kidney damage (nephrotic); recrudescence years later |
| ***P. ovale*** | **Ovale tertian (mild)** | 48 hours | RBC oval with fimbriated edges; **James's dots**; hypnozoites — relapse; mainly West Africa |
| *P. knowlesi* | **Quotidian** | **24 hours** | Parasite of monkeys (macaques), also in man in South-East Asia — "**the fifth human malaria parasite**" |

#### In man (asexual cycle — schizogony)

1. **Infection** — when an infected **female *Anopheles*** bites, **sporozoites** — long, spindle-shaped — enter the blood with the saliva; within ~30–60 minutes they disappear from the blood into **liver cells (hepatocytes)**.
2. **Pre-erythrocytic / exo-erythrocytic (liver) schizogony** — discovered by **Shortt and Garnham (1948)**; in the liver cell sporozoite → **cryptozoite** → multinucleate **schizont** → thousands of **merozoites (cryptomerozoites)**; **no symptoms** — this is the **incubation period** (~8–14 days; shorter in *P. falciparum*).
3. In ***P. vivax* and *P. ovale*** some sporozoites remain **dormant** in the liver — **hypnozoites** — described by **Krotoski (1980/82)**; they become active months later causing **relapse**.
4. **Erythrocytic schizogony** — the **Golgi cycle**: merozoite into an RBC → **signet ring stage** (nucleus on one side, a vacuole in the middle) → **amoeboid trophozoite** (feeds on haemoglobin; globin is digested and the toxic haem → **haemozoin (malaria pigment)** — dark brown, insoluble) → **schizont** → **merozoites** (12–24 in *P. vivax*) — **rosette stage** → the RBC bursts.
5. **When the RBCs burst**, merozoites + **haemozoin and other toxins** enter the blood → **chills and shivering (cold stage) → high fever (hot stage) → sweating (sweating stage)** — **every 48 hours** (vivax) — all RBCs burst **synchronously**.
6. **Gametocytes** — some merozoites form **male (microgametocyte)** and **female (macrogametocyte)** gametocytes in RBCs — **these do not develop further in man**; they wait to be sucked up by a mosquito.

- Symptoms: periodic fever, anaemia (destruction of RBCs), **enlargement of the spleen (splenomegaly)** and **liver** — hence "**hepatosplenomegaly**".

#### In the mosquito (sexual cycle — gamogony + sporogony)

1. The female *Anopheles* sucks blood → only the **gametocytes** survive in the mosquito's **stomach (midgut)** (the other stages are digested).
2. **Gametogenesis** — the male gametocyte forms **8 flagellated microgametes** by **exflagellation** (triggered by a fall in temperature and xanthurenic acid); one **macrogamete** from the female.
3. **Fertilisation** → diploid **zygote** — **the only diploid stage of the life cycle** (all others are haploid).
4. Zygote → motile, elongated **ookinete** → bores through the stomach wall and forms an **oocyst** on the outer surface.
5. **Sporogony** — in the oocyst **meiosis** (immediately after the zygote — **zygotic meiosis**) followed by many mitotic divisions → **thousands of sporozoites** (up to 10,000 in one oocyst) → the oocyst bursts → sporozoites travel through the haemocoel to the **salivary glands** → into man at the next bite.
6. The cycle in the mosquito takes ~**10–14 days** (depending on temperature) — the **extrinsic incubation period**.

#### Discovery and history

| Scientist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Charles Laveran (1880)** | Discovered the malaria parasite in a patient's blood (Algeria); Nobel 1907 |
| **Camillo Golgi (1885–86)** | Link between erythrocytic schizogony and the periodicity of fever |
| **Ronald Ross (1897)** | **Oocysts in the stomach of a mosquito (*Anopheles*, "dapple-winged")** — **Secunderabad** (India); complete cycle of bird malaria (*P. relictum*) in *Culex* (Kolkata, 1898); **Nobel 1902**; 20 August = **World Mosquito Day** |
| **Grassi, Bignami, Bastianelli (1898)** | Complete cycle of human malaria in *Anopheles* |
| **Shortt and Garnham (1948)** | Liver (pre-erythrocytic) stage |
| **Krotoski (1982)** | Hypnozoites |
| **Tu Youyou (Nobel 2015)** | **Artemisinin** (*Artemisia annua* — "qinghaosu") |

#### Treatment, prevention, vaccines

- **Quinine** — bark of *Cinchona*; **chloroquine** (erythrocytic stage; resistance widespread in *P. falciparum*); **primaquine** — destroys **hypnozoites and gametocytes** (**radical cure**; caution in G6PD deficiency); **ACT** (artemisinin-based combination therapy) — standard for *P. falciparum*; tafenoquine (single dose, hypnozoites).
- **Sickle-cell trait (HbAS)**, **G6PD deficiency**, **Duffy-negative blood group** (*P. vivax* needs the Duffy antigen to enter RBCs) — natural resistance to malaria (balanced polymorphism).
- **Vaccines** — **RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix)** — WHO recommendation **2021**, the first malaria vaccine (based on the circumsporozoite protein — CSP — of *P. falciparum*); **R21/Matrix-M** — WHO recommendation **2023** (Oxford; manufactured by the **Serum Institute of India**).
- Prevention — mosquito nets (**insecticide-treated — LLIN**), **Gambusia** fish (larvivorous), indoor residual spraying (IRS), removal of stagnant water; India's goal is **malaria elimination by 2030** (National Framework 2016–2030).

### 2. *Entamoeba histolytica* — amoebic dysentery

- **Discovery:** **Lösch (1875)**, named by **Schaudinn (1903)**.
- Habitat: **large intestine (colon)** of man; **monogenetic** — no vector/intermediate host.
- **Three stages:**
  - **Trophozoite (magna form)** — large (20–30 μm), pathogenic, **tissue-invading**; dissolves the mucosa with **histolysin** (protease) producing **flask-shaped ulcers**; ingested **RBCs** are seen (diagnostic sign); one lobopodium; nucleus with a central **karyosome** and fine peripheral chromatin ("cartwheel").
  - **Minuta form (precystic)** — small, non-pathogenic, in the intestinal lumen, feeds on bacteria.
  - **Cyst** — round, 10–15 μm; **mature cyst quadrinucleate (4 nuclei)** — **the infective stage**; **chromatoid bodies** (rod-shaped, blunt ends) and a glycogen vacuole.
- **Transmission:** **faeco-oral route** — **food and water contaminated with faeces**; **housefly and cockroach** are **mechanical carriers** of cysts; infected but symptomless **carriers (cyst passers)** are the main source.
- Cyst in the intestine → **excystation** (in the lower part of the small intestine) → one quadrinucleate amoeba → **8 small metacystic trophozoites** → large intestine.
- **Disease:** **amoebiasis / amoebic dysentery** — abdominal cramps, **stools with mucus and blood**; complication — **amoebic liver abscess** — "**anchovy sauce**" pus, lungs, brain.
- Diagnosis — trophozoites containing RBCs or quadrinucleate cysts in stool; treatment — **metronidazole**, tinidazole; for cysts **diloxanide furoate**.

| Species | Habitat | Cyst nuclei | Pathogenic? |
|---|---|---|---|
| *E. histolytica* | Large intestine | **4** | Yes |
| *E. coli* | Large intestine | **8** | No (commensal) |
| *E. gingivalis* | **Mouth (gums)** | **No cyst** — by kissing/droplets | Associated with pyorrhoea (not the cause) |
| *E. dispar* | Large intestine | 4 (like *E. histolytica*) | No — molecularly different |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "The primary route of transmission of *Entamoeba histolytica* infection is: (A) Direct skin contact (B) Airborne droplets (C) Contaminated food and water (D) Insect vectors" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)**. Flies can carry cysts only mechanically; they are not (biological) "vectors".

### 3. *Trypanosoma* — sleeping sickness and Chagas disease

- Class **Mastigophora/Zoomastigophora**, order Kinetoplastida. Body leaf-shaped, **one flagellum** that runs along the edge of an **undulating membrane** and is free in front; at its base the **kinetoplast** (DNA-rich part of the large mitochondrion).
- **Extracellular in blood plasma** (**not** inside RBCs) — *T. cruzi* is the exception (amastigotes in tissue cells).
- **Antigenic variation** — repeatedly changing the surface **VSG (Variant Surface Glycoprotein)** → escapes immunity; **waves** of parasitaemia; difficult to make a vaccine.

| Species | Disease | Vector | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***T. brucei gambiense*** | **West African (Gambian) sleeping sickness** — chronic; **Winterbottom's sign** (enlarged posterior cervical lymph nodes); finally CNS → sleepiness, coma | **Tsetse fly *Glossina palpalis*** (riverine) | West/Central Africa |
| ***T. b. rhodesiense*** | **East African sleeping sickness** — acute, fatal | ***Glossina morsitans*** (savanna) | East Africa; wild animal reservoir |
| *T. b. brucei* | **Nagana** of cattle | *Glossina* | — |
| ***T. cruzi*** | **Chagas disease** — heart (cardiomyopathy), **Romaña's sign** (swelling around the eye) | **Reduviid/kissing bug (*Triatoma*, *Rhodnius*)** — infection **through faeces** at the bite site (**stercorarian** transmission) | South/Central America |
| *T. evansi* | **Surra** — horses, camels, cattle; **in India** | **Tabanus (horse-fly)** — mechanical | India, Asia |

- **Morphological forms:** **trypomastigote** (flagellar base behind the nucleus, long undulating membrane — in blood), **epimastigote** (base in front of the nucleus — in the insect), **promastigote** (anterior, no undulating membrane — *Leishmania* in the insect), **amastigote** (round, no external flagellum — inside cells).
- Cycle in the tsetse fly: midgut → salivary gland (**metacyclic trypomastigote** — infective); **salivarian transmission**.

### 4. *Leishmania* — kala-azar

- Discovery: **Leishman and Donovan (1903)** — both independently; the amastigotes of the parasite are called **LD bodies (Leishman–Donovan bodies)**.
- **Digenetic** — man (and dogs, rodents) and the **sandfly *Phlebotomus*** (Old World) / *Lutzomyia* (New World).
- **In man — amastigote** (round/oval, 2–4 μm, nucleus + rod-shaped kinetoplast, no external flagellum) — inside **macrophages of the reticulo-endothelial system** — **spleen, liver, bone marrow, lymph nodes**; survives even in the phagolysosomes of macrophages.
- **In the sandfly — promastigote** (elongated, anterior flagellum, no undulating membrane) — multiplies in the midgut → pharynx/proboscis → into man at the bite.

| Species | Disease | Type |
|---|---|---|
| ***L. donovani*** | **Kala-azar** ("black fever" — greyish-black darkening of the skin) — **visceral leishmaniasis**; massive **splenomegaly**, hepatomegaly, irregular fever (rising twice a day — "double quotidian"), anaemia, leucopenia; **PKDL** (post kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis) | **India: Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh** — vector ***Phlebotomus argentipes***; **man is the only reservoir (anthroponotic)** |
| *L. tropica* / *L. major* | **Oriental sore / Delhi boil** — **cutaneous** | Cutaneous cases in Rajasthan (Thar) |
| *L. braziliensis* | **Espundia** — **mucocutaneous** — destruction of nose, mouth | South America |

- Diagnosis — **LD bodies in spleen/bone-marrow aspirate**; **rK39 rapid test**; aldehyde test (old).
- Treatment — **liposomal amphotericin B** (single dose, preferred in India), **miltefosine** (first oral drug), sodium stibogluconate (pentavalent antimony — resistance in Bihar), paromomycin.
- India reported achieving the kala-azar elimination target (fewer than 1 case per 10,000 population at block level) in **2023**; surveillance continues for WHO certification.

### 5. *Giardia intestinalis* (*G. lamblia*, *G. duodenalis*)

- **The first parasitic protozoan ever seen** — by **Leeuwenhoek (1681)** in his own stool; named after Lambl (1859).
- Habitat: **duodenum and jejunum (upper part of the small intestine)**; **monogenetic**.
- **Trophozoite** — **pear/kite-shaped**, **bilaterally symmetrical**, **2 nuclei** ("a face — two eyes"), **4 pairs (8) of flagella**, an **adhesive / sucking disc** on the ventral surface — attaches to the intestinal epithelium; **two median bodies**, **axostyle**; **no** mitochondria (mitosomes).
- **Cyst** — oval, **4 nuclei** — **infective**; **only 10–100 cysts are enough**.
- Transmission: **faeco-oral** — **contaminated water** (chlorine-resistant cysts), food; "**beaver fever**" / traveller's diarrhoea; male homosexual contact.
- **Giardiasis** — foul-smelling, frothy, **fatty stools (steatorrhoea)**, bloating, **malabsorption** — fat, vitamin A, B₁₂; damage to microvilli by the sucking disc; more common in people with **IgA deficiency**.
- Diagnosis — cysts/trophozoites in stool, **string test (Entero-test)**; treatment — **metronidazole**, tinidazole.

## Part B — Economic importance of insects

### 1. Beneficial insects

#### (a) Apiculture (bee-keeping)

| Species | Common name | Special |
|---|---|---|
| ***Apis dorsata*** | **Rock bee** | Largest, **aggressive**, **one huge comb** (in the open) on high rocks/branches; most honey (up to ~36 kg/comb/year) but **cannot be domesticated** |
| ***Apis cerana indica*** | **Indian honeybee** | Medium, gentle; **many parallel combs** in enclosed places; **domesticated** (~3–10 kg) |
| ***Apis florea*** | **Little bee** | Smallest; one palm-sized comb in bushes; little honey (~0.5 kg) |
| ***Apis mellifera*** | **European/Italian honeybee** | Exotic, gentle, less swarming; **the most productive domesticated bee** (~25–40 kg); in India since the 1960s (Punjab, Himachal) |
| *Trigona* / *Tetragonula* | **Stingless bee** | Medicinal "meliponine" honey |

- **Castes** — **queen** (one, fertile female, diploid; develops by feeding on **royal jelly**; ~1500–2000 eggs per day); **workers** (sterile females, diploid; wax glands in the abdomen, pollen baskets on the hind legs; comb building, food, defence, nursing); **drones/males** (**from unfertilised eggs — haploid, parthenogenesis — arrhenotoky**; mate only with the queen, then die). Sex determination — **haplodiploidy**.
- **Communication** — **Karl von Frisch (Nobel 1973)** — **round dance** — food within ~**50–100 m**; **waggle dance** — far away; direction = angle relative to the sun, distance = duration of the waggle.
- **Products** — **honey** (mainly **fructose + glucose**, ~17–20% water; nectar converted by the worker's **invertase**), **beeswax** (from the **abdominal wax glands** of workers — cosmetics, polish, candles), **royal jelly** (from the **pharyngeal/hypopharyngeal glands** of workers), **propolis** (plant resin — "bee glue"), **bee venom** (apitoxin — treatment of arthritis), **pollen**.
- **Pollination** — economic value many times greater than honey — sunflower, mustard, orchards.
- **Modern hive** — **Langstroth's (1851)** movable-frame hive ("**bee space**" — 6–9 mm); in India the **Newton hive**; **honey extractor** — centrifugal.
- **Enemies/diseases** — **mites (*Varroa*, *Tropilaelaps*)**, wax moth (*Galleria mellonella*), Thai sac-brood virus (in *A. cerana*), green bee-eater birds, wasps.
- India — **National Beekeeping and Honey Mission (NBHM, 2020)** — "**Sweet Revolution**"; Central Bee Research Institute — **Pune**.

#### (b) Sericulture (silkworm rearing)

| Silk | Insect | Food plant | Special |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mulberry silk** | ***Bombyx mori*** (fully domesticated; adult cannot fly) | **Mulberry (*Morus alba*)** | ~90% of the world's silk (~70%+ in India); Karnataka leads |
| **Tasar** | ***Antheraea mylitta*** (tropical), *A. proylei/pernyi* (oak tasar) | **Arjun, asan (*Terminalia*)**, sal; oak | Jharkhand leads (**copper-brown**) |
| **Muga** | ***Antheraea assamensis*** | **Som (*Persea bombycina*), soalu (*Litsea*)** | **Golden yellow**, **only Assam** — GI tag; unique in the world |
| **Eri / ahimsa silk** | ***Samia ricini*** (*Philosamia ricini*) | **Castor (*Ricinus*)** | Cocoon with an open end — spun even after the moth emerges ("**ahimsa (non-violent) silk**"); Assam, Meghalaya |

- **Life cycle (*B. mori*)** — **complete metamorphosis**: egg (~10 days; breeds with/without **diapause**) → **larva (caterpillar)** — **5 instars, 4 moults**, ~25–30 days; only mulberry leaves → **spins the cocoon** (~3 days; ~1000–1500 m continuous filament) → **pupa** (inside the cocoon, ~10–14 days) → **adult moth** (secretes **alkaline saliva/cocoonase** to make a hole in the cocoon — the filament breaks).
- **Silk glands** = **modified salivary glands (labial glands)**; secretion through the **spinneret** (on the labium-hypopharynx).
- **Silk** — the protein **fibroin** (inner, ~75–80%, rich in glycine–alanine–serine, β-pleated sheet) + **sericin** (outer gum, ~20–25%).
- **Stifling** — killing the pupa with **hot steam/dry heat/sunlight** so that the filament stays unbroken; **reeling** — loosening the sericin in hot water and unwinding the filament; **degumming** — removing the sericin.
- **Diseases** — **pebrine** (*Nosema bombycis* — a microsporidian; **Louis Pasteur** studied it and suggested control — 1865–70), **grasserie** (nuclear polyhedrosis virus — BmNPV), **flacherie** (bacteria + virus), **muscardine** (fungus *Beauveria bassiana*); **uzi fly** (*Exorista bombycis* — parasite).
- India — the world's **second largest silk producer** (after China) and **largest consumer**; **only India produces all four commercial silks**; Central Silk Board — **Bengaluru**; Central Tasar Research Institute — **Ranchi**.

#### (c) Lac culture

- **Lac insect** — ***Kerria lacca*** (old names *Laccifer lacca*, *Tachardia lacca*) — **Homoptera (Hemiptera)**, family **Kerriidae** (Tachardiidae); a scale insect.
- **Lac** = **resin** secreted by the **epidermal/dermal glands** of the **female insect** — a covering over the insect on the host twig.
- **Life cycle** — eggs hatch inside the female → **nymphs/crawlers** emerge and settle on twigs (**swarming**) → suck sap with sucking mouthparts and secrete lac; **males** — some winged, some wingless; the female is **wingless, legless, sedentary**; **two generations** a year.
- **Strains** — **Rangeeni** — on palas, ber; crops **Katki** and **Baisakhi**; **Kusumi** — on **kusum (*Schleichera oleosa*)**, best quality; crops **Aghani** and **Jethwi**.
- **Host plants** — **kusum, palas (*Butea monosperma*), ber (*Ziziphus*)**, semialata (*Flemingia*), ghont, arhar.
- **Products** — **stick lac** (on twigs) → **seed lac** (crushed and washed) → **shellac** (melted into thin sheets); **lac dye (laccaic acid)** and **lac wax**.
- **Uses** — varnish, polish, seals, gramophone records (earlier), bangles, toys, electrical insulators, coating of fruits/medicine tablets, jewellery, postal seals.
- India — the world's **largest lac producer** (~50–60%+); **Jharkhand** leads, followed by Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Maharashtra; **Indian Institute of Natural Resins and Gums (IINRG), Ranchi** (established 1925 — earlier the Indian Lac Research Institute).
- **Enemies** — the predatory moths ***Eublemma amabilis*** and ***Pseudohypatopa pulverea*** (*Holcocera*) — the biggest enemies of lac.

### 5. Pest control and insecticides

| Class | Examples | Persistence | Toxicity to mammals / action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Organochlorines (chlorinated hydrocarbons)** | **DDT**, BHC/lindane, aldrin, dieldrin, endosulfan, chlordane | **Highly persistent** (years) — **biomagnification**, accumulate in fat, thin eggshells in birds | Acute toxicity relatively low; keep the Na⁺ channels of nerves open (DDT); most are banned — **Stockholm Convention (POPs, 2001)**; in India DDT restricted to vector control only |
| **Organophosphates** | **Malathion**, parathion, **chlorpyrifos**, dichlorvos (DDVP), monocrotophos, dimethoate | **Less persistent** (degrade quickly) | **More toxic to mammals**; **irreversible inhibition of acetylcholinesterase** → ACh accumulation; antidote **atropine + pralidoxime (2-PAM)**; **Bhopal gas tragedy (1984)** — **methyl isocyanate (MIC)** — an intermediate in the manufacture of carbaryl (Sevin) |
| **Carbamates** | **Carbaryl**, carbofuran, aldicarb, propoxur | Less persistent | **Reversible inhibition of acetylcholinesterase**; atropine treatment (2-PAM usually not) |
| **Pyrethroids (synthetic)** | Cypermethrin, deltamethrin, permethrin, allethrin (mosquito coils/liquids) | Moderate; made photostable | **Low toxicity** to mammals, **highly** toxic to fishes; Na⁺ channels |
| **Botanicals** | **Pyrethrin** (flowers of *Chrysanthemum cinerariaefolium*), **nicotine** (tobacco), **rotenone** (*Derris* — also a fish poison), **azadirachtin** (**neem** — antifeedant, growth regulator) | Very little persistence | Usually low toxicity (nicotine is an exception — highly toxic) |
| **Neonicotinoids** | Imidacloprid, thiamethoxam | Systemic (spread in the plant) | Nicotinic ACh receptor; linked to **decline of honeybees (CCD)** — banned in Europe |
| **Insect growth regulators (IGR)** | **Juvenile hormone analogues** (methoprene), **chitin-synthesis inhibitors** (diflubenzuron) | — | Insect-specific — **"third-generation insecticides"** (Williams) |
| **Semiochemicals** | **Pheromone traps** (pink bollworm — **gossyplure**; *Helicoverpa* — helilure), fruit fly — methyl eugenol | — | **Behaviour-modifying**, not toxic — monitoring, **mass trapping**, **mating disruption** |
| **Fumigants** | Aluminium phosphide, methyl bromide | Gas | Stored-grain pests |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** "Which class of insecticides is more toxic to mammals but less persistent than chlorinated hydrocarbons? (A) Organophosphates (B) Carbamates (C) Botanicals (D) Semiochemicals" — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A) Organophosphates**. (Carbamates are also less persistent, but the textbook sentence "more toxic to mammals but less persistent than chlorinated hydrocarbons" is used for organophosphates.)

**Other control methods:**
- **Cultural** — crop rotation, timely sowing, **trap crop** — okra/marigold with cotton, deep ploughing, resistant varieties.
- **Mechanical/physical** — hand-picking, **light traps**, yellow sticky traps (whitefly), heat treatment.
- **Biological** — predators, parasitoids, pathogens — ***Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)** — **Cry protein (δ-endotoxin)** — activated in the alkaline insect gut; **Bt cotton** (India 2002 — the first GM crop approved for commercial cultivation), *Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium* (fungi), **NPV** (virus — for *Helicoverpa*).
- **Genetic — sterile male technique (SIT)** — **Knipling**; releasing males sterilised by radiation — eradication of the **screw-worm fly (*Cochliomyia hominivorax*)** (Curaçao, 1954; then USA); modern — **genetically modified *Aedes*** (OX513A), mosquitoes infected with ***Wolbachia*** bacteria (reduces dengue transmission).
- **Legal** — **Destructive Insects and Pests Act (DIP Act), 1914**; **Insecticides Act, 1968**; quarantine.
- **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)** — a **combination** of all methods, insecticides only when the **Economic Injury Level (EIL)** / **Economic Threshold Level (ETL)** is crossed; conservation of natural enemies. (ETL < EIL — action at ETL so that EIL is not reached.)

## Traps and exceptions

- The **definitive host of *Plasmodium* is the mosquito**, man is the **intermediate** host — because sexual reproduction occurs in the mosquito.
- Only the **female *Anopheles*** sucks blood (protein for egg development); males feed on nectar/plant sap.
- In *Plasmodium* the **zygote is the only diploid stage**; meiosis occurs in the oocyst (zygotic).
- Malarial fever is caused by **haemozoin and toxins** — not by "haemoglobin".
- *P. falciparum* has **no hypnozoites** → no relapse (but recrudescence is possible).
- The cyst of *Entamoeba histolytica* is **quadrinucleate**, of *E. coli* **octonucleate**; *E. gingivalis* has **no** cyst.
- *Leishmania* lives in **macrophages**, *Plasmodium* in **RBCs and liver cells**, *Trypanosoma* in **blood plasma** (outside RBCs).
- *Giardia* in the **small intestine (duodenum)**, *Entamoeba* in the **large intestine**; *Giardia* has no mitochondria.
- *Trichomonas vaginalis* has **no cyst** — hence only direct (sexual) transmission.
- **Housefly** — mechanical vector; **mosquito, sandfly, tsetse fly** — biological vectors.
- **The vector of dengue is *Aedes*** (bites in the daytime) — not *Anopheles*/*Culex*; **Japanese encephalitis — *Culex*** — not *Aedes*.
- **Lac is secreted by the female insect**; silk by the salivary glands of the **larva**; beeswax by the abdominal glands of the **worker** bee.
- **Muga only in Assam**; **eri — ahimsa silk**; **tasar — arjun/asan**.
- **Only the larva of the khapra beetle** is harmful.
- **Organophosphates — irreversible**, **carbamates — reversible** AChE inhibition.

## Quick revision

- *P. vivax* — 48 h, hypnozoites, Schüffner's dots; *P. falciparum* — deadly, crescent gametocytes, Maurer's dots; *P. malariae* — 72 h; *P. knowlesi* — 24 h.
- In man: sporozoite → liver → merozoite → RBC (ring → trophozoite → schizont) → gametocytes.
- In mosquito: gametes → zygote (2n) → ookinete → oocyst → sporozoites → salivary gland.
- Laveran 1880 (parasite), Ross 1897 (mosquito, Secunderabad, Nobel 1902), Shortt–Garnham 1948 (liver).
- RTS,S (2021), R21 (2023) — malaria vaccines; artemisinin — Tu Youyou.
- *Entamoeba* — large intestine, flask ulcers, quadrinucleate cyst, faeco-oral; metronidazole.
- *T. gambiense* — *G. palpalis*; *T. rhodesiense* — *G. morsitans*; *T. cruzi* — *Triatoma* (through faeces); *T. evansi* — surra.
- VSG — antigenic variation.
- *L. donovani* — kala-azar, *Phlebotomus argentipes*, LD bodies, Bihar; liposomal amphotericin B.
- *Giardia* — Leeuwenhoek, duodenum, 8 flagella, sucking disc, steatorrhoea.
- *Balantidium* — the only parasitic ciliate; *Babesia* — tick, first arthropod vector proven.
- *A. dorsata* — rock bee; *A. cerana indica* — Indian (domesticated); *A. florea* — little bee; *A. mellifera* — Italian.
- Von Frisch — round and waggle dances.
- *Bombyx mori* — mulberry; tasar — *A. mylitta*; muga — *A. assamensis* (Assam); eri — *Samia ricini* (castor).
- Fibroin + sericin; pebrine — *Nosema* (Pasteur).
- Lac — female *Kerria lacca*; Kusumi and Rangeeni; IINRG Ranchi; Jharkhand leads.
- *Rodolia* → cottony cushion scale; *Cactoblastis* → *Opuntia*; *Zygogramma* → *Parthenium*.
- Gundhi bug — paddy; Pyrilla — sugarcane; pink bollworm — cotton; khapra — stored grain.
- Organophosphates: more toxic, less persistent; DDT: more persistent, biomagnification.
- SIT — screw-worm fly; IPM — action at ETL.

## Practice questions (with answers)

7. Shape of the ulcer in amoebic dysentery? — **Flask-shaped**.

10. Indian vector of kala-azar? — ***Phlebotomus argentipes***.
11. What are LD bodies? — **Amastigotes of *Leishmania*** (in macrophages).
12. Sucking disc is found in which parasite? — ***Giardia intestinalis***.
13. The honeybee that gives the most honey but cannot be domesticated? — ***Apis dorsata***.
14. Which gland produces royal jelly? — **Hypopharyngeal gland of the worker**.
15. Golden silk comes from? — **Muga — *Antheraea assamensis*** (Assam).
16. Pebrine disease of the silkworm is caused by? — ***Nosema bombycis***.

20. Which insect was eradicated by the sterile male technique? — **Screw-worm fly (*Cochliomyia hominivorax*)**.
