This chapter takes the rest of Unit 9 — **extension of the chromosomal theory, linkage and crossing over, sex determination, polyploidy, human genetic disorders and mutation** — from NCERT 12 up to B.Sc./M.Sc. level (three-point mapping, genic balance theory, transition-transversion). The 2026 paper had an assertion-reason question on **transition versus transversion mutation**; syndrome-karyotype matching and pedigree analysis are also very likely.

> **In simple words:** Genes located on the same chromosome are inherited together (linkage), unless an exchange of pieces during meiosis (crossing over) separates them. The farther apart two genes are, the more exchange there is — this is how a "map" of genes is made. Sex is usually decided by special chromosomes (X, Y, Z, W). Faults in the number or structure of chromosomes, or a permanent change in DNA (mutation), cause diseases and also provide the raw material for evolution.

## 1. Linkage

- **Bateson and Punnett (1906)** — in sweet pea (*Lathyrus odoratus*), flower colour (purple P / red p) and pollen grain shape (long L / round l) — in the dihybrid test cross, more parental combinations were found instead of 1:1:1:1. They called this **coupling** and **repulsion**.
- **Morgan (1910–11)** — showed in *Drosophila* that this is because the genes lie on the same chromosome; the word "**linkage**" is Morgan's.
- **Coupling (cis, AB/ab)** — both dominants on the same homologue; **repulsion (trans, Ab/aB)** — dominants on different homologues.
- **Linkage groups** = haploid chromosome number (n): *Drosophila* 4, pea 7, maize 10, human **23** (23 in females; **24** in males if X and Y are counted separately).

| Linkage | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Complete** | No crossing over; only parental combinations | ***Drosophila* male**, **silkworm (*Bombyx mori*) female** |
| **Incomplete** | Some recombinants due to crossing over | Most organisms |

**Factors affecting linkage:** distance between genes (greater distance → weaker linkage), age (in *Drosophila* females crossing over decreases with age), temperature, X-rays (increase crossing over), sex.

## 2. Crossing over

- Exchange of segments between **non-sister chromatids** of homologous chromosomes; in **pachytene**, at the **four-chromatid (tetrad) stage**; one crossover makes **two** of the four chromatids recombinant.
- Term "crossing over" — **Morgan and Cattell (1912)**; "chiasmatype theory" — **Janssens (1909)**.
- **Cytological proof (1931):** **Curt Stern** — morphologically marked X chromosomes in *Drosophila*; **Harriet Creighton and Barbara McClintock** — a knob and terminal segment on chromosome 9 of **maize** — showed physical exchange of chromosome segments along with genetic recombination.
- Molecular models: **Holliday model (1964)** — Holliday junction; double-strand break repair model (Szostak, 1983); enzymes — **Spo11** (DSB), **RecA/Rad51/Dmc1**, resolvases.
- **Somatic (mitotic) crossing over** — in *Drosophila*, **Curt Stern (1936)** — "twin spots".
- **Unequal crossing over** — misalignment gives duplication in one and deletion in the other (*Drosophila* Bar eye, human red-green colour blindness genes).
- **Significance:** new gene combinations (variation → evolution, plant breeding), **gene mapping**, proof of linear arrangement of genes.

## 3. Gene mapping

- **Recombination frequency (RF) = (recombinant offspring / total offspring) × 100**.
- **1% RF = 1 map unit = 1 centimorgan (cM)** (in honour of Morgan).
- **Alfred Sturtevant (1913)** — first genetic map, 5–6 genes of the *Drosophila* X chromosome (while still an undergraduate student).
- Maximum observed RF between two genes = **50%** (like independent assortment) — because multiple crossovers cancel each other; so the distance of far-apart genes is found by **adding up short intervals**.
- NCERT's Morgan data: in *Drosophila* **yellow body (y) – white eye (w): 1.3%** recombination; **white eye (w) – miniature wing (m): 37.2%**.
- **Map distance ≠ physical distance** — crossover "hotspots" and fewer crossovers near the centromere; in humans on average 1 cM ≈ **1 Mb**.

### 3.1 Two-point test cross — solved example

AB/ab (coupling) × ab/ab → offspring: AB 420, ab 430, Ab 75, aB 75 (total 1000).
- Parental (more) = AB + ab = 850; recombinant (fewer) = Ab + aB = 150.
- RF = 150 / 1000 × 100 = **15% → 15 cM**.
- If the same genes were in **repulsion** (Ab/aB), Ab and aB would be more numerous (parental) and AB, ab fewer (recombinant).

### 3.2 Three-point test cross — solved example

Triple heterozygote (a b c / + + +) × a b c / a b c → 1000 offspring:

| Class | Phenotype | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Parental | a b c | 390 |
| Parental | + + + | 389 |
| Single crossover (region I) | a + + | 60 |
| Single crossover (region I) | + b c | 70 |
| Single crossover (region II) | a b + | 40 |
| Single crossover (region II) | + + c | 45 |
| Double crossover (DCO) | a + c | 3 |
| Double crossover (DCO) | + b + | 3 |
| | **Total** | **1000** |

**Step 1 — gene order:** largest classes = parental (abc, +++); smallest = DCO (a+c, +b+). Compare DCO with the parental — the gene that has "flipped" is in the **middle**: abc → a**+**c, only b changed → order **a – b – c**.
**Step 2 — distances:**
- a–b (region I): (60 + 70 + 3 + 3) / 1000 = 136/1000 = **13.6 cM**
- b–c (region II): (40 + 45 + 3 + 3) / 1000 = 91/1000 = **9.1 cM**
- a–c = 13.6 + 9.1 = **22.7 cM** (by counting the DCOs twice).
**Step 3 — interference:** expected DCO = 0.136 × 0.091 × 1000 = **12.38**; observed = 6.
- **Coefficient of coincidence (CoC)** = observed DCO / expected DCO = 6 / 12.38 = **0.485**
- **Interference (I) = 1 − CoC = 0.515** — one crossover suppresses a second crossover nearby by about half (positive interference). I = 0 → no interference; I = 1 → complete interference (DCO zero).

## 4. Sex determination

- **Henking (1891)** — saw a special nuclear structure during spermatogenesis of an insect (*Pyrrhocoris*), named it the "**X body**"; later it was recognised as a chromosome. **McClung (1902)** — linked X with sex; **Wilson and Stevens (1905)** — XX-XY and XX-XO systems.
- The heterogametic sex produces two kinds of gametes, the homogametic sex one kind.

### 4.1 Chromosomal systems

| System | Male | Female | Heterogametic sex | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XX–XY** | XY | XX | **Male** | Humans, mammals, *Drosophila*, some plants (*Melandrium/Silene*, *Coccinia*) |
| **XX–XO** | **XO** | XX | **Male** | **Grasshopper**, *Protenor* (bug), cockroach, most insects |
| **ZW–ZZ** | **ZZ** | **ZW** | **Female** | **Birds**, some reptiles (snakes), some fishes, **butterflies and moths (Lepidoptera)**, silkworm |
| **ZO–ZZ** | ZZ | **ZO** | Female | Some moths and butterflies (Lepidoptera) |
| **Haplodiploidy** | **Haploid (n)** — from unfertilised egg (**parthenogenesis/arrhenotoky**) | Diploid (2n) — from fertilised egg | — | **Honeybee**, ant, wasp (Hymenoptera) |

- **Honeybee:** queen (2n = 32), worker (2n, sterile female), male/drone (n = 16) — the male forms sperm by mitosis; a male has **no father, but has a grandfather**. Queen and worker differ by nutrition (**royal jelly**).

### 4.2 Sex determination in humans — the decisive role of Y

- Father's sperm: 50% X, 50% Y; mother's eggs all X → **the sex of the child is decided by the father's sperm**; in each pregnancy the probability of a boy/girl is 1/2.
- **SRY gene** (Sex-determining Region Y; **Yp11.2**; **Sinclair et al., 1990**) → **testis-determining factor (TDF)** → the bipotential gonad becomes a **testis** (≈ 6–7 weeks) → testosterone, AMH.
- Proof: **XXY (Klinefelter) = male**, **XO (Turner) = female** → in humans it is not the number of X but the **presence of Y** that is decisive.
- **XX male** (SRY translocated to X) and **XY female (Swyer syndrome, SRY deletion/mutation)**; **testicular feminisation / AIS** (XY, androgen receptor defect — female external appearance).
- **Pseudoautosomal regions (PAR1, PAR2)** — homologous parts at the ends of X and Y where pairing and crossing over occur.

### 4.3 *Drosophila* — genic balance theory (Calvin Bridges, 1921)

In *Drosophila*, Y does not determine sex (it is needed only for male fertility); sex depends on the **ratio of X chromosomes to autosome sets (A) (X/A)** (modern view — the X:A ratio controls the *Sxl* gene).

| Chromosome constitution | X/A ratio | Sex |
|---|---|---|
| 3X : 2A | 1.5 | **Metafemale / superfemale** |
| 4X : 4A, 3X : 3A, 2X : 2A | 1.0 | **Female** |
| 2X : 3A | 0.67 | **Intersex** |
| XY : 2A, XO : 2A | 0.5 | **Male** (XO male is **sterile**) |
| XY : 3A | 0.33 | **Metamale / supermale** |

**Rule:** X/A ≥ 1 → female; ≤ 0.5 → male; in between → intersex. In *Drosophila* **XXY = fertile female**, **XO = sterile male** — exactly the opposite of humans.

### 4.4 Other mechanisms

- **Environmental/temperature-dependent (TSD):** many **turtles** — high temperature (> ≈ 30°C) → **female**, low temperature → male; **alligators and crocodiles** — usually low temperature → female, high → male (in some, females at both extremes); some lizards.
- ***Bonellia viridis*** (marine echiuran worm) — if the **larva settles on the female's proboscis** it becomes **male** (tiny, living almost like a parasite inside the female); if it stays free it becomes female — environmental (hormonal) determination.
- ***Crepidula*** (slipper limpet) — sex by position in the stack; **protandry**.
- **Fishes** — **clownfish** (male → female, protandry), **wrasse** (female → male, protogyny) — social cues.
- **Somatic sex reversal** — in a hen, damage to the ovary leads to cock-like features.
- **Unicellular organisms/algae** — "+" and "−" mating types.

## 5. Sex-linked inheritance

- **X-linked recessive:** the male (XY) has only one copy → **hemizygous** — one recessive allele alone causes the disease; hence **more common in males**. **Criss-cross inheritance** — father → daughter (carrier) → grandson; **an X-linked gene never passes from father to son**.
- Examples: **haemophilia A** (factor VIII), **haemophilia B / Christmas disease** (factor IX) — "royal disease", carrier **Queen Victoria**; **red-green colour blindness** — NCERT: ≈ **8% of males, 0.4% of females**; **Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)** — dystrophin; **G6PD deficiency** (haemolysis from primaquine/fava beans).
- **X-linked dominant:** **vitamin D-resistant (hypophosphataemic) rickets** — **all daughters** of an affected father are affected, no sons.
- **Y-linked (holandric):** father → **all sons**; SRY, AZF (spermatogenesis); traditional example **hypertrichosis of the ear pinna** — its Y-linkage is now considered doubtful, but this is the answer in exams.
- **Sex-influenced** — autosomal gene whose dominance depends on sex: **baldness** (dominant in males, recessive in females — testosterone), horns in sheep.
- **Sex-limited** — expressed in only one sex: **beard and moustache**, breast development, **milk production** (cow), tail feathers of the cock.

**Solved problem 1:** Carrier woman (XᴴXʰ) × normal man (XᴴY) — offspring? — XᴴXᴴ (normal daughter), XᴴXʰ (carrier daughter), XᴴY (normal son), XʰY (**haemophilic son**) → **50% of sons affected, 50% of daughters carriers, no daughter affected**.
**Solved problem 2:** Colour-blind man (XᶜY) × normal (homozygous) woman → all sons **normal**, all daughters **carriers**.
**Solved problem 3:** Colour-blind woman (XᶜXᶜ) × normal man → all sons **colour-blind**, all daughters **carriers** (criss-cross).

**Barr body:** **Murray Barr and Bertram (1949)** — in nerve cells of female cats; **Mary Lyon (1961)** — **Lyon hypothesis**: in female mammals, at the embryonic stage one X in each cell is randomly inactivated (dosage compensation) → **mosaic** — e.g. the **calico/tortoiseshell cat** (usually female). Barr bodies = X − 1: normal woman 1, man 0, Turner 0, Klinefelter (XXY) 1, XXX 2. In *Drosophila* dosage compensation is the reverse — **the male's single X is twice as active**.

## 6. Changes in chromosome number — euploidy and aneuploidy

### 6.1 Aneuploidy — loss/gain of one or a few chromosomes

| Name | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monosomy | 2n − 1 | Turner (45,X) |
| Nullisomy | 2n − 2 | In wheat (tolerated — because it is polyploid) |
| Trisomy | 2n + 1 | Down (47,+21) |
| Double trisomy | 2n + 1 + 1 | — |
| Tetrasomy | 2n + 2 | 48,XXXX |

- Cause: **non-disjunction** — failure of homologues/sister chromatids to separate in meiosis I or II (**Bridges, 1916** — *Drosophila*; the final proof of the chromosomal theory). The risk rises with maternal age.
- **Datura (*Datura stramonium*)** — 12 possible trisomics, each with a different fruit shape (**Blakeslee and Belling, 1920s**).

### 6.2 Euploidy / polyploidy — increase of whole sets

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Monoploidy (x)/haploid** | One set | Male honeybee; haploid plants from anther culture |
| **Triploid (3x)** | Usually sterile (unbalanced pairing in meiosis) | **Banana** (*Musa*), **seedless watermelon** (4x × 2x), sugar beet, some apple varieties |
| **Autopolyploidy** | Multiplication of the set of the same species (AAAA) | **Potato (4x)**, groundnut, horticultural plants with large flowers and fruits; **multivalents** in tetraploids |
| **Allopolyploidy** | Sets from two or more different species (AABB) — hybrid + chromosome doubling; **bivalents** in meiosis → **fertile** | ***Raphanobrassica*** — radish (2n = 18) × cabbage (2n = 18) → **Karpechenko (1927/28)**, 2n = 36 (fertile, but root like cabbage and leaves like radish — practically useless); ***Triticale*** — wheat × rye (*Secale cereale*), **first man-made cereal** (hexaploid 6x = 42 or octoploid 8x = 56); **bread wheat *Triticum aestivum*** — **AABBDD, 2n = 6x = 42**; **cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*)** — AADD, 2n = 52; ***Brassica napus*** (AACC, 2n = 38) — **"U's triangle" (1935)**; tobacco (*Nicotiana tabacum*) |

- **Induction:** **colchicine** (*Colchicum autumnale*) — doubles chromosomes by blocking spindle formation (**Blakeslee and Avery, 1937**); others — acenaphthene, heat shock, N₂O.
- **Segmental allopolyploidy** — genomes partly homologous.
- **Significance:** larger cells and organs ("**gigas effect**"), seedlessness, overcoming hybrid sterility, **instant speciation (sympatric speciation)** — ≈ 30–70% of angiosperms (modern estimates almost all) went through polyploidy at some time.
- **Polyploidy is rare in animals** — reasons: most animals are **unisexual** and XY sex determination is upset by X/A imbalance; cross-fertilisation; complex developmental balance. Exceptions — some parthenogenetic animals, some **amphibians** (*Xenopus laevis* — ancient tetraploid), fishes (salmon, carp), some insects. In humans triploid embryos are usually aborted.
- **Endopolyploidy** — in somatic tissue: liver, **tapetum**, endosperm (3n).

## 7. Structural changes in chromosomes (structural aberrations)

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Deletion/deficiency** | Loss of a segment — terminal or intercalary | **Cri-du-chat** — **5p⁻** (Lejeune, 1963; cat-like cry); *Drosophila* Notch |
| **Duplication** | A segment present twice | *Drosophila* **Bar eye** (duplication of segment 16A — unequal crossing over, Sturtevant and Bridges); evolution of gene families (globin) |
| **Inversion** | Segment rejoined after turning 180° — **pericentric** — including the centromere; **paracentric** — outside the centromere | **Inversion loop** in meiosis; crossing over in a paracentric inversion gives a **dicentric bridge + acentric fragment**; crossover suppression ("**crossover suppressor**") — *Drosophila* ClB technique (Muller) |
| **Translocation** | Movement of a segment between non-homologous chromosomes — exchange in **reciprocal** translocation | **"+" (cross) figure**/ring in meiosis; **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22)** — **chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)** (*BCR-ABL*); Burkitt lymphoma t(8;14); translocation ring in *Oenothera lamarckiana* |
| **Robertsonian translocation (centric fusion)** | Fusion of two acrocentric chromosomes at the centromere | **Familial Down syndrome** t(14;21) — independent of parental age, ≈ 4% of all Down cases; human chromosome 2 = fusion of two ape chromosomes |
| **Isochromosome** | Transverse division of the centromere — both arms identical | i(Xq) — some Turner patients |
| **Ring chromosome** | Both ends break and join | — |

## 8. Human genetic disorders

### 8.1 Chromosomal disorders

| Disorder | Karyotype | Frequency (approx.) | Main features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Down syndrome** | **47,+21** (trisomy 21) | ≈ 1/700–800 live births; risk rises sharply at maternal age > 35 | Described by **Langdon Down (1866)**; trisomy found by **Lejeune (1959)**; short stature, round face, **slanting eyes (epicanthic fold)**, open mouth and thick tongue, **transverse (simian) palm crease**, mental retardation, congenital heart defects, leukaemia risk |

### 8.3 Pedigree analysis

**Symbols:** □ male, ○ female, ◇ sex unknown; filled symbol = affected; half-filled / dot in the centre = carrier; double horizontal line = **consanguineous marriage**; Roman numerals = generation.

| Inheritance | Clues for identification |
|---|---|
| **Autosomal dominant** | In every generation (vertical); at least one parent of an affected child is affected; both sexes equally; **two affected parents can have an unaffected child** → **definitely dominant** |
| **Autosomal recessive** | Generations may be skipped (horizontal); **affected child of two unaffected parents** → **definitely recessive**; if an affected **daughter** has an unaffected father it **cannot be X-linked recessive** → autosomal recessive; more common in consanguineous marriages |
| **X-linked recessive** | Mainly males; sons of an affected man are unaffected (no X from the father), but daughters are carriers; **all sons** of an affected woman are affected |
| **X-linked dominant** | **All daughters** of an affected father are affected, no sons |
| **Y-linked** | Only males, from father to **all sons** |
| **Mitochondrial** | All children of an affected **mother** are affected; none from an affected father |

## 9. Mutation

- Term "mutation" — **Hugo de Vries (1901)**, ***Oenothera lamarckiana*** (evening primrose) — "**mutation theory**" (later it was found that those changes were mainly **chromosomal (translocation rings, trisomy)**).
- **Morgan (1910)** — *Drosophila* white mutant; **H. J. Muller (1927)** — **artificial mutation by X-rays**, *Drosophila* (Nobel 1946); **Stadler (1928)** — X-rays in barley and maize; **Charlotte Auerbach (1940s)** — **mustard gas** — the first **chemical mutagen**.
- **Luria-Delbrück fluctuation test (1943)** — mutations are **random (spontaneous)**, **not directed** by the environment (phage); **Lederberg's replica plating (1952)** — confirmed it. (The 2026 question — insecticide resistance — was based on this same logic, Chapter 8.)

### 9.1 Types of gene (point) mutation

| Basis | Type | Description / example |
|---|---|---|
| **Base substitution** | **Transition** | **Purine ↔ purine (A ↔ G) or pyrimidine ↔ pyrimidine (C ↔ T)** |
| | **Transversion** | **Purine ↔ pyrimidine** (A/G ↔ C/T) — sickle-cell (A → T) |
| **Effect on protein** | **Silent/synonymous** | Codon changed, same amino acid (usually at the third position — wobble) |
| | **Missense** | A different amino acid — sickle-cell (Glu → Val); **neutral** if properties are similar |
| | **Nonsense** | Amino acid codon → **stop codon** → shorter protein |
| **Base addition/removal** | **Frameshift** | **Insertion** or **deletion** of a number of nucleotides that is not a multiple of 3 → the whole reading frame downstream changes; most severe |
| **Others** | **Dynamic/triplet repeat expansion** | Huntington's (CAG), fragile-X (CGG) |
| | **Forward and back/reverse mutation**; **suppressor** — a second mutation hides the effect of the first (intragenic/intergenic) | |
| | **Conditional** — temperature-sensitive (ts) | Himalayan rabbit |
| | **Somatic versus germinal** | Somatic not inherited (cancer); germinal passed to the next generation |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): Transition mutations are more common than transversion mutations. Reason (R): In a transition a nitrogenous base is replaced by one of the same class (purine ↔ purine or pyrimidine ↔ pyrimidine), causing less structural distortion in DNA. Options: (A) A false, R true (B) Both A and R true, R is the correct explanation of A (C) Both true, but R is not the correct explanation (D) A true, R false. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**. Transitions are in fact found about 2 times more often (even though the possible transversions are twice as many); a mismatch between bases of similar size distorts the helix less, so it escapes proofreading/repair; also, deamination of cytosine (C → T) and tautomeric shifts produce transitions. Note — in this paper the order of the A–R options kept changing, so read the letters carefully.

### 9.2 Mutagens

| Class | Mutagen | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical — non-ionising** | **UV (≈ 260 nm)** | **Thymine dimers** between adjacent pyrimidines |
| **Physical — ionising** | **X-rays, γ-rays** (⁶⁰Co), α, β, neutrons | Free radicals, **strand breaks**, chromosomal aberrations; effect **proportional to dose** (no "safe limit") |
| **Chemical — base analogues** | **5-bromouracil (5-BU)** (analogue of T, pairs with G — transition), **2-aminopurine** (analogue of A) | Mispairing during replication → **transition** |
| **Deaminating** | **Nitrous acid (HNO₂)** | C → U (→ T), A → hypoxanthine (pairs with C) → transition |
| **Hydroxylamine** | NH₂OH | Only C → T transition |
| **Alkylating** | **EMS** (ethyl methane sulphonate), **mustard gas**, nitrosoguanidine (NTG) | Alkylation of G → pairs with T; depurination |
| **Intercalating** | **Acridine orange, proflavine, ethidium bromide** | Insert between base pairs → **frameshift** |
| **Biological** | Transposons, viruses (retroviruses), **aflatoxin** (*Aspergillus flavus*) | Insertion, G → T |

- **Ames test (Bruce Ames, 1970s)** — **histidine-dependent (his⁻)** mutants of ***Salmonella typhimurium***; mutagenicity (and possible carcinogenicity) of a chemical is tested by measuring the rate of his⁺ reversion; with liver enzymes (S9) added.
- **Transposons ("jumping genes")** — **Barbara McClintock (1940s–50s)**, **Ac–Ds system** in **maize** (spots on kernels); Nobel **1983**. In bacteria IS elements, Tn; ≈ 45% of the human genome is transposon-derived (**Alu**, LINE-1).
- **Mutation breeding** — in India **Sharbati Sonora** wheat (γ-ray mutant of Sonora-64, **Dr M. S. Swaminathan**), **Aruna** castor.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Complete linkage** — *Drosophila* **male** and silkworm **female** — in both there is no crossing over in the heterogametic sex (Haldane-Huxley rule); do not write it the other way round.
- Maximum RF is **50%**, not 100%.
- In a three-point cross identify the **middle gene** from the DCO class — the gene that has "flipped" from the parental combination.
- In birds the **female is ZW (heterogametic)** — so the egg decides sex.
- XO: **male in grasshopper (normal)**, **female in humans (Turner)**, **sterile male in *Drosophila***.
- The honeybee male is **haploid** — sperm are formed by **mitosis**.
- Sickle-cell: **missense**, **6th** amino acid of the β-chain, **chromosome 11**; α-thalassaemia **chromosome 16**.
- **Triticale** = wheat × **rye** (not barley); **Raphanobrassica** = radish × cabbage (Karpechenko).
- Seedless watermelon is **triploid**, not tetraploid (hybrid of tetraploid × diploid).
- Klinefelter has **1 Barr body** (despite being male); Turner has **0** (despite being female).
- Acridine/proflavine — **frameshift**; 5-BU, HNO₂ — **transition**; UV — **dimers**.
- De Vries's *Oenothera* was actually a chromosomal aberration — the "mutation theory" was based on a wrong example.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. In AB/ab × ab/ab, 80 recombinants / 400 total — distance? — **20 cM**.
2. a–b = 10 cM, b–c = 15 cM, a–c = 25 cM — gene order? — **a – b – c** (b in the middle).
3. In a three-point cross expected DCO 20, observed 5 — interference? — **0.75** (CoC 0.25).
4. Complete linkage occurs in? — ***Drosophila* male** (and silkworm female).
5. Heterogametic sex in birds? — **Female (ZW)**.
6. Sex of 2X : 3A in *Drosophila*? — **Intersex** (X/A = 0.67).
7. Sex-determining gene in humans? — **SRY** (on Y).
8. Daughters of a haemophilic father (mother normal homozygous)? — **All carriers**.
9. Barr bodies in an XXY individual? — **1**.
10. What is 2n − 1 called? — **Monosomy**.
11. Triticale is from which two plants? — **Wheat × rye**.
12. What kind of polyploid is bread wheat? — **Allohexaploid (AABBDD)**.
13. Cause of familial Down syndrome? — **Robertsonian translocation t(14;21)**.
14. "Simian crease" occurs in which syndrome? — **Down syndrome**.
15. What type of mutation is sickle-cell? — **Point, missense (transversion A→T)** — Glu → Val.
16. Affected daughter of two unaffected parents — inheritance? — **Autosomal recessive**.
17. Proflavine induces which mutation? — **Frameshift** (intercalating).
18. Organism used in the Ames test? — ***Salmonella typhimurium*** (his⁻).
19. Which experiment showed that mutations are random? — **Luria-Delbrück fluctuation test (1943)**.
20. Bar eye results from which aberration? — **Duplication** (unequal crossing over).
