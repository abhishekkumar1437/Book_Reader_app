This chapter belongs to point 3(iii) of Section A of the syllabus, "Respiration and metabolism" — from NCERT Class 11 "Respiration in Plants" to B.Sc./M.Sc. (ETS complexes, inhibitors, PPP, glyoxylate cycle, alternative oxidase).
In UP PGT expect "**the correct sequence of Krebs cycle intermediates**", "**which step has substrate-level phosphorylation**", "**value of RQ**", "**inhibitor–complex matching**" and "statements on ATP counting". The biochemistry of animal respiration is also in [Zoology II — Respiration and Metabolism](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/shwasan-aur-upapachay).

> **In simple words:** Respiration is a controlled process of "burning" food to release energy — not all at once like a fire, but step by step, so that the energy can be stored in "coins" called ATP. Glucose is first split in half in the cytoplasm to form pyruvate (glycolysis); if O₂ is present, pyruvate goes into the mitochondrion and is fully converted to CO₂ (Krebs cycle), and the electrons released reach O₂ and make a lot of ATP (ETS). Without O₂, pyruvate forms alcohol or lactic acid (fermentation).

<div class="formula">

**C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy** (ΔG ≈ −686 kcal/mol ≈ −2870 kJ/mol)
ATP per glucose: **38 (NCERT/older count)** or **30–32 (modern, P/O ratios 2.5 and 1.5)**.

</div>

## 1. Glycolysis — the EMP pathway

**Embden, Meyerhof and Parnas (EMP)**; location — **cytoplasm**; the same in **both aerobic and anaerobic** respiration; **universal in all organisms**. Glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvate (3C), 10 steps.

| Step | Reaction | Enzyme | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Glucose → glucose-6-phosphate | **Hexokinase** | **−1 ATP** |
| 2 | G-6-P → fructose-6-phosphate | Phosphohexose isomerase | |
| 3 | F-6-P → **fructose-1,6-bisphosphate** | **Phosphofructokinase (PFK)** — **rate-limiting, the main regulator** (inhibited by ATP, citrate) | **−1 ATP** |
| 4 | F-1,6-BP → **DHAP + G3P** (2 × 3C) | **Aldolase** | |
| 5 | DHAP ⇌ G3P | Triose phosphate isomerase | |
| 6 | G3P + Pi + NAD⁺ → **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate** + NADH | **G3P dehydrogenase** | **+2 NADH** (the only oxidation step) |
| 7 | 1,3-BPGA → **3-PGA** | Phosphoglycerate kinase | **+2 ATP (substrate-level)** |
| 8 | 3-PGA → 2-PGA | Phosphoglyceromutase | |
| 9 | 2-PGA → **PEP** + H₂O | **Enolase** (inhibited by **fluoride**; Mg²⁺) | |
| 10 | PEP → **pyruvate** | **Pyruvate kinase** | **+2 ATP (substrate-level)** |

<div class="formula">

**Net gain of glycolysis (per glucose): 4 ATP made − 2 ATP used = 2 ATP + 2 NADH + 2 pyruvate.**
In plants entry is also via sucrose → (invertase) → glucose + fructose; starch → (phosphorylase) → glucose-1-phosphate.

</div>

## 2. Fate of pyruvate

| Condition | Products | Enzyme | Organisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aerobic** | Acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH → Krebs | **Pyruvate dehydrogenase complex** (mitochondrial matrix) | Most |
| **Alcoholic fermentation** | **Ethanol + CO₂** | **Pyruvate decarboxylase** (→ acetaldehyde) + **alcohol dehydrogenase** | **Yeast (*Saccharomyces*)**, waterlogged roots |
| **Lactic acid fermentation** | **Lactic acid** (no CO₂) | **Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** | ***Lactobacillus*** (curd), exercising muscle |

- In fermentation **NADH is reoxidised to NAD⁺** so that glycolysis can continue; **net gain only 2 ATP** (less than ~7% of the energy of glucose).
- **Yeasts die at about 13% alcohol** concentration (toxic) — so **distillation** is needed for higher concentrations.
- **Pasteur effect** — in the presence of O₂ **the rate of glycolysis/fermentation falls**.
- **Link reaction:** pyruvate + CoA + NAD⁺ → acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH; cofactors — **TPP (vitamin B₁), lipoic acid, FAD (B₂), NAD⁺ (niacin), CoA (pantothenic acid), Mg²⁺**.

## 3. Krebs cycle (TCA / citric acid cycle)

**Hans Krebs (1937; Nobel 1953)**; location — **mitochondrial matrix** (only **succinate dehydrogenase is in the inner membrane**; in prokaryotes, the cytoplasm).

| No. | Reaction | Enzyme | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Acetyl CoA (2C) + OAA (4C) → citrate (6C)** | Citrate synthase | — (condensation) |
| 2 | Citrate → (cis-aconitate) → **isocitrate** | **Aconitase** (Fe; inhibited by **fluoroacetate**) | |
| 3 | Isocitrate → **α-ketoglutarate (5C)** | Isocitrate dehydrogenase | **NADH + CO₂** |
| 4 | α-KG → **succinyl CoA (4C)** | α-KG dehydrogenase complex | **NADH + CO₂** |
| 5 | Succinyl CoA → **succinate** | Succinyl CoA synthetase | **GTP/ATP (substrate-level)** — ATP directly in plants |
| 6 | Succinate → **fumarate** | **Succinate dehydrogenase** (complex II; competitively inhibited by **malonate**) | **FADH₂** |
| 7 | Fumarate + H₂O → **malate** | Fumarase | |
| 8 | Malate → **OAA** | Malate dehydrogenase | **NADH** |

<div class="formula">

**Per turn (per acetyl CoA): 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 ATP (GTP) + 2 CO₂.**
**Per glucose (2 turns): 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 ATP + 4 CO₂** (+ 2 NADH + 2 CO₂ from the link reaction → 6 CO₂ in all).
**Remember the order (memory trick):** "**C**itrate **I**s **K**rebs' **S**pecial **S**ubstrate **F**or **M**aking **O**xaloacetate" — citrate, isocitrate, α-KG, succinyl CoA, succinate, fumarate, malate, OAA.

</div>

**Amphibolic pathway:** besides catabolism (energy) the Krebs cycle is also a hub of **anabolism** — **α-KG → glutamate** (amino acid), **OAA → aspartate**, **succinyl CoA → porphyrins (chlorophyll, cytochromes)**, acetyl CoA → fatty acids, isoprenoids. So respiration is called "**amphibolic**" (NCERT).

## 4. Electron transport system (ETS) and oxidative phosphorylation

Location — **inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae)**.

| Complex | Name | Components | Pumps H⁺? | Inhibitors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **NADH dehydrogenase** | FMN, Fe-S | Yes | **Rotenone**, amytal |
| **II** | **Succinate dehydrogenase** | FAD, Fe-S | **No** | Malonate (substrate level), TTFA |
| — | **Ubiquinone (UQ/CoQ)** | Mobile lipid-soluble carrier | | |
| **III** | **Cytochrome bc₁ complex** | cyt b, c₁, Fe-S (Rieske) | Yes | **Antimycin A** |
| — | **Cytochrome c** | Mobile, **peripheral protein**, on the intermembrane-space side | | |
| **IV** | **Cytochrome c oxidase** | **cyt a, a₃, Cu_A, Cu_B** | Yes | **Cyanide (CN⁻), CO, azide (N₃⁻), H₂S** |
| **V** | **ATP synthase** | **F₀** (in the inner membrane, proton channel; **oligomycin**-sensitive) + **F₁** (facing the matrix, catalytic head) | — | **Oligomycin** |
| — | **Uncouplers** | **2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP)**, **thermogenin (UCP1)** — ETS runs but no ATP is made; the energy becomes heat | | |

- **Final electron acceptor = O₂** → water is formed (**metabolic water**).
- **Chemiosmosis (Mitchell):** H⁺ are pumped from the matrix into the **intermembrane space** → proton-motive force → return through F₀ → ATP at F₁; **rotational catalysis** of F₁ (**Boyer — "binding change"**); Boyer and Walker — Nobel 1997.
- **P/O ratio:** old — NADH = 3, FADH₂ = 2; modern — NADH ≈ 2.5, FADH₂ ≈ 1.5 (≈ 10 H⁺ per NADH, 4 H⁺ per ATP).

### Special features of plants (B.Sc./M.Sc.)

- **Cyanide-resistant respiration / alternative oxidase (AOX):** electrons go directly from UQ to O₂ — bypassing complexes III and IV → **less ATP, more heat** — **thermogenesis**: the inflorescences of ***Arum*** (arum lily) and ***Symplocarpus*** (skunk cabbage) warm up by 10–25°C and volatilise foul-smelling compounds (to attract pollinators). Inhibited by **SHAM (salicylhydroxamic acid)**.
- **Rotenone-insensitive NAD(P)H dehydrogenases** — external and internal.
- In plants succinyl CoA synthetase makes **ATP** (GTP in animals).

## 5. ATP count

| Stage | Direct ATP | NADH / FADH₂ | Old count (3/2) | Modern (2.5/1.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | 2 | 2 NADH | 2 + 6 = **8** | 2 + 5 (or 3*) = 7 (or 5) |
| Link reaction | 0 | 2 NADH | **6** | 5 |
| Krebs cycle | 2 | 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ | 2 + 18 + 4 = **24** | 2 + 15 + 3 = 20 |
| **Total** | 4 | | **38** | **32 (or 30*)** |

*The cytoplasmic NADH of glycolysis cannot enter the mitochondrion directly — **malate-aspartate shuttle** (liver, heart) → 2.5 ATP per NADH → 32; **glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle** (muscle, brain; FADH₂ formed) → 1.5 → 30. **For the same reason, the old count is written as 36 in eukaryotes and 38 in prokaryotes.**

> **Common mistake:** NCERT calls 38 ATP the "**theoretical maximum**" — assumptions: a sequential, orderly pathway, NADH reaching the mitochondrion, no intermediate diverted to other syntheses, only glucose as the substrate. If both 36 and 38 are among the options, look at the context of the question (prokaryote/eukaryote, NCERT).

**Energy efficiency:** 38 × 7.3 kcal ≈ 277 kcal / 686 kcal ≈ **40%**; the rest is heat.

## 6. Respiratory quotient (RQ)

<div class="formula">

**RQ = volume of CO₂ released ÷ volume of O₂ consumed**

| Substrate/condition | RQ | Reason/example |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** | **1** | C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O |
| **Fat** | **<1 (~0.7)** | Tripalmitin: 2C₅₁H₉₈O₆ + 145O₂ → 102CO₂ + 98H₂O → 102/145 = **0.7** (less O, needs more O₂) |
| **Protein** | **~0.8–0.9** | |
| **Organic acids** | **>1** | Malic acid 1.33; **oxalic acid 4** (2(COOH)₂ + O₂ → 4CO₂ + 2H₂O) |
| **Anaerobic respiration** | **∞ (infinity)** | CO₂ released, O₂ zero |
| **CAM plants (night)** | **~0** | CO₂ is fixed, not released |
| Germinating **oily seeds** (castor, groundnut) | <1 | More O₂ needed to convert fat → sugar |
| Germinating **cereal grains** | ~1 | Starch |

</div>

**Measurement** — **Ganong's respirometer**; KOH absorbs CO₂.
**Blackman** — **floating** respiration (stored carbohydrate/fat) vs **protoplasmic** respiration (protein, during starvation).

## 7. Pentose phosphate pathway (PPP / HMP shunt)

**Warburg, Dickens, Lipmann, Horecker** (Warburg-Dickens-Lipmann pathway); location — **cytoplasm** (in plants also in plastids).

- **Oxidative phase:** glucose-6-phosphate → (**G6P dehydrogenase**, NADPH) → 6-phosphogluconate → (NADPH, CO₂) → **ribulose-5-phosphate**.
- **Non-oxidative (rearrangement) phase:** transketolase (TPP) and transaldolase → ribose-5-P, xylulose-5-P, sedoheptulose-7-P, **erythrose-4-P**, F-6-P, G3P.
- **Overall:** 6 G6P + 12 NADP⁺ → 5 G6P + 6 CO₂ + **12 NADPH**.
- **Significance:** (1) **NADPH** — fatty acid and steroid synthesis, reductive biosynthesis; (2) **ribose-5-P** — nucleotides, DNA/RNA, ATP, NAD; (3) **erythrose-4-P** — **shikimate pathway** → aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan) → **lignin, auxin (IAA), flavonoids, alkaloids**; (4) **no ATP is made directly**.
- **Relation to the Calvin cycle** — the Calvin cycle is called the "**reductive pentose phosphate pathway**" (same intermediates).

## 8. Lipid metabolism

**Breakdown:** **lipase** → **glycerol** + **fatty acids**. Glycerol → glycerol-3-P → **DHAP** → glycolysis. Fatty acids → **β-oxidation** (in plants in **glyoxysomes/peroxisomes**, in animals in mitochondria) — each round gives 2C (acetyl CoA) + 1 FADH₂ + 1 NADH.

<div class="formula">

**Palmitic acid (16C):** 7 rounds of β-oxidation → **8 acetyl CoA + 7 FADH₂ + 7 NADH**.
Old count: 8 × 12 + 7 × 2 + 7 × 3 = 131 − 2 (activation) = **129 ATP**; modern: 8 × 10 + 7 × 1.5 + 7 × 2.5 = 108 − 2 = **106 ATP**.
Fat ~9 kcal per gram, carbohydrate ~4 kcal — so storing fat in seeds is economical.

</div>

**Glyoxylate cycle** — **Kornberg and Krebs (1957)**; in the **glyoxysomes** of germinating **oily seeds** (castor, groundnut, sunflower); also in bacteria and fungi — **not in animals**.
- Two special enzymes — **isocitrate lyase** (isocitrate → succinate + glyoxylate) and **malate synthase** (glyoxylate + acetyl CoA → malate).
- **Both decarboxylation steps of Krebs are bypassed** → 1 succinate (4C) from 2 acetyl CoA → mitochondrion → OAA → PEP (PEP carboxykinase) → **gluconeogenesis → sucrose** → nourishment of the seedling.
- **Conversion of fat → sugar is possible only in plants/microbes** (animals make no net glucose from fatty acids).

**Synthesis:** fatty acid synthesis in plants occurs in **plastids** — **acetyl CoA carboxylase** (biotin) → malonyl CoA → **fatty acid synthase** (NADPH). Triglycerides — in the ER.

## 9. Protein/amino acid metabolism (basics)

- **Proteases/peptidases** → amino acids (stored proteins in germinating seeds).
- **Deamination** — removal of NH₃; the carbon skeleton enters Krebs (glucogenic/ketogenic).
- **Transamination** — **aminotransferases**, coenzyme **pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B₆)**; e.g. glutamate + OAA → α-KG + aspartate.
- **Assimilation of NH₄⁺** — **GS–GOGAT** (glutamine synthetase + glutamate synthase) — the main route; **reductive amination** (glutamate dehydrogenase: α-KG + NH₄⁺ + NADPH → glutamate) — details in Chapter 7.
- Plants do not excrete NH₃ — stored/transported as **amides (glutamine, asparagine)**; from legume nodules as **ureides (allantoin, allantoic acid)** — soybean.
- Secondary metabolites — **alkaloids** (morphine, nicotine, quinine), **terpenes**, **phenolics** — defence.

## 10. Factors affecting respiration and the climacteric

- **Temperature** — Q₁₀ ≈ 2 (0–35°C); enzymes denature at high temperature. Cold storage slows the respiration of fruits.
- **O₂** — anaerobic at low O₂; **extinction point** — the O₂ concentration at which anaerobic respiration stops (~5–10%).
- **CO₂ ↑ → respiration ↓** (controlled atmosphere storage).
- **Water** — respiration negligible in dry seeds; rapid after imbibition.
- **Injury/wounding** → respiration rises.
- **Respiratory climacteric** — a **sudden rise** in respiration and **ethylene** production just before ripening: **climacteric fruits** — **banana, mango, apple, tomato, papaya, guava, avocado**; **non-climacteric** — **lemon-orange, grape, pineapple, strawberry, watermelon, cherry** — these cannot be ripened with ethylene after picking.

## 2026-style practice questions (statements, sequence, matching, Assertion–Reason)

The questions below are written in the style of the 2026 paper (they are not actual previous questions). Solve them yourself first, then check the answer and reason.

**Q.1 (statement combination).** (I) Glycolysis occurs in both aerobic and anaerobic respiration. (II) Complex II pumps protons. (III) The Krebs cycle is amphibolic. (IV) The glyoxylate cycle is also found in animals. Correct — (A) I, III (B) I, II, III (C) II, IV (D) I, III, IV
**Answer: (A)** — II is wrong (complex II does not pump H⁺); IV is wrong (not in animals).

**Q.2 (sequence).** Krebs cycle intermediates — (I) Succinyl CoA (II) Isocitrate (III) Fumarate (IV) α-Ketoglutarate (V) Malate
**Answer:** II → IV → I → III → V.

**Q.3 (matching).** (a) Rotenone (b) Antimycin A (c) Cyanide (d) Oligomycin — (I) Complex IV (II) ATP synthase F₀ (III) Complex I (IV) Complex III
**Answer:** a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.

**Q.4 (Assertion–Reason).** A: The RQ of germinating castor seeds is less than 1. R: Fats contain little O, so their oxidation needs more O₂.
**Answer:** **Both true, R is the correct explanation.**

**Q.5 (situation).** The inflorescence of *Arum* lily becomes 10–15°C warmer than its surroundings and gives off a foul smell. Biochemical basis? — **Alternative oxidase (cyanide-resistant respiration) — complexes III and IV are bypassed, energy becomes heat instead of ATP; salicylic acid is the inducer.**

## Traps and exceptions

- **Glycolysis does not need O₂** — it is the first stage of both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
- **Substrate-level phosphorylation** — twice in glycolysis (1,3-BPGA → 3-PGA; PEP → pyruvate), once in Krebs (succinyl CoA → succinate).
- **The Krebs cycle does not use O₂ directly**, but without O₂ NAD⁺/FAD are not regenerated and the cycle stops.
- **Complex II does not pump protons** — so FADH₂ gives less ATP.
- **Cytochrome c and UQ are mobile carriers**, not complexes.
- **Cyanide blocks complex IV** — but in plants partial respiration continues because of AOX.
- **First 6C compound citrate; first 5C α-KG; first 4C succinyl CoA** (OAA is a 4C already present).
- **In fermentation CO₂ is released only in the alcoholic type**, not the lactic type.
- **RQ of fat <1, of organic acids >1**; anaerobic ∞; CAM at night ~0.
- **PPP makes NADPH, not ATP**; the glyoxylate cycle is in the **glyoxysome** (not the mitochondrion).
- **Enolase — fluoride**, **aconitase — fluoroacetate**, **succinate DH — malonate** (competitive).

## Quick revision

- Glycolysis — EMP, cytoplasm, net 2 ATP + 2 NADH.
- PFK — rate-limiting enzyme; aldolase — 6C → 2 × 3C.
- Pyruvate dehydrogenase — TPP, lipoic acid, CoA, FAD, NAD.
- Krebs — matrix; succinate DH in the inner membrane.
- Per turn 3 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 ATP + 2 CO₂.
- Complex I — rotenone; III — antimycin A; IV — CN⁻, CO; V — oligomycin; DNP — uncoupler.
- Cytochrome c oxidase — Cu and Fe (cyt a, a₃).
- 38 ATP (old) / 30–32 (modern).
- Yeast — ethanol + CO₂; *Lactobacillus* — lactic acid.
- Pasteur effect — O₂ reduces fermentation.
- RQ: carbohydrate 1, fat 0.7, protein 0.9, malic 1.33, oxalic 4, anaerobic ∞.
- PPP — NADPH, ribose-5-P, erythrose-4-P (shikimate).
- Glyoxylate cycle — Kornberg-Krebs 1957; isocitrate lyase + malate synthase.
- Palmitate — 129 (old) / 106 ATP.
- AOX — thermogenesis (*Arum*, *Symplocarpus*).
- Climacteric — banana, mango, apple, tomato; non-climacteric — lemon, grape, pineapple.
- Respiration is an amphibolic pathway.
