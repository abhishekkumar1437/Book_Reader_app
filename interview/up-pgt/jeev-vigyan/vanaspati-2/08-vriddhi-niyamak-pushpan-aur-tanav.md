This chapter covers points 3(v), (vi) and (vii) of Section A of the syllabus together — "Plant growth regulators", "Flowering and stress physiology", "Plant growth and movements"; from NCERT Class 11 "Plant Growth and Development" to B.Sc./M.Sc. (biosynthetic pathways, FT protein, SOS pathway, Cholodny-Went).
In UP PGT the most likely questions are "**hormone–discoverer–bioassay matching**", "**which statement about ABA/ethylene is correct**", "**examples of short-day and long-day plants**" and "**sequence of events (phytochrome/stomatal closure)**".

> **In simple words:** Plant hormones are "messenger chemicals" made in very small amounts. **Auxin** — produces roots and keeps the apex dominant; **gibberellin** — lengthens the stem; **cytokinin** — drives cell division and keeps leaves green; **ABA** — closes stomata in a crisis and puts seeds to sleep; **ethylene** — ripens fruit. Plants "measure" the length of day and night (photoperiodism) and cold (vernalisation) to decide the right time to flower.

## 1. Growth — basics

- Growth = **irreversible permanent increase** (size, weight, cell number); plants show **open/indeterminate growth** — meristems remain active throughout life.
- **Phases:** meristematic → elongation → maturation.
- **Arithmetic growth:** Lt = L₀ + rt (one daughter cell keeps dividing, the other differentiates) — a linear curve; e.g. root length.
- **Geometric/exponential growth:** W₁ = W₀eʳᵗ (r = relative growth rate/efficiency index) — both daughter cells divide.
- **Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve:** **lag** → **log (exponential)** → **stationary** phase.
- **Absolute growth rate** = total growth per unit time; **relative growth rate** = growth per unit initial size.
- **Measurement:** **arc auxanometer**, **crescograph (J.C. Bose)** — 10,000 times magnification.
- **Differentiation → dedifferentiation (e.g. interfascicular cambium) → redifferentiation**.
- **Plasticity / heterophylly:** developmental — **cotton, coriander, larkspur** (juvenile and mature leaves differ); environmental — **buttercup (*Ranunculus*)** (different leaves in water and in air).

## 2. Auxin — "to grow" (Greek *auxein*)

**Sequence of discovery:**

| Scientist (year) | Experiment |
|---|---|
| **Charles and Francis Darwin (1880)** | Coleoptile of ***Phalaris canariensis*** (canary grass) — does not bend towards light when the tip is covered; "*The Power of Movement in Plants*" |
| **Boysen-Jensen (1910–13)** | Bends when **gelatin** is placed between tip and base, but not with **mica** → the effect is chemical and diffusible |
| **Paál (1919)** | Placing the tip on one side causes bending to the opposite side even in the dark |
| **F.W. Went (1928)** | Placed ***Avena sativa*** (oat) coleoptile tips on an **agar block** → curvature produced by the block → "auxin" isolated; **Avena curvature test** |
| **Kögl and Haagen-Smit (1931); Kögl (1934)** | Auxin a, b and **heteroauxin = IAA** from human urine |

- **Natural:** **IAA (indole-3-acetic acid)**, **IBA (indole butyric acid)**, 4-chloro-IAA, PAA. **Synthetic:** **NAA (naphthalene acetic acid)**, **2,4-D**, 2,4,5-T, MCPA.
- **Precursor — tryptophan** (Zn needed); made in the shoot apical meristem and young leaves.
- **Polar transport** — in the shoot **basipetal (apex → base)**, **PIN proteins** (efflux carriers) and AUX1 (influx carrier) — the **chemiosmotic model**.
- **Acid growth hypothesis (Rayle and Cleland, 1970):** auxin → H⁺-ATPase activated → cell wall acidified → **expansins** activated → wall loosens → elongation by turgor.

**Functions/uses:**
1. **Apical dominance** — the apical bud suppresses lateral buds; cutting the apex (**pruning**) makes the plant bushy — **tea gardens, hedges**.
2. **Rooting of cuttings** — IBA, NAA.
3. **Parthenocarpy** — tomato.
4. **Prevents premature drop** of fruits and leaves (at low concentration); but promotes abscission of old leaves and fruits.
5. **Xylem differentiation**, cell division (with cytokinin).
6. **Herbicide** — **2,4-D kills broad-leaved (dicot) weeds** and leaves monocot crops (wheat, maize) unaffected; **"Agent Orange"** (2,4-D + 2,4,5-T; a defoliant in the Vietnam War, dioxin contamination).
7. **Synchronising flowering in pineapple** (NAA; actually acting through ethylene).

**Bioassays:** **Avena curvature test** (Went), **Avena coleoptile section test**, **split pea stem curvature test**, **root growth inhibition test**, cress root test.

## 3. Gibberellins (GA)

- **E. Kurosawa (1926)** — **bakanae disease ("foolish seedling")** of rice: plants abnormally tall and thin — caused even by the sterile filtrate of the fungus ***Gibberella fujikuroi*** (asexual stage ***Fusarium moniliforme***).
- **Yabuta and Sumiki (1938)** — isolated it and named it "gibberellin". Today **>100 GAs** (GA₁ … GA₁₃₆); **GA₃ (gibberellic acid)** is the most studied and commercial.
- **Chemical nature:** **terpenoid (diterpene)** — **ent-kaurene** pathway; acidic.

**Functions/uses:**
1. **Stem/internode elongation**; brings **genetic dwarfs (pea, maize)** to normal height — (hence the **dwarf pea/maize** bioassay).
2. **Bolting** — sudden internode elongation before flowering in **rosette plants** (cabbage, beet); **a substitute for the long-day and cold requirement**.
3. **Grapes** — longer stalks, larger fruits; improves the shape of apple.
4. **Sugarcane** — longer stem, **yield increased by ~20 tonnes per acre** (NCERT).
5. **Malting (brewing industry)** — synthesis of **α-amylase** in the **aleurone layer of barley seeds** → breakdown of starch — faster.
6. **Breaks seed/bud dormancy**; substitutes for light in light-requiring seeds (lettuce).
7. **Conifers** — shortens the juvenile phase for early seed production; parthenocarpy (apple, pear).
8. **Delays fruit ripening** (lemon) — extends the market period.

**Bioassays:** **dwarf pea/maize test**, **barley endosperm (aleurone) α-amylase test**, **lettuce hypocotyl elongation test**.
**Growth retardants (anti-gibberellins):** **CCC (cycocel/chlormequat)**, AMO-1618, phosfon-D, **paclobutrazol** — prevent lodging in wheat, induce flowering in mango.

## 4. Cytokinins — from "cytokinesis"

- **Skoog and Miller (1955)** — **kinetin (6-furfuryl-aminopurine)** from **autoclaved herring sperm DNA** — caused division in tobacco pith callus; **kinetin does not occur naturally in plants** (a DNA breakdown product).
- **Letham (1963)** — **zeatin** from **maize kernels (*Zea mays*)** — the first **natural** cytokinin; also in **coconut water** (van Overbeek, 1941 — embryo culture).
- **Derivatives of adenine (purine)**; synthesised in **root tips**, developing seeds, fruits. Synthetic — kinetin, **BAP (6-benzyl aminopurine)**.

**Functions:**
1. **Cell division** (with auxin).
2. **Organ formation in tissue culture** — **Skoog-Miller**: **high cytokinin : auxin → shoots**; **low → roots**; **equal → callus**.
3. **Delays senescence** — **Richmond-Lang effect (1957)**: applying kinetin to detached leaves keeps chlorophyll and proteins intact.
4. **Counters apical dominance** — growth of lateral buds.
5. **Nutrient mobilisation** — nutrients are drawn towards the site of application (sink).
6. Chloroplast development, new leaves, breaking dormancy.

**Bioassays:** **tobacco pith callus test**, **radish cotyledon expansion test**, **chlorophyll retention (senescence delay) test** (*Xanthium* leaf discs), ***Amaranthus* betacyanin test**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** How do the relative concentrations of auxin and cytokinin affect morphogenesis in tissue culture? (A) Absence of both is needed for organogenesis (B) High auxin:cytokinin → shoots, high cytokinin:auxin → roots (C) Equal concentrations always give direct embryogenesis without callus (D) **High auxin:cytokinin → roots, high cytokinin:auxin → shoots**
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)** — the classic result of Skoog and Miller (1957); (B) is its reverse, a trap.


## 5. Abscisic acid (ABA) — the "stress hormone"

- **Three independent discoveries:** **Addicott (1963)** — "**abscisin II**", an abscission-promoting substance from cotton bolls; **Wareing** — "**dormin**", a dormancy-inducing substance from buds of *Acer* (sycamore); and **inhibitor-β** (Bennet-Clark and Kefford, 1953). **Cornforth (1965)** — all three are the same → **ABA (named in 1967)**.
- **Chemical nature:** **sesquiterpene (15C)**; from **carotenoids (violaxanthin → xanthoxin)** — in chloroplasts/plastids.

**Functions:**
1. **Closes stomata** (water stress) — mechanism in Chapter 4.
2. Induces **seed and bud dormancy** — **antagonist of GA**; seed maturation, **LEA proteins**, desiccation tolerance; **prevents vivipary** — in ABA-deficient mutants (maize *vp*) seeds germinate on the cob itself.
3. **Growth inhibitor** — inhibits seed germination.
4. **Stress tolerance** — gene expression under drought, salinity, cold.
5. A role in **abscission** (hence the original name; but today **ethylene is regarded as the main controller of abscission**).
6. Promotes senescence.

**Bioassays:** **rice seedling growth inhibition**, **stomatal closure**, oat coleoptile growth inhibition, cotton abscission test.

## 6. Ethylene — the gaseous hormone

- **Neljubow (1901)** — **triple response** of pea seedlings in illuminating gas; **Cousins (1910)** — gas from oranges ripens bananas; **Gane (1934)** — plants make ethylene themselves.
- **Biosynthesis (Yang cycle — S.F. Yang):** **methionine → SAM → ACC** (**ACC synthase** — rate-limiting) → **ethylene** (**ACC oxidase**, O₂ needed).

**Functions:**
1. **Fruit ripening** — burst of respiration and ethylene in **climacteric** fruits (Chapter 6); for artificial ripening, **ethephon (2-chloroethyl phosphonic acid)** — releases ethylene in the plant. (The use of **acetylene** from calcium carbide is **banned**.)
2. **Triple response** — dicot seedlings in the dark: **stem elongation stops, swelling (thickening), horizontal growth**; **apical hook**.
3. **Senescence and abscission** (leaves, flowers, fruits).
4. Induction/synchronisation of flowering in **pineapple and mango**.
5. Increases **female flowers in cucumber** (sex expression) → higher yield; (GA increases male flowers).
6. Rapid elongation of internodes/petioles in **deep-water rice** — keeps leaves above water.
7. **Root hair** formation, root growth; breaks seed/bud dormancy (groundnut seeds, potato tubers).
8. **Epinasty** — downward bending of petioles (in waterlogging).

**Inhibitors:** **AVG (aminoethoxyvinylglycine)** — ACC synthase; **silver ions (Ag⁺, silver thiosulphate — STS)** — receptor block (extends the life of cut flowers); **1-MCP (1-methylcyclopropene)** — receptor; **CO₂** (high) — competitive.
**Bioassay/measurement:** **triple response (pea)**, **gas chromatography**.

## 7. Comparative summary of regulators

| Regulator | Precursor/nature | Discovery | Main bioassay | A characteristic use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Auxin | Tryptophan (indole) | Darwin, Went | Avena curvature | Rooting of cuttings, 2,4-D herbicide |
| Gibberellin | Terpenoid (ent-kaurene) | Kurosawa | Dwarf pea, barley α-amylase | Sugarcane yield, grapes, malting |
| Cytokinin | Adenine (purine) | Skoog-Miller, Letham | Tobacco callus, radish cotyledon | Delaying senescence, shoot formation |
| ABA | Carotenoid (sesquiterpene) | Addicott, Wareing | Rice seedling, stomata | Stomatal closure, dormancy |
| Ethylene | Methionine (gas) | Neljubow, Cousins | Triple response | Fruit ripening (ethephon) |

**Other regulators (B.Sc./M.Sc.):** **brassinosteroids** (*Brassica napus* pollen — brassinolide; plant steroid hormone; elongation, xylem differentiation); **jasmonates** (jasmonic acid — wound/herbivore resistance, proteinase inhibitors; tendril coiling); **salicylic acid** (**systemic acquired resistance — SAR**, PR proteins; **thermogenesis** in *Arum* — "calorigen"); **strigolactones** (germination of *Striga*/*Orobanche* seeds, inhibition of branching, mycorrhizal signal); **polyamines** (spermidine); **systemin** (the first plant peptide hormone, tomato); **karrikins** (germination from smoke).

**Interactions:** auxin–cytokinin (apical dominance; root-shoot ratio); **GA–ABA (dormancy and germination — antagonists)**; high auxin concentration → ethylene synthesis (which is why excess auxin inhibits growth).

> **Common mistake:** "ABA is the main hormone of abscission" — despite its name, **the main inducer of abscission is ethylene**; the main functions of ABA are **stomatal closure and dormancy**. Likewise **kinetin is not natural** — the first natural cytokinin is **zeatin**.

## 8. Photoperiodism

**Garner and Allard (1920)** — **Maryland Mammoth tobacco** (flowered only in the greenhouse in winter) and **Biloxi soybean** — the flowering response to day length.

| Class | When it flowers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-day plants (SDP) — actually long-night** | Light period **shorter than the critical** (night longer than the critical, **uninterrupted**) | ***Xanthium*** (cocklebur — critical photoperiod ~15.5 h), **chrysanthemum, soybean (Biloxi), Maryland Mammoth tobacco, *Cosmos*, *Dahlia*, poinsettia, rice (most varieties), sugarcane, cotton (some), strawberry (some)** |
| **Long-day plants (LDP) — short-night** | Light period **longer than the critical** | **Wheat, barley, oat, rye (most), spinach, radish, beet, lettuce, *Hyoscyamus niger* (henbane — ~11 h), *Arabidopsis*, potato (tuberisation in SD), rose (some)** |
| **Day-neutral (DNP)** | No effect of photoperiod | **Tomato, cucumber, sunflower, maize, chilli, cotton (most), pea (some)** |
| Intermediate-day, dual-day | | *Mikania*, *Bryophyllum* (LSDP — long then short) |

- **The critical factor = the dark period** (Hamner and Bonner, 1938, *Xanthium*) — in SDP a **night break**: a **flash of red light** in the middle of the long night → flowering **is prevented**; giving **far-red** immediately after → the effect is reversed (phytochrome). In LDP a night break **promotes** flowering.
- **The photoperiod is perceived by the leaf** — **Knott (1934)**, spinach; giving the right photoperiod to even one leaf is enough (*Xanthium*).
- **Florigen** — **Chailakhyan (1936)** — a flowering hormone travelling from the induced leaf to the apex; **grafting** experiments: an uninduced receptor also flowers when joined to an induced SDP donor. Modern identity — **FT protein (FLOWERING LOCUS T)** — *Arabidopsis* (**Hd3a** in rice) — induced in the leaf by **CONSTANS (CO)** protein → **through the phloem** to the shoot apical meristem → with **FD** → activates floral identity genes (*AP1*, *LFY*).
- **Photoinductive cycles** — **one** cycle is enough in *Xanthium*.
- **Uses:** off-season flowering of chrysanthemum for the market by giving artificial long nights; simultaneous flowering in breeding.

## 9. Phytochrome

- **Borthwick and Hendricks (1952)** — **Beltsville (USA)** — germination of **lettuce (*Lactuca*, Grand Rapids)** seeds: **red (660 nm) → germination**, **far-red (730 nm) → inhibition**, the **last light given** decides (**reversibility**). **Butler and colleagues (1959)** — isolated it and named it "phytochrome".
- **Structure:** a **chromoprotein** — **apoprotein** + **phytochromobilin (open-chain tetrapyrrole) chromophore**; a dimer.
- **Two forms:** **Pr** (red-absorbing, λmax ~660 nm, **inactive**, blue-green) ⇌ **Pfr** (far-red-absorbing, ~730 nm, **active**); Pr → Pfr by red light; Pfr → Pr by far-red or **slowly in darkness (dark reversion)**. Dark-grown plants have only Pr. Active Pfr moves **from the cytoplasm to the nucleus** and causes the breakdown of **PIFs** (phytochrome interacting factors).
- **Types (*Arabidopsis*):** **phyA (light-labile, far-red responses)** to **phyE** (phyB — light-stable, shade avoidance).
- **Responses:** seed germination, **de-etiolation**, leaf expansion, flowering (photoperiodism), **shade avoidance (stem elongation when the R:FR ratio falls)**, anthocyanin, chlorophyll synthesis.
- **Other photoreceptors:** **cryptochrome** (blue/UV-A — de-etiolation, biological clock), **phototropin** (blue — **phototropism, stomatal opening, chloroplast movement**), **UVR8** (UV-B).

## 10. Vernalisation

- Induction/acceleration of flowering by **low temperature (about 0–5°C, for a few weeks)**. Term by **Lysenko (1928)** — "**yarovization**"; **Klippart (1857)** — early observations on winter wheat.
- **Examples:** **winter varieties** — wheat, barley, rye (sown in autumn, experience cold under snow, flower in spring; do not flower if sown in spring); **biennials** — **cabbage, beet, carrot, turnip**, *Hyoscyamus* (biennial species).
- **Site of perception — the shoot apex (meristem)** (also the embryo) — only **actively dividing cells**; O₂, water and sugar are needed.
- **Hypothetical hormone "vernalin" (Melchers)** — never isolated.
- **GA substitutes for the cold requirement in some biennials** (Lang) — bolting.
- **Devernalisation** — the effect is cancelled by **high temperature (~35°C)** immediately after vernalisation.
- **Molecular (M.Sc.):** in *Arabidopsis*, **FLC (FLOWERING LOCUS C)** is a flowering repressor; cold → **epigenetic silencing of FLC** (histone methylation, VRN genes) → "memory of winter"; *FRIGIDA* increases FLC.

## 11. Dormancy, senescence and abscission

**Causes of seed dormancy:** **hard/impermeable seed coat** (legumes), immature embryo, **inhibitors** (ABA, phenolics, parasorbic acid), need for light/cold.
**Methods of breaking it:** **scarification** (mechanical, acid — H₂SO₄), **stratification** — moist cold treatment (apple), **GA₃**, KNO₃, **red light** (lettuce), thiourea.

**Senescence:** whole-plant (annuals — monocarpic; bamboo, *Agave*), leaf (deciduous), sequential (lower leaves), organ. **Cytokinin delays it; ethylene and ABA promote it.**
**Abscission:** **abscission zone** — **cellulase, polygalacturonase (pectinase)** dissolve the middle lamella; **ethylene induces it, auxin (coming from the leaf) prevents it**; protective layer (cork).

## 12. Stress physiology

**Biotic** — pathogens, herbivores; **abiotic** — drought, salinity, heat, cold, flooding, heavy metals, UV, ozone.

| Stress | Effect | Plant adaptation/response |
|---|---|---|
| **Drought/water deficit** | Loss of turgor, stomata close, photosynthesis ↓ | **ABA ↑ → stomata close**; **osmotic adjustment** — **proline, glycine betaine**, sugar alcohols (mannitol), trehalose; **LEA proteins/dehydrins**; deep roots, leaf rolling, thick cuticle, sunken stomata, CAM; **drought escape** (short-lived — desert annuals), **drought avoidance** and **tolerance** (resurrection plants — *Selaginella lepidophylla*) |
| **Salinity** | Osmotic stress (water uptake difficult) + **ion toxicity (Na⁺, Cl⁻)** + K⁺ deficiency | **Na⁺ exclusion** (from the root — **SOS pathway**: SOS3-SOS2 → **SOS1 Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter**), **compartmentation of Na⁺ in the vacuole** (**NHX antiporter**), compatible solutes; **halophytes** — **salt glands** (*Atriplex*, *Avicennia*), succulence (*Salicornia*, *Suaeda*), salt hairs |
| **Heat** | Protein denaturation, membrane fluidity ↑, PS II damage | **Heat-shock proteins (HSPs)** — **molecular chaperones** — HSP70, HSP90, small HSPs; transpirational cooling; leaf orientation, reflective hairs |
| **Chilling (0–15°C)** | In tropical plants (rice, maize, banana) **membrane fluidity falls** (liquid → gel) | **Unsaturated fatty acids ↑** (desaturase enzymes), cold acclimation (**CBF/DREB** genes), **antifreeze proteins** |
| **Freezing** | **Extracellular ice crystals** → dehydration of cells; intracellular ice is lethal | Sugars, **hardening** |
| **Flooding/submergence (hypoxia)** | Lack of O₂ | **Aerenchyma** — ethylene-induced, **pneumatophores**, ADH fermentation, internode elongation in deep-water rice (**SNORKEL** genes) or quiescence (**SUB1A** gene — "scuba rice") |
| **Oxidative (ROS)** | Superoxide, H₂O₂, OH• | **SOD (superoxide dismutase)**, **catalase**, **ascorbate peroxidase**, glutathione, carotenoids |
| **Heavy metals** | Enzyme inhibition | **Phytochelatins**, **metallothioneins**; hyperaccumulators — *Thlaspi*, *Brassica juncea* (**phytoremediation**) |
| **Pathogens** | | **Hypersensitive response (HR)**, **SAR (salicylic acid)**, ISR (jasmonate-ethylene), phytoalexins |

## 13. Plant movements

### A. Locomotory movements — change of position of the whole organism/cell

**Taxis:** **phototaxis** — *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*; **chemotaxis** — **fern antherozoids towards malic acid**, **moss antherozoids towards sucrose**; thermotaxis. **Cytoplasmic streaming (cyclosis)** — leaf cells of *Hydrilla*, *Vallisneria*.

### B. Movements of curvature — organs of a fixed plant

**1. Tropic — depends on the direction of the stimulus (growth movement):**

| Type | Stimulus | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Phototropism** | Unidirectional light | Stem positive, root negative; **blue light (phototropin)**; **Cholodny–Went theory (1928)**: light shifts auxin to the **shaded side** → more elongation there → bending towards light |
| **Gravitropism/geotropism** | Gravity | Root positive (in the **columella** cells of the root cap, **statoliths — amyloplasts with starch grains** settle down — **Haberlandt and Němec, 1900, starch-statolith hypothesis**); in the root auxin accumulates on the lower side and **inhibits growth** → bends down; stem negative; the **clinostat** neutralises the effect of gravity |
| **Hydrotropism** | Water | Root positive |
| **Thigmotropism** | Touch | **Tendrils** coil around a support (less growth on the touching side, more on the opposite side) |
| **Chemotropism** | Chemicals | Growth of the **pollen tube** towards the ovule |

**2. Nastic — independent of the direction of the stimulus, usually in dorsiventral organs; by turgor or growth:**

| Type | Stimulus | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Photonasty** | Light intensity | Dandelion (*Taraxacum*) opens by day; *Oenothera* (opens at night) |
| **Nyctinasty (sleep movements)** | Day-night | **Leaves of legumes (*Albizia*, tamarind, *Cassia*) fold at night** — changes in turgor of the **pulvinus** |
| **Thermonasty** | Temperature | **Tulip, *Crocus*** — open when temperature rises |
| **Seismonasty** | Shock/vibration/touch | ***Mimosa pudica*** (touch-me-not) — **K⁺ and Cl⁻ leave** the lower motor cells of the **pulvinus**, water follows → loss of turgor → leaflets and petiole droop; **electrical (action potential)** and chemical (**turgorin**) signals; recovers in ~10–20 minutes |
| **Thigmonasty/haptonasty** | Touch | **Insectivores** — tentacles of ***Drosera***, ***Dionaea*** (Venus flytrap — closes in ~0.1–0.5 s when two trigger hairs are touched) |
| Epinasty/hyponasty | Unequal growth | Opening of buds (upper surface grows more — epinasty), ethylene-induced drooping of petioles |

**3. Autonomic movements:** ***Desmodium gyrans*** (**Indian telegraph plant**) — the lateral leaflets jerk up and down without any external stimulus; **circumnutation** — spiral oscillation of the shoot tip (Darwin); flower development, opening of petals.

**4. Hygroscopic — of dead tissues:** **awns of oat/*Avena* and *Erodium***, dehiscence of sporangia (the **annulus** of ferns), the **peristome** of mosses, **elaters** of *Equisetum*, bursting of pods.

> **Exam angle:** The movement of touch-me-not is **nastic (seismonasty)** — a **turgor movement**, not a growth movement — so it is **reversible and fast**; coiling of tendrils is **tropic (thigmotropism)** — a **growth movement**, relatively slow and irreversible.

## 2026-style practice questions (statements, sequence, matching, Assertion–Reason)

The questions below are written in the style of the 2026 paper (they are not actual previous questions). Solve them yourself first, then check the answer and reason.

**Q.1 (statement combination).** (I) Kinetin occurs naturally in plants. (II) GA induces α-amylase in the aleurone layer of barley. (III) The precursor of ethylene is methionine. (IV) ABA is formed from carotenoids. Correct — (A) II, III, IV (B) I, II (C) I, III, IV (D) All
**Answer: (A)** — I is wrong: kinetin is a DNA breakdown product; the natural one is zeatin.

**Q.2 (sequence).** Photoperiodic flowering — (I) Perception of photoperiod in the leaf (II) Stabilisation of CONSTANS protein (III) Transport of FT protein through the phloem (IV) Activation of floral identity genes at the apex
**Answer:** I → II → III → IV.

**Q.3 (matching).** (a) Avena curvature (b) Dwarf pea (c) Radish cotyledon expansion (d) Triple response — (I) Cytokinin (II) Ethylene (III) Auxin (IV) Gibberellin
**Answer:** a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.

**Q.4 (Assertion–Reason).** A: When the long night of a short-day plant is broken by a flash of red light, flowering is prevented. R: Red light converts Pr into Pfr.
**Answer:** **Both true, R is the correct explanation** (the effect is reversed if far-red is given immediately).

**Q.5 (situation).** A plant growing in saline soil accumulates proline in its leaves and pumps Na⁺ out of its roots. Explanation? — **Proline — a compatible solute (osmotic adjustment, maintaining water uptake); SOS1 Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter — protection from ion toxicity.**

## Traps and exceptions

- **Darwin used the coleoptile of *Phalaris*, Went that of *Avena*.**
- **2,4-D kills dicot (broad-leaved) weeds** — not monocots.
- **Kinetin is not natural; zeatin is the first natural** cytokinin (maize).
- **Ethylene is the only gaseous** classical hormone; precursor **methionine**.
- **ABA and GA are antagonists** (dormancy); **auxin and cytokinin** are antagonists in apical dominance.
- **A short-day plant is really a long-night plant**; the critical period is that of darkness.
- *Xanthium* is an SDP, *Hyoscyamus niger* an LDP — both have nearly similar critical photoperiods (~11–15 h) but opposite responses — it is "shorter/longer than the critical value" that decides the class, not the absolute hours.
- **The active form of phytochrome is Pfr**; Pr is inactive.
- **Vernalisation is perceived by the shoot apex; photoperiod by the leaf.**
- **Florigen = FT protein** (not RNA — an earlier claim of FT mRNA was retracted).
- **Touch-me-not — turgor movement; tendril — growth movement.**
- **Fern antherozoids — malic acid; moss antherozoids — sucrose.**
