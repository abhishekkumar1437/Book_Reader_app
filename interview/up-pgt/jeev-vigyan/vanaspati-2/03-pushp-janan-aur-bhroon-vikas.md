This chapter belongs to the "reproduction and development" part of point 2 of Section A of the syllabus — from NCERT Class 12 "Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants" to B.Sc. embryology (types of embryo sac, types of endosperm, embryogenesis).
In UP PGT 2026, "What does the embryo sac develop from?" was asked directly. Ploidy (n/2n/3n), discoverers (Nawaschin, Strasburger), example-matching and numerical questions of the "how many meiotic divisions" type are favourites of this chapter.

> **In simple words:** Pollen grains (male) form in the anther of the stamen; the embryo sac (female), which contains the egg cell, forms in the ovule inside the ovary. The pollen grain reaches the stigma (pollination), a tube grows out of it to the ovule and releases two male gametes — one fuses with the egg to form the embryo, the other fuses with two polar nuclei to form the endosperm (food). This **double fertilisation** is the hallmark of angiosperms alone.

## 1. Anther and microsporogenesis

A typical anther is **bilobed**, with two chambers in each lobe — **dithecous** and **tetrasporangiate**. Malvaceae has **monothecous** anthers.

### Anther wall (outside to inside)

| Layer | Features and function |
|---|---|
| **Epidermis** | Protection |
| **Endothecium** | **Fibrous thickenings of α-cellulose**; help in **dehiscence**; the cells of the **stomium** lack thickenings — the anther splits there |
| **Middle layers** | 1–3, short-lived, degenerate |
| **Tapetum** | Innermost; **nourishes the sporogenous tissue**; cells with dense cytoplasm, often **multinucleate and polyploid**; **secretory/glandular** or **amoeboid/periplasmodial** type; **Ubisch bodies** — precursors of sporopollenin; **callase enzyme** — dissolves the callose wall of the tetrad to release the microspores; pollenkitt |

> **Common mistake:** **If the tapetum degenerates at the wrong time (too early or too late), male sterility results** — the use of **cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS)** in hybrid seed production is based on this.

**Microsporogenesis:** Sporogenous tissue → **pollen mother cell (PMC/microspore mother cell, 2n)** → **meiosis** → **microspore tetrad (n)**. Types of tetrad — **tetrahedral** — most common, dicots; **isobilateral** — common in monocots; **decussate** — *Magnolia*; **T-shaped** — *Aristolochia*; **linear** — *Halophila*. If the pollen grains stay together in the tetrad it is **compound pollen** — *Typha*, *Drimys*; a whole chamber as one unit = **pollinium** — **Asclepiadaceae (*Calotropis*) and orchids**; in *Calotropis* two pollinia joined by a **translator** = the **translator apparatus**.

### Pollen grain — the male gametophyte

- **Exine** — **sporopollenin** — **the most resistant organic material known**; unaffected by high temperature, strong acids and alkalis, and enzymes → **pollen grains are preserved as fossils** (palynology). Places where sporopollenin is absent = **germ pores**. **Intine** — **cellulose and pectin**; forms the pollen tube.
- **Unequal mitosis** of the microspore → a large **vegetative/tube cell** — food, large irregular nucleus; and a small, spindle-shaped **generative cell** — floating in the cytoplasm of the vegetative cell.
- **In over 60% of angiosperms pollen grains are shed at the 2-celled stage**; in the rest the generative cell divides to form **two male gametes** → shed at the **3-celled** stage (e.g. Poaceae, Brassicaceae).
- **Viability:** **rice, wheat — about 30 minutes**; Rosaceae, Leguminosae, Solanaceae — **several months**. **Pollen banks** — **cryopreservation** in **liquid nitrogen (−196°C)**.
- **Allergy:** ***Parthenium hysterophorus*** (carrot grass/congress grass — came to India with imported wheat), *Chenopodium*, *Amaranthus* — asthma/bronchitis. Pollen tablets — nutritional supplements.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In angiosperms, where does microsporogenesis take place and what is its immediate product? (A) In the ovary; diploid spores after meiosis (B) In the ovule; diploid megaspores after mitosis (C) In the anther; haploid microspores after meiosis (D) In the stigma; pollen grains after fertilisation
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)** — meiosis of the PMC (2n) gives a tetrad of haploid microspores.

## 2. Pistil and ovule

Pistil = **stigma** + **style** + **ovary**. Ovule = **funicle** → **hilum**; **integuments** — one (**unitegmic** — Gamopetalae) or two (**bitegmic** — Polypetalae, monocots); **micropyle**; **chalaza** — base; **nucellus** — nourishment. A large nucellus = **crassinucellate**; a thin one = **tenuinucellate**.

| Ovule type | Position of the axis | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Orthotropous (atropous)** | Straight — micropyle, chalaza and funicle in one line | ***Polygonum***, *Piper*, *Cycas* (primitive type) |
| **Anatropous** | Inverted by 180°, micropyle next to the funicle; **raphe** | **Most common** (most families) — sunflower, *Ricinus*, most Gamopetalae |
| **Campylotropous** | The ovule itself is curved, embryo sac slightly curved | ***Capsella***, Brassicaceae, pea, gram (Fabaceae) |
| **Hemianatropous** | 90° | *Ranunculus*, *Primula* |
| **Amphitropous** | Embryo sac horseshoe-shaped | *Alisma*, *Butomus* |
| **Circinotropous** | Funicle long, encircles the ovule | ***Opuntia*** (Cactaceae) |

## 3. Megasporogenesis and the embryo sac

In the micropylar part of the nucellus a single **megaspore mother cell (MMC, 2n)** → **meiosis** → **linear tetrad of four megaspores** → usually **one chalazal megaspore is functional**, the three micropylar ones degenerate.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In angiosperms the embryo sac develops from — (A) Pollen grain (B) Functional megaspore (C) Microspore (D) Megaspore mother cell
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B) Functional megaspore** — in the monosporic (Polygonum) type. (D) The mother cell forms megaspores by meiosis; only in the tetrasporic type do all four megaspore nuclei take part in the embryo sac.

**Polygonum type (monosporic):** The nucleus of the functional megaspore undergoes **three successive free-nuclear mitotic divisions** → 2 → 4 → 8 nuclei (4 at each pole). Then cell walls form:

- **Micropylar end — egg apparatus:** 1 **egg cell** + 2 **synergids** — at the tip of the synergids is the **filiform apparatus** — attracts and guides the pollen tube.
- **Chalazal end:** 3 **antipodal cells**.
- **Centre:** a large **central cell** with 2 **polar nuclei** — they fuse before fertilisation into the **secondary nucleus (2n)**.

## 4. Pollination

| Type | Meaning | Note/examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Autogamy** | On the stigma of the same flower | **Cleistogamous** flowers never open — ***Viola*** (pansy), ***Oxalis***, ***Commelina*** — **assured seed set** with no need for a pollinator; *Commelina* has both **chasmogamous** and cleistogamous (underground) flowers |
| **Geitonogamy** | On another flower of the same plant | **Functionally cross-pollination** (needs an agent), **genetically self-pollination** (same genotype) |
| **Xenogamy** | On a different plant | Genetic variation |

### Agents of pollination

| Agent | Adaptations in the plant | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind (anemophily)** | Light, smooth, very numerous pollen grains; **large feathery/hairy stigma**; usually **one ovule** per ovary; flowers small, colourless, odourless | **Grasses, maize** (the **"tassels"** = style and stigma — **the silk of the cob**), *Cannabis*, *Pinus* (winged pollen) |
| **Water (hydrophily)** — only ~30 genera, mostly monocots | Pollen grains protected by a **mucilaginous covering** | ***Vallisneria*** — **on the water surface (epihydrophily)**: female flowers reach the surface on long coiled stalks, male flowers break free and float; ***Hydrilla***; ***Zostera*** (seagrass) — **under water (hypohydrophily)**, **long ribbon-like pollen grains** |
| In water but wind/insect pollinated | | **Water hyacinth, lotus** (insects), *Nymphaea* |
| **Insects (entomophily)** | Large, colourful, fragrant, nectar; sticky pollen grains | Most; **honeybees** are the main pollinators |
| **Birds (ornithophily)** | Red-orange, odourless, abundant nectar | **Silk cotton (*Bombax*)**, ***Erythrina*** (coral tree), *Callistemon* (bottlebrush), *Butea* (palash); **sunbirds, hummingbirds** |
| **Bats (chiropterophily)** | Night-blooming, strong smell, large | ***Kigelia*** (sausage tree), ***Adansonia*** (baobab), *Anthocephalus* (kadamb) |
| Snails (malacophily) | | *Arisaema*, some Araceae |

### Special pollination mechanisms

- **Yucca–yucca moth (*Yucca*–*Tegeticula*, formerly *Pronuba*)** — the moth lays eggs in the ovary and pollinates; **obligate mutualism**; neither can complete its life cycle without the other.
- **Fig–fig wasp (*Ficus*–*Blastophaga*)** — the wasp lays eggs in return for pollination.
- ***Salvia*** — **lever/turnstile mechanism**.
- ***Ophrys*** (orchid) — a petal **resembles a female bee** → the male bee performs **pseudocopulation**.
- ***Amorphophallus*** — according to NCERT the tallest "flower" (~6 feet; actually an inflorescence); ***Rafflesia*** — the largest single flower (~1 metre; total root parasite).
- Animals that **steal nectar and pollen** do not pollinate — "pollen/nectar robbers".

### Outbreeding devices

1. **Dichogamy** — **protandry**: anthers mature first — **sunflower, *Salvia***; **protogyny**: stigma matures first — *Magnolia*, *Aristolochia*, *Plantago*.
2. **Herkogamy** — a physical barrier (*Calotropis* — pollinia).
3. **Heterostyly** — ***Primula*** — "pin" (long style) and "thrum" (short style) flowers; studied by Darwin.
4. **Self-incompatibility** — genetic; controlled by **S-alleles**.
   - **Gametophytic (GSI)** — determined by the S-allele of the pollen grain **itself (n)**; the tube is stopped in the style (**S-RNase**) — **Solanaceae (*Nicotiana*, *Petunia*)**, Rosaceae, Poaceae.
   - **Sporophytic (SSI)** — determined by the genotype of the **parent plant (2n)** (proteins coming from the tapetum); germination is stopped on the stigma surface itself — **Brassicaceae**, Asteraceae.
5. **Unisexuality** — **monoecy** — prevents autogamy but not geitonogamy (maize, castor); **dioecy** — prevents both (papaya).

## 5. Pollen-pistil interaction and fertilisation

The stigma **recognises the right pollen** (chemical signals) → germination → the **pollen tube** emerges through a germ pore and grows through the style to the ovule. Routes of entry:

| Route | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Through the micropyle (porogamy)** | Most common |
| **Through the chalaza (chalazogamy)** | ***Casuarina*** — discovered by **Treub (1891)** |
| **Through the integument (mesogamy)** | ***Cucurbita*** |

The tube enters one **synergid** (guided by the filiform apparatus) → releases the two male gametes:
- **Syngamy:** male gamete + egg → **zygote (2n)** → embryo.
- **Triple fusion:** male gamete + 2 polar nuclei → **primary endosperm nucleus (PEN, 3n)** → endosperm.
- **Double fertilisation** = syngamy + triple fusion — discovered by **S.G. Nawaschin, 1898** — in ***Lilium martagon*** and ***Fritillaria tenella***; independently by **Guignard, 1899**.

> **Exam angle:** In one embryo sac **5 nuclei take part in fertilisation** — 2 male gametes + 1 egg + 2 polar nuclei. After fertilisation the **synergids and antipodals degenerate**; ovule → seed, integuments → seed coat, ovary → fruit, ovary wall → pericarp.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Statements on modern trends in angiosperm embryology — (I) In vitro fertilisation and embryo culture help overcome post-fertilisation barriers and study early development. (II) Molecular markers and gene-expression studies reveal the genetic regulation of embryogenesis. (III) Double fertilisation was discovered only recently by molecular techniques and was classically unknown. Correct combination? (A) I and II only (B) I, II and III (C) I and III only (D) II and III only
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — statement III is wrong: double fertilisation was discovered by **Nawaschin (1898)** and Guignard (1899) through classical microscopic studies. (In vitro pollination/fertilisation — a contribution of the Delhi University school of **Kanta and Maheshwari**; **P. Maheshwari** — father of Indian plant embryology.)


## 6. Endosperm

| Type | Development | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Nuclear** — most common | **Free-nuclear divisions** of the PEN, walls form later from the periphery | **Coconut** (water = free-nuclear, kernel = cellular), wheat, maize, *Capsella* |
| **Cellular** | A wall after every division | ***Petunia***, ***Datura***, *Impatiens* (balsam) |
| **Helobial** | First division cellular (large micropylar, small chalazal chamber), then free-nuclear | **Order Helobiales** — *Eremurus*, *Asphodelus* (monocots) |

- **Ruminate endosperm** — irregular, wrinkled surface — **areca nut (*Areca*)**, nutmeg.
- **Perisperm** — **remaining nucellus (2n)** — **black pepper, beet**.
- **Xenia** — **direct effect of pollen on the endosperm** (grain colour in maize); **metaxenia** — effect of pollen on the **maternal tissue (fruit)** (date palm).
- **The endosperm of gymnosperms is haploid (n)** — it forms before fertilisation and is the female gametophyte itself.

## 7. Development of the embryo

The endosperm develops first (to nourish the embryo). The zygote is at the **micropylar end**.

**Dicot (*Capsella bursa-pastoris*) — crucifer/onagrad type:** zygote → transverse division → a **large basal cell** (towards the micropyle) + a **terminal cell**. The basal cell → **suspensor** (pushes the embryo into the endosperm, nourishment), and its uppermost cell, the **hypophysis** → tip of the radicle and root cap. The terminal cell → **proembryo** → **globular** → **heart-shaped** → mature embryo (**embryonal axis**, two cotyledons, **epicotyl** → plumule, **hypocotyl** → radicle).

**Monocot (grass):** the single cotyledon = **scutellum**; **coleoptile** over the plumule, **coleorhiza** over the radicle; some grasses have an **epiblast** (regarded as the remnant of a second cotyledon).

**Types of embryogenesis (Johansen) — B.Sc.:** **Onagrad (crucifer)** — Brassicaceae, Onagraceae; **asterad** — Asteraceae, Lamiaceae; **solanad** — Solanaceae; **caryophyllad** — Caryophyllaceae; **chenopodiad**; **piperad** — Piperaceae (first division vertical).

## 8. Seed and fruit — in brief

- Seed = **mature fertilised ovule**; **albuminous** — wheat, maize, castor, coconut; **exalbuminous** — pea, gram, groundnut (endosperm used up by the embryo).
- **Dormancy**; oldest living seed — ***Lupinus arcticus*** (~10,000 years, Arctic tundra); ***Phoenix dactylifera*** (date palm) — a **~2000-year-old** seed germinated at **Masada (Israel)**.
- **Orchid seeds** — extremely minute, thousands in one fruit; also *Orobanche*, *Striga* (parasites).

## 9. Apomixis, polyembryony, parthenocarpy

| Phenomenon | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Apomixis** — term by **Winkler (1908)** | Formation of **seeds without fertilisation** — asexual reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction | **Asteraceae**, **grasses** |
| — **Recurrent apomixis** | **Diploid embryo sac** (without meiosis) — **diplospory**: from the MMC; **apospory**: from a somatic cell of the nucellus | *Taraxacum*, *Parthenium*, *Hieracium* |
| — **Adventive embryony** | **Embryos directly from cells of the nucellus/integument** | **Lemon-orange (*Citrus*), mango** — **nucellar embryos** |
| **Polyembryony** | **More than one embryo** in a seed | **Leeuwenhoek (1719)** — discovered in **orange** seeds; *Citrus*, mango, jamun (*Syzygium*) |
| **Parthenocarpy** — term by **Noll (1902)** | **Fruit without fertilisation** — usually **seedless** | **Banana**, grape (some varieties), pineapple; induced by spraying **auxin/gibberellin** |
| **Parthenogenesis** | Embryo from an unfertilised egg | |

> **Exam angle:** Hybrid seeds have to be bought every year (segregation); if **apomixis genes** are introduced into hybrids, farmers could sow the same seed for years — a well-known NCERT point. Nucellar embryos are **clones of the mother plant**.

## 10. Ploidy (chromosome number) table

| Structure | Ploidy (angiosperms) |
|---|---|
| Nucellus, integuments, MMC, PMC, anther wall, tapetum | 2n |
| Megaspore, microspore, egg, synergids, antipodals, vegetative and generative cells, male gametes | n |
| Polar nucleus (each) | n |
| Secondary nucleus | 2n |

| Zygote, embryo, seed coat, pericarp | 2n |
| Perisperm | 2n |
| Gymnosperm endosperm | n |

## 11. Numerical questions

<div class="formula">

- 1 PMC (meiosis) → **4 pollen grains** → 8 male gametes.
- 1 MMC (meiosis) → 4 megaspores → **1 embryo sac** (Polygonum).
- To form **N seeds**: meiotic divisions = **N (MMC) + N/4 (PMC)**. Example: **100 seeds → 100 + 25 = 125 meiotic divisions**.
- To form 1 embryo sac (Polygonum) — 1 meiotic + **3 mitotic** divisions.
- 2 male gametes from 1 pollen grain — 1 meiotic (PMC) + **2 mitotic** (microspore and generative cell).
- **If the endosperm has 24 chromosomes** → n = 8 → 16 in the embryo/leaf, 8 in the egg.

</div>

## 2026-style practice questions (statements, sequence, matching, Assertion–Reason)

The questions below are written in the style of the 2026 paper (they are not actual previous questions). Solve them yourself first, then check the answer and reason.

**Q.1 (statement combination).** (I) The tapetum is the innermost layer of the anther wall. (II) Sporopollenin is a component of the intine. (III) In most angiosperms pollen grains are shed at the 2-celled stage. (IV) Pollinia are found in orchids and *Calotropis*. Correct — (A) I, III, IV (B) I, II (C) II, III (D) All
**Answer: (A)** — II is wrong: sporopollenin is in the **exine**.

**Q.2 (sequence).** Correct order of development of the Polygonum-type embryo sac — (I) Meiosis of the MMC (II) Three free-nuclear mitotic divisions (III) The chalazal megaspore becomes functional (IV) Wall formation and 7 cells
**Answer:** I → III → II → IV.

**Q.3 (matching).** (a) Chalazogamy (b) Mesogamy (c) Surface water pollination (d) Pseudocopulation — (I) *Vallisneria* (II) *Ophrys* (III) *Casuarina* (IV) *Cucurbita*
**Answer:** a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.

**Q.4 (Assertion–Reason).** A: Geitonogamy is genetically the same as self-pollination. R: In it, pollen grains reach another flower of the same plant.
**Answer:** **Both true, R is the correct explanation** — one plant = one genotype.

**Q.5 (situation).** Hybrid seeds have to be bought fresh every year. Which biotechnological goal would solve this problem? — **Introducing apomixis genes into hybrids** — the seeds would be maternal clones, with no segregation.

## Traps and exceptions

- **The embryo sac develops from the functional megaspore, not the MMC** (Polygonum).
- **Synergids** attract the pollen tube (**filiform apparatus**); antipodals do not.
- **Geitonogamy is genetically self-pollination.**
- **Cleistogamous flowers are always self-pollinated**; *Commelina* has both types.
- **Nawaschin — double fertilisation (1898)**; **Strasburger — syngamy (1884) and the Polygonum embryo sac**; **Amici (1824)** — observed the pollen tube.
- **The tapetum is the innermost layer** of the anther wall (not the endothecium).
- **Sporopollenin is in the exine**, not the intine.
- **Malvaceae — monothecous anthers** (not bilobed).
- **The endosperm develops first, the embryo later.**
- **Coconut water is free-nuclear endosperm**, not cellular.
- **Parthenocarpic fruits are seedless**, but not every seedless fruit is parthenocarpic (in triploid watermelon fertilisation occurs, the embryo aborts).

## Quick revision

- Anther wall: epidermis → endothecium → middle layers → tapetum.
- Tapetum — nourishment, Ubisch bodies, callase; abnormality → male sterility.
- Sporopollenin — most resistant organic material; absent at germ pores.
- Pollinia — *Calotropis*, orchids.
- Pollen viability of rice-wheat ~30 minutes; pollen bank −196°C.
- Most common ovule — anatropous; orthotropous — *Polygonum*; circinotropous — *Opuntia*.
- Polygonum — 8 nuclei, 7 cells; Strasburger 1879.
- *Oenothera* — 4-nucleate; *Peperomia*, *Penaea* — 16-nucleate; *Allium* — bisporic.
- Cleistogamy — *Viola*, *Oxalis*, *Commelina*.
- *Vallisneria* — surface water pollination; *Zostera* — under water.
- Birds — silk cotton, *Erythrina*; bats — *Kigelia*, *Adansonia*.
- *Yucca* — *Tegeticula* moth; *Ophrys* — pseudocopulation.
- Heterostyly — *Primula*.
- GSI — Solanaceae; SSI — Brassicaceae.
- Chalazogamy — *Casuarina* (Treub); mesogamy — *Cucurbita*.
- Double fertilisation — Nawaschin 1898 (*Lilium*, *Fritillaria*).
- Cellular endosperm — *Petunia*, *Datura*; helobial — monocots.
- Perisperm — black pepper, beet; ruminate — areca nut.
- Polyembryony — Leeuwenhoek 1719, orange; adventive embryony — *Citrus*, mango.
- Apomixis — Winkler 1908; parthenocarpy — Noll 1902.
