## Chapter 1 — Angiosperm morphology

1. ⭐ Difference between potato and sweet potato — **potato is a stem (tuber), sweet potato a root (tuberous)** (eyes on the potato = buds).
2. ⭐ Respiratory roots of *Rhizophora* — **pneumatophores, negatively geotropic** (breathing pores).
3. Velamen tissue — **aerial roots of epiphytic orchids**.
4. Colocasia and elephant foot yam — **corm** (vertical underground stem).
5. ⭐ Photosynthetic organ of *Opuntia* — **phylloclade (stem)**; leaves are spines.
6. *Asparagus* — **cladode**.
7. ⭐ "Thorns" of rose — **prickles** (neither thorns nor spines).
8. Tendril of pea — **terminal leaflets**; of pumpkin — **axillary bud (stem)**.
9. ⭐ Cyathium — ***Euphorbia*/poinsettia** (coloured bracts, one female and many one-stamened male flowers).
10. Hypanthodium — ***Ficus*** (fig-wasp pollination).
11. Verticillaster — **tulsi (Lamiaceae)**.
12. ⭐ Twisted/valvate/imbricate/vexillary — **hibiscus/aak/gulmohar/pea**.
13. ⭐ Tetradynamous androecium — **mustard**; syngenesious — **sunflower**; monadelphous — **hibiscus**.
14. Parietal placentation with a replum — **mustard**.
15. ⭐ Epigynous flowers — **guava, cucumber, sunflower**.
16. ⭐ Edible part of coconut — **endosperm** (fruit — fibrous drupe).

## +3/−1 marking — guessing strategy

<div class="formula">

**Expected value (EV)** — +3 for correct, −1 for wrong:
- **After eliminating two options** (guess from the remaining 2): EV = (3 − 1)/2 = **+1** → **always attempt**.
- **After eliminating one option** (from the remaining 3): EV = (3 − 2)/3 = **+0.33** → worth attempting, but riskier.
- **Without eliminating anything** (blind guess, 1 of 4): EV = (3 − 3)/4 = **0** → no gain, only uncertainty — **skip it**.

</div>

- In **statement-combination questions**, first check the statement that looks **most certainly wrong** (e.g. "biological activity is highest in the B-horizon") — this often removes two options at once.
- In **Assertion–Reason** the order of options was changed in every question — read the letters carefully each time.
- In **matching**, start with the most certain pair (e.g. Gir — lion); most options are eliminated by a single pair.
- Long options with words like **"only", "always", "never", "without any effect"** are usually wrong; in biology the "best explanation" is often the one that **links two processes in the right direction**.
- Keep about 90 minutes for the 90 biology questions — 30–40 seconds for factual questions from this book (morphology, deficiency diseases, treaties), 1–1.5 minutes for situation-based ones.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Look-alike names:** *Peperomia* (16-nucleate) vs *Polygonum* (8-nucleate); **hadrocentric** (ferns) vs **leptocentric** (*Dracaena*); **khadar** (new) vs **bangar** (old).
- **Reversed relationships:** more auxin → roots (not shoots); temperature ↑ → O₂ solubility ↓; SDP = long-night plant.
- **"Most" facts:** most abundant protein — RuBisCO; most resistant organic material — sporopollenin; element needed least — Mo; largest terrestrial biome — taiga; highest GWP — SF₆.
- **NCERT vs newer figures:** ATP 38 vs 30–32; India's hotspots 3 (NCERT) vs 4; if both appear in the options, look at the context of the question.
