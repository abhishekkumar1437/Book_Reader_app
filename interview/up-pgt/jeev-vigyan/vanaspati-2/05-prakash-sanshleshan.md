This chapter covers point 3(ii) of Section A of the syllabus, "Photosynthesis" — from NCERT Class 11 "Photosynthesis in Higher Plants" to B.Sc./M.Sc. (sequence of electron carriers, S-states, C₄ subtypes, ATP counts).
In the statement-combination questions of UP PGT, the most likely topics from here are **"the correct sequence of carriers in the Z-scheme"**, **"which statements on C₃ vs C₄ are correct"**, **discovery–scientist matching** and **ATP/NADPH counts**. The 2026 paper asked about **Liebig's law of the minimum** (limiting factor) — Blackman's law is its physiological form.

> **In simple words:** A leaf is a solar factory. In the first stage (light reaction, in the thylakoids) light energy splits water, O₂ is released and the energy currency ATP and the "reducing power" NADPH are made. In the second stage (Calvin cycle, in the stroma) ATP and NADPH are used to turn CO₂ from the air into sugar. Plants of hot, dry places (maize, sugarcane — C₄; prickly pear — CAM) have evolved clever tricks to "store" CO₂ first.

<div class="formula">

**Overall equation:** 6CO₂ + 12H₂O —(light, chlorophyll)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
**The O₂ released comes from water** (not from CO₂) — Ruben and Kamen (¹⁸O), van Niel's hypothesis.

</div>

## 2. Photosynthetic pigments

**Chloroplast:** double membrane; stacks of **thylakoids** (flattened sacs) = **grana**, connected by **stroma lamellae**; **stroma** — enzymes (RuBisCO), DNA, 70S ribosomes, starch grains. **Light reaction — thylakoid membrane; dark reaction — stroma.**

| Pigment | Features | Absorption peaks | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chlorophyll a (Chl a)** — C₅₅H₇₂O₅N₄Mg | **–CH₃** (methyl) group; **main/primary pigment** — in the reaction centre | ~430 and ~662 nm | All oxygenic photosynthesisers (including cyanobacteria) |
| **Chlorophyll b** — C₅₅H₇₀O₆N₄Mg | **–CHO** (aldehyde) group; accessory | ~453 and ~642 nm | Higher plants, green algae |
| Chlorophyll c, d | | | Brown algae/diatoms (c), red algae (d) |
| **Carotenoids** — **carotenes** (β-carotene — orange; precursor of vitamin A), **xanthophylls** (yellow — lutein, zeaxanthin, violaxanthin, fucoxanthin) | Accessory; **photoprotection** (quenching of excess energy and singlet oxygen; **xanthophyll cycle**) | 400–500 nm (blue-violet) | |
| **Phycobilins** — phycoerythrin (red), phycocyanin (blue) | Water-soluble, **phycobilisomes** | Green-yellow light | **Red algae, cyanobacteria** |

- Structure of chlorophyll — **porphyrin ring (head)** — **Mg²⁺** in the centre, four pyrrole rings; **phytol tail** (anchors in the membrane). **Haemoglobin has Fe** — a porphyrin structure similar to chlorophyll.
- **In paper chromatography, from top to bottom:** **carotene (orange-yellow) → xanthophyll (yellow) → Chl a (blue-green) → Chl b (yellow-green)**.
- **Photosynthetically active radiation (PAR): 400–700 nm**. The **absorption spectrum** (which light a pigment absorbs) and the **action spectrum** (in which light photosynthesis is greatest) both have **blue and red peaks**; the action spectrum roughly matches the absorption spectrum of Chl a — evidence that Chl a is the main pigment. **Green light is absorbed least** (reflected — so the leaf looks green).

### Emerson effect

- **Red drop:** at wavelengths **above 680 nm** (far-red), Chl a still absorbs, yet the **quantum yield falls sharply**.
- **Enhancement effect:** when far-red (>680) light is given together with shorter-wavelength (~650) light, the rate is **greater than the sum** of the two separate rates → **two photosystems work in series**.
- **Quantum requirement** — **8 photons** per O₂ (or per CO₂); **quantum yield = 1/8 = 0.125**.

## 3. Photosystems

| Feature | Photosystem I (PS I) | Photosystem II (PS II) |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction centre | **P700** (Chl a) | **P680** (Chl a) |
| Location | **Stroma lamellae** and the outer margins of grana (open to the stroma) | **Appressed membranes of the grana** |
| Primary electron acceptor | A₀ (Chl a), A₁ (phylloquinone/vitamin K₁), Fe-S centres (Fx, FA, FB) | **Pheophytin** → Q_A → Q_B |
| Electron donor | Plastocyanin (PC) | **Water** (via the oxygen-evolving complex) |
| Function | NADPH; cyclic phosphorylation | Splitting of water, O₂ |
| Discovered first/later | First | Later (but comes first in the Z-scheme) |

Each photosystem = **reaction centre** + **light-harvesting complex (LHC/antenna)** — hundreds of chlorophyll and carotenoid molecules that pass energy to the centre by **resonance transfer**.

## 4. Z-scheme and photolysis of water

<div class="formula">

**Non-cyclic electron flow (Z-scheme):**
H₂O → **OEC (Mn₄CaO₅)** → P680 → **pheophytin** → **Q_A → Q_B (plastoquinone, PQ)** → **cytochrome b₆f complex** → **plastocyanin (PC, Cu-containing)** → P700 → A₀ → A₁ → Fe-S → **ferredoxin (Fd)** → **ferredoxin-NADP⁺ reductase (FNR)** → **NADP⁺ → NADPH**

**Photolysis of water:** 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + O₂ + 4e⁻ — by the **oxygen-evolving complex (OEC)** of PS II, on the **lumen (inner) side** of the thylakoid; **Mn²⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻** are required. **Kok's S-states** — S₀ → S₄; one O₂ after four photons (four flashes).

## 5. Photophosphorylation

| Feature | **Non-cyclic** | **Cyclic** |
|---|---|---|
| Photosystems | **Both PS I and PS II** | **PS I only** |
| Electrons | From water to NADP⁺ — **do not return** | P700 → Fd → **cyt b₆f** → PC → **back to P700** |
| Products | **ATP + NADPH + O₂** | **ATP only** |
| Splitting of water | Occurs | **No** — no O₂ |
| Location | Grana | **Stroma lamellae** |
| When | Normally | **Shortage of NADP⁺**, **light above 680 nm**, C₄ bundle sheath cells (extra ATP) |

**Chemiosmosis (Mitchell):** H⁺ accumulate in the lumen — (1) **splitting of water** in the lumen, (2) **PQ** takes H⁺ from the stroma and releases them into the lumen (at cyt b₆f), (3) **NADP⁺ reductase** uses up H⁺ in the stroma → **lumen pH ~4–5, stroma ~8** → H⁺ return to the stroma through the **CF₀ channel** of **ATP synthase (CF₀–CF₁)** → **CF₁ (projecting into the stroma)** makes ADP + Pi → ATP. (In mitochondria it is the reverse — H⁺ in the intermembrane space, F₁ in the matrix.)

## 6. Calvin cycle (C₃ cycle / reductive pentose phosphate pathway)

Location — **stroma**; not directly dependent on light, but it runs only in the day because of ATP-NADPH and enzyme activation (thioredoxin, pH, Mg²⁺).

1. **Carboxylation:** **RuBP (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, 5C)** + CO₂ —(**RuBisCO**)→ unstable 6C → **2 × 3-PGA (3-phosphoglyceric acid, 3C)** — **the first stable product**.
2. **Reduction:** 3-PGA + ATP → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate + NADPH → **glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P/triose phosphate)**.
3. **Regeneration:** G3P (5/6 part) + ATP → RuBP (ribulose-5-phosphate kinase).

<div class="formula">

**Per CO₂: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH.**
**For one glucose (6 CO₂): 6 turns, 18 ATP, 12 NADPH.**
3 CO₂ → net gain of 1 G3P (9 ATP, 6 NADPH). G3P → sucrose (in the cytoplasm) or starch (in the stroma).

</div>

**RuBisCO (ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase)** — **the most abundant protein on Earth**; 8 large (coded by chloroplast DNA) + 8 small (coded by nuclear DNA) subunits; affinity for **both CO₂ and O₂** — when O₂ is high, oxygenase activity (photorespiration). A slow enzyme.

## 7. Photorespiration — C₂ cycle / glycolate pathway

At high temperature, high O₂ and low CO₂ (stomata closed), the **oxygenase** activity of RuBisCO:
RuBP + O₂ → **1 × 3-PGA + 1 × phosphoglycolate (2C)**.

**Three organelles:** **chloroplast** (glycolate formed) → **peroxisome** (glycolate → glyoxylate + H₂O₂ (catalase); → **glycine**) → **mitochondrion** (2 glycine → **serine + CO₂ + NH₃**) → peroxisome (serine → glycerate) → chloroplast (→ 3-PGA).

- **No ATP or NADPH is made**; instead **ATP is used up**; **~25% of fixed carbon is lost as CO₂**.
- **Almost negligible in C₄ plants** (high CO₂ concentration in the bundle sheath cells).
- **Post-illumination burst of CO₂** — shown by Decker.
- Possible benefits — dissipation of excess light energy (photoprotection), amino acids (glycine, serine).

## 8. C₄ pathway (Hatch–Slack pathway)

**Kranz anatomy:** around the vascular bundle are **large, thick-walled bundle sheath cells with many chloroplasts and no intercellular spaces**, with a ring of mesophyll outside them ("Kranz" = wreath in German). **Dimorphic chloroplasts:** **granal** in the mesophyll, **agranal/few grana** in the bundle sheath (NADP-ME type) — less PS II.

1. **Mesophyll cell:** CO₂ → HCO₃⁻ (carbonic anhydrase); **PEP (phosphoenolpyruvate, 3C)** + HCO₃⁻ —(**PEP carboxylase, PEPCase**)→ **oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C)** — **the first stable product** → **malic acid** or **aspartic acid**.
2. The 4C acid moves through plasmodesmata into the **bundle sheath** → **decarboxylation** → **CO₂ (into the Calvin cycle via RuBisCO)** + **pyruvate (3C)**.
3. Pyruvate returns to the mesophyll → **pyruvate phosphate dikinase (PPDK)** + ATP → PEP (costs 2 ATP-equivalents).

<div class="formula">

**In C₄, for one glucose: 30 ATP + 12 NADPH** (C₃: 18 ATP + 12 NADPH) — the extra 12 ATP go to regenerating PEP.
**PEPCase has a very high affinity for CO₂ (HCO₃⁻) and does not react with O₂** → a CO₂ "pump" → CO₂ concentration in the sheath ~10 times higher → photorespiration negligible.

</div>

**C₄ subtypes (by decarboxylating enzyme):** **NADP-malic enzyme** — **maize, sugarcane, sorghum** (sheath chloroplasts agranal); **NAD-malic enzyme** — *Amaranthus*, *Atriplex*, *Panicum miliaceum*; **PEP carboxykinase (PEP-CK)** — *Panicum maximum* (guinea grass), *Chloris*.

**C₄ examples:** maize, sugarcane, sorghum, pearl millet, *Amaranthus*, *Atriplex*, *Cyperus rotundus* (motha — the worst weed), *Euphorbia* (some), *Portulaca*, *Salsola*, doob (*Cynodon*). About **~3% of angiosperm** species, but ~23% of global primary production (estimate). **Single-cell C₄** — *Bienertia*, *Suaeda aralocaspica* (without Kranz, B.Sc./M.Sc.).

### C₃ vs C₄ vs CAM

| Feature | C₃ | C₄ | CAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| First stable product | 3-PGA (3C) | OAA (4C) | OAA → malate (night) |
| Primary CO₂ acceptor | RuBP | PEP | PEP |
| Carboxylating enzyme | RuBisCO | PEPCase (mesophyll) + RuBisCO (sheath) | PEPCase (night) + RuBisCO (day) |
| Separation | — | **Spatial** — two cell types | **Temporal** — night/day, same cell |
| Kranz anatomy | No | **Yes** | No (succulent, large vacuoles) |
| Photorespiration | High | Negligible | Negligible/low |
| CO₂ compensation point | **High (~40–100 ppm)** | **Low (0–10 ppm)** | Low (in darkness) |
| Optimum temperature | 20–25°C | **30–45°C** | ~35°C |
| Light saturation | At low intensity (~10–25% of full sunlight) | **Does not saturate (keeps rising up to high intensity)** | |
| Water use efficiency | Low | ~2× C₃ | **Highest** |
| ATP per glucose | 18 | 30 | ~30–39 |
| Examples | Wheat, rice, potato, soybean, most trees | Maize, sugarcane, sorghum | *Bryophyllum*, *Opuntia*, pineapple, *Agave*, *Kalanchoe* |

### CAM (Crassulacean acid metabolism)

- **Night:** stomata open → PEPCase → OAA → **malic acid** → **stored in the vacuole** → **acidity rises at night** (pH falls).
- **Day:** stomata closed (water conservation) → malate leaves the vacuole and is decarboxylated → CO₂ → Calvin cycle; acidity falls during the day.
- **Heyne (1815)** noticed that *Bryophyllum* leaves taste sour in the morning and bland in the evening.
- **Facultative CAM** — *Mesembryanthemum crystallinum* — switches from C₃ to CAM under stress.

> **Common mistake:** "C₄ plants have no Calvin cycle" — **wrong**; the Calvin cycle runs in the **bundle sheath cells**, and the C₄ pathway is only a "pre-concentration" of CO₂. Likewise, **in CAM too it is ultimately the Calvin cycle** that makes the sugar.

## 9. Factors affecting photosynthesis

### Blackman's law of limiting factors (1905)

> "When a chemical process is affected by several factors, its rate is **determined by the factor that is nearest to its minimum value (the most limiting)**; the rate will increase only if that factor is increased."

This is the process-rate form of **Liebig's law of the minimum (1840)** — "plant growth is limited by the nutrient that is available in the smallest quantity (relative to need)".

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A crop grown at optimum temperature, light and water shows poor growth despite adequate macronutrients; adding one specific micronutrient restores growth. Explanation in terms of limiting factors? (A) Limiting factors act only under extreme stress (B) Growth depends on total nutrient availability; micronutrient deficiency has negligible effect (C) **Liebig's law — the factor least available relative to need limits growth despite the abundance of others** (D) Shelford's law — growth is maximum only when all factors are at their highest together.
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)** — (D) is a wrong statement of Shelford's law of tolerance (Shelford: both deficiency **and excess** are limiting).

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Light** | Rate rises linearly with intensity, then saturates; in C₃ saturation at **~10% of full sunlight** (NCERT) → light is rarely limiting in nature (except in shade/dense forest); excessive light → **solarisation/photo-oxidation**, chlorophyll breakdown. **Light compensation point** — where photosynthesis = respiration |
| **CO₂** | **The main limiting factor** (~0.04% / ~420 ppm in air); in C₃ the rate rises up to ~450 ppm; **CO₂ enrichment in greenhouses** (tomato, capsicum) increases yield; C₄ saturates at ~360 ppm |
| **Temperature** | Dark (enzymatic) reactions are temperature-sensitive; C₄ has a higher optimum |
| **Water** | Little direct effect; indirect through **stomatal closure** and wilting of leaves (water is hardly ever limiting as a reactant) |
| **O₂** | More O₂ → photorespiration → rate falls (**Warburg effect**) |
| Internal | Amount of chlorophyll, leaf age, source-sink relations, stomata |

## 10. Bacterial photosynthesis and chemosynthesis (in brief)

- **Anoxygenic** — **purple and green sulphur bacteria** (*Chromatium*, *Chlorobium*); **bacteriochlorophyll**; donor **H₂S** (no O₂); only one photosystem.
- **Cyanobacteria** — oxygenic, Chl a, both photosystems; the O₂ in Earth's atmosphere is their gift.
- **Chemoautotrophs** — *Nitrosomonas* (NH₃ → NO₂⁻), *Nitrobacter* (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻), *Thiobacillus*, iron bacteria — energy from inorganic oxidation.

## Traps and exceptions

- **O₂ comes from water**, not CO₂; **the O of glucose comes from CO₂**.
- **Chl a has a methyl (–CH₃), Chl b an aldehyde (–CHO)**.
- **PS II was discovered after PS I** but comes **first** in electron flow; the names follow the order of discovery.
- **Cyclic phosphorylation makes no O₂ or NADPH**.
- **Plastocyanin has Cu**, ferredoxin Fe-S, OEC Mn.
- **Calling the Calvin cycle the "dark reaction" is misleading** — it does not run at night (light-dependent enzyme activation).
- **The first product in C₄ is OAA**, not malate; **in CAM too the first product is OAA**.
- **RuBisCO = the most abundant protein (plants/Earth)**; in the human body the most abundant is collagen.
- **Photorespiration makes no ATP**; it has nothing to do with respiration — the name is misleading.
- **In C₄ plants RuBisCO is in the bundle sheath, not the mesophyll**.
- **Kranz anatomy = C₄**; but *Bienertia* has single-cell C₄ without Kranz.

## Quick revision

- Priestley — mint-candle; Ingenhousz — light; Engelmann — *Cladophora*, action spectrum.
- van Niel — O₂ from water; Hill reaction — isolated chloroplasts, no CO₂; Ruben-Kamen — ¹⁸O.
- Calvin — *Chlorella*, ¹⁴C, Nobel 1961; Hatch-Slack — C₄ (1966).
- Emerson — red drop, enhancement effect; quantum requirement 8.
- PAR 400–700 nm; green light absorbed least.
- Chromatography from the top: carotene, xanthophyll, Chl a, Chl b.
- PS I — P700, stroma lamellae; PS II — P680, grana.
- OEC — Mn, Ca, Cl; Kok's S-states.
- Z-scheme: H₂O → P680 → pheo → PQ → b₆f → PC → P700 → Fd → FNR → NADPH.
- Cyclic — PS I only, ATP only.
- ATP synthase CF₁ faces the stroma; lumen is acidic.
- Calvin: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH per CO₂; glucose 18 ATP + 12 NADPH.
- RuBisCO — most abundant protein; carboxylase + oxygenase.
- Photorespiration — chloroplast, peroxisome, mitochondrion; ~25% carbon loss.
- C₄ — PEPCase, OAA, Kranz, 30 ATP; maize, sugarcane, sorghum.
- CAM — malic acid at night; *Bryophyllum*, pineapple, *Opuntia*.
- Blackman 1905 — limiting factors; Liebig 1840 — law of the minimum.
- CO₂ — the main limiting factor; C₃ light saturation ~10% of sunlight.
