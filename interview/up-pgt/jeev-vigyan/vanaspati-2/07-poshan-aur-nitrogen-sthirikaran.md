This chapter belongs to point 3(iv) of Section A of the syllabus, "Plant nutrition (nutrients, nitrogen fixation)" — from NCERT Class 11 "Mineral Nutrition" (old edition) to B.Sc./M.Sc. (structure of nitrogenase, Nod factors, GS-GOGAT).
In UP PGT expect "**element–deficiency disease matching**" (whiptail–Mo, khaira–Zn), "**which element is mobile**", "**statements about nitrogenase**" and **limiting-factor** situations like the one in 2026. Biofertilisers are in [Economic Botany and Biofertilisers](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/vanaspati-1/aarthik-vanaspati-aur-jaiv-urvarak).

> **In simple words:** A plant takes C and O from the air and H from water; the remaining elements (N, P, K etc.) come from the soil as ions. Some elements are needed in large amounts (macronutrients), some in a pinch (micronutrients) — but both are "essential"; a shortage of even one stops overall growth. Air is 78% nitrogen, but plants cannot take it directly — some bacteria (like *Rhizobium* in the nodules of pulses) convert it to ammonia.

## 1. Hydroponics and essentiality

- **Hydroponics** — **Julius von Sachs (1860)** — growing plants in a nutrient solution without soil; a method for identifying essential elements; today used for commercial production of tomato, cucumber, lettuce. Aeroponics — roots in the air, sprayed with the solution.
- **Criteria of essentiality — Arnon & Stout (1939):**
  1. Without the element the plant **cannot complete its life cycle** (cannot form seeds).
  2. The requirement must be **specific** — **it cannot be replaced by any other element**.
  3. The element must be **directly involved in metabolism**.

### Essential elements (17 according to NCERT)

| Class | Amount (dry weight) | Elements |
|---|---|---|
| **Macronutrients** — 9 | **>10 mmol/kg** | **C, H, O, N, P, K, S, Ca, Mg** (C, H, O from air and water) |
| **Micronutrients** — 8 | **<10 mmol/kg** | **Fe, Mn, Cu, Mo, Zn, B, Cl, Ni** |
| **Beneficial** | | **Na** (C₄ and CAM plants, halophytes), **Si** (grasses, rice, *Equisetum* — rigidity, disease resistance), **Co** (legumes — N₂ fixation, for vitamin B₁₂ in *Rhizobium*), **Se** |

> **Memory trick (micronutrients):** "**Mo**re **Cu**te **Fe**rrets **Z**ip **B**y **M**y **Cl**ean **Ni**ckel" — Mo, Cu, Fe, Zn, B, Mn, Cl, Ni.

**Classification by function (NCERT):** (i) **structural** — C, H, O, N; (ii) **energy-related compounds** — Mg (chlorophyll), P (ATP); (iii) **enzyme activators** — Mg²⁺ (RuBisCO, PEPCase), Zn²⁺ (alcohol dehydrogenase), Mo (nitrogenase); (iv) **osmotic potential** — K⁺ (stomata).

## 2. Each element — form absorbed, functions and deficiency symptoms

| Element | Form absorbed | Main functions | Deficiency symptoms / disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | **NO₃⁻** (main), NH₄⁺, some organic | Proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll, hormones, vitamins | **Chlorosis of older leaves**, stunted growth, early flowering |
| **P** | **H₂PO₄⁻**, HPO₄²⁻ | Membranes (phospholipids), ATP, NADP, nucleic acids, phosphorylation | Leaves **dark green to purple-red (anthocyanin)**, delayed maturity, weak roots |
| **K** | **K⁺** | **Osmosis/turgor, stomatal opening**, anion-cation balance, **activator of over 60 enzymes**, protein synthesis | **Scorching of margins and tips of older leaves (marginal scorch/necrosis)**, weak stem (lodging), short internodes |
| **Ca** | **Ca²⁺** | **Middle lamella (calcium pectate)**, spindle, membrane permeability, second messenger (calmodulin) | **Young leaves and meristems** affected, death of the tip, **blossom-end rot of tomato**, **bitter pit** of apple |
| **Mg** | **Mg²⁺** | **Central atom of chlorophyll**, ribosome structure, activator of RuBisCO-PEPCase, DNA/RNA synthesis | **Interveinal chlorosis of older leaves** |
| **S** | **SO₄²⁻** | **Cysteine, methionine**, CoA, **biotin, thiamine**, **ferredoxin**, smell of mustard-onion | **Chlorosis of young leaves** (like N, but in young leaves) |
| **Fe** | **Fe³⁺** (main; also after reduction to Fe²⁺ at the root) | **Ferredoxin, cytochromes**, catalase; needed for **chlorophyll formation** (but not a component of chlorophyll) | **Interveinal chlorosis of young leaves**; "lime-induced chlorosis" in calcareous soil |
| **Mn** | **Mn²⁺** | **Photolysis of water (OEC)**, activator of respiratory and photosynthetic enzymes | **Grey speck of oats**, **marsh spot** of pea, interveinal chlorosis |
| **Zn** | **Zn²⁺** | Carboxylases, alcohol dehydrogenase, carbonic anhydrase; **auxin synthesis** (tryptophan) | **Khaira disease of rice** (India — **Y.L. Nene, Pantnagar, 1966**), **little leaf**, **rosetting**, **white bud of maize** |
| **Cu** | **Cu²⁺** | **Plastocyanin, cytochrome c oxidase**, ascorbic acid oxidase | **Die-back/exanthema of *Citrus***, **reclamation disease** of cereals |
| **B** | **BO₃³⁻**, B₄O₇²⁻ (boric acid) | **Ca²⁺ uptake and use**, **pollen germination, pollen tube growth**, cell elongation-differentiation, **carbohydrate (sugar) translocation** | **Heart rot of beet**, **brown heart/browning** of turnip/cauliflower, **internal cork** of apple, top disease of tobacco, hollow stem |
| **Mo** | **MoO₄²⁻** (molybdate) | **Nitrogenase, nitrate reductase** — N metabolism | **Whiptail of cauliflower** — leaves narrow, whip-like; the element **needed in the smallest amount** |
| **Cl** | **Cl⁻** | **Splitting of water** with Mn, anion-cation balance, cell solute | Wilting, stubby roots, bronze colour |
| **Ni** | **Ni²⁺** | **Urease** (urea → NH₃), hydrogenase | **Leaf-tip necrosis (urea toxicity)**, **"mouse-ear"** of pecan |

### Mobility and site of symptoms

| | Elements | Where symptoms appear first |
|---|---|---|
| **Mobile** — remobilised from older to younger leaves | **N, P, K, Mg** (Cl, Mo, Zn partly) | **Older/lower leaves** (these are also withdrawn first during senescence) |
| **Immobile** — structural components, not easily released | **Ca, S, Fe, B, Cu** (Mn partly) | **Young/apical leaves and meristems** |

> **Memory trick:** "**NPK-Mg desert the old** first" — deficiency of mobile elements shows in older leaves; "**Ca-S-Fe-B hit the young**" — immobile, shows in young leaves.

**Types of deficiency symptoms (NCERT):** **chlorosis** (N, K, Mg, S, Fe, Mn, Zn, Mo), **necrosis/tissue death** (Ca, Mg, Cu, K), **inhibition of cell division** (low N, K, S, Mo; also Ca, B), **delayed flowering** (N, S, Mo).

### Critical concentration and toxicity

- **Critical concentration** — the concentration below which growth falls.
- **Toxicity** — the range for micronutrients is narrow; the concentration that **reduces dry weight by ~10%** is considered toxic.
- **Mn toxicity** — Mn **competes with the uptake of Fe and Mg**, **prevents Mg from binding to enzymes**, and **inhibits the translocation of Ca** to the shoot apex → **brown spots surrounded by chlorotic veins** → **actually symptoms of Fe, Mg and Ca deficiency**.
- **Al toxicity** — in acidic soil (pH < 5).

> **Exam angle:** Soil pH changes nutrient availability — **in alkaline soil the availability of Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B falls** (precipitation), **in acidic soil Mo and P fall**, and Al-Mn become toxic. Acidity is corrected with lime, alkalinity with gypsum (usar/reh soils — widespread in UP).

| Process | Conversion | Organisms |
|---|---|---|
| **N₂ fixation** | N₂ → NH₃ | Biological (~most natural fixation), industrial (**Haber-Bosch**, 200 atm, ~500°C, Fe catalyst), atmospheric (**lightning** — N₂ + O₂ → NOₓ), ultraviolet |
| **Ammonification** | Organic N (dead remains, urea) → NH₃ | Decomposer bacteria and fungi (*Bacillus*, *Actinomycetes*) |
| **Nitrification** — two steps | **NH₃ → NO₂⁻** | ***Nitrosomonas***, *Nitrococcus* |
| | **NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻** | ***Nitrobacter*** — both are **chemoautotrophs** |
| **Assimilation** | NO₃⁻/NH₄⁺ → organic N | Plants |
| **Denitrification** | NO₃⁻ → N₂O → **N₂** | ***Pseudomonas***, ***Thiobacillus denitrificans*** — anaerobic, waterlogged soil |
| **Anammox** | NH₄⁺ + NO₂⁻ → N₂ | *Brocadia* (Planctomycetes) — M.Sc. |

## 4. Biological nitrogen fixation (BNF)

Only **prokaryotes** (bacteria and cyanobacteria) — "**diazotrophs**".

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Free-living — aerobic** | ***Azotobacter***, ***Beijerinckia*** (acidic soil) |
| **Free-living — anaerobic** | ***Clostridium*** |
| **Free-living — photosynthetic** | *Rhodospirillum* (purple non-sulphur), *Chromatium*, *Chlorobium* |
| **Free-living — cyanobacteria** | ***Anabaena***, ***Nostoc***, *Aulosira* (rice fields), *Tolypothrix*, *Oscillatoria* (some, without heterocysts) |
| **Symbiotic — legumes** | ***Rhizobium***, *Bradyrhizobium* (soybean), *Sinorhizobium*, *Mesorhizobium*, *Azorhizobium* (*Sesbania rostrata* — **nodules on the stem**) |
| **Symbiotic — non-legumes (actinorhizal)** | ***Frankia*** (actinomycete) — ***Alnus***, ***Casuarina***, *Myrica* |
| **Symbiotic — cyanobacteria** | ***Anabaena azollae*** — ***Azolla*** (aquatic fern; biofertiliser for rice); *Nostoc* — *Anthoceros*, *Gunnera*, lichens; ***Anabaena/Nostoc*** — **coralloid roots** of ***Cycas*** |
| **Associative** | ***Azospirillum*** (roots of grasses, maize, sorghum) |

### Nitrogenase

<div class="formula">

**N₂ + 8e⁻ + 8H⁺ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi**

- Two components: **Fe-protein (dinitrogenase reductase)** — binds ATP, donates electrons; **MoFe-protein (dinitrogenase)** — reduces N₂ at the **FeMo-cofactor (FeMo-co)**. (Alternative V-Fe and Fe-Fe nitrogenases also exist.)
- Electron donor — **ferredoxin/flavodoxin**.
- **Extremely sensitive to O₂** (irreversible inactivation) → protective devices: **leghaemoglobin** (nodule), **heterocysts** (cyanobacteria — thick wall, no PS II → no O₂ produced), **respiratory protection** (rapid respiration) and conformational protection in *Azotobacter*.
- **H₂ by-product** — loss of energy; in some *Rhizobium* an **uptake hydrogenase (Hup)** recycles it.
- **Very high ATP cost** — so legume plants give a large part of their photosynthate to the nodules.
- Nitrogenase also reduces **acetylene (C₂H₂) to ethylene** — N₂ fixation is measured by the **acetylene reduction assay (ARA)**.

</div>

**Leghaemoglobin:** gives the nodule its **pink-red** colour; an **O₂ scavenger** — keeps free O₂ low while giving a controlled supply of O₂ for the respiration of the bacteria (bacteroids). Traditional view: **the globin part comes from plant genes, the haem part from the bacterium** (recent research shows the plant also has a role in haem synthesis). It is an excellent example of plant–bacterium cooperation. Turns **green** in old nodules (from breakdown to biliverdin-like pigments).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In legume nodules low O₂ is needed for N₂ fixation despite active respiration. Which mechanism maintains this balance? (A) Lowering O₂ by suppressing respiration (B) **Leghaemoglobin buffers O₂ — allowing high respiration while keeping O₂ low for nitrogenase** (C) Direct diffusion of N₂ into mitochondria (D) Conversion of fixed N into storage compounds consumes the excess O₂
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.


### Formation of a legume nodule

1. The root secretes **flavonoids** (luteolin, genistein etc.) → these activate the bacterial **NodD** protein.
2. NodD → expression of the **nod genes (nodA, nodB, nodC — common; nodH, nodQ etc. — host-specific)** → **Nod factors (lipo-chito-oligosaccharides, LCO)**.
3. Nod factors → **curling of root hairs** ("shepherd's crook"); bacteria are trapped.
4. The root hair wall dissolves → **infection thread** — a tube of cell-wall material, growing towards the cortex.
5. At the same time **cell divisions in the cortex (inner/outer)** → nodule primordium.
6. The bacteria are released into the cytoplasm **enclosed in a membrane (peribacteroid membrane — "symbiosome")** → differentiate into **bacteroids** (they fix N₂ and do not divide).
7. The **vascular tissue** of the nodule connects with the root.

**Genes:** of the bacterium — **nod** (nodule formation), **nif** (nitrogenase — *nifH* = Fe-protein, *nifD, nifK* = MoFe-protein), **fix** (others needed for fixation); of the plant — **nodulins** — early (ENOD) and late (leghaemoglobin, uricase).
**Nodule types:** **indeterminate** — apical meristem, elongated-cylindrical — **pea, alfalfa, clover**; **determinate** — round, no meristem — **soybean, bean (*Phaseolus*), groundnut**.

**Export from the nodule:** tropical legumes (soybean, cowpea) — **ureides (allantoin, allantoic acid)**; temperate legumes (pea) — **amides (glutamine, asparagine)**.

## 5. Nitrate reduction and ammonia assimilation

**Nitrate reduction:**
- NO₃⁻ → NO₂⁻ — **nitrate reductase** (**Mo**, FAD, cytochrome b₅₅₇; **cytoplasm**; donor NAD(P)H) — induced by nitrate and light.
- NO₂⁻ → NH₄⁺ — **nitrite reductase** (**chloroplast/plastid**; donor **reduced ferredoxin**; siroheme and Fe-S).

**Ammonia assimilation (NH₄⁺ is toxic — converted at once into organic form):**
1. **Reductive amination:** α-ketoglutarate + NH₄⁺ + NADPH → **glutamate** + H₂O (**glutamate dehydrogenase, GDH**) — at high NH₄⁺.
2. **GS–GOGAT pathway (main):** glutamate + NH₄⁺ + ATP → **glutamine** (**glutamine synthetase, GS**); glutamine + α-KG + reductant → **2 glutamate** (**GOGAT — glutamate synthase**).
3. **Transamination:** NH₂ from glutamate to other keto acids → other amino acids (**aminotransferases**). **NCERT: glutamic acid is the main amino acid from which the other 17 amino acids are formed by transamination.**
4. **Amides** — glutamine, **asparagine** — storage and transport of N (in the xylem), high N ratio.

## 2026-style practice questions (statements, sequence, matching, Assertion–Reason)

The questions below are written in the style of the 2026 paper (they are not actual previous questions). Solve them yourself first, then check the answer and reason.

**Q.1 (statement combination).** (I) Mo is the element needed in the smallest amount. (II) Symptoms of Ca deficiency appear first in older leaves. (III) Mg is a component of chlorophyll, Fe is not. (IV) Ni is a component of urease. Correct — (A) I, III, IV (B) I, II (C) II, III (D) All
**Answer: (A)** — II is wrong: Ca is immobile, so symptoms appear in **young** leaves/meristems.

**Q.2 (matching).** (a) Whiptail (b) Khaira (c) Heart rot (d) Grey speck — (I) B (II) Mn (III) Mo (IV) Zn
**Answer:** a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.

**Q.3 (sequence).** Formation of a legume nodule — (I) Secretion of Nod factors (II) Secretion of flavonoids (III) Infection thread (IV) Curling of root hairs (V) Differentiation of bacteroids
**Answer:** II → I → IV → III → V.

**Q.4 (Assertion–Reason).** A: The heterocysts of cyanobacteria are the sites of N₂ fixation. R: Heterocysts lack PS II, so no O₂ is produced.
**Answer:** **Both true, R is the correct explanation.**

**Q.5 (situation).** A crop grown in alkaline (pH 8.5) soil shows interveinal chlorosis in young leaves, although the total iron in the soil is adequate. Explanation? — **At high pH Fe becomes insoluble (precipitated) and unavailable; Fe is immobile, so the symptoms appear in young leaves ("lime-induced chlorosis").**

## Traps and exceptions

- **Fe is not a component of chlorophyll**, but is needed for its synthesis; **Mg is a component of chlorophyll**.
- **Mo is needed in the smallest amount**; **Fe in the largest amount among micronutrients** (some books even call Fe a macronutrient — NCERT puts it in the "micro" group but says it is needed "in larger amounts than other micronutrients").
- **Ni** — the micronutrient most recently (1987) accepted as essential (urease).
- **Mn toxicity actually shows the symptoms of Fe, Mg and Ca deficiency.**
- **Both N and S cause chlorosis** — but N in older leaves (mobile), S in young leaves (immobile).
- **Both Mg and Fe cause interveinal chlorosis** — Mg in older leaves, Fe in young leaves.
- **Nitrifying bacteria are chemoautotrophs**; denitrifiers reduce soil fertility.
- **Leghaemoglobin removes O₂**; it does not supply O₂ to nitrogenase — it keeps free O₂ in the infected cell extremely low (nanomolar).
- **Heterocysts lack PS II** (no O₂ production); ATP comes from PS I.
- *Frankia* is an **actinomycete** (not *Rhizobium*); *Azolla* has *Anabaena* (not *Rhizobium*).
- **Soybean nodules are determinate, pea nodules indeterminate.**

## Quick revision

- Hydroponics — Sachs 1860; criteria of essentiality — Arnon and Stout 1939.
- 17 essential elements: 9 macro (C H O N P K S Ca Mg), 8 micro (Fe Mn Cu Mo Zn B Cl Ni).
- Macro >10 mmol/kg; micro <10 mmol/kg dry weight.
- Mobile — N P K Mg (older leaf); immobile — Ca S Fe B Cu (young leaf).
- K — stomata, enzyme activator, marginal scorch.
- Ca — middle lamella; blossom-end rot (tomato).
- Mg — chlorophyll; interveinal chlorosis of older leaves.
- Mn — splitting of water; grey speck of oats.
- Zn — auxin; khaira of rice, little leaf.
- Cu — plastocyanin; die-back of *Citrus*.
- B — pollen germination, Ca use; heart rot of beet.
- Mo — nitrogenase, nitrate reductase; whiptail of cauliflower.
- Ni — urease.
- *Nitrosomonas* NH₃ → NO₂⁻; *Nitrobacter* NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻; *Pseudomonas* — denitrification.
- Nitrogenase: Fe-protein + MoFe-protein; 16 ATP per N₂; O₂-sensitive.
- Leghaemoglobin — O₂ scavenger, pink.
- nod genes — Nod factors (LCO); nif — nitrogenase.
- *Frankia* — *Alnus*, *Casuarina*; *Anabaena* — *Azolla*, *Cycas*.
- GS-GOGAT — main ammonia assimilation pathway.
- Nitrate reductase — cytoplasm, Mo; nitrite reductase — chloroplast, Fd.
