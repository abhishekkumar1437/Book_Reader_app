This chapter covers the "histology, growth" part of point 2 of Section A of the syllabus — from NCERT Class 11 "Anatomy of Flowering Plants" to B.Sc. anatomy (apical organisation, wood types, anomalous growth).
In UP PGT 2026 this part produced a question on **the characteristic of meristems** (direct question) and on **anomalous secondary growth** (Assertion–Reason). Types of vascular bundles, endarch/exarch protoxylem, and "which plant has which anomaly" are favourite topics for matching.

> **In simple words:** A plant has two kinds of cells — those that **divide** (meristems — like the machines in a factory) and those that **have stopped dividing and taken up a job** (permanent tissues). Xylem carries water upward, phloem distributes food. The plant grows taller from the apical meristems (primary growth) and thicker from the lateral cambium (secondary growth). The layer of wood formed each year is an "annual ring". Some plants increase their thickness in an unusual way — this is anomalous secondary growth.

## 1. Meristem

**Characteristics:** Cells small, isodiametric, **dense cytoplasm**, **large prominent nucleus**, vacuoles very small or absent, thin primary wall, no intercellular spaces, active division.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Plant tissues are classified on the basis of their capacity for division and differentiation. Which is the best characteristic of meristematic tissue? (A) Cells highly vacuolated, specialised for storage and conduction (B) Dead at maturity, only mechanical support (C) Thick secondary wall, no capacity to divide (D) Cells divide actively, have dense cytoplasm and a prominent nucleus.
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)** — (A) describes parenchyma, (B)–(C) describe sclerenchyma.

### Classification

| Basis | Type | Example/location |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Apical** | Root and shoot tips — length |
| | **Intercalary** | At the base of nodes/internodes — **grasses, bamboo**, *Equisetum*, mint; grass regrows after grazing |
| | **Lateral** | Vascular cambium, cork cambium (phellogen) — thickness |
| Origin | **Primary** | Directly from the embryo — apical, intercalary, intrafascicular cambium |
| | **Secondary** | By **dedifferentiation** of permanent tissue — **interfascicular cambium, cork cambium**, vascular cambium of the root |
| Function (Haberlandt) | Protoderm, ground meristem, procambium | → epidermis, ground tissue, primary vascular tissue |
| Plane of division | Mass — in all planes; plate — in two planes; rib/file — in one plane (cortex, pith) | |

### Theories of apical organisation (B.Sc./M.Sc.)

| Theory | Proposed by (year) | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Apical cell theory | **Nägeli, 1858** | A single tetrahedral apical cell — true for algae, bryophytes, most pteridophytes |
| **Histogen theory** | **Hanstein, 1868** | Three histogens — **dermatogen** → epidermis; **periblem** → cortex; **plerome** → stele. In the root a fourth, **calyptrogen** → root cap (Janczewski, 1874; monocots) |
| **Tunica–corpus** | **A. Schmidt, 1924** | Shoot apex — outer **tunica** (only anticlinal divisions → surface growth) and inner **corpus** (divisions in all planes → growth in volume) |
| Körper–Kappe | **Schüepp, 1917** | Root apex — T-shaped planes of division |
| **Quiescent centre** | **Clowes, 1956/1961** | A group of slowly dividing cells in the root apex — a "reserve" for replenishment after injury |

> **Remember:** The histogen theory applies well to the **root**, the **tunica–corpus to the shoot**. In tunica–corpus the layers are based on **the plane of cell division**, not on the future tissue — that is the difference from Hanstein.

## 2. Simple permanent tissues

| Tissue | Cells | Wall | Living/dead | Special types and functions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Parenchyma** | Isodiametric, intercellular spaces | Thin, cellulose | Living | **Chlorenchyma** — leaf; **aerenchyma** — aquatic plants (buoyancy); storage; parenchyma is **totipotent** — tissue culture and wound healing |
| **Collenchyma** | Elongated | **Thickening of pectin and cellulose at the corners** | Living | In the **hypodermis** of dicot stems, in petioles; **usually absent in monocots and roots**; flexible support. Types — **angular**, **lamellar** — on tangential walls, **lacunar** — next to intercellular spaces |
| **Sclerenchyma** | Elongated or irregular | **Thick lignified secondary wall, pits** | **Dead** | **Fibres** — long, pointed ends; **sclereids** — short |

**Types of sclereids:** **Brachysclereids/stone cells** — pulp of **pear, guava** (grittiness); **macrosclereids** — seed coat of pulses (Malpighian cells); **osteosclereids** (bone-shaped) — seed coat; **astrosclereids** (star-shaped) — *Nymphaea*, tea leaf; **trichosclereids** — aquatic plants. The hard shell of walnut and coconut is also made of sclereids.

**Fibres — commercial:** **Bast/phloem fibres** — jute (*Corchorus*), sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*), flax (*Linum*), hemp (*Cannabis*); **leaf fibres** — *Agave* (sisal); **fruit fibres** — coconut (coir); **seed hairs** — cotton (not a fibre, but an **epidermal hair of the seed coat**).

## 3. Complex permanent tissues

### Xylem — conduction of water and minerals, mechanical support

| Element | Living/dead | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Tracheids** | Dead | Long, pointed ends, **imperforate ends** — water moves through pits (bordered pits — torus-margo in *Pinus*); main water conductors in pteridophytes and gymnosperms |
| **Vessels (tracheae)** | Dead | A chain of cells whose **cross walls dissolve (perforation plate)**; **a characteristic of angiosperms** |
| Xylem parenchyma | **Living** (the only living element of xylem) | Food (starch, fat) and tannins; **ray parenchyma** for radial conduction |
| Xylem fibres | Dead | Support |

> **Common mistake:** "Gymnosperms have no vessels" — exception: the **Gnetales (*Gnetum*, *Ephedra*, *Welwitschia*)**. "Angiosperms always have vessels" — exception: the **vesselless angiosperms** — *Trochodendron*, *Tetracentron*, *Drimys* (Winteraceae), *Amborella*. Some pteridophytes (*Selaginella*, *Pteridium*) have vessels.

**Protoxylem and metaxylem:** The first-formed **protoxylem** — narrow vessels, annular/spiral thickening; the later **metaxylem** — wide, reticulate/pitted thickening.

| Position | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Endarch** | Protoxylem towards the centre (pith) — development **centrifugal** | **Stem** |
| **Exarch** | Protoxylem towards the periphery — development **centripetal** | **Root** |
| Mesarch | Protoxylem in the middle | Fern shoots, *Cycas* leaflet (diploxylic) |

### Phloem — conduction of food

| Element | Features |
|---|---|
| **Sieve tube elements** | Living but **without a nucleus at maturity**; cross walls form **sieve plates**; **P-protein (phloem protein)**; in cold/injury **callose** plugs the pores |
| **Companion cell** | Nucleated, active; connected to the sieve tube by **pit fields/plasmodesmata**; formed **from the same mother cell**; controls the sieve tube |
| Phloem parenchyma | **Absent in most monocots** |
| Phloem fibres (bast) | **The only dead element of phloem**; usually absent in primary phloem |

In **pteridophytes and gymnosperms**, instead of sieve tubes and companion cells there are **sieve cells** and **albuminous/Strasburger cells**. Protophloem and metaphloem.

### Types of vascular bundles

| Type | Arrangement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | Xylem and phloem on separate radii, alternating | **All roots** |
| **Conjoint collateral open** | On the same radius, phloem outside, cambium in between | **Dicot stem** |
| Conjoint collateral closed | No cambium | **Monocot stem**, leaves |
| **Bicollateral** | Phloem and cambium on both sides of xylem (outer phloem–outer cambium–xylem–inner cambium–inner phloem) | **Cucurbitaceae** (pumpkin), Solanaceae, Apocynaceae |
| **Concentric — amphicribral/hadrocentric** | Xylem in the centre, phloem all around | **Ferns**, *Selaginella* |
| **Concentric — amphivasal/leptocentric** | Phloem in the centre, xylem all around | ***Dracaena***, ***Yucca*** (secondary bundles), *Acorus* |

> **Memory trick:** "**Hadro**" = xylem (hadrome) in the **centre** → hadrocentric = ferns; "**Lepto**" = phloem (leptome) in the **centre** → leptocentric = Dracaena. Amphi**cribral** — **cribral (sieve = phloem)** all around.

## 4. Tissue systems (Sachs)

1. **Epidermal system** — epidermis (usually one layer; **multilayered** in the leaf of *Nerium*, *Ficus*), **cuticle** — cutin (absent in roots); **stomata** — **kidney-shaped** guard cells in dicots, **dumbbell-shaped** in grasses; subsidiary cells; **trichomes** — multicellular on the shoot; **root hairs** — unicellular. In grass leaves, **bulliform (motor) cells** become flaccid under water stress and make the leaf **roll**.
2. **Ground system** — cortex, pericycle, medullary rays, pith; in the leaf, **mesophyll**.
3. **Vascular system** — xylem, phloem, (cambium).

## 5. Primary internal structure — comparison

| Feature | Dicot root (gram) | Monocot root (maize) | Dicot stem (sunflower) | Monocot stem (maize) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer layer | **Epiblema/rhizodermis** — root hairs, no cuticle | Epiblema | Epidermis + cuticle + multicellular hairs | Epidermis + cuticle |
| Hypodermis | — | — | **Collenchyma** | **Sclerenchyma** |
| Endodermis | Distinct, **Casparian strips (suberin)**, **passage cells** opposite protoxylem | Distinct | Usually a **starch sheath** | Indistinct |
| Pericycle | Parenchymatous — **lateral roots (endogenous), part of the cambium, cork cambium** | Lateral roots | **Sclerenchymatous semilunar patches (bundle cap)** | — |
| Vascular bundles | Radial, **2–6 (diarch to hexarch)** | Radial, **polyarch (>6)** | Conjoint collateral **open**, **in a ring** | Conjoint collateral **closed**, **scattered (atactostele)**, smaller and denser at the periphery |
| Bundle sheath | — | — | — | **Sclerenchymatous bundle sheath** |
| Protoxylem | Exarch | Exarch | Endarch | Endarch, **protoxylem cavity (water cavity/lysigenous)** |
| Pith | Small or absent | **Large, well developed** | Large | Not differentiated |
| Phloem parenchyma | Present | Present | Present | **Absent** |
| Secondary growth | Occurs | No | Occurs | No (exception — *Dracaena*) |

### Leaf

| Feature | Dorsiventral — dicot | Isobilateral — monocot |
|---|---|---|
| Stomata | More on the **lower surface** (hypostomatic) | About equal on both surfaces (amphistomatic) |
| Mesophyll | **Palisade tissue** above + **spongy tissue** below | Not differentiated |
| Bulliform cells | No | In the upper epidermis (grasses) |
| Bundle sheath | | In C₄ grasses, **Kranz anatomy** — large bundle sheath cells with chloroplasts (Chapter 5) |

> **Exam angle:** Position of stomata — **floating leaves of aquatic plants (lotus, *Nymphaea*) — upper surface only (epistomatic)**; **submerged aquatic plants (*Hydrilla*, *Vallisneria*) — no stomata**; xerophytes (*Nerium*) — **sunken stomata** in hairy pits (stomatal crypts).

## 6. Secondary growth

### Vascular cambium — dicot stem

1. The **intrafascicular cambium** within the bundles + the **interfascicular cambium** formed from the medullary rays → a complete **cambial ring**.
2. The cambium has two kinds of initials — **fusiform initials** (long → vessels, tracheids, sieve tubes) and **ray initials** (short → radial rays).
3. The cambium produces **more secondary xylem towards the inside** and **less secondary phloem towards the outside**; the primary xylem remains in the centre, the primary phloem gets crushed.
4. **Cambial divisions are periclinal** — for thickness; **anticlinal** divisions also occur to increase the circumference.

**Annual rings:** **Spring/early wood** formed in spring — wide vessels, light, less dense; **autumn/late wood** formed in autumn — narrow vessels, dark, dense. One pair = one ring = one year. Estimating age by counting rings = **dendrochronology** (A.E. Douglass). **Tropical (evergreen) and coastal trees do not form distinct rings** — the seasonal difference there is small.

**Heartwood and sapwood:**

| | Heartwood (duramen) | Sapwood (alburnum) |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Centre | Periphery |
| Cells | Dead; vessels blocked by **tyloses** (balloon-like ingrowths of parenchyma) | Includes living parenchyma |
| Colour | Dark — tannins, resins, gums, oils | Light |
| Function | Mechanical support, **no water conduction**; durable, insect-resistant | **Water conduction** |

**Cork cambium (phellogen) and periderm:** Usually arises from the cortex (sometimes from the epidermis/pericycle); on the outside it forms **cork (phellem)** — dead, **suberised**, waterproof; on the inside, **secondary cortex (phelloderm)** — living, parenchymatous.
**Periderm = phellem + phellogen + phelloderm.** **Bark** = all tissues outside the vascular cambium (including secondary phloem). Early (soft) and late (hard) bark; **ring bark** — *Betula* (bhojpatra), **scale bark** — *Eucalyptus*, guava. **Lenticels** — openings in the cork with loose **complementary cells**, for gas exchange. Commercial cork — ***Quercus suber*** (cork oak).

### Secondary growth in the root

The cambium arises from the **conjunctive tissue below the phloem bundles** and from **the pericycle opposite the protoxylem** — wavy at first, later circular. The cork cambium arises from the **pericycle**.

### Types of wood

| Basis | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Vessels | **Porous/hard wood** — vessels present | Dicots — shisham, teak |
| | **Non-porous/soft wood** — no vessels | Gymnosperms — pine, deodar |
| Vessel distribution | **Ring-porous** — large vessels concentrated in spring wood | Cold-temperate — *Quercus*, *Morus*, *Fraxinus* |
| | **Diffuse-porous** — evenly scattered | Tropical, *Acer*, *Betula* |
| Amount of parenchyma | **Manoxylic** — loose, more parenchyma, broad rays | ***Cycas*** |
| | **Pycnoxylic** — compact, narrow rays | ***Pinus*** |

> **Common mistake:** "Softwood" does not mean "soft timber" — it means **vesselless (gymnosperm)** wood; the "softwood" of *Taxus* is harder than many hardwoods. Balsa (*Ochroma*) is a "hardwood" but the lightest of all.

## 7. Anomalous secondary growth

A deviation from the normal pattern (one cambial ring, xylem inside and phloem outside). Causes — (1) **abnormal position of the cambium**, (2) **abnormal activity of a normal cambium**, (3) **extra/accessory or successive cambia**, (4) secondary growth in monocots.

| Plant | Family | Anomaly |
|---|---|---|
| ***Boerhaavia diffusa*** (punarnava) | Nyctaginaceae | **Medullary bundles** already in the primary structure — three rings: **2 large** in the centre, **6–14** in the middle ring, **15–20 small** bundles in the outer ring; the cambium of the outer bundles becomes active, then **successive extra cambial rings arise from the pericycle** → rings of xylem-phloem in **conjunctive tissue** |
| ***Bignonia*** | Bignoniaceae | **Abnormal activity of a normal cambium** — in four segments the cambium produces **less xylem inside and more phloem outside** → **four phloem wedges** in the xylem, xylem cross/star-shaped; a climbing liana — flexibility |
| ***Dracaena***, *Yucca*, *Agave*, *Aloe*, *Sansevieria* | Asparagaceae/Agavaceae (monocots) | **Secondary cambium from the cortex/pericycle, outside** the vascular bundles → **secondary vascular bundles (amphivasal)** and conjunctive tissue inside, a little parenchyma outside; the **"dragon's blood tree"** |
| *Achyranthes*, *Amaranthus*, *Chenopodium*, *Bougainvillea* | Amaranthaceae, Nyctaginaceae | **Successive/accessory cambial rings** (from the pericycle or outer derivatives); *Achyranthes* also has medullary bundles |
| Root of ***Beta vulgaris*** (beetroot) | Amaranthaceae | **Successive concentric cambial rings** → alternating rings of xylem and phloem (visible in a cut beetroot) |
| *Salvadora*, *Strychnos*, *Leptadenia* | | **Interxylary/included phloem** — islands of phloem inside the xylem |
| *Nyctanthes* (harsingar) | Oleaceae | **Cortical bundles** — with inverted orientation |
| *Aristolochia* | | Normal growth, but the xylem is segmented by broad medullary rays — treated as an example of "normal" growth |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): Anomalous secondary growth in stems usually results from the formation of successive cambia or from abnormal activity of the vascular cambium. Reason (R): In some plants extra cambia arise **only** from the vascular cambium and form **a single continuous vascular cylinder**.
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A) true, (R) false** — extra cambia usually arise from the **pericycle, cortex or phloem derivatives** (*Boerhaavia*, *Beta*, *Dracaena*) and form **successive rings or separate bundles**, not a single continuous cylinder.

> **Memory trick:** "**Bo**erhaavia — **Bo**th (medullary bundles + successive rings); **Bi**gnonia — **Bi**zarre cambium (wedges); **Dra**caena — **Dra**gon monocot (secondary growth in a monocot)."

## 2026-style practice questions (statements, sequence, matching, Assertion–Reason)

The questions below are written in the style of the 2026 paper (they are not actual previous questions). Solve them yourself first, then check the answer and reason.

**Q.1 (statement combination).** Consider the following statements — (I) Interfascicular cambium is a secondary meristem. (II) In the root the vascular cambium arises partly from the pericycle. (III) Bundles in monocot stems are open. (IV) Phloem fibres are the only dead element of phloem. Correct statements — (A) I, II, IV (B) I, III (C) II, III, IV (D) All
**Answer: (A)** — III is wrong: monocot bundles are **closed** (no cambium).

**Q.2 (matching).** (a) *Bignonia* (b) *Boerhaavia* (c) *Dracaena* (d) *Salvadora* — (I) Secondary cambium in a monocot (II) Interxylary phloem (III) Phloem wedges (IV) Medullary bundles and successive cambia
**Answer:** a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II.

**Q.3 (Assertion–Reason).** A: Annual rings are not distinct in tropical evergreen trees. R: There, cambial activity remains nearly uniform throughout the year.
**Answer:** **Both A and R true, R is the correct explanation** — the difference between spring and autumn wood comes only from seasonal change.

**Q.4 (sequence).** Correct order from outside to inside in a dicot stem — (I) Hypodermis (II) Endodermis (III) Pericycle (IV) Phloem (V) Cambium (VI) Xylem
**Answer:** I → II → III → IV → V → VI (after the epidermis; the pith is innermost).

**Q.5 (situation).** The central wood of a tree was hollowed out, yet it remained green for years. Best explanation? — **Water is conducted by the peripheral sapwood; the heartwood is dead and gives only mechanical support.**

## Traps and exceptions

- **Monocots usually have no secondary growth**, but *Dracaena*, *Yucca*, *Agave* show anomalous secondary growth; **the thickness of palms (*Roystonea*, coconut)** is not secondary growth but comes from a **primary thickening meristem**.
- The **only living** element of xylem = xylem parenchyma; the **only dead** element of phloem = phloem fibres.
- **The sieve tube is living but has no nucleus** (like human RBCs).
- **The companion cell is a characteristic of angiosperms**; gymnosperms have albuminous cells.
- **Collenchyma is living** (a living supporting tissue); **sclerenchyma is dead**.
- **Lateral roots are endogenous (from the pericycle)**; branches and leaves are **exogenous**.
- **Intrafascicular cambium is a primary, interfascicular cambium a secondary** meristem.
- **Tyloses** are ingrowths of xylem parenchyma; **callose** plugs phloem pores — do not mix them up.
- **Bundles of the maize stem show a protoxylem cavity** (lysigenous).
- **Dodder (*Cuscuta*)** — rootless; **Lemna** — root pocket on the root.

## Quick revision

- Meristem: dense cytoplasm, large nucleus, vacuoles absent, no intercellular spaces.
- Intercalary meristem — grasses, bamboo, *Equisetum*.
- Histogen — Hanstein 1868; tunica–corpus — Schmidt 1924; quiescent centre — Clowes.
- Collenchyma — pectin-cellulose thickening at the corners; hypodermis of dicot stems.
- Stone cells — pear; astrosclereids — *Nymphaea*, tea.
- Vessels — angiosperms (+ *Gnetum*); vesselless angiosperms — *Drimys*, *Trochodendron*.
- Exarch — root; endarch — stem; mesarch — ferns.
- Bicollateral bundles — pumpkin (Cucurbitaceae).
- Hadrocentric — ferns; leptocentric — *Dracaena*.
- Bulliform cells — rolling of grass leaves.
- Epistomatic — floating leaves (lotus); no stomata — submerged aquatic plants; sunken stomata — *Nerium*.
- Casparian strips — suberin; passage cells — opposite the protoxylem.
- Dicot root 2–6 protoxylem groups; monocot root polyarch.
- Tyloses — heartwood; heartwood does not conduct water.
- Periderm = phellem + phellogen + phelloderm; cork — *Quercus suber*.
- Manoxylic — *Cycas*; pycnoxylic — *Pinus*.
- *Boerhaavia* — medullary bundles + successive cambia; *Bignonia* — phloem wedges; *Dracaena* — secondary growth in a monocot.
- Beetroot — concentric successive rings; *Salvadora* — interxylary phloem.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Which meristem increases the length of the plant? — **Apical** (primary growth).
2. Grass regrows after grazing because of the — **intercalary meristem**.
3. Who gave the tunica-corpus theory? — **A. Schmidt (1924)**.
4. Discovery of the quiescent centre in the root — **Clowes (F.A.L. Clowes)**.
5. Grittiness in pear pulp — **stone cells (brachysclereids)**.
6. Living element of xylem — **xylem parenchyma**.
7. What controls the sieve tube? — **The companion cell** (helper of the enucleate sieve tube).
8. Vascular bundles in the pumpkin stem — **bicollateral**.
9. Secondary bundles of *Dracaena* — **amphivasal (leptocentric)**.
10. Position of protoxylem in the root — **exarch**.
11. Maize root — **polyarch, large pith**.
12. Rolling of the grass leaf under water stress — due to **bulliform cells**.
13. Annual rings are formed of which tissue? — **Secondary xylem** (spring + autumn wood).
14. The vessels of heartwood are blocked by — **tyloses**.
15. Inner part of the periderm — **phelloderm (secondary cortex)**.
16. Softwood belongs to — **gymnosperms (vesselless)**.
17. Wedges in the xylem of *Bignonia* — **abnormal activity of a normal cambium** (more phloem in some segments).
18. How many large medullary bundles are in the centre of *Boerhaavia*? — **Two**.
19. Rings in the beetroot root — from **successive concentric cambia**.
20. Thickness of the palm stem — **primary thickening meristem** (not true secondary growth).
