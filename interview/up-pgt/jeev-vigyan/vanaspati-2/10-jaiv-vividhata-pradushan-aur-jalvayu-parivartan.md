> **In simple words:** The variety of life on Earth — genes, species and ecosystems — is our "living capital". Habitat destruction, over-exploitation, alien invasive species and pollution are reducing it fast. We save it in two ways — keeping organisms in their own home (national parks, sanctuaries) or outside their home (zoos, seed banks). The CO₂ released by burning fossil fuels is warming the Earth and making the sea acidic; the remedy is clean energy and restrained use of resources.

## 1. Biodiversity — concept and patterns

- The term **"biodiversity" — Walter Rosen (1985)**; popularised by **E.O. Wilson** (1988).
- **Three levels (NCERT):**
  1. **Genetic diversity** — within a species; variation in the amount and potency of **reserpine** in ***Rauwolfia vomitoria*** (Himalaya); India has **more than 50,000** varieties of rice and **more than 1,000** of mango.
  2. **Species diversity** — **the Western Ghats have more amphibian species than the Eastern Ghats**.
  3. **Ecological diversity** — India has deserts, rain forests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands, estuaries and alpine meadows — far more than a country like Norway.
- **Global numbers:** IUCN (2004) — **~1.5 million** described species; **Robert May's** estimate — **~7 million** species in all. **Insects are more than ~70%** of animal species; among plants, **angiosperms** are the most numerous.
- **India:** only **2.4%** of the world's land area, but **~8.1%** of global species diversity; one of the world's **12 (17 according to Mittermeier) megadiversity countries**; ~45,000 plant and ~90,000 animal species (NCERT).

### Patterns

- **Latitudinal gradient** — diversity **decreases** from the equator towards the poles. Examples (NCERT): **Colombia — ~1,400 bird species, New York — 105, Greenland — 56, India — more than 1,200**; the Amazon rain forest — more than 40,000 plant species. **Reasons:** the tropics (1) have remained relatively undisturbed for millions of years (fewer glaciations) — more time for diversification; (2) have less variable, more predictable seasons — niche specialisation; (3) **more solar energy → higher productivity**.
- **Altitudinal gradient** — diversity decreases with altitude.
- **Species–area relationship — Alexander von Humboldt:**

<div class="formula">

**S = CA^Z** or **log S = log C + Z log A** (S = species richness, A = area, C = y-intercept, Z = slope/regression coefficient)
- In ordinary regions **Z ≈ 0.1–0.2** (roughly independent of the taxonomic group or region).
- Over **very large areas (whole continents)** the slope is much steeper — **Z ≈ 0.6–1.2**; for **frugivorous birds and mammals** of tropical forests of different continents — **Z = 1.15**.

</div>

- **Values (NCERT):** **narrowly utilitarian** (food, medicine — more than 25,000 plant species used in traditional medicine; **bioprospecting**), **broadly utilitarian** (ecosystem services — the Amazon called the "lungs of the planet", **~20% of O₂** — as stated by NCERT; pollination; climate regulation), **ethical**.
- **Biopiracy** — **basmati rice** (patented by an American company in 1997), **neem**, **turmeric** (patent revoked, 1997 — India's successful challenge).

## 2. Biodiversity hotspots

- Concept — **Norman Myers (1988)**; criteria (Conservation International): (1) at least **1,500 endemic vascular plant species** (>0.5% of the global total), (2) at least **70% of the original habitat lost**.
- **36 hotspots** in the world (25 at first, then 34, 35; the North American Coastal Plain added in 2016 → 36); on **~2.4%** of the Earth's land area (NCERT: the older figure of "<2%"), but more than half of the world's plant species are endemic to them.
- **India's 4 hotspots:** **(1) Himalaya**, **(2) Indo-Burma** (North-East, Andaman), **(3) Western Ghats and Sri Lanka**, **(4) Sundaland** (Nicobar Islands). (NCERT 12 mentions 3 for India — Western Ghats-Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya.)
- NCERT: strict protection of all hotspots could **reduce the current extinction rate by ~30%**.
- **Endemic** species — found in only one region — Western Ghats: lion-tailed macaque (*Macaca silenus*), Nilgiri tahr, purple frog; plants — *Nepenthes khasiana* (Meghalaya).

## 3. Loss of biodiversity

- **The sixth mass extinction** — the present rate is **100–1,000 times** higher than in pre-human times; **784 species** have become extinct in the last 500 years (IUCN 2004 — 338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants — NCERT).
- **Recent extinctions:** **dodo (Mauritius)**, **quagga (Africa)**, **thylacine (Australia)**, **Steller's sea cow (Russia)**, **three subspecies of tiger — Bali, Javan, Caspian**, passenger pigeon (over-exploitation). India — the **cheetah** (declared extinct in 1952; **reintroduced from Namibia and South Africa into Kuno, MP in 2022**), the pink-headed duck.
- **The "Evil Quartet" — Jared Diamond:**
  1. **Habitat loss and fragmentation** — **the most important cause**; tropical rain forests reduced from 14% of the Earth's surface to less than 6%; felling in the Amazon for soybean and pasture. Fragmentation affects mammals and birds that need large areas and migratory species.
  2. **Over-exploitation** — Steller's sea cow, passenger pigeon; marine fish.
  3. **Alien/invasive species** — **Nile perch in Lake Victoria** → **200+ cichlid fish species extinct**; ***Parthenium* (carrot grass), *Lantana*, *Eichhornia* (water hyacinth)**; the **African catfish (*Clarias gariepinus*)** brought for aquaculture threatens native catfish.
  4. **Co-extinction** — a host with its specific parasites; plant–pollinator mutualism.
- **IUCN Red List categories:** **Extinct (EX)** → **Extinct in the Wild (EW)** → **Critically Endangered (CR)** → **Endangered (EN)** → **Vulnerable (VU)** [these three = "Threatened"] → **Near Threatened (NT)** → **Least Concern (LC)**; **Data Deficient (DD)**, **Not Evaluated (NE)**. **Red Data Book** — IUCN (headquarters **Gland, Switzerland**); in India BSI and ZSI.

## 4. Conservation — in situ and ex situ

| In situ — in the natural habitat | Ex situ — outside the habitat |
|---|---|
| **Biosphere reserves** — UNESCO **MAB (Man and Biosphere) Programme, 1971**; three zones — **core** (no human interference), **buffer** (limited research, education), **transition/manipulation** (human settlement, agriculture) | **Zoos, botanical gardens** — **Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden, Howrah (1787)** — the great banyan |
| **National parks** — strict protection, no private rights/grazing | **Seed banks** — **NBPGR (National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources), New Delhi**; **Svalbard Global Seed Vault** (Norway, 2008) |
| **Wildlife sanctuaries** — some human activities (wood collection, grazing) allowed | **Cryopreservation (−196°C, liquid N₂)** — gametes, embryos, pollen |

| **Sacred groves** — **Khasi and Jaintia Hills of Meghalaya**, **Aravalli of Rajasthan**, **Western Ghats of Karnataka and Maharashtra**, **Sarguja, Chanda, Bastar of MP/Chhattisgarh** (NCERT) | **Captive breeding and reintroduction** — gharial (Kukrail, Lucknow), vulture breeding centres (Pinjore; **Maharajganj, UP — Jatayu Conservation Centre**) |

## 6. Air pollution

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Particulate matter (PM₁₀, PM₂.₅)** | Vehicles, construction, stubble burning, industry | **PM₂.₅ (smaller than 2.5 µm) is the most harmful** — goes deep into the lungs; respiratory disease, lung damage, premature death (according to CPCB) |
| **SO₂** | Coal burning (thermal power), refineries | Respiratory disease, **acid rain**, leaf damage (chlorosis, necrosis), **lichens are extremely sensitive** |
| **NOₓ** | Vehicles, high-temperature combustion | Acid rain, **photochemical smog**, ozone (tropospheric) |
| **CO** | Incomplete combustion in vehicles | **~200–250 times higher affinity for haemoglobin → carboxyhaemoglobin** → less O₂ transport, headache, death |
| **Tropospheric O₃, PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate)** | **Secondary pollutants** — NOₓ + hydrocarbons + sunlight | **Photochemical smog (Los Angeles type)** — eye irritation, bronzing of leaves |
| **Classical smog (London, 1952)** | Smoke + SO₂ + fog | Thousands of deaths |
| Lead (Pb) | Leaded petrol (phased out in India by 2000) | Nerve damage (children) |

**Control:**
- **Electrostatic precipitator** — removes **>99% of particulate matter** from smoke (particles are charged by electrodes and collected on plates).
- **Scrubber** — removes gases such as **SO₂** with a **spray of water or lime**.
- **Catalytic converter** — **platinum-palladium-rhodium**: CO → CO₂, **NO → N₂**, unburnt hydrocarbons → CO₂ + H₂O; **needs unleaded petrol** (lead inactivates the catalyst).
- **Delhi — CNG in public transport (by 2002)**; **Bharat Stage-VI (BS-VI) norms — 1 April 2020** (skipping BS-V); **National Clean Air Programme (NCAP, 2019)**.
- **Air Quality Index (AQI)** — 8 pollutants (PM₁₀, PM₂.₅, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃, Pb), 6 categories.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In a city, after heavy vehicular traffic and industrial emissions, cases of respiratory distress rose, lichen diversity fell and crop leaves were damaged; acid deposition was also recorded during rain. Which pollutant–effect relationship best explains this pattern? ### Noise pollution

- **Unwanted high-level sound**; unit **decibel (dB)**; harmful **above 80 dB**; **150 dB or more** (jet plane/rocket take-off) — **can rupture the eardrum**, permanent deafness (NCERT).
- Effects — sleeplessness, raised heart rate and blood pressure, stress, hearing loss.
- **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000**; **silence zones** (within 100 m of hospitals, schools, courts); residential standard — 55 dB by day, 45 dB at night; sound-absorbing materials, tree belts.

## 7. Water pollution

- **Domestic sewage** — **biodegradable organic matter** → microbial decomposition **uses up dissolved O₂ (DO)** → fish die.
- **BOD (biochemical oxygen demand)** — the O₂ needed by microbes to oxidise the organic matter in 1 litre of water (20°C, 5 days — BOD₅) — **the higher the BOD, the greater the pollution** (clean water <5 mg/L — a common standard); **COD (chemical oxygen demand)** — includes non-biodegradable matter, always higher than BOD.
- **Eutrophication** — the "ageing" of a lake as it becomes enriched with **N and P**; **natural** (thousands of years) and **man-made/accelerated/cultural eutrophication** (sewage, fertiliser runoff, detergent phosphates). Sequence: nutrients ↑ → **algal bloom** → light blocked → algae die → **heavy O₂ consumption in decomposition (hypoxia/anoxia)** → **fish and higher trophic levels destroyed**, foul smell, H₂S; some cyanobacteria (*Microcystis*) produce toxins. **Water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*) — "terror of Bengal"**, the world's most troublesome aquatic weed.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In a freshwater pond with periodic nutrient enrichment, primary productivity first rises, then dissolved O₂ falls and higher trophic levels decline. Best explanation of the breakdown of ecosystem homeostasis? (A) **Increased productivity leads to decomposition-driven O₂ depletion that exceeds feedback control and shifts the equilibrium** (B) Nutrient enrichment stabilises homeostasis (C) Nutrients suppress microbial activity and save O₂ (D) Organisms maintain homeostasis by immediate adjustment
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — once the capacity of negative feedback is exceeded, positive feedback (more algae → more decomposition → less O₂ → more deaths) takes over.

- **Biomagnification** — increase in the concentration of **non-degradable, fat-soluble** toxic substances **at successive trophic levels**. **DDT** — water 0.003 ppb → zooplankton 0.04 ppm → small fish 0.5 ppm → large fish 2 ppm → **fish-eating birds 25 ppm** (NCERT example); in birds **Ca metabolism is disturbed → thin eggshells → premature breaking** → population decline (bald eagle, pelican). **Bioaccumulation** — build-up in one organism over time.
- **Heavy metals and diseases:** **mercury (Hg) — Minamata disease** (Japan, 1950s, methyl-mercury, from fish); **cadmium (Cd) — itai-itai** (brittle bones, Japan); **arsenic — blackfoot disease**, skin lesions — groundwater (West Bengal, Bihar, **Ballia and eastern UP**); **fluoride — fluorosis** (dental, skeletal; Rajasthan, some districts of UP — Unnao, Agra etc.); **nitrate — methaemoglobinaemia ("blue baby syndrome")**; lead — plumbism.
- **Thermal pollution** — hot water from power plants → **dissolved O₂ falls**, temperature-sensitive organisms destroyed.
- **Integrated wastewater treatment — Arcata (California)** — natural purification in artificial marshes (6 marshes, 60 hectares); **Friends of the Arcata Marsh (FOAM)** (NCERT). **Ecosan toilets** — dry composting toilets (Kerala, Sri Lanka).
- **Sewage treatment** — primary (physical — screening, sedimentation), **secondary/biological** (**activated sludge** — **flocs** of aerobic microbes, BOD falls; **anaerobic sludge digesters** → **biogas** — CH₄, H₂S, CO₂), tertiary.
- **Ganga Action Plan (1985)**, **Yamuna Action Plan (1993)**, **Namami Gange (2014–15)**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Statements on selective toxicity, antidote mechanisms and environmental management — (I) Selective toxicity occurs when a toxicant targets a biochemical pathway/receptor present in the pest but absent or less sensitive in the host. - **Solutions:** **organic farming**, **integrated pest management (IPM)**, biopesticides (*Bacillus thuringiensis*, *Trichoderma*, NPV), biofertilisers; **Ramesh Chandra Dagar (Sonipat, Haryana) — "Haryana Kisan Welfare Club"** — integrated organic farming (NCERT). DDT is banned for agriculture in India (only limited use in vector control).
- **Solid waste:** municipal, industrial, **e-waste** (sent from developed to developing countries for recycling; Cu, Fe, Si, Ni, Au are extracted — harmful), **hospital waste — incineration**; **plastics — Ahmed Khan (Bengaluru) — "polyblend"** (recycled plastic mixed with bitumen for roads) (NCERT); **ban on single-use plastic — 1 July 2022**; **sanitary landfill**, composting, **vermicompost**.

## 9. Radioactive pollution

- Sources — nuclear tests, nuclear accidents (**Three Mile Island 1979, Chernobyl 1986, Fukushima 2011**), nuclear waste, medicine, mining.
- **Ionising radiation (α, β, γ)** → DNA mutations, **cancer (leukaemia)**, birth defects; lethal at high doses. **Strontium-90** (replaces Ca in bones), **iodine-131** (thyroid), **caesium-137**.
- **Disposal of nuclear waste** — after suitable pre-treatment, **sealed in metal containers and buried in rocks about 500 m below the surface** (NCERT; though the public opposes it).
- Units — becquerel, gray, **sievert**.

## 10. Ozone depletion

- **Stratospheric ozone ("good ozone")** — 10–50 km; absorbs **UV-B and UV-C**; thickness measured in **Dobson units (DU)**. **Tropospheric ozone** — a pollutant ("bad ozone").
- **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** — refrigerators, AC, aerosols; in the stratosphere **UV releases Cl atoms** → Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂; ClO + O → Cl + O₂ → **Cl is available again as a catalyst** — one Cl atom can destroy thousands (~100,000) of O₃ molecules. Also **halons (Br)**, carbon tetrachloride, methyl bromide.
- **Ozone hole — over Antarctica** (Farman, Gardiner and Shanklin, 1985) — **polar stratospheric clouds** and the polar vortex; greatest in spring (September–October).
- **Effects of UV-B:** DNA damage, **skin cancer**, **cataract**, **snow-blindness** — **inflammation of the cornea** due to absorption of UV-B by the cornea; reduced phytoplankton and crop production.
- **Montreal Protocol (1987)** — the most successful environmental treaty; the ozone layer is expected to **recover by the 2060s**; **Kigali Amendment (2016)** — phase-down of HFCs (ozone-safe but powerful greenhouse gases).

## 11. Acid rain

- Rain with **pH < 5.6** (normal rain is ~5.6 because of CO₂). SO₂ + H₂O → **H₂SO₄** (~60–70%); NOₓ → **HNO₃** (~30%).
- **The term "acid rain" — Robert Angus Smith (1872)**.
- **Effects:** acidification of lakes → fish destroyed (Scandinavia, Canada); **leaching of Ca, Mg, K** from soil and **Al toxicity**; forest decline (**the Black Forest of Germany**); corrosion of buildings — **"marble cancer" of the Taj Mahal** (Mathura refinery, foundries) → **Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ)**, Supreme Court (M.C. Mehta case, 1996).
- **Control:** low-sulphur fuels, scrubbers, catalytic converters, **liming** of lakes.

## 12. Marine pollution and ocean acidification

**Marine pollution:**
- **Oil spills** — birds' feathers stick together (insulation lost), photosynthesis blocked; **bioremediation** — the "**superbug**" of ***Pseudomonas putida*** — **Ananda Mohan Chakrabarty** — the first patented GMO (US Supreme Court, 1980).
- **Plastics and microplastics (<5 mm)** — "**Great Pacific Garbage Patch**"; sea turtles, birds, entry into the food chain.
- **Dead zones** — anoxic water due to coastal eutrophication (**Gulf of Mexico** — fertiliser runoff from the Mississippi).
- Heavy metals, sewage, thermal pollution; **coral bleaching** — with rising temperature corals expel their symbiotic algae (**zooxanthellae**) → white, starving.

**Ocean acidification — "the evil twin of climate change":**

<div class="formula">

CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → HCO₃⁻

- The oceans absorb **about a quarter to a third (~25–30%)** of man-made CO₂.
- Since the Industrial Revolution the pH of surface seawater has fallen from **~8.2 to ~8.1** — on the logarithmic scale this is a **~30% increase in H⁺ concentration**.
- **The availability of carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻) falls** → **CaCO₃ (aragonite, calcite) saturation falls** → **calcifying organisms** — **corals, snails-oysters (molluscs), pteropods, coccolithophores, foraminifera, sea urchins** — find it hard to build shells/skeletons, which may dissolve; effects on the food chain and the fishing industry.

</div>

## 13. Greenhouse effect, global warming and climate change

- **Greenhouse effect** — natural and necessary: the atmosphere lets incoming short-wave solar radiation through, but **greenhouse gases** absorb and re-emit the **long-wave infrared (IR)** radiation returning from the Earth. **Without it the Earth's average temperature would be ~−18°C**; it is now **~15°C** (NCERT). **Joseph Fourier (1824)** gave the concept; **Svante Arrhenius (1896)** — calculated the warming from a doubling of CO₂.
- **Greenhouse gases and relative contributions (NCERT figure):** **CO₂ ~60%**, **CH₄ ~20%**, **CFCs ~14%**, **N₂O ~6%**. Water vapour is the most abundant natural greenhouse gas.
- **Global warming potential (GWP, 100 years, CO₂ = 1):** **CH₄ ~27–30**, **N₂O ~273**, **CFCs/HFCs — thousands**, **SF₆ ~23,500 (the highest)**.
- **Sources of CH₄** — **paddy fields (waterlogged, anaerobic methanogens)**, enteric fermentation in cattle (rumination), wetlands, landfills; **N₂O** — nitrogenous fertilisers.
- **CO₂ concentration** — pre-industrial ~280 ppm → now **~420+ ppm** (**Keeling curve**, Mauna Loa, Hawaii; since 1958).
- **Temperature rise:** IPCC AR6 (2021) — **~1.1°C** since pre-industrial times (2011–2020); **WMO — 2024 was the first calendar year with an average of ~1.55°C** (above 1.5°C).
- **Effects:** **melting of glaciers and polar ice**, **sea-level rise** (thermal expansion + ice melt) — coastal and island countries (Maldives, Bangladesh) at risk; extreme weather events (heat waves, floods, droughts, cyclones); changes in the frequency and intensity of **El Niño**; coral bleaching; shift of species towards the poles and higher altitudes; crop yields (wheat is heat-sensitive) and distribution of pests and diseases; release of CH₄ from thawing permafrost (positive feedback).
- **IPCC (1988)** — WMO and UNEP; Nobel Peace Prize 2007 (with Al Gore).
- **Remedies:** less fossil fuel, **energy efficiency**, renewable energy, **afforestation and carbon sequestration**, **carbon capture and storage (CCS)**, carbon markets, lifestyle — **Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment, 2022)**.
- **India's NDC/targets (Panchamrit, COP-26 Glasgow 2021):** **net-zero by 2070**; **500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030**; **45% cut in emission intensity** compared with 2005 (2030); **50% of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030** (reported to have been achieved already in 2025); an additional carbon sink of 2.5–3 billion tonnes. **National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC, 2008) — 8 missions** (solar, energy efficiency, sustainable habitat, water, Himalayan ecosystem, Green India, sustainable agriculture, strategic knowledge).

## 14. Environmental management

- **Sustainable development** — "**meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs**" — **Brundtland Report (1987)**; **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) — 17 goals, 2015–2030** (SDG-2: zero hunger, SDG-13: climate action, SDG-14: life below water, SDG-15: life on land).
- **Environmental impact assessment (EIA)** — evaluation of likely impacts before a project; in India the **EIA notification 1994**, revised **2006**; public hearing compulsory.
- **Environmental audit**, **ISO 14001** (environmental management system), **Ecomark (1991)**, life-cycle analysis.
- **Principles:** **"polluter pays"**, **precautionary principle**, **public trust doctrine** (Indian courts).
- **Waste management hierarchy** — prevention > reduction > reuse > recycling > recovery (energy) > disposal; **Solid Waste Management Rules 2016** — segregation at source; **Swachh Bharat Mission (2014)**.
- **Ecological restoration** — afforestation, **phytoremediation**, **bioremediation**, reclamation of mined land; **Green India Mission**; **Nagar Van Yojana**.
- **Integrated pest management, integrated nutrient management, conservation agriculture (zero tillage)** — **Happy Seeder** to stop stubble burning.
- **Public participation** — **JFM**, Chipko; **tree-planting drives in UP** (record attempts to plant crores of saplings in a single day).

## 16. Food security

- **Definition (World Food Summit, FAO, 1996):** "physical, social and economic access for all people, at all times, to **sufficient, safe and nutritious food** for an active and healthy life". **Four pillars — availability, access, utilisation, stability**.
- **Green Revolution (1960s)** — **Norman Borlaug** (Nobel Peace 1970; Mexican dwarf wheat), **M.S. Swaminathan** (father in India); semi-dwarf wheat varieties **Sonalika, Kalyan Sona**; rice **IR-8** (IRRI, Philippines), **Jaya** (India); wheat production rose from 11 million tonnes in 1960 to 75 million tonnes by 1990 (NCERT). **Side effects** — groundwater depletion, soil salinity, overuse of chemicals, loss of crop diversity.
- **Biofortification** — **wheat Atlas 66** (high protein), **Golden Rice** (β-carotene, vitamin A), **IARI varieties** — vitamin A-rich carrot, spinach, pumpkin; vitamin C-rich bitter gourd, bathua, mustard, tomato; iron- and calcium-rich spinach and bathua; protein-rich beans (NCERT); **ICAR's zinc-rich wheat/rice varieties**.
- **Other revolutions** — White (milk; Verghese Kurien, Operation Flood 1970), Blue (fisheries), Yellow (oilseeds), Pink (prawns/onion), Golden (horticulture), Silver (eggs).
- **National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013** — covers **75% of the rural and 50% of the urban** population; **5 kg of grain per person per month**; **Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) — 35 kg per family**; maternity benefits for pregnant and lactating women; **Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana** — free grain (extended for five years from January 2024).
- **Shree Anna (millets)** — **International Year of Millets 2023** (India's proposal) — climate-resilient, nutritious; **Indian Institute of Millets Research (IIMR), Hyderabad** — global centre of excellence.
- **Food security and climate change** — heat- and drought-tolerant varieties (**Swarna-Sub1 — submergence-tolerant rice**), **climate-smart agriculture**, reducing storage losses (FCI), **One Nation One Ration Card**, **Soil Health Card (2015)**.
- **UP's contribution** — the largest producer of **wheat, sugarcane and potato**; also a leader in milk production.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Habitat loss = the most important cause of biodiversity loss**, not invasive species.
- **Z is usually 0.1–0.2; 0.6–1.2 at the continental scale**; frugivorous birds-mammals 1.15.
- **India has 4 hotspots** (Sundaland — including Nicobar); NCERT says 3 — check the context of the question.
- **No grazing/private rights in a national park; limited permission in a sanctuary.**
- **The core zone of a biosphere reserve** — human interference completely banned; not the buffer.
- **Stratospheric ozone protects, tropospheric ozone pollutes.**
- **The ozone hole is over Antarctica**, less over the Arctic (weaker polar vortex).
- **CO mainly blocks O₂ transport**, it does not cause acid rain; **CH₄ is a greenhouse gas**, not a cause of acid rain.
- **A catalytic converter needs unleaded petrol.**
- **Higher BOD = more pollution** (less DO).
- **Biomagnification of DDT disturbs Ca metabolism (eggshells) in birds.**
- **Organophosphates — more toxic, less persistent; organochlorines — less acutely toxic, more persistent.**
- **In ocean acidification a 0.1 drop in pH is a ~30% rise in H⁺** (logarithmic scale).
- **The greenhouse effect itself is not harmful**; its **enhanced** form causes global warming.
- **Montreal — ozone; Kyoto/Paris — climate; Ramsar — wetlands; CITES — trade; Cartagena — biosafety; Nagoya — benefit-sharing.**
