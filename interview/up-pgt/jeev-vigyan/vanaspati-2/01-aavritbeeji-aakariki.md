This chapter covers the first part of point 2 of Section A of the official syllabus, "Morphology and morphological modifications of root, stem, leaves etc." — NCERT Class 11 "Morphology of Flowering Plants" and B.Sc. first-year morphology.
The UP PGT 2026 paper is UPSC-style — "Match the following (modification–example)", "Which of statements I, II, III are correct", "Assertion–Reason". So with every modification remember both its **correct example** and **which organ it is a modification of**. Floral formulae of the families (Brassicaceae, Asteraceae, Malvaceae, Liliaceae, Solanaceae) are in the [Plant Taxonomy and Families](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/vanaspati-1/padap-vargikee-aur-kul) chapter.

> **In simple words:** The plant body is divided into two parts — the root system below (from the radicle) and the shoot system above (from the plumule). The root absorbs water and minerals, the stem bears leaves and flowers, and the leaf makes food. Sometimes these organs take on another job — the root starts storing food (carrot), the stem becomes a thorn (Bougainvillea), the leaf becomes a tendril (pea). These are called **modifications**. Which organ something is, is decided by its **position and origin**, not its shape.

## 1. Root

The root develops from the radicle; it is **positively geotropic** and **negatively phototropic**, and it has no nodes-internodes, leaves or buds.

| Root system | Origin | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Tap root | Radicle grows directly into the primary root, which gives secondary and tertiary branches | Dicots — mustard, gram |
| Fibrous root | Primary root short-lived; many roots from the base of the stem | Monocots — wheat, rice |
| Adventitious root | From any part other than the radicle | Grasses, banyan, *Monstera* |

### Regions of the root (from tip upwards)

1. **Root cap** — protects the tip; in aquatic plants (*Pistia*, *Eichhornia*) there is a **root pocket in place of the root cap**.
2. **Meristematic zone** — active division.
3. **Elongation zone** — cells elongate; the root grows in length because of this zone.
4. **Maturation zone** — this is where the **root hairs** that absorb water are found.

### Root modifications

> **Common mistake:** **Sweet potato is a root, potato is a stem.** The potato has "eyes" (buds) that mark nodes; the sweet potato has no buds, only adventitious roots arise from it. Similarly, **radish-carrot-turnip are roots; ginger-turmeric-colocasia-onion are stems.**

## 2. Stem

The stem develops from the plumule; it has **nodes** and **internodes**, leaves arise at the nodes, and an axillary bud is present in the axil of the leaf. Positively phototropic.

### Underground stems — food storage and vegetative propagation

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhizome** | Horizontal, scale leaves, internodes distinct | **Ginger**, **turmeric**, banana, lotus, *Canna* |
| **Tuber** | Tips of stolons swell; eyes = axillary buds | **Potato**, Jerusalem artichoke |
| **Corm** | Vertical, solid, round, scales at nodes | **Colocasia** (arbi), **elephant foot yam/suran** (*Amorphophallus*), *Crocus* (saffron), *Gladiolus* |
| **Bulb** | Stem short, flat disc-like; fleshy scale leaves store food | **Onion** (tunicated), **garlic** (compound, buds = "cloves"), *Lilium* (scaly) |

> **Remember:** In onion the edible part is the **fleshy scale leaves** (the stem is disc-like); in garlic, the **buds (bulblets/cloves)**. Rhizome is horizontal, corm is vertical — that is the main difference.

### Sub-aerial stems — vegetative propagation

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Runner** | Creeps on the ground, roots from nodes | Doob grass (*Cynodon*), *Oxalis*, *Centella* |
| **Stolon** | First grows up, then arches down to touch the ground | Strawberry, mint (*Mentha*), jasmine |
| **Sucker** | Arises from the underground part and grows obliquely upward | *Chrysanthemum*, pineapple, banana |
| **Offset** | One internode long, thick, aquatic | ***Pistia***, ***Eichhornia*** (water hyacinth) |

### Aerial modifications

| Modification | Organ of origin | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Stem tendril** | Axillary bud | Cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon (Cucurbitaceae), grapevine (from the terminal bud) |
| **Thorn** | Axillary bud | ***Bougainvillea***, ***Citrus*** (lemon), pomegranate, *Duranta* |
| **Phylloclade** | Stem flattened and green, many internodes | ***Opuntia*** (prickly pear), *Euphorbia*, *Casuarina*, *Muehlenbeckia* |
| **Cladode** | One internode long, green | ***Asparagus***, *Ruscus* |
| **Bulbil** | Bud fills with food and detaches | *Dioscorea* (yam), *Agave*, *Oxalis* |
| Fleshy stem | | *Opuntia* |

> **Common mistake:** There are two kinds of sharp structures — the **thorn**, a modification of the stem (connected to vascular tissue, arises deep, e.g. Bougainvillea, lemon), and the **spine**, a modification of a leaf/stipule (*Opuntia*, stipular spines of *Zizyphus* (ber)). **The "thorns" of rose are neither thorns nor spines — they are epidermal outgrowths, "prickles"**, which break off easily.

## 3. Leaf

The leaf is a flat lateral structure at the node of the stem, of exogenous origin; a bud is present in its axil (not in the axils of the leaflets of a compound leaf — this is the difference between a compound leaf and a branch).

**Parts:** Leaf base — with stipules; in legumes the swollen base is the **pulvinus** — the basis of movement in touch-me-not (*Mimosa*); in monocots the leaf base forms a **sheath** that encircles the stem. Petiole; lamina — midrib, veins.

### Venation

| Type | Where | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| **Reticulate** | Dicots | ***Calophyllum*** (parallel in a dicot), *Eryngium* |
| **Parallel** | Monocots | ***Smilax***, *Dioscorea*, *Alocasia* (reticulate in monocots) |

### Compound leaf

- **Pinnate** — leaflets on the central axis, the **rachis**: unipinnate — neem (*Azadirachta*, imparipinnate), rose, tamarind (paripinnate); bipinnate — *Acacia* (babool), gulmohar (*Delonix*), touch-me-not; tripinnate — drumstick (*Moringa*), *Melia*.
- **Palmate** — all leaflets at the tip of the petiole: unifoliate — lemon (*Citrus* — articulated/winged petiole), bifoliate — *Balanites*, trifoliate — *Oxalis*, bael (*Aegle*), quadrifoliate — *Marsilea*, multifoliate — ***Bombax*** (silk cotton).

### Phyllotaxy

| Type | Leaves per node | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Alternate | 1 | Hibiscus, mustard, sunflower |
| Opposite — superposed | 2, in the same plane | Guava, *Quisqualis* |
| Opposite — decussate | 2, next pair at right angles | **Aak** (*Calotropis*), tulsi |
| Whorled/verticillate | More than 2 | **Kaner** (*Nerium*), *Alstonia* (saptaparni) |

### Leaf modifications

| Modification | Part | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf tendril** | Whole leaf | ***Lathyrus aphaca*** (wild pea) — stipules do the job of the leaf |
| | Terminal leaflets | **Pea** (*Pisum sativum*) |
| | Stipules | *Smilax* |
| | Leaf tip | *Gloriosa* (glory lily) |
| **Leaf spine** | Leaf | *Opuntia*; scale leaves in *Asparagus* |
| | Stipules | *Zizyphus* (ber), *Acacia* |
| **Phyllode** | Petiole flattened and green, lamina falls off | ***Acacia auriculiformis*** (Australian acacia), *A. melanoxylon* |
| **Pitcher** | Lamina forms the pitcher, tip forms the lid | ***Nepenthes***; *Sarracenia* |
| **Bladder** | Leaf segments | ***Utricularia*** (bladderwort, aquatic) |
| Glandular hairs | Leaf surface | ***Drosera*** (sundew) — mucilage |
| Snap-trap | | *Dionaea* (Venus flytrap) |
| Food storage | Scale leaves | Onion, garlic |
| Bud protection | Scale leaves | Trees of cold regions |
| Reproduction (leaf buds) | In notches of the leaf margin | ***Bryophyllum*** (pattharchatta) |

> **Exam angle:** Insectivorous plants (*Nepenthes*, *Drosera*, *Utricularia*, *Dionaea*) grow in **nitrogen-poor** soil — they obtain N from insects, but being green they are also **autotrophs**. In India, *Nepenthes khasiana* is endemic to Meghalaya.

## 4. Inflorescence

The arrangement of flowers on the floral axis. The axis = **peduncle**; a leafless axis arising directly from the ground is called a **scape** (onion).

| Basis | Racemose | Cymose |
|---|---|---|
| Main axis | Grows indefinitely | Ends in a flower at the tip and stops |
| Order of flowers | **Acropetal** — older below, younger above | **Basipetal** — oldest flower at the apex |
| Order of opening (on a flat axis) | **Centripetal** — outside to inside | **Centrifugal** — inside to outside |

### Types of racemose

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Raceme | Long axis, pedicellate flowers | Radish, mustard (Brassicaceae), *Delphinium* |
| Panicle (compound raceme) | Branched raceme | **Gulmohar**, neem; the mango inflorescence is also usually treated as a panicle |
| Spike | Sessile flowers, long axis | *Achyranthes* (chirchita), amaranth (*Amaranthus*) |
| Spikelet | Unit of grasses — with glumes | Wheat, rice, barley (Poaceae) |
| Catkin | Pendulous, unisexual flowers | Mulberry (*Morus*), *Salix*, *Betula* |
| Spadix | Fleshy axis, covered by a large bract, the **spathe** | **Banana**, colocasia, palms, female inflorescence of maize |
| Corymb | Lower pedicels longer, all flowers in one plane | Candytuft (*Iberis*), *Cassia* |
| Umbel | All pedicels from one point; a **whorl of bracts (involucre)** at the base | Coriander, fennel, carrot (Apiaceae; compound umbel), onion (simple) |
| Capitulum/head | Flat receptacle; **ray florets** outside, **disc florets** inside | **Sunflower**, marigold, *Tridax* (Asteraceae) |

### Cymose and special inflorescences

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Monochasial — helicoid | Branches on one side only | *Begonia*, *Drosera*, *Hamelia* |
| Monochasial — scorpioid | Branches on alternate sides | *Heliotropium*, *Freesia* |
| Dichasial | Two branches below the terminal flower | *Bougainvillea*, *Clerodendrum*, *Dianthus*, jasmine |
| Polychasial | Many branches | Aak (*Calotropis*), *Nerium* |
| **Cyathium** | Cup-like involucre; one central female flower (on a gynophore), surrounded by many male flowers (each with only one stamen); nectar glands | ***Euphorbia***, ***Poinsettia*** (Euphorbiaceae) |
| **Hypanthodium** | Cup-like fleshy receptacle with an **ostiole** at the top; inside are male (upper), female and gall flowers | ***Ficus*** — banyan, peepal, fig, gular |
| **Verticillaster** | At each node, opposite dichasial→monochasial cymes, looking like a whorl | **Tulsi** (*Ocimum*), *Salvia* (Lamiaceae) |
| Thyrse | Mixed — cymose branches on a racemose axis | Grapevine (*Vitis*) |

> **Memory trick:** "**C**yathium — **E**uphorbia, **H**ypanthodium — **F**icus, **V**erticillaster — **T**ulsi" → "CE–HF–VT". In *Ficus*, pollination is done by the **fig wasp (*Blastophaga*)** — obligate mutualism.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Poinsettia (*Euphorbia pulcherrima*) shows one large, showy "flower". What actually forms this appearance? (A) Brightly coloured bracts surrounding inconspicuous flowers (B) Stamens modified to look like petals (C) Large sepals (D) Fusion of carpels into one ovary
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — the red "petals" are actually **bracts**; the small yellow-green cups in the middle are **cyathium** inflorescences.


## 5. Flower

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In angiosperms the distinction between vegetative and reproductive structures is basic, but some modifications blur it. Which example best shows **a reproductive structure formed from a modified vegetative organ**? (A) A flower bud formed from a modified shoot with leaf-like organs (B) The tuberous root of sweet potato (C) The leaf spine of *Opuntia* (D) The stem tendril of *Cucurbita*
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — **the flower is a modified, condensed shoot** (thalamus = stem, floral organs = modified leaves); the other three are modifications for vegetative functions (storage, protection, climbing).


The flower is a **modified, condensed part** of the shoot, specialised for reproduction. Four whorls on the thalamus (receptacle): **calyx** → **corolla** → **androecium** → **gynoecium**. If sepals and petals look alike, it is a **perianth** — *Lilium*, onion.

### Symmetry and sex

- **Actinomorphic** — divisible into two equal halves along any diameter: mustard, datura, chilli. **Zygomorphic** — along only one plane: pea, gulmohar, bean, *Cassia*. **Asymmetric** — *Canna*.
- **Bisexual**, **unisexual**; male and female flowers on the same plant = **monoecious** — maize, cucumber, castor; on separate plants = **dioecious** — papaya, date palm, mulberry, *Vallisneria*. Male, female and bisexual flowers all on one plant = **polygamous** — mango.

### Position of the ovary

| Type | Ovary | Other whorls | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypogynous** | **Superior** | Below the ovary | Mustard, hibiscus, brinjal, China rose |
| **Perigynous** | Half-inferior | Almost level with the ovary, on the rim of a cup-shaped thalamus | **Plum, rose, peach** (Rosaceae), pea (some) |
| **Epigynous** | **Inferior** | Above the ovary | **Guava, cucumber, sunflower (ray florets)**, apple |

### Aestivation — arrangement of petals/sepals in the bud

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Valvate | Margins only touch | **Aak** (*Calotropis*) |
| Twisted | One margin of each overlaps the next | **Hibiscus**, cotton, lady's finger (Malvaceae) |
| Imbricate | Irregular — one fully inside, one fully outside | **Gulmohar**, *Cassia* |
| **Vexillary/papilionaceous** | 5 petals — a large **standard (vexillum)**, 2 **wings**, 2 fused **keel** petals; standard outermost, keel innermost | **Pea, bean** (Fabaceae/Papilionoideae) |

> **Common mistake:** In gulmohar and *Cassia* (Caesalpinioideae) the aestivation is **ascending imbricate**, where **the posterior petal is innermost**; whereas in pea (vexillary) **the posterior standard is outermost**. This pair is asked often.

### Androecium

- **Epipetalous** — stamens attached to petals: brinjal, datura (Solanaceae); **epiphyllous** — attached to the perianth: *Lilium*.
- **Monadelphous** — all filaments in one bundle (staminal tube): **hibiscus**, Malvaceae. **Diadelphous** — two bundles, (9)+1: **pea**. **Polyadelphous** — many bundles: **lemon** (*Citrus*), *Bombax*.
- **Syngenesious** — anthers fused, filaments free: **sunflower** (Asteraceae). **Synandrous** — both filaments and anthers fully fused: Cucurbitaceae (some).
- **Tetradynamous** — 6 stamens, 4 long + 2 short: **mustard** (Brassicaceae). **Didynamous** — of 4, 2 long + 2 short: tulsi (Lamiaceae).
- A sterile stamen = **staminode**.

### Gynoecium and placentation

If the carpels are free it is **apocarpous** — lotus, rose, *Michelia*; if fused, **syncarpous** — mustard, tomato.

| Placentation | Arrangement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal | Unilocular ovary, two rows on one ridge | **Pea** (Fabaceae) |
| Axile | Multilocular; on the central axis | **Hibiscus, tomato, lemon**, *Petunia* |
| Parietal | On the wall; becomes bilocular by a **false septum (replum)** | **Mustard**, *Argemone*, cucumber (parietal in Cucurbitaceae) |
| Free central | No septa, on a central axis | *Dianthus*, *Primula* |
| Basal | Single ovule, at the base | **Sunflower, marigold** (Asteraceae) |
| Superficial | On the surface of the septa | Water lily (*Nymphaea*) |

### Symbols of the floral formula

⚥ bisexual, ♂ male, ♀ female, ⊕ actinomorphic, % zygomorphic, K calyx, C corolla, P perianth, A androecium, G gynoecium, G̲ (line below) = superior ovary, Ḡ (line above) = inferior ovary, ( ) = cohesion, a line/arc drawn across two whorls = adhesion (e.g. epipetalous).

## 6. Fruit

After fertilisation the **ovary becomes the fruit** and the **ovule becomes the seed**. Pericarp = outer (epicarp) + middle (mesocarp) + inner (endocarp). From the ovary alone = **true fruit**; if the thalamus/other parts are also included = **false fruit (pseudocarp)** — apple, pear (fleshy thalamus), cashew (fleshy pedicel), jackfruit, mulberry, fig, strawberry.

### Simple fruits — one flower, one (syncarpous or single) ovary

| Class | Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry dehiscent | Legume/pod | One carpel, splits along both sutures | Pea, gram, bean |
| | Follicle | Along one suture | **Aak** (*Calotropis*), *Michelia* (aggregate) |
| | Siliqua | Bicarpellary, replum, splits from below upward | **Mustard** |
| | Silicula | Short, broad siliqua | *Capsella*, *Iberis* |
| | Capsule | Multicarpellary | Cotton, lady's finger, datura, poppy (porous) |
| Dry indehiscent | Caryopsis | **Pericarp and seed coat fused** | **Wheat, rice, maize** (Poaceae) |
| | Cypsela | Inferior ovary, **pappus** — modified calyx | **Sunflower, marigold** (Asteraceae) |
| | Nut | Hard pericarp, one seed | Cashew (true fruit = nut), *Trapa* (water chestnut) |
| | Achene | One seed, pericarp free | *Clematis*, *Ranunculus*; the "seeds" on a strawberry |
| | Samara | Winged | *Holoptelea*, *Acer* (double samara), sal (wings = enlarged sepals) |
| Schizocarpic | Cremocarp | Two **mericarps**, hanging from a **carpophore** | **Coriander, fennel, cumin** (Apiaceae) |
| | Lomentum | Pod breaks at constrictions | *Mimosa*, *Desmodium*, *Acacia arabica* |
| | Regma | 3+ segments | **Castor** (*Ricinus*), *Geranium* |
| | Carcerulus | | Tulsi, *Salvia* (4 nutlets) |
| Fleshy | **Drupe** | **Hard endocarp (stone)** | **Mango** (mesocarp eaten), **coconut** (fibrous mesocarp = coir; edible = endosperm), ber, olive, almond |
| | Berry | Whole pericarp fleshy, many seeds | **Tomato**, grape, brinjal, papaya, guava, banana (seedless) |
| | Pepo | Inferior ovary, parietal, hard epicarp | Cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon (Cucurbitaceae) |
| | **Hesperidium** | **Juicy hairs** of the endocarp are eaten | **Lemon, orange** (*Citrus*) |
| | **Pome** | **Fleshy thalamus** is eaten; false fruit | **Apple, pear** |
| | Balausta | Juicy **seed coat (testa)** is eaten | **Pomegranate** |
| | Amphisarca | Hard rind, pulp inside | Bael (*Aegle*), wood apple (kaitha) |

### Aggregate fruits — one flower, many free carpels (apocarpous)

- Etaerio of follicles — *Michelia* (champa), *Calotropis* (paired); etaerio of achenes — *Ranunculus*, **strawberry** (on a fleshy thalamus); etaerio of berries — **custard apple (*Annona squamosa*)**, *Polyalthia*; etaerio of drupes — raspberry.

### Composite/multiple fruits — from a whole inflorescence

- **Sorosis** — from a spike/spadix: **jackfruit** (*Artocarpus*; edible = perianth and bracts, receptacle), **mulberry** (*Morus*; edible = juicy perianth), **pineapple** (*Ananas*).
- **Syconus** — from a hypanthodium: **fig, banyan, gular** (*Ficus*).

> **Exam angle — edible parts:** Mango — mesocarp; coconut — endosperm (water = free-nuclear, kernel = cellular endosperm); lemon — juicy hairs; apple — thalamus; pomegranate — seed coat; litchi — aril (outgrowth of the seed); wheat — endosperm (and embryo); cashew — pedicel and cotyledons; banana — mesocarp and endocarp; guava/tomato/papaya — whole pericarp and placenta; jackfruit — perianth; mulberry — perianth; groundnut — cotyledons; grape — pericarp and placenta.

## 7. Seed

A mature fertilised ovule. **Seed coat** — the outer **testa** (from the integument) and the inner **tegmen**; **hilum** — scar of the funicle; **micropyle** — water entry during germination.

| | Dicot (gram/bean) | Monocot (maize) |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | Two, fleshy (food storage) | One — the **scutellum** — absorbs food from the endosperm |
| Endosperm | Usually absent (exalbuminous) — gram, pea, bean | Present (albuminous); the outer layer of the maize endosperm is the **aleurone layer** — protein-rich |
| Sheaths | — | **Coleoptile** over the plumule, **coleorhiza** over the radicle |
| Albuminous dicot exceptions | Castor, *Annona* | — |
| Exalbuminous monocot exceptions | — | Orchids, *Sagittaria*, *Alisma* |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** The part of the embryonal axis **above** the cotyledons is called — (A) Hypocotyl (B) Hilum (C) Epicotyl (D) Radicle
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C) Epicotyl** — it ends in the plumule; the part below the cotyledons is the hypocotyl, which ends in the radicle.

**Germination:** **Epigeal** — the **hypocotyl** elongates and the cotyledons come above the ground: bean, castor, cotton, tamarind, onion (exception among monocots). **Hypogeal** — the **epicotyl** elongates and the cotyledons stay in the soil: gram, pea, maize, wheat, mango. (Some books treat groundnut as hypogeal and some as epigeal — avoid such options.) **Vivipary** — the seed germinates while still in the fruit, on the plant itself: mangroves — ***Rhizophora***, *Sonneratia*.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Some monocots lack a tap root, but some dicots have plenty of adventitious roots** — "tap root = dicot" is a general rule, not an absolute one.
- **Potato = stem (tuber), sweet potato = root; ginger = rhizome (stem); colocasia/elephant foot yam = corm; onion = bulb.**
- **Rose — prickle; Bougainvillea/lemon — thorn (stem); *Opuntia*/ber — spine (leaf/stipule).**
- In *Opuntia* the **phylloclade (stem)** is green and the leaves are modified into **spines** — two modifications in one plant.
- *Lathyrus aphaca* — whole leaf a tendril, the **stipules** photosynthesise; pea — only the terminal leaflets are tendrils.
- The tendril of *Cucurbita* is of the **stem** (axillary bud); that of pea is of the **leaf**.
- **Coconut is a drupe (not a berry)**; **banana is a berry**; **tomato is a berry**; **strawberry is an etaerio of achenes (false fruit)**.
- **The sunflower is not "one flower" — it is a capitulum inflorescence.**
- **The capsule of poppy (*Papaver*) opens by pores (porocidal).**
- Ovary position: **apple and guava are epigynous (inferior ovary)** — that is why remains of sepals are seen at the top of an apple.

## Quick revision

- Root pocket instead of root cap — *Pistia*, *Eichhornia*.
- Pneumatophores — *Rhizophora*, *Avicennia* (mangroves); negatively geotropic.
- Velamen — aerial roots of epiphytic orchids (*Vanda*).
- Prop roots — banyan; stilt roots — maize, sugarcane, *Pandanus*.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Which geotropism do the respiratory roots of *Rhizophora* show? — **Negative geotropism** (they come up out of the swampy, O₂-poor soil).
2. What kind of modification is ginger? — **Rhizome (stem)** (internodes, nodes and scale leaves distinct).
3. Sweet potato is a — **tuberous adventitious root** (no buds).
4. What does the photosynthesis in *Opuntia*? — **The phylloclade** (the leaves have become spines).
5. In pea, the tendril is a modification of — **the terminal leaflets** (leaf).
6. The thorn of *Bougainvillea* is a — **thorn (axillary bud, stem)**.
7. Where is velamen tissue found? — **In the aerial roots of epiphytic orchids** (moisture absorption).
8. How many stamens does each male flower of a cyathium have? — **One** (one central female flower).
9. Fig develops from which inflorescence? — **Hypanthodium → syconus fruit**.
10. Example of twisted aestivation — **hibiscus** (Malvaceae).
11. Androecium of pea — **diadelphous (9)+1**.
12. Placentation of mustard — **parietal, with a replum** (appears bilocular).
13. Edible part of apple — **fleshy thalamus** (pome, false fruit).
14. Edible part of coconut — **endosperm** (fruit = fibrous drupe).
15. Fruit of lemon — **hesperidium**; edible = **juicy hairs of the endocarp**.
16. Fruit of coriander — **cremocarp** (two mericarps, carpophore).
17. What kind of fruit is jackfruit? — **Composite (sorosis)**.
18. Example of an epigynous flower — **guava/cucumber/sunflower** (inferior ovary).
19. The "leaf" of *Acacia auriculiformis* is actually a — **phyllode** (flattened petiole).
20. In the maize seed, what takes food from the endosperm? — **The scutellum** (the single cotyledon).
