Re-reading all the B.Sc. books in 44 days is not possible. The aim of this chapter is — **which book to open when, what to note, and how to keep what you have read in memory until the exam.** The books of this collection are the main text; standard textbooks are only for reference, when you need depth in some type study or diagram.

> **In simple words:** Main text = this collection + NCERT 11–12. Reference = 1–2 B.Sc. books per unit. Notes = only tables and your own mistakes. Revision = +1, +3, +7 days. Practice = end-of-chapter questions, question banks and two mocks.

## 1. NCERT — the base layer

| Book | Language | Why |
|---|---|---|
| NCERT Biology Class 11 | Both Hindi and English | Taxonomy, plant and animal kingdoms, morphology, tissues, cell, biomolecules, plant and human physiology |
| NCERT Biology Class 12 | Both Hindi and English | Reproduction, genetics, molecular basis, evolution, health, biotechnology, ecology |

> **Exam angle:** About 35–40% of the 2026 questions could be solved directly from NCERT. Look once at NCERT's **highlighted words, tables and diagram labels** in both languages — the question paper is bilingual and many terms (such as "beejandadwar/micropyle", "parshvapad/parapodia") look unfamiliar in Hindi.

> **Remember:** In the new NCERT editions (after 2023) some chapters/parts have been removed (rationalised syllabus). The PGT syllabus is larger than that — the removed parts (such as some portions of plant physiology) are included in this collection. If you can find an old NCERT copy, it is useful too.

## 2. Standard textbooks — unit-wise reference

Only books widely used in B.Sc./M.Sc. Choose **one book per unit**; there is no need to buy all of them.

### Botany

| Unit | Book (author) | What for |
|---|---|---|
| Algae, fungi, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms | **Botany for Degree Students** series — B.R. Vashishta / P.C. Vashishta (S. Chand) | Type studies, life-cycle diagrams — the level of the 2026 cryptogam questions |
| Complete B.Sc. Botany (summary form) | **College Botany** — B.P. Pandey (S. Chand) | All units in one series |
| Plant taxonomy, families | **Plant Systematics** — Gurcharan Singh | Comparative features of families, classification systems, APG |
| Angiosperm embryology | **An Introduction to the Embryology of Angiosperms** — P. Maheshwari; **The Embryology of Angiosperms** — S.S. Bhojwani and S.P. Bhatnagar | Types of embryo sac, endosperm, modern trends |
| Plant physiology | **Fundamentals of Plant Physiology** — V.K. Jain (S. Chand); for depth **Plant Physiology** — L. Taiz and E. Zeiger | Beyond NCERT — C₄/CAM, photoperiodism, stress |
| Microbiology | **Microbiology** — M.J. Pelczar and co-authors | Bacteria, viruses, economic importance of microbes |
| Plant pathology | **Plant Diseases** — R.S. Singh | Disease — pathogen — symptoms — control |
| Ecology | **Fundamentals of Ecology** — E.P. Odum | Basic definitions of ecosystem, energy flow, limiting factors (Liebig/Shelford) |

### Zoology

| Unit | Book (author) | What for |
|---|---|---|
| Non-chordates, chordates, type studies | **Modern Text Book of Zoology: Invertebrates** and **Vertebrates** — R.L. Kotpal (Rastogi) | Larvae, comparative anatomy, classification |
| Non-chordates, chordates (alternative) | **Invertebrate Zoology** and **Chordate Zoology** — E.L. Jordan and P.S. Verma (S. Chand) | The same, in simpler language |
| Cell, genetics, molecular, evolution, ecology | **Cell Biology, Genetics, Molecular Biology, Evolution and Ecology** — P.S. Verma and V.K. Agarwal (S. Chand) | Almost all units of Zoology III in one book |
| Genetics (depth) | **Principles of Genetics** — E.J. Gardner and co-authors | Gene interaction, linkage, mutation |
| Physiology | **Textbook of Medical Physiology** — Guyton and Hall (selected chapters only) | Baroreceptors, chemoreceptors, MAP — the level of the 2026 integrated questions |
| Embryology | **An Introduction to Embryology** — B.I. Balinsky; **Developmental Biology** — S.F. Gilbert | Cleavage, embryonic membranes, differentiation |
| Animal behaviour | **Animal Behaviour** — Reena Mathur (Rastogi) | Innate/learned behaviour, migration, communication |

> **Common mistake:** Reading the thick book of every unit from the beginning. In 44 days this is impossible. Open a book only when some type study/diagram in a chapter of this collection feels incomplete — **only that page**.

> **Remember:** Hindi editions of many books are also published, but edition and translation quality vary — look at the pages before buying. If you study from a Hindi edition, still note **the English form of every technical term**.

### General Knowledge

This collection's [General Knowledge book](/interview/up-pgt/samanya-gyan/pattern-aur-ranniti) + one daily newspaper (national/Uttar Pradesh news) + a list of the main schemes of the Uttar Pradesh government. No separate thick book is needed for 30 questions.

## 3. Notes — what and how

### Five notebooks (or five parts of one notebook)

| Notebook | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Botany tables | Type-study comparisons (algae/bryophytes/pteridophytes/gymnosperms), family features, economic plants (plant — botanical name — family — part — product), diseases |
| 2. Zoology tables | Phylum/class — features — examples; larva — phylum; comparative anatomy (heart, kidney, skeleton); hormones, enzymes |
| 3. "Who — what — when" | Discoverers, years, books (Genera Plantarum — Bentham and Hooker; the term ecosystem — Tansley; double fertilisation — Nawaschin 1898) |
| 4. **Error log** | Every wrong/guessed question (method below) |
| 5. General Knowledge + current affairs | 5 lines daily |

> **Memory trick:** In notes, **tables, not sentences** — with a "what is different" column. The comparative questions of 2026 (Riccia vs Marchantia, Selaginella vs Equisetum, Acanthaceae vs Verbenaceae) are solved directly from such a table.

### Sample comparison table

| Feature | Riccia | Marchantia | Funaria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parts of sporophyte | Capsule only | Foot, seta, capsule | Foot, seta, capsule (complex) |
| Elaters | No | Yes | No (peristome teeth) |
| Asexual reproduction | Fragmentation | Gemma cups | Secondary protonema etc. |

## 4. Spaced Revision

What you read is half forgotten in a week without repetition. The "revision" column of each day in the [45-day plan](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/yojana/yojana-kaise-chalayen) is based on this rule:

<div class="formula">

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Same night | Read the chapter's **quick revision** (15–25 lines) aloud | 10 minutes |
| **+1 day** | Write 10 facts without looking; then check | 10 minutes |
| **+3 days** | The chapter's **practice questions** again | 15 minutes |
| **+7 days** | **Traps and exceptions** + your own table | 15 minutes |
| Final stage (Days 31–43) | Starred questions of the question banks | As per the plan |

</div>

## 5. Error Log

Record every wrong question and **every right question on which you had guessed** — both.

| Date | Source (chapter/mock, Q) | Topic | Style | Type of mistake | Correct fact (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 November | Not a mock — gymnosperms practice 7 | Gnetales | Statement | J | Vessels only in Gnetales |

### Five types of mistake

| Code | Type | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **K** | Did not know (Knowledge) | Add to the table, repeat at +1/+3/+7 |
| **J** | Fell into a trap (Trap — "all", "only", swaps) | Re-read the "Traps and exceptions" section; make 5 such statements yourself |
| **R** | Did not read the question properly (Reading — "NOT", "false", A–R letters) | Habit of circling negative words in the question |
| **G** | Unnecessary guess (Guess — without eliminating two options) | Guessing rules ([Chapter 2](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/pariksha-margdarshika/pariksha-yojana-aur-ankan)) |
| **T** | Lack of time (Time) | Time plan; the 40-second rule for one-liners |

> **Exam angle:** Count every week — how many K, J, R, G, T? If **J + R + G** are more than half of all mistakes, you do not need to read more; you need to improve **exam skill** — and that improves fastest.

## 6. Practice material and its order

| Material | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| 15–20 practice questions at the end of each chapter | Every chapter | Same day + 3 days later |
| "PYQ (UP PGT 2026)" in the chapters | Every chapter | While reading, with the answer covered |
| Book question banks (~100 questions per book) | [Botany I](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/vanaspati-1/prashn-bank), [Botany II](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/vanaspati-2/prashn-bank), [Zoology I](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-1/prashn-bank), [Zoology II](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/prashn-bank), [Zoology III](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-3/prashn-bank) | Weekly review days and Days 31–43 |
| General Knowledge question bank | [General Knowledge — Question Bank](/interview/up-pgt/samanya-gyan/prashn-bank) | After Day 30 |
| Full mock (120 questions, 2 hours, +3/−1) | [Mock Test 1](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/abhyas/mock-test-1), [Mock Test 2](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/abhyas/mock-test-2) | Days 31 and 37 |
| The actual 2026 paper | [Chapter 4](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/pariksha-margdarshika/pichhla-prashnapatra-2026-vishleshan) and questions scattered through the chapters | Again on Day 40 |

### How to take a mock

1. Sit at the **real time** (morning or afternoon — whichever is your shift), 2 hours, without interruption, mobile in another room.
2. Fill circles on an OMR-like sheet — the time to fill 120 circles is also part of the practice.
3. Before checking answers, write next to each question: **P** (sure), **E2** (guess after eliminating two), **E1** (after eliminating one), **X** (left).
4. Marks: 4S − H. Then see — was the accuracy on E2 questions around 50%? On E1 around 33%? If much lower, your "elimination" is not reliable.
5. **As much time for analysis as for the mock** (2 hours) — every mistake into the error log.

## 7. Study time for 44 days

| Slot | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Biology | ~4 hours | That day's 1–2 chapters (as per the plan) |
| General Knowledge | ~1.5 hours | One GK chapter + 20 minutes of current affairs |
| Revision/practice | ~0.5–1 hour | +1, +3, +7 rule; error log |
| **Total** | **~6 hours** | |

> **In simple words:** If you are studying alongside a job and 6 hours is not possible, keep General Knowledge at 1 hour and Biology at 3 hours, but never skip the revision column.

## Traps and exceptions

- A book collection ≠ preparation. In 44 days, **one main source** (this collection + NCERT) read repeatedly is better than many books read once.
- Do not spend time making notes beautiful — tables, short words, your own language.
- The error log should contain not only wrong questions but also those **right by guessing** — they can go wrong next time.
- It is the **analysis**, not the mock score, that matters.
- The old 2010/2013 papers had no negative marking — take facts from them, not strategy.
