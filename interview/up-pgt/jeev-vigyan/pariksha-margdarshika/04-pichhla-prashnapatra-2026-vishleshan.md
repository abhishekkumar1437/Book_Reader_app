This chapter is the **compass** of your preparation. All 125 questions of the Biology Lecturer exam held on 9 May 2026 (old Advt. 02/2022) have been sorted by topic, style and level. Your exam (Advt. 06/2026) will be set by **the same commission**, **seven months later** — so this is the best indicator of level and style.

> **In simple words:** The paper was at **B.Sc. level**, **Botany : Zoology ≈ 1 : 1**, the heaviest part was **cryptogams + gymnosperms + families** (32 questions), and about **one quarter of the questions** were statement-combination, matching, assertion–reason or sequence type. The difficulty lay not in the facts but in the **long, balanced options**.

> **Remember:** The **official answer key of this paper is not available to us** (the provisional key was released on 12 May 2026 behind a login). Wherever an answer is given below, it is a **"Reasoned answer (official key not available)"** — only for questions where the fact is certain.

## 6. 21 actual questions — in English

(The original paper was bilingual; the questions are given here in English. A "reasoned answer" is not the official key.)

### Zoology side

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q4 — In polychaetes, which structure mainly enhances both locomotion and respiration?
> (A) Parapodia (B) Clitellum (C) Prostomium (D) Nephridia
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — Parapodia help in swimming/crawling and, through their thin, vascularised surface, in gas exchange.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q12 — A desert vertebrate keeps water loss to a minimum in nitrogen excretion and contracts its skeletal muscles rapidly while running. Which explanation links both adaptations?
> (A) Ureotelism needs no water conservation; sarcomeres shorten by calcium-independent contraction (B) Uricotelism removes nitrogen with minimal water, and ATP-driven actin–myosin sliding produces contraction (C) Ammonia excretion reduces water loss; contraction by ATP-independent actin polymerisation (D) Urea is excreted in solid form; contraction by passive elastic recoil
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)**

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q16 — Echinoderm larvae are bilaterally symmetrical, adults radial. Best explanation of the developmental transition in starfish with respect to the larva and metamorphosis?
> (A) The pluteus larva is radially symmetrical from the start… (B) The auricularia larva after encystment… (C) The bipinnaria larva becomes the adult directly without any structural reorganisation (D) The brachiolaria larva attaches to a substratum, then pentaradial symmetry arises in the adult through extensive reorganisation of internal structures
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (D)** — Starfish: bipinnaria → brachiolaria (attaching arms) → metamorphosis. Pluteus = sea urchin/brittle star; auricularia = sea cucumber.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q19 — Herdmania and Amphioxus are both protochordates, but their adult organisation shows opposite trends. Best explanation?
> (A) Chordate features completely lost in the adults of both… (B) Vertebral column in both… (C) In Herdmania chordate features occur only in the larva, the adult is sessile and degenerate; Amphioxus retains basic chordate features, including a permanent notochord, throughout life (D) The reverse
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)** — Herdmania shows **retrogressive metamorphosis**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q28 — In a genetic experiment the observed numbers deviate from the expected Mendelian ratio. The chi-square value comes out **less** than the critical value at the given significance level. Correct interpretation?
> (A) Null hypothesis rejected, deviation significant (B) Null hypothesis not rejected, the observed data match the expected without any deviation (C) Sample inadequate… (D) Null hypothesis not rejected, the deviation is due to random sampling within the limits of probability
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (D)** — The trap in (B) is "without any deviation".

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q44 — Match List-I with List-II: (a) Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (b) Keoladeo National Park (c) Gir Wildlife Sanctuary (d) Sundarbans Biosphere Reserve / (I) Habitat of the Asiatic lion, dry deciduous forest (II) Mangrove ecosystem under tidal influence (III) India's first biosphere reserve, Western Ghats (IV) Important wetland for migratory waterbirds
> (A) a-IV, b-III, c-I, d-II (B) a-III, b-IV, c-I, d-II (C) a-III, b-I, c-IV, d-II (D) a-II, b-IV, c-I, d-III
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Nilgiri became the first biosphere reserve in 1986.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q60 — Statements on cellular differentiation: (I) In multicellular organisms the main basis of differentiation is differential gene expression, not gene loss (II) Cytoplasmic determinants and positional signals decide cell fate in early development (III) Once differentiated, all cells irreversibly lose the ability to change fate **under any condition** (IV) Epigenetic changes such as DNA methylation and histone modification maintain the differentiated state
> (A) I and III (B) I, II and IV (C) II, III and IV (D) All
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — (III) is refuted by regeneration, cloning (Dolly) and iPS cells.

### Botany side

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q65 — About the seeds of Ginkgo: (I) They look like small plums (II) They are enclosed in true fruits (III) They have a fleshy outer covering. Correct statements?
> (A) II and III (B) I and III (C) I, II and III (D) I and II
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Gymnosperms have no ovary and no fruit; the flesh is the outer layer of the seed coat (sarcotesta).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q67 — Assertion (A): Anomalous secondary growth in stems often results from successive cambia or abnormal activity of the vascular cambium. Reason (R): In some plants the extra cambia arise **only** from the vascular cambium and form **a single continuous** vascular cylinder.
> (A) Both true, R not the explanation (B) A true, R false (C) A false, R true (D) Both true, R the correct explanation
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Successive cambia (e.g. Boerhaavia, beetroot) usually arise from the pericycle/cortex and form several concentric rings; both "only" and "a single continuous" are wrong. Note — here the option "both true, R the correct explanation" is at **(D)**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q79 — Batrachospermum (red alga) and Padina (brown alga) — correct correlation of thallus organisation and life cycle?
> (A) Both have similar life cycles, isomorphic alternation of generations… (B) Batrachospermum — freshwater filamentous thallus, carposporophyte after fertilisation; Padina — marine, parenchymatous thallus, with alternation of generations (C) Padina has no sporophyte stage… (D) Batrachospermum diploid-dominant, with conceptacles; Padina haploid-dominant…
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Conceptacles are a feature of Fucus (brown), not of red algae.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q81 — Match: (a) Gloeotrichia (b) Oscillatoria (c) Scytonema (d) Cyanobacteria (general) / (I) True branching, heterocysts, N₂ fixation (II) Oscillating movement without flagella, no heterocysts (III) Colonial filamentous form with basal heterocysts and tapering trichomes (IV) Prokaryotic organisation, photosynthetic pigments but no chloroplasts
> (A) a–II, b–I, c–IV, d–III (B) a–III, b–II, c–I, d–IV (C) a–III, b–IV, c–I, d–II (D) a–I, b–III, c–II, d–IV
> **Note:** a–III, b–II, d–IV are certain, so only **(B)** remains. But note — **Scytonema shows "false branching"**; true branching occurs in forms like Stigonema. So the question has an error; even in such a case **choose the answer from the other three certain matches**, and remember the option of filing an objection after the result.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q89 — In bryophytes the complexity of the sporophyte varies with its dependence on the gametophyte. Difference between the life cycles of Riccia and Marchantia?
> (A) Marchantia has no sexual reproduction… (B) The sporophyte of Riccia is highly reduced but structurally independent… (C) The sporophyte of Riccia is highly reduced — not differentiated into foot, seta and capsule; in Marchantia foot, seta and capsule are differentiated (D) Both are the same
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)** — The sporophyte of Riccia is only a capsule.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q92 — How are heterospory and gametophyte reduction seen in Selaginella and Equisetum?
> (A) Both heterosporous… (B) Selaginella heterosporous, endosporic gametophyte; Equisetum homosporous, relatively independent gametophyte (C) The reverse (D) Selaginella has no sporophyte…
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — The spores of Equisetum are alike in form (with elaters), even though the gametophytes may be functionally unisexual.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q94 — Two families with bilabiate, zygomorphic flowers — which feature separates Acanthaceae from Verbenaceae?
> (A) Retinaculum in the fruit of Acanthaceae — explosive seed dispersal; absent in Verbenaceae (B) Inferior ovary in Acanthaceae… (C) No bilabiate corolla in Acanthaceae… (D) Apocarpous in Acanthaceae…
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — Both have a superior, syncarpous ovary; the retinaculum (jaculator) is the hallmark of Acanthaceae.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q104 — Legume nodules need low O₂ for N₂ fixation despite active respiration. How is this balanced?
> (A) By suppressing respiration… (B) Leghaemoglobin buffers O₂ — low O₂ for nitrogenase, yet high respiration possible (C) Direct diffusion of N₂ into mitochondria… (D) O₂ consumption by storage of fixed nitrogen…
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)**

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q109 — The feature that most reliably separates Cruciferae (Brassicaceae) from Capparidaceae?
> (A) Tetradynamous stamens in Cruciferae — four long, two short (B) No petals in Cruciferae… (C) Cruciferae superior, Capparidaceae inferior ovary (D) Unisexual flowers in Cruciferae…
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — Capparidaceae usually has many stamens and a gynophore/gynandrophore; the ovary is superior in both.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q110 — Assertion (A): In many pteridophytes the transition from protostele to more complex steles such as siphonostele is linked with structural complexity and leaf evolution. Reason (R): The formation of leaf gaps in the vascular cylinder is linked with the origin of megaphyllous leaves and contributes to the siphonostelic condition.
> (A) Both true, R the correct explanation (B) A true, R false (C) Both true, R not the explanation (D) A false, R true
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — Here the "R correct explanation" option is at **(A)** (in Q67 it was at (D)).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q111 — In Fucus meiosis occurs at gamete formation; in Ectocarpus in a separate sporangial stage. Consequence for the life cycle?
> (A) Fucus has no independent haploid stage; Ectocarpus has both haploid and diploid independent generations (B) Ectocarpus has no sporophyte… (C) Fucus isomorphic, Ectocarpus diploid-dominant… (D) Both the same
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — Fucus = diplontic; Ectocarpus = isomorphic diplohaplontic.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q120 — Statements on soil: (I) Laterite soil is rich in iron and aluminium oxides but poor in N, P and humus (II) The **B-horizon** is the main zone of organic accumulation and biological activity (III) Saline-alkaline soil is reclaimed by drainage and gypsum (IV) Alluvial soil is usually fertile
> (A) II and III (B) All (C) I, III and IV (D) I and II
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)** — Organic accumulation is in the O/A horizon; the B-horizon is the zone of illuviation.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q122 — Statements on gymnosperms: (I) Mainly temperate, dominant in coniferous forests (II) Vessels in the xylem of **all** gymnosperms (III) Classified into Cycadopsida, Coniferopsida, Gnetopsida, Ginkgopsida (IV) Xerophytic features such as needle-like leaves, thick cuticle
> (A) III and IV (B) I, III and IV (C) II and III (D) I and II
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Vessels occur only in Gnetales (Gnetum, Ephedra, Welwitschia); the statement with "all" is wrong.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q123 — A field shows widespread mosaic, leaf curling and stunting; the disease is spreading fast despite fungicide. Most effective management?
> (A) Increase nitrogen fertiliser (B) Remove infected plants and control insect vectors (C) Broad-spectrum fungicide repeatedly (D) Deep ploughing
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — The symptoms are of a viral disease; fungicide is ineffective.

### Three conclusions

1. **Botany I (cryptogams, gymnosperms, families, microbes, economic)** alone can give ~35 questions — it deserves the most time.
2. **Animal type studies + chordates** (~15) and **animal physiology** (~7) are the second big part.
3. The "outside" topics (biostatistics, toxicology, behaviour, wildlife) give ~8 questions — one chapter, two days, almost certain marks.

## Traps and exceptions

- In print, Q1–63 look like "Zoology", but among them 7, 11, 13, 15, 41, 48, 54, 63 are general ecology/classification.
- **The option order of A–R changed in every question** — "both true, R the correct explanation" was (B) in Q26/46/83, (A) in 57/110, (D) in 67/74.
- In Q74, A (Chlamydomonas) and R (Volvox) are about **different organisms** — R cannot explain A at all.
- Faulty questions like Q81 (Scytonema "true branching") can come — choose the answer from the remaining certain matches.
- This paper had no GK — your paper will have 30 GK questions.
