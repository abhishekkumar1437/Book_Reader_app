In the UP PGT 2026 paper the facts were often ordinary B.Sc. facts, but the **way of asking** was UPSC-like. This chapter has one or two actual examples of each style and a **fixed step-by-step method** — so that in the exam you do not have to think on every question about "where do I begin".

> **In simple words:** Every style has one mantra — **first find what is surely wrong**. The wrong statement in statement questions, the sure pair in matching, the separate truth of A and R in A–R, the first/last step in sequences, and words like "only/always/without any" in long options. If two options go, answering pays (EV = +1).

## 1. Distribution of styles (2026, 125 questions)

| Style | In 2026 | Estimate in 90-question pattern | Average time |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-line fact | ~60 | ~43 | 30–40 seconds |
| Long balanced options (scenario/integrated/comparative) | ~33 | ~24 | 1.5 minutes |
| Statement combination | 13 | ~9 | 1.5–2 minutes |
| List matching | 8 | ~6 | 1–1.5 minutes |
| Assertion–Reason | 7 | ~5 | 1 minute |
| Sequence arrangement | 4 | ~3 | 1 minute |

## 2. Long, balanced options

This is the most distinctive style of 2026 — the question gives a scenario ("a desert vertebrate…", "a patient…", "rapid ascent to altitude…") and the four options are four complete "explanations".

### Five signals for spotting a wrong option

| Signal | Example words | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute words** | only, always, exclusively, under any condition, without any | Almost every rule in biology has exceptions |
| **Claim of independence** | independent of, ATP-independent, nerve-independent | Biological processes are regulated |
| **Swapped words** | ammonia ↔ uric acid; central ↔ peripheral chemoreceptors; auxin ↔ cytokinin | The examiner places the "partner" of the right word |
| **Reversed relation** | "water potential rises → water enters" | Direction reversed |
| **One part of a two-part option wrong** | "X is right, whereas Y…" | Both parts must be true |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q62 — Rapid ascent to altitude causes hyperventilation and O₂ transport changes. Which explanation links the regulation of breathing with gas transport?
> (A) Central chemoreceptors directly detect low O₂… CO₂ transport unchanged (B) Carotid and aortic chemoreceptors detect low O₂ pressure and increase breathing; the reduced CO₂ changes pH and the affinity of haemoglobin (C) Peripheral chemoreceptors detect **increased** O₂ and suppress breathing… (D) Altitude does not affect the regulation of breathing…
>
> Method: (D) "does not affect" — eliminate. (C) "increased O₂" — O₂ falls at altitude — eliminate. (A) Central chemoreceptors mainly detect CO₂/H⁺, not O₂ (swap) — eliminate.
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)**

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q53 — Uromastix is a herbivorous reptile of dry habitats. Which combination explains its diet and water conservation?
> (A) Short intestine, absence of microbial flora… (B) Pointed teeth like carnivores… dilute urine (C) Highly acidic stomach, short intestine… undeveloped kidneys (D) Long intestine, a developed caecum for cellulose digestion, and efficient kidneys producing concentrated urine
> Method: herbivore → long intestine; dry habitat → concentrated excretion. (B) "dilute urine" is contradictory.
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (D)**

> **Exam angle:** Long-option questions **look hard but are not**. In 2026, in most of them two options went on the first reading. Instead of skipping them in round 1, **cut two options in 15 seconds** — if two are cut, answer.

## 3. Statement combination

Format: 3–4 statements, then "which combination is correct?" — (A) I, II, III (B) I and II (C) …

### Method

1. **Check the most doubtful statement first** — the one with an absolute word, "recently discovered", "all" or a number.
2. If it turns out wrong → **eliminate all options that contain it**.
3. Then take the surest true statement → eliminate the options that do not contain it.
4. Usually checking two statements gives the answer — do not spend time on all four.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q7 — Statements on artificial and natural classification: (I) An artificial system is based on a few easily seen characters and may place unrelated organisms together (II) A natural system uses many characters and tries to show overall similarity and evolutionary relationships (III) Because of its simplicity, the artificial system is **superior in showing phylogenetic relationships** (IV) Natural systems are more stable because they use a wide range of morphological, anatomical and genetic characters
> (A) I, II, III, IV (B) II, III, IV (C) I and III (D) I, II and IV
> Method: (III) is clearly wrong → (A), (B), (C) all contain III → all three eliminated.
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (D)** — answer found by checking one statement.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q3 — Statements on blood, blood pressure and blood volume: (I) Mean arterial pressure (MAP) depends on cardiac output and total peripheral resistance (II) An increase in blood volume increases venous return, which under normal conditions can raise cardiac output and arterial pressure (III) Plasma proteins contribute to maintaining blood volume by creating oncotic pressure
> (A) I, II and III (B) I and II (C) I and III (D) II and III
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — MAP = CO × TPR; Frank-Starling; oncotic pressure of albumin. Note: **"all correct" is also an answer** — do not assume "some statement must be wrong".

> **Common mistake:** Insisting on finding a wrong statement in every statement question. In 2026, all statements were probably correct in Q3, 30, 37 and 101. General signal: if a statement is not absolute and is balanced like a textbook ("may", "can", "contribute to"), it is usually true.

### Common "trap words" in statements (from 2026)

| Question | Part of the wrong statement |

## 4. Assertion–Reason

### Three-step method

1. Is **A alone** true or false? (without looking at R)
2. Is **R alone** true or false?
3. If both are true — is R **the reason for A**? (read it with "because": "A, because R")
4. Now **read the option letters carefully** — the order changes in every question.

| Situation | Which letter it was at in 2026 |
|---|---|
| Both true, R the correct explanation | Q26, 46, 83 → **(B)**; Q57, 110 → **(A)**; Q67, 74 → **(D)** |

> **Remember:** In A–R, "(A)" has two meanings — **Assertion (A)** and **option (A)**. Re-read the full sentence of the option before filling the circle on the answer sheet.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q26 — Assertion (A): Tapeworms have no mouth and no digestive cavity. Reason (R): They absorb nutrients from the host directly through the body surface.
> (A) A true, R false (B) Both true, R the correct explanation (C) Both true, R not the explanation (D) A false, R true
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Parasitic adaptation: digested food is available, so the digestive system is reduced.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q57 — Assertion (A): In Scoliodon, swimming propulsion is mainly by lateral undulations of the body, not by active flapping of the paired fins. Reason (R): Myotome muscles arranged in segmental blocks contract alternately on the two sides to produce a wave travelling from head to tail.
> (A) Both true, R the correct explanation (B) A false, R true (C) A true, R false (D) Both true, R not the explanation
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (A)** — here the same situation is at **(A)**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q74 — Assertion (A): The life cycle of Chlamydomonas is mainly haplontic, the zygote being the only diploid stage. Reason (R): Volvox has both asexual and sexual reproduction, and zygotic meiosis restores the haploid stage after fertilisation.
> (A) A true, R false (B) Both true, R not the explanation (C) A false, R true (D) Both true, R the correct explanation
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Both are true, but R talks about **another organism** (Volvox); it cannot explain A.

> **Common mistake:** "R is also true and the topic is the same, so it must be the explanation." No — apply the "because" test. Different organism, different level (structure vs evolution), or merely a "co-occurring" fact — then it is not the explanation.

## 5. Match the Following

### Method

1. Start with the **most specific** entry of List-II (e.g. "parasitic barnacle" = Sacculina).
2. Eliminate the options that do not contain that pair.
3. Take a second sure pair — usually only one option remains after two pairs.
4. Confirm the remaining option with the other pairs (if time permits).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q36 — Match: (a) Nauplius (b) Zoea (c) Mysis (d) Sacculina / (I) Parasitic barnacle that alters the physiology and reproduction of the host (II) Free-swimming larva with three pairs of appendages and a median eye (III) Larva resembling an adult prawn, with developed thoracic appendages (IV) Planktonic larva with a spiny carapace and segmented abdomen
> (A) a-II, b-III, c-IV, d-I (B) a-III, b-IV, c-II, d-I (C) a-II, b-IV, c-III, d-I (D) a-IV, b-II, c-III, d-I
> Method: d-I is in all — not useful. a-II (nauplius: 3 pairs of appendages, median eye) → (B), (D) eliminated. b-IV (zoea: spiny carapace) → (A) eliminated.
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)**

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q38 — Match: (a) Enterokinase (b) Bile salts (c) Lacteals of intestinal villi (d) Trypsin / (I) Proteolysis of polypeptides in the small intestine (II) Emulsification of fats (III) Absorption of chylomicrons into lymph (IV) Activation of trypsinogen
> (A) a-IV, b-II, c-I, d-III (B) a-IV, b-II, c-III, d-I (C) a-II, b-I, c-III, d-IV (D) a-I, b-III, c-II, d-IV
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)**

> **Exam angle:** In the 2026 matching questions, List-II was **not one word but a full description** ("three pairs of appendages and a median eye"). So memorise tables in the form "name — defining feature", not just "name — name".

## 6. Sequence

### Method

1. Identify the **first step** → eliminate options whose first step differs.
2. Identify the **last step** → eliminate more.
3. In the two remaining options, only **one middle pair** will differ — think only about that.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q113 — In Funaria the spore germinates on a moist surface and first forms a filamentous structure, on which buds form leafy plants; sex organs form on these; after fertilisation a diploid structure remains attached to the leafy plant and produces spores by meiosis. Correct sequence?
> (A) Spore → leafy gametophyte → protonema → sporophyte → gametes (B) Spore → sporophyte → protonema → gametophyte → spore (C) Spore → protonema → gametophyte → sporophyte → spore (D) Gametophyte → spore → protonema → sporophyte → zygote
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)**

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q116 — In bright sunlight and moderate humidity, guard cells actively accumulate K⁺, Cl⁻ and malate. Likely sequence of events?
> (A) Solute concentration falls → stomata open → water loss rises (B) Water potential rises → water leaves → stomata close → CO₂ entry falls (C) Water potential falls → water enters → stomata open → CO₂ diffusion rises (D) Turgor rises → transpiration falls → cooling falls
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (C)** — Solutes rise → Ψ falls → water in → turgor → stomata open.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Q75 — Correct order of appearance of plant groups in geological time?
> (A) Pteridophytes → bryophytes → gymnosperms → angiosperms (B) Bryophytes → pteridophytes → gymnosperms → angiosperms (C) Bryophytes → gymnosperms → pteridophytes → angiosperms (D) Gymnosperms → pteridophytes → bryophytes → angiosperms
> **Reasoned answer (official key not available): (B)** — Traditional/textbook order (bryophyte spores come first in the fossil record).

> **Common mistake:** In a sequence question, the "most logical" order may not match any option. In Q1 (migratory birds: fat storage → metabolic adjustment → start of flight → use of stored lipids) the logical order II → IV → III → I does not appear in any option; in such a case, eliminate as many options as the **first-last step method** allows, and if two do not go, leave it.

## 7. One-line facts

In 2026 about half the questions were like this — "Black pepper is obtained from: (A) dried fruit…", "Which feature in Psilotum…", "The main function of hCG…". These are **quick marks**.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| 30–40 seconds, answer on the first reading | Spend 2 minutes wondering "is there a trap?" |
| Pay attention to meaning — "Irish moss" is not a moss but a red alga (*Chondrus*) | Trust the common name |
| Compare the Hindi and English versions when one version is unclear | Read only one version |

> **Remember:** The question paper is bilingual. The Hindi rendering of technical terms (such as "retinaculum", "parapodia", "transversion") is sometimes unfamiliar — **read the English version**. If the two versions differ, check the instructions printed on the cover of the question booklet to see which one is valid.

## 8. Style-wise decision table under +3/−1

| Situation | Decision |
|---|---|
| In a statement question, one statement is surely wrong and it removes 2+ options | Answer |
| In A–R, the truth of A and R is sure, only the "explanation" is doubtful | Answer (usually 2 options remain → EV +1) |
| In matching, not a single sure pair | Leave |
| In a sequence, the first or last step is sure | Usually 2 options go → answer |
| Long options — absolute words in two | Answer |
| One-line fact, topic itself unfamiliar | Leave |

## 9. How to practise

1. Solve the **practice questions** at the end of every chapter, then solve the "PYQ" given in the chapter without looking at the answer.
2. Every week, **make 10 statement questions yourself** — learn to make a wrong statement by adding "only/all/never" to a correct fact. One who thinks like the examiner spots the trap.
3. When writing A–R answers, write the **situation**, not the letter ("both true, explanation") — then match it with the options.
4. In [Mock Test 1](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/abhyas/mock-test-1) and [Mock Test 2](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/abhyas/mock-test-2), write the style next to each wrong answer — the style with the most mistakes is next week's practice.

## Traps and exceptions

- The "correct explanation" option in A–R is sometimes (A), sometimes (B), sometimes (D) — read it every time.
- In statement questions, "all correct" can also be the answer.
- A and R about different organisms → not the explanation.
- In matching, do not start with a pair common to all options (e.g. d-I in Q36) — it eliminates no option.
- Errors in the paper are possible (Q81) — choose the answer from the remaining sure information.
- "Irish moss", "Reindeer moss", "Spanish moss" — none of these is a true moss (red alga, lichen, angiosperm respectively).

## Quick revision

1. Mantra — first find the surely wrong.
2. Long options — only/always/exclusively/independent of/without any.
3. Statements — most doubtful statement first; eliminate the options containing it.
4. "All correct" is also possible.
5. A–R — A separately, R separately, then the "because" test.
6. The A–R option order changes.
7. Different organism → not the explanation.
8. Matching — start with the most specific pair.
9. List-II in matching is descriptive — memorise "name-feature".
10. Sequence — first and last step.
11. One-liners — 30–40 seconds.
12. Unclear Hindi → read the English version.
13. Two options eliminated → answer (EV +1).
14. Nawaschin — double fertilisation 1898.
15. Irish moss = *Chondrus* (red alga).

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. In a statement question, "All plant cells have chloroplasts" — true/false? — **False** ("all" — root cells are an exception).
2. A: The whale is a mammal. R: The whale lives in water. — **Both true, R not the explanation**.
3. A: Fungi are heterotrophic. R: Fungi lack chlorophyll. — **Both true, R the correct explanation**.
4. In a matching question, which pair is useless to start with? — **The one common to all options**.
5. What is "Spanish moss"? — **An angiosperm** (*Tillandsia*, Bromeliaceae).
6. What is "Reindeer moss"? — **A lichen** (*Cladonia*).
7. In a sequence question, how many options usually go by the first-last step method? — **Two or three**.
8. "Double fertilization was recently discovered by molecular techniques" — what is wrong? — It was discovered **by Nawaschin in 1898**.
9. In A–R, if A is false, how many options remain? — **One** ("A false, R true" — in the standard four-option format).
10. "Central chemoreceptors directly detect reduced oxygen" — what is wrong? — Central chemoreceptors mainly detect **CO₂/H⁺**.
11. "Equal auxin and cytokinin **always** lead to direct embryogenesis" — signal? — **Absolute "always"** — wrong.
12. How to handle a long-option question in round 1? — **Cut two options in 15 seconds**; if they go, answer, otherwise "?".
13. In which style of question did "Leghaemoglobin" come? — **Direct, long options** (Q104).
14. In A–R, both true but the relation doubtful — answer or leave? — **Answer** (two options left, EV +1).
15. Identification of the zoea larva? — **Spiny carapace and segmented abdomen**.
