The official syllabus looks short — two sections, 18 units, a few lines. But behind every line hides a whole B.Sc. book. In this chapter every line is opened up into **"what to study, to what depth, and where in this collection"**, and matched against the 9 May 2026 paper.

> **In simple words:** Syllabus = the whole of NCERT 11–12 Biology **+** B.Sc. type studies, families, physiology and ecology. The 2026 paper also asked some "outside" topics — **biostatistics, toxicology, behaviour, wildlife conservation, palaeobotany** and **other plant families** beyond the five in the syllabus. These are also included in this collection.

## Level codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **N** | NCERT 11–12 is enough |
| **N+** | NCERT + some B.Sc. facts/examples |
| **B** | B.Sc. core — type studies, life cycles, comparative features |
| **M** | B.Sc. to M.Sc. — conceptual depth (stelar evolution, epigenetics, statistical tests) |

Level split in 2026: N 16, N+ 31, **B 67**, M 11 (out of 125) — i.e. **the base level of the paper is B.Sc.**

## Priority map

| Priority | Units | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A (first, deepest)** | Plant diversity (algae → gymnosperms, families), non-chordate types + larvae, chordates + anatomy, ecology-soil, animal physiology | Heavy weight in 2026 + B.Sc. level |
| **B (complete, but at NCERT+ depth)** | Microbes-lichens, economic botany, evolution, morphology-embryology, biostatistics/toxicology/behaviour/wildlife | Medium weight, easy marks |
| **C (do not skip — NCERT solid)** | Plant physiology, cell, molecular biology, Mendel, biotechnology, immunity, plant pathology, tissues | Few in 2026, but clearly in the syllabus; the next paper may take a different sample |

> **Common mistake:** "No genetics questions came in 2026, so skip it." One paper is one sample. Priority C means **less time**, not zero time — in the [45-day plan](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/yojana/yojana-kaise-chalayen) these get one day each.

## Traps and exceptions

- Only **five** families are named in the syllabus, but **at least eight other** families were asked in 2026 — Rosaceae, Fabaceae, Cucurbitaceae, Acanthaceae, Verbenaceae, Capparidaceae, Euphorbiaceae, Poaceae.
- "Liliaceae" — in the new APG classification this family has been made much smaller (onion in Amaryllidaceae, asparagus in Asparagaceae). In the exam the traditional NCERT/Bentham-Hooker Liliaceae is expected; see the difference in [Plant taxonomy and families](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/vanaspati-1/padap-vargikee-aur-kul).
- The syllabus does not name "Scoliodon", but it comes under Unit 3 (aquatic adaptations of fishes) — Q57 of 2026.
- Ecology is in Section A (Botany), but in 2026 several of its questions were printed among the Zoology section (Q1–63) — the printing order is not the syllabus division.
- "Human reproductive system" is in the embryology unit — read NCERT 12 "Human Reproduction" fully.

## Quick revision

1. Subject code — 06; Section A Botany (7 units), Section B Zoology (11 units).
2. Base level of 2026 — B.Sc. (67 of 125 questions).
3. Heaviest unit — plant diversity (32/125).
4. Named families — Brassicaceae, Asteraceae, Malvaceae, Liliaceae, Solanaceae.
5. Named non-chordate types — Amoeba, Sycon, Hydra, Ascaris, Cockroach, Palaemon, Pila, starfish.
6. Named chordate anatomy — frog, Uromastyx, pigeon, rabbit.
7. Outside the syllabus but asked — biostatistics (4), toxicology (2), behaviour (3), wildlife (3), palaeobotany (3).
8. Additional families — 6 questions.
9. Plant physiology — 3 in 2026, but 7 lines in the syllabus.
10. Economic botany — in all three of 2010, 2013, 2026.
11. Bacterial genetics — Q42 (transformation/conjugation).
12. Soil — Q115, 120.
13. Priority A — plant diversity, animal types, chordates, ecology, animal physiology.
14. Meaning of priority C — less time, not zero.
15. NCERT directly covers about 35–40% of the questions.
