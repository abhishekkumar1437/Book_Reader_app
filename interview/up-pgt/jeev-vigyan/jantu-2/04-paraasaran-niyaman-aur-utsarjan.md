This chapter starts from NCERT Class 11 "Excretory Products and their Elimination" and goes up to B.Sc./M.Sc. points — osmoregulation in fishes, evolution of the kidney (pronephros → metanephros), the arithmetic of filtration pressure, the counter-current multiplier and the urea cycle. UP PGT 2026 asked an integrated question linking **uricotelism** of a desert vertebrate with muscle contraction, and the **metanephric kidney** came in a comparative-anatomy matching question — so you must understand the reason behind "which animal, which waste, and why".

> **In simple words:** When proteins and nucleic acids break down, nitrogen comes out as **ammonia**, which is highly toxic. Animals that have plenty of water (aquatic animals) simply wash it away; on land water must be saved, so ammonia is converted into less toxic **urea** or almost insoluble **uric acid**. **Excretion** = removing nitrogenous wastes; **osmoregulation** = maintaining the balance of water and salts in the body. In humans both jobs are done mainly by the nephron of the kidney.

English chapter for more depth: [Excretion and Osmoregulation](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/excretion-and-osmoregulation).

## 1. Nitrogenous wastes

| Property | Ammonia (NH₃) | Urea | Uric acid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toxicity | **Highest** | Moderate | **Lowest** |
| Solubility in water | Very high | High | Almost insoluble |
| Water needed to remove 1 g nitrogen (approx.) | ~300–500 mL | ~50 mL | ~10 mL (or less) |
| Energy to make | None | More (urea cycle, liver) | Highest |
| Removal | By diffusion across body surface/gills (NH₄⁺) | Dissolved in urine | Semi-solid paste / pellet |
| Condition | **Ammonotelism** | **Ureotelism** | **Uricotelism** |
| Examples | Most bony fishes, aquatic amphibians and **tadpoles**, aquatic insects, most aquatic invertebrates (*Hydra*, *Palaemon*) | **Mammals**, adult (terrestrial) amphibians — **frog**, cartilaginous fishes (shark), some turtles | **Birds**, lizards and snakes, **insects**, land snails |

- **Guanotelic:** spiders — guanine.
- **Change during development:** tadpole (ammonotelic) → frog (ureotelic) — during metamorphosis the urea-cycle enzymes of the liver (especially carbamoyl phosphate synthetase) are induced by thyroxine.
- **Lungfish (*Protopterus*)** — ammonotelic in water, ureotelic during aestivation.
- **Embryos of birds and reptiles** — enclosed in a **cleidoic egg**, toxic waste cannot get out; hence uric acid (stored in the allantois). The mammalian embryo passes urea through the placenta into the mother's blood.
- Humans also make a little uric acid (from purine breakdown) — in excess, crystals form in joints → **gout**.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A desert vertebrate keeps water loss to a minimum during nitrogen excretion and shows rapid skeletal muscle contraction while running. Which explanation links both adaptations?
> (A) Ureotelism needs no water conservation, while sarcomeres shorten by Ca²⁺-independent contraction
> (B) Uricotelism removes nitrogen with minimal water, while ATP-driven actin–myosin sliding produces contraction
> (C) Ammonotelism reduces water loss, while muscle contraction depends on ATP-independent actin polymerisation
> (D) Urea is excreted in solid form, while contraction occurs by passive elastic recoil
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)** — uric acid is insoluble and is excreted with almost no water (desert lizards, birds). Calling ammonotelism water-saving in (C) and calling urea "solid" in (D) are factually wrong. The muscle part: [Locomotion and Movement](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/gati-aur-chalan).

## 2. Osmoregulation

- **Osmoconformers:** the osmolarity of body fluids changes to match the outside water — most marine invertebrates, **hagfish**.
- **Osmoregulators:** keep the inside constant whatever the outside — most vertebrates.
- **Euryhaline** — can tolerate a wide range of salinity (salmon, eel, tilapia); **stenohaline** — narrow range (most fishes).

| Animal | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Freshwater teleost** | Body hypertonic to the outside water → **water enters**, salts leave | Does **not drink** water; well-developed glomeruli, **large volume of dilute urine**; **chloride cells** of the gills actively **take up** salts |
| **Marine teleost** | Body hypotonic → **water leaves**, salts enter | **Drinks sea water**; chloride cells of the gills pump salts **out**; **little urine**, glomeruli small/few (some are **aglomerular** — toadfish, pipefish, sea horse) |
| **Cartilaginous fish (shark, ray)** | In the sea | Retains **urea** and **TMAO (trimethylamine oxide)** in the blood, becoming slightly **hypertonic** to sea water; does not drink; excess NaCl removed by the **rectal gland** |
| Marine birds and reptiles (turtles, marine iguana) | Excess salt from sea water/food | **Salt glands** — near the nasal/lacrimal glands |
| **Desert animals** (kangaroo rat, camel) | Shortage of water | **Metabolic water**; very long loops of Henle → highly concentrated urine; the kangaroo rat (*Dipodomys*) never drinks water; nocturnal and burrow-dwelling |
| *Amoeba*, *Paramecium* (freshwater) | Water enters | **Contractile vacuole** — mainly osmoregulation (not excretion; ammonia leaves by diffusion) |

> **Exam angle:** "Freshwater fish — does not drink, lots of dilute urine, takes salts in through gills"; "marine fish — drinks, little urine, pushes salts out through gills". **Migratory fishes:** the salmon is **anadromous** (moves from sea to river to spawn), the eel **catadromous** (from river to sea) — in both, the direction of gill cell transport reverses under the control of cortisol/prolactin (prolactin = freshwater adaptation).

### Types of kidney in vertebrates (evolutionary sequence)

| Kidney | Position | Functional in |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronephros** | Most anterior; most primitive | Embryos of all vertebrates; adult **hagfish** and larvae of some bony fishes; temporarily in amphibian larvae |
| **Mesonephros** | Middle | Adult **fishes and amphibians** (opisthonephros); temporary in amniote embryos — its duct (Wolffian duct) becomes the vas deferens in males |

## 4. Human excretory system

A pair of **kidneys**, a pair of **ureters**, a **urinary bladder** and a **urethra**.

- Kidneys — bean-shaped, dark red-brown; between the last thoracic (T12) and third lumbar (L3) vertebrae, close to the dorsal wall of the abdominal cavity (**retroperitoneal**); the right one slightly lower (because of the liver); each 10–12 cm long, 120–170 g.
- On the inner concave side is the **hilum** — renal artery, vein, ureter, nerves.
- Longitudinal section: outer **cortex**, inner **medulla** — conical **renal pyramids** (opening into calyces); extensions of the cortex between the pyramids — **columns of Bertini**; calyx → **renal pelvis** → ureter.

### Nephron — the structural and functional unit

~**10 lakh (1 million)** nephrons in each kidney.

| Part | Features |
|---|---|
| **Glomerulus** | Capillary tuft formed by the **afferent arteriole**; blood leaves through the **efferent arteriole**; afferent diameter is **larger** than efferent → high pressure |
| **Bowman's capsule** | Double-walled cup; cells of the inner wall are **podocytes** — with **filtration slits** between them |
| Renal corpuscle / **Malpighian body** | Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule (in the cortex) |
| **Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** | Cuboidal epithelium, **brush border** (microvilli) — maximum reabsorption |
| **Loop of Henle** | Descending and ascending limbs (thin and thick parts); in the medulla |
| **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)** | In the cortex; regulated reabsorption |
| **Collecting duct** | DCTs of many nephrons open into it; runs through the medulla and opens at the tip of the pyramid (papilla) |

- **Cortical nephrons (~85%)** — short loop of Henle, dipping only a little into the medulla; vasa recta absent or rudimentary.
- **Juxtamedullary nephrons (~15%)** — long loop, deep into the medulla; **vasa recta** from the efferent arteriole — important for concentrated urine.
- **Peritubular capillaries** — from the efferent arteriole, around the tubules.
- **Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA):** **JG cells** of the afferent arteriole (renin) + **macula densa** of the DCT (Na⁺/Cl⁻ sensor) + lacis cells.

## 5. Urine formation — three steps

### (1) Glomerular filtration (ultrafiltration)

Blood plasma (except proteins and formed elements) is filtered through three layers — capillary endothelium, basement membrane, podocyte filtration slits — into Bowman's capsule.

<div class="formula">

**Net filtration pressure** = glomerular hydrostatic pressure − (capsular hydrostatic pressure + plasma oncotic pressure)
≈ 55 − (15 + 30) = **~10 mm Hg**

**GFR (Glomerular Filtration Rate)** — in a healthy person **~125 mL/minute = ~180 L/day**; urine ~1.5 L/day → **~99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed**.

About 1100–1200 mL of blood flows through the kidneys per minute (~1/5 of cardiac output).

</div>

**Autoregulation of GFR:** macula densa → diameter of the afferent arteriole (**tubuloglomerular feedback**); when GFR falls, renin from the JG cells → RAAS.
Measuring GFR — **inulin clearance** (gold standard; inulin is filtered but neither reabsorbed nor secreted); in practice creatinine clearance.

### (2) Tubular reabsorption and (3) tubular secretion

| Part | Reabsorption | Secretion | Permeability to water |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PCT** | ~**65–70%** of the filtrate; **all glucose and amino acids** (Na⁺ co-transport), Na⁺ (active), K⁺, HCO₃⁻, water (obligatory) | H⁺, NH₃, drugs (penicillin), creatinine | Permeable (isotonic filtrate) |
| **Loop of Henle — descending limb** | **Water only** | — | **Permeable to water, almost impermeable to salts** → filtrate becomes concentrated |
| **Loop of Henle — ascending limb** | Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻ (active in the thick part — **Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ co-transporter, NKCC2**) | — | **Impermeable to water** → filtrate becomes dilute ("**diluting segment**") |
| **DCT** | Na⁺ (aldosterone-dependent), HCO₃⁻, some water (conditional) | **K⁺, H⁺, NH₃** — blood pH and K⁺ balance | Some, under ADH |
| **Collecting duct** | **Water (ADH-dependent — aquaporin-2)**, some urea into the medullary interstitium (for osmolarity) | H⁺, K⁺ | Permeable when ADH is present |

> **Remember:** **Renal threshold for glucose ~180 mg/dL** — above this the carriers are saturated → glucose in urine (**glycosuria**, diabetes mellitus). Healthy urine contains no glucose.

## 6. Counter-current mechanism — concentrating the urine

Mammals (and birds) can make **hypertonic** urine — in humans up to ~**1200 mOsm/L** (**about four times** plasma ~300).

1. In the two limbs of the loop of Henle the filtrate flows in **opposite directions**; in the vasa recta too, blood flows in opposite directions (counter-current).
2. The **ascending limb** actively pumps NaCl into the medullary interstitium but does not let water out.
3. Water leaves the **descending limb** by osmosis into the concentrated interstitium → the fluid in the tubule also becomes more and more concentrated.
4. This repetition builds an **osmotic gradient** in the medulla from the cortex (~300) to the inner medulla (~1200 mOsm/L) — **counter-current multiplier = loop of Henle**.
5. **Vasa recta = counter-current exchanger** — the blood flow does not wash away this gradient.
6. **Urea** leaving the collecting duct also contributes to the osmolarity of the inner medulla (**urea recycling**).
7. With ADH present, the collecting duct gives up water as it passes through this concentrated medulla → **concentrated urine**.

> **Exam angle:** Desert animals (kangaroo rat, camel) have the longest loops of Henle and medulla — the most concentrated urine (kangaroo rat up to ~5000 mOsm/L, about 17 times plasma). The beaver (water-rich habitat) has short loops. In birds only some nephrons have loops — maximum ~2 times; birds save water mainly through uric acid.

## 7. Hormonal regulation of kidney function

| Hormone | Source | Stimulus | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADH / vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone)** | Made in the hypothalamus (supraoptic nucleus), released from the **posterior pituitary** | Blood osmolarity ↑ (hypothalamic **osmoreceptors**), blood volume ↓ | **Aquaporin-2** in DCT and collecting duct → water reabsorption ↑ → less and concentrated urine; vasoconstriction |
| **Aldosterone** | Adrenal cortex (zona glomerulosa) | Angiotensin II, K⁺ ↑ | Na⁺ (and water) reabsorption ↑ in DCT/collecting duct, K⁺ secretion ↑ |
| **Renin → angiotensin II (RAAS)** | JG cells | GFR/blood pressure ↓ | Vasoconstriction (glomerular pressure ↑), aldosterone and ADH ↑ |
| **ANF / ANP** | Atria of the heart | Volume/blood pressure ↑ | Vasodilation, Na⁺ excretion — a **check** on RAAS |
| Parathormone | Parathyroid | Ca²⁺ ↓ | Ca²⁺ reabsorption ↑, phosphate excretion ↑ |

- ADH deficiency → **diabetes insipidus** — very large volume of dilute urine (polyuria), thirst; no glucose in urine (different from diabetes mellitus).
- **Alcohol** inhibits ADH secretion → more urine.
- Other hormones of the kidney — **erythropoietin** (RBC formation), **calcitriol** (active vitamin D, 1,25-(OH)₂-D₃), renin.

## 8. Urea cycle / ornithine cycle

Hans Krebs and Kurt Henseleit (1932) — in the **liver**; the first two steps in the **mitochondria**, the rest in the **cytoplasm**.

1. NH₃ + CO₂ + 2 ATP → **carbamoyl phosphate** (enzyme carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-I; mitochondria)
2. Carbamoyl phosphate + **ornithine** → **citrulline** (ornithine transcarbamoylase; mitochondria)
3. Citrulline + **aspartate** + ATP → **argininosuccinate** (cytoplasm)
4. Argininosuccinate → **arginine** + **fumarate** (fumarate links to the Krebs cycle — the "Krebs bicycle")
5. Arginine + H₂O —(**arginase**)→ **urea** + ornithine (cycle complete)

<div class="formula">

**Overall:** NH₃ + CO₂ + aspartate + 3 ATP → urea + fumarate (4 high-energy bonds used)

The **two nitrogens** of urea — one from free **NH₃**, the other from **aspartate**; the carbon — from CO₂.

</div>

> **Memory trick:** "**O**ld **C**ats **A**lways **A**void **U**rea" — **O**rnithine → **C**itrulline → **A**rgininosuccinate → **A**rginine → **U**rea (+ ornithine back).

## 9. Micturition, composition of urine and other excretory organs

- **Micturition:** when the bladder fills, stretch receptors → spinal cord (sacral region) → contraction of the **detrusor muscle** and relaxation of the internal sphincter — the **micturition reflex**; the external sphincter is voluntary (controlled by the brain).
- **Urine:** adult ~1–1.5 L/day; pale yellow (**urochrome** pigment); slightly acidic (pH ~6); ~**25–30 g urea** per day.
- **Abnormal constituents:** glucose (glycosuria), ketone bodies (**ketonuria** — diabetes, starvation), protein (**proteinuria** — kidney damage), blood (haematuria).
- **Other excretory organs:**
  - **Lungs** — CO₂ (~200 mL/minute), water vapour;
  - **Liver** — via bile: bilirubin-biliverdin, cholesterol, inactivated steroid hormones, drugs;
  - **Skin** — sweat (NaCl, a little urea, lactic acid; main function thermoregulation), **sebum** (sterols, hydrocarbons, waxes);
  - **Saliva** — a little nitrogenous waste.

## 10. Disorders of the excretory system

| Disorder | Description |
|---|---|
| **Uraemia** | Accumulation of urea in the blood due to kidney failure |
| **Haemodialysis** | "Artificial kidney" — arterial blood is cooled, **heparin** is added, and it is passed through semipermeable cellophane tubes; outside is the dialysing fluid (plasma-like, without nitrogenous wastes) → wastes diffuse out; then **antiheparin** is added and the blood returned to a vein |
| **Kidney transplantation** | Last resort; a close relative as donor — less rejection; immunosuppressive drugs |
| **Renal calculi** | Mainly **calcium oxalate**; also uric acid, phosphate |
| **Glomerulonephritis** | Inflammation of the glomerulus (often after a streptococcal infection, immune complexes) |
| Diabetes insipidus | ADH deficiency |

## Traps and exceptions

- **The adult frog is ureotelic**, but the tadpole is ammonotelic.
- **Sharks (cartilaginous fishes) are ureotelic** — they retain urea for osmotic balance; bony fishes are ammonotelic.
- **The main function of the contractile vacuole is osmoregulation**, not excretion; it is usually absent in marine protozoa.
- **The marine bony fish drinks water**; the freshwater fish does not.
- **Descending limb is permeable to water**, ascending limb impermeable — reversing them is the most common mistake.
- **The afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent** (that is why pressure in the glomerulus is high).
- **Loop of Henle = counter-current multiplier, vasa recta = counter-current exchanger** — do not swap them.
- **Maximum reabsorption is in the PCT** (not in the DCT or collecting duct).
- **ADH is released from the posterior pituitary** but is **made in the hypothalamus**.
- **In diabetes insipidus the urine is not sweet.**
- **Urea is made in the liver**, not in the kidney; the kidney only removes it.
- **Cortical nephrons are more numerous (~85%)**, but juxtamedullary nephrons are important for concentrated urine.
- **Metanephros only in adult amniotes** (reptiles, birds, mammals); mesonephros in fishes and amphibians.
- The urea cycle uses **3 ATP**, but **4 high-energy bonds** (one ATP → AMP + PPi).
