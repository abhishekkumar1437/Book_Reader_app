This chapter starts from NCERT Class 11 "Digestion and Absorption" and goes up to B.Sc./M.Sc. level points — histology of the gut wall, the cells of the glands, zymogen activation, gastrointestinal hormones, absorption carriers and hypothalamic regulation of appetite. UP PGT 2026 asked **one Match question** (enterokinase, bile salts, lacteals, trypsin) and **one case-based question** (regulation of appetite and gastric secretion) from this unit — that is, all three levels: "which cell, which secretion, which control".

> **In simple words:** Food is made of large molecules (starch, proteins, fats), but the body can absorb only small molecules (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids). Breaking the large into the small is **digestion**, carried out by **enzymes** — in the mouth, stomach and small intestine. These small molecules then pass through finger-like **villi** into the blood or lymph — this is **absorption**. For each part remember three things: which cell, which secretion, which product.

English chapter for more depth: [Digestion and Nutrition](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/digestion-and-nutrition).

## 1. Modes of Nutrition

| Mode | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Autotrophic | Making its own food from inorganic substances | Green plants, *Euglena* (in light) |
| Holozoic | Ingestion of solid food → digestion → absorption → assimilation → egestion | *Amoeba*, *Hydra*, humans |
| Saprozoic / Saprotrophic | Dissolved food from decaying organic matter, taken in through the body surface | Some protozoa, fungi |
| Parasitic | Food from a host | *Plasmodium*, tapeworm (*Taenia*), *Ascaris* |
| Mixotrophic | Autotrophic + heterotrophic | *Euglena* (saprozoic in the dark) |

**Five steps of holozoic nutrition:** Ingestion → Digestion → Absorption → Assimilation → Egestion (defecation).

By type of food — herbivores, carnivores, omnivores; microphagous feeders (e.g. filter feeders — *Sycon*, oysters, *Amphioxus*, blue whale) and macrophagous feeders; fluid feeders (mosquito, leech, butterfly).

**Intracellular vs extracellular digestion:** In protozoa and sponges digestion is **intracellular**, in the food vacuole; in *Hydra* it is both (begins extracellularly in the gastrovascular cavity, then intracellular); in vertebrates it is mainly **extracellular**. The food vacuole of protozoa is first acidic and then alkaline — details in [Zoology I](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-1/amoeba-sycon-hydra).

## 2. Nutrients and energy values

| Nutrient | Gross calorific value (bomb calorimeter) | Physiological value |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 4.1 kcal/g | 4.0 kcal/g |
| Protein | 5.65 kcal/g | 4.0 kcal/g |
| Fat | 9.45 kcal/g | 9.0 kcal/g |

> **Remember:** The gross value of protein (5.65) is much higher than its physiological value (4.0) because protein is not completely burnt in the body — its nitrogen leaves as **urea**, carrying energy with it. This is a favourite NCERT fact.

**Essential amino acids** — those the body cannot make: **9** in adults — histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine. Arginine is semi-essential in children.

> **Memory trick:** "PVT TIM HaLL" — Phe, Val, Thr, Trp, Ile, Met, His, Leu, Lys.

**Essential fatty acids:** linoleic acid (ω-6) and α-linolenic acid (ω-3); arachidonic acid can be made from linoleic acid, so it is semi-essential.

**Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):** energy expenditure at complete rest, awake, after 12–14 hours of fasting, at a comfortable temperature; adult male about 1500–1800 kcal/day; thyroxine is the main regulator; male > female, children > elderly (per unit surface area).

**Respiratory Quotient** — in Chapter 02.

### Protein–Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

| Feature | Kwashiorkor | Marasmus |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Mainly **protein** deficiency (calories roughly adequate) | Deficiency of both **protein + calories** |
| Age | 1–5 years (after weaning) | Usually under 1 year (infants) |
| Oedema | **Present** (low plasma protein → low oncotic pressure) | Absent |
| Subcutaneous fat | Some remains | Completely lost, "skin and bones" |
| Others | Swollen belly, fatty liver, loss of hair colour, dermatitis | Extreme wasting, old-man-like face |

## 3. Alimentary canal — structure

**Route:** Mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum → jejunum → ileum) → large intestine (caecum → colon → rectum) → anal canal → anus.

### Wall of the alimentary canal — four layers (outside to inside)

| Layer | Structure | Special |
|---|---|---|
| Serosa | Thin squamous epithelium (mesothelium) + connective tissue | Replaced by adventitia in the oesophagus |
| Muscularis externa | Outer longitudinal + inner circular smooth muscle; a third **oblique** layer in the stomach | Between them the **myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus** — motility |
| Submucosa | Loose connective tissue, blood and lymph vessels | **Meissner's (submucosal) plexus** — secretion; **Brunner's glands** in the duodenum |
| Mucosa | Epithelium + lamina propria + muscularis mucosae | Irregular folds (**rugae**) in the stomach, villi in the small intestine, goblet cells (mucus) |

> **Exam angle:** Auerbach = movement (Auerbach – Action of muscles), Meissner = secretion (Meissner – Mucosal secretion). Together they form the **enteric nervous system** — the "second brain".

### Teeth and dental formula

Human teeth are **thecodont** (set in sockets of the jaw), **diphyodont** (two sets — milk and permanent) and **heterodont** (four types — incisor, canine, premolar, molar). The hardest substance is **enamel** — from ectoderm (ameloblasts); dentine — from mesoderm (odontoblasts).

| Animal | Dental formula (upper/lower jaw, half) | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Human (adult) | 2123 / 2123 | 32 |
| Human (milk teeth) | 2102 / 2102 | 20 (no premolars) |
| Rabbit | 2033 / 1023 | 28 (no canines → **diastema**) |
| Dog | 3142 / 3143 | 42 |
| Cow | 0033 / 3133 | 32 (no upper incisors, dental pad) |

> **Common mistake:** The "wisdom tooth" is the third molar and erupts between 17–25 years; milk teeth have molars but no premolars.

**Tongue:** attached to the floor of the mouth by the frenulum; papillae — filiform (most numerous, without taste buds), fungiform, circumvallate (largest, in a V-shape at the back), foliate.

### Stomach

J-shaped; parts — cardiac, fundus, body, pyloric (including the antrum). The gastro-oesophageal (cardiac) sphincter lies between the oesophagus and stomach, and the **pyloric sphincter** between the stomach and duodenum.

| Cell of the gastric gland | Secretion |
|---|---|
| Chief / peptic / zymogen cells | Pepsinogen, prorennin (infants), gastric lipase |
| Parietal / oxyntic cells | **HCl** and **Castle's intrinsic factor** — for absorption of vitamin B₁₂ |
| Mucous neck cells | Mucus + HCO₃⁻ (protect the wall) |
| G-cells (antrum) | **Gastrin** hormone |
| ECL cells (enterochromaffin-like) | Histamine (raises HCl via H₂ receptors) |

> **B.Sc. point:** In the parietal cell the **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase (proton pump)** sends H⁺ into the lumen; carbonic anhydrase forms H₂CO₃; HCO₃⁻ goes into the blood — this is why blood becomes alkaline after a meal (the **alkaline tide**). Omeprazole is a proton-pump inhibitor; ranitidine is an H₂ blocker.

### Small intestine

The longest part (~6–7 m; less in the living state). Three structures that increase the surface area — **plicae circulares (circular folds)**, **villi**, **microvilli (brush border)**.

| Structure | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Villi | Whole small intestine (not in the stomach or large intestine) | Contain blood capillaries + a **lacteal** (lymph vessel) |
| Crypts of Lieberkühn | At the base of the villi | Intestinal juice; stem cells |
| **Paneth cells** | At the base of the crypts | Lysozyme, defensins (immunity) |
| **Brunner's glands** | Only in the submucosa of the **duodenum** | Alkaline mucus — protection from acid |
| **Peyer's patches** | Mainly the **ileum** | Lymphoid tissue (GALT), M-cells |

### Large intestine

The caecum and the **vermiform appendix** attached to it — vestigial in humans but containing lymphoid tissue; colon — ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid; rectum; anal canal. Functions: absorption of water, minerals and some drugs; secretion of mucus; synthesis of vitamin K and some B vitamins (biotin) by **bacteria** (*E. coli* etc.). The large intestine has no villi.

### Comparative: stomach of ruminants

Four chambers — **rumen** → **reticulum** → **omasum** → **abomasum (the true stomach — gastric juice is secreted here)**. In the rumen and reticulum, microbes (bacteria, ciliate protozoa, anaerobic fungi) use **cellulase** to convert cellulose into volatile fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate); a by-product is **methane** (a greenhouse gas). In the camel the omasum is rudimentary (functionally three chambers).

**Rabbit:** very large caecum (cellulose fermentation, *hindgut fermenter*); **coprophagy / caecotrophy** — eating soft faeces again to obtain vitamin B and protein. Details: [Frog, Uromastyx, Pigeon, Rabbit](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-1/medhak-uromastyx-kabootar-khargosh).

## 4. Digestive glands

### Salivary glands — three pairs

| Gland | Location | Duct |
|---|---|---|
| Parotid — the largest | In front of and below the ear | **Stensen's duct**; mumps is a viral infection of this gland |
| Submandibular / submaxillary | Below the lower jaw | **Wharton's duct** |
| Sublingual — the smallest | Below the tongue | **Ducts of Rivinus / Bartholin's duct** (several) |

Saliva: pH ≈ 6.8, ~1–1.5 L/day; water (~99%), Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻, mucus, **salivary amylase / ptyalin**, **lysozyme** (antibacterial). About **30% of starch** is converted to maltose in the mouth.

### Liver

The **largest gland** of the body (adult ~1.2–1.5 kg); covered by **Glisson's capsule**; units — **hepatic lobules**, hexagonal, with a central vein in the centre; **Kupffer cells** in the sinusoids — fixed macrophages. Bile is made by liver cells, stored and concentrated in the **gall bladder**; cystic duct + hepatic duct = **common bile duct**; this joins the pancreatic duct to form the **hepatopancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater)**, which opens into the duodenum through the **sphincter of Oddi**.

**Bile:** ~0.5–1 L/day, alkaline; **no digestive enzyme**; bile salts (sodium glycocholate, taurocholate) — **emulsification** of fats and micelle formation, giving lipase an active surface; bile pigments — **bilirubin** (yellow) and **biliverdin** (green), from the breakdown of haemoglobin. About 95% of bile salts are reabsorbed from the ileum and return to the liver — **enterohepatic circulation**.

**Other functions of the liver:** glycogen storage (glycogenesis, glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis), **urea cycle** (ornithine cycle), plasma proteins (albumin, fibrinogen, prothrombin), heparin, detoxification, storage of vitamins A, D, B₁₂ and iron, RBC formation in the foetus.

### Pancreas — a mixed gland

- **Exocrine part (~99%):** acini — pancreatic juice (pH ~7.8–8.4, ~1.2–1.5 L/day); duct cells add HCO₃⁻.
- **Endocrine part (~1–2%): Islets of Langerhans** — α-cells: glucagon; β-cells (~60–70%): insulin; δ-cells: somatostatin; PP/F-cells: pancreatic polypeptide. (Details in Chapter 07)

## 5. Digestion — enzyme table (most important)

| Site / juice | Enzyme | Substrate → product | pH / activation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mouth (saliva) | Salivary amylase (ptyalin) | Starch → maltose (+ isomaltose, α-dextrins) | 6.8; Cl⁻ is the activator |
| Stomach | **Pepsin** | Proteins → proteoses and peptones (peptides) | 1.5–2; pepsinogen activated by **HCl**, then by **autocatalysis** |
| Stomach (infants) | **Rennin (chymosin)** | Casein (soluble) → paracasein (curd with Ca²⁺) | Major in calves; negligible in humans |
| Stomach | Gastric lipase | Fats → fatty acids + glycerol (a little) | — |
| Pancreatic juice | **Trypsin** | Proteins/peptones → dipeptides (cuts after Lys/Arg) | Trypsinogen → trypsin by **enterokinase** |
| Pancreatic juice | Chymotrypsin | Proteins → peptides (after aromatic amino acids) | Chymotrypsinogen → by trypsin |
| Pancreatic juice | Carboxypeptidase | Amino acids from the C-terminal of peptides | Procarboxypeptidase → by trypsin |
| Pancreatic juice | Elastase | Elastin | Proelastase → by trypsin |
| Pancreatic juice | Pancreatic amylase (amylopsin) | Remaining polysaccharides → disaccharides | ~7–8 |
| Pancreatic juice | **Pancreatic lipase (steapsin)** | Triglycerides → 2-monoglycerides + fatty acids | Needs bile salts + **colipase** |
| Pancreatic juice | Nucleases (DNase, RNase) | Nucleic acids → nucleotides | — |
| Intestinal juice / brush border | Maltase, isomaltase, sucrase, lactase | Disaccharides → monosaccharides | — |
| Intestinal juice / brush border | Dipeptidases, aminopeptidases | Peptides → amino acids | — |
| Intestinal juice | Nucleotidases → nucleosidases | Nucleotides → nucleosides → sugar + bases | — |
| Brush border | **Enterokinase / enteropeptidase** | Trypsinogen → trypsin | (not itself digestive; an activator) |

> **Remember:** Sucrose → glucose + fructose; lactose → glucose + galactose; maltose → glucose + glucose. Humans lack cellulase, the enzyme that digests cellulose.

## 6. Gastrointestinal hormones

| Hormone | Source cell / site | Stimulus | Main action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | G-cells, gastric antrum | Peptides, distension of the stomach, vagus | HCl and pepsinogen secretion ↑, gastric motility ↑ |
| **Secretin** — the **first hormone** discovered (Bayliss and Starling, 1902) | S-cells, duodenum | Acidic chyme (pH < 4.5) | **HCO₃⁻-rich** watery juice from the pancreas; gastric secretion ↓ |
| **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** (= pancreozymin) | I-cells, duodenum-jejunum | Fatty acids, amino acids | **Gall bladder contraction**, relaxes the sphincter of Oddi, **enzyme-rich** pancreatic juice; satiety |
| **GIP** (Gastric inhibitory peptide / Glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide) | K-cells, duodenum | Glucose, fat | Gastric secretion and motility ↓, **insulin secretion ↑** (incretin) |
| GLP-1 | L-cells, ileum-colon | Nutrients | Insulin ↑, satiety (semaglutide is its analogue) |
| Somatostatin | D-cells (stomach, pancreatic δ) | Acid | Almost all GI secretions ↓ |
| Motilin | M-cells, duodenum | Fasting | Interdigestive migrating motor complex (MMC) |
| VIP | Enteric nerves | — | Relaxes smooth muscle, water secretion ↑ |
| **Ghrelin** | X/A-like cells of the stomach (fundus) | Empty stomach | **Hunger ↑** ("hunger hormone"), GH secretion ↑ |
| **Leptin** | Adipose tissue (adipocytes) | Fat stores ↑ | **Hunger ↓** (satiety), energy expenditure ↑ |

> **Memory trick:** **S**ecretin = **S**odium bicarbonate; **C**CK = **C**ontract gall bladder; **G**hrelin = **G**rowling stomach (hunger); **L**eptin = **L**ean (keeps you lean).

### Phases of gastric secretion

| Phase | Stimulus | Contribution (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Cephalic | Sight, smell, taste, thought of food → **vagus nerve** | ~30% (Pavlov's "sham feeding" experiment) |
| Gastric | Distension of the stomach, peptides → gastrin | ~60% |
| Intestinal | Chyme in the duodenum → slight stimulation at first, then **inhibition** (enterogastrones — secretin, CCK, GIP) | ~10% |

### Regulation of appetite

- **Hypothalamus** — the lateral hypothalamus = **feeding centre**; its destruction causes refusal of food; the ventromedial nucleus = **satiety centre**; its destruction causes hyperphagia and obesity.
- **Arcuate nucleus:** NPY/AgRP neurons — appetite-increasing (orexigenic); POMC/CART neurons (α-MSH) — appetite-reducing (anorexigenic).
- Ghrelin stimulates NPY neurons; leptin and insulin stimulate POMC and suppress NPY; PYY, GLP-1 and CCK are short-term satiety signals (through vagal afferent fibres).
- The hypothalamus controls cephalic-phase secretion through the vagus (parasympathetic) — low appetite means less vagal stimulation, less gastric acid and slower digestion.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A patient has reduced appetite, decreased gastric acid secretion and slow digestion after meals. Which explanation links appetite regulation to the control of digestive secretions?
> (A) Hormonal appetite control suppresses digestive secretion during meals until energy balance is restored
> (B) Digestive secretion starts only after intestinal absorption, independent of neural and hormonal control
> (C) Hypothalamic centres and hormones like ghrelin and leptin regulate digestive secretion by controlling vagal activity
> (D) Appetite depends only on stomach distension, while secretion runs independently at a constant basal rate
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)** — the appetite centres are in the hypothalamus; ghrelin/leptin signal to them, and the hypothalamus drives cephalic-phase secretion through the vagus. (B) and (D) are wrong because of extreme words like "only/independent".

## 7. Movements of the alimentary canal

- **Deglutition (swallowing):** voluntary (mouth) → involuntary (pharynx, oesophagus); swallowing centre in the medulla; the **epiglottis** closes the opening of the windpipe (glottis); the ball of food = **bolus**.
- **Peristalsis:** a wave of circular and longitudinal muscle contraction — pushes food forward (from oesophagus to rectum).
- **Segmentation:** local contraction of circular muscles in the small intestine — mixing.
- **Mass movement:** in the large intestine 1–3 times a day; the **gastrocolic reflex** after meals.
- Food stays in the stomach ~3–4 hours; the acidic semi-liquid leaving the stomach = **chyme**; when it becomes alkaline in the small intestine it is called **chyle** (fat-laden lymph is also called chyle).
- **Vomiting:** vomiting centre in the medulla; **defecation:** reflex on filling of the rectum; the external anal sphincter is voluntary (skeletal muscle), the internal one involuntary.

## 8. Absorption and assimilation

| Substance | Mechanism | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose, galactose | **Active transport — co-transport with Na⁺ (SGLT1)**; then into blood via GLUT2 | Blood → hepatic portal vein |
| Fructose | **Facilitated diffusion (GLUT5)** | Blood |
| Amino acids, di/tripeptides | Na⁺/H⁺ co-transport (active) | Blood |
| Fatty acids, monoglycerides | Simple diffusion from **micelles** into the cell → triglycerides resynthesised inside → **chylomicrons** with a protein coat | **Lacteal → lymph → thoracic duct** → blood |
| Short-chain fatty acids | Directly | Blood |
| Water | Osmosis (mostly in the small intestine; the rest in the large intestine) | — |
| Vitamin B₁₂ | With **intrinsic factor**, in the **ileum** | — |
| Iron, calcium | Active, in the **duodenum** (Ca²⁺ — dependent on vitamin D) | — |

**What is absorbed where:** mouth — some drugs (nitroglycerin, under the tongue); stomach — water, simple sugars, **alcohol**, aspirin; small intestine — most nutrients; large intestine — water, minerals, some drugs.

**Assimilation:** use of the absorbed substances by tissues — building cells, energy, storage (glycogen, fat).

## 9. Digestive disorders

| Disorder | Cause / symptoms |
|---|---|
| Jaundice | Bilirubin ↑ in blood (>2 mg/dL) — skin and eyes yellow; types: pre-hepatic (haemolytic), hepatic (hepatitis), post-hepatic (obstructive — gallstones) |
| Diarrhoea | Frequent watery stools, reduced absorption, dehydration — treated with ORS |
| Constipation | Irregular, hard stools |
| Indigestion | Enzyme deficiency, anxiety, overeating |
| Peptic ulcer | *Helicobacter pylori* (Marshall and Warren — Nobel 2005), NSAIDs, excess acid |
| Gallstones | Precipitation of cholesterol/pigment |
| Lactose intolerance | Lactase deficiency → bloating and diarrhoea from milk |
| Coeliac disease | Autoimmune reaction to gluten (gliadin), villi destroyed |
| Malnutrition | Kwashiorkor, marasmus (above) |

## Traps and exceptions

- **Bile has no enzyme** — yet it is essential for fat digestion (emulsification).
- **Enterokinase is not a digestive enzyme** but an activator; it comes from the brush border, not from the pancreas.
- **Secretin** is the first hormone discovered, not **insulin**; the word "hormone" was coined by Starling in 1905.
- **Villi are only in the small intestine**; the stomach has rugae, and the large intestine has neither villi nor circular folds.
- **Brunner's glands only in the duodenum**, **Peyer's patches mainly in the ileum** — swapping the two is a common mistake.
- **Fat goes into lymph, not blood** (chylomicrons); glucose and amino acids go first to the liver through the portal vein.
- **Fructose** by facilitated diffusion, **glucose/galactose** by Na⁺ co-transport — calling both "active" is wrong.
- **Rennin** is a digestive enzyme; **renin** is a kidney enzyme (blood pressure) — watch the spelling.
- **Intrinsic factor** comes from the parietal cell (not the chief cell); its deficiency causes **pernicious anaemia**.
- **Rabbits have no canines**, cows have no upper incisors.
- Kwashiorkor **has oedema**, marasmus does not.
- **Kupffer cells** in the liver, **Paneth cells** in the intestinal crypts, **oxyntic cells** in the stomach.
- No protein or fat digestion in the mouth (lingual lipase is negligible — some in infants).

2. Enzyme that activates trypsinogen? — **Enterokinase** (from the intestinal brush border).
3. Activator of pepsinogen? — **HCl** (then autocatalysis).

6. Where are Brunner's glands found? — In the **submucosa of the duodenum**.
7. What do lacteals absorb? — **Chylomicrons (fat)**, into lymph.
8. The first hormone discovered? — **Secretin** (Bayliss and Starling, 1902).
9. Hormone for contraction of the gall bladder? — **Cholecystokinin (CCK)**.
10. Which is called the "hunger hormone"? — **Ghrelin** (from the stomach).
11. Where is leptin made? — In **adipose tissue** (satiety signal).
12. Where is the satiety centre? — **Ventromedial nucleus of the hypothalamus**.
13. Physiological energy value of protein? — **4.0 kcal/g** (gross 5.65).
14. Which malnutrition shows oedema? — **Kwashiorkor** (low plasma protein).
15. By what method is glucose absorbed in the intestine? — **Secondary active co-transport with Na⁺ (SGLT1)**.
16. The true stomach of ruminants? — **Abomasum**.
17. Kupffer cells are found in which organ? — **Liver** (phagocytic cells).
18. Which phase of gastric secretion depends on the vagus? — **Cephalic phase**.
19. Secretion of Paneth cells? — **Lysozyme and defensins** (intestinal immunity).
20. Which vitamin is synthesised by bacteria of the large intestine? — **Vitamin K** (and biotin).
