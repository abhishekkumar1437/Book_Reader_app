This chapter starts from NCERT Class 11 "Body Fluids and Circulation" and goes up to B.Sc. (Guyton level) points — pressure-volume changes of the cardiac cycle, ECG waves, the Frank–Starling law, Starling forces at the capillary, mean arterial pressure, and short-term and long-term regulation of blood pressure. UP PGT 2026 asked **two questions** from this chapter: a **statement-combination** question on blood pressure and blood volume, and a mechanism question on the **baroreceptor reflex** — both at Guyton level.

> **In simple words:** The heart is a double pump — the right side sends impure (deoxygenated) blood to the lungs, the left side sends pure blood to the whole body. Blood goes out through arteries, exchanges materials in capillaries and returns through veins. Fluid that leaks out of capillaries becomes lymph and rejoins the blood. Blood pressure = how much blood the heart pushes out (cardiac output) × how much the vessels resist (peripheral resistance).

English chapter for more depth: [Blood, Circulation and Lymph](/interview/bpsc-tre/class-11-12-zoology/physiology/blood-circulation-and-lymph).

## 1. Types of circulatory system

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Blood leaves the vessels and flows through open spaces — the **haemocoel** / sinuses; low pressure | Arthropoda (cockroach, *Palaemon*), most Mollusca (*Pila*) |
| **Closed** | Blood always stays inside vessels; high pressure, controlled flow | Annelida (earthworm), **Cephalopoda** (octopus, *Sepia*), vertebrates |
| Water vascular system | Not blood; a water vascular system | Echinoderms (starfish — blood-lymph system rudimentary) |

**On the basis of origin of the heartbeat:**
- **Myogenic** — beat originates in special cells of the heart muscle (SA node); vertebrates, Mollusca.
- **Neurogenic** — beat originates in a nerve ganglion; many arthropods (*Limulus*, crustaceans), some annelids.

### Comparison of heart and circulation in vertebrates

| Class | Chambers | Circulation | Special |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pisces | **2** (1 atrium + 1 ventricle) + sinus venosus, conus/bulbus arteriosus | **Single** — only deoxygenated blood in the heart ("**venous heart**") | — |
| Amphibia | **3** (2 atria + 1 ventricle); sinus venosus well developed | **Incomplete double** — mixing in the ventricle (some separation by the spiral valve) | — |
| Reptilia | **3** (incomplete septum in the ventricle) | Incomplete double | **Crocodile has 4 chambers** (slight mixing through the foramen of Panizza) |
| Birds and mammals | **4** | **Complete double** | **Right** aortic arch in birds, **left** in mammals |

> **Remember:** The sinus venosus is well developed in fishes and amphibians, reduced in reptiles, and in birds and mammals it is incorporated into the wall of the right atrium as the **SA node**.

## 2. Blood — a fluid connective tissue

~5–5.5 L in an adult (~7–8% of body weight); pH ~7.4.

### Plasma — ~55%

90–92% water; **plasma proteins 6–8%** — **albumin** (most abundant; main source of osmotic/oncotic pressure, made in the liver), **globulins** (α, β — transport; γ — antibodies), **fibrinogen** (coagulation; liver). Also glucose, amino acids, lipids, salts (Na⁺ the most abundant cation, Cl⁻ the most abundant anion), hormones, urea.
**Serum** = plasma − clotting factors (fibrinogen etc.).

### Formed elements — ~45% (haematocrit / PCV)

| Cell | Count | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Red blood cell (RBC, erythrocyte)** | Male 5–5.5 million/mm³ (female 4.5–5) | **Enucleate** in mammals, biconcave; lifespan **120 days**; formed in **red bone marrow** in adults (liver, spleen in the embryo); **spleen = graveyard of RBCs**; Hb 12–16 g/100 mL; **erythropoietin** (kidney) stimulates production |
| **White blood cell (WBC, leucocyte)** | 6000–8000/mm³ | Nucleated, colourless; short-lived |
| **Platelets (thrombocytes)** | 1.5–3.5 lakh (150,000–350,000)/mm³ | Fragments of **megakaryocytes**; coagulation; deficiency → **thrombocytopenia** (dengue) |

| WBC | % (DLC) | Nucleus | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophil** | 60–65% (most) | 3–5 lobed | Phagocytosis — first line of defence; pus |
| **Eosinophil** | 2–3% | Bilobed | Increase in **allergy, worm infections** |
| **Basophil** | 0.5–1% (least) | S-shaped | **Histamine, heparin, serotonin** |
| **Lymphocyte** | 20–25% | Large, round | B and T cells — specific immunity |
| **Monocyte** | 6–8% | Kidney-shaped (largest WBC) | **Macrophages** in tissues |

> **Memory trick:** "**N**ever **L**et **M**onkeys **E**at **B**ananas" — in decreasing order N > L > M > E > B.

> **Common mistake:** RBCs of the camel and llama are **oval** but **enucleate** (mammals); RBCs of birds, reptiles, amphibians and fishes are oval and **nucleated**.

### Blood groups

**ABO system** — Karl Landsteiner (1900–01; Nobel 1930).

| Group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Can donate to | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | A | anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| B | B | anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| AB | A + B | None | AB only | All — **universal recipient** |
| O | None | anti-A + anti-B | All — **universal donor** | O only |

- Discovery of group AB — Decastello and Sturli (1902).
- **Bombay phenotype (hh)** — no H antigen at all; looks like "O" in ABO testing but can receive only Bombay blood (1952, Mumbai).
- **Rh system** — Landsteiner and Wiener (1940), from the rhesus monkey (*Macaca mulatta*); about 93–95% of Indians are Rh⁺. Rh⁻ mother and Rh⁺ foetus → **erythroblastosis fetalis** in the second pregnancy; prevention — **anti-D (RhoGAM)** given to the mother immediately after the first delivery (within 72 hours).

### Blood coagulation

Injury → **tissue factor / thromboplastin** from tissues (extrinsic pathway) and factors from platelets (intrinsic pathway) → **prothrombinase** (factor Xa) →

Prothrombin (factor II) —(Ca²⁺)→ **thrombin** → fibrinogen (I) → **fibrin** threads (stabilised by factor XIII) → RBCs trapped, forming a clot.

- **Ca²⁺ (factor IV)** is needed at almost every step; there is no factor **VI**.
- **Vitamin K** — needed in the liver to make factors **II, VII, IX, X** (remember "1972" — 10, 9, 7, 2).
- **Haemophilia A** — factor VIII; **haemophilia B (Christmas disease)** — factor IX; both X-linked recessive.
- **Anticoagulants:** heparin (mast cells/basophils; activates antithrombin III), **hirudin** (leech), dicoumarol/warfarin (vitamin K antagonists); in the test tube — sodium citrate, oxalate, EDTA (remove Ca²⁺).
- Normal clotting time 2–8 minutes; bleeding time 1–5 minutes.

## 3. Lymph and tissue fluid

At the arterial end of capillaries fluid moves out → **tissue / interstitial fluid**; most of it returns at the venous end; the rest (~10%, ~2–3 L/day) returns through **lymph vessels**.

### Starling forces — fluid exchange at the capillary

| Force | Direction | Approximate value |
|---|---|---|
| Capillary hydrostatic pressure | Outward | ~35 mm Hg at arterial end, ~15 mm Hg at venous end |
| Plasma **oncotic / colloid osmotic pressure** (mainly albumin) | Inward | ~25 mm Hg |

> **Exam angle:** Plasma proteins (especially albumin) hold fluid inside the vessels by oncotic pressure and so maintain **blood volume**. If albumin falls (liver disease, kwashiorkor, kidney disease — nephrotic syndrome), fluid collects in the tissues — **oedema**. Lymphatic blockage (filariasis — *Wuchereria bancrofti*) also causes oedema (elephantiasis).

**Lymph:** like plasma but with less protein; no RBCs or platelets; rich in **lymphocytes**; can clot. Flow — lymph capillaries → vessels (with valves) → **lymph nodes** → **thoracic duct** (into the left subclavian vein) and **right lymphatic duct** (into the right subclavian). Frogs have **lymph hearts**.
**Functions:** return of proteins and fluid, transport of fat (chylomicrons) (lacteals), immunity.

## 4. Human heart

- In the thoracic cavity between the two lungs, slightly tilted to the left; about the size of a fist (~250–300 g); of **mesodermal** origin; double membrane **pericardium**, with pericardial fluid in between.
- Four chambers — two thin-walled **atria** and two thick-walled **ventricles**; **the left ventricle has the thickest wall** (pumps to the whole body). Between the atria is the **interatrial septum** (in the embryo the **foramen ovale** — after birth the **fossa ovalis**), between the ventricles the interventricular septum; between atrium and ventricle the **atrioventricular septum**.

| Valve | Location | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Tricuspid** | Right atrium → right ventricle | 3 cusps |
| **Bicuspid / Mitral** | Left atrium → left ventricle | 2 cusps |
| **Semilunar** | Ventricles → pulmonary artery and aorta | 3 half-moon cusps |

AV valves are attached by **chordae tendineae** to the **papillary muscles** of the ventricles — they prevent the valves from turning inside out during ventricular contraction. Valves allow blood to flow in one direction only.

**Blood flow:** body → venae cavae → **right atrium** → tricuspid → **right ventricle** → pulmonary artery (the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood) → lungs → pulmonary veins (veins carrying oxygenated blood) → **left atrium** → bicuspid → **left ventricle** → aorta → body.

**Coronary circulation:** blood supply to the heart muscle itself — right and left **coronary arteries** from the base of the aorta; venous blood returns to the right atrium through the **coronary sinus**.

### Conducting system of the heart

| Structure | Location | Intrinsic rate (per minute) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sinoatrial node / SA node — "pacemaker"** | Right upper wall of the right atrium, near the opening of the superior vena cava | **70–75** (highest — hence it controls the rhythm) |
| **Atrioventricular node / AV node** | Lower left corner of the right atrium, near the interatrial septum | 40–60; **delay of ~0.1 s** in the impulse (lets the atria empty first) |
| **Bundle of His** → right and left branches | Interventricular septum | — |
| **Purkinje fibres** | In the ventricular muscle | 15–40; fastest conduction |

> **Remember:** Heart muscle generating its own impulse = **autorhythmicity**; the resting membrane of SA node cells slowly depolarises by itself (**pacemaker potential**, "funny" Na⁺ current, then Ca²⁺). If the SA node fails, an artificial pacemaker is used.

## 5. Cardiac cycle

At 72 beats/minute, one cycle = 60/72 = **0.8 s**.

| Phase | Duration | Events |
|---|---|---|
| **Atrial systole** | 0.1 s | Last ~**30%** filling of the ventricles (70% already filled passively) |
| **Ventricular systole** | 0.3 s | AV valves close (**first sound "lub"**) → **isovolumetric contraction** — all valves closed → pressure rises and the semilunar valves open → **ejection** |
| **Joint diastole** | 0.4 s | Semilunar valves close (**second sound "dub"**) → **isovolumetric relaxation** → AV valves open → rapid filling of the ventricles |

Total atrial diastole 0.7 s, total ventricular diastole 0.5 s. When the heart rate rises, **diastole** is shortened more.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| End-diastolic volume (EDV) | ~120–130 mL |
| End-systolic volume (ESV) | ~50–60 mL |
| **Stroke volume** = EDV − ESV | **~70 mL** |
| **Ejection fraction** = SV/EDV | ~55–65% |
| **Cardiac output** = SV × heart rate | 70 × 72 ≈ **5 L/minute** (much higher in athletes) |

**Heart sounds:** S1 "lub" — closure of the AV valves, long and low, start of ventricular systole; S2 "dub" — closure of the semilunar valves, short and sharp, start of ventricular diastole. Stethoscope — Laennec (1816). Abnormal sound (murmur) — valve defect.

> **B.Sc. point — Frank–Starling law:** The more blood fills the ventricle (EDV, **preload** ↑), the more the heart muscle fibres are stretched and the stronger the contraction — giving a larger stroke volume. That is, **venous return ↑ → cardiac output ↑**. **Afterload** = the arterial pressure against which the ventricle has to pump.

| Wave / interval | Meaning | Normal duration |
|---|---|---|
| P wave | **Depolarisation of the atria** (just before atrial systole) | ~0.08–0.1 s |
| PR interval | Time for the impulse from SA node to ventricles (including AV delay) | **0.12–0.20 s**; if prolonged → **AV block** |
| QRS complex | **Depolarisation of the ventricles** (start of ventricular systole); atrial repolarisation is hidden in it | 0.06–0.10 s |
| T wave | **Repolarisation of the ventricles** — end of T = end of systole | — |
| ST segment | Elevated/depressed → **myocardial infarction (heart attack)** / ischaemia | — |

> **Exam angle:** Counting the QRS complexes in a given time gives the heart rate. Atrial repolarisation shows no separate wave. The action potential of heart muscle is long (~0.25–0.3 s, a **plateau** due to Ca²⁺) with a **long refractory period** — so the heart shows **no tetanus**.

## 7. Double circulation and portal systems

- **Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → lungs → left atrium.
- **Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → body → right atrium.
- **Hepatic portal system:** intestine → **hepatic portal vein** → liver → hepatic vein → vena cava (nutrients are processed in the liver first).
- **Renal portal system:** in fishes, amphibians, reptiles and birds (rudimentary); **absent in mammals**.
- **Hypophyseal portal system:** hypothalamus → anterior pituitary (Chapter 07).
- Portal system = a vein that **begins in capillaries and ends in capillaries**.

### Blood vessels

| | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | Three layers, thick muscular-elastic **tunica media** | Three layers, thin; wide lumen | Only a single layer of **endothelium** |
| Valves | Absent (except at the base of aorta/pulmonary artery) | **Present** | Absent |
| Pressure | High, pulsatile | Low | — |
| Special | **Arterioles** = "resistance vessels" — main site of peripheral resistance | "**Capacitance vessels**" — ~60–70% of the blood is in the veins | Exchange; largest total cross-section → **lowest flow velocity** |

## 8. Blood pressure and its regulation

Normal adult: **120/80 mm Hg** (systolic/diastolic); measured with a **sphygmomanometer** (Riva-Rocci) in the brachial artery; **Korotkoff sounds**.

<div class="formula">

**Pulse pressure** = systolic − diastolic = 120 − 80 = **40 mm Hg**

**Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)** ≈ diastolic + ⅓ (pulse pressure) = 80 + 13 ≈ **93 mm Hg**

**MAP = cardiac output (CO) × total peripheral resistance (TPR)**; CO = stroke volume × heart rate

**Poiseuille's law:** resistance ∝ (viscosity × length) / r⁴ — if the radius of an arteriole is halved, resistance rises **16 times**.

</div>

**Factors that determine blood pressure:** cardiac output (heart rate, force of contraction), peripheral resistance (diameter of arterioles, blood viscosity), **blood volume**, elasticity of the arteries (falls with age → systolic pressure rises). Blood volume ↑ → venous return ↑ → EDV ↑ → (Frank–Starling) cardiac output ↑ → blood pressure ↑.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Consider the statements regarding blood, blood pressure and blood volume —
> (I) Mean arterial pressure depends on cardiac output and total peripheral resistance.
> (II) An increase in blood volume increases venous return, which under normal physiological conditions can raise arterial pressure by increasing cardiac output.
> (III) Plasma proteins contribute to maintaining blood volume by generating oncotic pressure, which influences fluid exchange.
> Which combination is correct? (A) I, II and III (B) I and II only (C) I and III only (D) II and III only
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — all three statements are standard Guyton facts: MAP = CO × TPR; the Frank–Starling mechanism; the oncotic pressure of albumin.

### (a) Short-term regulation (in seconds) — neural

**Baroreceptor reflex:**

1. Blood pressure ↑ → stretch receptors of the **carotid sinus** (at the base of the internal carotid; sensory nerve — **glossopharyngeal, IX**) and the **aortic arch** (**vagus, X**) send more impulses.
2. The **cardiovascular centre** of the medulla — the cardioinhibitory part is stimulated, the vasomotor (vasoconstrictor) part is suppressed.
3. **Vagal (parasympathetic) activity ↑** → acetylcholine (M₂ receptors) → SA node rate ↓ → **heart rate ↓ (bradycardia)**; **sympathetic activity ↓** → arterioles dilate (peripheral resistance ↓), force of contraction ↓.
4. Result — blood pressure moves back towards normal. When blood pressure falls, exactly the opposite happens (heart rate ↑, vasoconstriction).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** On a sudden rise in arterial pressure, heart rate is adjusted unconsciously and reflexly. Which explanation links cardiac anatomy with the neural regulation of the heartbeat?
> (A) Carotid and aortic baroreceptors increase vagal activity, which lowers the sinoatrial node rate and heart rate
> (B) Baroreceptors activate sympathetic outflow and produce tachycardia
> (C) The sinoatrial node slows automatically due to reduced venous return, independent of autonomic control
> (D) Raised pressure directly stimulates the ventricular muscle and increases the contraction rate without neural input
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)** — rise in pressure → baroreceptors → medulla → vagus ↑ → SA node slows. (The same question also appears in the autonomic nervous system section of [Neural coordination](/interview/up-pgt/jeev-vigyan/jantu-2/tantrika-tantra-aur-gyanendriyan).)

**Other neural control:**
- **Sympathetic** — noradrenaline (β₁ receptors) → heart rate and force of contraction ↑ (positive chronotropic and inotropic); also adrenaline from the adrenal medulla.
- **Parasympathetic (vagus)** — only lowers the rate (mainly on the SA and AV nodes); negligible effect on the ventricles. At rest, because of "**vagal tone**", the heart rate (~72) stays below the intrinsic rate of the SA node (~100).
- **Chemoreceptor reflex** (carotid/aortic bodies) — O₂ ↓, CO₂ ↑ → vasoconstriction, blood pressure ↑.
- **Bainbridge reflex** — stretch of the right atrium → heart rate ↑ (B.Sc.).

### (b) Intermediate and long-term regulation — hormonal and renal

| Mechanism | Stimulus | Action | Effect on BP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)** | Renal blood flow/pressure ↓ → **renin** from **juxtaglomerular (JG) cells** | Angiotensinogen (liver) → angiotensin I → **ACE (mainly lungs)** → **angiotensin II** — powerful vasoconstrictor; **aldosterone** (Na⁺ and water reabsorption), ADH and thirst ↑ | ↑ |
| **ADH / vasopressin** | Osmolarity ↑, volume ↓ | Water reabsorption (collecting duct); vasoconstriction at high doses | ↑ |
| **Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF / ANP)** | Stretch of the atria (volume ↑) | Vasodilation, excretion of Na⁺ and water, inhibition of renin and aldosterone — **antagonist of RAAS** | ↓ |
| Adrenaline / noradrenaline | Stress | Heart rate and vasoconstriction | ↑ |
| **Pressure natriuresis** | Long-term rise in pressure | Kidneys excrete more Na⁺ and water → volume ↓ | ↓ (long-term "set point" — Guyton) |
| Local — NO (nitric oxide), endothelin | — | NO is a dilator, endothelin a constrictor | — |

> **Memory trick:** RAAS **R**aises blood pressure; **ANP** is **A**gainst it.

## 9. Disorders of the circulatory system

| Disorder | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hypertension** | ≥ **140/90** mm Hg (repeatedly); damages heart, kidneys, brain |
| Arteriosclerosis / atherosclerosis | Deposition of cholesterol, calcium and fibrous tissue in arteries (plaque) |
| **Coronary artery disease (CAD)** | Narrowing of the coronary arteries |
| **Angina pectoris** | Shortage of O₂ to the heart muscle → chest pain |
| **Heart attack / MI** | Death of heart muscle due to sudden stoppage of blood supply |
| **Cardiac arrest** | Heart stops beating |
| **Heart failure** | Heart cannot pump enough; congestion in the lungs ("congestive") |
| Heart block | Conduction blocked at the AV node — prolonged PR interval |

## Traps and exceptions

- The **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood and the **pulmonary vein** oxygenated blood — exceptions to the rule "artery = pure blood". Definition of an artery: a vessel that carries blood away from the heart.
- **The fish heart is a "venous heart"** — only deoxygenated blood.
- **Crocodile** — a reptile, yet it has 4 chambers.
- **The first sound "lub" is due to closure of the AV valves** (not the semilunar valves).
- **The P wave is not atrial systole**; it is atrial depolarisation (the electrical event comes before contraction).
- **No tetanus in the heart** — long refractory period.
- **Thickest wall is of the left ventricle**; thinnest of the atria.
- **The SA node is in the right** atrium, not the left.
- **The vagus lowers heart rate**; in the baroreceptor reflex, when blood pressure rises **vagal activity rises and sympathetic activity falls** (choosing the reverse is a common error).
- **Carotid sinus = baroreceptor**; **carotid body = chemoreceptor** — the two are different.
- **ACE is mainly in the lungs**; renin from the JG cells of the kidney; angiotensinogen from the liver.
- **ANP lowers blood pressure**; aldosterone raises it.
- **Renal portal system is absent in mammals.**
- **Flow velocity is lowest in capillaries** (total cross-section the largest) — suitable for exchange.
- **Universal donor O⁻, universal recipient AB⁺**; the Bombay group looks like O but is not O.
- **Serum has no fibrinogen.**

1. Open circulation — Arthropoda, most Mollusca; closed — Annelida, Cephalopoda, vertebrates.
2. Fish 2 chambers (single circulation); amphibians 3; birds and mammals 4 (complete double).
3. Plasma ~55%; plasma proteins 6–8%; albumin is the source of oncotic pressure.
