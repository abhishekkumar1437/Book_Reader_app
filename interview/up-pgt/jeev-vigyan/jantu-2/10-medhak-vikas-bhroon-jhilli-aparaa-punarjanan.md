This chapter completes the remaining part of the embryology unit of the syllabus — organogenesis, development and metamorphosis of frog, embryonic (extra-embryonic) membranes in birds, placenta in mammals, regeneration, and human pregnancy, parturition and lactation. UP PGT 2026 had a "best explanation" style question on embryonic membranes (Q9) and a direct question on hCG (Q39).

> **In simple words:** The frog egg is fertilized in water and becomes a tadpole, which is fish-like (gills, tail, herbivorous). Thyroid hormone changes it into an adult frog (lungs, legs, carnivorous) — this is metamorphosis. The bird embryo forms four membranes inside the dry shell: a water sac (amnion), a yolk sac, a waste sac (allantois) and an outer covering (chorion). In mammals the chorion and allantois together attach to the mother's uterus — this is the placenta.

## 1. Development of frog

The Indian frog (*Hoplobatrachus tigerinus*, old name *Rana tigrina*) breeds in the rainy season.

### 1.1 From amplexus to fertilization

- **Amplexus** — the male climbs on the female's back and holds her with the **nuptial pads** on the thumbs of his forelimbs; for several hours to days. This is **pseudo-copulation** — fertilization is **external**.
- The female releases thousands of eggs (spawn) into water; each egg has a **jelly coat** secreted by the oviduct (swells by absorbing water; sticks the eggs together, protects from predators, retains heat).
- Egg: **mesolecithal, moderately telolecithal**; the upper **animal hemisphere** is black (melanin — protection from UV, heat absorption), the lower **vegetal hemisphere** is yolk-laden and light.
- After sperm entry, **cortical rotation** (~30°) → exactly opposite the entry point the **grey crescent** = future dorsal side; the **dorsal lip** will form here.

### 1.2 Cleavage and blastula

| Cleavage | Plane | Result |
|---|---|---|
| First | Meridional, divides the grey crescent into two equal halves | 2 blastomeres (future right–left sides) |
| Second | Meridional, perpendicular to the first | 4 equal blastomeres |
| Third | **Latitudinal, above the equator** | 4 small **micromeres** (above) + 4 large **macromeres** (below) |
| Later | Holoblastic unequal | 16–32 cells = **morula** |

- **Blastula:** **blastocoel** displaced into the animal hemisphere; roof several cell layers thick — a modified coeloblastula.

### 1.3 Gastrulation

1. Just below the grey crescent, **bottle cells** contract to form a groove — the **dorsal lip of the blastopore**.
2. Cells of the marginal zone go inside by **involution** over the dorsal lip → future notochord, mesoderm and roof of the archenteron.
3. Cells of the animal hemisphere spread by **epiboly** and cover the whole embryo (future ectoderm).
4. The blastopore extends to the lateral lips and ventral lip and becomes circular; the round part of yolk-laden macromeres visible in it = **yolk plug**.
5. The **archenteron** enlarges and the **blastocoel** disappears.
6. Blastopore → later the **anus** (deuterostome).

### 1.4 Neurulation and organogenesis

- The notochord-mesoderm induces the dorsal ectoderm into the **neural plate** → edges rise as **neural folds** → **neural groove** in the middle → folds meet to form the **neural tube**; the front part swells into the brain (fore-, mid-, hindbrain), the rear into the spinal cord.
- Cells that separate from the crests of the folds = **neural crest** → ganglia, pigment cells, adrenal medulla, facial cartilage.
- Division of mesoderm:

| Part | Derivatives |
|---|---|
| **Dorsal (epimere) — somites** | **Sclerotome** → vertebrae, ribs; **myotome** → skeletal muscles; **dermatome** → dermis of the back |
| **Intermediate (mesomere)** | **Kidneys** (pronephros → mesonephros), ducts of gonads |
| **Lateral plate (hypomere)** | **Somatic** layer + ectoderm = **somatopleure**; **splanchnic** layer + endoderm = **splanchnopleure**; **coelom** in between; heart, blood vessels, gut muscles |

- **Eye development (induction chain):** **optic vesicle** from the forebrain (diencephalon) → induces the overlying ectoderm into the **lens placode** → lens vesicle; the optic vesicle invaginates into the **optic cup** — inner layer neural retina, outer layer pigmented retina; the lens induces the **cornea** from the overlying ectoderm. (Spemann, 1901 — removing the optic vesicle prevented lens formation in some species.)

### 1.5 Tadpole

- In about 1–2 weeks (depending on temperature) the **tadpole** hatches from the egg. Initially it has no mouth, attaches by an **adhesive organ / sucker** below the head, and depends on yolk.
- Then **3 pairs of external gills** → later **internal gills** covered by the **operculum**; the external opening of the opercular chamber is the **spiracle** (one in *Rana*, on the left side).
- Mouth has **horny jaws and labial teeth** — for scraping algae; long coiled intestine (herbivorous); **lateral line system**; tail with fins; two-chambered heart, fish-like circulation.
- **Hindlimbs** appear first; forelimbs form under the operculum and emerge later (the left one through the spiracle).

## 2. Metamorphosis

### 2.1 Changes in metamorphosis

| System | Tadpole | Adult frog |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | Fully aquatic | Amphibious |
| Food | Herbivorous (algae) | Carnivorous (insects) |
| Mouth | Horny jaws, small mouth | Horny parts shed, wide mouth, protrusible **tongue**, teeth in upper jaw |
| Intestine | Long, coiled | Short (stomach develops); digestive enzymes change |
| Respiration | Gills (and skin) | **Lungs**, skin, bucco-pharynx |
| Circulation | 2-chambered heart, 4 pairs of afferent branchial arches | 3-chambered heart; arches reduced to carotid, systemic, pulmo-cutaneous |
| Haemoglobin | Larval Hb — **higher affinity for O₂** | Adult Hb — lower affinity |
| Excretion | **Ammonotelic** | **Ureotelic** — **urea cycle enzymes** in the liver (e.g. carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-I, arginase) increase many-fold |
| Visual pigment | **Porphyropsin** (retinal-2, vitamin A₂) | **Rhodopsin** (retinal-1, vitamin A₁); eyelids, nictitating membrane form |
| Sensory | Lateral line | Lateral line lost; **tympanum** and middle ear form |
| Tail | Present | **Resorbed** — lysosomal enzymes (cathepsins), **matrix metalloproteinases**, **programmed cell death (apoptosis)** |
| Skin | Thin | Thick, mucous and poison glands, keratinised outer layer |

> **In simple words:** Metamorphosis involves three kinds of change — **regressive** (tail, gills, horny teeth destroyed), **progressive/constructive** (legs, lungs, tongue, tympanum formed) and **remodelling** (intestine, liver enzymes, haemoglobin, skin).

- **J. F. Gudernatsch, 1912** — feeding horse thyroid gland to tadpoles caused **precocious metamorphosis** (tiny frogs).
- **B. M. Allen, 1916** — removing the thyroid (or pituitary) of tadpoles prevented metamorphosis; they remained **giant tadpoles**.
- **Thyroxine (T₄)** from the thyroid; converted in target tissues by **type-II deiodinase** into the more active **T₃**; T₃ binds nuclear **thyroid hormone receptors (TRα, TRβ)** and changes gene expression. The same hormone has opposite effects depending on the tissue — cell division in the legs, cell death in the tail.
- **Iodine** is essential — tadpoles in iodine-free water do not metamorphose; they do when iodine is added.
- **Hypothalamic factor:** in amphibian larvae the main stimulator of TSH secretion is **CRH (corticotropin-releasing hormone)**; **TRH** (the main stimulator of TSH in mammals) does not effectively raise TSH in tadpoles.
- **Prolactin** — the **"juvenile hormone"** of amphibians — maintains the larval state by opposing the effect of T₃ (a role like JH in insects).
- **Corticosteroids** (corticosterone) — enhance the effect of T₃ (by increasing T₄ → T₃ conversion).
- Stages: **premetamorphosis** (low TH, body growth) → **prometamorphosis** (TH rises, hindlimbs) → **metamorphic climax** (TH peak, forelimbs emerge, tail resorption).

### 2.3 Neoteny

- **Neoteny** = sexual maturity while retaining larval features (external gills, tail fin). The broader term is **paedomorphosis**; if reproductive maturity comes early, it is **progenesis/paedogenesis**.
- **Axolotl (*Ambystoma mexicanum*)** — **facultative** neoteny; the hypothalamus-pituitary axis does not release enough TSH, but the tissues are sensitive to thyroid hormone — **on giving thyroxine it metamorphoses into a terrestrial salamander**.
- ***Necturus*** (mudpuppy), ***Proteus*** (olm), ***Siren*** — **obligate** neoteny; tissues insensitive to thyroid hormone — no metamorphosis even when T₄ is given.

> **Exam angle:** "What is the terrestrial form of the axolotl larva called?" — ***Ambystoma* (like the tiger salamander)**; instead of "Who discovered metamorphosis?", the question asked is "Who showed the role of the thyroid?" — **Gudernatsch (1912)**.

**Insect comparison (brief):** in insects metamorphosis is driven by **ecdysone (20-hydroxyecdysone, prothoracic gland)**, while **juvenile hormone (corpora allata)** maintains the larval state; PTTH from the brain. Simple (incomplete) metamorphosis — nymph → growth by ecdysis → adult (cockroach); complete — larva → pupa → adult (butterfly).

## 3. Embryonic (extra-embryonic) membranes in birds

Reptiles, birds and mammals = **Amniota** — their embryos form four extra-embryonic membranes, which made development possible on land in the **cleidoic egg**. Fishes and amphibians are **Anamniota** — they have only a yolk sac.

**Chick egg:** calcium carbonate **shell** (porous) → two **shell membranes** (separate at the blunt end to form the **air space**) → **albumen** (white; **chalazae** — twisted cords that keep the yolk centred) → **vitelline membrane** → yolk; on top of the yolk the **blastodisc/germinal disc**. By the time the egg is laid (~24 hours in the hen's oviduct) the embryo has reached the blastula/early gastrula stage; incubation **21 days**.

| Membrane | Origin | Function in birds | In mammals (humans) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yolk sac** | **Splanchnopleure** (endoderm + splanchnic mesoderm); forms first | Surrounds the yolk, digests and absorbs it; first **blood islands** — first haematopoiesis; drawn into the abdomen at the end of incubation | Small, yolkless; early haematopoiesis and source of **primordial germ cells** |
| **Amnion** | **Somatopleure** (ectoderm + somatic mesoderm) — from the amniotic folds meeting above | **Amniotic fluid** surrounding the embryo — protection from shocks, prevents desiccation, prevents the embryo from sticking, free movement | Same; testing of amniotic fluid = **amniocentesis** |
| **Chorion (serosa)** | **Somatopleure** — outer part of the amniotic folds | Outermost membrane, surrounds all others; gas exchange together with the allantois | **Foetal part** of the placenta (**chorionic villi**); secretes **hCG** |
| **Allantois** | **Splanchnopleure** — ventral outgrowth of the hindgut | Storage of **uric acid** (excretion); fuses with the chorion to form the **chorio-allantoic membrane (CAM)** — **respiratory organ**, **Ca²⁺ absorption** from the shell, helps absorb albumen | Blood vessels of the **umbilical cord** (umbilical arteries and vein) come from it; remnant **urachus** |

> **Memory trick:** "**Soma outside, Splanchno inside**" — the **somatopleure** gives the **amnion and chorion** (both cover the embryo), the **splanchnopleure** gives the **yolk sac and allantois** (both are sacs connected to the gut).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In amniotes, extra-embryonic membranes form by coordinated folding of embryonic layers and acquire specific functions during development. Which is the best explanation of the functional coordination of these membranes during embryogenesis?
> (A) The amnion directly takes part in nutrient transfer, while the yolk sac mainly carries out gas exchange through vascularisation
> (B) The chorion is the innermost membrane surrounding the embryo, while the allantois stores yolk reserves and helps in early cleavage
> (C) In egg-laying amniotes the yolk sac and amnion together form the placenta, ending the role of the chorion and allantois
> (D) The allantois forms a vascular interface with the chorion and contributes to excretion and respiratory exchange, while the amnion provides a fluid-filled protective environment
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)** — the chorion is the outermost (not innermost), the yolk sac provides nutrition (not gas exchange), and egg-laying animals have no placenta at all.

## 4. Placenta in mammals

**Definition (Mossman, 1937):** close apposition or fusion of the foetal membranes with the maternal uterine mucosa for physiological exchange (nutrients, O₂, CO₂, wastes, hormones, antibodies). Placenta = **foetal part** (chorion) + **maternal part** (uterine endometrium / **decidua**).

### 4.1 Based on the foetal membrane

| Type | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Yolk-sac / choriovitelline placenta** | Chorion + yolk sac | Most **marsupials** (kangaroo, opossum); temporarily in many eutherians (e.g. early in rabbits, rodents) |
| **Chorio-allantoic placenta** | Chorion + blood vessels of the allantois | All **eutherian** mammals; exception among marsupials **bandicoot (*Perameles*)** |

> **Exam angle:** The main difference between eutherians (placental mammals) and marsupials — eutherians have a **complex, long-lasting chorio-allantoic placenta**; marsupials have a short-lived, simple yolk-sac placenta and the rest of the young's development takes place in the marsupium (pouch). (See mammal classification in Zoology I.)

### 4.2 Based on shape (distribution of chorionic villi)

| Type | Distribution of villi | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffuse** | Uniform over the whole chorionic surface | **Pig, horse**, camel, donkey, whale, lemur |
| **Cotyledonary** | In separate tufts (cotyledons), which attach to the uterine **caruncles** | **Ruminants — cow, sheep, goat, deer** |
| **Zonary** | A band/girdle in the middle | **Carnivores — dog, cat**, seal, and also the **elephant** |
| **Discoidal** | One round disc | **Humans**, apes, **rodents (rat)**, **rabbit**, **bats**, insectivores |
| **Bidiscoidal** | Two discs | **Monkeys (rhesus, *Macaca*)** |

### 4.3 Histological types — Grosser's (1909) classification

There can be at most **6 layers** between maternal and foetal blood — maternal side: (1) endothelium of uterine blood vessel, (2) connective tissue, (3) uterine epithelium; foetal side: (4) chorionic epithelium (trophoblast), (5) connective tissue, (6) endothelium of foetal blood vessel. The more maternal layers the trophoblast destroys, the more intimate the placenta.

| Type | Maternal layers destroyed | Layers remaining | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epitheliochorial** | None | 6 | **Pig, horse**, donkey, whale, lemur |
| **Syndesmochorial** | Uterine epithelium | 5 | **Cow, sheep, goat** (ruminants) |
| **Endotheliochorial** | Epithelium + connective tissue | 4 | **Dog, cat** (carnivores) |
| **Haemochorial** | All three maternal layers — trophoblast directly in maternal blood | 3 | **Humans, apes, monkeys**, rodents, bats, insectivores |
| **Haemoendothelial** | All three maternal layers + foetal epithelium and connective tissue also nearly lost | ~1 | **Rabbit**, rat, guinea pig (in older textbooks) |

> **Remember:** In the modern view, the ruminant placenta is called "synepitheliochorial" (the maternal epithelium is not destroyed but fuses with foetal binucleate cells), and "haemoendothelial" is considered a subtype of haemochorial (human — haemomonochorial, rabbit — haemodichorial, rat — haemotrichorial). In exams the traditional Grosser names are still used.

> **Memory trick:** "**Pig-Horse Epithelio, Cow-Sheep Syndesmo, Dog-Cat Endothelio, Human Haemo**" — and for shape "**Pig-Horse Diffuse, Cow-Sheep Cotyledonary, Dog-Cat Zonary, Human Discoidal**" — the order of animals is the same in both classifications.

### 4.4 Deciduate vs non-deciduate

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-deciduate / indeciduate** | No maternal tissue is shed at birth, negligible bleeding (villi simply pull out of the crypts) | Epitheliochorial and syndesmochorial — pig, horse, cow, sheep |
| **Deciduate** | Part of the maternal endometrium (decidua) is shed with the placenta, bleeding | Endotheliochorial, haemochorial — dog, cat, human, rodents |
| **Contradeciduate** | The placenta is not expelled; it is absorbed by maternal tissue inside the uterus | **Mole (*Talpa*)**, bandicoot (*Perameles*) |

### 4.5 Human placenta

- **Discoidal, haemochorial, deciduate, chorio-allantoic**; at full term about **500 g**, diameter ~15–20 cm, thickness ~2–3 cm.
- **Implantation** — about **6–7 days** after fertilization the trophoblast of the blastocyst sinks into the endometrium; trophoblast → inner **cytotrophoblast** + outer invasive **syncytiotrophoblast**.
- **Chorionic villi** remain immersed in the **intervillous spaces** filled with maternal blood; parts of the decidua — **basalis** (maternal part of the placenta), **capsularis**, **parietalis**.
- **Umbilical cord:** **2 umbilical arteries** (deoxygenated blood from the foetus towards the placenta) + **1 umbilical vein** (oxygenated blood towards the foetus), in **Wharton's jelly**.
- **Placental barrier** — maternal and foetal blood do not mix; **IgG** crosses the placenta (passive immunity); IgM does not. In Rh incompatibility, maternal anti-D IgG crosses and causes **erythroblastosis foetalis**. Alcohol, nicotine, many drugs, rubella and HIV can also cross.

### 4.6 Placental hormones (placenta = temporary endocrine gland)

| Hormone | Source | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin)** | Syncytiotrophoblast | **Maintenance of the corpus luteum** (LH-like effect) so that progesterone continues to be made; appears in blood ~8–10 days after fertilization, peaks at ~8–10 weeks; its presence in urine = basis of the **pregnancy test**; testosterone from Leydig cells of the male foetus |
| **hPL (human placental lactogen) / hCS** | Syncytiotrophoblast | Alters maternal metabolism — insulin resistance, lipolysis, so that glucose reaches the foetus; breast development |
| **Oestrogen** (mainly **oestriol**) | Placenta (from DHEA-S of the foetal adrenal — "foeto-placental unit") | Growth of uterus and breasts; increases oxytocin receptors |
| **Progesterone** | First the corpus luteum, after ~8–12 weeks the **placenta** ("luteo-placental shift") | Maintenance of endometrium, keeps the myometrium quiet — "hormone of pregnancy" |
| **Relaxin** | NCERT: from the **ovary (corpus luteum)** late in pregnancy; the placenta and decidua also make it | Loosens ligaments of the pubic symphysis, softens the cervix |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** The main function of hCG is —
> (A) to initiate ovulation (B) maintenance of the corpus luteum (C) to stop foetal growth (D) to form the placenta
> Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)** — hCG acts like LH and saves the corpus luteum from degenerating, so that progesterone continues to be made and the endometrium is not shed. Ovulation is caused by the LH surge.

## 5. Human pregnancy

- **Gestation** — about **9 months**: ~266 days from fertilization; ~280 days (40 weeks) from the date of the last menstrual period (LMP).
- Early events: fertilization (ampulla) → travel through the oviduct while cleaving → morula/blastocyst in the uterus at ~4–5 days → **implantation on ~day 7**.
- **Inner cell mass** → bilaminar disc (epiblast + hypoblast) → gastrulation (third week, primitive streak) → all three germ layers; its **stem cells** can form all tissues.

**Sequence of foetal development according to NCERT:**

| Time | Development |
|---|---|
| By the end of 1 month | The **heart** forms (the heartbeat is heard with a stethoscope later) |
| By the end of 2 months | **Limbs and digits** form |
| By the end of 12 weeks (first trimester) | Most major **organ systems** are formed (limbs and external genitalia developed) |
| 5th month | **First movements** of the foetus and **hair** on the head |
| By the end of 24 weeks (second trimester) | Fine hair (lanugo) on the body, **eyelids separate**, **eyelashes** form |
| By the end of 9 months | Fully developed, ready for delivery |

## 6. Parturition

- Parturition = expulsion of the fully developed foetus; induced by a **complex neuroendocrine mechanism**.
- Signals come from the **fully developed foetus and placenta** → mild uterine contractions = **foetal ejection reflex** → release of **oxytocin** from the maternal posterior pituitary → strong uterine contractions → still more oxytocin — **positive feedback** — until the baby is delivered.
- Supporting factors: foetal **cortisol** (lowers the progesterone/oestrogen ratio in the placenta), **prostaglandins** (PGF₂α, PGE₂ — cervical ripening, contractions), increase in **oxytocin receptors** and gap junctions in the myometrium (due to oestrogen), relaxin.
- **Ferguson reflex** — oxytocin release from stretching of the cervix/vagina.
- Three stages of labour: (1) dilation of the cervix, (2) expulsion of the baby, (3) **expulsion of the placenta (after-birth)**.

## 7. Lactation

| Hormone | Function |
|---|---|
| **Prolactin** (anterior pituitary) | **Production / synthesis** of milk — during pregnancy high oestrogen-progesterone block its effect; lactation starts as soon as they fall after delivery |
| **Oxytocin** (posterior pituitary) | **Milk ejection / let-down** — contraction of myoepithelial cells; a **neuroendocrine reflex** triggered by suckling |
| Oestrogen, progesterone, hPL | Development of the duct and alveolar system during pregnancy |

- **Colostrum** — the yellow, thick milk of the first few days of lactation; rich in **IgA** antibodies → **natural passive immunity** for the newborn; high in protein, low in fat and lactose.
- Breastfeeding suppresses GnRH via prolactin → **lactational amenorrhoea** — natural contraception with full breastfeeding for about 6 months after delivery (NCERT).

## 8. Regeneration

Regeneration = rebuilding of a lost or damaged part. Early work: **Abraham Trembley, 1740–1744** — complete hydras from pieces of *Hydra*; **Spallanzani, 1768** — regeneration of legs, tail and jaws of salamanders and the head of snails; **T. H. Morgan, 1901** — the book "Regeneration", the terms **morphallaxis** and **epimorphosis**.

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Morphallaxis** | **Reorganisation/repatterning** of the remaining tissue, very little new cell division; the new organism is small at first | ***Hydra*** |
| **Epimorphosis** | Dedifferentiation/migration of cells at the cut site → **blastema** → cell division → redifferentiation | **Salamander/newt limb**, lizard tail, *Planaria* (from neoblasts) |
| **Compensatory regeneration** | Differentiated cells themselves divide and restore the **mass** of the organ, not its original shape (lobes) | **Mammalian liver** (mass restored in a few weeks even after removing two-thirds — the "Prometheus" example) |
| **Physiological** | Replacement of normal wear and tear | RBCs, epidermis of skin, intestinal epithelium, hair |

### 8.1 Key examples

- ***Planaria*** — even about a **1/279th piece** can form a complete organism (Morgan); pluripotent **neoblast** cells scattered throughout the body; **polarity** is maintained — the head end forms a head; splitting the head lengthwise gives a **two-headed** planarian.
- ***Hydra*** — morphallaxis; **hypostome = "head organiser"**, Wnt signalling; interstitial cells (I-cells).
- **Salamander/axolotl limb** — (1) epidermis spreads over the wound to form the **apical epidermal cap**; (2) cells beneath dedifferentiate to form the **blastema**; (3) regeneration is **nerve-dependent** (Singer — no regeneration if the nerve is cut); (4) the blastema has "positional memory" — only the part distal to the cut forms; giving **retinoic acid** makes more proximal parts form too (proximalisation).
- **Lens regeneration in newts (Wolffian regeneration)** — on removing the lens, pigmented epithelial cells of the **dorsal iris** form a new lens by **transdifferentiation** (Colucci 1891, Wolff 1895).
- **Lizard tail — autotomy** — breaking off the tail itself to escape enemies; **fracture (autotomy) planes** in the caudal vertebrae; the regenerated tail has no vertebrae but an **unsegmented cartilage tube**, and often a different colour/scale pattern.
- **Other examples of autotomy:** legs of crabs/prawns, arms of starfish (in *Linckia* one arm + part of the central disc can form a complete organism), **evisceration** in sea cucumbers.
- **Limits in mammals:** regeneration of the fingertip (including the nail bed) is possible in children and mice; deer **antlers** — the only mammalian organ regenerated every year.

> **Exam angle:** "What type of regeneration is seen in *Hydra*?" — **morphallaxis**; "salamander limb?" — **epimorphosis (blastema)**; "liver?" — **compensatory hyperplasia**. In general, regenerative ability **decreases as the level of organisation increases** (there are exceptions, but this is the trend).

## Traps and exceptions

- **The allantois is the respiratory organ of birds** (with the chorion), not the yolk sac; yolk sac = nutrition and first blood cells.
- **Amnion and chorion** from somatopleure, **yolk sac and allantois** from splanchnopleure — questions try to trap you by reversing this.
- **The outermost membrane is the chorion**; the innermost (directly surrounding the embryo) is the **amnion**.
- **Fishes and amphibians are anamniotes**; they have a yolk sac but no amnion.
- **The elephant placenta is zonary** (like the dog), while the **horse is diffuse** — do not guess on the basis of "large herbivore" alone.
- **Monkey (rhesus) is bidiscoidal**, human discoidal.
- **The bandicoot**, though a marsupial, has a chorio-allantoic placenta.
- **The pregnancy test is based on hCG**, not LH or progesterone.
- **Prolactin makes milk, oxytocin ejects it** — questions reverse the two.
- **Positive feedback in parturition** — rare in the body (others: LH surge, blood clotting).
- **In amphibian larvae the main stimulator of TSH is CRH**, not TRH (opposite of mammals).
- **Prolactin is anti-metamorphic in amphibians** ("juvenile hormone") — but in mammals it is the lactation hormone.
- **The axolotl metamorphoses with thyroxine**, *Necturus* does not.
- **The regenerated lizard tail has no vertebrae** — it has a cartilage tube.
- **Relaxin:** NCERT says it comes from the ovary late in pregnancy; if both "placenta" and "ovary" are among the options, the NCERT answer is "ovary".

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. What type of fertilization does the frog have? — **External** (amplexus only helps release eggs and sperms together).
2. The part that fills the blastopore in the frog gastrula? — **Yolk plug** (macromere cells).
3. How is the tadpole's tail destroyed during metamorphosis? — Resorption by **lysosomal enzymes and apoptosis**.
4. Mineral essential for metamorphosis? — **Iodine** (component of thyroxine).
5. Hormone that opposes metamorphosis in amphibians? — **Prolactin** (juvenile hormone).
6. What will happen if thyroxine is given to an axolotl? — It will **metamorphose into a terrestrial salamander**.
7. Change in excretion during metamorphosis? — **Ammonotelic to ureotelic** (urea cycle enzymes increase).
8. Which membrane forms the respiratory organ of the chick? — **Allantois** (CAM with the chorion).
9. From which layer is the amnion formed? — **Somatopleure** (ectoderm + somatic mesoderm).
10. Shape of the cow placenta? — **Cotyledonary**.
11. Histological type of the dog placenta? — **Endotheliochorial**, zonary in shape.
12. How many maternal layers remain between maternal and foetal blood in the human placenta? — **Zero** (haemochorial; trophoblast in direct contact with maternal blood).
13. Pig placenta? — **Diffuse, epitheliochorial, non-deciduate**.
14. Which hormone does a pregnancy test kit detect? — **hCG** (in urine).
15. Meaning of "luteo-placental shift"? — **The main source of progesterone changes from the corpus luteum to the placenta after ~8–12 weeks**.
16. Hormone giving positive feedback in parturition? — **Oxytocin**.
17. Which antibody does the newborn get from colostrum? — **IgA** (passive immunity).
18. Type of regeneration in *Hydra*? — **Morphallaxis** (discovered by Trembley).
19. Mass of dedifferentiated cells in salamander limb regeneration? — **Blastema**.
20. What type is regeneration of the mammalian liver? — **Compensatory regeneration** (mass returns, not the lobes).
