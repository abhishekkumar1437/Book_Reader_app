This chapter takes the first part of the "Animal Embryology" unit of the official syllabus — Gametogenesis, Fertilization in lower and higher animals, Types of eggs and cleavage, and the human reproductive system and physiology — from NCERT 12 to B.Sc./M.Sc. level. In UP PGT 2026, embryology produced "statement-combination" style questions (e.g. Q60 on cellular differentiation), so remember not just definitions but both the "why" and the "in which animal".

> **In simple words:** Sperms are formed in the testis and ova in the ovary by meiosis, so both are haploid (n). A sperm and an ovum together form the zygote (2n). The zygote divides repeatedly without growing — this is called cleavage. The more yolk an egg has, the more incomplete its cleavage. Then cells move to form three layers (germ layers), from which all organs are formed.

## 1. Male reproductive system

### 1.1 Testis

- The testes lie outside the abdominal cavity in the **scrotum**, where the temperature is about **2–2.5 °C lower** than the body — necessary for spermatogenesis. Testes remaining in the abdomen = **cryptorchidism** → sterility.
- Each testis is about 4–5 cm long and 2–3 cm wide; outer covering **tunica albuginea**.
- Each testis has about **250 testicular lobules**; each lobule has **1–3 highly coiled seminiferous tubules** (NCERT).

| Cell | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Spermatogonia** | On the basement membrane of the tubule | Increase in number by mitosis; some form primary spermatocytes |
| **Sertoli cells** — "nurse cells" | Inside the tubule, from base to lumen | Nourish sperms, **blood–testis barrier** (tight junctions), secrete **inhibin** and **androgen-binding protein (ABP)**, phagocytose residual cytoplasm of spermiogenesis; in the embryo **AMH (Anti-Müllerian hormone)** |
| **Leydig / interstitial cells** | In interstitial tissue between tubules | Produce **testosterone** under the influence of LH (ICSH) |

### 1.2 Duct pathway and accessory glands

**Pathway:** seminiferous tubule → rete testis → vasa efferentia → **epididymis** (storage and maturation of sperms, acquiring motility) → **vas deferens** → ejaculatory duct (joined by the duct of the seminal vesicle) → **urethra** → external urethral meatus.

| Accessory gland | Number | Secretion | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seminal vesicle** | 1 pair | **Fructose**, prostaglandins, fibrinogen-like proteins, alkaline | About **60–70%** of semen volume; fructose is the sperm's energy source |
| **Prostate** | 1 (unpaired) | Citrate, acid phosphatase, **PSA**, Zn²⁺, slightly acidic | About 20–30% of volume; PSA = marker of prostate cancer |
| **Bulbourethral / Cowper's gland** | 1 pair | Mucus | Lubrication of the penis, neutralising urethral acidity |

Semen = sperms + seminal plasma; pH about 7.2–7.8. According to NCERT, one ejaculation has **200–300 million** sperms; for normal fertility at least **60% normal shape** and **40% vigorous motility** are needed.

## 2. Female reproductive system

### 2.1 Ovary

- One pair, almond-shaped (2–4 cm), in the pelvic cavity; attached to the uterus by the **ovarian ligament**. Outer layer **germinal epithelium** — the name is misleading; it does not form ova.
- Inner part: follicles in the **cortex**, blood vessels in the **medulla**.

**Folliculogenesis:**

| Stage | Identification |
|---|---|
| **Primordial follicle** | Primary oocyte + one layer of flat follicle cells |
| **Primary follicle** | One layer of cuboidal **granulosa** cells; **zona pellucida** begins to form |
| **Secondary follicle** | Many layers of granulosa cells + outer **theca** |
| **Tertiary follicle** | Fluid-filled cavity, the **antrum**; theca → theca interna + theca externa |
| **Graafian follicle** | Mature; secondary oocyte on the **cumulus oophorus**; surrounded by the **corona radiata** |

After ovulation, the ruptured follicle → **corpus luteum** (progesterone) → if fertilization does not occur → **corpus albicans**. Follicles that do not mature are destroyed by **atresia**.

> **Remember:** Theca interna makes **androgens** under LH; granulosa cells convert them to **oestrogen** by FSH-induced **aromatase** — the "two-cell two-gonadotropin theory".

### 2.2 Oviduct, uterus, vagina

- **Oviduct / Fallopian tube**, about 10–12 cm; parts (from ovary towards uterus): **infundibulum** with **fimbriae** at its edge → **ampulla** (widest part, **usual site of fertilization**) → **isthmus** → uterine part (interstitial/intramural part).
- **Uterus** — inverted pear-shaped; three layers of the wall: **perimetrium**, thick smooth-muscle **myometrium** (contractions during parturition), inner glandular **endometrium** (changes during the menstrual cycle).
- **Cervix** → cervical canal → **vagina**; external genitalia = **vulva**: mons pubis, labia majora and minora, **clitoris** (homologue of the penis), **hymen**.
- **Mammary gland** — modified sweat gland; alveoli → mammary tubules → mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct.

## 3. Gametogenesis

### 3.1 Spermatogenesis

```
Spermatogonium (2n, 46)  —mitosis→  Primary spermatocyte (2n, 46)
Primary spermatocyte —meiosis I→ 2 secondary spermatocytes (n, 23; each chromosome with 2 chromatids)
Secondary spermatocyte —meiosis II→ 4 spermatids (n, 23)
Spermatid —spermiogenesis→ 4 spermatozoa
Release from Sertoli cells = spermiation
```

- Begins at puberty and continues throughout life; one complete cycle in humans takes about **64–74 days**.
- There is no division in **spermiogenesis** — only transformation: **acrosome** from the Golgi, condensation of the nucleus (histone → protamine), axoneme of the tail from the centriole, mitochondria spiral in the middle piece, excess cytoplasm discarded as the "residual body".

### 3.3 Oogenesis

```
Embryonic stage: primordial germ cells → oogonia (2n) —mitosis→ millions
Oogonia → primary oocyte (2n) — meiosis I begins, arrested in prophase I (diplotene/dictyotene) [before birth]
Each cycle after puberty: primary oocyte completes meiosis I just before ovulation
   → secondary oocyte (n, large) + first polar body (n, small)
Secondary oocyte is ovulated while arrested at metaphase II
On sperm entry meiosis II is completed → ovum (n) + second polar body
```

- At birth about 1 million primary oocytes per ovary (about 2 million in both); by puberty, due to atresia, reduced to **60,000–80,000 primary follicles** per ovary according to NCERT; only about **400–450** are ovulated in a lifetime.
- By unequal cytokinesis, 1 primary oocyte → **1 ovum + 2 or 3 polar bodies** (the first polar body may or may not divide).
- The meiosis I arrest can last from about 12 to 50 years — with higher maternal age the risk of **nondisjunction** increases (Down syndrome).

### 3.4 Spermatogenesis vs oogenesis

| Point | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Seminiferous tubule | Ovary (cortex); meiosis II in the oviduct |
| Onset | From puberty | In the embryonic stage (multiplication of oogonia ends before birth) |
| Continuity | Lifelong, continuous | Cyclic, up to menopause (~45–50 years) |
| Product | 1 primary cell → 4 sperms | 1 → 1 ovum + polar bodies |
| Arrest | None | Prophase I (diplotene) and metaphase II |
| Transformation stage | Yes (spermiogenesis) | No |
| Cytoplasm | Equal division | Unequal division |

> **Exam angle:** "At which stage does ovulation occur in humans?" — **secondary oocyte (metaphase II)**. Dog and horse ovulate at the primary oocyte stage; sea urchin as a complete ovum (both meiotic divisions completed); in *Ascaris* the sperm enters the primary oocyte.

## 4. Hormonal control of reproduction

| Hormone | Source | Target/function in male | Target/function in female |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GnRH** | Hypothalamus (pulsatile secretion) | FSH, LH from anterior pituitary | Same |
| **FSH** | Anterior pituitary | Sertoli cells → spermatogenesis, ABP, inhibin | Follicle growth, oestrogen (aromatase) |
| **LH (ICSH in males)** | Anterior pituitary | Leydig cells → testosterone | Ovulation, corpus luteum formation |
| **Testosterone** | Leydig cells | Spermatogenesis, secondary sexual characters, negative feedback | (Androgen from theca → precursor of oestrogen) |
| **Inhibin** | Sertoli / granulosa cells | Selective inhibition of **FSH** only | Inhibition of FSH |
| **Oestrogen** | Graafian follicle | — | Proliferation of endometrium; at high levels **positive** feedback on LH (LH surge) |
| **Progesterone** | Corpus luteum, placenta | — | Secretory endometrium, maintenance of pregnancy |

## 5. Menstrual cycle

Primates (monkeys, apes, humans) have a **menstrual cycle**; other mammals (cow, sheep, rat, dog) have an **oestrous cycle** — with no menstrual bleeding. First menstruation = **menarche**, last = **menopause**. Average duration **28 days**.

| Phase | Days (28-day cycle) | Ovary | Uterus | Hormones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Menstrual phase** | 1–5 | Previous corpus luteum degenerates | Functional layer of endometrium breaks down and is shed with blood | Progesterone and oestrogen low |
| **Follicular / Proliferative phase** | 6–13 | Primary follicle → Graafian follicle | Regeneration and proliferation of endometrium | FSH, LH rise gradually; oestrogen rises |
| **Ovulation** | ~14 | Graafian follicle ruptures, secondary oocyte released | — | Mid-cycle **LH surge** (FSH also rises somewhat) |
| **Luteal / Secretory phase** | 15–28 | Corpus luteum | Endometrium thick, glandular, ready for implantation | Progesterone high (peak ~day 21) |

> **Remember:** Dual effect of oestrogen — negative feedback at low concentration, but when it stays above ~200 pg/mL for about 36–48 hours, **positive feedback** → LH surge → ovulation about 24–36 hours later. The length of the follicular phase can vary; the luteal phase stays nearly constant (~14 days).

> **Common mistake:** "In the menstrual cycle, ovulation is caused by the FSH peak" — wrong; the decisive event is the **LH surge**. If fertilization occurs, the embryo's **hCG** maintains the corpus luteum (next chapter).

## 6. Fertilization — general steps

Fertilization = fusion of the nuclei of the haploid sperm and ovum (**amphimixis / karyogamy**), forming the diploid zygote and activating development (egg activation). Steps:

1. **Contact and recognition (chemotaxis + species-specific recognition)**
2. **Acrosome reaction** — exocytosis of the acrosome
3. **Penetration of egg envelopes** and fusion of plasma membranes
4. **Block to polyspermy**
5. **Egg activation** (Ca²⁺ wave, rapid rise in metabolism, completion of meiosis)
6. **Fusion of pronuclei**

| Type of fertilization | Examples |
|---|---|
| **External** — in water | Most aquatic invertebrates (sea urchin, starfish), most bony fishes, frogs/toads |
| **Internal** | Insects, spiders, *Ascaris*, earthworm (cross-fertilization — in the cocoon), sharks, reptiles, birds, mammals |

### 6.1 Fertilization in lower animals — the sea urchin model

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| Chemotaxis | Small peptides from the egg jelly — **resact** in *Arbacia*, **speract** in *Strongylocentrotus* — attract and activate sperms (species-specific) |
| Acrosome reaction | **Fucose-sulphate polymer** of the jelly layer causes Ca²⁺ entry into the sperm → acrosomal exocytosis; actin polymerisation forms the **acrosomal process** |
| Species-specific binding | The protein **bindin** on the process binds to bindin receptors on the egg's **vitelline envelope** |
| Membrane fusion | Sperm–egg membrane fusion, **fertilization cone** |
| **Fast block** | Na⁺ entry changes the egg membrane potential from about **−70 mV to +20 mV** (depolarisation) — within 1–3 seconds, lasting about 1 minute; other sperms do not attach to the positive membrane (L. Jaffe) |
| **Slow block — cortical reaction** | IP₃ releases a **Ca²⁺ wave** from the ER → exocytosis of **cortical granules** → the vitelline envelope lifts and forms the hard **fertilization membrane**; the **hyaline layer** forms |

> **Exam angle:** Fast block = **electrical (depolarisation, Na⁺)**; slow block = **mechanical/chemical (Ca²⁺, cortical granules)**. In mammals there is no clear evidence of a fast electrical block — there the main block is the **zona reaction**.

**Fertilization in frog:** During **amplexus** the female releases eggs into water and the male immediately releases sperms over them (external fertilization). The sperm enters from the **animal hemisphere**; exactly opposite to it the **grey crescent** forms (cortical rotation, about 30°) — this is the future **dorsal** side. At the time of sperm entry, the frog oocyte is at metaphase II.

### 6.2 Fertilization in higher animals — mammals (humans)

1. **Capacitation** — in the female reproductive tract (a few hours) cholesterol and the glycoprotein coat are removed from the sperm membrane, the membrane becomes unstable, and "hyperactivation" of motility appears. Only a capacitated sperm can fertilize (Chang and Austin, 1951).
2. **Penetration of the corona radiata** — by hyaluronidase (PH-20) on the sperm membrane.
3. **Binding to the zona pellucida** — in the classic mouse model **ZP3** is the primary sperm receptor and also induces the acrosome reaction; newer research (after 2014) also considers the N-terminus of **ZP2** a decisive binding site in humans/mice.
4. **Acrosome reaction** → penetration of the zona by **acrosin**.
5. **Membrane fusion** — the sperm protein **Izumo1** binds to the egg receptor **Juno** (discovered in 2014).
6. **Zona reaction** — cortical granule enzymes (e.g. ovastacin) cleave ZP2 → the zona hardens and other sperms cannot bind.
7. The secondary oocyte **completes meiosis II** → ovum + second polar body; male and female **pronuclei** form; in humans they do not fuse, but their chromosomes come together on the first cleavage spindle (syngamy).

- Site of fertilization: **ampullary–isthmic junction**. The ovum remains fertilizable for about 12–24 hours after ovulation, and sperms for about 48–72 hours in the female tract (some sources up to 5 days).
- Sex is determined by the sperm (X or Y).

### 6.3 Parthenogenesis

Development from an egg without fertilization.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Natural, arrhenotoky** — males from unfertilized eggs | **Honeybee drones** (haploid), wasps, ants |
| **Thelytoky** — only females | Some lizards (*Aspidoscelis*/whiptail), some insects |
| **Cyclic** — sexual reproduction and parthenogenesis alternate | **Aphids**, rotifers, *Daphnia* |
| **Artificial** | **Jacques Loeb** — sea urchin eggs with hypertonic sea water / butyric acid; **Bataillon** — frog eggs by pricking with a needle; parthenogenesis is also known in turkeys |

> **Remember:** In honeybees the queen and workers are **diploid (from fertilized eggs)**, drones are **haploid (from unfertilized eggs)** — sex determination by "haplodiploidy".

## 7. Types of eggs

### 7.1 Based on the amount of yolk

| Type | Yolk | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Alecithal / Microlecithal / Oligolecithal** | Very little or negligible | **Eutherian mammals (humans)**, *Amphioxus*, sea urchin, marsupials |
| **Mesolecithal** | Moderate | **Frog**, other amphibians, lungfish, *Petromyzon* |
| **Macrolecithal / Polylecithal / Megalecithal** | Very large | **Birds, reptiles, egg-laying mammals (Prototheria)**, sharks, most teleost fishes |

### 7.2 Based on the distribution of yolk

| Type | Distribution | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Isolecithal / Homolecithal** | Yolk evenly spread | Humans, *Amphioxus*, sea urchin |
| **Telolecithal** | Yolk towards the **vegetal pole**; nucleus and cytoplasm towards the **animal pole** | **Frog** (moderately telolecithal), **hen** (highly telolecithal) |
| **Centrolecithal** | Yolk in the centre, cytoplasm at the periphery | **Insects**, most arthropods |

**Based on the shell:** **Cleidoic egg** — hard shell, development on land (reptiles, birds, egg-laying mammals); **non-cleidoic** — development in water (fishes, amphibians).

**Based on development:** **Mosaic (determinate) eggs** — parts already determined (annelids, molluscs, ascidians); **regulative eggs** — a complete embryo forms even if early cells are separated (sea urchin, vertebrates).

> **Memory trick:** "**Man Micro, Frog Meso, Fowl Mega, Fly Centro**" — human = microlecithal, frog = mesolecithal, hen = megalecithal, fly/insect = centrolecithal.

## 8. Cleavage

Rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote with **no cell growth** — the total volume of the embryo stays about the same, and the cells (**blastomeres**) keep getting smaller; G1 and G2 phases are almost absent; the nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio increases.

- **Balfour's law**: the rate of cleavage is inversely proportional to the amount of yolk.
- **Cleavage planes**: **meridional** — passing through the animal–vegetal axis; **latitudinal** — parallel to the equator; **equatorial**; **vertical** — parallel to the axis but away from the centre.

### 8.1 Cleavage based on yolk

| Cleavage | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Holoblastic equal** | Whole egg, nearly equal blastomeres | *Amphioxus*, sea urchin, eutherian mammals (approximately) |
| **Holoblastic unequal** | Whole egg; small **micromeres** at the animal pole, large **macromeres** at the vegetal pole | **Frog** and other amphibians, *Petromyzon* |
| **Meroblastic discoidal** | Only the **blastodisc** at the animal pole divides | **Birds, reptiles**, teleost fishes, sharks, **cephalopods** (*Sepia*, *Loligo*) |
| **Meroblastic superficial** | Nuclei divide first (syncytium), then cells form at the periphery | **Insects** (*Drosophila*) |

### 8.2 Based on symmetry

| Pattern | Features | Examples | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | Upper cells lie exactly above the lower ones | Echinoderms, *Amphioxus*, amphibians | **Indeterminate** — deuterostomes |
| **Spiral** | Spindles oblique; upper tier rotated 45°, cells sit in the grooves | **Annelids, molluscs** (except cephalopods), Platyhelminthes, Nemertea | **Determinate** — protostomes; 4d cell → mesoderm |
| **Bilateral** | The first plane itself fixes the future bilateral symmetry | **Tunicates/ascidians** | Determinate |
| **Rotational** | In the second cleavage one blastomere divides meridionally and the other latitudinally | **Mammals**, nematodes (*C. elegans*) | Indeterminate in mammals |

**Features of mammalian cleavage:** very slow (each division 12–24 hours), **asynchronous**, rotational, **compaction** at the 8-cell stage; early genome activation (4–8 cell stage in humans). At about 16 cells **morula** → **blastocyst** (outer **trophoblast** + **inner cell mass**).

> **Exam angle:** "Determinate cleavage" = the fate of each blastomere is fixed in advance; removing one blastomere gives an incomplete embryo (mosaic development). "Indeterminate" = separating early blastomeres gives complete embryos (basis of identical twins; Driesch, 1892 — sea urchin).

## 9. Types of blastula

| Type | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Coeloblastula** | Single-layered wall, central blastocoel | *Amphioxus*, sea urchin; in frog a multi-layered roof and a blastocoel displaced into the animal hemisphere |
| **Stereoblastula** | No cavity | Some annelids, molluscs |
| **Discoblastula** | Flat **blastoderm** on the yolk; below it the **subgerminal cavity**; **area pellucida** and **area opaca** | Birds, reptiles, teleosts |
| **Periblastula** | Layer of cells around central yolk | Insects |
| **Blastocyst** | Trophoblast + inner cell mass + blastocoel | Mammals |

> **Common mistake:** "Amphiblastula" is not of amphibians; it is the larva of **sponges (*Sycon*)**.

## 10. Gastrulation

Orderly movement of blastula cells (**morphogenetic movements**), by which the three **germ layers** form, along with the **archenteron** and the **blastopore**.

| Movement | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Epiboly** | Spreading of the outer cell layer to cover the inner part | Animal hemisphere cells of frog, fishes, birds |
| **Emboly** | Collective name for all inward movements | — |
| **Invagination** | Inward pocketing of a layer like a sac | Sea urchin, *Amphioxus* |
| **Involution** | Outer layer turning in at an edge and rolling inside | **Dorsal lip** of frog |
| **Ingression** | Migration of individual cells inside | Primary mesenchyme of sea urchin; **primitive streak** of chick |
| **Delamination** | Splitting of one layer into two parallel layers | Formation of **hypoblast** in chick; mammalian inner cell mass |
| **Convergent extension** | Cells intercalate, making the tissue longer and narrower | Dorsal mesoderm (notochord) of frog |

**Fate of the blastopore:** **Protostomia** — blastopore → mouth (annelids, arthropods, molluscs); **Deuterostomia** — blastopore → anus (echinoderms, hemichordates, chordates).

### 10.1 Germ layer derivatives

| Ectoderm | Mesoderm | Endoderm |
|---|---|---|
| Epidermis of skin, hair, nails, sweat/sebaceous/mammary glands | Dermis below the epidermis | Epithelium of the alimentary canal (except the mouth and anal ends) |
| **Entire nervous system** (brain, spinal cord, nerves) | All muscles (some exceptions like iris muscles — ectodermal) | Glandular cells of **liver, pancreas** |
| **Lens** of the eye, corneal epithelium, retina (from the neural tube) | Bone, cartilage, connective tissue | Epithelium of **lungs / trachea** |
| Inner ear, olfactory epithelium | **Heart, blood, blood vessels**, lymph | **Thyroid, parathyroid, thymus** |
| **Adrenal medulla** (from the neural crest) | **Kidneys**, gonads, **adrenal cortex** | Epithelium of urinary bladder and urethra |
| Anterior and posterior pituitary, tooth enamel | Notochord, linings of the coelom, spleen | Epithelium of middle ear and Eustachian tube |

> **Remember:** The **neural crest** is called the "fourth germ layer" — it forms the peripheral nervous system, ganglia, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, facial cartilage/bone, and tooth dentine (odontoblasts).

### 10.2 Fate map

- **W. Vogt, 1929** — placed agar pieces containing **vital dyes** — **Nile blue sulphate, neutral red, Bismarck brown** — on the amphibian blastula, observed the position of the stained cells after gastrulation, and made a map of the future organs on the blastula. (Vogt's early work from 1925; the full map published in 1929.)
- Modern methods: fluorescent dextran, HRP injection, genetic lineage tracing (e.g. Cre-lox), quail–chick chimeras (Le Douarin).
- In the frog fate map: animal pole → epidermis, dorsal part → neural plate, marginal zone → mesoderm (notochord above the dorsal lip), vegetal part → endoderm.

### 10.3 Organiser and induction

- **Induction** — determination of the developmental path of one tissue (the responder — which has **competence**) by another tissue (the inducer).
- **Hans Spemann & Hilde Mangold, 1924** — transplanted the **dorsal lip of the blastopore** of the gastrula of one species of newt (*Triturus*) (unpigmented, *T. cristatus*) to the ventral side of the gastrula of another species (pigmented, *T. taeniatus*) → a **secondary embryo** formed (neural tube, notochord, somites), most of which came from host cells. Dorsal lip = **primary organiser**; Spemann received the **1935 Nobel Prize**.
- **Molecular basis:** the organiser secretes **BMP-4 antagonists** — **chordin, noggin, follistatin**; when BMP is blocked, the "default" fate of ectoderm is neural tissue (default model of neural induction). The organiser itself is induced by the **Nieuwkoop centre** (dorsal vegetal cells, β-catenin).
- **Equivalent organisers:** **Hensen's node** in birds, the **node** in mammals, the **embryonic shield** in fishes.
- **Secondary induction:** the **optic vesicle** induces the **lens** from the overlying ectoderm; the lens then induces the **cornea** (chain induction).

> **Remember:** "Primary organiser = dorsal lip of blastopore = Spemann–Mangold 1924 = Nobel 1935 (Spemann only; Mangold died in 1924)."

## 11. Differentiation

- **Differentiation** = cells with the same genome becoming specialised in structure and function. Its basis is **differential gene expression**, not gene loss — evidence: **John Gurdon, 1962** — nucleus of a frog (*Xenopus*) intestinal cell put into an enucleated egg gave a complete frog (nuclear transplantation); the sheep **Dolly (1996, Wilmut)**; **Yamanaka (2006)** — **iPS cells** from four factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc). Gurdon and Yamanaka received the 2012 Nobel.
- **Exceptions (real gene loss/change):** mammalian red blood cells (nucleus itself lost), **chromatin diminution** in *Ascaris* (Boveri), **V(D)J rearrangement** in B and T lymphocytes.
- **Two routes of cell-fate determination:** (1) **autonomous specification** — **cytoplasmic determinants** such as the yellow crescent of ascidians (Conklin), *bicoid* mRNA in *Drosophila*; (2) **conditional specification** — **positional signals** and induction (morphogen gradients).
- **Maintenance** of the differentiated state by epigenetic marks — **DNA methylation, histone modification**, chromatin remodelling.
- Differentiation is usually stable but **not irreversible**: **dedifferentiation** (salamander limb regeneration), **transdifferentiation** (lens from iris in newts — Wolffian regeneration), iPS reprogramming.
- **Potency:** totipotent (zygote, early blastomeres) → pluripotent (inner cell mass, ES cells) → multipotent (haematopoietic stem cell) → unipotent.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Consider the following statements regarding cellular differentiation —

> (II) In early development, cytoplasmic determinants and positional signals play a role in directing cell fate.
> (III) Once differentiated, all cells irreversibly lose the ability to change their fate under any circumstances.
> (IV) Epigenetic changes such as DNA methylation and histone modification contribute to maintaining the differentiated state.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Number of polar bodies:** from 1 primary oocyte, 1 ovum and **up to 3** polar bodies; if the question asks "from a secondary oocyte", only **1** polar body.
- **The human ovum is never "ovulated"** — what is ovulated is the **secondary oocyte**; the word "ovum" is correct only after meiosis II is completed (after fertilization).
- **ZP3 vs ZP2:** the textbook (mouse model) answer is ZP3; if only one is in the options, choose **ZP3**.
- **Frog cleavage is holoblastic unequal** (not meroblastic); the first and second cleavages are meridional, the third is **latitudinal, above the equator** (towards the animal pole).
- **Cephalopods, although molluscs,** show **meroblastic discoidal** cleavage, not spiral.
- **Cleavage in insects** is superficial — first about 13 nuclear divisions without cell walls (syncytial blastoderm).
- **Mammalian eggs are alecithal**, but eggs of egg-laying mammals (platypus, echidna) are **macrolecithal, with meroblastic cleavage**.
- **Inhibin inhibits only FSH**, not LH.
- **The prostate is unpaired**; seminal vesicles and Cowper's glands are paired.
- **Sertoli cells do not make testosterone** (they bind it with ABP); testosterone is made by Leydig cells.
- **Oestrous cycle vs menstrual cycle:** no menstrual bleeding in the oestrous cycle; the endometrium is reabsorbed.
- **The site of fertilization** is the ampulla, not the uterus; implantation in the oviduct = **ectopic pregnancy**.
- **"Amphiblastula"** is the larva of sponges, not the blastula of amphibians.

7. How many ova will form from 100 primary oocytes? — **100** (one from each).

10. What causes the fast block to polyspermy in sea urchin? — **Depolarisation of the egg membrane** (Na⁺ entry).
11. The fertilization membrane is formed by which reaction? — **Cortical reaction** (Ca²⁺-triggered exocytosis of cortical granules).
12. The process by which mammalian sperm become capable of fertilization in the female tract? — **Capacitation**.
13. First sign of the future dorsal side in the frog egg? — **Grey crescent** (opposite sperm entry).
14. Haploid members in honeybees? — **Drones (males)** (from unfertilized eggs).
15. Egg and cleavage of insects? — **Centrolecithal, superficial meroblastic**.

20. Who used vital dyes for the fate map? — **W. Vogt (1929)**.
