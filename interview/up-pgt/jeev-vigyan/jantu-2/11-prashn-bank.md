## 01 Nutrition and Digestion

1. ⭐ What converts trypsinogen into active trypsin? — **Enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** (from the intestinal mucosa).
2. ⭐ Function of bile salts? — **Emulsification of fats**, which increases the action of lipase.
3. ⭐ Into what are chylomicrons absorbed? — Into the **lacteal (lymph vessel) of the intestinal villus**.
4. Cells that secrete HCl in the stomach? — **Parietal / oxyntic cells** (also intrinsic factor).
5. Cells that secrete pepsinogen? — **Chief / peptic cells**.
6. ⭐ Which is the "hunger hormone" and where does it come from? — **Ghrelin**, from the stomach; **leptin** from adipose tissue signals satiety.
7. Hormone that causes gall bladder contraction and pancreatic enzyme secretion? — **CCK (cholecystokinin)**.
8. Hormone that causes secretion of HCO₃⁻-rich juice from the pancreas? — **Secretin** (the first hormone discovered, Bayliss–Starling 1902).
9. Phagocytic cells of the liver? — **Kupffer cells**.
10. Site of vitamin B₁₂ absorption? — **Ileum of the small intestine** (with intrinsic factor).
11. The "true" stomach of ruminants? — **Abomasum**.

## 02 Respiration, Gas Transport and Metabolism

12. ⭐ What detects low pO₂ at high altitude? — **Peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid and aortic bodies)**; central chemoreceptors are mainly sensitive to CO₂/H⁺.
13. ⭐ Bohr effect? — **Fall in the O₂-affinity of Hb when CO₂/H⁺ rises** — the ODC shifts to the right.
14. Haldane effect? — **Oxygenation lowers the CO₂-carrying capacity of Hb** (CO₂ released in the lungs).
15. In what form is most CO₂ transported? — **Bicarbonate (~70%)**; carbamino-Hb ~20–25%, dissolved ~7%.
16. In the chloride shift (Hamburger), what enters the RBC? — **Cl⁻** (HCO₃⁻ goes out).
17. Factors that shift the ODC to the left? — **Low CO₂, low H⁺, low temperature, low 2,3-BPG, foetal Hb (HbF)**.
18. Basic centre of respiratory rhythm? — **Pre-Bötzinger complex of the medulla**; pneumotaxic centre in the pons.
19. Normal tidal volume? — **About 500 mL**.
20. ATP from complete oxidation of one molecule of glucose (new count)? — **About 30–32 ATP** (old textbooks: 36–38).
21. Respiratory quotient (RQ) for fat? — **About 0.7** (carbohydrate 1.0, protein ~0.8).

## 03 Heart, Circulation, Blood and Lymph

22. ⭐ On what does mean arterial pressure depend? — **Cardiac output × total peripheral resistance**.
23. ⭐ How do plasma proteins maintain blood volume? — Through **colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure** (mainly albumin).
24. ⭐ What do baroreceptors do when arterial pressure rises suddenly? — **Increase vagal activity, lowering the SA node rate and heart rate**.
25. Pacemaker of the heart? — **SA node** (in the right atrium).
26. P wave in the ECG? — **Atrial depolarisation**; QRS — ventricular depolarisation; T — ventricular repolarisation.
27. Significance of the delay at the AV node? — **Gives the atria time to empty before the ventricles contract** (~0.1 s).
28. Duration of a normal cardiac cycle (at 72/minute)? — **0.8 second**.
29. What causes the "lub" sound? — **Closure of the AV (tricuspid, bicuspid) valves**; "dub" — semilunar valves.
30. Starling's law of the heart? — **Greater venous return → greater stretch → greater force of contraction**.
31. Universal donor blood group? — **O Rh⁻**; universal recipient — AB Rh⁺.
32. In blood clotting, what converts fibrinogen into fibrin? — **Thrombin**.
33. In which animals does complete double circulation occur? — **Birds and mammals** (4-chambered heart).

## 04 Osmoregulation and Excretion

34. ⭐ In what form does a desert vertebrate excrete nitrogen to save water? — **Uric acid (uricotelic)**.
35. Examples of ammonotelic animals? — **Bony fishes, tadpoles, aquatic invertebrates**.
36. Examples of ureotelic animals? — **Mammals, adult amphibians, cartilaginous fishes** (sharks retain urea in the blood).
37. Where does the urea cycle take place? — **Liver** (partly in mitochondria, partly in cytoplasm; Krebs–Henseleit).
38. ⭐ What forms the counter-current multiplier? — **Loop of Henle**; vasa recta is the counter-current exchanger.
39. Feature of the ascending limb of Henle's loop? — **Impermeable to water, active removal of NaCl** (thick part).
40. Effect of ADH? — Increases **aquaporin-2 in the collecting duct**, raising water reabsorption.
41. Where does aldosterone act? — **Distal tubule/collecting duct — Na⁺ reabsorption, K⁺ secretion**.
42. Source of renin? — **Juxtaglomerular cells** (JGA).
43. Normal GFR? — **About 125 mL/minute (~180 L/day)**.
44. How does a marine bony fish osmoregulate? — **Drinks sea water, excretes salt through the gills, passes a little concentrated urine**.

## 05 Movement and Locomotion

45. ⭐ Molecular mechanism of skeletal muscle contraction? — **ATP-driven actin–myosin sliding filament** — Huxley and Hanson (1954).
46. Which band does not shorten during contraction? — **A band** (I band and H zone shorten).
47. To what does Ca²⁺ bind to expose the binding sites on actin? — **Troponin-C** (tropomyosin moves away).
48. Store of Ca²⁺? — **Sarcoplasmic reticulum**.
49. Cause of rigor mortis? — **Lack of ATP** — myosin heads do not detach from actin.
50. Example of a pivot joint? — **Atlas–axis**.
51. Saddle joint? — **Carpometacarpal joint of the thumb**.
52. White (fast-twitch) muscle fibres have? — **Less myoglobin, fewer mitochondria, more anaerobic respiration**.

## 06 Neural Coordination and Sense Organs

53. ⭐ Why is the action potential "all-or-none"? — **Full depolarisation occurs as soon as the threshold is reached**; the amplitude does not change with stimulus strength.
54. Resting membrane potential? — **About −70 mV**.
55. Depolarisation is due to entry of which ion? — **Na⁺**; repolarisation — K⁺ efflux.
56. Ratio of the Na⁺–K⁺ pump? — **3 Na⁺ out : 2 K⁺ in**, 1 ATP.
57. Conduction in a myelinated fibre? — **Saltatory** — jumping from one node of Ranvier to the next.
58. Neurotransmitter at the neuromuscular junction? — **Acetylcholine**.
59. Centre for body temperature and hunger? — **Hypothalamus**.
60. Colour-vision cells in the retina? — **Cones**; in dim light **rods** — rhodopsin.
61. Blind spot? — **The point where the optic nerve leaves** (no rods or cones).
62. Receptor organ of hearing? — **Organ of Corti** (on the basilar membrane of the cochlea).
63. Receptors of balance (gravity/linear acceleration)? — **Maculae of the utricle and saccule**; rotation — cristae of the semicircular canals.

## 07 Hormones, Pheromones, Vitamins and Animal Behaviour

64. ⭐ What do glucocorticoids do during stress? — **Make glucose from protein/amino acids in the liver (and kidney) — gluconeogenesis**.
65. ⭐ Innate behaviour? — **Genetically programmed, uniform in all members of the species** (not learned from experience).
66. ⭐ Sequence of events in a migratory bird? — **Fat accumulation by hyperphagia → metabolic and muscle adjustment → fat mobilisation → directional flight using environmental cues**.
67. ⭐ Primary environmental cue for bird migration? — **Photoperiod** — through endocrine control.
68. ⭐ Sex pheromone of the silkworm moth? — **Bombykol** (Butenandt, 1959 — the first pheromone identified).
69. Pheromone of the honeybee queen? — **Queen substance (9-oxodecenoic acid)** — suppresses the ovaries of workers.
70. Two main types of pheromones? — **Releaser (immediate behaviour)** and **primer (long-term physiological change)**.
71. In which animals are the Lee–Boot and Whitten effects seen? — **In mice** (primer pheromones); Bruce effect — abortion due to the smell of an unfamiliar male.
72. Where are ADH and oxytocin made? — **Hypothalamus** (posterior pituitary only stores and releases them).
73. ⭐ Main function of hCG? — **Maintenance of the corpus luteum**.
74. Rickets is due to deficiency of which vitamin? — **Vitamin D (calciferol)**.
75. Beri-beri? — **Thiamine (B₁)**; pellagra — niacin (B₃); scurvy — vitamin C.
76. Vitamin needed for blood clotting? — **Vitamin K** (for γ-carboxylation of prothrombin etc. in the liver).
77. Night blindness? — Deficiency of **vitamin A (retinol)**.

## 08 Immune System and Enzymes

78. ⭐ The largest antibody, which does not cross the placenta? — **IgM** (pentamer; made first in the primary response).
79. Antibody that crosses the placenta? — **IgG**.
80. ⭐ To whom does MHC-I present antigen? — **CD8⁺ (cytotoxic) T cells**; MHC-II → CD4⁺ helper T.
81. Which cells does HIV destroy? — **CD4⁺ helper T cells**.
82. Antibody of allergy (type I hypersensitivity)? — **IgE** (histamine from mast cells).
83. What kind of disease is rheumatoid arthritis? — **Autoimmune**.
84. Principle of a vaccine? — **Making memory cells** — active acquired immunity (Jenner, 1796 — smallpox).
85. ⭐ Meaning of Km? — **The substrate concentration at which velocity is half of Vmax**; low Km = higher affinity.
86. ⭐ In competitive inhibition? — **Km increases, Vmax unchanged** (e.g. malonate on succinic dehydrogenase).
87. In non-competitive inhibition? — **Vmax decreases, Km unchanged**.
88. Class 1 of enzyme classification (IUBMB)? — **Oxidoreductases**; class 6 ligases (class 7 translocases, added in 2018).
89. Protein part + cofactor = ? — **Holoenzyme**; protein part alone — apoenzyme.
90. Loosely bound organic cofactors? — **Coenzymes**, e.g. NAD⁺; tightly bound — prosthetic groups (e.g. haem).

## 09 Human Reproductive System, Gametogenesis, Fertilisation and Cleavage

91. ⭐ Primary basis of differentiation? — **Differential gene expression** (not gene loss); epigenetic marks maintain the state.
92. ⭐ At what stage does human ovulation occur? — **Secondary oocyte, arrested at metaphase II**.
93. Cells that secrete testosterone? — **Leydig (interstitial) cells** — under LH/ICSH.
94. What does inhibin inhibit? — **FSH only**.
95. ⭐ Fast and slow block in the sea urchin? — **Fast = membrane depolarisation (Na⁺); slow = Ca²⁺-driven cortical reaction, fertilisation membrane**.
96. Sperm receptor of the mammalian egg coat (textbook)? — **ZP3**.
97. ⭐ Frog egg and cleavage? — **Mesolecithal telolecithal; holoblastic unequal**.
98. Cleavage in birds? — **Meroblastic discoidal**.
99. ⭐ Spiral determinate cleavage? — **Annelids, molluscs (protostomes)**.
100. Primary organiser? — **Dorsal lip of the blastopore** — Spemann and Mangold, 1924.
101. Fate map with vital dyes? — **Vogt, 1929**.
102. From what does a honeybee drone develop? — **Unfertilised egg (parthenogenesis)**.

## 10 Frog Development, Embryonic Membranes, Placenta and Regeneration

103. ⭐ Which membrane carries out excretion and respiratory exchange in the chick? — **Allantois** (vascular interface with the chorion — CAM); amnion is the fluid-filled protection.
104. ⭐ Hormone that induces frog metamorphosis, and its antagonist? — **Thyroxine (T₃/T₄) induces; prolactin opposes**.
105. Who showed the role of the thyroid in metamorphosis? — **Gudernatsch (1912)**.
106. The axolotl is an example of? — **Neoteny** (can metamorphose with thyroxine).
107. ⭐ Human placenta? — **Discoidal, haemochorial, deciduate, chorioallantoic**.
108. Placenta of cow/sheep? — **Cotyledonary, syndesmochorial, non-deciduate**.
109. ⭐ Feature that separates eutherians from marsupials? — **Complex, long-lasting (chorioallantoic) placenta**.
110. Positive feedback in childbirth? — **Oxytocin** (foetal ejection reflex).
111. Milk production / ejection? — **Prolactin / oxytocin**.
112. Regeneration in *Hydra* and the salamander limb? — **Morphallaxis / epimorphosis (blastema)**.

## Answering strategy for +3 / −1 marking

In the new pattern, a correct answer gets **+3**, a wrong answer or filling more than one option gets **−1**, and leaving it gets **0**. Expected value (EV) of a guess:

<div class="formula">

- **Two options eliminated** (guessing from the remaining 2): EV = (3 − 1) / 2 = **+1** → definitely answer.
- **One option eliminated** (guessing from the remaining 3): EV = (3 − 2) / 3 = **+0.33** → small gain; answer if you have a grip on the topic.
- **No option eliminated** (blind guess): EV = (3 − 3) / 4 = **0** → no gain; leave it.

</div>

- **Statement-combination questions:** first find the statement that is definitely wrong (e.g. statement III of Q60) — as soon as one statement is wrong, two options usually get cut, and the EV reaches +1.
- **Integrated questions (e.g. Q12, Q62):** check both parts of each option; if even one part is wrong, the whole option is wrong. Strike out phrases like "ATP-independent", "Ca²⁺-independent contraction", "central chemoreceptors directly detect O₂" at once.
- **Matching (e.g. Q38):** start with the most certain pair (enterokinase → trypsinogen activation); often two options are cut by that alone.
- **Time:** 120 questions / 120 minutes — direct questions from this book in 30–40 seconds, integrated questions up to 90 seconds; in the first round all the certain questions, in the second round the "two options cut" ones.
- **OMR:** filling more than one bubble is also −1; do not let a situation arise where you have to change an answer — first mark on the question booklet, then fill all together.

## Traps and exceptions (from the whole book)

- Central chemoreceptors are sensitive to CO₂/H⁺, not O₂ (wrong option A of Q62).
- When pressure rises, baroreceptors increase **parasympathetic (vagal), not sympathetic** activity (wrong option B of Q29).
- Urea is not excreted in solid form; solid/semi-solid **uric acid** is (wrong option D of Q12).
- Muscle contraction is Ca²⁺- and ATP-dependent; it is not by "passive elastic recoil".
- The chorion is the outermost membrane; oviparous amniotes have no placenta (Q9).
- Ovulation is due to LH, not hCG (Q39).
- Differentiated cells can change their fate — dedifferentiation, transdifferentiation, iPS (Q60).
- Innate behaviour can also be **triggered** by environmental cues (e.g. photoperiod); calling it "unchangeable" or "hormone-free" is wrong (Q31).
- Glucocorticoids do not increase insulin; rather they raise blood glucose and reduce glucose use in muscle (Q24).

## Quick revision

1. Enterokinase → trypsinogen; bile salts → emulsification; lacteal → chylomicrons.
2. Ghrelin (stomach) hunger; leptin (fat) satiety; digestive secretion via the vagus.
3. Altitude — peripheral chemoreceptors; hyperventilation → low CO₂ → alkalosis → Hb affinity rises.
4. MAP = CO × TPR; oncotic pressure — albumin.
5. Baroreceptors — vagus ↑, heart rate ↓.
6. Uric acid — water conservation (birds, reptiles, insects).
7. Sliding filament — ATP + Ca²⁺; A band constant.
8. All-or-none — full depolarisation at threshold.
9. Glucocorticoids — gluconeogenesis.
10. Pheromones — releaser and primer; bombykol the first.
11. Km — substrate concentration at Vmax/2; in competitive inhibition Km ↑.
12. IgG crosses the placenta; IgA colostrum; IgE allergy; IgM first.
13. Differentiation — differential gene expression + epigenetic maintenance.
14. Ovulation — secondary oocyte (MII).
15. Frog — holoblastic unequal; bird — discoidal; insect — superficial; mammal — rotational.
16. Organiser — dorsal lip (1924); fate map — Vogt (1929).
17. Metamorphosis — T₃/T₄ (+), prolactin (−); Gudernatsch 1912.
18. Allantois + chorion — respiration/excretion; amnion — protection.
19. Human placenta — discoidal, haemochorial; hCG — corpus luteum.
20. Regeneration — *Hydra* morphallaxis, salamander epimorphosis, liver compensatory.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. In the Michaelis–Menten equation, when [S] = Km, the velocity is? — **Vmax/2** (by definition).
2. Km and Vmax in uncompetitive inhibition? — **Both decrease** (the inhibitor binds only to the ES complex).
3. Aquaporin-2 is controlled by which hormone? — **ADH (vasopressin)**.
4. Why do sharks retain urea in the blood? — **Osmotic balance** (to stay isosmotic with sea water; along with TMAO).
5. Shape of the ODC of myoglobin? — **Hyperbolic** — that of Hb is sigmoid.
6. In birds, what aids ventilation in addition to the lungs? — **Air sacs** — unidirectional airflow.
7. Which sperm protein binds Juno on the egg? — **Izumo1**.
8. The most abundant plasma protein? — **Albumin** (made in the liver).
9. Brain part that controls the secretion of the pituitary gland? — **Hypothalamus** (through releasing and inhibiting hormones; so calling the pituitary the "master gland" is incomplete).
10. Who demonstrated imprinting? — **Konrad Lorenz** (goslings; Nobel 1973 — with Tinbergen and Frisch).
11. Who discovered the "waggle dance" of the honeybee? — **Karl von Frisch** (signals direction and distance of food).
12. Dermatome, myotome and sclerotome are parts of what? — **Somites (dorsal/paraxial mesoderm)**.
13. Why does cell size decrease during cleavage? — **No G1/G2, no growth** — only division.
14. Antibody that crosses the placenta and gives passive immunity to the newborn? — **IgG**.
15. Neurotransmitter that slows the heartbeat in humans? — **Acetylcholine (from the vagus, muscarinic M2 receptors)**.
16. Effect of parathormone? — **Raises blood Ca²⁺** (bone resorption, renal reabsorption, vitamin D activation).
17. Result of loss of immune "tolerance"? — **Autoimmune disease** (e.g. type 1 diabetes, myasthenia gravis).
18. Where does the allantois arise from in the chick? — **Ventral outgrowth of the hindgut** — splanchnopleure.
