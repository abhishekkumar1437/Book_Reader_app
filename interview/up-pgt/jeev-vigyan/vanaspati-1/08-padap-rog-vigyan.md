This chapter completes the syllabus point "Plant pathology — causes, effects, control and treatment; biological control of weeds, diseases and parasites" from NCERT (Class 12 "Microbes in Human Welfare" — biocontrol) to B.Sc. plant pathology.
In 2026 only **one question** came from this (management of a virus disease with mosaic-leaf curl — case based), but the 2013 paper asked about black rust of wheat; UP is an agricultural state (sugarcane, wheat, potato, mango), so keep disease-pathogen matching and control principles ready.

> **In simple words:** A plant is called "diseased" when some agent continuously disturbs its normal functions. Most diseases are caused by fungi, bacteria, viruses, phytoplasmas or nematodes; some by nutrient deficiency or pollution. A disease spreads only when a susceptible plant, a virulent pathogen and favourable weather are all present together. For prevention, a combined use (IPM) of disease-free seed, crop rotation, resistant varieties, chemicals and beneficial organisms is best.

## 2. Concept of disease and terminology

- **Disease:** **continuous** disturbance of the normal physiology of a plant by a causal agent; **injury** — a momentary agent (e.g. insect bite, hail).
- **Disease triangle:** **susceptible host + virulent pathogen + favourable environment**; adding **time** gives the **disease tetrahedron/pyramid**, and **humans** are also added.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pathogenicity | Ability to cause disease (qualitative) |
| Virulence | Quantitative ability to cause disease / ability to make a particular variety diseased |
| Obligate parasite (biotroph) | Only on living tissue — rusts, powdery mildews, downy mildews, *Albugo*, viruses |
| Necrotroph | Kills tissue and feeds on it — *Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Sclerotium*, *Alternaria* |
| Facultative parasite / facultative saprophyte | Usually one, occasionally the other |
| Inoculum | The part of the pathogen that causes infection — primary (first of the season) and secondary |
| Disease cycle | Dissemination → contact → penetration → infection → incubation → symptoms → sporulation → survival |
| Monocyclic | One cycle in a season — loose smut, wilts (soil-borne) |
| Polycyclic | Many cycles in a season — rusts, late blight, blast |
| Endemic, epidemic (epiphytotic), pandemic, sporadic | Levels of spread of a disease |
| Signs vs symptoms | Signs = the pathogen itself is visible (spores, mycelium, sclerotia); symptoms = reaction of the host |

## 3. Koch's postulates

**Robert Koch** (1876 — anthrax; 1882 — tuberculosis); also applicable to plant diseases:

1. The pathogen must be **always** present in **all diseased** plants (not in healthy ones).
2. It must be **isolated and grown in pure culture** and its characters recorded.
3. Inoculating a **healthy susceptible** plant with the pure culture must produce **the same disease**.
4. The pathogen must be **re-isolated** from the artificially diseased plant and be the same as the original.

- **Exceptions/limitations:** obligate parasites (**rusts, powdery mildews, viruses, phytoplasmas, many nematodes**) do not grow on artificial media — for them **modified postulates** (inoculation from infected tissue/vector in place of pure culture); **Rivers's postulates (1937)** for viruses; **molecular Koch's postulates (Falkow, 1988)** — for pathogenicity genes; complex diseases (by several pathogens), symptomless carriers.

## 7. Disease resistance and epidemiology

- **Defence mechanisms:** **pre-existing** — cuticle, wax, thick walls, phenolic compounds, saponins (avenacin in oats); **induced** — **hypersensitive response (HR)** — rapid death of cells at the infection site; **phytoalexins** (Müller and Börger, 1940 — in potato; pisatin in pea, glyceollin in soybean); PR proteins (chitinase, glucanase); lignin, callose deposition.
- **SAR (systemic acquired resistance):** broad resistance in the whole plant after a local infection — **salicylic acid** signal; **ISR (induced systemic resistance)** — by **beneficial root bacteria (PGPR)**, **jasmonic acid/ethylene** signal.
- **Gene-for-gene (Flor):** for each resistance (R) gene of the host there is a corresponding **avirulence (Avr)** gene in the pathogen; R + Avr = resistance (incompatible reaction).
- **Vertical (race-specific)** — one/a few major genes (oligogenic), complete but broken by a new race ("boom and bust cycle"); **horizontal (race non-specific)** — many minor genes (polygenic), partial but durable.
- **Multiline varieties** — **Jensen (1952), Borlaug** — a mixture of uniform lines with different resistance genes.
- **Disease forecasting:**
  - Potato late blight — **Dutch rules (Van Everdingen, 1926)** — night dew ≥4 hours, minimum temperature ≥10 °C, mean cloudiness ≥0.8, rainfall ≥0.1 mm in the next 24 hours; **Beaumont period (1947, UK)** — 48 consecutive hours with minimum temperature ≥10 °C and humidity ≥75%; in India the **JHULSACAST** (CPRI) model.
  - Wheat rusts — spore monitoring in the hills.
- **Loss assessment:** **ETL (economic threshold level)** — the level at which control should start so that the **EIL (economic injury level)** is not reached; ETL < EIL.

## 8. Principles of control — Whetzel (1929)

| Principle | Aim | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Exclusion** | Preventing the pathogen from entering a new area | **Quarantine**, certified seed |
| **Eradication** | Destroying a pathogen that has already arrived | Uprooting infected plants (roguing), destroying alternate/collateral hosts, crop rotation, soil solarisation, fumigation |
| **Protection** | A chemical/physical barrier between host and pathogen | Protective fungicide sprays, vector control |
| **Resistance (immunisation)** | Resistant varieties; immunisation | Sr/Lr/Xa genes, cross protection, SAR inducers |
| **Therapy** | Treating the infected plant | Heat therapy, systemic fungicides, meristem culture |
| **Avoidance** | Choosing a time/place when the disease does not occur | Changing sowing date, seed plot technique |

### 8.1 Regulatory — quarantine

- **Destructive Insects and Pests Act (DIP Act), 1914** — India's basic quarantine law; **Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order, 2003**; DPPQS, Faridabad.
- **Domestic quarantine:** potato wart (Darjeeling), golden cyst nematode (Nilgiri, Kodaikanal), banana bunchy top, San Jose scale, coffee berry borer etc.
- Examples of diseases that entered India from outside: late blight of potato (from Europe, around the 1870s), downy mildew of grapes, coffee rust, banana bunchy top (from Sri Lanka), flag smut of wheat.

### 8.2 Cultural methods

- **Crop rotation** (for soil-borne pathogens — wilts, nematodes); **field sanitation** — burning/burying diseased debris; **roguing**.
- **Destroying alternate/collateral hosts** — barberry eradication in the USA.
- **Deep summer ploughing**; adjusting **sowing date**; **seed rate/spacing** (humidity in a dense crop); **drainage**; **balanced fertiliser** (excess N increases blast, BLB; K increases resistance).
- **Soil solarisation** — **Katan (1976)**; covering moist soil with transparent polythene in summer for 4–6 weeks — soil temperature 45–55 °C.
- **Trap/intercrops** — marigold (nematodes), trap crops for *Striga*; **flooding**; **organic amendments** (neem cake, mustard cake — nematodes).
- **Resistant varieties** — the cheapest and most eco-friendly measure.

### 8.3 Physical methods

- **Hot water treatment:** **loose smut of wheat** — (Jensen) 52 °C for ~10 minutes (after pre-soaking); **solar heat treatment (Luthra)** — soaking seed for 4 hours and drying in the afternoon sun (May–June).
- **Sugarcane setts:** grassy shoot — **moist hot air 54 °C, 2.5 hours**; ratoon stunting — hot water 50 °C, 2 hours.
- **Thermotherapy** — virus-free plants; **radiation**, refrigeration/controlled storage; **meristem culture**.

| Group | Chemical | Use / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper (inorganic)** | **Bordeaux mixture** — CuSO₄ + quicklime (usually **1% — 1:1:100**, i.e. 1 kg CuSO₄ : 1 kg lime : 100 L water); **Burgundy mixture** (CuSO₄ + Na₂CO₃); **Cheshunt compound** (CuSO₄ + ammonium carbonate — damping off); copper oxychloride | Protective; downy mildews, leaf spots, cankers; **Bordeaux paste** (10%) — bark wounds |

| Mercurials (now banned) | Ceresan, agallol | Earlier seed treatment |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A field has widespread mosaic, leaf curl and stunting. Despite fungicides the disease is spreading rapidly. The most effective management? (A) More nitrogen to increase resistance (B) Removing infected plants and controlling insect vectors (C) Broad-spectrum fungicides at regular intervals (D) Deep ploughing to destroy dormant pathogens — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**; the symptoms are of a virus disease (e.g. whitefly-borne leaf curl) — fungicides are useless on viruses, and soft growth from excess N attracts vectors even more.

### 8.8 Integrated pest management (IPM)

- **Definition (FAO):** a compatible combination of all suitable techniques (cultural, mechanical, biological, chemical, resistant varieties) so that pest numbers stay **below the economic injury level**, with minimum harm to the environment.
- The "integrated control" concept — **Stern, Smith, van den Bosch, Hagen (1959)**.
- **Components:** monitoring (field surveys, pheromone/light/yellow sticky traps), **ETL-based decisions**, resistant varieties, cultural methods, mechanical (hand picking, nets), conservation/augmentation of biocontrol agents, selective chemicals **as a last resort**.
- **Benefits:** pesticide residues decrease, less resistance/resurgence, natural enemies protected, lower cost.
- **India:** **NIPHM, Hyderabad** (National Institute of Plant Health Management); **Central IPM Centres (CIPMCs)**; **Farmer Field Schools (FFS)**; Sikkim — the first fully organic state (2016).

## Traps and exceptions

- Late blight — *Phytophthora* (Oomycete); early — *Alternaria* (imperfect fungus) — reversing them is a common mistake.
- Bengal famine — **brown spot** (*Helminthosporium*), not blast; Irish famine — late blight.
- Khaira = zinc deficiency; whiptail = molybdenum; browning/hollow stem = boron; black heart = oxygen deficiency; black tip of mango = brick kiln smoke.
- Powdery mildew is an **ectoparasite** (upper surface), downy mildew an **endoparasite** (lower surface).
- Metalaxyl only on Oomycetes; carbendazim ineffective on Oomycetes.
- Carboxin — the first systemic fungicide; Bordeaux mixture — the first widely used fungicide (Millardet).
- Fungicides/antibiotics useless on virus diseases; vector control + roguing.
- Streptocycline — bacteria; tetracycline — phytoplasma.
- Koch's postulates cannot be fulfilled for obligate parasites (rusts, viruses, phytoplasma).
- *Dactylopius ceylonicus* (India) — the first successful biological control of a weed; *Cactoblastis* (Australia) — the most famous; *Rodolia* — the first successful biological control of an insect.
- *Zygogramma* — parthenium; *Neochetina* — water hyacinth; *Cyrtobagous* — salvinia.
- ETL is always lower than EIL.
- The cause of Karnal bunt is *Tilletia indica* — though a "bunt", it is partial; not the whole grain.
