This chapter goes from NCERT Class 11 "Biological Classification" (Kingdom Fungi, lichens, mycorrhiza) to B.Sc. Mycology — Ainsworth's classification and the life cycles of nine type genera.
In 2026 there were **3 questions on lichens** (reproduction and classification, SO₂ indicator, mutualism), and lichen diversity was mentioned in one question on pollution; there was no direct question on fungal types, but in older UP papers (2013) facts such as the source of penicillin and black rust of wheat have come repeatedly — so keep both life cycles and economic facts ready.

> **In simple words:** Fungi (moulds, yeasts, mushrooms) are chlorophyll-free eukaryotes that digest food outside and absorb it. Their body is a network (mycelium) of fine threads (hyphae), whose wall is made of chitin. A lichen is a partnership of a fungus and an alga/cyanobacterium — the alga makes food, the fungus gives water-minerals and protection. Mycorrhiza is a partnership of a fungus and a plant root.

## 1. General characters

- **Eukaryotic, chlorophyll-free, heterotrophic** — **absorptive** nutrition: they secrete extracellular enzymes and digest food outside.
- Types of nutrition: **saprophytic** (decaying matter — *Mucor*, *Agaricus*), **parasitic** — obligate (*Puccinia*, *Albugo*, powdery mildews) or facultative (*Alternaria*, *Fusarium*), **symbiotic** (lichens, mycorrhiza), **predatory** (*Arthrobotrys* — nematode-trapping rings).
- **Thallus:** unicellular (yeast) or **hyphal**; hyphae **aseptate, multinucleate — coenocytic** (*Mucor*, *Albugo*) or **septate** (Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes). In Basidiomycetes **dolipores** in the septa and **clamp connections**.
- **Cell wall: chitin** (a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine) + glucans; **cellulose in Oomycetes** (so these are now not "true fungi").
- **Stored food: glycogen, oil** (no starch).
- Hyphal modifications: **haustoria** (parasites — into host cells), **rhizomorphs** (*Armillaria*), **sclerotia** (hard resting bodies — *Claviceps*, *Sclerotium*), **stroma**, plectenchyma (prosenchyma, pseudoparenchyma).
- **Growth:** hyphae grow **at the tip** — the **Spitzenkörper** — a centre of vesicles; optimum temperature 20–30 °C, acidic pH (~5–6), moisture.

## 2. Reproduction

| Type | Methods |
|---|---|
| **Vegetative** | Fragmentation, fission (*Schizosaccharomyces*), **budding** (*Saccharomyces*), **sclerotia**, rhizomorphs |
| **Asexual spores** | **Zoospores** — *Albugo*, *Phytophthora*, *Pythium* (Oomycetes, biflagellate); **sporangiospores** — *Mucor*, *Rhizopus* (non-motile, endogenous); **conidia** — exogenous, on conidiophores (*Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Alternaria*); **chlamydospores** — thick-walled (*Fusarium*, smut fungi); **oidia/arthrospores** — fragments of hyphae |
| **Sexual — three stages** | **Plasmogamy** (fusion of cytoplasm) → (in many a **dikaryotic stage n+n** — Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes) → **karyogamy** (nuclear fusion, 2n) → **meiosis** → haploid spores |
| **Methods of plasmogamy** | (i) **Planogametic copulation** — chytrids; (ii) **gametangial contact** — through an antheridial tube — *Albugo*, *Phytophthora*, *Penicillium*; (iii) **gametangial copulation** — *Mucor*, *Rhizopus* (zygospore); (iv) **spermatisation** — spermatia with receptive hyphae — *Puccinia*, *Neurospora*; (v) **somatogamy** — two vegetative hyphae — *Agaricus*, most Basidiomycetes |
| **Sexual spores** | **Oospores** (Oomycetes), **zygospores** (Zygomycetes), **ascospores** (in an ascus, endogenous, usually 8), **basidiospores** (on a basidium, exogenous, usually 4) |

- **Heterothallism:** **A.F. Blakeslee (1904)** — in *Mucor*; two compatible strains (+ and −) needed. **Homothallic** — a single mycelium is self-fertile.
- **Parasexuality:** **Pontecorvo and Roper (1952)**, *Aspergillus nidulans* — without a true sexual cycle, **heterokaryosis → fusion of vegetative nuclei (diploid) → mitotic crossing over → haploidisation**; a source of variation in Deuteromycetes.
- **Heterokaryosis** — genetically different nuclei in one mycelium.

## 4. *Albugo candida* — white rust of crucifers

- **Class Oomycetes**; **obligate parasite**; on mustard, rai, radish, cabbage.
- **Mycelium:** intercellular, aseptate, multinucleate; small **knob-like haustoria** in host cells.
- **Symptoms:** white, shiny, raised **pustules** on leaves, stems; **hypertrophy** and distortion of the inflorescence — "**staghead**" — floral parts thick, green, sterile.
- **Asexual:** **club-shaped sporangiophores** in a row (palisade) below the epidermis; at the tip of each, **sporangia in a basipetal chain**, with a **disjunctor pad** between them; white powder when the epidermis ruptures; in water a sporangium gives **4–12 biflagellate, kidney-shaped zoospores** (one tinsel, one whiplash) — or germinates directly (depending on temperature).
- **Sexual (oogamous):** **oogonium** (central **ooplasm** inside — one egg, **periplasm** outside) + **antheridium**; one male nucleus through the **fertilisation tube** of the antheridium — **gametangial contact**; **oospore** — thick, **warty** wall; **perennation** in host tissue.
- Oospore germination → zoospores in a vesicle. Oomycetes are considered **diploid** — meiosis in the gametangia (gametic meiosis).
- **Control:** crop rotation, destroying diseased debris, **metalaxyl** seed treatment + **mancozeb** spray, resistant varieties.

## 5. *Mucor* — pin mould

- **Zygomycetes**; **saprophytic** — moist bread, dung (*M. mucedo* — "dung fungus"), rotting fruits; white cottony mycelium, then black "pins".
- **Mycelium:** aseptate, multinucleate, branched; (difference from *Rhizopus*: *Mucor* has **no stolons and rhizoids**; *Rhizopus* has stolons, rhizoids, and sporangiophores in clusters above the rhizoids).
- **Asexual:** a round **sporangium** at the tip of an upright **sporangiophore**; inside, a dome-shaped **columella**; many non-motile **sporangiospores** (aplanospores); released by dissolving/rupture of the wall; **chlamydospores**, **oidia**, and in sugar-rich liquid a yeast-like budding stage — the **torula stage** (*M. racemosus*).
- **Sexual:** most **heterothallic** (*M. mucedo* — **Blakeslee, 1904**; + and − strains); from two compatible hyphae **progametangia** → by a septum, **gametangia** + **suspensors** → **gametangial copulation** → **zygospore** — thick, black, **warty** wall; after rest, **meiosis** → on germination a **germ sporangium** → spores (+ or −). **Zygotic meiosis** — haplontic cycle.
- **Importance:** food spoilage; rennet-like enzymes from *Mucor* (cheese); *Rhizopus* — fumaric acid, cortisone bioconversion, tempeh (*R. oligosporus*); **mucormycosis ("black fungus")** — *Rhizopus*, *Mucor* — in diabetic/steroid-treated patients (outbreak in India in the 2021 COVID period).
- *Pilobolus* — "shotgun fungus", throws its sporangium up to 2 m (phototropic).

## 6. *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* — yeast

- **Hemiascomycetes**; **unicellular** (pseudomycelium — a chain of buds); oval cell, large vacuole, glycogen and fat granules.
- **Vegetative: multipolar budding**; **fission** in *Schizosaccharomyces*.
- **Sexual:** two mating types **a and α** (mating by pheromones); vegetative cells themselves act as gametes; fusion → diploid cell → meiosis → **4 ascospores in an ascus**; **no fruiting body (ascocarp) — naked ascus**.
- **Three types of life cycle (Guilliermond, 1940):**

| Type | Dominant stage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Haplobiontic | Haploid; zygote immediately meiotic | ***Schizosaccharomyces octosporus*** |
| Diplobiontic | Diploid; ascospores fuse immediately | ***Saccharomycodes ludwigii*** |
| Haplodiplobiontic | Both stages multiply by budding | ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** |

- **Fermentation:** the **zymase** enzyme complex (**Eduard Buchner, 1897** — cell-free fermentation; Nobel 1907) — glucose → ethanol + CO₂; **Louis Pasteur** — fermentation by living yeast.
- **Uses:** **baking** (bread rises by CO₂), **brewing** — beer (*S. cerevisiae*, lager — *S. pastorianus*), wine (*S. ellipsoideus* — a form of *S. cerevisiae*), **industrial ethanol** (from sugarcane molasses — ethanol blending), **SCP**, B-group vitamins, **recombinant vaccines** (hepatitis B vaccine — the first recombinant vaccine, 1986).
- **Model organism:** **the first completely sequenced eukaryotic genome (1996)** — ~12 Mb, **16 chromosomes**, ~6,000 genes; cell-cycle research (Hartwell — Nobel 2001).

## 7. *Penicillium* — blue-green mould

- **Plectomycetes** (Eurotiales); saprophytic — rotting lemons/oranges, bread, leather; ~300 species.
- **Mycelium:** septate, branched; each cell uninucleate/multinucleate.
- **Asexual — conidia:** at the tip of an upright **conidiophore**, **brush-like (penicillus — "little brush")** branches — **rami → metulae → phialides (sterigmata)** → **conidia in basipetal chains** (green-blue). Difference from *Aspergillus*: in *Aspergillus* the tip of the conidiophore is a swollen **vesicle**, not a brush.
- **Sexual (in some species, e.g. *P. vermiculatum*):** **ascogonium** + antheridium; ascogenous hyphae, **crozier (hook) formation**; 8-spored asci in a closed, round fruiting body, the **cleistothecium**. (Modern names: sexual stages *Talaromyces*, *Eupenicillium*.)
- **Economic importance:**
  - **Penicillin** — **Alexander Fleming (1928)**, *P. notatum* (today considered *P. rubens*); **Florey and Chain** (1940) purified it and brought it into therapy (Nobel 1945); industrial production from **_P. chrysogenum_**; it is a β-lactam antibiotic — blocks wall synthesis.
  - **Griseofulvin** (*P. griseofulvum*) — medicine for ringworm (fungal).
  - **Cheese:** *P. roqueforti* (Roquefort — blue veins), *P. camemberti* (Camembert — white rind).
  - Harm: fruit spoilage (*P. digitatum* — green mould of orange, *P. italicum* — blue mould, *P. expansum* — apple, patulin toxin).

## 8. *Puccinia graminis tritici* — black/stem rust of wheat

- **Teliomycetes (Uredinales)**; **obligate parasite**; **heteroecious** — two hosts: **primary — wheat**, **alternate — barberry (*Berberis vulgaris*)**; **macrocyclic** — **five kinds of spores**.

| Stage | Spore | Host / site | Nuclear condition | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Pycniospores (spermatia)** | Barberry — **upper** surface, flask-shaped **pycnium (spermogonium)** | n (+ or −) | **Receptive (flexuous) hyphae**, periphyses; insects carry spermatia with the nectar → **spermatisation** (heterothallic) |
| **I** | **Aeciospores** | Barberry — **lower** surface, cup-like **aecia (cluster cups)** | n+n (dikaryotic) | In chains; infect only **wheat** |
| **II** | **Uredospores** | Wheat — brick-red pustules on stem, leaf — "**red rust stage**" | n+n | Unicellular, spiny, 4 germ pores; **repeating stage** — wheat to wheat again and again; long distance by wind |
| **III** | **Teliospores** | Wheat — black pustules as the crop matures — "**black stage**" | n+n → **karyogamy → 2n** | **Two-celled**, thick-walled, stalked; **perennation** (winter) |
| **IV** | **Basidiospores (sporidia)** | Teliospore germinates → **promycelium (basidium)** — **meiosis** → 4 spores (2 +, 2 −) | n | Infect only **barberry** |

- **Situation in India — work of K.C. Mehta (1940s):** in the plains the fungus does not survive the summer and the role of barberry is negligible; the disease survives as uredospores on wheat/self-sown plants in the **Nilgiri-Palni hills (south) and the Himalaya (north)**, and every year **uredospores come by wind** to the plains. (Black rust mainly from south-central India, brown and yellow from the north.)
- **The three rusts of wheat:**

| Rust | Fungus | Special |
|---|---|---|
| **Black/stem** | *Puccinia graminis* f. sp. *tritici* | Most destructive; race **Ug99** (Uganda, 1999) — broke the *Sr31* gene |

## 9. *Agaricus* — mushroom

- **Hymenomycetes (Agaricales)**; saprophytic — humus-rich soil, manure; *A. campestris* (field mushroom), ***A. bisporus*** (**button mushroom** — commercial cultivation).
- **Mycelium:** **primary** (uninucleate, from a basidiospore) → **somatogamy** of two compatible primary hyphae → **secondary, dikaryotic (n+n)** mycelium (long-lived; dikaryotisation maintained by **clamp connections** — clamps are few in some species of *Agaricus*) → organised **tertiary mycelium** = **basidiocarp (fruiting body)**.
- **Basidiocarp:** **stipe**, the umbrella-like **pileus** above, in the young stage a **partial veil (velum)** joining the pileus and stipe — ruptures to leave an **annulus** on the stipe; radial **gills (lamellae)** below the pileus.
- **Section of a gill:** **trama** in the middle (loose hyphae) → **subhymenium** on both sides → **hymenium** — **basidia** + sterile **paraphyses** (+ some large **cystidia**).
- **Basidium:** young dikaryotic → **karyogamy (2n)** → **meiosis** → 4 haploid nuclei → **4 exogenous basidiospores** on 4 **sterigmata** (**2** in *A. bisporus*); the spores are **ballistospores** — shot off by the "Buller's drop" mechanism.
- **Edible mushrooms:** *A. bisporus* (button), ***Pleurotus*** (**oyster/dhingri**), ***Volvariella volvacea*** (**paddy straw mushroom**), *Lentinula edodes* (shiitake), *Calocybe indica* (milky mushroom — India), *Morchella* (guchhi — an ascomycete, expensive), *Ganoderma lucidum* (reishi — medicinal), *Cordyceps* (insect fungus, medicinal).
- **Poisonous:** ***Amanita phalloides*** ("death cap" — **α-amanitin** — RNA polymerase II inhibitor, liver damage), ***Amanita muscaria*** ("fly agaric" — muscarine, ibotenic acid), *Psilocybe* (psilocybin — hallucinogenic). No simple folk rule for telling poisonous from edible is reliable.
- **Fairy ring** — mushrooms growing in a ring as the mycelium spreads outward in the soil.
- **India:** **ICAR-Directorate of Mushroom Research (DMR), Solan** (Himachal) — "Mushroom City of India"; cultivation — compost → mixing **spawn** (seed) (spawning) → spread of mycelium → **casing** (a layer of soil) → fruiting. Mushrooms contain protein (~3–4% fresh, ~25–35% dry weight), vitamin D (from UV).

## 10. *Alternaria*, *Cercospora*, *Colletotrichum* — imperfect fungi

| Character | *Alternaria* | *Cercospora* | *Colletotrichum* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class (Ainsworth) | Hyphomycetes (Dematiaceae — dark coloured) | Hyphomycetes | **Coelomycetes** (Melanconiales) |
| Conidiophores | Single/small clusters, dark | In **clusters (fascicles)**, **emerging through stomata**, dark, sympodial | **Acervulus** — cushion-like, below the cuticle/epidermis; with dark stiff hairs in between — **setae** |
| Conidia | **Muriform** — transverse + longitudinal septa, **beaked**, dark brown, **acropetal chains** | **Long, thin, multiseptate**, hyaline or pale (scolecospores) | **Unicellular, hyaline**, sickle-shaped/cylindrical, in mucilage (spread by raindrops) |
| Teleomorph | *Pleospora*/*Lewia* | ***Mycosphaerella*** | ***Glomerella*** |
| Main diseases | ***A. solani*** — **early blight** of potato and tomato — spots with **concentric rings ("target board")**; ***A. brassicae*** — blight/leaf spot of mustard; *A. alternata* — allergy, fruit rot | **Tikka** of groundnut — **early**: *C. arachidicola* (*Mycosphaerella arachidis*); **late**: *Phaeoisariopsis personata* (*Cercosporidium personatum*, *Mycosphaerella berkeleyi*); *C. beticola* — sugar beet; *C. oryzae* — narrow brown leaf spot of rice | ***C. falcatum*** (*Glomerella tucumanensis*) — **red rot of sugarcane** — "**cancer of sugarcane**"; *C. capsici* — **die-back and fruit rot** of chilli; *C. gloeosporioides* — **anthracnose** of mango, papaya; *C. lindemuthianum* — anthracnose of bean |

- **Distinguishing tikka:** **early** — ~30 days after sowing, round brown spots with a **yellow halo**, sporulation mainly on the **upper** surface; **late** — ~50–60 days after, dark black, small, round spots, usually no halo, sporulation on the **lower** surface. Control — carbendazim + mancozeb, chlorothalonil.
- **Anthracnose** = sunken, dark, necrotic spots — a characteristic symptom of acervulus-forming fungi (*Colletotrichum*, *Gloeosporium*).

## 11. Lichens

- **A symbiotic association of two organisms** — the **fungal partner (mycobiont)** + the **photosynthetic partner (photobiont/phycobiont)** — a green alga (***Trebouxia*** the most common, *Trentepohlia*) or a cyanobacterium (***Nostoc***, *Scytonema*); some have both (a third partner — cyanobacteria in cephalodia).
- **Dual hypothesis** — **S. Schwendener (1867)**. New discovery (Spribille et al., 2016): in many lichens a third partner — a **basidiomycete yeast**.
- **Mutualism:** the alga gives **carbohydrates** (ribitol/sugar alcohols from green algae, glucose from cyanobacteria; also **fixed nitrogen** from cyanobacteria); the fungus gives **protection** (drought, intense light), absorption-retention of **water and minerals**, and support. Some scientists also call it "controlled parasitism", but the textbook answer is mutualism.
- **Fungal partner:** mostly **Ascomycetes** (**ascolichens** — ~98%), some Basidiomycetes (**basidiolichens** — *Dictyonema*/*Cora*), Deuteromycetes (lichen imperfecti). **The name and classification of a lichen are based on the fungal partner** (under the ICN the name of a lichen is the name of its fungus).

| Type (morphology) | Characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Crustose** | Crust-like, completely attached to the substratum | *Graphis*, *Lecanora*, *Rhizocarpon* |
| **Foliose** | Leaf-like, lobed, loosely attached by rhizoid-like **rhizinae** | *Parmelia*, *Physcia*, *Peltigera*, *Xanthoria* |
| **Fruticose** | Shrub-like, branched, erect or hanging | ***Usnea*** (old man's beard), ***Cladonia*** (*C. rangiferina* — **reindeer moss**), *Ramalina* |

| Internal structure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Homoiomerous** | Algal cells evenly scattered among the hyphae — *Collema*, *Leptogium* (cyanolichens) |
| **Heteromerous** | Layers — upper cortex (compact hyphae) → **algal layer** → **medulla** (loose hyphae) → lower cortex → rhizinae — most foliose/fruticose |

- **Reproduction:**
  - **Vegetative — both partners spread together:** fragmentation; **soredia** — a few algal cells wrapped in hyphae, powdery, released from **soralia**; **isidia** — small, corticated outgrowths of the surface that break off and spread.
  - **Sexual — only the fungal partner:** ascospores in an **apothecium** (cup-like) or **perithecium**; the spore germinates and forms a new lichen only on **re-association (relichenisation) with a suitable alga**. The alga reproduces only asexually (inside the lichen).
  - Spermatia in spermogonia.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A lichenologist finds that some lichens spread by propagules containing both symbionts, while in others the fungus forms spores that must re-associate with a compatible photobiont; classification gives priority to one partner. (A) Temporary, both independent (B) Commensalism — the alga benefits, the fungus is unaffected (C) Parasitism — the fungus exploits the alga (D) Mutualism — the photobiont provides carbohydrates, the fungal partner protection and mineral absorption — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**.

### 11.1 Ecological and economic importance of lichens

- **Pioneers:** on bare rocks **crustose lichens** come first — weathering of rock by **lichen acids** (oxalic acid etc.) → soil formation → foliose lichens → mosses (lithosere succession).
- **Air pollution indicators — especially SO₂:** lichens have **no cuticle/protective layer and no roots**; they absorb water and nutrients **directly from the atmosphere (rain, dew, dust)** over the whole thallus, and cannot excrete pollutants — so SO₂ destroys chlorophyll (phaeophytinisation) and kills the algal partner. **"Lichen deserts" in polluted cities**; fruticose most sensitive, crustose most tolerant. Lichens also **accumulate** heavy metals and radioactive substances (caesium-137 in reindeer moss after Chernobyl).
- **Food:** *Cladonia rangiferina* (reindeer moss — food of reindeer in the tundra), *Cetraria islandica* (**Iceland moss**), ***Parmotrema perlatum* (*Parmelia*) — "stone flower / chharila (pathar ka phool)"** — in garam masala and Hyderabadi dishes.
- **Dyes/indicators:** **litmus** — ***Roccella tinctoria*** (and *Lecanora*); **orcein** (stain); traditional Harris Tweed dyes.
- **Perfume:** *Evernia prunastri* (**oak moss**), *Pseudevernia furfuracea* — perfume fixatives.
- **Medicine:** **usnic acid** (*Usnea*) — antibiotic properties; traditional medicines.
- **Dating:** lichenometry — age of glacial debris from the slow growth of *Rhizocarpon geographicum*.
- **Harm:** weathering of monuments, rock paintings.

## 12. Mycorrhiza

- **A symbiotic association of a fungus and the root of a higher plant**; term by **A.B. Frank (1885)**; in ~80–90% of land plants.
- **Benefits (to the plant):** better absorption of **phosphorus** (main), Zn, Cu, nitrogen and water (hyphae explore a far larger soil volume than root hairs); **drought and salt tolerance**; **protection from soil-borne pathogens**; aggregation of soil particles (**glomalin** protein). **To the fungus** — carbohydrates from the plant.

| Type | Structure | Fungus | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ectomycorrhiza** | A **mantle/sheath** of hyphae around the root + a network **between** cortical cells — the **Hartig net**; no entry into cells; roots short, thick, branched, few root hairs | Mainly **Basidiomycetes** (*Amanita*, *Boletus*, ***Pisolithus tinctorius***, *Laccaria*), some Ascomycetes (truffle — *Tuber*) | ***Pinus***, oak, eucalyptus, beech, birch, deodar — temperate forest trees |
| **Endomycorrhiza — arbuscular (AM/VAM)** | Hyphae **inside** cortical cells — tree-like **arbuscules** (sites of nutrient exchange) and **vesicles** (storage); no outer mantle | **Glomeromycota** — ***Glomus*** (*Rhizophagus*), *Gigaspora*, *Acaulospora* — obligate symbionts (no pure culture) | Most crops, grasses, herbs — the most widespread; **usually absent in Brassicaceae and Chenopodiaceae** |
| **Ericoid** | Coils in cells of hair-like roots | Ascomycetes | Ericaceae (heath) — acidic soils |
| **Orchid mycorrhiza** | Coils (pelotons) in cells | *Rhizoctonia*-like Basidiomycetes (*Tulasnella*, *Ceratobasidium*) | Orchids — **essential for germination of the dust-like seeds** |
| **Ectendomycorrhiza** | Mantle + Hartig net + cell entry | — | Some *Pinus*, *Arbutus* |

- **Mycoheterotrophs:** *Monotropa* (Indian pipe) — without chlorophyll, gets carbon from other trees through mycorrhiza.
- **"Wood wide web"** — exchange of carbon/signals between trees through mycorrhizal networks (research ongoing, some claims disputed).
- **VAM as a biofertiliser** — Chapter 9.

## 13. Economic importance of fungi

| Benefit | Fungus → product/use |
|---|---|
| **Antibiotics** | Penicillin (*P. chrysogenum*), griseofulvin (*P. griseofulvum*), cephalosporin (*Acremonium* / *Cephalosporium acremonium*) |
| **Immunosuppressant** | **Cyclosporin A** — ***Trichoderma polysporum*** (*Tolypocladium inflatum*) — organ transplantation |
| **Cholesterol-lowering** | **Statins** — ***Monascus purpureus*** (lovastatin; red yeast rice), *Aspergillus terreus* |
| **Organic acids** | **Citric acid — *Aspergillus niger*** (soft drinks, the largest fermentation product), gluconic acid (*A. niger*), itaconic acid (*A. terreus*), fumaric acid (*Rhizopus*) |
| **Enzymes** | Amylase (*Aspergillus oryzae* — Japanese "taka-diastase", soy sauce/sake), pectinase (*A. niger* — clarifying fruit juices), cellulase (*Trichoderma reesei*), lipase (*Candida*) |
| **Food** | Mushrooms, yeast (bread, beer, wine), cheese (*Penicillium*), soy sauce/tempeh (*Aspergillus*, *Rhizopus*), **mycoprotein** (*Fusarium venenatum* — "Quorn"), SCP |
| **Plant hormone** | **Gibberellin** — ***Gibberella fujikuroi*** (*Fusarium fujikuroi*) — **bakanae disease ("foolish seedling")** of rice — **E. Kurosawa (1926)** |
| **Medicines/alkaloids** | **Ergot** — ***Claviceps purpurea*** (sclerotia on rye) — **ergometrine** (stopping post-partum bleeding), ergotamine (migraine), **lysergic acid → LSD**; harm — **ergotism** ("St. Anthony's fire") |
| **Biocontrol** | ***Trichoderma viride/harzianum*** (antagonist of soil pathogens), ***Beauveria bassiana*** (white muscardine of insects), ***Metarhizium anisopliae*** (green muscardine — locusts, termites), *Lecanicillium* (*Verticillium*) *lecanii* (aphids, whiteflies), *Arthrobotrys* (nematode-eating), *Ampelomyces quisqualis* (parasite of powdery mildew) |
| **Research** | ***Neurospora crassa*** — **"one gene–one enzyme" (Beadle and Tatum, 1941)** — "the *Drosophila* of the plant kingdom"; yeast — model eukaryote; *Aspergillus nidulans* — parasexuality |
| **Ecological** | Decomposers (lignin breakdown — white rot fungi), mycorrhiza, lichens |

| Harm | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Plant diseases** | Rusts, smuts, blights, wilts, mildews (Chapter 8) — fungi are the biggest cause of plant diseases |
| **Human diseases (mycoses)** | **Ringworm (tinea)** — *Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*; athlete's foot; **candidiasis** — *Candida albicans*; **aspergillosis** — *A. fumigatus*; **mucormycosis**; histoplasmosis; cryptococcal meningitis; pneumocystis pneumonia (*Pneumocystis jirovecii* — in AIDS) |
| **Mycotoxins** | **Aflatoxin** — ***Aspergillus flavus***, *A. parasiticus* — moist groundnut, maize, grains — **liver cancer** (aflatoxin B₁ among the most potent natural carcinogens); ergotism; patulin; fumonisins (*Fusarium*) |
| **Spoilage of food and goods** | Rotting of bread, pickles, fruit; rotting of wood (*Serpula lacrymans* — "dry rot"); leather, paper, textiles |

## Traps and exceptions

- Oomycetes (*Albugo*, *Phytophthora*, *Pythium*) have a **cellulose** wall and diploid hyphae — in the modern view they are not true fungi (Stramenopiles); in the exam, Mastigomycotina according to Ainsworth.
- *Mucor* has no stolons and rhizoids — *Rhizopus* has them.
- Heterothallism was discovered by **Blakeslee (1904)** in *Mucor*; parasexuality — **Pontecorvo** (*Aspergillus*).
- Yeast has **no fruiting body** — naked ascus.
- *Penicillium* — brush (penicillus); *Aspergillus* — swollen vesicle.
- In *Puccinia*, **aeciospores infect wheat** and **basidiospores infect barberry** — reversing them is a common mistake.
- The uredospore stage is the repeating stage; karyogamy occurs in the teliospore.
- In India the role of barberry in the black rust cycle is negligible (Mehta).
- **2** spores on the basidium of *A. bisporus*, normally 4.
- *Colletotrichum* has an **acervulus + setae**; *Cercospora* has clusters of conidiophores emerging through stomata.
- A lichen is **named after the fungal partner**; soredia-isidia carry both partners; ascospores only the fungus.
- Lichens are **sensitive** to SO₂ (not tolerant) — hence indicators.
- AM fungi cannot be grown in pure culture (obligate symbionts); Brassicaceae usually lack mycorrhiza.
- Cyclosporin is from *Trichoderma polysporum*, not *Penicillium*; statins from *Monascus*.

## Quick revision

- Fungal wall — chitin; storage — glycogen; Oomycete wall — cellulose.
- Ainsworth (1973) — Myxomycota + Eumycota (Mastigo-, Zygo-, Asco-, Basidio-, Deuteromycotina).
- Heterothallism — Blakeslee 1904 (*Mucor*); parasexuality — Pontecorvo and Roper 1952.
- *Albugo* — white rust, knob-like haustoria, basipetal sporangia, warty oospore, staghead.
- *Mucor* — columella, zygospore, gametangial copulation, torula stage.
- *Saccharomyces* — budding, a/α, 4 spores in a naked ascus, zymase (Buchner 1897), first eukaryotic genome 1996.
- *Penicillium* — penicillus, cleistothecium; penicillin — Fleming 1928; production — *P. chrysogenum*.
- *Puccinia* — heteroecious, 5 spores (0 pycnio, I aecio, II uredo, III telio, IV basidio); K.C. Mehta.
- *Agaricus* — somatogamy, dikaryotic mycelium, gills, 4 exogenous spores on the basidium; DMR Solan.
- *Alternaria* — muriform beaked conidia, early blight.
- *Cercospora* — tikka (early *C. arachidicola*, late *Phaeoisariopsis personata*).
- *Colletotrichum* — acervulus, setae, red rot of sugarcane, anthracnose.
- Lichens — Schwendener 1867; crustose-foliose-fruticose; soredia, isidia; SO₂ indicator; litmus — *Roccella*.
- Mycorrhiza — Frank 1885; ecto (Hartig net, *Pinus*) and AM (arbuscules, *Glomus*).
- Citric acid — *A. niger*; aflatoxin — *A. flavus*; gibberellin — *Gibberella fujikuroi*.
- One gene–one enzyme — *Neurospora* (Beadle and Tatum 1941).
