This chapter goes from the gymnosperm part of NCERT Class 11 to B.Sc. type study — *Cycas*, *Pinus*, *Ephedra*, *Gnetum* (and *Ginkgo*) — and fossil gymnosperms.
The 2026 paper had **5 questions** on gymnosperms — seeds of *Ginkgo*, *Cycas* (sago + coralloid roots), a statement-combination on characters of gymnosperms (the "vessels in all" trap), ephedrine from *Ephedra*, and the economic importance of pine. That is, economic uses are asked along with the types.

> **In simple words:** Gymnosperms are plants whose ovules are not enclosed in an ovary but lie exposed on an open leaf-like structure (megasporophyll) or on the scales of a cone — so after fertilisation the seeds also remain "naked", and no fruit is formed. Pine, deodar and cycas are examples. In them pollen reaches the ovule directly by wind, so water is not needed for fertilisation.

## 1. General characters

- **Seeds naked** — ovules exposed on megasporophylls; **no ovary, style, stigma**; no fruit.
- Mostly perennial, woody trees/shrubs (no herbs); many evergreen, conical.
- Tap roots; **mycorrhiza** in *Pinus*, cyanobacteria in the **coralloid roots** of *Cycas*.
- **Xylem with tracheids, no vessels** — **exception: Gnetales** (*Ephedra*, *Gnetum*, *Welwitschia*) have vessels; **no companion cells in phloem** — in their place **albuminous (Strasburger) cells**.
- Leaves — large compound (*Cycas*), needle-like (*Pinus* — xerophytic characters: thick cuticle, sunken stomata), broad (*Gnetum*), fan-shaped (*Ginkgo*).
- **Heterosporous**; microsporophylls and megasporophylls usually in cones (strobili); monoecious (*Pinus*) or dioecious (*Cycas*, *Ginkgo*, *Ephedra*).
- **Pollination usually by wind**, pollen directly onto the **micropyle** of the ovule — a **pollination drop** draws it in; **pollen chamber**.
- A **pollen tube** forms — water not needed for fertilisation (**siphonogamy**); *Cycas* and *Ginkgo* still have **motile, multiflagellate antherozoids** (zoidogamy + siphonogamy — the pollen tube acts mainly as a haustorium).
- Female gametophyte within the nucellus, with **archegonia** (except *Gnetum* and *Welwitschia*).
- **Endosperm = female gametophyte, haploid (n)**, formed **before fertilisation** (triploid in angiosperms, after fertilisation).
- **Single fertilisation** (no double fertilisation as in angiosperms; in *Ephedra* the second male nucleus fuses with the ventral canal nucleus but no endosperm forms).
- **Polyembryony** common — **simple** (from fertilisation of several archegonia) and **cleavage** (several embryos from one zygote — *Pinus*).
- Usually many cotyledons (3–18 in *Pinus*, usually ~8), 2 in *Cycas*.

## 2. Classification

| System | Division |
|---|---|
| **Chamberlain (1934/35)** | Two classes — **Cycadophytae** (manoxylic wood, large leaves, large pith — Cycadofilicales, Bennettitales, Cycadales) and **Coniferophytae** (pycnoxylic wood, small leaves — Cordaitales, Ginkgoales, Coniferales, Gnetales) |
| **Sporne (1965)** — popular in B.Sc. | **Cycadopsida** (Pteridospermales, Bennettitales, Pentoxylales, Cycadales); **Coniferopsida** (Cordaitales, Coniferales, Taxales, Ginkgoales); **Gnetopsida** (Ephedrales, Welwitschiales, Gnetales) |
| **Modern (Christenhusz 2011 etc.)** | Four subclasses — **Cycadidae, Ginkgoidae, Gnetidae, Pinidae**; the traditional "Ginkgopsida" is also considered a separate class |

- **Types of wood:** **manoxylic** — loose, more parenchyma, broad rays (*Cycas*); **pycnoxylic** — compact, narrow rays, commercial timber (*Pinus*, deodar).
- **Position of Gnetales:** on the basis of morphology (vessels, a double-fertilisation-like event, flower-like cones) earlier considered the closest relatives of angiosperms (**anthophyte hypothesis**); molecular data place them **within/near the conifers (sister to Pinaceae — the "Gnepine" hypothesis)** — vessels are convergent evolution.

## 4. *Cycas* — "sago palm"

- In India *C. circinalis*, *C. revoluta* (gardens), *C. rumphii*, *C. pectinata* (North-East), ***C. beddomei*** (Andhra Pradesh — endemic, endangered).
- **Habit:** palm-like; unbranched, thick columnar stem covered with an armour of leaf bases; a crown of leaves at the top.
- **Roots:** primary tap root + **coralloid roots** — negatively geotropic, dichotomous, coral-like; ***Anabaena*/*Nostoc*** in the middle layer of the cortex (algal zone) — **nitrogen fixation**.
- **Leaves — dimorphic:** (i) brown **scale leaves** (protective); (ii) green, large, **unipinnately compound** leaves — **circinate vernation** when young (a fern-like character); leaflets with only a **midrib, no lateral veins**.
- **Structure of the leaflet (xerophytic):** thick cuticle, **sunken stomata** (lower surface only), hypodermis; **transfusion tissue** on both sides of the midrib — makes up for the lack of lateral veins; the vascular bundle of the midrib is **diploxylic** — centripetal + centrifugal xylem.
- **Rachis:** vascular bundles arranged in an **inverted omega (Ω)** shape; mucilage canals.
- **Stem:** large pith, **manoxylic wood**; leaf traces are **girdle traces** — encircling the stem; in old stems **successive cambium rings** (polyxylic — anomalous secondary growth).
- **Reproduction — dioecious:**
  - **Male cone:** terminal, large (~50 cm); many woody **microsporophylls**, bearing on their lower surface hundreds of microsporangia **in sori** (groups of 3–5).
  - **Female — no compact cone:** megasporophylls in a **loose crown** at the stem apex like leaves; each sporophyll leaf-like, hairy, upper part pinnate (*C. revoluta*) or toothed (*C. circinalis*), with **2–6 (more) ovules** on both sides of the lower part. (Growth from the stem apex continues — an "open" female cone.)
- **Ovule:** **orthotropous**, the largest; **one thick integument — three layers:** outer fleshy (sarcotesta), middle hard stony (sclerotesta), inner fleshy; a **pollen chamber** at the top of the nucellus; 2–8 archegonia in the female gametophyte, above them an **archegonial chamber**.
- **Male gametophyte:** microspore → prothallial cell + generative cell + tube cell (pollination at the 3-celled stage); later generative → stalk + body cell → **2 large, top-shaped antherozoids with thousands of cilia in a spiral band**.
- The pollen tube acts as a **haustorium** in the nucellus; antherozoids swim in the fluid of the archegonial chamber to the egg.
- **Seed:** embryo in the female gametophyte (haploid endosperm), **2 cotyledons**; seed orange-red, large.

- **Economic/ecological importance:** **sago-like starch** from the pith of the stem and from seeds (but because of the toxic **cycasin** and BMAA, detoxification by washing/fermenting is necessary — linked to a neurological disease in Guam); ornamental; nitrogen to the soil from coralloid roots; leaves for decoration/religious purposes. (True commercial sago comes from the palm *Metroxylon sagu* and in India mostly from **cassava**.)

## 5. *Pinus* — pine

- **Indian species:** *P. roxburghii* (chir — main source of resin/leesa), *P. wallichiana* (kail/blue pine), *P. gerardiana* (**chilgoza** — edible seeds), *P. kesiya* (Khasi pine, North-East), *P. merkusii*.
- **Habit:** evergreen, tall, **conical (excurrent)** trees; branches in whorls.
- **Dimorphic shoots:** (i) **long shoots** — unlimited growth, only scale leaves; (ii) **dwarf/spur shoots** — limited growth, after scale leaves **needles** at the tip.
- **Number of needles per dwarf shoot:** 1 (*P. monophylla*) · 2 (*P. sylvestris*, *P. merkusii*) · 3 (*P. roxburghii*, *P. gerardiana*) · 5 (*P. wallichiana*); by needle number the dwarf shoot is "unifoliar, bifoliar, trifoliar, pentafoliar".
- **Structure of the needle (xerophytic):** thick cuticle, thick-walled epidermis, **sunken stomata** (on all surfaces), sclerenchymatous hypodermis; mesophyll not differentiated — inward **peg-like folds** in the cell walls (**arm palisade**); **resin canals**; distinct **endodermis**, inside it pericycle, **transfusion tissue**, 1–2 vascular bundles.
- **Stem:** eustele; normal secondary growth, **pycnoxylic wood**; **bordered pits with a torus** on tracheids; resin canals; distinct annual rings; no vessels.
- **Monoecious** — male and female cones on the same tree, on different branches.
  - **Male cones:** in clusters, on long shoots in place of dwarf shoots; **2 microsporangia** on the lower surface of each **microsporophyll**; **winged pollen** (two air sacs) — in spring in such quantity that there are "**sulphur showers**".
  - **Female cone:** pairs of scales — a thin **bract scale** below, a woody **ovuliferous scale** above, on whose upper surface near the base are **2 inverted (anatropous) ovules**, micropyle facing the axis.
- **Male gametophyte:** microspore → **2 prothallial cells** (degenerate) + generative cell + tube cell (pollination at the 4-nucleate stage); generative → stalk + body cell → **2 non-motile, unequal male gametes**.
- **Female gametophyte:** **free-nuclear division** in the megaspore (~2000 nuclei) → cell walls → 2–5 archegonia (towards the micropyle).
- **Long interval from pollination to fertilisation** — about **one year**; seed ripening takes about 2 years or more (after pollination) — textbooks often say "the female cone takes ~3 years to full maturity".
- **Embryo development:** free-nuclear division in the zygote → **4 tiers of 16 cells** (proembryo) — upper (open), rosette, suspensor, embryonal; **cleavage polyembryony** — four embryos, only one develops; embryos also from the rosette (rosette embryos).
- **Seed:** **winged** (wing from the upper layer of the ovuliferous scale) — wind dispersal; embryo with 3–18 cotyledons.

## 6. *Ginkgo biloba* — maidenhair tree

- Native to China; the **only living species** of Ginkgoales — a "**living fossil**"; pollution-tolerant, planted in cities; trees survived the Hiroshima atomic bombing.
- Leaves **fan-shaped**, **dichotomous venation**, notch in the middle (bilobed — *biloba*); **deciduous** (golden in autumn) — unusual among gymnosperms.
- Long and dwarf shoots; **dioecious**.
- Ovules in pairs, on a long stalk, with a "collar"; **motile, multiflagellate antherozoids** (Hirase, 1896).
- **Seeds:** small, **plum/cherry-like**; outer **fleshy layer (sarcotesta)** — foul smell from **butyric acid** when ripe; inside a hard stony layer (sclerotesta) — the "ginkgo nut" (eaten roasted). **This is not a fruit** — the fleshy part is the seed's own integument.
- **Uses:** leaf extract (flavonoids, ginkgolides) — medicinal products for memory/blood circulation; ornamental.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** About the seeds of *Ginkgo* — (I) They look like small plums; (II) They are enclosed in true fruits; (III) They have a fleshy outer covering. Correct statements? (A) II, III only (B) I, III only (C) I, II, III (D) I, II only — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**; gymnosperms have no fruit — the fleshy layer belongs to the integument.

## 7. *Ephedra*

- ~40–50 species; **xerophytic shrubs** — dry, cold mountains (in India *E. gerardiana* — Himalaya, *E. foliata*, *E. intermedia*); *E. sinica* — Chinese "**ma huang**".
- **Stem:** green, **jointed (nodes-internodes), ribbed** (looks like *Equisetum*), photosynthetic; leaves **scale-like, whorled/opposite at the nodes**, fused at the base.
- **Vessels** present — **foraminate (with round pores) perforation plates** (origin different from angiosperm vessels — from tracheids).
- **Dioecious**; compound cones; a stamen-like **antherophore** structure in the male cone; the integument of the ovule elongates into a **micropylar tube** that secretes the pollination drop; an extra covering (perianth/envelope) outside the ovule.
- Archegonia in the female gametophyte; of the two male nuclei of the pollen tube one fuses with the egg, the other with the **ventral canal nucleus** (W.E. Friedman, 1990) — **a double-fertilisation-like event**, but the second fusion forms an **extra embryo**, not endosperm.
- **Economic importance:** the alkaloid **ephedrine** — in bronchitis/**asthma**, nasal congestion (decongestant), raising blood pressure; pseudoephedrine; misused as a stimulant (banned in sports).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** *Ephedra* is an economically important gnetophyte because of its medicinal value. Its primary use? (A) Source of ephedrine used in medicines for respiratory disorders (B) Food crop (C) Timber (D) Fibre — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)**.

## 8. *Gnetum*

- ~30–40 species; tropical moist forests; most are **climbing lianas** (in India *G. ula* — Western Ghats, *G. montanum* — North-East), some trees (*G. gnemon* — "melinjo", South-East Asia).
- Leaves **broad, opposite, reticulate venation** — like a dicot leaf (unique among gymnosperms).
- **Vessels**; anomalous secondary growth in the stem (successive cambia) — in lianas.
- Cones compound, flower-like units in whorls of "**collars (cupules)**"; **three coverings** around the ovule.
- **No archegonia** — the female gametophyte is partly free-nuclear; free nuclei themselves act as eggs (similarity to the angiosperm embryo sac).
- Seeds fleshy, red; ***G. gnemon* seeds and leaves are edible** ("emping" chips in Indonesia).
- **Angiosperm-like characters:** vessels, reticulate veins, absence of archegonia, flower-like cones — so it was earlier considered a possible ancestor of angiosperms.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habit | Palm-like, unbranched | Conical tree | Xerophytic shrub | Liana/tree |
| Leaf | Large pinnate, circinate | Needles, on dwarf shoots | Scales, green stem | Broad, reticulate veins |
| Wood | Manoxylic, polyxylic | Pycnoxylic | Vessels | Vessels |
| Sex | Dioecious | Monoecious | Dioecious | Usually dioecious |
| Female cone | None — loose megasporophylls | Cone (ovuliferous scales) | Compound cone | Compound, collars |
| Antherozoids | Motile, multiflagellate | Non-motile | Non-motile | Non-motile |

### 8.1 Leaf anatomy — *Cycas* leaflet vs *Pinus* needle

| Character | *Cycas* leaflet | *Pinus* needle |
|---|---|---|
| Shape (in section) | Flat, dorsiventral (upper and lower sides differ) | Triangular (3-needled) or semicircular (2-needled) |
| Stomata | Only on the **lower** surface, sunken | On **all surfaces**, deeply sunken |
| Hypodermis | Sclerenchymatous, continuous above | Sclerenchymatous, several layers (thick at corners) |
| Mesophyll | **Differentiated** — palisade above, spongy below | **Undifferentiated** — folded (arm) parenchyma |
| Transfusion tissue | On both sides of the midrib, horizontal | Inside the endodermis, around the vascular bundles |
| Endodermis | Indistinct | **Distinct**, Casparian strips |
| Vascular bundle | One, **diploxylic** (in the midrib) | 1 or 2, conjoint, collateral |
| Resin/mucilage | Mucilage canal | **Resin canals** in the mesophyll |

### 8.2 *Pinus* — life cycle summary

1. In spring, winged pollen (4-nucleate stage) from male cones reaches the pollination drop at the micropyle of the female cone by wind.
2. After pollination the scales of the female cone close; the pollen tube grows slowly in the nucellus; the female gametophyte develops (free-nuclear → cellular → archegonia).
3. About a year later, **fertilisation** — one male gamete with the egg; the other degenerates.
4. Proembryo (4 × 4 cells) → cleavage polyembryony → one embryo remains.
5. The cone becomes woody and matures; when the scales open, winged seeds are dispersed by wind.
6. Seed = a combination of **three generations**: seed coat (diploid — parent sporophyte), endosperm (haploid — female gametophyte), embryo (diploid — new sporophyte generation).

> **Remember:** A gymnosperm seed belongs to "three generations" — this question is asked often; in an angiosperm seed the endosperm is triploid (from double fertilisation), not the gametophyte.

## 9. Fossil gymnosperms (brief)

| Group | Period | Main points | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pteridospermales (seed ferns)** | Upper Devonian–Jurassic, abundant in the Carboniferous | Fern-like leaves but **seeds** — a link between ferns and gymnosperms | ***Lyginopteris oldhamia*** (seed *Lagenostoma*; Oliver & Scott 1904), *Medullosa*, *Glossopteris* (some authors) |
| **Bennettitales (Cycadeoidales)** | Triassic–Cretaceous | *Cycas*-like habit but **bisexual "flower"-like cones** — basis of the anthostrobilus theory of the origin of the angiosperm flower | ***Williamsonia sewardiana*** (Rajmahal; reconstructed by Sahni), *Cycadeoidea* |
| **Pentoxylales** | Jurassic | Stem with 5 vascular segments; discovered by **Birbal Sahni (1948)** | *Pentoxylon sahnii*, *Nipaniophyllum* (leaf), *Carnoconites* (seed cone) |
| **Cordaitales** | Carboniferous–Permian | Long strap-like leaves, ancestors of conifers | *Cordaites* |
| **Progymnosperms** | Devonian | Wood gymnosperm-like, reproduction fern-like (spores) | *Archaeopteris* |

## 10. Gymnosperms, pteridophytes and angiosperms — comparison

| Character | Pteridophytes | Gymnosperms | Angiosperms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed | None | Naked | Enclosed in ovary (fruit) |
| Vessels | Usually absent | Usually absent (Gnetales exception) | Usually present (some primitive ones like *Amborella*, *Drimys* are exceptions) |
| Companion cells | None | None (albuminous cells) | Yes |
| Medium of fertilisation | Water | Pollen tube (also motile antherozoids in *Cycas*, *Ginkgo*) | Pollen tube |
| Archegonium | Yes | Yes (*Gnetum*, *Welwitschia* exceptions) | No |
| Endosperm | — | Haploid, before fertilisation | Triploid, after fertilisation (double fertilisation) |
| Pollination | — | Wind (mainly) | Wind, insects, water, birds etc. |

## Traps and exceptions

- Gymnosperms have **no fruit** — the fleshy parts of *Ginkgo*, *Taxus* (red **aril**), *Juniperus* ("berry" = fleshy cone) are not fruits.
- Vessels only in **Gnetales**; "vessels in all gymnosperms" — wrong.
- *Cycas* **forms no female cone**; it forms a male cone.
- *Cycas* and *Ginkgo* have **motile antherozoids** despite the pollen tube — the largest antherozoids are those of *Cycas*.
- The gymnosperm endosperm is **haploid** — it is the female gametophyte itself.
- *Pinus* **monoecious**, *Cycas* **dioecious**.
- Ovules of *Pinus* **anatropous (inverted)**, of *Cycas* **orthotropous**.
- No archegonium in *Gnetum*; present in *Ephedra*.
- *Ginkgo*, though a gymnosperm, is **deciduous** with dichotomous venation.
- *Welwitschia* keeps only **two leaves** throughout life.
- In India sago is made commercially from cassava (*Manihot esculenta*), not from *Cycas*.
- Tallest — *Sequoia sempervirens*; heaviest/largest — *Sequoiadendron giganteum*.

## Quick revision

- Gymnosperms — naked seeds, haploid endosperm, no companion cells, vessels only in Gnetales.
- Sporne — Cycadopsida, Coniferopsida, Gnetopsida.
- Living fossils — *Cycas*, *Ginkgo*, *Metasequoia*.
- *Cycas* — coralloid roots (*Anabaena*), circinate vernation, diploxylic bundle, Ω rachis, girdle traces, dioecious, no female cone, largest antherozoids and ovules.
- *Pinus* — long and dwarf shoots, needles (1/2/3/5), pycnoxylic, torus, winged pollen, ovuliferous and bract scales, cleavage polyembryony.
- *P. roxburghii* — 3 needles, resin; *P. wallichiana* — 5; *P. gerardiana* — chilgoza.
- *Ginkgo* — fan-shaped dichotomous veins, deciduous, plum-like seeds (fleshy integument), motile antherozoids (Hirase 1896).
- *Ephedra* — green ribbed stem, scale leaves, vessels, micropylar tube, ephedrine (asthma).
- *Gnetum* — broad reticulate-veined leaves, vessels, no archegonium.
- Taxol — *Taxus*; Canada balsam — *Abies balsamea*; turpentine-rosin — *Pinus*.
- Seed fern — *Lyginopteris*; Bennettitales — *Williamsonia*; Pentoxylales — Sahni.
- Oldest tree — *Pinus longaeva*; two leaves — *Welwitschia*.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Ploidy of the endosperm in gymnosperms? — **Haploid (n)** (female gametophyte).
2. Which gymnosperm order has vessels? — **Gnetales** (*Ephedra*, *Gnetum*, *Welwitschia*).
3. Which organism lives in the coralloid roots of *Cycas*? — **Cyanobacteria** (*Anabaena*/*Nostoc*).
4. What makes up for the lack of lateral veins in the leaflet of *Cycas*? — **Transfusion tissue**.
5. Arrangement of vascular bundles in the rachis of *Cycas*? — **Inverted omega (Ω)**.
6. The largest antherozoids of the plant kingdom? — Those of ***Cycas***.
7. Needles per dwarf shoot in *Pinus roxburghii*? — **Three**.
8. Chilgoza comes from which species? — ***Pinus gerardiana***.
9. Type of wood of *Pinus*? — **Pycnoxylic** (compact).
10. On which scale are the ovules of *Pinus*? — **Ovuliferous scale** (on the upper surface, 2 anatropous ovules).
11. Formation of several embryos from one zygote in *Pinus*? — **Cleavage polyembryony**.
12. Who discovered motile antherozoids in *Ginkgo*? — **Hirase (1896)**.
13. What is the fleshy part of the *Ginkgo* seed? — **Outer layer of the integument (sarcotesta)**, not a fruit.
14. Main use of ephedrine? — **Asthma/respiratory disorders** (bronchodilator, decongestant).
15. Gymnosperm without archegonia? — ***Gnetum*** (and *Welwitschia*).
16. Taxol comes from which genus? — ***Taxus*** (*T. wallichiana* in India).
17. Source of Canada balsam? — ***Abies balsamea***.
18. *Williamsonia* is a fossil of which order? — **Bennettitales** (Rajmahal, Sahni).
19. A famous example of a seed fern? — ***Lyginopteris oldhamia*** (Carboniferous).
20. Gymnosperm with two leaves throughout life? — ***Welwitschia mirabilis***.
21. Type of mesophyll in the *Pinus* needle? — **Undifferentiated, folded (arm) parenchyma**.
22. Where are the stomata in the *Cycas* leaflet? — **Only on the lower surface, sunken**.
23. How many generations in a gymnosperm seed? — **Three** (seed coat 2n, endosperm n, embryo 2n).
24. In place of companion cells in the phloem of gymnosperms? — **Albuminous (Strasburger) cells**.
25. Who reported the double-fertilisation-like event in *Ephedra*? — **W.E. Friedman (1990)** — the second male nucleus with the ventral canal nucleus.
