## 12. Attempt strategy: +3 / −1 marking

- **Correct = +3, wrong or multiple marking = −1, blank = 0.** Four options.
- **Expected value — the maths of guessing:**
  - **If you can eliminate two options** (choosing from 2): EV = (3 − 1)/2 = **+1** → **definitely attempt**.
  - **If you can eliminate one option** (choosing from 3): EV = (3 − 2)/3 = **+0.33** → slightly positive; attempt if you understand the topic.
  - **A completely blind guess** (from 4): EV = (3 − 3)/4 = **0** → no gain, only risk — **leave it**.
- **Apply to the questions of this book:**
  - **Matching questions:** a single sure match (e.g. "tetradynamous — Cruciferae") usually eliminates two or three options.
  - **Statement-combination:** identify one statement as surely wrong (overstatement) → eliminate all options containing it.
  - **Assertion-reason:** first judge A and R separately as true/false; then see whether R is the **causal explanation** of the same phenomenon or about another organism/fact (e.g. question 74 — R is about *Volvox*, A about *Chlamydomonas* → not the explanation).
  - **Sequence questions:** fix the first and last items (e.g. *Funaria* — starts with "spore", ends with "spore") — most options are eliminated at once.
  - **Long "best explanation" options:** eliminate any option with a factual error (e.g. "fruits of gymnosperms", "both DNA and RNA in a virus"); usually the most balanced, complete option is correct.
- **Time:** 120 questions / 120 minutes — an average of 1 minute; direct fact questions in 20–30 seconds, so that long analytical questions can get 1.5–2 minutes.
