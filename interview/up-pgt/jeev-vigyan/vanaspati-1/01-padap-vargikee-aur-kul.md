This chapter starts from NCERT Class 11 "The Living World", "Plant Kingdom" and "Morphology of Flowering Plants" (the families part) and goes up to B.Sc. Taxonomy — systems of classification, the code of nomenclature (ICN), herbarium technique and the five prescribed families.
In UP PGT 2026, taxonomy gave a statement-combination question (artificial vs natural system) and a direct definition question (taxon); from the families, questions matching floral formula, placentation, stamen types and economic plants are very likely.

> **In simple words:** Taxonomy is the science of identifying plants (Identification), naming them (Nomenclature) and placing them in groups (Classification). Earlier, people made groups from a few easy characters (such as the number of stamens) — this was the "artificial" system. Then, by combining as many characters as possible, the "natural" system was made, and now the APG system, based on evolutionary relationships (Phylogeny) and DNA, is in use. Every plant has only one valid scientific name, which is decided by the rules of the ICN.

## 1. Basic terminology

| Term | Meaning | Worth remembering |
|---|---|---|
| Taxonomy | Principles of identification, nomenclature, classification | Term given by A.P. de Candolle (1813) |
| Systematics | Study of diversity and its evolutionary relationships | Term from Linnaeus's book "Systema Naturae" |
| Taxon (plural Taxa) | A group of organisms at any level of classification | E.g. "Poaceae" is a taxon, "family" is a category (Category/Rank) |
| Category | A level of the hierarchy — kingdom, division, class, order, family, genus, species | In plants "Division" instead of "Phylum" |
| Species | Basic unit; natural population of interbreeding individuals | Biological species concept — Ernst Mayr |
| Binomial nomenclature | Generic name + specific epithet | Linnaeus, Species Plantarum (1753) |

**Taxonomic hierarchy — plants:** Kingdom → Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Going up, common characters decrease and the number of members increases.

| Category | Mango | Wheat |
|---|---|---|
| Division | Angiospermae | Angiospermae |
| Class | Dicotyledonae | Monocotyledonae |
| Order | Sapindales | Poales |
| Family | Anacardiaceae | Poaceae |
| Genus | *Mangifera* | *Triticum* |
| Species | *indica* | *aestivum* |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** What is a group of organisms at a particular level of classification called? (A) Species (B) Genus (C) Taxon (D) Phylum — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C) Taxon**; species, genus and phylum are themselves categories; the collective term for all of them is taxon.

**Stages of taxonomy (Turrill, 1938):** Alpha taxonomy (α — identification and naming on the basis of external morphology), beta, gamma … and omega taxonomy (Ω — integration of all data: morphology, anatomy, cytology, biochemistry, molecular; the ideal goal).

**Modern branches:**

| Branch | Basis | Example/person |
|---|---|---|
| Numerical taxonomy (Taximetrics) | Equal weight to all characters, similarity coefficients by computer | Sneath & Sokal (1963); idea from Adanson (Adansonian) |
| Cytotaxonomy | Chromosome number, size, karyotype | Species arising by polyploidy |
| Chemotaxonomy | Secondary metabolites — alkaloids, flavonoids, betalains | Betalains in Caryophyllales, not anthocyanins |
| Molecular taxonomy | DNA sequences — *rbcL*, *matK* (chloroplast), ITS (nuclear) | DNA barcode of plants = *rbcL* + *matK* |
| Phylogenetic (Cladistics) | Shared derived characters (Synapomorphy), monophyletic groups | Willi Hennig (1950/1966) |

## 2. Types of classification systems

| Type | Basis | Example | Merits/demerits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artificial | One or a few easily seen characters | Theophrastus (habit — tree, shrub, herb), Linnaeus's sexual system (number of stamens, 24 classes) | Unrelated plants come together; identification easy |
| Natural | Maximum characters, overall similarity | de Jussieu, de Candolle, **Bentham & Hooker** | Better relationships, but not based on evolutionary sequence |
| Phylogenetic | Evolutionary relationships | **Engler & Prantl**, Bessey, **Hutchinson**, Takhtajan, Cronquist | Primitive → advanced sequence |
| Molecular-phylogenetic | DNA sequences + cladistics | **APG** (1998, 2003, 2009, 2016) | Today's standard |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Statements about artificial and natural classification — (I) The artificial system depends on a few easily observable characters and may place unrelated organisms together; (II) The natural system includes many characters and tries to show overall similarity and evolutionary relationships; (III) Because of its simplicity, the artificial system is superior in showing phylogenetic relationships; (IV) Natural systems are more stable because they take many morphological, anatomical and genetic characters. Correct combination? (A) I, II, III, IV (B) II, III, IV only (C) I, III only (D) I, II, IV only — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**; statement III is wrong because the artificial system does not show phylogeny at all.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Systems of angiosperms were built on different bases such as convenience, overall similarity and evolutionary relationships. Which best describes the phylogenetic system? (A) Based only on economic use (B) On a few selected characters, without regard to natural relationships (C) Only on simple characters like habit-habitat (D) On the basis of evolutionary relationships and common ancestry — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**; (B) and (C) describe the artificial system.

**Terms of cladistics:** Ancestral character (Plesiomorphy — old, carried from a common ancestor), derived character (Apomorphy), shared derived character (**Synapomorphy** — only this defines a clade), monophyletic (ancestor + all its descendants), paraphyletic (ancestor + some descendants; e.g. "dicotyledons", "reptiles", "Pteridophyta"), polyphyletic (from different ancestors; e.g. "algae"). Old systems worked on external similarity and did not distinguish ancestral from derived characters, so artificial groups were formed.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Early taxonomists grouped organisms by visible similarities. What is the limitation of this method? (A) Dependence on fossil evidence without distinguishing ancestral and derived characters (B) Ignoring derived and ancestral character states, which produces artificial groups (C) Dependence on molecular data (D) Exclusion of plants and fungi — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**; early taxonomists neither had molecular data nor depended on fossils.

## 3. Bentham & Hooker system

- **Genera Plantarum** of George Bentham and Joseph Dalton Hooker (Latin, 3 volumes, 1862–1883) — the best example of a natural system. **202 families** of seed plants (they called them "orders") and about **97,205 species**.
- Descriptions were based on direct study of actual specimens (Kew herbarium) — so the herbaria and floras of India and the British Commonwealth are still arranged in this sequence.

<div class="formula">

**Outline of Bentham-Hooker**

- **I. Dicotyledons**
  - (a) **Polypetalae** — petals free: series Thalamiflorae (thalamus-flowered), Disciflorae (disc-flowered), Calyciflorae (calyx-flowered)
  - (b) **Gamopetalae** — petals fused: Inferae (inferior ovary), Heteromerae, Bicarpellatae
  - (c) **Monochlamydeae** — perianth in one whorl or absent; 8 series
- **II. Gymnospermae** — placed **between** dicotyledons and monocotyledons
- **III. Monocotyledons** — 7 series

</div>

| Five families | Position in Bentham-Hooker |
|---|---|
| Cruciferae (Brassicaceae) | Polypetalae → Thalamiflorae → Parietales |
| Malvaceae | Polypetalae → Thalamiflorae → Malvales |
| Compositae (Asteraceae) | Gamopetalae → Inferae → Asterales |
| Solanaceae | Gamopetalae → Bicarpellatae → Polemoniales |
| Liliaceae | Monocotyledons → Coronarieae |

**Merits:** Based on actual specimens, practical for identification, placed Ranales (polypetalous, many stamens) at the beginning — which are primitive by the modern view too.
**Demerits:** Not phylogenetic; placed gymnosperms between angiosperms; Monochlamydeae an artificial group (unrelated families together); placed monocots after dicots; advanced families like orchids at the beginning of monocots.

## 4. Engler & Prantl system

- Adolf Engler and Karl Prantl — **Die Natürlichen Pflanzenfamilien** (1887–1915); the first comprehensive phylogenetic classification of the entire plant kingdom (from algae to angiosperms). Several editions of Engler's "Syllabus der Pflanzenfamilien".
- Divided the plant kingdom into **13 divisions**; seed plants in the last division **Embryophyta Siphonogama** — subdivisions Gymnospermae and Angiospermae (gymnosperms correctly separated).
- Among angiosperms, **monocots first, dicots later**; two classes of dicots: Archichlamydeae (petals free or absent) and Metachlamydeae (petals fused).
- **Pseudanthial theory (Wettstein):** the angiosperm flower arose from a compound cone of gymnosperms (like Gnetales) — so plants with **unisexual, wind-pollinated, apetalous catkins (Amentiferae)** (e.g. *Casuarina*, *Salix*, *Quercus*) were considered most primitive.
- **Demerits:** This assumption is considered wrong today — Amentiferae are actually reduced and advanced; placing monocots before dicots is also unacceptable.
- **Merits:** Covered the whole plant kingdom; herbaria of America-Europe follow this sequence; detailed descriptions and illustrations of families.

## 5. Hutchinson system

- John Hutchinson (Kew) — "The Families of Flowering Plants": volume 1 dicots (1926), volume 2 monocots (1934); third edition 1973 (about 411 families).
- Gave **24 principles** — e.g. trees are more primitive than herbs; polypetalous more primitive than gamopetalous; bisexual more primitive than unisexual; many free stamens more primitive than fused; superior ovary more primitive than inferior; solitary flower more primitive than inflorescence.
- **Anthostrobilus theory (Euanthial theory — Arber & Parkin, 1907):** the angiosperm flower arose from a bisexual cone (like Bennettitales) — so **Magnoliales/Ranales** are primitive.
- Divided dicots into two branches: **Lignosae** (fundamentally woody — beginning with Magnoliales) and **Herbaceae** (fundamentally herbaceous — beginning with Ranales).
- Monocots in three divisions: Calyciferae (sepals and petals distinct), Corolliferae (perianth), Glumiflorae (glumes — grasses).
- **Demerits:** The basic division based on habit (woody/herbaceous) turned out artificial — e.g. Umbelliferae (herbaceous) had to be kept far from Araliaceae (woody).

## 6. APG system (APG IV, 2016)

- **Angiosperm Phylogeny Group** — based on DNA sequences (mainly *rbcL*, *atpB*, 18S rDNA, later more genes) and cladistics; recognises only **monophyletic** groups.
- APG I (1998), APG II (2003), APG III (2009), **APG IV (2016): 64 orders, 416 families**.
- "Dicotyledons" are not a natural group (paraphyletic) — so APG has no class "Dicotyledonae". The **eudicots**, with tricolpate (three-pored) pollen, are a true clade.

<div class="formula">

**Main outline of APG IV (from base to top)**

1. **ANA grade** (basal angiosperms): Amborellales (*Amborella trichopoda* — the most primitive living angiosperm, New Caledonia), Nymphaeales (water lilies), Austrobaileyales
2. **Magnoliids:** Magnoliales, Laurales, Piperales, Canellales
3. **Monocots:** Alismatales, Liliales, Asparagales, … Poales (commelinids)
4. **Eudicots:** Ranunculales at the base → **core eudicots** → **superrosids** (rosids: fabids + malvids) and **superasterids** (asterids: lamiids + campanulids)

</div>

| Syllabus family | APG IV order | Clade | Main change in APG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brassicaceae | Brassicales | Rosids → malvids | Close relation to Capparaceae, Cleomaceae; order with glucosinolates |
| Malvaceae | Malvales | Rosids → malvids | Old **Tiliaceae (jute), Sterculiaceae (cocoa), Bombacaceae (silk cotton/semal)** merged into it |
| Asteraceae | Asterales | Asterids → campanulids | Almost unchanged |
| Solanaceae | Solanales | Asterids → lamiids | Close relative of Convolvulaceae |
| Liliaceae | Liliales | Monocots | Greatly narrowed: onion-garlic → **Amaryllidaceae** (Allioideae); *Asparagus* → **Asparagaceae**; *Aloe* → **Asphodelaceae**; *Smilax* → Smilacaceae; *Gloriosa*, *Colchicum* → **Colchicaceae** |

> **Exam angle:** NCERT still teaches onion (*Allium cepa*) as the representative of Liliaceae. If the question says "according to NCERT" or has a traditional context, answer Liliaceae; if it says "according to APG IV", Amaryllidaceae. Similarly jute is in Tiliaceae in NCERT/older books, in Malvaceae in APG.

## 7. Botanical nomenclature — ICN

- Earlier **ICBN** (International Code of Botanical Nomenclature); from the **Melbourne Congress (2011)** the name is **ICN — International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants**. Applies to algae, fungi and plants (ICNP for bacteria, ICZN for animals, ICTV for viruses).
- The code is revised every ~6 years at the International Botanical Congress (IBC): Vienna Code (2006), Melbourne (2012), Shenzhen Code (2018, 19th IBC 2017), Madrid (20th IBC, July 2024; Madrid Code published in 2025).
- Parts of the code: **6 Principles**, rules (Articles), Recommendations.

### 7.1 Main principles and rules

| Rule | Description |
|---|---|
| Independence | Botanical nomenclature is independent of zoological nomenclature — the same name can be used for a plant and an animal |
| Type method | Every name is permanently attached to a nomenclatural type — usually a herbarium specimen |
| Priority | The earliest validly published name is accepted; starting point for seed plants **1 May 1753** (Species Plantarum) |
| One correct name | Each taxon has only one correct name (exceptions described in the code) |
| Latin form | Scientific names are treated as Latin |
| Retroactive | Rules apply retroactively unless expressly limited |

- **Effective publication:** distribution of printed matter; from **1 January 2012** publication in online PDF with ISSN/ISBN is also valid.
- **Valid publication:** effective publication + correct form + description/diagnosis + mention of the type. **From 1 January 2012 the diagnosis may be in Latin or English** (earlier only Latin).
- **Tautonym** — generic and specific names identical (*Linaria linaria*) — **not allowed** in botany; allowed in zoology (*Naja naja*, *Rattus rattus*).
- **Author citation:** *Mangifera indica* L. (L. = Linnaeus); *Brassica juncea* (L.) Czern. — the one in brackets is the original author, the later one made the new combination.
- **Conserved names (Nomina conservanda):** exceptions to priority — to save widely used names.
- **Rules of writing:** first letter of the generic name capital, specific epithet small; italics in print, each underlined separately when handwritten.

### 7.3 Types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Holotype | The single specimen designated by the author |
| Isotype | Duplicate of the holotype (from the same collection) |
| Syntype | When the author chose no holotype and cited two or more specimens |
| Paratype | Cited in the original description, but not holotype/isotype/syntype |
| Lectotype | Chosen later from the original material when there is no holotype or it is lost |
| Neotype | When the original material is completely destroyed — chosen from new material |
| Epitype | Supporting specimen to interpret an ambiguous type |

> **Memory trick:** "**H**ot **I**ce **L**eaves **N**othing" — Holo (the author's) → Iso (its copy) → Lecto (chosen later from the original) → Neo (original destroyed, new).

## 8. Herbarium, botanical gardens and taxonomic aids

### 8.1 Herbarium

A scientific collection of dried, pressed plant specimens mounted on sheets. The term was popularised by Linnaeus; the technique comes from Luca Ghini (Italy, 16th century).

**Steps of the technique:** Collection (with flower/fruit; place, date, habit, colour in a field notebook) → pressing (plant press, blotting paper, changed repeatedly) → drying → poisoning (earlier an alcoholic solution of **mercuric chloride HgCl₂**; today deep-freezing/naphthalene) → mounting (standard sheet about **29 × 41.5 cm**) → label (at the lower right corner; scientific name, family, locality, date, collector, number) → storage in cabinets in family sequence (Bentham-Hooker in India).

| Herbarium | Place | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew | England | Among the largest herbaria in the world (~7 million specimens) |
| Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle | Paris | Very large collection |
| **Central National Herbarium (CAL)** | Howrah (Kolkata) | Largest in India; under BSI; about 2 million specimens |
| NBRI Herbarium (LWG) | **Lucknow (UP)** | National Botanical Research Institute, CSIR |
| Blatter Herbarium | Mumbai | St. Xavier's College |

### 8.3 Other taxonomic aids

- **Flora:** description of the plants of a region — e.g. J.D. Hooker's "Flora of British India" (7 volumes, 1872–1897), Duthie's "Flora of the Upper Gangetic Plain" (UP region).
- **Monograph:** complete account of a single taxon (genus/family).
- **Manual:** brief information and keys for identification.
- **Key:** dichotomous — pairs of contrasting characters (couplet); each alternative is called a "lead". Two forms: **indented (yoked)** and **bracketed (parallel)**.
- **Museums, zoological parks** — for animal taxonomy.

## 9. The five prescribed families

**Symbols of the floral formula:** Br = bracteate, Ebr = ebracteate, ⊕ = actinomorphic, % = zygomorphic, ⚥ = bisexual, ♂/♀ = unisexual, Epk = epicalyx, K = calyx, C = corolla, P = perianth, A = androecium, G = gynoecium; brackets = fused; line below G (or writing "sup.") = superior ovary, line above = inferior; arc joining petals and stamens = epipetalous. Because of Unicode limits, this book writes "G(2) superior/inferior".

### 9.1 Brassicaceae (Cruciferae) — mustard family

<div class="formula">

**Floral formula:** Ebr ⊕ ⚥ K2+2 C4 (cruciform) A2+4 G(2) superior

</div>

- **Habit:** mostly annual herbs; pungent juice — **glucosinolates** (e.g. sinigrin) and **myrosin cells** (enzyme myrosinase → mustard oil/isothiocyanate).
- **Leaf:** alternate, exstipulate, simple; often lyrate.
- **Inflorescence:** racemose — corymbose raceme, a raceme that elongates later.
- **Sepals:** 4, in two whorls (2+2), free.
- **Petals:** 4, free, diagonal — **cruciform** corolla; each petal with a claw and a limb.
- **Androecium:** 6 — **tetradynamous**: 2 short outer (lateral), 4 long inner (in pairs in the median plane); nectar glands at the base.
- **Gynoecium:** bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior; initially unilocular, becomes bilocular by formation of a **false septum (replum)**; **parietal placentation**.
- **Fruit:** **siliqua** — length more than 3 times the breadth (mustard); **silicula** — short, broad (*Capsella*, *Iberis*, *Lepidium*). Seeds non-endospermic.
- **Embryo development:** the embryo of *Capsella bursa-pastoris* (shepherd's purse) — the type of dicot embryo development, "Crucifer/Onagrad type".
- **Model plant:** *Arabidopsis thaliana* — small genome (~135 Mb), 5 chromosome pairs (2n=10), life cycle ~6 weeks; the first plant whose genome was sequenced (2000).

### 9.2 Asteraceae (Compositae) — sunflower family

- Among the largest families of dicots (about **32,000 species**; along with Orchidaceae the largest family of angiosperms).
- **Inflorescence: capitulum (head)** — sessile florets on a flat receptacle, an **involucre** of bracts below; the inflorescence itself looks like a single flower.
- Two kinds of florets:

| Character | Ray floret | Disc floret |
|---|---|---|
| Position | At the periphery | In the centre |
| Symmetry | Zygomorphic (%) | Actinomorphic (⊕) |
| Petals | Ligulate, 5 fused | Tubular, 5 fused |
| Sex | Pistillate (♀) or sterile | Bisexual (⚥) |
| Stamens | Absent | 5, epipetalous, **syngenesious** |

<div class="formula">

**Disc floret:** Br ⊕ ⚥ K(pappus) C(5) A(5) [anthers fused, filaments free, epipetalous] G(2) inferior

**Ray floret:** Br % ♀ K(pappus) C(5) A0 G(2) inferior

</div>

- **Sepals:** modified into hair/scale-like **pappus** — helps wind dispersal of the fruit (e.g. *Taraxacum*, *Sonchus*).
- **Androecium:** 5 — filaments free but **anthers fused** to form a tube (syngenesious); anthers introrse — **piston/brush mechanism**: the style growing up through the tube pushes the pollen out.
- **Gynoecium:** bicarpellary, syncarpous, **inferior**, unilocular, **one ovule, basal placentation**; stigma bifid.
- **Fruit: cypsela** — a one-seeded dry fruit formed from an inferior ovary (earlier also called "achene").
- **Stored food:** usually **inulin** (e.g. tuberous roots of *Dahlia*, chicory), not starch.

### 9.3 Malvaceae — cotton family

<div class="formula">

**Floral formula:** Br ⊕ ⚥ Epk3–∞ K(5) C5 A(∞) G(5–∞) superior

</div>

- **Habit:** herbs, shrubs, trees; **stellate hairs**; mucilage cells/cavities; bast fibres.
- **Leaf:** alternate, simple, **stipulate**, palmate venation.
- **Flower:** large, solitary axillary, showy; below the sepals an **epicalyx** — a whorl of bracteoles (3 large heart-shaped in *Gossypium*, 5–8 in *Hibiscus*; absent in *Abutilon*).
- **Sepals:** 5, fused, **valvate** aestivation.
- **Petals:** 5, free but joined at the base to the staminal tube; **twisted** aestivation.
- **Androecium:** many, **monadelphous** — filaments fuse to form a **staminal column** that surrounds the style; anthers **monothecous**, reniform; pollen large, spiny.
- **Gynoecium:** five to many carpels, syncarpous, superior, **axile placentation**; the style emerges through the staminal tube.
- **Fruit:** loculicidal capsule (cotton, lady's finger) or schizocarp (*Malva*, *Abutilon*). Cotton seed bears seed-coat hairs (lint + fuzz).

### 9.4 Liliaceae — lily family (NCERT representative: onion)

<div class="formula">

**Floral formula (NCERT):** Br ⊕ ⚥ P(3+3) A3+3 G(3) superior

</div>

- **Habit:** perennial herbs; underground **bulb (onion, garlic)**, rhizome, corm; some climbers (*Smilax* — stipular tendrils, *Gloriosa* — leaf-tip tendrils), some tree-like (*Yucca*, *Dracaena* — anomalous secondary growth in monocots).
- **Leaf:** usually radical, parallel venation; in *Asparagus* and *Ruscus* leaves are scales, the stem a **cladode/phylloclade**.
- **Inflorescence:** mostly racemose; in onion an **umbel** — a cymose umbel covered by a spathe.
- **Perianth:** 6 — in two whorls (3+3), petaloid, free or fused; valvate.
- **Androecium:** 6 (3+3), epiphyllous.
- **Gynoecium:** tricarpellary, syncarpous, superior, trilocular, **axile placentation**.
- **Fruit:** capsule (onion, lily) or berry (*Asparagus*, *Smilax*). Seeds endospermic.

### 9.5 Solanaceae — potato family

<div class="formula">

**Floral formula:** Ebr ⊕ ⚥ K(5) C(5) A5 [epipetalous] G(2) superior

</div>

- **Habit:** herbs, shrubs, sometimes small trees; **alkaloids** in many; **bicollateral vascular bundles** in the stem — internal (intraxylary) phloem.
- **Leaf:** alternate, simple (sometimes pinnately compound — potato, tomato), exstipulate; **displacement** of leaves (paired leaves) in the floral region is common.
- **Inflorescence:** solitary axillary or cymose; extra-axillary helicoid cyme in *Solanum*.
- **Sepals:** 5, fused, **persistent** — remains with the fruit; in *Physalis* it swells and covers the fruit (inflated calyx).
- **Petals:** 5, fused — rotate (potato), infundibuliform (*Datura*, *Petunia*), tubular; valvate.
- **Androecium:** 5, **epipetalous**, alternate with the petals; anthers often dehisce by apical pores (*Solanum*).
- **Gynoecium:** bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior; **carpels obliquely placed** — about 45° to the median line; bilocular, many ovules on a **swollen placenta**, **axile**. In *Datura* four-chambered due to a false septum.
- **Fruit:** **berry** (tomato, brinjal, chilli) or **capsule** (*Datura* — spiny, *Nicotiana*, *Petunia*). Seeds endospermic.

### 9.6 Comparison of the five families

| Character | Brassicaceae | Malvaceae | Asteraceae | Solanaceae | Liliaceae |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | Two | Two | Two | Two | One |
| Petals | 4 free, cruciform | 5 free, twisted | 5 fused | 5 fused | Perianth 6 |
| Stamens | 6 tetradynamous | Many, monadelphous | 5 syngenesious | 5 epipetalous | 6 epiphyllous |
| Carpels | 2 | 5–∞ | 2 | 2 (oblique) | 3 |
| Ovary | Superior | Superior | **Inferior** | Superior | Superior |
| Placentation | Parietal | Axile | Basal | Axile (swollen) | Axile |
| Fruit | Siliqua | Capsule | Cypsela | Berry/capsule | Capsule/berry |
| Special | False septum, myrosin | Epicalyx, monothecous anther | Capitulum, pappus | Persistent calyx, bicollateral bundles | Bulb, parallel veins |

> **Exam angle:** The most common form of the matching question — "tetradynamous — Brassicaceae; monadelphous — Malvaceae; syngenesious — Asteraceae; epipetalous + oblique carpels — Solanaceae; epiphyllous 3+3 — Liliaceae". The second form — matching "parietal, axile, basal placentation" with the family.

## 10. Other frequently asked families — identification characters

In the 2026 paper, families were asked beyond the five prescribed ones, as **close pairs** (Acanthaceae vs Verbenaceae, Cruciferae vs Capparidaceae, the hypanthium of Rosaceae, Cucurbitaceae). So the table below is for "one-character identification".

| Family | Decisive identification characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fabaceae — Papilionoideae (Faboideae)** | % flower; **papilionaceous corolla** — 1 standard (vexillum), 2 wings (alae), 2 fused keel petals (carina); **vexillary (descending imbricate)** aestivation; **diadelphous (9)+1** stamens; monocarpellary, marginal placentation; fruit **legume**; root nodules. Formula: % ⚥ K(5) C1+2+(2) A(9)+1 G1 | Pea, gram, arhar, soybean, *Butea* (palash) |
| **Fabaceae — Caesalpinioideae** | % flower; **ascending imbricate** aestivation (standard innermost); 10 free stamens | *Cassia*, *Bauhinia*\*, *Tamarindus*, gulmohar (*Delonix*) |
| **Fabaceae — Mimosoideae** | ⊕ flower, valvate; stamens many/long, showy; head/spike inflorescence | *Acacia*, *Mimosa pudica*, *Albizia*, *Prosopis* |
| **Rosaceae** | **Hypanthium** (cup-like thalamus) → perigynous to epigynous; stamens many; carpels 1 to many, often free; stipulate leaves; fruit — pome (apple, pear), drupe (peach, plum), etaerio (strawberry, raspberry), hip (rose) | Rose, apple, almond, apricot |
| **Apiaceae (Umbelliferae)** | Hollow internodes; sheathing petiole; **compound umbel**; inferior bicarpellary ovary, **stylopodium**; fruit **cremocarp** (two mericarps on a carpophore), oil ducts (vittae) | Coriander, cumin, fennel, carrot, asafoetida (*Ferula*) |
| **Lamiaceae (Labiatae)** | **Square stem**, opposite-decussate leaves, aromatic glandular hairs; **verticillaster**; bilabiate corolla; **didynamous** epipetalous stamens; ovary 4-lobed, **gynobasic style**; fruit **carcerulus** (4 nutlets) | Tulsi, mint, *Salvia*, *Leucas* (dronpushpi); in APG also teak, *Vitex*, *Clerodendrum* |
| **Verbenaceae** | Bilabiate or nearly actinomorphic flower; didynamous; ovary **not lobed**, **style terminal**; 4 chambers by a false septum, 1 ovule per chamber; fruit **drupe** or schizocarp | *Lantana*, *Verbena*, *Duranta* |
| **Acanthaceae** | Large bracts-bracteoles; **cystoliths** in leaves; bilabiate corolla; 4 didynamous or 2 stamens; bilocular ovary, axile; fruit **loculicidal capsule, explosive**; seeds on a **retinaculum (jaculator — hook-like funicle)** | Adusa (*Justicia adhatoda*), kalmegh (*Andrographis*), *Barleria*, *Ruellia* |
| **Capparaceae (Capparidaceae)** | **Gynophore** or androgynophore; stamens 4 to many, **not tetradynamous**; unilocular ovary, parietal placentation, **no false septum**; fruit berry/capsule; stipules often spines | Karil/kair (*Capparis decidua*), caper (*C. spinosa*); *Cleome* (Cleomaceae in APG) |
| **Euphorbiaceae** | **Latex**; unisexual flowers; **cyathium in *Euphorbia*** — in a cup-like involucre one long-stalked female flower in the centre surrounded by many male flowers (each reduced to one stamen), nectar glands; tricarpellary, trilocular, superior; fruit **regma**; **caruncle** on seed (castor) | Castor, rubber (*Hevea*), cassava (*Manihot*), *Jatropha*, poinsettia |
| **Cucurbitaceae** | Climbing vines with **tendrils**; unisexual, usually monoecious; stamens fused, anthers **S-shaped (sinuous)**; **inferior**, tricarpellary, unilocular, **parietal** placentation; fruit **pepo**; bicollateral vascular bundles | Bottle gourd, bitter gourd, cucumber, pumpkin, ash gourd (*Benincasa*) |
| **Poaceae (Gramineae)** | **Culm** with hollow internodes; leaf sheath, **ligule**; unit of inflorescence **spikelet** — glumes, lemma, palea; perianth = 2 **lodicules**; 3 versatile stamens; unilocular, one ovule, 2 feathery stigmas; fruit **caryopsis** (pericarp fused with seed coat) | Wheat, rice, maize, sugarcane, bamboo |
| **Orchidaceae** | Largest family of monocots; % flower; **labellum** (lower lip); stamens-style fused into a **column (gynostemium)**; **pollinia**; ovary inferior, **twisted (resupinate, 180°)**, parietal; seeds dust-like, without endosperm, dependent on mycorrhiza; roots with **velamen** in epiphytes | Vanilla, *Vanda*, *Dendrobium* |
| **Rubiaceae** | Opposite leaves + **interpetiolar stipules**; inferior bicarpellary ovary; in *Mussaenda* one sepal large and petaloid | Coffee, cinchona, kadamb (*Neolamarckia cadamba*), *Ixora*, manjistha (*Rubia*) |
| **Apocynaceae** (including Asclepiadaceae in APG) | Latex; **twisted** corolla; two carpels — ovaries free but styles/stigmas fused; fruit **paired follicles**; seeds with a tuft of hairs (coma); in Asclepiadoideae **pollinia + translator**, gynostegium, corona | Sadabahar, sarpagandha, kaner, aak (*Calotropis*) |

## Traps and exceptions

- "Taxon" and "category" are different — "family" is a category, "Solanaceae" a taxon.
- Linnaeus's sexual system was **artificial**, even though he gave binomial nomenclature.
- Bentham-Hooker is a **natural** system, not phylogenetic — calling it phylogenetic is a common mistake.
- Engler-Prantl, though phylogenetic, was based on the wrong assumption "Amentiferae are primitive"; Hutchinson considered Ranales/Magnoliales primitive.
- APG IV has **64 orders, 416 families**; there is no class "dicotyledons" — there are "eudicots".
- The name ICBN changed to ICN in 2011 (Melbourne); diagnosis in Latin **or** English from 2012.
- Tautonyms are forbidden in botany, allowed in zoology.
- *Gloriosa superba* and *Colchicum* — both give colchicine; both in Colchicaceae in APG.
- In Asteraceae the ovary is **inferior** — the only inferior one among the five.
- The anther of Malvaceae is **monothecous**, whereas a normal anther is dithecous.
- In Brassicaceae, despite parietal placentation, the ovary looks bilocular because of the false septum.
- The potato tuber is a **stem**, sweet potato (*Ipomoea batatas*, Convolvulaceae) a **root**.
- Old name of tomato *Lycopersicon esculentum*; today *Solanum lycopersicum*.
- Current name of lady's finger *Abelmoschus esculentus* (old *Hibiscus esculentus*).

## Quick revision

- Term taxonomy — A.P. de Candolle (1813); Father of Taxonomy — Linnaeus; Father of Botany — Theophrastus.
- Species Plantarum — 1753; starting point of nomenclature of seed plants 1 May 1753.
- Genera Plantarum (Bentham-Hooker) — 1862–1883, 202 families, Polypetalae–Gamopetalae–Monochlamydeae.
- In Bentham-Hooker, gymnosperms between dicots and monocots.
- Engler-Prantl — Die Natürlichen Pflanzenfamilien; monocots first; pseudanthial theory.
- Hutchinson — Lignosae and Herbaceae; 24 principles; anthostrobilus theory.
- APG IV (2016) — 64 orders, 416 families; *Amborella* the most primitive living angiosperm.
- ICN — name from Melbourne 2011; Shenzhen Code 2018; type method, priority.
- Holotype — the author's single specimen; lectotype — later from original material; neotype — original destroyed.
- Herbarium sheet ~29 × 41.5 cm; poisoning HgCl₂; India's largest — CAL, Howrah.
- NBRI and CIMAP — both in Lucknow (CSIR).
- DNA barcode in plants — *rbcL* + *matK*.
- Brassicaceae — tetradynamous, cruciform, false septum, siliqua.
- Malvaceae — epicalyx, monadelphous, monothecous anthers, twisted petals.
- Asteraceae — capitulum, pappus, syngenesious, inferior ovary, basal placentation, cypsela, inulin.
- Solanaceae — epipetalous, oblique carpels, swollen placenta, persistent calyx, berry.
- Liliaceae — P(3+3) A3+3 G(3), axile; onion in Amaryllidaceae in APG.
- *Arabidopsis thaliana* — Brassicaceae, 2n=10, first sequenced plant genome (2000).
- Jute, cocoa, semal — Malvaceae in APG.

## Practice questions (with answers)
