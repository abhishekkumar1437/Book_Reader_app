This chapter completes the syllabus part "Economic botany" (medicinal and aromatic, food, fodder, fibre, fruits-vegetables, ethnobotany, ornamental, oil, timber, energy plants, miscellaneous uses) and the "biofertilisers" part of microbiology, from NCERT (Class 12 "Strategies for Enhancement in Food Production", "Microbes in Human Welfare") to B.Sc. economic botany.
In 2026 there were **7 questions** on economic botany — black pepper (dried fruit), tea-coffee (alkaloid + processing), storage tissues of oil and sugar, fibre vs timber, importance of the grass family, *Ephedra* and pine (Chapter 5). The most frequently asked form — the correct combination of **plant + useful part + active substance**.

> **In simple words:** Humans take almost everything — food, clothes, medicine, timber, fuel, fragrance — from plants. For the exam, remember three facts for every useful plant: **scientific name (and family)**, **which part is useful** (seed, fruit, stem, root, bark, leaf, flower), and **its main substance** (e.g. caffeine, quinine, menthol). Biofertilisers are living microbes that supply nutrients to plants by fixing atmospheric nitrogen or by making soil phosphorus soluble.

## 1. Origin of crops and ethnobotany

- **A. de Candolle** — "Origin of Cultivated Plants" (1882).
- **N.I. Vavilov (1926, 1935)** — **centres of origin/diversity** — where a crop has maximum genetic diversity; **8 main centres** (with some sub-centres): (1) China, (2) **India** (Hindustan) — sub-centre Indo-Malaya, (3) Central Asia, (4) Near East, (5) Mediterranean, (6) Ethiopia (Abyssinia), (7) South Mexico–Central America, (8) South America (Peru-Ecuador-Bolivia; sub-centres Chiloe, Brazil-Paraguay).
- **Crops of the Indian centre:** rice, sugarcane (*Saccharum barberi* etc.), mango, banana (some wild ancestors), black pepper, cardamom, brinjal, cucumber, pigeon pea, mung, urd, sesame, desi cotton (*G. arboreum*), jute, indigo, citrus (North-East).
- **Crops that came from outside (examples):** potato, tomato, chilli, maize, groundnut, tobacco, pineapple, papaya, cashew, rubber (South America/Mexico); wheat, barley (Near East); coffee (Ethiopia); soybean, tea (China).
- **Ethnobotany:** the study of the traditional relationship between humans (especially tribes/local communities) and plants — term by **J.W. Harshberger (1895–96)**; in India **Dr. S.K. Jain** — "Father of Indian ethnobotany" (associated with NBRI, Lucknow); **AICRPE** (All India Coordinated Research Project on Ethnobiology, 1982).
- **Examples:**
  - ***Trichopus zeylanicus*** ("arogyapacha") — an anti-fatigue plant of the **Kani tribe** of Kerala → TBGRI made the drug "**Jeevani**" → **benefit-sharing** with the tribe (1990s) — a world-famous model under the CBD.
  - **Sacred groves** — devrai (Maharashtra), kavu (Kerala), oran (Rajasthan), sarna (Jharkhand), law kyntang (Meghalaya, Mawphlang) — in situ conservation.
  - **Bishnoi community** — protection of khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*) — **Amrita Devi (1730)**; **Chipko movement (1973)**.
  - **Biopiracy** disputes — **neem**, **turmeric** (US patent 1995, revoked at CSIR's request in 1997), **basmati** (RiceTec, 1997).
  - **Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)** — CSIR, 2001.

- **Ethanol blending:** E20 (petrol blended with 20% ethanol) target 2025 — from sugarcane molasses/juice and grains.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Plant oils and sugars differ in source tissue and storage form. Which option correctly matches an oil plant and a sugar plant with their storage tissues? (A) Coconut — oil in root; sugar beet — sucrose in leaves (B) Castor — oil in pericarp; sugar beet — glucose in stem (C) Mustard — oil in endosperm; sugarcane — starch in roots (D) Groundnut — oil in cotyledons; sugarcane — sucrose in stem — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(D)**; in castor the oil is in the endosperm and in sugar beet the sucrose is in the root.

## 17. Biofertilisers

**Definition:** a product of living microbes which, when applied to seed, plant surface or soil, colonises the root zone (rhizosphere) and increases the **availability of nutrients** — N₂ fixation, making phosphorus/potash/zinc soluble or mobile, secreting growth hormones. They are cheap, renewable, eco-friendly and improve soil health; **supplements** to chemical fertilisers, not complete substitutes.

### 17.1 Nitrogen-fixing biofertilisers

| Biofertiliser | Type | Crop | Main facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Rhizobium*** | **Symbiotic**, in legume root nodules; Gram-negative rod | Pulses, soybean, groundnut, berseem | **Bacteroids** in the nodule; **leghaemoglobin**; *nod* and *nif* genes; an estimated 50–100 kg N/ha (more for some crops) per season; **crop-specific (cross-inoculation groups)** |
| ***Bradyrhizobium japonicum*** | Symbiotic | **Soybean** | — |
| *Mesorhizobium ciceri* / *Rhizobium leguminosarum* / *Sinorhizobium meliloti* | Symbiotic | Chickpea / pea-lentil / lucerne | — |
| ***Azorhizobium caulinodans*** | Symbiotic — **stem nodules** | ***Sesbania rostrata*** (green manure) | — |
| ***Frankia*** | Symbiotic **actinomycete** — nodules on **non-legumes** (actinorhizal) | ***Alnus***, ***Casuarina***, *Myrica* | Forestry |
| ***Azotobacter*** (*A. chroococcum*) | **Free-living, aerobic** | Non-legumes — wheat, rice, vegetables, cotton | Along with N fixation, **growth substances** (IAA, gibberellins, vitamins), antifungal substances; **cysts**; high respiration protects nitrogenase from O₂ ("respiratory protection") |
| ***Azospirillum*** (*A. brasilense*, *A. lipoferum*) | **Associative**, **microaerophilic** — on and inside the root | **Grasses — maize, sorghum, pearl millet, sugarcane, rice** | Work of **J. Döbereiner (Brazil)**; increases root growth (IAA) |
| ***Gluconacetobacter diazotrophicus*** | **Endophytic** | **Sugarcane** | Tolerates acidic, sugar-rich environment |
| *Clostridium pasteurianum* | Free-living, **anaerobic** | — | The first free-living N₂ fixer discovered (Winogradsky, 1893) |
| **Blue-green algae (BGA/cyanobacteria)** — *Anabaena*, *Nostoc*, ***Aulosira fertilissima***, *Tolypothrix*, *Calothrix* | Free-living, photoautotrophic; **heterocysts** | **Rice** (flooded fields) | **"Algalisation"** — **G.S. Venkataraman** (IARI); ~20–30 kg N/ha per season (commonly quoted); increase soil organic matter |
| ***Azolla*–*Anabaena azollae*** | A cyanobacterium (*Nostoc azollae*/*Trichormus azollae*) in the leaf cavities of the water fern (*Azolla pinnata*) | **Rice** | Fast growth (doubles in 2–3 days); green manure; about 30–40 kg N/ha per crop (commonly quoted); cattle/poultry feed; used for centuries in China-Vietnam |

**Nitrogenase and leghaemoglobin:**
- **N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi**; the enzyme **nitrogenase** — two components: **Fe-protein (dinitrogenase reductase)** and **MoFe-protein (dinitrogenase)**; **extremely sensitive to O₂**.
- In the nodule, **leghaemoglobin** (pink-red; haem part from the bacterium, globin part from the plant — coded by plant genes) binds O₂ and keeps the **free O₂ level very low** (nitrogenase protected), but the bound O₂ supplies the **intense aerobic respiration** of the bacteroids (a huge demand for ATP) — an **O₂ buffer**.
- Other means of O₂ protection: heterocysts (no PS-II, thick wall — cyanobacteria), respiratory protection (*Azotobacter*), an O₂ diffusion barrier in the nodule cortex.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** In legume nodules, low O₂ is needed for N₂ fixation despite active respiration. Which mechanism maintains this balance? (A) Suppression of respiration lowers O₂ (B) Leghaemoglobin buffers O₂ — a low level for nitrogenase, while high respiration remains possible (C) N₂ diffuses directly into mitochondria (D) Conversion of fixed N into storage compounds consumes extra O₂ — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

### 17.3 Production, application and regulation

- **Carrier:** lignite, charcoal, peat, vermiculite (solid) — shelf life ~6 months; **liquid biofertilisers** — ~1–2 years, higher cell count.
- **Quality standards (FCO):** usually ≥10⁷ (cells/g) in a solid carrier, ≥10⁸ (cells/mL) in liquid — biofertilisers **included from 2006** in the **Fertiliser Control Order (FCO), 1985**.
- **Methods of application:**
  1. **Seed treatment** — ~200 g carrier-based culture + jaggery solution (for sticking) per 10 kg seed; dry in shade and sow immediately.
  2. **Seedling root dip** — before transplanting rice, vegetables.
  3. **Soil treatment** — mixed with compost/farmyard manure.
  4. **Sugarcane sett treatment** — *Azotobacter*/*Gluconacetobacter* + PSB.
- **Precautions:** store in a cool, shady place; protect from sunlight; **do not mix directly with fungicides/chemical fertilisers** (fungicide seed treatment first, biofertiliser later); the right *Rhizobium* strain for the right crop; expiry date.
- **Institutions/schemes:** **National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF), Ghaziabad (UP)** — biofertiliser quality control laboratory; **Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY, 2015)**; **Soil Health Card (2015)**; National Mission on Natural Farming.
- **Related organic inputs:** **green manure** — dhaincha (*Sesbania aculeata*), *S. rostrata* (stem nodules), sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*); **vermicompost** — earthworm ***Eisenia fetida***; compost decomposer (NCOF "Waste Decomposer"); neem-coated urea (100% from 2015).

> **Memory trick:** "**Rhizo** eats **dal**, **Azolla** bathes in **paddy**, **Spirillum** chews **grass**, **PSB** dissolves **phosphorus**" — *Rhizobium*–pulses, *Azolla*/BGA–rice, *Azospirillum*–grasses, PSB–phosphorus.

## Traps and exceptions

- Black pepper — **dried fruit** (not seed); clove — **bud**; cinnamon — **bark**; saffron — **stigma**; mace — **aril**; asafoetida — **oleo-gum-resin**.
- Groundnut oil in the **cotyledons**; castor and coconut in the **endosperm**; palm oil and olive in the **mesocarp**.
- In sugar beet the sugar is in the **root**, in sugarcane in the **stem** — sucrose in both (not glucose).
- Cotton — seed hairs (surface fibre); jute/flax — bast (phloem); sisal — leaf; coir — mesocarp of the fruit.
- Teak, sal, shisham — timber, not fibre.
- The "fermentation" in tea is actually not microbial but **enzymatic oxidation**; it does not occur in green tea.
- The flavour of coffee comes mainly from **roasting**; the coffee "bean" is actually a seed.
- In cocoa the main alkaloid is **theobromine**, caffeine is low.
- Lac is an insect product, not a plant product; musk is an animal product.
- *Azospirillum* for grasses, *Rhizobium* for legumes — reversing them is a common mistake; *Azotobacter* free-living aerobic, *Clostridium* free-living anaerobic.
- The globin part of leghaemoglobin is made from the **plant's** genes.
- VAM **does not dissolve** phosphorus, it mobilises it and helps absorption; PSB **dissolve** it.
- *Frankia* forms nodules on non-legumes (*Alnus*, *Casuarina*).
- Sago in India comes from **cassava**.
- The useful part of isabgol is the **seed husk**; of sarpagandha the **root**; of cinchona the **bark**; opium — **latex of the unripe capsule**; digitalis — **leaf**.
