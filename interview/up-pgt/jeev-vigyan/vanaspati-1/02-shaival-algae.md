This chapter starts from the algae part of NCERT Class 11 "Plant Kingdom" and goes up to B.Sc. type-study level phycology.
The 2026 paper had **6 questions** on algae — thallus organisation, the haplontic cycle of *Chlamydomonas*–*Volvox* (assertion-reason), *Batrachospermum* vs *Padina*, *Fucus* vs *Ectocarpus*, the morphology of *Chara*, and generation dominance in green algae. That is, questions combine "type of life cycle + type of thallus".

> **In simple words:** Algae are simple, chlorophyll-bearing, mainly aquatic organisms whose body is not divided into root-stem-leaf (a thallus). Their sex organs are usually unicellular with no covering of sterile cells around them; the zygote does not form an embryo. By colour (pigments), stored food and flagella they are divided into classes such as green, brown and red.

## 1. General characters

- Part of **Thallophyta**; body a **thallus** — no true root, stem, leaf or vascular tissue.
- Autotrophic (chlorophyll *a* in all); most aquatic — fresh water, sea; some on moist soil, tree bark, snow (*Chlamydomonas nivalis* — "red snow"), hot springs.
- **Sex organs unicellular** (exception *Chara* — multicellular, but still not considered a true sterile jacket) and **no embryo**.
- Reproduction: vegetative (fragmentation), asexual (motile spores — zoospores; non-motile — aplanospores, akinetes), sexual (isogamy, anisogamy, oogamy).
- Symbiotic forms: lichens (with fungi), *Anabaena* + *Azolla*, cyanobacteria in the coralloid roots of *Cycas*, *Chlorella* + *Hydra* (zoochlorellae), corals + dinoflagellates (zooxanthellae).
- Parasitic alga: ***Cephaleuros virescens*** — **red rust** of tea, coffee, mango.

## 2. Thallus organisation — diversity

| Organisation | Characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|

| Colonial / coenobium | Colony of fixed number and shape | *Volvox*, *Pandorina*, *Hydrodictyon* (water net) |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** A phycologist sees, in fresh water and the sea, everything from unicellular motile cells to large multicellular thalli with tissue-like differentiation, where structural complexity does not strictly reflect phylogenetic advancement. The explanation? (A) Algal thalli range from unicellular to complex multicellular, and complexity does not necessarily reflect evolutionary advancement (B) Multicellular algae have specialised conducting systems like vascular plants (C) Unicellular motile algae lack a rigid wall (D) Thalli are almost uniformly filamentous — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)**; algae are a polyphyletic group — brown algae, though very complex, are not "more advanced" than green algae (from which land plants arose).

## 3. Classification

### 3.1 Fritsch (F.E. Fritsch, 1935, 1945) — 11 classes

In "The Structure and Reproduction of the Algae", on the basis of pigments, stored food, flagella and wall: **Chlorophyceae, Xanthophyceae, Chrysophyceae, Bacillariophyceae, Cryptophyceae, Dinophyceae, Chloromonadineae, Euglenineae, Phaeophyceae, Rhodophyceae, Myxophyceae (Cyanophyceae)**.
G.M. Smith (1955) placed them in 7 divisions (-phyta). Father of Indian phycology — **M.O.P. Iyengar**.

### 3.2 Comparison of the main classes based on pigments

| Class | Pigments | Stored food | Cell wall | Flagella | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chlorophyceae** (green) | Chlorophyll *a*, *b*; carotene, xanthophyll | **Starch** (in pyrenoids) | Cellulose | 2–8, equal, apical, whiplash | *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Ulothrix*, *Chara*, *Ulva* |
| **Phaeophyceae** (brown) | Chlorophyll *a*, *c*; **fucoxanthin** | **Laminarin, mannitol** | Cellulose + **algin** (fucoidin) | 2, unequal, lateral (one tinsel, one whiplash) | *Ectocarpus*, *Fucus*, *Dictyota*, *Laminaria*, *Sargassum*, *Padina* |
| **Rhodophyceae** (red) | Chlorophyll *a* (*d* in some); **r-phycoerythrin**, r-phycocyanin | **Floridean starch** | Cellulose, pectin, polysulphate esters (**agar, carrageenan**) | **None** (no flagella at any stage) | *Polysiphonia*, *Porphyra*, *Gelidium*, *Gracilaria*, *Batrachospermum*, *Chondrus* |
| Bacillariophyceae (diatoms) | *a*, *c*, fucoxanthin | Chrysolaminarin, oil | **Silica** frustule (two halves) | Sometimes in the male gamete | *Navicula*, *Pinnularia* |
| Xanthophyceae (yellow-green) | *a*, *c*; no starch | Oil | Pectin | Unequal (heterokont) | *Vaucheria*, *Botrydium* |
| Dinophyceae | *a*, *c*, **peridinin** | Starch, oil | Cellulose plates (theca) | 2 — one in a transverse groove, one longitudinal | *Gonyaulax* (red tide), *Noctiluca* (bioluminescence) |
| Euglenophyceae | *a*, *b* | **Paramylon** | No wall, pellicle | 1–2 | *Euglena* |
| Cyanophyceae (blue-green) | *a*; c-phycocyanin, c-phycoerythrin | **Cyanophycean starch** (glycogen-like) | Peptidoglycan | None | *Nostoc*, *Anabaena* — now **cyanobacteria** (Chapter 6) |

> **Remember:** Red algae are found at greater depths — phycoerythrin absorbs the blue-green light waves (which reach the depths). The deepest (up to ~260 m) photosynthetic organisms are red algae.

> **Common mistake:** In NCERT, *Chlamydomonas* and *Volvox* are in Chlorophyceae; NCERT places *Euglena* in Protista (Euglenoids), not in algae. Diatoms and dinoflagellates are also in Protista in NCERT (Chrysophytes, Dinoflagellates).

## 4. Types of life cycle

| Type | Dominant stage | Where meiosis occurs | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haplontic** | Haploid (n) thallus; only the zygote is 2n | **In the zygote (zygotic meiosis)** | *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*, *Chara*, *Ulothrix* |
| **Diplontic** | Diploid (2n) thallus; only gametes n | **During gamete formation (gametic meiosis)** | *Fucus*, *Sargassum*, diatoms |
| **Isomorphic haplodiplontic** | Both n and 2n free-living, alike | During spore formation (sporic) | *Ectocarpus*, *Ulva*, *Cladophora*, *Dictyota*, *Padina* |
| **Heteromorphic** | Both free-living, but different in form — usually large sporophyte, microscopic gametophyte | Sporic | *Laminaria*, *Macrocystis* (kelps); *Urospora* (the reverse) |
| **Triphasic** | Gametophyte (n) + carposporophyte (2n) + tetrasporophyte (2n) | In the tetrasporangium | *Polysiphonia* (red alga) |
| Biphasic red alga | Gametophyte + carposporophyte (+ "Chantransia" stage) | At the apex of Chantransia | *Batrachospermum* |

## 5. *Chlamydomonas* — unicellular motile green alga

- **Habitat:** freshwater pools, ponds (nitrogen-rich water); "red snow" — *C. nivalis*.
- **Structure:** oval/pear-shaped; cellulose (actually glycoprotein) wall; **2 equal whiplash flagella** at the anterior end; **one cup-shaped chloroplast**, usually with **one pyrenoid** (*C. reinhardtii*); red **eyespot (stigma)** in the anterior part — phototaxis; **2 contractile vacuoles**; nucleus in the cavity of the cup.
- **Asexual reproduction:** under favourable conditions the protoplast forms 2–16 **zoospores**; under unfavourable conditions aplanospores, **hypnospores**; **palmella stage** — cells lose flagella and divide in mucilage.
- **Sexual reproduction — all three types in a single genus:**
  - **Isogamy:** *C. reinhardtii*, *C. eugametos* — some species heterothallic (+ and − strains).
  - **Anisogamy:** *C. braunii* — larger female, smaller male gamete.
  - **Oogamy:** *C. coccifera* — non-motile egg, motile sperm.
- Zygote → thick-walled **zygospore** (resting) → **meiosis** → 4 (or 8) haploid zoospores.
- **Model organism:** *C. reinhardtii* — research on photosynthesis, flagella, optogenetics (channelrhodopsin).

## 6. *Volvox* — coenobium

- **"Rolling alga"**; in fresh water; hollow spherical **coenobium** — about **500 to 60,000** cells in one layer, in a mucilaginous matrix; cells like *Chlamydomonas* (2 flagella, eyespot), connected to each other by **cytoplasmic strands (plasmodesmata-like)**.
- **Polarity:** large eyespots in vegetative cells at the anterior pole; reproductive cells (**gonidia**) in the posterior part.
- **Division of labour:** vegetative (sterile) vs reproductive cells — a model for the evolution of multicellularity.
- **Asexual:** a gonidium divides to form a daughter coenobium — during development **inversion** occurs — the cells turn inside out to bring the flagella outside; daughter colonies are inside the parent and are released when the parent bursts.
- **Sexual:** **oogamous**; antheridium (sperm plates/bundles) and oogonium; monoecious (*V. globator* — **protandrous**) or dioecious (*V. aureus*).
- Zygote (red, spiny wall — haematochrome) → **meiosis** → usually one zoospore remains (the other nuclei degenerate) → new coenobium.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): The life cycle of *Chlamydomonas* is mainly haplontic and the zygote is the only diploid stage. Reason (R): *Volvox* has both asexual and sexual reproduction, and after fertilisation zygotic meiosis restores the haploid stage. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **Both true, but R is not the correct explanation of A** (R is about another organism).

## 7. *Spirogyra* — "pond silk" / "water silk"

- **Habitat:** free-floating, slimy (mucilage sheath) green filaments in still fresh water; abundant in spring.
- **Structure:** unbranched filament, all cells alike (no base-apex distinction, no holdfast); wall — inner cellulose, outer pectin; **ribbon-shaped spiral chloroplasts** (1 to 16), margins wavy, many **pyrenoids**; large central vacuole; nucleus suspended in the middle by **cytoplasmic strands**.
- **Vegetative:** fragmentation — the main method; asexual spores rare (akinetes, aplanospores); **no flagellated stage anywhere**.
- **Sexual — conjugation:**
  - **Scalariform:** two filaments side by side, conjugation tubes form — a ladder-like appearance; all cells of one filament are "male" (the protoplast moves across), those of the other "female" — **physiological anisogamy**; heterothallic.
  - **Lateral:** between adjacent cells of the same filament — (i) **chain-forming/indirect** — tube in the lateral wall; (ii) **direct** — pore in the transverse wall; homothallic.
- Zygote → **zygospore** (3-layered wall: exospore, mesospore, endospore) → meiosis → 4 haploid nuclei, **3 degenerate**, 1 functional → one new filament.
- **Azygospore (parthenospore):** a zygospore-like spore formed without fusion.

## 8. *Chara* — stonewort

- In fresh water, on pond beds; encrusted with **calcium carbonate**, rough — hence "stonewort"; some have a garlic-like smell; believed to help reduce mosquito larvae.
- **Structure (2026 question):** main axis with distinct **nodes and internodes**; at the nodes **whorls of branches of limited growth** ("leaves") — **horsetail (*Equisetum*)-like appearance**; in most species a **cortex** on the internodes (*Nitella* has no cortex); multicellular, branched **rhizoids**, oblique septa; growth from an apical cell.
- **Sex organs — the most complex among algae:** at the nodes of limited branches; **oogonium = nucule** above, **antheridium = globule** below.
  - **Globule:** 8 **shield cells** (orange-red), inside **manubrium** → **capitulum** → **antheridial filaments**, each cell of which forms one biflagellate sperm.
  - **Nucule:** one egg, surrounded by **5 spiral tube cells**, at the top **5 corona cells** (in one tier); in *Nitella* **10 corona cells** (in two tiers) — a distinguishing character.
- Zygote → oospore (resting) → meiosis (4 nuclei, 3 degenerate) → **protonema** → new plant. Cycle **haplontic**.
- **Evolutionary importance:** Charophyta (*Chara*, *Coleochaete*) are the **closest algal relatives of land plants** (molecular evidence; phragmoplast, the same chlorophyll *a*, *b*, starch).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Which morphological character distinguishes *Chara*? (A) A main axis with distinct nodes and internodes, bearing horsetail-like whorled lateral branches (B) An undifferentiated simple body (C) Specialised vascular tissue for conduction of water and nutrients (D) A unicellular thallus — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(A)**.

## 9. *Ectocarpus* — brown alga (Phaeophyceae)

- **Habitat:** marine, on rocks or on other large algae (epiphyte); more common in cold seas; also on Indian coasts.
- **Thallus:** **heterotrichous** — prostrate system (rhizoidal, attached to the substratum) + erect branched filaments; uniseriate cells; growth intercalary/diffuse; ribbon/disc-like brown chromatophores in the cell, pyrenoid-like structures.
- **The sporophyte (2n)** forms two kinds of sporangia:
  - **Unilocular sporangium** — here **meiosis** → haploid zoospores (n) → gametophyte.
  - **Plurilocular (neutral) sporangium** — mitosis → diploid zoospores (2n) → sporophyte again (self-repetition).
- **Gametophyte (n):** plurilocular gametangia → pear-shaped gametes with **2 unequal lateral flagella**.
- **Sexual:** isogamous, but in *E. siliculosus* **physiological anisogamy** — the female gamete settles early and releases the pheromone **ectocarpene**, the male gametes gather around it ("clump formation").
- **Life cycle:** **isomorphic alternation of generations (isomorphic haplodiplontic)** — both generations look alike externally, free-living. (Haploid gametes can also develop without fusion — parthenogenesis.)
- *E. siliculosus* — model organism of brown algae (genome 2010).

## 10. *Polysiphonia* — red alga (Rhodophyceae)

- **Habitat:** marine, often epiphytic; thallus finely branched, red-purple.
- **Structure:** **polysiphonous** — around one central siphon (axial cell row) 4–24 **pericentral siphons**, all of equal length; cells connected by **pit connections**; prostrate rhizoids + erect filaments; **trichoblasts** (colourless branched hairs).
- **No flagellated stage** — the male gametes are non-motile **spermatia**, carried by water currents.
- **Triphasic life cycle:**
  1. **Gametophyte (n)** — dioecious (male and female plants separate). The male bears **spermatangia** (one spermatium in each). The female bears a **carpogonial branch** (4 cells) — the terminal cell is the **carpogonium**, whose long receptive part is the **trichogyne**; this arrangement is called a **procarp**.
  2. Fertilisation → **carposporophyte (2n)** — parasite-like on the female gametophyte itself, **gonimoblast filaments** → **carpospores (2n)**; around it an urn-like **pericarp** formed from gametophyte tissue; this whole structure = **cystocarp**.
  3. Carpospore → free-living **tetrasporophyte (2n)** — looks like the gametophyte (isomorphic). In its **tetrasporangia** **meiosis** → 4 **tetraspores (n)**, tetrahedral → 2 male + 2 female gametophytes.

## 11. Comparative types — *Fucus*, *Padina*, *Batrachospermum*, *Ulva*, *Laminaria*

| Alga | Class | Thallus | Life cycle | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***Fucus*** (rockweed) | Brown | Dichotomous, parenchymatous, flat; holdfast, stipe; **air bladders** in *F. vesiculosus* | **Diplontic** — meiosis in the gametangia; no free-living haploid generation | Sex organs in pits — **conceptacles** — of swollen tips (**receptacles**); **8 eggs** in the oogonium, **64 sperms** in the antheridium; oogamous |
| ***Sargassum*** | Brown | Branched, "leaf-stem"-like, air bladders | Diplontic | Free-floating in the Sargasso Sea; source of algin |
| ***Padina*** ("peacock's tail") | Brown (Dictyotales) | **Fan-like, parenchymatous**, lightly calcified, concentric bands of hairs, inrolled margin (marginal meristem) | **Isomorphic alternation of generations** | **Tetrasporangia** on the sporophyte (meiosis → 4 non-motile tetraspores); gametophyte dioecious, oogamous; marine |
| ***Batrachospermum*** ("frog-spawn alga") | Red | **Fresh water** (fast-flowing clean streams); **filamentous**, mucilaginous, branches in whorls — bead-like appearance | Gametophyte (n) + **carposporophyte after fertilisation** + "**Chantransia**" stage | Carpospore (2n) → Chantransia (filamentous, multiplies by monospores) → meiosis in its apical cell → gametophyte; among the few freshwater red algae |
| ***Ulva*** (sea lettuce) | Green | Leaf-like sheet two cells thick | Isomorphic | Food; "green tide" on polluted (nitrogen-rich) coasts |
| ***Laminaria*** (kelp) | Brown | Large sporophyte — holdfast, stipe, lamina; trumpet hyphae | **Heteromorphic** — microscopic gametophyte | Iodine, algin, "kombu" food |
| ***Porphyra*** | Red | Thin sheet | Heteromorphic — "Conchocelis" stage (diploid, in shells) — Kathleen Drew-Baker (1949) | **Nori** — food in Japan |

## 12. Evolutionary trends in algae

| Trend | Primitive → advanced | Example series |
|---|---|---|
| Thallus | Unicellular motile → colony → filament → heterotrichous → parenchymatous | *Chlamydomonas* → *Volvox* → *Ulothrix* → *Fritschiella* → *Ulva* |
| Sexual reproduction | Isogamy → anisogamy → oogamy | *C. eugametos* → *C. braunii* → *C. coccifera*; *Volvox*, *Chara*, *Fucus* (oogamous) |
| Sex organs | Unicellular, naked → with a sterile covering of sorts | Most algae → *Chara* (tube and shield cells) |
| Life cycle | Haplontic → isomorphic → heteromorphic / diplontic | *Chlamydomonas* → *Ulva*, *Ectocarpus* → *Laminaria*; *Fucus* |
| Cell division | — | **Phragmoplast** in *Chara*, *Coleochaete* (like land plants) |

- **Volvocine lineage:** *Chlamydomonas* (1 cell) → *Gonium* (4–16) → *Pandorina* (16) → *Eudorina* (32) → *Pleodorina* (32–128, some vegetative) → *Volvox* (thousands, clear division of labour) — a model for the evolution of multicellularity and cell specialisation.
- **Origin of land plants:** green algae (chlorophyll *a*, *b*, starch, cellulose wall) — especially **Charophyceae/Zygnematophyceae** (the *Spirogyra* group) — were found in molecular studies to be the closest relatives of land plants.

| Use | Alga / product |
|---|---|
| **Food** | *Porphyra* (nori), *Ulva* (sea lettuce), *Laminaria* (kombu), *Undaria* (wakame), *Chlorella*; **single cell protein (SCP)** — *Spirulina* (actually a cyanobacterium, ~60–70% protein), *Chlorella* |

| **Iodine, potash** | Kelps — *Laminaria*, *Fucus* |
| **Diatomaceous earth (kieselguhr)** | Deposits of silica frustules of diatoms — filters, abrasives, absorbent in dynamite, heat insulation |
| **Biofertilisers** | *Anabaena*, *Nostoc*, *Aulosira* (in rice fields), *Azolla–Anabaena* (Chapter 9) |
| **Biofuel** | ***Botryococcus braunii*** — hydrocarbons (up to 30–40%); biodiesel from algae |
| **Research** | *Chlorella* — Calvin cycle study (Calvin experimented on *Chlorella*); *Acetabularia* — role of the nucleus (Hämmerling); *Chlamydomonas* |
| **Space research** | *Chlorella* — O₂ and food in a closed system |
| **Medicine** | Chlorellin (antibiotic) from *Chlorella*; anthelmintics from red algae |
| **Harmful** | **Water bloom** — *Microcystis* (microcystin toxin), *Anabaena*; **red tide** — *Gonyaulax*/*Alexandrium* (saxitoxin — shellfish poisoning), *Karenia brevis*; *Cephaleuros* — red rust of tea; O₂ depletion in eutrophication |

## Traps and exceptions

- Red algae have **no flagella at any stage** — the male gametes (spermatia) are non-motile.
- *Batrachospermum*, though red, lives in **fresh water**; *Chara*, though green, has sex organs with a multicellular-like covering.
- *Fucus* is **diplontic** — this is not an exception among brown algae but a character of the Fucales; *Ectocarpus* isomorphic, *Laminaria* heteromorphic.
- *Spirogyra* has no flagellated cell; even in conjugation the gametes are amoeboid.
- All three types of sexual reproduction occur in the single genus *Chlamydomonas*.
- In the zygospores of *Spirogyra* and *Chara*, after meiosis **3 of the 4 nuclei degenerate** — only one plant is formed.
- *Nitella* has 10 corona cells, *Chara* 5 — reversing them is a common mistake.
- *Spirulina* is called an "alga" but is a cyanobacterium (prokaryote).
- Irish moss is an alga, reindeer moss a lichen, club moss a pteridophyte.
- *Acetabularia*, though unicellular, is several cm long; *Macrocystis* is the largest alga.
- In kelp (*Laminaria*) the large plant seen is the **sporophyte (2n)**; the gametophyte is microscopic.
- In *Polysiphonia* the carposporophyte is not free-living, it is on the female gametophyte; the tetrasporophyte is free-living.

## Quick revision

- Father of phycology (India) — M.O.P. Iyengar; Fritsch — 11 classes.
- Green algae — chlorophyll *a*, *b*, starch, cellulose.
- Brown — fucoxanthin, laminarin/mannitol, algin, 2 unequal lateral flagella.
- Red — phycoerythrin, floridean starch, agar/carrageenan, no flagella.
- Diatoms — silica frustule, kieselguhr; dinoflagellates — red tide.
- *Chlamydomonas* — cup-shaped chloroplast, eyespot, 2 flagella; from isogamy to oogamy.
- *Volvox* — coenobium, gonidia, inversion, oogamous.
- *Spirogyra* — spiral ribbon chloroplast, scalariform/lateral conjugation, physiological anisogamy.
- *Chara* — node-internode, globule below, nucule above, 5 corona cells.
- *Ectocarpus* — heterotrichous, unilocular (meiotic) and plurilocular sporangia, isomorphic.
- *Polysiphonia* — polysiphonous, trichogyne, cystocarp, tetraspores, triphasic.
- *Fucus* — diplontic, conceptacles, 8 eggs, 64 sperms.
- *Padina* — fan-shaped, isomorphic, tetrasporangia.
- *Batrachospermum* — freshwater red alga, Chantransia stage.
- *Macrocystis* — the largest alga; *Botryococcus* — hydrocarbons.
- *Cephaleuros virescens* — red rust of tea (parasitic alga).
- Agar — *Gelidium*, *Gracilaria*; carrageenan — *Chondrus*, *Kappaphycus*.
