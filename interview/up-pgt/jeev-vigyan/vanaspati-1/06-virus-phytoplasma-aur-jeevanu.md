This chapter goes from NCERT Class 11 "Biological Classification" (Monera, viruses) and Class 12 "Microbes in Human Welfare" to B.Sc. Microbiology.
In 2026 this area gave a total of **5 questions** — the basic nature of viruses, a statement-combination on bacterial survival, ecological characters in bacterial classification, matching of cyanobacteria and an integrated question on transformation-conjugation-episome; the questions were mostly in the "best explanation" form with long options.

> **In simple words:** Viruses are not cells — they are DNA **or** RNA enclosed in a protein shell and can make copies of themselves only inside a living cell. Bacteria are true cells, but they have no nuclear membrane (prokaryotes). Archaea are also prokaryotes but chemically very different from bacteria — many live in extreme places such as hot springs, salty water and marshes. Phytoplasmas are very small bacteria without a cell wall that live in the phloem of plants and spread disease.

## 2. General characters of viruses

- **Acellular, obligate intracellular parasites**; inert outside the cell (virion) — can be crystallised; "a link between living and non-living".
- Genetic material **DNA or RNA — never both together**; ss or ds, linear or circular.
- No metabolism, ribosomes or ATP production of their own; use the host's machinery.
- **Antibiotics ineffective**; antiviral drugs, vaccines, **interferon** (a protein secreted by the host cell).
- Size ~20–300 nm (giant viruses — mimivirus ~400–750 nm, pandoravirus ~1 µm).
- **Structure:** nucleic acid + protein coat **capsid** (subunits **capsomeres**) = **nucleocapsid**; some have an outer lipid-protein **envelope** (from the host membrane, e.g. HIV, influenza) — enveloped viruses are quickly destroyed by alcohol/soap.
- **Symmetry:** **helical** — TMV; **icosahedral (20 faces)** — adenovirus, polio; **complex** — T4 phage, pox.
- **Plant viruses usually ssRNA**; bacteriophages usually dsDNA; animal viruses DNA or RNA.
- **Classification:** ICTV (International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses); **Baltimore classification (1971)** — 7 classes based on the way mRNA is made: I dsDNA, II ssDNA, III dsRNA, IV (+)ssRNA, V (−)ssRNA, VI ssRNA-RT (retroviruses, HIV), VII dsDNA-RT (hepatitis B, CaMV).

## 3. Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)

- **Shape:** rod-shaped, about **300 nm × 18 nm**; **helical symmetry**.
- **Genetic material:** single-stranded, positive-sense (+) **ssRNA**, ~**6,395 nucleotides** (~6.4 kb); RNA ~5%, protein ~95%.
- **Capsid:** **~2,130 identical protein subunits** (each **158 amino acids**, ~17.5 kDa); about **16⅓ subunits** per turn; pitch of the helix ~2.3 nm; ~130 turns; a hollow central canal ~4 nm in diameter; the RNA is coiled in a groove between the protein subunits.
- **Transmission:** **mechanical** — sap, hands, tools, fingers of smokers; extremely stable (survives for years in dry leaves); no insect vector.
- **Symptoms:** light and dark green patches on leaves — **mosaic**, leaf distortion, stunting; also in tomato, chilli.
- **Local lesions** on ***Nicotiana glutinosa*** — measurement of virus quantity by Holmes's (1929) lesion count; hypersensitive response — the *N* gene.
- **Historical importance:** the first virus discovered, the first crystallised, the first seen with the electron microscope, the one that proved RNA to be genetic material.
- **Control:** disease-free seed/seedlings, removing infected plants, washing hands (the old remedy of washing with milk), resistant varieties, **cross protection** — a plant infected with a mild strain is protected from a severe strain (McKinney, 1929).

## 4. Bacteriophage — T4

- A T-even (T2, T4, T6) phage infecting *E. coli*; **complex symmetry**; "tadpole" shape.
- **Structure:** **head** — elongated (prolate) icosahedron, containing **dsDNA** (~169 kbp, linear); in the DNA **5-hydroxymethylcytosine (HMC)** instead of cytosine — protection from host restriction enzymes; **collar**; **tail** — a **contractile sheath** around a hollow **core**; a hexagonal **base plate** — 6 **tail pins (spikes)** and **6 long tail fibres**.
- **Lytic cycle — T4 (virulent phage):**
  1. **Adsorption:** tail fibres attach to receptors on the bacterial wall.
  2. **Penetration:** **lysozyme** of the tail makes a hole in the wall; the sheath contracts, DNA goes in like an injection; the protein coat stays outside ("ghost").
  3. **Biosynthesis:** breakdown of host DNA, replication of phage DNA and synthesis of phage proteins — during this period no complete virion is found in the cell — the **eclipse period**.
  4. **Assembly/maturation**.
  5. **Release:** lysis of the cell by **endolysin** (and holin); **burst size** ~100–200 phages; the whole cycle about 25–30 minutes at 37 °C.
- **Lysogenic cycle — λ (lambda) phage (temperate phage):** phage DNA integrates at a specific site (*att*) of the host chromosome to form a **prophage**; replicated along with the bacterium; the **λ repressor (cI)** prevents lysis; **induction** by UV or chemicals → the prophage comes out and enters the lytic cycle.
- **Lysogenic conversion:** the prophage gives the bacterium new properties — e.g. the gene for **diphtheria toxin** in *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* from β-phage; the **cholera toxin** of *Vibrio cholerae* from CTXφ phage.
- **Phage plaque:** a clear zone on a bacterial lawn. Other phages: **φX174** (circular ssDNA, 5,386 nucleotides — the first DNA genome sequenced, Sanger 1977), **MS2** (ssRNA — the first gene/genome sequenced, Fiers), **M13** (ssDNA, filamentous — cloning vector), **P1**, **P22**.
- **Phage therapy** — renewed interest against antibiotic-resistant bacteria.

## 6. Viroids, virusoids and prions

| Character | Viroid | Prion |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | **T.O. Diener, 1971** | **S.B. Prusiner, 1982** |
| Composition | Only a **small, circular, single-stranded RNA** — no protein coat | Only **protein** — no nucleic acid |
| Size | ~246–401 nucleotides (PSTVd — 359) | PrP protein (~253 amino acids) |
| Coding | Codes for no protein | Product of the host gene *PRNP* |
| Multiplication | By host RNA polymerase II, **rolling circle** replication; some have ribozyme properties | By converting normal **PrPᶜ (rich in α-helices)** into pathogenic **PrPˢᶜ (rich in β-sheets)** — the "template" model |
| Diseases | Potato spindle tuber (**PSTVd** — the first), **coconut cadang-cadang** (the smallest, 246 nt, Philippines), citrus exocortis, chrysanthemum stunt, avocado sunblotch | Sheep — **scrapie**; cattle — **BSE (mad cow)**; humans — **kuru** (Fore tribe of Papua New Guinea, ritual cannibalism), **CJD**, vCJD, fatal familial insomnia (FFI), GSS; deer — CWD |
| Resistance | — | Highly resistant to heat, UV, formalin, proteases |
| Affected | Only plants | Mainly animals (nervous tissue — **spongiform encephalopathy, TSE**) |

- **Virusoids:** satellite RNAs — enclosed in the capsid of a helper virus, not independently infectious.
- **Satellite virus:** depends on a helper virus, codes for its own capsid (e.g. tobacco necrosis satellite virus).

## 7. Mycoplasma and phytoplasma

**Mycoplasma (PPLO — pleuropneumonia-like organisms):**
- **The smallest free-living cells** (~0.1–0.3 µm; genome of *Mycoplasma genitalium* ~580 kb — among the smallest); **no cell wall** → pleomorphic, **unaffected by penicillin** (penicillin blocks wall synthesis), but **sensitive to tetracycline**.
- Sterols in the membrane (in many species); can live without oxygen; class **Mollicutes**.
- Diseases: pleuropneumonia of cattle (*M. mycoides* — the first discovered, Nocard and Roux 1898), atypical pneumonia in humans (*M. pneumoniae*).

**Phytoplasma (earlier MLO):**
- Wall-less, pleomorphic Mollicutes restricted to the **phloem (sieve tubes)** of plants; **not yet cultured on artificial media** (so Koch's postulates are not fulfilled); genus "*Candidatus* Phytoplasma"; identified by 16S rRNA.
- **Vectors:** mainly **leafhoppers**, planthoppers, psyllids — they multiply in the vector too (circulative-propagative).
- **Symptoms:** **virescence** (floral parts green), **phyllody** (floral organs leaf-like), **witches' broom**, **little leaf**, yellowing, stunting, sterility.
- **Control:** **tetracycline** (temporary recovery), vector control, removing infected plants, heat therapy (moist hot air treatment of sugarcane setts).

| Phytoplasma disease | Vector / note |
|---|---|
| **Little leaf of brinjal** | Leafhopper ***Hishimonus phycitis*** |
| **Sesame phyllody** | Leafhopper *Orosius albicinctus* |
| **Sandal spike** | A destructive disease of the sandalwood forests of Karnataka-Tamil Nadu |
| **Grassy shoot of sugarcane** | Through infected setts and leafhoppers |
| Coconut root wilt (Kerala) | Planthopper, lace bug |
| Aster yellows, mulberry dwarf, apple proliferation, rice yellow dwarf | Leafhoppers |

- **Spiroplasma:** helical, motile, **culturable** Mollicutes — corn stunt, citrus "stubborn" disease.

## 8. Archaea (archaebacteria) vs eubacteria

- **Carl Woese (1977)** — identified archaea as distinct from 16S rRNA sequences; **three domains** (1990): Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya. In many molecular characters archaea are **closer to eukaryotes**.

| Character | Archaea | Eubacteria |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | **No peptidoglycan**; **pseudopeptidoglycan (pseudomurein** — N-acetyltalosaminuronic acid, β-1,3 bonds**)**, protein S-layer, polysaccharide; **no muramic acid** | Peptidoglycan (NAM + NAG, β-1,4 bonds) — muramic acid |
| Membrane lipids | **Ether bonds**, branched **isoprenoid** (phytanyl) chains; in some a **tetraether monolayer** — stable at high temperature | **Ester bonds**, unbranched fatty acids, bilayer |
| RNA polymerase | Several subunits, eukaryote-like; TATA box-like promoters | One simple type (α₂ββ′ω + σ) |
| Initiator tRNA | **Methionine** | **Formylmethionine** |
| Histones | Histone-like proteins (in many) | None (HU-like proteins) |
| Introns | In some tRNA/rRNA genes | Rare |
| Antibiotics | **Insensitive** to penicillin, streptomycin, chloramphenicol | Sensitive |
| Pathogens | No known human pathogen | Many |

| Archaeal group | Habitat | Example / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Methanogens** | Marshes, rice fields, **rumen** of ruminants, sewage | *Methanobacterium*, *Methanobrevibacter* — **methane** from CO₂ + H₂; **gobar gas/biogas** plants; strict anaerobes |
| **Halophiles** | Extremely salty water (Dead Sea, salt pans) | ***Halobacterium*** — **bacteriorhodopsin** (purple membrane) — ATP from a light-driven proton pump (chlorophyll-free photosynthesis) |
| **Thermoacidophiles** | Hot acidic springs | *Sulfolobus* (~80 °C, pH 2–3), *Thermoplasma* (wall-less) |
| Hyperthermophiles | Marine hydrothermal vents | *Pyrolobus fumarii* (~113 °C), *Methanopyrus kandleri* strain 116 (**122 °C** — the highest known); *Pyrococcus furiosus* — **Pfu DNA polymerase** |

> **Common mistake:** *Thermus aquaticus* (**Taq polymerase**, PCR) is a **eubacterium**, not an archaeon; *Pyrococcus* (Pfu) is an archaeon.

## 9. Bacterial cell — structure

**By shape:** cocci (*Streptococcus* chains, *Staphylococcus* clusters, diplococci pairs, sarcina cubes), bacilli (*E. coli*, *Bacillus*), comma/vibrio (*Vibrio cholerae*), spiral (spirillum; spirochaete — *Treponema*).

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Glycocalyx** | Loose — **slime layer**; firm — **capsule** — protection from phagocytes, virulence (S strain of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*) |
| **Cell wall** | Peptidoglycan (murein) — broken down by lysozyme; penicillin blocks its synthesis (transpeptidase) |
| **Gram-positive** | Thick (20–80 nm) peptidoglycan, **teichoic acids**; no outer membrane — the crystal violet-iodine complex is retained → **purple** |
| **Gram-negative** | Thin peptidoglycan + **outer membrane** (**lipopolysaccharide LPS — endotoxin**, porins), **periplasmic space**; colour removed by alcohol → **red/pink** with safranin |
| **Gram staining** | **Christian Gram (1884)**: crystal violet → iodine (mordant) → alcohol/acetone (decolouriser) → safranin (counterstain) |
| **Acid-fast** | Waxy wall with **mycolic acids** — *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *M. leprae*; **Ziehl-Neelsen stain** |
| **Plasma membrane** | Respiratory enzymes, ETS; **mesosome** (infolding — now mostly considered a preparation **artefact**) |
| **Nucleoid** | One circular dsDNA chromosome (*E. coli* ~4.6 Mb); no nuclear membrane; no histones |
| **Plasmid** | Extrachromosomal, independently replicating circular DNA — F, R, Col, Ti |
| **Ribosome** | **70S** (50S + 30S); targets of streptomycin (30S), chloramphenicol and erythromycin (50S) |
| **Inclusions** | **Volutin/metachromatic granules** (polyphosphate — *Corynebacterium*), **PHB** (poly-β-hydroxybutyrate — biodegradable plastic), glycogen, **gas vacuoles** (cyanobacteria), **magnetosomes** (*Magnetospirillum*), **carboxysomes** (RuBisCO) |
| **Flagellum** | Protein **flagellin**; basal body (rings) + hook + filament; **rotary motor** — driven by proton motive force (not ATP); arrangement — **monotrichous** (*Vibrio*), **lophotrichous** (tuft at one end — *Pseudomonas fluorescens*), **amphitrichous**, **peritrichous** (*E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Proteus*) |
| **Pili / fimbriae** | Short, many — for attachment; **sex pilus (F-pilus)** — conjugation |
| **Endospore** | In *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*; **one** per cell; **calcium dipicolinate**, cortex, thick coat — resistant to boiling, radiation, drought; **not reproduction — survival** (numbers do not increase); destroyed by autoclaving (121 °C, 15 lb/in², 15–20 minutes) |

## 11. Reproduction and growth

- **Binary fission** — the main method; in *E. coli* under favourable conditions the **generation time is ~20 minutes**; in *M. tuberculosis* ~15–20 hours.
- **Budding** (*Hyphomicrobium*), **fragmentation**, **conidia** (*Streptomyces* — fungus-like filamentous actinomycetes), **cysts** (*Azotobacter*), **endospores** (for survival).
- **Growth curve (batch culture):** **lag phase** → **logarithmic/exponential (log)** — no secondary metabolites, highest antibiotic sensitivity → **stationary** — secondary metabolites like antibiotics, endospores → **death (decline)**.
- **Characters for bacterial classification:** morphology (shape, Gram stain, flagella, spores), biochemical/physiological (enzymes, fermentation), serological (antigens), ecological (habitat, temperature, salinity), genetic/molecular (G+C %, 16S rRNA, DNA hybridisation) — **Bergey's Manual** is the standard reference.

## 12. Genetic recombination

Bacteria have no sexual reproduction, but three methods of **horizontal (lateral) gene transfer** — in all of them the transfer is **one-way** (donor → recipient) and the recipient becomes a **merozygote** (partial diploid).

| Method | Discovery | Process | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transformation** | **F. Griffith (1928)** — *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (S and R strains, mice); **Avery, MacLeod, McCarty (1944)** — the "transforming principle" is DNA | The recipient cell takes up **free (naked) DNA** from the medium | **Competence** in the recipient; in the laboratory CaCl₂ + heat shock, electroporation |
| **Conjugation** | **Lederberg and Tatum (1946)** — *E. coli* K-12; Davis's U-tube experiment (1950) — **direct contact** needed | The donor (F⁺) makes contact by a **sex pilus** and transfers DNA (rolling circle) | **F factor (fertility plasmid)** |
| **Transduction** | **Zinder and Lederberg (1952)** — *Salmonella typhimurium*, phage **P22** | A **bacteriophage** carries bacterial DNA from one cell to another | Phage; unaffected by DNase (DNA protected in the capsid) |

**Types of conjugation:**
- **F⁺ × F⁻** → only the F plasmid is transferred; the recipient becomes **F⁺**; chromosomal genes are not transferred.
- **Hfr × F⁻** (Hfr = high frequency of recombination; Cavalli-Sforza 1950, Hayes 1953) — the F factor is **integrated** into the chromosome; chromosomal genes are transferred in order; complete transfer takes ~**100 minutes**; contact usually breaks earlier, so the recipient **remains F⁻**.
- **Interrupted mating** — **Wollman and Jacob (1955–57)**: by breaking contact with a blender at fixed times, the order and distance (in minutes) of genes — **bacterial chromosome mapping**.
- **F′ (F-prime)** — the F factor, excising incorrectly, takes some chromosomal genes with it → **sexduction (F-duction)**.

**Types of transduction:**
- **Generalised:** in the lytic cycle **any** bacterial DNA segment is packed by mistake into the phage head — P1, P22.
- **Specialised (restricted):** by faulty excision of a lysogenic phage (λ), only genes **near the integration site** (*gal* and *bio* for λ).

**Episome and plasmid:**
- **Plasmid** — term by **J. Lederberg (1952)**; independently replicating extrachromosomal DNA.
- **Episome** — term by **Jacob and Wollman (1958)**; a genetic element that **can stay free (autonomous) and can also integrate** into the chromosome — e.g. the **F factor** (free in F⁺, integrated in Hfr) and the **λ prophage**. Every episome is a plasmid, but not every plasmid is an episome.
- **Types of plasmid:** F (fertility), **R (antibiotic resistance** — spread of multidrug resistance**)**, Col (colicin), virulence plasmids, degradative (*Pseudomonas* — hydrocarbons), **Ti (tumour-inducing — *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*, crown gall; vector for plant genetic engineering)**, **Ri (*A. rhizogenes* — hairy root)**.
- **Transposons (jumping genes)** — Barbara McClintock (maize, 1940s; Nobel 1983); in bacteria IS elements and Tn.

## 13. Cyanobacteria (blue-green "algae")

- Prokaryotic, **oxygenic photosynthetic**; chlorophyll *a*, phycobiliproteins (phycocyanin, phycoerythrin) in **phycobilisomes**; **no chloroplasts** — thylakoids in the cytoplasm; stored food cyanophycean starch; **no flagella** (some filaments glide/oscillate).
- **Heterocyst:** thick wall, **no PS-II** (no O₂ production), **nitrogenase** — the site of N₂ fixation; polar nodules.
- **Akinetes** (thick-walled resting spores), **hormogonia** (filament fragments — vegetative reproduction).
- **Endosymbiont theory:** chloroplasts from ancient cyanobacteria; the "Great Oxidation Event" ~2.4 billion years ago; **stromatolites** — the oldest fossils (~3.5 billion years).

| Genus | Identification characters |
|---|---|
| ***Nostoc*** | Bead-like filaments in a round mucilaginous colony; **intercalary heterocysts**; symbiotic in *Anthoceros*, *Cycas*, lichens |
| ***Anabaena*** | Free filaments, intercalary heterocysts; symbiotic in *Azolla* (*A. azollae*) |
| ***Oscillatoria*** | Unbranched, **no heterocysts**, **oscillating/gliding movement** (without flagella); hormogonia |
| ***Gloeotrichia*** | Radial filaments in a mucilaginous colony; **basal heterocyst**, filaments tapering towards the tip, a large akinete near the base (Rivulariaceae; also *Rivularia*) |
| ***Scytonema*** | **False branching** — the filament breaks and emerges from the sheath (usually near a heterocyst); intercalary heterocysts |
| ***Stigonema*, *Mastigocladus*** | **True branching** — division of a cell in a second plane; heterocysts |
| ***Spirulina*** (*Arthrospira*) | Spiral filament, no heterocysts; **SCP**, ~60–70% protein |
| ***Microcystis*** | Unicellular colony, gas vacuoles; toxic water blooms (microcystin) |
| *Aulosira*, *Tolypothrix*, *Calothrix* | N₂ fixers of rice fields |

## Traps and exceptions

- A virus has DNA **or** RNA — never both together (in mimivirus both molecules are found but the genome is DNA — note this if the question is high-level).
- Plant viruses usually ssRNA; but CaMV dsDNA, geminiviruses ssDNA, wound tumour dsRNA.
- Viroid — only RNA, no protein; prion — only protein, no nucleic acid.
- Phytoplasma is sensitive to **tetracycline**, not penicillin (no wall).
- An endospore is not reproduction — one spore from one cell, one cell from one spore.
- In Hfr × F⁻ the recipient usually remains F⁻; in F⁺ × F⁻ the recipient becomes F⁺.
- Generalised transduction — lytic (P1, P22); specialised — lysogenic (λ).
- Episome = both free and integrated forms; not every plasmid is an episome.
- *Thermus aquaticus* is a eubacterium; *Halobacterium* is an archaeon (despite "bacterium" in the name).
- The mesosome is now considered an artefact.
- *Scytonema* — false branching; *Stigonema* — true branching; *Oscillatoria* — no heterocysts.
- The first patented GMO — *Pseudomonas putida* (Chakrabarty), not the first GM crop.
- Lysozyme breaks peptidoglycan, not the pseudomurein of archaea.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. Who said "contagium vivum fluidum"? — **M.W. Beijerinck** (1898).
2. Genetic material of TMV? — **Single-stranded (+) RNA** (~6,395 nucleotides).
3. Number of capsomeres in TMV? — **About 2,130**.
4. Unusual base in T4 phage DNA? — **5-hydroxymethylcytosine**.
5. In which cycle is a prophage formed? — **Lysogenic** (λ phage).
6. How does a bacterium get the gene for diphtheria toxin? — **Lysogenic conversion** (β-phage).
7. Vector of bhindi yellow vein mosaic? — **Whitefly *Bemisia tabaci***.
8. The smallest known viroid disease? — **Coconut cadang-cadang** (246 nt).
9. Cause of kuru disease? — **Prion** (Fore tribe).
10. Vector of little leaf of brinjal? — **Leafhopper *Hishimonus phycitis*** (phytoplasma).
11. Phytoplasma is sensitive to which antibiotic? — **Tetracycline**.
12. Type of bond in archaeal membrane lipids? — **Ether bond** (branched isoprenoid).
13. Light-driven proton pump of *Halobacterium*? — **Bacteriorhodopsin**.
14. Endotoxin of the Gram-negative wall? — **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**.
15. Energy source of the bacterial flagellum? — **Proton motive force** (not ATP).
16. Who discovered conjugation? — **Lederberg and Tatum** (1946, *E. coli* K-12).
17. Position of the F factor in an Hfr cell? — **Integrated into the chromosome** (episome).
18. Example of specialised transduction? — **λ phage** (*gal*, *bio* genes).
19. Site of N₂ fixation in cyanobacteria? — **Heterocyst**.
20. The first patented living GMO? — ***Pseudomonas putida*** (Chakrabarty, 1980).
