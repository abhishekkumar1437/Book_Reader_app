This chapter goes from the Pteridophyta part of NCERT Class 11 to B.Sc.-level type study, stelar evolution and palaeobotany.
The 2026 paper had **7 questions** on Pteridophyta (three on heterospory-seed habit, *Psilotum*, *Marsilea* sporocarp, *Selaginella* vs *Equisetum*, an assertion-reason on stelar evolution) and **3 questions** on plant evolution/palaeobotany (geological sequence of groups, Gondwana fossils, incompleteness of the fossil record) — this is the heaviest chapter of this book.

> **In simple words:** Pteridophytes (ferns and their relatives) are the first plants in which tissues carrying water and food — xylem and phloem — were formed. The plant seen in them is the **sporophyte (2n)**, which has root, stem and leaf. The spore forms a small, free-living **prothallus (gametophyte)**, which still needs water for fertilisation. Some pteridophytes (*Selaginella*, *Marsilea*) form two kinds of spores — this later became the foundation for the evolution of the seed.

## 1. General characters

- **First vascular plants (vascular cryptogams)**; no seeds are formed — "**seedless vascular plants**".
- **Sporophyte (2n) dominant, free-living**; differentiated into root, stem (usually an underground rhizome), leaf.
- Xylem mainly of **tracheids** — vessels generally absent; **exceptions:** vessels have been found in some species of *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*, *Pteridium*, *Marsilea*. **No companion cells** in phloem.
- Leaves — **microphyll**: small, one vein, no leaf gap (*Lycopodium*, *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*); **megaphyll**: large, branched veins, a **leaf gap** in the stele (ferns).
- Sporangia on **sporophylls**; in many aggregated into a **cone/strobilus** (*Lycopodium*, *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*).
- Most **homosporous**; some **heterosporous** — *Selaginella*, *Marsilea*, *Salvinia*, *Azolla*, *Isoetes*.
- Gametophyte = **prothallus** — small, multicellular, free-living in most, photosynthetic (in some underground, mycorrhiza-dependent); antheridia and archegonia; **water essential** for fertilisation.
- Antherozoids — **biflagellate** in *Lycopodium*, *Selaginella*; **multiflagellate** in *Equisetum* and ferns.
- "Snakes of the plant kingdom" — usually in moist, shady places, but some xerophytes (*Selaginella lepidophylla* — resurrection plant).
- **Carboniferous period** — "**age of pteridophytes**"; **coal** was formed from the giant lycopsid trees of that time (*Lepidodendron*, *Sigillaria*) and *Calamites*.

## 2. Classification

| Class (Reimers 1954; Sporne 1975 — basis of NCERT) | Main characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Psilopsida** | No roots, leaves absent or scale-like (enations), dichotomous stem, synangia/terminal sporangia, protostele | *Rhynia* (fossil), *Psilotum*, *Tmesipteris* |
| **Lycopsida** | Microphylls, spiral; sporangium on the **adaxial** surface of the leaf; strobilus | *Lycopodium*, *Selaginella*, *Isoetes*, *Lepidodendron* (fossil) |
| **Sphenopsida** | Stem with nodes-internodes, ribbed; leaves in whorls, scale-like; **sporangiophores** | *Equisetum*, *Calamites* (fossil) |
| **Pteropsida (Pteridopsida)** | Megaphylls (fronds), **circinate vernation**, leaf gaps; sori | *Pteris*, *Dryopteris*, *Adiantum*, *Marsilea*, *Azolla*, *Salvinia*, *Ophioglossum* |

**Modern (PPG I, 2016 — molecular):** Pteridophyta in two classes — **Lycopodiopsida (lycophytes)** and **Polypodiopsida (ferns = monilophytes)**. Ferns now **also include horsetails (*Equisetum*) and whisk ferns (*Psilotum*)** — *Psilotum* is actually a close relative of Ophioglossales, not a living descendant of primitive psilophytes.
- "Pteridophyta" is a **paraphyletic** group — ferns are closer to seed plants, lycophytes a separate branch.
- **Sporangium types of ferns:** **eusporangiate** (sporangium from several initial cells, thick wall, many spores — *Ophioglossum*, *Marattia*) and **leptosporangiate** (from one cell, one-layered wall, fixed number — usually 64 spores — *Pteris*, *Dryopteris*, *Marsilea*).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** What does the evolutionary relationship among ferns, whisk ferns and horsetails show? (A) Horsetails an independent lineage unrelated to ferns (B) Whisk ferns evolved by reduction from seed plants (C) A common ancestor split into different lineages that represent these groups (D) Ferns directly from bryophytes — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)**; in molecular phylogeny all three are members of the monilophyte clade.

## 3. Stele — types and evolution

**Stele** = vascular tissue + pericycle + pith (if present), the central part surrounded by the endodermis. **Stelar theory** — **Van Tieghem and Douliot (1886)**. **Jeffrey (1898–1902)** gave the evolutionary sequence of steles.

| Stele | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Protostele** — the most primitive; solid xylem in the centre, no pith | | |
| — Haplostele | Round solid xylem, phloem all around | *Rhynia*, many species of *Selaginella*, *Lygodium* |
| — Actinostele | Star-shaped xylem, phloem between the arms | *Psilotum*, *Lycopodium serratum* |
| — Plectostele | Xylem in parallel plates, phloem in between | *Lycopodium clavatum*, *L. volubile* |
| — Mixed protostele | Phloem scattered among xylem groups | *Lycopodium cernuum* |
| **Siphonostele** — **pith** in the centre | | |
| — Ectophloic | Pith → xylem → phloem (outside only) | *Osmunda*, *Equisetum* (rhizome) |
| — Amphiphloic | Phloem on both sides of xylem | *Marsilea* (rhizome), *Adiantum pedatum* |
| **Solenostele** | Siphonostele in which leaf gaps are far apart, only one gap at a level | *Marsilea*, *Pteris vittata* |
| **Dictyostele** | Leaf gaps close together and overlapping — stele net-like, in segments (**meristeles**, each concentric amphiphloic/hadrocentric) | *Dryopteris*, *Adiantum capillus-veneris*, some species of *Pteris* |
| **Polycyclic** | Two or more concentric rings | *Pteridium aquilinum*, *Matonia* |
| **Polystele** | Many independent protosteles in one axis | *Selaginella laevigata*, *S. willdenowii* |
| **Eustele** | Vascular bundles in a ring around the pith | Dicot and gymnosperm stem |
| **Atactostele** | Scattered vascular bundles | Monocot stem |

**Theories of stelar evolution:**
- **Jeffrey's theory of extrastelar origin of pith:** the pith came inwards through the leaf gaps from tissue **outside the stele** (cortex); the origin of the siphonostele is linked to **megaphylls and leaf gaps**.
- **Intrastelar theory (Boodle, Gwynne-Vaughan etc.):** the pith formed by conversion of central xylem cells into parenchyma.
- **Jeffrey's classification:** **cladosiphonic** (only branch gaps — Lycopsida, Sphenopsida, microphyllous) vs **phyllosiphonic** (leaf gaps — Pteropsida, megaphyllous).
- Evolutionary sequence: protostele → siphonostele → solenostele → dictyostele → eustele/atactostele.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Assertion (A): In many pteridophytes the transition from protostele to more complex steles such as siphonostele is linked to increasing structural complexity and leaf evolution. Reason (R): The development of leaf gaps in the vascular cylinder is linked to the origin of megaphylls and contributes to the formation of the siphonostelic condition. — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **Both true and R is the correct explanation of A** (Jeffrey's view; note that supporters of the intrastelar theory do not consider it a complete explanation — so this question is debatable).

## 4. *Rhynia* — among the oldest vascular plants

- **Discovery:** **Rhynie chert** of Scotland — silica-petrified fossils of the Lower Devonian (~410 million years); **Kidston and Lang (1917–1921)**.
- Species: *R. gwynne-vaughanii*; *R. major* was later renamed ***Aglaophyton major*** because it has no true tracheids (only water-conducting cells).
- **Structure:** **no roots, no leaves**; underground horizontal **rhizome** (with rhizoids) + upright, **dichotomous**, green aerial axes (~20 cm); stomata and cuticle on the axes.
- **Stele:** **protostele (haplostele)** — central xylem (annular tracheids), phloem around it.
- **Sporangium:** at the tips of axes, oval, thick-walled, **homosporous**; no dehiscence mechanism.
- **Importance:** **Telome theory (W. Zimmermann, 1930/1952)** — megaphylls and other organs arose from *Rhynia*-like dichotomous axes (telomes) by **overtopping, planation, webbing (syngenesis), reduction, recurvation**.
- The oldest known vascular plant — ***Cooksonia*** (Silurian, ~430 million years).

## 5. *Psilotum* — whisk fern (living)

- Tropical-subtropical, epiphytic or on soil; *P. nudum*.
- **No roots** — rhizoids on an underground rhizome, mycorrhiza.
- Aerial stem **dichotomous**, green, angular; in place of leaves small, scale-like **enations** — without vascular tissue.
- **Stele:** protostele in the rhizome, **actinostele** in the aerial stem.
- **Synangium:** three fused sporangia (trilocular), on a short dichotomous lateral branch; **homosporous**; **no cone**.
- Gametophyte underground, cylindrical, non-photosynthetic, mycorrhiza-dependent; some even have vascular tissue.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Which character is present in *Psilotum*? (A) True roots and leaves (B) Dichotomous stem (C) Cone (D) Large compound leaves — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

## 6. *Lycopodium* — club moss / ground pine

- Moist mountain forests; creeping on the ground or hanging as epiphytes (*L. phlegmaria*, *L. squarrosum*). In newer systems many species are in ***Huperzia*** and ***Phlegmariurus*** (huperzine A — studied in Alzheimer's disease).
- **Sporophyte:** dichotomous/monopodial stem, small, spiral **microphylls**; adventitious roots.
- **Stele:** various forms of protostele — actinostele, plectostele, mixed; xylem **exarch**.
- **Sporangia:** kidney-shaped, single, on the adaxial surface of the sporophyll; sporophylls either leaf-like (*L. selago* — no separate cone) or in a terminal **strobilus** (*L. clavatum*).
- **Homosporous**.
- **Gametophyte (prothallus):** in many species **underground, mycorrhiza-dependent, long-lived (several years)**; bisexual; antherozoids **biflagellate**.
- **Uses:** spores — "**lycopodium powder**" / "vegetable sulphur" — highly inflammable, old flash powder and fireworks; coating of pills; physics experiment (Kundt's tube).

## 7. *Selaginella* — spike moss

- ~700 species; moist, shady places, some xerophytes. **Resurrection plant:** *S. lepidophylla* (America), ***S. bryopteris*** (India — popular as "Sanjeevani").
- **Stem:** prostrate or erect, dichotomous.
- **Leaves:** microphylls, each with a small tongue-like outgrowth on the adaxial surface at its base — the **ligule** — hence a "ligulate" lycopsid (*Lycopodium* has no ligule); in many species **anisophyllous** — four rows: 2 small dorsal, 2 large ventral (*S. kraussiana*).
- **Rhizophore:** a leafless, positively geotropic organ arising from the point of dichotomy, at whose tip roots form — its nature (root/stem/unique organ) is disputed.
- **Stele:** protostele or **polystele**; the stele suspended in an air cavity by **trabeculae** — trabeculae = radially elongated endodermal cells, with Casparian strips; **vessels** in some species.
- **Heterosporous:** in a quadrangular strobilus a **megasporangium** (usually **4 megaspores** — one megaspore mother cell functional) and a **microsporangium** (many microspores).
- **Endosporic gametophytes:** the male gametophyte inside the microspore itself (1 prothallial cell + 1 antheridium) — biflagellate antherozoids; the female gametophyte inside the megaspore, the spore wall cracks at the triradiate mark and archegonia become visible.
- In some species the megaspore **is retained in the megasporangium** and fertilisation and embryo development happen there (*S. rupestris*, *S. monospora*) — **a foreshadowing of the seed habit**.

## 8. *Equisetum* — horsetail / scouring rush

- The **only living genus** of Sphenopsida; ~15–20 species; in India *E. debile*, *E. arvense*, *E. ramosissimum*.
- **Stem:** underground rhizome + aerial stem, distinct **nodes-internodes**; **ridges and furrows** on the internodes; **silica** in the epidermis — rough (used for scouring utensils — "scouring rush"); branches in whorls at the nodes.
- **Leaves:** small, scale-like, non-photosynthetic, fused in a whorl at the node into a **sheath**; the green stem carries out photosynthesis.
- **Internal structure:** central **pith cavity**; **vallecular canals** in the cortex below the furrows; **carinal canals** in the vascular bundles below the ridges (from breakdown of protoxylem) — three kinds of cavities; stele like an ectophloic siphonostele/eustele.
- **Strobilus:** terminal; hexagonal shield-like **peltate sporangiophores** in whorls on the axis, each with 5–10 hanging sporangia on its underside; in *E. arvense* a separate, non-green, unbranched **fertile stem** (in spring) — **dimorphic stems**.
- **Spores:** **homosporous**, but the spores bear four ribbon-like hygroscopic **elaters** (from the perispore) — they keep the spores bound in clusters; green, short-lived.
- **Functional heterospory:** identical spores, but depending on nutrition some form male, some bisexual prothalli.
- **Gametophyte:** free-living, green, terrestrial, lobed; antherozoids **multiflagellate**.
- Fossil relative — ***Calamites*** (Carboniferous, 20–30 m trees).

## 9. *Pteris* and *Dryopteris* — true ferns (leptosporangiate)

| Character | *Pteris* (brake fern) | *Dryopteris* (wood/shield fern) |
|---|---|---|
| Rhizome | Short, creeping/erect; **ramenta** (brown scales) | Stout, erect/oblique; ramenta abundant |
| Stele | **Solenostele** in *P. vittata*; dictyostele/polycyclic in some | **Dictyostele** |
| Leaf (frond) | Unipinnately compound, circinate vernation | Bi/tripinnate, circinate vernation |
| Position of sorus | **Continuous along the margin** of the pinna (marginal, coenosorus) | On veins on the lower surface of the pinna, in rows on both sides of the midrib |
| Indusium | **False indusium** — the rolled leaf margin | **True, reniform indusium** |

**Sporangium (leptosporangium):** long stalk + biconvex capsule; one-layered wall; on the margin an **incomplete annulus** — a row of cells with thick inner and radial walls; **stomium** — thin-walled lip cells. On drying, tension from the **cohesion** of water in the annulus cells → splitting at the stomium → the annulus springs back with a jerk and throws out the spores (catapult mechanism).

**Gametophyte (prothallus):** green, **cordate (heart-shaped)**, one cell thick (central part thick — cushion), **bisexual**; **antheridia** among the rhizoids in the posterior part, **archegonia** on the cushion near the anterior notch; antherozoids **multiflagellate, spiral**; chemotaxis towards **malic acid** released from the archegonium; usually protandrous → cross-fertilisation. The young sporophyte stays attached to the prothallus by its foot, then becomes free-living.

**Abnormalities (common in ferns):**
- **Apogamy:** a sporophyte directly from the gametophyte **without fertilisation** (from a vegetative cell) — first seen by **Farlow (1874)** in *Pteris cretica*.
- **Apospory:** a gametophyte (diploid prothallus) directly from the sporophyte **without meiosis/spores** — Druery (1884), *Athyrium*.
- *Pteris vittata* — **arsenic hyperaccumulator** — used in phytoremediation.

## 10. *Marsilea* — water fern ("four-leaved")

- In marshes/shallow water; in India *M. minuta*, *M. quadrifolia*; in Australia *M. drummondii* — sporocarp flour "**nardoo**" (Aboriginal food).
- **Sporophyte:** creeping rhizome, roots and leaves from the nodes; leaf on a long petiole, **palmately compound with four leaflets** (clover-like); leaflets close at night (nyctinasty).
- **Stele of the rhizome:** **amphiphloic siphonostele (solenostele)**; air chambers in the cortex (hydrophytic character).
- **Sporocarp:** a hard bean/kidney-shaped structure at the base of the petiole — a **modified pinna (leaflet)**; inside, **sori** in two rows, each sorus with **both mega- and microsporangia** (bisexual sorus) — **heterosporous**.
  - Extremely hard wall — **viable for decades (~100 years in some reports)**; survival in unstable habitats with drought and flood.
  - When the wall breaks in water, the mucilaginous **sorophore** (gelatinous ring) swells and pulls the sori out.
- Gametophytes endosporic; from the megaspore a female prothallus with one archegonium; male — 1 prothallial cell + 2 antheridia (16 multiflagellate antherozoids).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Functional significance of the sporocarp in *Marsilea*? (A) Eliminating the separate gametophyte stage and forming gametes directly (B) A modified structure enclosing the sporangia that allows delayed germination (C) A photosynthetic organ in place of leaves (D) Only food storage — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

**Other water ferns:** *Azolla* (*Anabaena azollae* symbiont, biofertiliser in rice — Chapter 9), *Salvinia* (*S. molesta* — invasive weed; in a whorl of three leaves one is a root-like modified leaf; no true roots). Other notable ones: *Ophioglossum* (adder's tongue; ***O. reticulatum* has the highest known chromosome number, 2n ≈ 1260**), *Adiantum* (maidenhair; *A. caudatum* — "walking fern", new plants from leaf tips), *Cyathea* (tree fern), *Isoetes* (quillwort — secondary growth, ligule), *Lygodium* (climbing fern), *Pteridium* (bracken — carcinogenic ptaquiloside).

## 11. Heterospory and seed habit

**Heterospory** — spores of two sizes: small **microspores** (→ male gametophyte) and large **megaspores** (→ female gametophyte). Its direct consequence — the gametophytes are **endosporic**, reduced and **differentiated into male and female**.

**Causes of origin (experimental/theoretical):** **abortion** of spores in the megasporangium — fewer spores, more nutrition → larger spores (Williamson, Goebel; Goebel's nutrition experiments on *Selaginella*). Fossil evidence — in the Devonian, heterospory arose **independently** in many lineages.

**Steps in seed formation (seed habit):**
1. Heterospory.
2. Reduction of the megaspores to **one** functional megaspore.
3. **Retention of the megaspore in the megasporangium** (not released).
4. **Endosporic** development of the female gametophyte, nourished by the sporophyte.
5. A protective **integument** around the megasporangium — i.e. formation of an ovule; sporangium = nucellus.
6. **In situ fertilisation** and embryo development; release of the seed with dormancy.

- In *Selaginella* (*S. rupestris*, *S. monospora*) up to **steps 1–4 and in situ fertilisation**; **no integument** → so it is "close to a seed", not a seed.
- Fossil seed-like structures: ***Lepidocarpon*** (Carboniferous lycopsid — integument-like covering on the megasporangium), *Miadesmia*. The oldest true seed — ***Elkinsia*** (Upper Devonian, ~365 million years), seed fern ***Lyginopteris*** (Carboniferous).

## 12. Palaeobotany — basics

### 12.1 Types of fossils

| Type | Formation | Example/importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Compression** | Plant flattened by pressure of sediment, a thin layer of carbon remains | Coal-film fossils of leaves; study of cuticle |
| **Impression** | Only an imprint of the shape, no organic matter | Leaf impressions of *Glossopteris* |
| **Petrifaction/permineralisation** | Minerals (silica, calcite, pyrite) fill the cells — **internal cellular structure preserved** | **Rhynie chert (*Rhynia*)**, **coal balls**, petrified plants of the Rajmahal Hills |
| **Mould & cast** | Plant decays leaving an empty mould; minerals fill it forming a cast | Pith casts of *Calamites* |
| **Compaction** | Pressed without chemical change | Seeds, fruits |
| **Amber** | Enclosed in resin | Insects, pollen |
| **Microfossils** | Pollen, spores — **palynology** | Age determination, palaeoclimate, oil-coal exploration |

- **Form/organ genera:** separate names for different organs of the same plant — e.g. *Lepidodendron* (stem), *Stigmaria* (root), *Lepidostrobus* (cone), *Lepidophyllum* (leaf); later a "reconstructed" plant.
- Why the **fossil record is incomplete**: soft tissues decay quickly; fossilisation needs rapid burial, absence of oxygen, sediment; later destroyed by erosion, metamorphism, volcanism, tectonics; in most terrestrial habitats fossils do not form at all.
- **Age determination:** radioactive methods — carbon-14 (up to ~50,000 years), potassium-argon, uranium-lead (hundreds of millions of years).

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Why is the fossil record mainly incomplete? (A) Fossils are equally preserved in all environments (B) Fossils only in volcanic rocks (C) Many organisms were not fossilised and others were destroyed by geological processes (D) Destroyed as soon as formed — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)**; fossils are usually found in sedimentary rocks, not volcanic rocks.

### 12.2 Plant groups on the geological time scale

| Period (million years ago, approx.) | Plant event |
|---|---|
| Ordovician (~470) | Spores of the first land plants (bryophyte level) |
| Silurian (~430) | *Cooksonia* — the first vascular plant |
| Devonian (~419–359) | *Rhynia*, *Zosterophyllum*; first forests (*Archaeopteris* — progymnosperm); heterospory; first seeds (Upper Devonian) |
| Carboniferous (~359–299) | **Age of pteridophytes** — *Lepidodendron*, *Sigillaria*, *Calamites*, seed ferns (*Lyginopteris*), *Cordaites*; coal formation |
| Permian (~299–252) | ***Glossopteris*** flora — **Gondwana** (India, Africa, South America, Australia, Antarctica) — evidence of continental drift; Gondwana coals of India |
| Triassic-Jurassic (~252–145) | **Age of gymnosperms/cycads** — Cycadales, **Bennettitales (*Williamsonia*)**, Ginkgoales, conifers; **Pentoxylales** (Rajmahal) |
| Cretaceous (~145–66) | **Rise of angiosperms** (*Archaefructus* ~125 mya) and rapid diversification |
| Tertiary-Quaternary | Dominance of angiosperms; grasslands |

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Correct chronological order of appearance of the major plant groups linked to terrestrial adaptation and vascular evolution? (A) Pteridophytes → bryophytes → gymnosperms → angiosperms (B) Bryophytes → pteridophytes → gymnosperms → angiosperms (C) Bryophytes → gymnosperms → pteridophytes → angiosperms (D) Gymnosperms → pteridophytes → bryophytes → angiosperms — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

### 12.3 Birbal Sahni and Indian palaeobotany (UP connection)

- **Prof. Birbal Sahni (1891–1949)** — "**Father of Indian palaeobotany**"; first Professor of Botany at Lucknow University.
- **Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP), Lucknow** — established in 1946 (then the "Institute of Palaeobotany"); foundation stone of the building laid by Pt. Nehru on 3 April 1949; Sahni died a week later (10 April 1949). Now an autonomous institute under DST.
- **Contributions:** study of the Jurassic flora of the **Rajmahal Hills (Jharkhand)**; a new order **Pentoxylales** (*Pentoxylon sahnii*, 1948) — a stem with five vascular segments; reconstruction of ***Williamsonia sewardiana*** (Bennettitales); *Glossopteris* flora, intertrappean fossils of the Deccan Traps, *Homoxylon*; also contributed to geological age determination (Salt Range).
- **Other fossils:** ***Lyginopteris oldhamia*** — a Carboniferous **seed fern (Pteridospermales)**; Oliver and Scott (1904), by showing the seed *Lagenostoma lomaxii* associated with it, proved that some plants with fern-like leaves bore seeds. ***Glossopteris*** — index of Gondwana; *Vertebraria* its root.

## Traps and exceptions

- **Vessels are generally absent** in pteridophytes, but *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*, *Pteridium*, *Marsilea* are exceptions.
- *Psilotum* has no root, no true leaf, no cone — only synangia.
- Ligule in *Selaginella*, not in *Lycopodium*; *Isoetes* also has a ligule.
- *Equisetum* is homosporous (spores with elaters), even though functionally some male prothalli form.
- *Pteris* — false indusium; *Dryopteris* — true reniform indusium.
- The sporocarp of *Marsilea* = a modified pinna; both mega- and microsporangia in the same sorus.
- *Selaginella* is very close to a seed, but has **no integument** — so not a seed.
- Reversing apogamy (sporophyte without fertilisation) and apospory (gametophyte without spores) is a common mistake.
- The highest chromosome number — *Ophioglossum reticulatum* (a pteridophyte), not an animal or angiosperm.
- *Aglaophyton* (old *Rhynia major*) has no true tracheids.
- In PPG I, *Equisetum* and *Psilotum* are in the ferns (Polypodiopsida).
- Fossils form in sedimentary rocks, not igneous ones.

## Quick revision

- First vascular plants; sporophyte dominant; prothallus free-living; fertilisation in water.
- Reimers's four classes: Psilopsida, Lycopsida, Sphenopsida, Pteropsida.
- Stelar theory — Van Tieghem and Douliot (1886); evolutionary sequence — Jeffrey.
- Protostele — *Rhynia*, *Selaginella*; actino — *Psilotum*; plecto — *Lycopodium clavatum*; soleno — *Marsilea*; dictyo — *Dryopteris*; polystele — *Selaginella laevigata*.
- *Rhynia* — Rhynie chert, Devonian, Kidston and Lang; telome theory — Zimmermann.
- *Lycopodium* — homosporous, underground long-lived prothallus, lycopodium powder.
- *Selaginella* — ligule, rhizophore, trabeculae, heterosporous, 4 megaspores.
- *Equisetum* — silica, leaf sheath, sporangiophores, spores with elaters, three cavities.
- *Pteris* — marginal sorus, false indusium; *Dryopteris* — reniform indusium, dictyostele.
- Apogamy — Farlow 1874, *Pteris cretica*.
- *Marsilea* — sporocarp, sorophore, heterosporous; nardoo.
- *Azolla*–*Anabaena* — biofertiliser; *Pteris vittata* — arsenic hyperaccumulator.
- Steps of seed habit — heterospory, one megaspore, retention, endosporic gametophyte, integument, in situ fertilisation.
- Birbal Sahni — BSIP Lucknow (1946), *Pentoxylon*, *Williamsonia sewardiana*, Rajmahal.
- *Glossopteris* — Permian, Gondwana; *Lyginopteris* — seed fern.

## Practice questions (with answers)

1. *Rhynia* is a fossil of which period? — **Devonian** (Rhynie chert, Scotland).
2. Who gave the telome theory? — **W. Zimmermann**.
3. Type of stele in *Lycopodium clavatum*? — **Plectostele**.
4. Structure holding the stele in the air cavity in *Selaginella*? — **Trabeculae** (modified endodermis).
5. How many megaspores are usually in the megasporangium of *Selaginella*? — **Four**.
6. Hygroscopic bands on the spores of *Equisetum*? — **Elaters** (from the perispore).
7. Cavity below the ridges in *Equisetum*? — **Carinal canal** (vallecular below the furrows).
8. Indusium of *Pteris*? — **False indusium** (rolled leaf margin).
9. Stele of *Dryopteris*? — **Dictyostele**.
10. What has a role in dehiscence of the fern sporangium? — **Annulus** (cohesive tension) and stomium.
11. Fern antherozoids are attracted towards which substance? — **Malic acid**.
12. Formation of a sporophyte from a gametophyte without fertilisation? — **Apogamy**.
13. What is the sporocarp of *Marsilea* morphologically? — **A modified pinna (leaflet)**.
14. Plant with the highest chromosome number? — ***Ophioglossum reticulatum*** (2n ≈ 1260).
15. Which step of the seed habit is missing in *Selaginella*? — Formation of an **integument**.
16. Genus called the whisk fern? — ***Psilotum***.
17. In which city is BSIP? — **Lucknow** (Birbal Sahni, 1946).
18. Who discovered Pentoxylales? — **Birbal Sahni** (Rajmahal Hills).
19. Fossil type that preserves cellular structure? — **Petrifaction**.
20. Index genus of Gondwana flora? — ***Glossopteris*** (Permian).
