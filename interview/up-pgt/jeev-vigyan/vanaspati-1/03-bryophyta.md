This chapter goes from the Bryophyta part of NCERT Class 11 "Plant Kingdom" to B.Sc. type study — *Riccia*, *Marchantia*, *Anthoceros*, *Funaria* and *Sphagnum*.
In 2026 there were **5 questions** on Bryophyta: *Riccia* vs *Marchantia* sporophyte, the sequence of the *Funaria* life cycle, the hyaline cells of *Sphagnum*, the structure of the archegonium, and the "Irish moss" trap. That is, comparative structure and the sequence of the life cycle are most important.

> **In simple words:** Bryophytes are small green plants of moist, shady places — liverworts, hornworts and mosses. The green plant we see is the **gametophyte (n)**. Male and female sex organs form on it; the sperms swim through water to reach the egg — that is why they are called "amphibians". The **sporophyte (2n)** formed by fertilisation stays attached to the gametophyte itself and takes food from it.

## 1. General characters

- The first land plants, but **water is essential** for fertilisation — "**amphibians of the plant kingdom**".
- **Gametophyte (n) dominant**, free-living, photosynthetic; thalloid (liverworts) or leafy (mosses).
- No true root, stem, leaf or **vascular tissue (xylem-phloem)**; in place of roots, **rhizoids** — unicellular in liverworts, multicellular with oblique septa in mosses.
- Some mosses have water-conducting cells — **hydroids** (water) and **leptoids** (food), e.g. *Polytrichum*.
- Sex organs **multicellular and with a sterile jacket**: male — **antheridium**, female — **archegonium**.
- Antherozoids **biflagellate**, swim in water; chemotaxis towards chemicals released from the neck of the archegonium (sucrose etc. in mosses).
- The zygote forms an **embryo** (hence part of the Embryophyta).
- **Sporophyte (2n)** — foot, seta, capsule; **partly or fully dependent** on the gametophyte; meiosis in the capsule → **homospores**.
- Alternation of generations clear, **heteromorphic**.

### 1.1 Archegonium — flask-like structure

- **Neck:** 6 vertical rows of jacket cells; inside, **neck canal cells**; cover cells at the top.
- **Venter:** swollen lower part; inside, **one egg** and above it a **ventral canal cell**.
- At maturity the canal cells turn into mucilage — a passage for the sperm.
- The archegonium forms on the **gametophyte** (not on the sporophyte).

## 2. Classification

| Traditional class (Proskauer 1957, Rothmaler 1951 etc.) | Modern division | Main characters | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hepaticopsida (liverworts)** | Marchantiophyta | Thallus dorsiventral, unicellular rhizoids, ventral scales; many chloroplasts, no pyrenoid; sporophyte simple, usually elaters; no stomata | *Riccia*, *Marchantia*, *Pellia*, *Porella* (leafy liverwort), *Sphaerocarpos* |
| **Anthocerotopsida (hornworts)** | Anthocerotophyta | Thallus simple, no internal differentiation; **one chloroplast + pyrenoid** in each cell; sporophyte a long "horn", **basal intercalary meristem**, **columella**, **stomata**, pseudo-elaters | *Anthoceros*, *Notothylas* |
| **Bryopsida (mosses)** | Bryophyta | Protonema stage; leafy gametophyte; multicellular rhizoids; sporophyte complex — peristome, operculum, columella | *Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum*, *Pogonatum* |

- Today the three groups are considered separate divisions; their mutual relationship was disputed in molecular studies — newer studies (after 2018) have begun to treat bryophytes as a **monophyletic** group and the sister of vascular plants.
- Science of Bryophyta = **bryology**; Father of Indian bryology — **S.R. Kashyap** ("Liverworts of the Western Himalayas and the Punjab Plain").

## 3. *Riccia* — the simplest liverwort

- **Habitat:** moist soil, walls; *R. fluitans* aquatic (free-floating). In India *R. discolor*, *R. glauca*, *R. himalayensis*.
- **Thallus:** small, flat, **dichotomous**, often a **rosette**; a **median groove** on the dorsal surface, meristem in the notch at the apex.
- **Ventral surface:** a single row of multicellular **scales** + unicellular rhizoids — **smooth-walled** and **tuberculate (pegged)** (peg-like ingrowths inside — water conduction).
- **Internal structure:** upper **photosynthetic zone** — vertical **assimilatory filaments** with **air chambers** between them, above them **simple air pores** (not barrel-shaped); lower **storage zone** (starch).
- **Sex organs:** **sunken** in the dorsal groove (no stalk/receptacle); most species monoecious (*R. glauca*), some dioecious.
- **Sporophyte — the simplest among bryophytes:** only a **capsule** — **foot and seta absent**; capsule wall one layer, which later degenerates; **no elaters** (some sterile "nurse cells"); all sporogenous cells form spores.
- Spores are released only when the **gametophyte decays** (no dispersal mechanism); spores in tetrads.
- **Vegetative reproduction:** fragmentation by death of the posterior part, adventitious branches, tubers, persistent apices in some.

## 4. *Marchantia*

- **Habitat:** moist, shady soil, river banks; *M. polymorpha* cosmopolitan, *M. palmata*, *M. nepalensis* in India.
- **Thallus:** large, dichotomous, polygonal areas on the dorsal surface (each an air chamber), in the middle of each area a **barrel-shaped (compound) air pore** — cells in 4–5 tiers; the pores cannot open and close but the structure is complex.
- **Ventral:** two kinds of scales (appendiculate and simple) and smooth and tuberculate rhizoids.
- **Vegetative reproduction:** multicellular, bilobed, green **gemmae** in **gemma cups** on the dorsal surface — dispersed by raindrops; a characteristic feature of *Marchantia*.
- **Dioecious** — male and female thalli separate (*M. polymorpha*); sex chromosomes (U/V). (Sex chromosomes in plants were first discovered in the liverwort *Sphaerocarpos* — C.E. Allen, 1917.)
- **Antheridiophore:** an **8-lobed** flat disc on a long stalk; antheridia in pits on the upper surface.
- **Archegoniophore:** an umbrella-like disc on a stalk, usually with **9 finger-like rays**; archegonia in **inverted hanging rows** between the rays (because the dorsal side of the disc grows over), covered on both sides by the **perichaetium** (fringed covering).
- **Sporophyte:** **foot, seta (short), capsule** — all three parts; spores + **elaters** in the capsule (long, spirally thickened, hygroscopic cells — help spore dispersal); coverings — **calyptra** (from the venter of the archegonium), **pseudoperianth**, perichaetium; the capsule splits at the apex by teeth.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** Difference in life-cycle features of *Riccia* and *Marchantia*? (A) *Marchantia* has no sexual reproduction (B) The sporophyte of *Riccia* is reduced but structurally independent (C) The sporophyte of *Riccia* is extremely reduced — not differentiated into foot, seta, capsule, whereas in *Marchantia* foot, seta, capsule are differentiated (D) The sporophytes of both are comparable — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(C)**; *Riccia* has only a capsule, and the sporophyte of no bryophyte is independent.

## 5. *Anthoceros* — hornwort

- **Thallus:** dark green, lobed, **no internal differentiation** (no air chambers) — uniform parenchymatous cells; **each cell usually has one large chloroplast and a pyrenoid** (an algal character).
- Colonies of ***Nostoc*** in **mucilage cavities** on the ventral surface — nitrogen fixation (symbiosis).
- Rhizoids only smooth; no scales.
- **Antheridia endogenous** — from a hypodermal cell, in a closed chamber — unique among bryophytes; archegonia sunken in the thallus.
- **Sporophyte:** a long, cylindrical, green "horn" — **foot** + **basal intercalary meristem** + capsule; **no seta**.
  - **Indefinite growth** because of the meristem — spores keep forming for a long time.
  - **Chloroplasts and stomata** in the capsule wall — photosynthesises itself → **semi-independent sporophyte**.
  - A sterile **columella** in the centre, sporogenous tissue around it; along with spores, **pseudo-elaters** — multicellular, without spiral thickening.
  - Splits from the apex downwards into **two valves**.
- **Evolutionary importance:** a green, stomata-bearing, meristematic capsule — **Campbell** considered it a possible link in the origin of pteridophytes (a step towards an independent sporophyte).

## 6. *Funaria hygrometrica* — "cord moss"

- **Habitat:** moist walls, burnt ground (ash-covered places — "fire moss"), soil; cosmopolitan.
- **Name:** *hygrometrica* — the seta is hygroscopic; twists and untwists with moisture.

### 6.1 Gametophyte — two stages

1. **Protonema:** the spore germinates into a branched, green, filamentous, alga-like structure — two parts: **chloronema** (transverse septa, many chloroplasts) and **caulonema** (oblique septa, fewer chloroplasts); **buds** form on it.
2. **Leafy gametophore:** from the bud an upright axis ("stem"), with small leaves in spiral order on it (with a midrib, lamina one cell thick), at the base **multicellular, branched rhizoids with oblique septa**.

### 6.2 Reproduction

- **Monoecious, autoicous** — on the same plant but on different branches: **male branch** at the apex of the main axis (antheridia, **club-shaped paraphyses** among them, spreading **perigonial leaves** around — a flower-like "male flower"); **female branch** on a lateral branch formed later (archegonia + paraphyses + perichaetial leaves).
- **Protandrous** — antheridia mature first → ensures cross-fertilisation.
- Antherozoids biflagellate; swim in rainwater to reach the archegonium; one egg in each archegonium, only one sporophyte forms.

### 6.3 Sporophyte — the most complex among bryophytes

- **Foot** (embedded in the gametophyte, absorption), **long seta**, **capsule**.
- **Parts of the capsule:** (i) **apophysis** — basal sterile part, with **stomata** and chloroplasts — photosynthetic; (ii) **theca** — the middle spore-bearing part: **columella** in the centre, around it the **spore sac**, outside it air spaces and filaments; (iii) **operculum (lid)**; between operculum and theca the **annulus** (a ring of large cells).
- **Peristome:** the teeth seen when the operculum falls — **diplolepidous** — **16 outer** (red, thick, hygroscopic) and **16 inner** teeth; in dry air the teeth bend outward and release spores slowly — **a controlled mechanism of spore dispersal**.
- **Calyptra** — a cap formed from the archegonial wall, over the capsule.
- Being green, the sporophyte is partly self-sufficient for food, but depends on the gametophyte for water/minerals.

## 7. *Sphagnum* — peat moss (bog moss)

- The moss of acidic bogs; keeps growing at the top, while the lower part dies and forms **peat**.
- **Two kinds of cells in the leaves:** (i) narrow, living, green **chlorophyllous cells** (form a network); (ii) large, **dead, colourless hyaline cells** — with spiral thickenings and **pores**; like a sponge these absorb **15–20 times (some sources 20–25 times) their own weight of water**.
- **Making the environment acidic:** the cell wall releases H⁺ ions and takes up cations (ion exchange) → pH falls → decomposition slows → peat accumulates; preservation of "bog bodies".
- **Sporophyte:** almost no seta; the capsule is raised on the elongated stalk of the gametophyte, the **pseudopodium**; **dome-shaped columella** (the spore sac arches over the columella); no peristome; air pressure in the capsule blows off the operculum **explosively** ("air gun" mechanism).
- **Uses:** fuel (peat), packing for transporting live plants/flowers, garden soil conditioner (water retention), **wound dressing** in World War I (absorbent, mildly antibiotic), smoky flavour of Scotch whisky.

> **PYQ (UP PGT 2026):** The sponge-like nature of *Sphagnum* moss is mainly due to the presence of? (A) Vascular tissue (B) Hyaline cells (C) Rhizoids (D) Thick cuticle — Reasoned answer (official key not available): **(B)**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gametophyte | Thallus, rosette | Thallus, large | Thallus, simple | Protonema + leafy | Leafy, branches in clusters |
| Air pores | Simple | Barrel-shaped | None | — | — |
| Vegetative reproduction | Fragmentation, tubers | **Gemma cups** | Tubers, fragmentation | From protonema, secondary protonema | Fragmentation |
| Sexuality | Usually monoecious | Dioecious | Monoecious/dioecious | Monoecious (autoicous) | Monoecious/dioecious |
| Position of sex organs | Sunken in dorsal groove | Receptacles (on stalks) | Sunken; antheridia endogenous | Branch apices | On branches |
| Parts of sporophyte | **Capsule only** | Foot + seta + capsule | Foot + meristem + capsule | Foot + seta + complex capsule | Foot + capsule (on pseudopodium) |

| Spore release | On decay of thallus | Splitting by teeth | Into two valves | Peristome | Explosive operculum |

## 9. Evolution of the sporophyte and theories of origin

- **Theory of sterilisation (F.O. Bower):** with evolution, part of the sporogenous tissue became **sterile** — *Riccia* (almost all sporogenous) → *Marchantia* (foot, seta, elaters sterile) → *Anthoceros* (columella, wall) → *Funaria* (apophysis, columella, peristome) — a **progressive trend**.
- **Reduction theory (Kashyap, Goebel, Church):** the reverse order — the simple sporophyte of *Riccia* is **reduced**, not primitive.
- **Origin:** (i) **algal origin** — from green algae (heterotrichous ones like *Fritschiella* or ones like *Coleochaete*) — more accepted; (ii) **pteridophytic origin** — by reduction (Haskell, Kashyap etc.).
- **Evolutionary dead end:** no advanced group arose from bryophytes — the traditional view; new molecular evidence treats bryophytes and vascular plants as two branches of a common ancestor.

## 10. Ecological and economic importance

- **Pioneers:** mosses follow lichens on bare rocks — the second stage of **xerosere/lithosere** succession; soil formation.
- **Prevent soil erosion** — dense mats; water retention.
- **Peat** — fuel (Ireland, Scotland), horticulture; peatlands are large **carbon stores** of the world (on ~3% of land but a large share of soil carbon).
- **Pollution indicators** — mosses are sensitive to heavy metals and SO₂ (air pollution measured by the moss-bag technique).
- **Medicinal/other** — traditional liver medicine from *Marchantia* (the name "liverwort" from the liver-shaped thallus — Doctrine of Signatures); *Polytrichum* — among the tallest mosses; *Dawsonia superba* — among the largest mosses.
- **In India:** the Eastern Himalaya and Western Ghats are centres of bryophyte diversity.

## 11. Alternation of generations in plant groups — comparison

| Character | Bryophyta | Pteridophyta | Gymnosperms | Angiosperms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant generation | Gametophyte (n) | Sporophyte (2n) | Sporophyte | Sporophyte |
| Independence of gametophyte | Free-living, large | Free-living, small (prothallus) | Dependent on sporophyte, extremely reduced | Extremely reduced (pollen — 2–3 cells; embryo sac — 7 cells, 8 nuclei) |
| Independence of sporophyte | Dependent (through foot) | Free-living (except the embryo stage) | Free-living | Free-living |
| Vascular tissue | None | Yes (tracheids) | Yes (tracheids; vessels in Gnetales) | Yes (vessels, companion cells) |
| Spores | Homosporous | Most homo, some hetero | Hetero | Hetero |
| Archegonium | Yes | Yes | Yes (except *Gnetum*) | No |
| Water for fertilisation | Needed | Needed | No (pollen tube) | No |
| Seed | None | None | Naked | Enclosed in fruit |

> **Exam angle:** With evolution, **progressive reduction of the gametophyte and dominance of the sporophyte** — this is the biggest trend. Bryophyta is the only group in which the gametophyte is dominant.

## Traps and exceptions

- In bryophytes the **dominant generation is the gametophyte**; from pteridophytes onwards the sporophyte is dominant.
- The sporophyte of no bryophyte is fully independent — even that of *Anthoceros* is only **semi-independent**.
- *Riccia* has **neither foot nor seta** — but it has a capsule; saying "no sporophyte" is wrong.
- The antheridium of *Anthoceros* is endogenous; one chloroplast per cell (alga-like).
- In *Anthoceros*, an **intercalary meristem** in place of the seta.
- In *Funaria* the **leaves, stem, root** are not true — phylloids, cauloids, rhizoids.
- Rhizoids multicellular (oblique septa) in mosses, unicellular in liverworts.
- No peristome in *Sphagnum*; diplolepidous peristome in *Funaria*.
- Irish moss (alga), reindeer moss (lichen), club moss (pteridophyte), Spanish moss (angiosperm) — none is a true moss; peat moss is a true moss.
- *Marchantia* dioecious, *Funaria* monoecious.
- The archegonium is a structure of the gametophyte; it has **one** egg.

## Quick revision

- Bryophytes — amphibians of the plant kingdom; gametophyte dominant; no vascular tissue.
- Father of Indian bryology — S.R. Kashyap.
- Archegonium — neck (6 rows), neck canal cells, ventral canal cell, one egg.
- *Riccia* — simple air pores, sunken sex organs, capsule only, no elaters.
- *Marchantia* — barrel pores, gemma cups, dioecious, 8-lobed antheridiophore, 9-rayed archegoniophore, elaters.
- *Anthoceros* — *Nostoc* symbiont, one chloroplast/cell, endogenous antheridia, meristematic capsule, stomata, columella, pseudo-elaters.
- *Funaria* — protonema (chloronema + caulonema), autoicous, protandrous, apophysis-stomata, 16+16 peristome teeth.
- *Sphagnum* — hyaline cells, pseudopodium, peat, acidic bog.
- Sex chromosomes in plants first — *Sphaerocarpos* (Allen, 1917).
- Sterilisation theory — Bower; sporophyte complexity increases from *Riccia* towards *Funaria*.
- Sequence in a lithosere — crustose lichens → foliose lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest.
