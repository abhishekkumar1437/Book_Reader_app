3–4 polity questions are expected. These questions are almost always on **Article numbers, amendment years, "firsts" and the structure of institutions**. Since this is a teachers' exam, remember **Article 21A, 45, 51A(k), RTE 2009, NEP 2020** in particular. At the end come the legislature and current office-holders of Uttar Pradesh.

> **In simple words:** The Constitution tells us who holds power (executive, legislature, judiciary), how it is divided between the Centre and the States, and what the rights and duties of a citizen are.

## Preamble

- "We, the people of India…" — **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic**.
- "Socialist", "Secular", "Integrity" — added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**; the only amendment to the Preamble so far.
- The Preamble is part of the Constitution — **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)**; but it is not enforceable in court.

> **Remember:** **Education** was originally in the State List; it moved to the **Concurrent List** by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**. Forests, wildlife, weights and measures, and administration of justice also became concurrent by the same amendment.

## Fundamental Rights (Part III)

| Right | Articles |
|---|---|
| Equality | 14–18 (17 — abolition of untouchability; 18 — abolition of titles) |
| Freedom | 19–22 (19 — six freedoms; 21 — **life and personal liberty**) |
| **Right to education** | **21A** — 6–14 years, **86th Amendment 2002**; RTE Act **2009**, in force **1 April 2010** |
| Against exploitation | 23–24 (24 — children below 14 not in factories) |
| Freedom of religion | 25–28 |
| Cultural and educational | 29–30 (30 — educational institutions for minorities) |
| Constitutional remedies | **32** — Ambedkar: "heart and soul of the Constitution" |

- **Right to property** — removed from Fundamental Rights by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and placed in **Article 300A** (legal right).
- Writs — habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto (Supreme Court — 32; High Court — 226).
- Even during an emergency **Articles 20 and 21** are not suspended (44th Amendment).

## Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| 39A | Equal justice, free legal aid |
| 40 | Organisation of village panchayats |
| 44 | Uniform Civil Code |
| **45** | Early childhood care and education for children **below 6 years** (after the 86th Amendment) |
| 48 | Agriculture and animal husbandry; prohibition of cow slaughter |

| 50 | Separation of judiciary from executive |

- Fundamental Duties — **Swaran Singh Committee**, **42nd Amendment (1976)**; originally 10, now **11**.
- **51A(k)** — parents/guardians to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6–14 (86th Amendment 2002).

## Union Executive

| Office | Key facts |
|---|---|

| Pardon | Art. 72 (President), 161 (Governor) |

## Parliament

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Lok Sabha | Maximum 550 (now 543 elected); minimum age 25; term 5 years; **UP has 80 seats (the most)** |
| Rajya Sabha | Maximum 250 (238 + 12 nominated); permanent House, one-third retire every two years; age 30; **UP has 31 seats (the most)** |
| Money Bill | Art. 110; only in the Lok Sabha; certified by the Speaker |
| Joint sitting | Art. 108 — presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker |
| Budget | Art. 112 — "Annual Financial Statement" |
| New Parliament building | Inaugurated 28 May 2023; architect **Bimal Patel** |

## Judiciary

- Supreme Court — Art. 124; established 28 January 1950; maximum 34 judges including the CJI; retirement 65 years.
- Current CJI — **Justice Surya Kant (53rd, since 24 November 2025; term until 9 February 2027)**.
- High Courts — Art. 214; retirement 62 years.
- **Allahabad High Court** — established at **Agra** in 1866, shifted to Allahabad in **1869**; **Lucknow bench**; **the largest** High Court in the country by sanctioned strength of judges (160).

## Emergencies

| Type | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| National emergency | 352 | 1962 (China), 1971 (Pakistan), **1975 (internal disturbance, 25 June)** |
| President's Rule | 356 | First in Punjab (1951); **10 times in UP so far** |
| Financial emergency | 360 | Never imposed |

> **Common mistake:** The 44th Amendment (1978) replaced "internal disturbance" with "**armed rebellion**". In 2024 it was announced that 25 June would be observed as "Samvidhan Hatya Diwas" — this is different from 26 November (Constitution Day).

## Major amendments

| Amendment | Year | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1951 | 9th Schedule; Art. 15(4) |
| 7th | 1956 | Linguistic reorganisation of states |
| 42nd | 1976 | "Mini Constitution" — 3 words in the Preamble, Fundamental Duties, education concurrent |
| 44th | 1978 | Right to property removed; safeguards on emergency |
| 52nd | 1985 | Anti-defection (10th Schedule) |
| 61st | 1989 | Voting age **21 → 18** |
| 73rd / 74th | 1992 | Panchayats (in force from 24 April 1993) / Municipalities |
| 86th | 2002 | **Art. 21A, 45 changed, 51A(k)** |
| 101st | 2016 | **GST** (1 July 2017) |
| 103rd | 2019 | Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) — **10%** reservation |

> **Remember:** In 2026 **Kerala was renamed "Keralam"** (Kerala (Alteration of Name) Act, 2026; from 25 August 2026) — under **Article 3**; this is not counted as a constitutional amendment (Art. 368).

## Panchayati Raj

- **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957)** — three-tier system; first panchayat — **Nagaur, Rajasthan (2 October 1959)**.
- **73rd Amendment** — Gram Sabha, three tiers, 5-year term, at least **one-third** reservation for women, State Election Commission (243K), State Finance Commission (243I).
- National Panchayati Raj Day — **24 April**.
- **UP** — UP Panchayat Raj Act **1947**; three tiers — Gram Panchayat, Kshetra Panchayat, Zila Panchayat; **one-third** reservation for women in panchayats in UP (50% in many states).

## Traps and exceptions

- 21A came from the **86th** Amendment; the RTE Act is of **2009**, in force from **2010**.
- "Socialist, Secular, Integrity" in the Preamble — 42nd; "Unity" was in the original.
- The presiding officer of the Rajya Sabha is the Vice-President as "Chairman", not "Speaker".
- States with a Legislative Council — UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana (6).
- Anglo-Indian nomination (Art. 331, 333) ended by the 104th Amendment (2020) — the UP Assembly is now just **403** (earlier 403 + 1).
- Allahabad HC was earlier at **Agra**.
