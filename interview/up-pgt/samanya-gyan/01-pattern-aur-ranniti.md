In the new UPESSC pattern, the written exams for both Lecturer (PGT) and TGT have **30 General Knowledge questions**, common to both exams. Compared with the 90 subject questions this section is small, but it carries **90 marks**, i.e. **one-quarter** of the total 360 written marks. While Biology needs deep understanding for each question, in GK the right preparation brings **more marks in less time**. This chapter explains what to study, how much to study and how to finish it in 44 days.

> **In simple words:** 30 questions × 3 marks = 90 marks. A wrong answer costs 1 mark. If you get 22 of 30 right and 4 wrong (leaving 4), your score = 66 − 4 = **62/90**. Scoring 60+ in GK by giving it 40–45 minutes a day is entirely possible.

## The exam at a glance

| Point | Fact (official advertisement 06/2026) |
|---|---|
| Written exam | **15–16 December 2026**, offline OMR |
| Total questions | **120** = 30 General Knowledge + 90 subject (Biology) |
| Time | **2 hours** (120 minutes) |
| Options | 4 |
| Marking | Correct **+3**, wrong or more than one bubble **−1**, unattempted **0** |
| Marks of GK section | **30 × 3 = 90** |
| Selection | 400 marks = 360 written + 40 interview |

> **Remember:** The old UPSESSB pattern (up to advertisement 02/2022) had **no** General Knowledge section — all 125 questions were from the subject. So no old UP PGT GK question paper is available. The level has been estimated from the revised UP TGT/PGT syllabus, UPSSSC PET and the UP-GK questions of UPPSC.

## The GK section in the exam hall

1. **Do GK first** — at most **18–20 minutes** for 30 questions. This section is quick and builds confidence; the remaining 100 minutes are for the 90 Biology questions.
2. **Three rounds:** (i) what you know for sure, immediately; (ii) where two options can be eliminated, mark and return later; (iii) what you do not know at all, leave it.
3. Fill the **OMR** every 10 questions, not all at once at the end.

### Maths of negative marking (+3 / −1, four options)

| Situation | Probability of being right | Expected value (EV) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blind guess (no option eliminated) | 1/4 | (3 − 3)/4 = **0** | Leave it |
| One option eliminated | 1/3 | (3 − 2)/3 = **+0.33** | Small gain; attempt if time allows |
| Two options eliminated | 1/2 | (3 − 1)/2 = **+1** | **Definitely attempt** |
| Sure answer | 1 | **+3** | — |

> **Common mistake:** Answering in current affairs because a name "sounds familiar". A blind guess gives zero on average, but one wrong "hunch" eats a third of one of your correct answers. Guess only when at least **two options are surely wrong**.
