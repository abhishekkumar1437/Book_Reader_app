Half of the 7–9 UP GK questions may come from this chapter. The question format is almost fixed — **"which is not correctly matched"** (fair–district, craft–city, dance–region, freedom fighter–district) and **"when/where"** (Chauri-Chaura, Kakori, 1857). Remember every table along with its "district".

> **In simple words:** Remember UP's culture by dividing it into three regions — **Braj** (Mathura-Agra: Raslila, Charkula, Lathmar Holi, Braj Bhasha), **Awadh** (Lucknow-Ayodhya: Kathak, Thumri, Chikankari, Awadhi), **Purvanchal-Bundelkhand** (Varanasi: Banarasi sari, Kajari, Bhojpuri; Jhansi-Mahoba: Alha, Rai, Bundeli).

## Administrative history — the journey to becoming "Uttar Pradesh"

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1775 | Banaras region to the Company (Treaty of Faizabad, Asaf-ud-Daulah) |
| 1801 | After the Subsidiary Alliance, Awadh ceded Rohilkhand and the Doab to the Company |
| 1833–34 | **Agra Presidency** (separated from the Bengal Presidency) |
| 1836 | **North-Western Provinces** — Lieutenant Governor, capital Agra |
| 1856 | Annexation of Awadh (Chief Commissioner) |
| 1858 | Capital **Agra → Allahabad** |
| 1877 | North-Western Provinces and Awadh unified (under one head) |
| **1902** | Name — "**United Provinces of Agra and Oudh**" |
| 1920–21 | Capital **Lucknow** (Governor Harcourt Butler); Legislative Council at Lucknow in 1921 |
| 1937 | Name — "**United Provinces**"; first Congress government under Pant |
| **24 January 1950** | Name — "**Uttar Pradesh**" (this day is celebrated as "**UP Day**" since 2018) |
| **9 November 2000** | Uttaranchal (now Uttarakhand) separated |

> **Remember:** Celebration of UP Day (24 January) began in **2018** on the initiative of the then Governor **Ram Naik**; the **ODOP** scheme was also launched on this day.

## Nawabs of Awadh

| Nawab | Period | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| **Saadat Khan "Burhan-ul-Mulk"** | 1722–39 | **Founder** of the independent Nawabi dynasty of Awadh; capital Faizabad |
| Safdarjung | 1739–54 | Safdarjung's Tomb in Delhi |
| **Shuja-ud-Daulah** | 1754–75 | Defeated at **Buxar (1764)**; Treaty of Allahabad (1765) |
| **Asaf-ud-Daulah** | 1775–97 | Capital **Faizabad → Lucknow (1775)**; **Bara Imambara (1784, famine relief; Bhulbhulaiya)**, **Rumi Darwaza** — "Jisko na de Maula, usko de Asaf-ud-Daulah" (whom God does not give, Asaf-ud-Daulah gives) |
| Saadat Ali Khan II | 1798–1814 | **Subsidiary Alliance (1801)** |
| Ghazi-ud-Din Haidar | 1814–27 | First "**King**" (Badshah) of Awadh (1819) |
| Muhammad Ali Shah | 1837–42 | **Chhota Imambara** |
| **Wajid Ali Shah** | 1847–56 | Last Nawab; patron of Kathak-Thumri, pen-name "**Akhtar Piya**"; annexation in **1856**, exiled to **Matiaburj** in Calcutta |

> **Memory trick:** "**Saadat founded, Asaf adorned, Wajid lost**" — founder Saadat Khan, Asaf-ud-Daulah who made Lucknow the capital, and the last, Wajid Ali Shah.

## Traps and exceptions

- The founder of Awadh was **Saadat Khan**; **Asaf-ud-Daulah** made Lucknow the capital (1775).
- "United Provinces of Agra and Oudh" — **1902**; "Uttar Pradesh" — **24 January 1950**.
- Tatya Tope, Chandrashekhar Azad, Govind Ballabh Pant — active in UP, but **not** born in present-day UP.
- **Three** of the four Kakori martyrs were from **Shahjahanpur**; Lahiri was hanged at **Gonda**.
- **Charkula** is from Braj; **Chholiya** from Uttarakhand; **Rai** from Bundelkhand.
- Kajari — **Mirzapur**; Alha — **Bundelkhand**; Birha — **Ahirs**.
- Urdu became the second official language in **1989**; the Hindi Official Language Act is of **1951**.
- Sumitranandan Pant's birthplace is now in **Uttarakhand**.
