Between 1757 and 1858, Bihar moved from Mughal–Nawabi rule to the East India Company and then to the British Crown. The decisive battle for India's east, **Buxar (1764)**, was fought on Bihar's soil, and the oldest hero of 1857, **Babu Kunwar Singh of Jagdishpur**, was from Bihar. BPSC regularly asks about Mir Qasim, Buxar, the Wahabi movement, Peer Ali, the Arrah siege and Kunwar Singh.

> **In simple words:** After Plassey (1757), the Company controlled Bengal's Nawab. When Nawab Mir Qasim resisted from his new capital at Munger, he was defeated, and the allied Indian armies lost at Buxar in 1764. In 1765 the Company got the right to collect revenue (Diwani) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. Company rule brought heavy land revenue, opium and indigo farming, and famines. Anger exploded in 1857, when soldiers at Danapur and the old zamindar Kunwar Singh fought the British for almost a year.

All-India background: [Europeans and British Expansion](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/europeans-and-british-expansion), [British Policies, Reforms and Economic Impact](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/british-policies-reforms-and-economic-impact), [Revolt of 1857](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/revolt-of-1857), [Tribal and Peasant Movements](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/tribal-and-peasant-movements).

## From Plassey to Buxar

### Bihar after Plassey (1757)

- After **Plassey (23 June 1757)**, **Mir Jafar** became Nawab of Bengal (which included Bihar). **Raja Ram Narayan** stayed as deputy governor of Bihar at Patna.
- The Mughal prince **Ali Gauhar (later Shah Alam II)** invaded Bihar several times (1759–61) to win back Bengal and Bihar, but the Company's forces drove him back.

### Mir Qasim and Munger

**Mir Qasim** became Nawab in **1760**. He was able and wanted real power.

- He shifted his capital from **Murshidabad to Munger** (1762–63) to be away from Company pressure in Calcutta.
- He **modernised his army** with the help of the Armenian **Gurgin Khan** and set up a **gun and musket factory at Munger**.
- He objected to Company servants misusing **dastaks** (free trade passes) for their private trade; in 1763 he **abolished internal duties for everyone**, so Indian merchants could compete. The Company saw this as an attack.
- War began in 1763. He lost at **Katwa, Giria and Udhua Nala** (Bengal).
- In anger, his men under **Walter Reinhardt (Samru)** killed English prisoners at Patna — the **Patna massacre (October 1763)**.
- He fled to Awadh and formed an alliance with **Shuja-ud-Daula** (Nawab of Awadh) and the Mughal emperor **Shah Alam II**.

### Battle of Buxar (22–23 October 1764)

| Side | Leaders |
|---|---|
| Company | **Major Hector Munro** |
| Indian alliance | **Mir Qasim**, **Shuja-ud-Daula**, **Shah Alam II** |

- Fought near **Buxar** on the Ganga, in present **Buxar district**.
- The Company won decisively. Historians call Buxar more important than Plassey: Plassey was won mainly through **conspiracy** (Mir Jafar's betrayal), Buxar through **battle** against the combined power of the Mughal emperor and two Nawabs.

### Treaty of Allahabad (1765) and the Diwani

- **Robert Clive** signed the **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)** with Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daula.
- Shah Alam II gave the Company the **Diwani** — the right to collect **land revenue** — of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.
- This began the **dual government (1765–72)**: the Company collected revenue (diwani), while the Nawab kept police and justice (nizamat) without money. For Bihar, **Raja Shitab Rai** acted as the **naib diwan** at Patna.
- The **great famine of 1769–70** struck Bengal and Bihar under this system; about one-third of Bengal's population is believed to have died.
- **Warren Hastings** ended the dual system in **1772**.

> **Prelims angle:** Buxar — **1764**, **Hector Munro**; Diwani — **1765**, **Treaty of Allahabad**, given by **Shah Alam II**. Bihar's naib diwan — **Shitab Rai** (Bengal's — Muhammad Reza Khan).

## Company rule in Bihar — economy and society

### Golghar (1786)

- After the 1770 famine, Warren Hastings ordered a grain store. **Captain John Garstin** built the **Golghar** at Patna in **1786**.
- It is a beehive-shaped granary about **29 m high**, with spiral stairs outside (about **145 steps**); its designed capacity is often given as **about 1,40,000 tonnes** of grain.
- Because of a design error (doors opening inward), it was never filled to capacity.

### Permanent Settlement (1793)

**Lord Cornwallis** introduced the **Permanent Settlement** in **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** in **1793**. **Zamindars** became owners of land and had to pay a **fixed revenue** for ever. If they failed, their land was auctioned (the **sunset law**).

Effects on Bihar:

- Zamindars, many absentee, squeezed tenants with high rents and illegal cesses (**abwabs**).
- Peasants had no secure rights; this was the root of Bihar's **agrarian unrest** — the Kisan Sabha movement and the post-independence **zamindari abolition** of 1950.
- Big estates such as **Darbhanga, Bettiah, Hathwa, Tekari, Dumraon** grew powerful.

### Opium

- The Company made opium a **state monopoly** (from 1773, consolidated in 1797). Bihar (with eastern UP) was the main **poppy-growing** area.
- The **Patna (Bihar) Opium Agency** had its factory at **Gulzarbagh, Patna**; opium went to China — a key part of the triangular trade that led to the **Opium Wars**.
- Farmers were forced to grow poppy under advances and low fixed prices.

### Indigo

- European planters set up **indigo** (blue dye) estates in **Tirhut, Saran and Champaran** from the late 18th century.
- In Champaran, the **tinkathia system** forced tenants to grow indigo on **3 kathas out of every 20** (3/20 of their land). Protests broke out in 1867 (Lalsaraiya) and in **1907–08** (Sathi and Bettiah area, led by **Sheikh Gulab** and **Sitaram Rai**), leading up to Gandhi's **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)** — see [Bihar in the Freedom Struggle](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-in-the-freedom-struggle).

### Other changes

- **Patna Kalam** painters, trained in the Mughal style, began painting everyday scenes for British buyers ("Company painting") — see [Art and Culture](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/art-culture-festivals-and-languages).
- The **Son canal system** (anicut at **Dehri**, completed in the 1870s) brought irrigation to the Shahabad (Bhojpur–Rohtas) region.
- **Railways**: the East Indian Railway line through Bihar was opened in the early 1860s.
- **Patna College** was founded in **1863** — the oldest college in Bihar.

### The Wahabi movement and Patna

The **Wahabi movement** in India (better called the **Tariqah-i-Muhammadiya**) aimed to purify Islam and later turned against British rule.

- Founder in India: **Syed Ahmad of Rae Bareli** (1786–1831). He was killed fighting the Sikhs at **Balakot (1831)**.
- **Patna** became the main centre. The leaders **Wilayat Ali** and **Inayat Ali** of **Sadiqpur (Patna)** organised a network that sent men and money to the frontier base at **Sittana** (North-West Frontier).
- Aim shifted from fighting the Sikh kingdom of Punjab to fighting the British (after 1849).
- The British crushed it through a series of trials: **Ambala (1864)**, **Patna (1865)**, Malda and Rajmahal (1870). Leaders like **Maulvi Ahmadullah** were sentenced to transportation (Andaman).
- **W. W. Hunter's** book ***The Indian Musalmans*** (1871) was written in the background of this movement.

> **Bihar connection:** Patna's Wahabi leaders were in contact with soldiers and townspeople during 1857. This is why Commissioner **William Tayler** arrested Wahabi maulvis at Patna in June 1857.

## The Revolt of 1857 in Bihar

Bihar (then part of the **Bengal Presidency**) was one of the main centres of the revolt in the east. The rising here was both a **sepoy mutiny** (Danapur) and a **people's war** (Kunwar Singh and the Bhojpur peasants).

### Main events

| Date (1857–58) | Place | Event |
|---|---|---|
| **12 June 1857** | **Rohini** (Deoghar, now Jharkhand) | Soldiers of the 5th Irregular Cavalry killed officers — the first outbreak in the Bihar region |
| June 1857 | Patna | Commissioner **William Tayler** arrested leading Wahabi maulvis by inviting them to a meeting |
| **3 July 1857** | **Patna** | Rising led by **Peer Ali**, a bookseller; **Dr. Lyell** killed. Peer Ali was captured and **hanged on 7 July 1857** with others |
| July 1857 | **Muzaffarpur (Tirhut)** | **Waris Ali**, a police jamadar, was arrested for contact with rebels and hanged — remembered as an early martyr of 1857 in Bihar |
| **25 July 1857** | **Danapur** | Three sepoy regiments (**7th, 8th and 40th Native Infantry**) revolted, crossed the Son and marched to **Arrah** to join Kunwar Singh |
| **27 July – 3 August 1857** | **Arrah** | Siege of the "**Arrah House**" (a small fortified house) defended by **Herwald Wake** and engineer **Vicars Boyle** with a handful of Europeans and Sikh soldiers |
| 29–30 July 1857 | Near Arrah | A British relief force under **Captain Dunbar** was ambushed and badly beaten |
| **3 August 1857** | Arrah | **Major Vincent Eyre** relieved Arrah |
| 12–13 August 1857 | **Jagdishpur** | Eyre captured Kunwar Singh's base; Kunwar Singh moved west |
| Aug 1857 – Mar 1858 | Rewa, Banda, Kalpi, Kanpur, Lucknow | Kunwar Singh moved through central India and Awadh, joining other rebels |
| **March 1858** | **Azamgarh (Atraulia)** | Defeated a British force under **Colonel Milman** and occupied Azamgarh |
| April 1858 | **Shivpur Ghat** (crossing the Ganga, near Ballia) | His wrist/arm was hit by a bullet; he **cut off the arm with his own sword and offered it to the Ganga** (famous legend) |
| **23 April 1858** | **Jagdishpur** | Defeated a British force under **Captain Le Grand** and re-entered Jagdishpur |
| **26 April 1858** | Jagdishpur | **Kunwar Singh died** from his wounds |
| 1858–59 | Bhojpur, Kaimur hills | His brother **Amar Singh** continued guerrilla fighting |

### Babu Veer Kunwar Singh

- **Zamindar of Jagdishpur** (Bhojpur, then Shahabad district), of the **Ujjainiya (Parmar) Rajput** family.
- He was about **80 years old** in 1857 — the **oldest major leader** of the revolt.
- His estate was in debt; the Company's Board of Revenue had refused him help. His grievance was both personal and against Company rule.
- He used **guerrilla warfare** (hit-and-run attacks), moving fast across Bihar, central India and Awadh.
- Associates: **Amar Singh** (brother), **Hare Krishna Singh** (commander), **Nishan Singh** (hanged at **Sasaram** in 1858).
- His victory at Jagdishpur on **23 April 1858** is celebrated in Bihar as **"Vijayotsav" / Kunwar Singh Vijay Diwas**.
- **Veer Kunwar Singh University**, Arrah (est. **1992**), and the **Kunwar Singh Park** at Patna honour him.

> **Mains angle:** Use Kunwar Singh to show that 1857 in Bihar had **popular support**: peasants of Bhojpur joined him, and his long campaign beyond Bihar shows a wider vision. Add Peer Ali (urban, Patna), Danapur sepoys (military), Waris Ali (police) and the Wahabi network (religious-political) — four streams in one province.

> **Common mistake:** Do not say "Kunwar Singh died in battle." He **won** the battle at Jagdishpur on 23 April and **died three days later** (26 April 1858) of his wounds.

### Other 1857 centres in the old Bihar region (now Jharkhand)

| Centre | Leader | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ranchi, Hazaribagh, Doranda | **Thakur Vishwanath Shahdeo**, **Pandey Ganpat Rai** | Chotanagpur rising |
| Palamu | **Nilambar and Pitambar** (Kharwar/Bhogta) | Tribal rising |
| Singhbhum | Ho tribals, Raja Arjun Singh of Porahat | — |

These were in the Bihar–Chotanagpur area in 1857 and are part of "undivided Bihar" history.

### Why the revolt failed in Bihar

1. **Lack of coordination** between Patna, Danapur, Arrah and other centres.
2. Many big zamindars (for example the **Darbhanga, Hathwa, Dumraon** houses) **supported the British**.
3. Rebels had old weapons; the British had railways, the telegraph and Enfield rifles.
4. The British quickly brought troops up the Ganga by steamer.
5. Kunwar Singh's death (April 1858) removed the main leader.

### Results

- Queen Victoria's **Proclamation (1 November 1858)** ended Company rule.
- Bihar remained part of the **Bengal Presidency** until 1912.
- The memory of Kunwar Singh became part of Bhojpuri folk songs and later inspired nationalists.

### Mains questions

**1. "The Battle of Buxar, not Plassey, laid the real foundation of British rule in India." Discuss with reference to Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Plassey (1757) was won through conspiracy; Buxar (1764), fought in Bihar, was a real military victory.
- **Body:** Mir Qasim's reforms at Munger and the dastak conflict; the alliance of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II; Munro's victory; Treaty of Allahabad and Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; the Company became the real ruler with legal authority from the Mughal emperor; Bihar's revenue and saltpetre now served the Company; dual government and the 1770 famine.
- **Conclusion:** Buxar turned the Company from traders into rulers; Bihar was the first province to feel its full weight.

**2. Assess the role of Babu Kunwar Singh in the Revolt of 1857. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** The 80-year-old zamindar of Jagdishpur became the leading rebel of eastern India.
- **Body:** Background and grievances; leadership of the Danapur sepoys; Arrah siege; guerrilla campaign through Rewa, Banda, Kanpur, Lucknow, Azamgarh; crossing of the Ganga and the legend of the arm; victory of 23 April 1858; death on 26 April; Amar Singh carried on; popular support from Bhojpur peasants; place in folk songs.
- **Conclusion:** He showed courage and tactical skill; his memory is a symbol of Bihar's resistance (Vijayotsav, VKS University).

**3. Describe the impact of Company rule on the economy of Bihar between 1765 and 1857. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Diwani made Bihar a revenue source for the Company.
- **Body:** 1769–70 famine; Permanent Settlement and rise of zamindars; rack-renting and abwabs; opium monopoly and forced poppy cultivation; indigo and tinkathia; decline of crafts and the Patna textile trade; saltpetre export; little investment except later canals.
- **Conclusion:** These created the agrarian crisis that fed 1857, the Champaran Satyagraha and the Kisan Sabha.
