"Geography of Bihar" is a named Prelims head and part of GS Paper II in Mains. Prelims asks about latitude, neighbours, the highest point, which districts touch Nepal, soil types and physiographic regions. Mains asks you to link physical geography to floods, agriculture and development. This chapter is the base for the next two chapters on rivers and resources.

> **In simple words:** Bihar is a landlocked state in the middle Ganga plain. A tiny strip of Shivalik hills lies in the far north-west, a flat and fertile alluvial plain covers most of the state, and low plateau hills mark its southern edge. The Ganga flows west to east through the middle and divides the state into North Bihar and South Bihar.

### 1. The Shivalik (Himalayan foothill) region

- Found only in the **north-west of West Champaran**, along the Nepal border; area less than 1,000 km².
- Made of young **Tertiary** sedimentary rocks — the outermost range of the Himalaya.
- Three parts: **Someshwar range** (north), **Ramnagar Dun** hills (south), and the **Harha (Dun) valley** between them.
- **Highest point of Bihar: Someshwar peak** (about **880 m**; some sources give 874 m), on the Someshwar range near the **Someshwar fort**.
- Covered with forests of **sal**; part of **Valmiki National Park and Tiger Reserve**.

### 2. The Ganga plain (the largest part)

The plain is made of **alluvium** — fine silt, sand and clay deposited by rivers. It is one of the most fertile and most densely populated regions in the world. The Ganga splits it.

#### (a) North Bihar plain (north of the Ganga)

- Larger than the south plain. Built by Himalayan rivers — **Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla-Balan, Kosi, Mahananda**.
- Very gentle slope from north-west to south-east; the water table is high.
- **Terai belt**: a marshy, forested strip along the Nepal border (West and East Champaran to Kishanganj), with fine soils and high moisture.
- Huge **alluvial fans** (cone-shaped deposits where a river leaves the hills): the **Kosi fan** (one of the largest inland fans in the world) and the **Gandak fan**.
- Rivers shift course often — especially the **Kosi**, which has moved about 100–150 km westward over the last two centuries.
- Most flood-prone part of India (see the next chapter).

#### (b) South Bihar plain (south of the Ganga)

- Narrower; built by the Ganga and by southern rivers — **Karmanasa, Son, Punpun, Phalgu, Kiul, Chandan**.
- Slopes north towards the Ganga; rises gently to the plateau in the south.
- Contains **isolated hills** of old hard rock: **Rajgir hills** (five hills around Rajgir), **Barabar hills** (Jehanabad), **Gaya hills** (Pretshila, Ramshila, Brahmayoni), **Sheikhpura hills**, **Kharagpur hills** (Munger), **Jethian** hills.
- Has the **tal** lands — the **Mokama Tal**, **Barahiya Tal**, **Fatuha Tal** — between Patna and Lakhisarai.
- Irrigated by the old **Son canal** system — the "rice bowl" of Bihar (Rohtas, Bhojpur, Buxar).

### 3. The southern plateau fringe

- A broken belt along the Jharkhand border — the northern edge of the **Chotanagpur plateau** and the **Vindhyan Kaimur plateau**.
- **Kaimur plateau** (Kaimur and Rohtas districts): flat-topped **Vindhyan sandstone** hills, steep cliffs, waterfalls (Telhar Kund, Karkat), the **Rohtasgarh** fort, **Kaimur Wildlife Sanctuary** (Bihar's largest).
- Chotanagpur fringe in **Gaya, Nawada, Jamui, Banka, Munger**: old **granite and gneiss** rocks, red soils, forests of sal and mahua; **Kakolat waterfall** (Nawada).
- **Hot springs**: **Rajgir** (Brahmakund, Saptadhara), **Bhimbandh** (Munger), **Sitakund** (Munger) — water rises along deep cracks in old rocks.

| Division | Main rocks | Height | Key places |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shivalik | Tertiary sandstone, conglomerate | Highest (to ~880 m) | Someshwar, Ramnagar, Valmikinagar |
| North plain | Quaternary alluvium (new) | ~50–100 m | Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Purnia |
| South plain | Alluvium (older) with hill inliers | ~50–150 m | Patna, Gaya, Nalanda |
| Plateau fringe | Vindhyan sandstone; Archaean granite–gneiss; Dharwar quartzite | ~150–500 m | Kaimur, Rajgir, Kharagpur, Jamui |

> **Prelims angle:** Highest peak — **Someshwar (West Champaran)**. Hot springs — **Rajgir, Bhimbandh, Sitakund**. Bihar's only Himalayan part — **north-west West Champaran**. Kaimur plateau — **Vindhyan** rocks; Rajgir hills — very old rocks (Dharwar quartzite).

## Special landforms of the plains

These local words are asked in Prelims and used in Mains answers on floods and fisheries.

| Landform | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Chaur** | A large, saucer-shaped **low-lying depression** that stays waterlogged for much of the year | North Bihar (Darbhanga, Samastipur, Begusarai, Muzaffarpur) |
| **Maun** | An **oxbow lake** — a curved lake left behind when a river changes course | **Kanwar Jheel (Kabartal)**, Begusarai |
| **Tal** | A wide **low basin** south of the Ganga that floods in the monsoon and grows **rabi crops (pulses)** after the water drains | **Mokama Tal**, **Barahiya Tal** — the "pulse bowl" |
| **Diara** | **Riverine land and islands** in and along the riverbed, formed by shifting rivers; flooded each year, farmed in the dry season | Along the Ganga and Kosi |
| **Bhangar** | Older, higher alluvium, above flood level | Parts of south Bihar |
| **Khadar** | Newer alluvium on floodplains, renewed by floods | North Bihar |
| **Alluvial fan** | Cone of sediment where a river leaves the hills | Kosi fan, Gandak fan |

> **Bihar connection:** Chaurs and mauns cover large areas of north Bihar. Turning them into **fish farms, makhana ponds and wetlands** is a key part of the state's fisheries and Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali plans — good Mains point.

## Geology in brief

- **Archaean (oldest) rocks**: granite and gneiss in the southern fringe (Jamui, Banka, Gaya, Nawada).
- **Dharwar** rocks: quartzite and schist in the Rajgir and Kharagpur hills.
- **Vindhyan** rocks: sandstone, shale and **limestone** in the Kaimur plateau (source of limestone for cement).
- **Tertiary** rocks: the Shivalik hills.
- **Quaternary alluvium**: the whole Ganga plain — in places hundreds of metres thick.

## Soils of Bihar

| Soil | Where | Features | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Piedmont swamp soil** | North-west West Champaran (foot of the hills) | Marshy, heavy | Rice |
| **Terai soil** | Along the Nepal border | Fine, moist, rich in organic matter | Rice, jute, sugarcane |
| **New alluvium (khadar)** | Floodplains of north Bihar | Light, renewed by floods; sandy loam | Rice, maize, wheat, jute |
| **Balsundari** | North-west plain (Champaran, Muzaffarpur, Vaishali, Samastipur) | **Calcareous** (rich in lime), light | **Sugarcane**, maize, tobacco, chillies |
| **Old alluvium (bhangar)** | South Bihar plain | Firm, less fertile than khadar | Rice, wheat |
| **Tal soil** | Mokama–Barahiya Tal | Heavy clay, waterlogged in monsoon | **Pulses (masoor, chana)** in rabi |
| **Karail–Kewal** | South-west (Rohtas, Bhojpur, Buxar, Kaimur, Aurangabad, Gaya) | **Heavy clay**, cracks when dry, black-grey | Paddy, wheat, gram |
| **Red and yellow soil** | Plateau fringe (Banka, Jamui, Nawada, Gaya, Kaimur) | Porous, poor in nitrogen | Coarse grains, pulses, oilseeds |

Common problems: **waterlogging** in chaurs and tals, **soil erosion** on the plateau fringe, **sand casting** (sand deposited on fields after floods, especially after the 2008 Kosi breach), and **low organic carbon** (see soil health card data).

## Agro-climatic zones of Bihar

Bihar is divided into **four agro-climatic zones** for farm planning.

| Zone | Name | Districts (examples) | Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone I** | North-West Alluvial Plain | West & East Champaran, Saran, Siwan, Gopalganj, Muzaffarpur, Vaishali, Sitamarhi, Sheohar, Darbhanga, Madhubani, Samastipur, Begusarai | Light soils, sugarcane, rice, maize |
| **Zone II** | North-East Alluvial Plain | Purnia, Katihar, Araria, Kishanganj, Saharsa, Supaul, Madhepura, Khagaria | Highest rainfall; jute, rice, maize, makhana |
| **Zone III-A** | South-East Alluvial Plain | Bhagalpur, Banka, Munger, Jamui, Lakhisarai, Sheikhpura | Mixed; pulses in tal areas |
| **Zone III-B** | South-West Alluvial Plain | Patna, Nalanda, Bhojpur, Buxar, Rohtas, Kaimur, Gaya, Jehanabad, Arwal, Nawada, Aurangabad | Lowest rainfall; canal irrigation; rice–wheat; drought-prone parts |

## Earthquake risk

- Bihar lies close to the Himalayan collision zone, so it faces high **seismic** (earthquake) risk.
- Under the long-used BIS seismic map, Bihar falls in **Zones III, IV and V**; the districts along the Nepal border and in the Kosi region (for example **Madhubani, Supaul, Araria, Darbhanga, Sitamarhi**) are in **Zone V** (very high risk). A **revised national seismic zonation map** was released by BIS in 2025 — check the latest classification of Bihar's districts.
- Major past earthquakes: **15 January 1934** (Bihar–Nepal; Munger and Motihari badly hit), **21 August 1988** (Bihar–Nepal border), **25 April 2015** (Nepal earthquake; deaths in Bihar).
- Soft alluvium **amplifies shaking** and can cause **liquefaction** (wet sand behaving like liquid).

### Mains questions

**1. Describe the physiographic divisions of Bihar and explain how they influence its economy. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar is mostly plain, with hills at the north-west and south edges.
- **Body:** Shivalik — forests, tiger reserve, tourism; Terai — rice, jute, high moisture; North plain — fertile but flood-prone, chaurs and fisheries, makhana; South plain — canal irrigation, rice–wheat, tal pulses, hill tourism (Rajgir, Gaya); Plateau fringe — limestone, stone, forests, drought; earthquake risk; very high population density on the plain.
- **Conclusion:** Policy must be region-specific — flood management in the north, water harvesting in the south, forests and tourism at the edges.

**2. "North Bihar and South Bihar face opposite water problems." Discuss with reference to physical geography. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** The Ganga divides two different hydrological regions.
- **Body:** North — Himalayan rivers, heavy silt, shifting courses, alluvial fans, chaurs, floods and waterlogging; South — rain-fed, seasonal rivers, plateau slope, lower rainfall in the south-west, droughts and falling groundwater; soils differ (khadar vs karail–kewal, red soils).
- **Conclusion:** Integrated basin planning — flood control in the north, ahar–pyne revival and watershed work in the south (Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali).
