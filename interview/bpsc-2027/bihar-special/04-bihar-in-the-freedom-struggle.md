"The Indian National Movement and the role of Bihar" is a named Prelims syllabus head, and "Bihar's role in the freedom struggle" is named in GS Paper I of Mains. That makes this one of the highest-value chapters in the whole shelf. Expect Prelims questions on dates, places and people (Raj Kumar Shukla, Tarapur, the seven martyrs, Sadaquat Ashram) and Mains questions such as "Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement."

> **In simple words:** Bihar gave India Gandhi's first satyagraha (Champaran, 1917), a strong Non-Cooperation movement with its own national university, one of the bloodiest flag satyagrahas (Tarapur, 1932), the organised peasant movement of Swami Sahajanand, and the fiercest rising of 1942. It also gave leaders like Rajendra Prasad, Jayaprakash Narayan, Anugrah Narayan Sinha and Sri Krishna Sinha.

## Early nationalism in Bihar (1885–1916)

- Bihar delegates attended the early Congress sessions. The **Bihar Provincial Conference** held its **first session at Patna in 1908**, presided over by **Ali Imam**; the **Bihar Provincial Congress Committee** was formed around the same time.
- **Bihari Students' Conference (1906)**: an organisation of Bihari students in which young **Rajendra Prasad** played a leading role; it trained many future leaders.
- **Muzaffarpur bomb case (30 April 1908)**: the Bengal revolutionaries **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki** threw a bomb at a carriage at **Muzaffarpur**, thinking it carried the judge **Kingsford**; two British women were killed instead. Prafulla Chaki shot himself at **Mokama** when about to be caught; **Khudiram Bose was hanged at Muzaffarpur jail on 11 August 1908**, aged 18.
- **Separate province**: led by **Sachchidananda Sinha**, **Mahesh Narayan**, **Ali Imam**, **Hasan Imam** and **Mazharul Haque**, with ***The Bihar Times*** (1894). On **12 December 1911** the Delhi Durbar announced a new province; **Bihar and Orissa** came into being on **22 March 1912** (celebrated as **Bihar Diwas**).
- The **27th session of the Indian National Congress** was held at **Bankipore (Patna)** in **December 1912**, presided over by **R. N. Mudholkar**.
- **Patna High Court** (1916) and **Patna University** (1917) followed — see [Bihar after Independence](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-after-independence).
- **Home Rule League**: a Bihar branch was formed in **1916** with **Mazharul Haque** as president.
- **Hasan Imam** of Bihar presided over the **special session of Congress at Bombay (1918)**.
- ***The Searchlight*** (English, 1918) became the main nationalist newspaper of Bihar.

## Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

This was **Gandhi's first satyagraha in India**. It is the single most important Bihar topic in the freedom struggle.

### Background — the tinkathia system

- European **indigo planters** in **Champaran** forced tenants to grow indigo on **3 kathas out of every 20** (the **tinkathia** system) and sell it at low prices.
- When German synthetic dye made indigo unprofitable (early 20th century), planters demanded **sharahbeshi** (higher rent) or **tawan** (lump-sum payment) to release tenants from indigo. Illegal cesses (**abwabs**) were also common.
- Earlier protests took place in 1867 and 1907–08 (led by **Sheikh Gulab** and **Sitaram Rai** near Bettiah).

### Events

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **December 1916** | **Raj Kumar Shukla**, a peasant from Champaran (village Murli Bharahwa), met Gandhi at the **Lucknow Congress session** and begged him to come |
| **10 April 1917** | Gandhi reached **Patna** with Shukla, then went to **Muzaffarpur**, where **J. B. Kripalani** (then a professor there) received him |
| **15 April 1917** | Gandhi reached **Motihari** |
| 16 April 1917 | District authorities ordered him to leave Champaran; Gandhi **refused to obey** and said he would accept the punishment — the first act of civil disobedience on Indian soil |
| April 1917 | The Lieutenant Governor (**Sir Edward Gait**) withdrew the case; Gandhi was allowed to inquire |
| April–June 1917 | Gandhi and his team recorded **statements of thousands of peasants** |
| **June 1917** | Government set up the **Champaran Agrarian Committee** with Gandhi as a member (chairman **F. G. Sly**) |
| **1918** | **Champaran Agrarian Act** abolished the **tinkathia** system; planters agreed to refund part (about **25%**) of the illegal money taken |

### Gandhi's team in Champaran

**Rajendra Prasad**, **Brajkishore Prasad**, **Anugrah Narayan Sinha**, **Mazharul Haque**, **J. B. Kripalani**, **Ramnavami Prasad**, **Dharnidhar Prasad**, **Shambhusharan Varma**, **Mahadev Desai** and **Narhari Parikh**. **Kasturba Gandhi** also came and worked in village schools.

### Constructive work

- Gandhi opened **basic schools** at **Barharwa Lakhansen**, **Bhitiharwa** and **Madhuban**. The **Bhitiharwa Ashram** (West Champaran) is now a memorial.
- Volunteers taught hygiene and sanitation in villages.

### Significance

1. First successful test of **satyagraha** in India.
2. Brought Gandhi into national leadership, and brought **Rajendra Prasad** and other Bihari lawyers into full-time public life.
3. Linked the national movement with **peasant grievances**.
4. Showed that **truth-based fact-finding** (recording statements) was a powerful weapon.
5. Rajendra Prasad wrote ***Satyagraha in Champaran***. The centenary was celebrated in **2017**.

> **Prelims angle:** The man who invited Gandhi — **Raj Kumar Shukla**. The Congress session where they met — **Lucknow 1916**. The system abolished — **tinkathia** (3/20). The law — **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918**. Gandhi's other early movements: **Ahmedabad mill strike** and **Kheda** (both 1918) — not in Bihar.

## Non-Cooperation and Khilafat (1920–22)

- The **Bihar Provincial Conference at Bhagalpur (1920)**, presided over by **Rajendra Prasad**, supported Non-Cooperation even before the Congress formally adopted it at Calcutta (September 1920).
- **Mazharul Haque** founded the **Sadaquat Ashram** at Patna (on land given by Khairun Mian) — it became the **headquarters of the Bihar Congress**; Rajendra Prasad later lived here.
- **Bihar Vidyapeeth**, a national university for students who left government institutions, was inaugurated by **Gandhi on 6 February 1921**. The **Bihar National College** and many national schools were set up.
- Mazharul Haque started the newspaper ***The Motherland*** (1921).
- Students and lawyers left courts and colleges; foreign cloth was burnt; liquor shops were picketed.
- The visit of the **Prince of Wales (December 1921)** was boycotted in Patna.
- Khilafat leaders such as **Shafi Daudi** and **Maulana Mazharul Haque** worked for Hindu–Muslim unity.
- After **Chauri Chaura (February 1922)** Gandhi withdrew the movement.

## Swarajists, Simon Commission and Civil Disobedience (1923–34)

- The **Swaraj Party** had a Bihar unit; some leaders contested council elections.
- The **Simon Commission** was boycotted in **Patna (December 1928)** with black flags and the slogan "Simon, go back".

### Civil Disobedience in Bihar (1930–34)

- **Salt satyagraha** began in Bihar in **April 1930**, first in **Champaran and Saran**; in Patna, volunteers made salt at **Nakhas pond**.
- Bihar was famous for the **no-chaukidari-tax movement** — villagers refused to pay the tax that paid for village watchmen.
- At **Bihpur (Bhagalpur)**, volunteers including **Rajendra Prasad** were injured in a police lathi-charge (1930).
- Foreign cloth and liquor were boycotted, and women joined picketing.

### Tarapur massacre (15 February 1932)

- At **Tarapur** (then Munger district, now Munger), a group of volunteers tried to **hoist the tricolour on the police station (thana)**.
- Police fired on the crowd. **34 people** are counted as martyrs (bodies of only some were identified).
- **15 February** is observed as **Tarapur Shaheed Diwas**. It is counted among the largest single police firings on freedom fighters in Bihar.

### The 1934 earthquake

- A huge earthquake struck Bihar on **15 January 1934**. **Rajendra Prasad** led the **Bihar Central Relief Committee**.
- **Gandhi** said it was a "divine chastisement" for the sin of untouchability; **Rabindranath Tagore** publicly disagreed, calling it unscientific. This exchange is a known exam point.
- The district officer **W. G. Archer** noticed **Mithila paintings** on the broken walls of houses in Madhubani while surveying damage — this led to their wider fame (see [Art and Culture](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/art-culture-festivals-and-languages)).

## Socialists and the Kisan Sabha

### Congress Socialist Party

- The **Bihar Socialist Party** was formed in **1931**.
- The **Congress Socialist Party (CSP)** was launched at a meeting in **Patna in May 1934** (formal conference at Bombay, October 1934), with **Acharya Narendra Dev** as president and **Jayaprakash Narayan** as general secretary.
- Bihar socialists: **JP**, **Ram Briksh Benipuri**, **Ganga Sharan Sinha**, **Phulan Prasad Verma**, later **Karpoori Thakur**.

### Swami Sahajanand Saraswati and the Kisan Sabha

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who | **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** (1889–1950), a sanyasi born in Ghazipur (UP), working from his **Sitaram Ashram at Bihta** (Patna) |
| **Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha** | Founded **1929** at **Sonepur** |
| **All India Kisan Sabha** | Founded at **Lucknow, April 1936** — **Sahajanand** first president, **N. G. Ranga** general secretary |
| Issues | Zamindari oppression, illegal cesses, eviction from **bakasht** land |
| **Bakasht struggle** (1936–39) | Fight over **bakasht** land — land that zamindars took back from tenants and claimed as their "self-cultivated" land. Centres: **Reora** (Gaya), **Barahiya Tal** (Lakhisarai/Munger, led by **Karyanand Sharma**), **Amwari** (Siwan/Saran, where **Rahul Sankrityayan** was beaten and jailed), **Majhiyawan** (Gaya) |
| Leaders | **Karyanand Sharma**, **Jadunandan Sharma**, **Rahul Sankrityayan**, **Panchanan Sharma** |
| Writings | *Mera Jivan Sangharsh* (autobiography), *Kisan Kya Karen* |
| Slogan | "**Jo ann-vastra upjayega, ab so kanoon banayega**" (those who grow food and cloth will now make the law) — commonly quoted |

> **Mains angle:** The Kisan Sabha made the zamindari question central to Bihar politics. Its pressure led directly to the **Bihar Land Reforms Act, 1950**, which made Bihar the **first state to abolish zamindari**.

### Backward-caste and Dalit assertion

- **Triveni Sangh (1933)**: an alliance of **Yadav, Kurmi and Koeri** peasants formed in **Shahabad** to oppose upper-caste domination and contest elections.
- **Jagjivan Ram** (born at **Chandwa, Bhojpur**, 1908) organised the **All India Depressed Classes League (1935)** and became a national Dalit leader; later Deputy Prime Minister (1977–79).

## Revolutionaries from Bihar

- **Yogendra Shukla** (Muzaffarpur) — a founder member of the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Army**; sent to the **Cellular Jail (Andaman)**; later helped JP escape in 1942.
- **Baikunth Shukla** (Vaishali) — shot **Phanindranath Ghosh**, the approver whose evidence helped hang **Bhagat Singh**, at **Bettiah (1932)**; hanged at **Gaya jail on 14 May 1934**.
- **Basawan Singh (Sinha)** — revolutionary and later socialist trade unionist.
- **Kishori Prasanna Sinha** and others of the Bihar wing of the HSRA.

## The 1937 provincial government

Under the **Government of India Act, 1935**, provinces got elected governments.

- After the 1937 elections, Congress first refused office. **Mohammad Yunus** (Muslim Independent Party) became the **first Prime Minister (Premier) of Bihar** on **1 April 1937** for a short period.
- In **July 1937** Congress accepted office: **Sri Krishna Sinha** became Premier, with **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** as the second most senior minister (finance).
- The ministry passed tenancy laws reducing some burdens on peasants.
- It resigned in **1939** when Britain dragged India into the Second World War without consultation.
- The **Ramgarh session of Congress (March 1940)**, presided over by **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**, was held at Ramgarh (then Bihar, now Jharkhand).

## Quit India Movement in Bihar (1942)

Bihar saw perhaps the **most intense mass rising of 1942** in India. The Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** called the August rising "by far the most serious rebellion since 1857", and Bihar and eastern UP were its centres.

### Patna Secretariat martyrs — 11 August 1942

- On **9 August 1942**, leaders including **Rajendra Prasad** (at **Sadaquat Ashram**) were arrested.
- On **11 August 1942**, students marched to the **Patna Secretariat** to hoist the national flag. On the order of the district magistrate **W. G. Archer**, police opened fire.
- **Seven students** were killed. Their memorial — the **Shaheed Smarak (Martyrs' Memorial)** — stands in front of the Secretariat; sculptor **Devi Prasad Roy Chowdhury**; foundation laid on **15 August 1947**.

| Martyr | School / college |
|---|---|
| **Umakant Prasad Sinha** | Ram Mohan Roy Seminary |
| **Ramanand Singh** | Ram Mohan Roy Seminary |
| **Satish Prasad Jha** | Patna Collegiate School |
| **Jagatpati Kumar** | Bihar National College |
| **Devipada Choudhary** | Miller High English School |
| **Rajendra Singh** | Patna High English School |
| **Ramgovind Singh** | Punpun High English School |

> **Memory trick:** "**U**ncle **R**am **S**aw **J**oyful **D**evotees **R**aise **R**ags (flags)" — **U**makant, **R**amanand, **S**atish, **J**agatpati, **D**evipada, **R**ajendra, **R**amgovind.

### The rising across Bihar

- Railway lines, telegraph wires and police stations were attacked across north and central Bihar; for some days British control collapsed in parts of **Saran, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Munger and Patna** districts.
- The government used **aerial machine-gunning** of crowds in some places and imposed collective fines.
- **Tara Rani Srivastava** of Saran: her husband **Phulendu Babu** was shot while leading a march to the **Siwan** police station; she bandaged him and went on to hoist the flag — a story often told for women's role.

### JP and the Azad Dasta

- **Jayaprakash Narayan** escaped from **Hazaribagh Central Jail** on **Diwali night, 9 November 1942**, with **Yogendra Shukla, Suraj Narayan Singh, Ramanand Mishra, Shaligram Singh and Gulab Chand Gupta**.
- He organised the **Azad Dasta** (freedom squads) for guerrilla action, with bases in the **Nepal Terai**.
- In 1943 JP and **Ram Manohar Lohia** were arrested in Nepal and then freed from the **Hanuman Nagar** jail by Azad Dasta fighters.
- JP became a hero of the underground movement.

> **Mains angle:** For "Bihar's role in Quit India", structure: (1) arrests and the Patna Secretariat firing; (2) mass action in districts; (3) repression including aerial firing; (4) underground movement — JP's escape, Azad Dasta, Nepal bases; (5) role of students, women, peasants and socialists; (6) significance — showed the British that Indians would not wait.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Satyagraha | Non-violent resistance based on truth, accepting punishment for breaking unjust laws |
| Tinkathia | Forced indigo on 3 of every 20 kathas |
| Sharahbeshi / tawan | Rent increase / lump-sum payment demanded by planters to free tenants from indigo |
| Bakasht land | Land zamindars took back from tenants and claimed as self-cultivated |
| Chaukidari tax | Village tax used to pay watchmen; refused during Civil Disobedience |
| Azad Dasta | JP's underground guerrilla squads, 1942–43 |
| Kisan Sabha | Peasants' association |
| Premier | Title of the head of a provincial government under the 1935 Act |
| National university | Institution set up by nationalists outside British control (e.g. Bihar Vidyapeeth) |
| Approver | An accused person who turns witness for the prosecution |

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement. (38 marks, about 300 words)**

- **Intro:** 8 August 1942 resolution; Bihar became one of the two main centres of the rising.
- **Body:** Arrest of Rajendra Prasad at Sadaquat Ashram; 11 August Patna Secretariat firing and the seven martyrs; destruction of rail and telegraph lines; temporary collapse of British authority in districts; repression — aerial firing, collective fines; women such as Tara Rani Srivastava; JP's escape and the Azad Dasta from Nepal; role of students, peasants, socialists.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's sacrifice showed the people's will; Linlithgow's "most serious rebellion since 1857" remark reflects it.

**2. "Champaran was the laboratory of Gandhian satyagraha." Examine. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 1917 — Gandhi's first satyagraha in India.
- **Body:** Tinkathia, sharahbeshi, tawan; Raj Kumar Shukla; civil disobedience at Motihari; fact-finding by recording statements; non-violence and openness; Agrarian Committee and 1918 Act; constructive work — schools, hygiene; new leaders (Rajendra Prasad, A. N. Sinha, Kripalani).
- **Conclusion:** Methods tested here — truth, non-violence, constructive work, mass contact — were used in every later movement.

**3. Evaluate the contribution of the Kisan Sabha movement to the freedom struggle and social change in Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Swami Sahajanand and the Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha (1929).
- **Body:** Zamindari oppression under the Permanent Settlement; demands; bakasht struggles (Reora, Barahiya, Amwari); AIKS 1936; link with Congress socialists; pressure on the 1937 ministry; mass political education of peasants.
