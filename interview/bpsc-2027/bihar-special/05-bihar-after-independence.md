This chapter joins history to current affairs. BPSC asks how Bihar's borders changed (1912, 1936, 2000), how zamindari was abolished, who the "firsts" were (first CM, first woman CM), and what the **JP movement** of 1974 was. Mains asks you to analyse Bihar's political and administrative journey. The last part — the 2023 caste survey and the change of Chief Minister in 2026 — is current affairs, so keep updating it.

> **In simple words:** Bihar was carved out of Bengal in 1912 together with Orissa. Orissa left in 1936 and Jharkhand in 2000, leaving today's Bihar. After 1947 Bihar was the first state to abolish zamindari. Congress ruled until 1967; then came coalition governments, the JP movement (1974), the rise of backward-caste politics (1990s), and from 2005 a focus on law and order, roads, schools and women's empowerment.

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| 1765 | Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa to the Company; Bihar part of the **Bengal Presidency** |
| 1905 | Partition of Bengal — Bihar stayed with western Bengal |
| **12 December 1911** | **Delhi Durbar** of King George V announced the annulment of the Bengal partition and the creation of a new province |
| **22 March 1912** | **Province of Bihar and Orissa** created, capital **Patna**; first Lieutenant Governor **Sir Charles Stuart Bayley**. **Bihar Diwas** is celebrated on 22 March |
| 1920 | Became a Governor's province; **Lord Satyendra Prasanna Sinha** became its Governor — the **first Indian to be governor of a province** |
| **1 April 1936** | **Orissa** became a separate province under the Government of India Act, 1935; the remaining province was named **Bihar** |
| 1956 | Under the **Bihar and West Bengal (Transfer of Territories) Act, 1956**, the **Purulia** area (from Manbhum) and a part of **Purnia** district were transferred to **West Bengal** |
| **15 November 2000** | **Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000** — **Jharkhand** created from the southern plateau (18 districts); the date is **Birsa Munda's birth anniversary** |

**What Bihar lost in 2000:** Jharkhand took most of the **mineral belt** (coal, iron, mica, copper, bauxite, uranium), the big industries (**Bokaro, Jamshedpur, Ranchi's HEC, Sindri**), most **forests** and many hill **tourist sites**. Bihar was left with the flood-prone Ganga plain, a big population and little industry. This is the base of Bihar's demand for **special category status** and special packages (see [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar)).

> **Prelims angle:** Orissa separated on **1 April 1936**; Jharkhand on **15 November 2000**; Bihar Diwas on **22 March**. The first Indian provincial governor was **Lord S. P. Sinha** (Bihar and Orissa, 1920).

## The first government and land reforms (1946–61)

- **Sri Krishna Sinha** ("Bihar Kesari") was the **first Chief Minister of Bihar** (1946–61) — the longest single continuous term in the early decades. **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** ("Bihar Vibhuti") was **Deputy CM and Finance Minister** until his death in 1957.
- **Zamindari abolition**: the Bihar Abolition of Zamindaris Act (1948) was replaced by the **Bihar Land Reforms Act, 1950**. **Bihar was the first state to pass a law abolishing zamindari.**
- The **Patna High Court** struck down the Act (case of **Maharaja Kameshwar Singh of Darbhanga**, 1951) for violating equality. Parliament responded with the **First Constitutional Amendment (1951)**, which created the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land-reform laws; the Bihar Land Reforms Act was among the first laws placed in it. The Supreme Court later upheld the Act in ***State of Bihar v. Kameshwar Singh* (1952)**.
- **Land ceiling**: the **Bihar Land Reforms (Fixation of Ceiling Area and Acquisition of Surplus Land) Act, 1961** set limits on land holdings, but implementation was weak.
- **Bhoodan**: **Vinoba Bhave** walked through Bihar (1952–54) and received large land gifts; the **Bihar Bhoodan Yajna Act, 1954** gave it legal backing. Much donated land turned out to be poor or disputed.
- **Kosi agreement (1954)** and **Gandak agreement (1959)** with Nepal for barrages and canals (see [Rivers, Floods and Climate](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/rivers-floods-and-climate-of-bihar)).
- **Rajendra Setu** at **Mokama** (opened **1959**) — the first rail-cum-road bridge over the Ganga in Bihar.
- **Barauni oil refinery** (built with Soviet help, commissioned **1964**) — still Bihar's biggest industrial unit.

> **Mains angle:** Land reform in Bihar was **first in law but weak in practice** — zamindari ended, but ceiling surplus land and tenancy records (bataidari) were never fully settled. This explains later agrarian conflict and the Bandyopadhyay Commission recommendations (2008) on sharecroppers, which were not implemented.

## Coalitions, Karpoori Thakur and social justice (1967–80)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1967** | First **non-Congress government**: **Mahamaya Prasad Sinha** (Samyukta Vidhayak Dal) became CM; **Karpoori Thakur** was Deputy CM and Education Minister — he removed **English as a compulsory pass subject** in the matric exam |
| 1968 | **B. P. Mandal** briefly CM (later chairman of the **Mandal Commission**, 1979); **Bhola Paswan Shastri** became CM — the **first Dalit CM of Bihar**; first spell of **President's rule** (1968) |
| 1970–71 | **Karpoori Thakur**'s first term as CM |
| 1973–75 | **Abdul Ghafoor** — first Muslim CM of Bihar; the JP movement began against his government |
| **1977–79** | Karpoori Thakur CM again (Janata Party) |
| **1978** | **Karpoori Thakur formula** of reservation in state jobs based on the **Mungeri Lal Commission** report (1976): **26%** for backward groups — **12%** for the most backward (Annexure I), **8%** for other backward classes (Annexure II), **3%** for women and **3%** for the economically poor of any caste |

**Karpoori Thakur** (1924–88), born at **Pitaunjhia (now Karpoori Gram), Samastipur**, from the Nai (barber) community, is called "**Jannayak**" (people's leader). He was awarded the **Bharat Ratna (posthumously)**, announced in **January 2024**. Bihar's farmer support scheme of 2026 is named after him (see the schemes chapter).

## The JP Movement and Total Revolution (1974–77)

This is a key topic for both Prelims and Mains.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **18 March 1974** | Students under the **Bihar Chhatra Sangharsh Samiti** protested in Patna against price rise, unemployment, corruption and the education system; police firing |
| **8 April 1974** | **Jayaprakash Narayan** led a **silent march** in Patna — he took charge of the movement |
| **5 June 1974** | At a huge rally in **Gandhi Maidan, Patna**, JP called for "**Sampoorna Kranti**" (**Total Revolution**) |
| 1974–75 | Demand to dissolve the Bihar Assembly; gheraos and satyagraha; the movement spread nationwide |
| **12 June 1975** | Allahabad High Court set aside Indira Gandhi's election |
| **25 June 1975** | JP's rally at Ramlila Maidan, Delhi; **Emergency** declared that night; JP jailed |
| **1977** | **Janata Party** won the Lok Sabha election; Congress lost power at the Centre for the first time |

**Total Revolution** meant change in seven areas: **social, economic, political, cultural, ideological/intellectual, educational and spiritual**. JP also argued for the **right to recall** elected representatives and for clean, decentralised politics.

**Leaders who emerged from the JP movement:** **Lalu Prasad**, **Nitish Kumar**, **Sushil Kumar Modi**, **Ram Vilas Paswan** and many others who dominated Bihar politics for the next fifty years.

JP received the **Bharat Ratna (posthumously) in 1999**. Patna's airport (**Jay Prakash Narayan International Airport**) and the **J. P. Setu** (Digha–Sonepur rail-road bridge, 2016) are named after him.

> **Mains angle:** "Assess the significance of the JP movement." Points: student origin in Bihar; moral leadership of JP; Total Revolution idea; national spread; Emergency and its end; birth of Janata Party; new generation of Bihar leaders; strengthening of civil liberties (44th Amendment, 1978); but later failures — Janata split, idealism lost.

## The Mandal era (1990–2005)

- **10 March 1990**: **Lalu Prasad** became CM after the Janata Dal won. The national **Mandal Commission** reservations (announced August 1990) gave backward-caste politics a strong push.
- Gains: political voice for backward castes, Dalits and minorities ("**social justice**"); he stopped L. K. Advani's Rath Yatra at **Samastipur** (October 1990), and Bihar avoided major communal riots in that period.
- Problems: weak governance, fall in investment, law-and-order concerns, and the **fodder scam** (exposed 1996) — illegal withdrawals from the animal husbandry department's treasury.
- **1997**: the **Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD)** was formed; **Rabri Devi** became the **first woman Chief Minister of Bihar** (25 July 1997).
- **March 2000**: **Nitish Kumar** became CM for the first time, for about a week (lost majority).
- **15 November 2000**: Jharkhand created.
- **February 2005**: a hung assembly; the assembly was dissolved before meeting, and the Supreme Court later (**Rameshwar Prasad case, 2006**) held the dissolution unconstitutional — an important case on the Governor's role.

## The governance turn (2005 onwards)

From **24 November 2005**, **Nitish Kumar** (JD(U), with the BJP in the NDA for most periods) led Bihar for nearly two decades, with a short gap (**Jitan Ram Manjhi**, May 2014 – February 2015). The focus was on **law and order, roads, bridges, schools, electricity and women's empowerment**.

| 2007 | 50% reservation for women in urban local bodies |

### Mains questions

**1. Trace the territorial evolution of Bihar from 1912 to 2000 and its economic consequences. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar's present borders are the result of four changes in 88 years.
- **Body:** 1912 creation with Orissa (demand of educated Biharis); 1936 Orissa separation; 1956 transfer of Purulia; 2000 Jharkhand — loss of minerals, industries, forests, revenue; Bihar left with flood-prone plain, high density, low urbanisation; the demand for special category status and special packages; later focus on agriculture, services and new industry.
- **Conclusion:** Bifurcation forced Bihar to find a new growth model based on agriculture, services and human capital.

**2. Assess the significance of the JP movement for Bihar and Indian democracy. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** A student protest of March 1974 that became a national movement.
- **Body:** Causes — price rise, unemployment, corruption; JP's leadership and Total Revolution; non-violent mass action; the Emergency; 1977 Janata victory; new leaders from Bihar; civil liberties restored (44th Amendment); limits — Janata collapse, caste politics later.
- **Conclusion:** It renewed the idea that people can hold rulers accountable — a lasting Bihar contribution to Indian democracy.

**3. "Land reform in Bihar was a success in law but a failure in implementation." Discuss. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar was first to abolish zamindari (1950).
- **Body:** Kameshwar Singh case and the First Amendment; zamindari ended; ceiling law 1961 — loopholes, benami holdings; poor land records; bataidars unprotected; Bhoodan land disputed; agrarian violence in central Bihar; recent steps — land survey and digitisation of records.
- **Conclusion:** Completing land records and tenancy reform remains central to rural development in Bihar.
