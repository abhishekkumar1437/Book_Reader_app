Culture is a named part of GS Paper I ("art, culture, literature") and appears every year in Prelims as match-the-following questions: painting with district, festival with region, writer with work, GI product with place. This chapter collects all of Bihar's cultural facts in tables so you can revise them fast.

> **In simple words:** Bihar's culture has four big regions: **Mithila** (north-east — Maithili, Madhubani painting, Sama-Chakeva), **Bhojpur** (west — Bhojpuri, Bidesiya, Bhikhari Thakur), **Magadh** (south — Magahi, Gaya, Rajgir) and **Anga** (east — Angika, Bhagalpur, Manjusha art, Bihula). Chhath is the festival that unites all of them.

## Painting traditions

### Madhubani (Mithila) painting

- **Where:** Mithila, especially **Madhubani** district — villages **Jitwarpur** and **Ranti** are famous centres.
- **Who:** traditionally painted by **women** on walls and floors for rituals, especially weddings.
- **Ritual forms:** **Kohbar** (painting in the bridal chamber with symbols of fertility — lotus, bamboo, fish, parrots, turtles) and **Aripan** (floor painting with rice paste, like rangoli).
- **Styles:**

| Style | Feature | Traditional community |
|---|---|---|
| **Bharni** | Spaces filled with bright colour; gods and goddesses | Brahmin women |
| **Kachni** | Fine line work, often in one or two colours | Kayastha women |
| **Tantrik** | Tantric deities and symbols | — |
| **Godna** | Based on tattoo patterns; repeated lines and circles | Dalit (Paswan) artists |
| **Gobar / Kohbar** | Painted with cow-dung base; Kohbar themes | — |

- **Colours:** natural — turmeric (yellow), indigo (blue), soot/lamp black, flowers, leaves; brushes of twigs and bamboo. No empty space is left — borders are filled with flowers or geometric patterns.
- **History of fame:** after the **1934 earthquake**, British officer **W. G. Archer** saw the paintings on broken walls and photographed them; during the **1966–68 drought**, the All India Handicrafts Board (artist **Bhaskar Kulkarni**) encouraged women to paint on paper to earn income.
- **GI tag:** Madhubani paintings — **2007**.
- **Padma Shri artists** (frequently asked): **Jagdamba Devi** (first, 1975), **Sita Devi** (1981), **Ganga Devi** (1984), **Mahasundari Devi** (2011), **Baua Devi** (2017), **Godavari Dutta** (2019), **Dulari Devi** (2021).
- Madhubani railway station's walls were painted by local artists (2017–18).

### Manjusha art

- **Where:** **Bhagalpur** (ancient Anga/Champa).
- **Theme:** the folk legend of **Bihula and Bishahari (Mansa, the snake goddess)** — Bihula brings her husband **Bala Lakhendra** back to life after a snakebite.
- **Form:** painted on boxes ("**manjusha**" means box) made of jute, bamboo and paper, offered during **Bishahari Puja** in Shravan–Bhadra (August).
- **Features:** only **three colours** traditionally — **pink, green and yellow**; strong outlines; **snakes** everywhere; figures shown with large eyes and no background. It is often called India's earliest **scroll-style** (sequential story) folk painting.
- **Known artist:** **Chakravarti Devi**.
- **GI tag:** yes (Manjusha art — check the registry for the year).

### Patna Kalam (Patna School of Painting)

- **When:** mid-18th to early 20th century.
- **Origin:** Mughal-trained painters moved from Delhi and Murshidabad to **Patna** as the Mughal court declined.
- **Feature:** the **first Indian school to paint the daily life of common people** — vendors, craftsmen, festivals, servants — instead of only kings and courts. Usually **no landscape background**, soft colours, shading influenced by European art; painted on paper, ivory and **mica**.
- **Buyers:** British officers who wanted pictures of "Indian life" — so it is also called a **Company painting** style.
- **Artists:** **Sewak Ram**, **Hulas Lal**, **Shiv Lal**, **Shiv Dayal Lal**, **Jai Ram Das**, **Mahadev Lal**; the last great master was **Ishwari Prasad Verma**.
- Collections: **Patna Museum**, **Bihar Museum**, Khuda Bakhsh Library.

### Tikuli art

- **Where:** **Patna**.
- **Origin:** "tikuli" means **bindi**. In Mughal times, women's decorative bindis were made of glass with gold foil.
- **Today:** revived in the 1950s–80s as **enamel painting on hardboard** discs and panels, with Madhubani-style motifs.

> **Prelims angle:** Match — **Madhubani**: Mithila, Kohbar, women; **Manjusha**: Bhagalpur, Bihula, snakes, three colours; **Patna Kalam**: Mughal-origin, common life, Ishwari Prasad Verma; **Tikuli**: Patna, bindi, hardboard.

## Folk dance and theatre

| Form | Region | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Jat-Jatin** | Mithila, Kosi belt | Couple dance (husband Jat and wife Jatin) in the rainy season; shows love, quarrels and poverty; also prays for rain |
| **Jhijhiya** | Mithila | Women dance with **earthen pots with lamps on their heads** during Navaratri/Dussehra; to protect from evil and witchcraft |
| **Domkach** | Bhojpur, Magadh, Mithila | Performed by **women at the groom's house on the wedding night** when the men go with the barat; humorous |
| **Bidesiya** | Bhojpur | Folk drama-dance on **migration** — a man leaves for work in the city and the wife waits; created by **Bhikhari Thakur** |
| **Launda naach** | Bhojpur | Male dancers in female attire; **Ramchandra Manjhi** received the **Padma Shri (2021)** |
| **Paika / Paiki** | Bhojpur, Magadh | Martial dance with shields and swords |
| **Kathghorwa** | Bhojpur, Magadh | Dancer wears a **wooden/bamboo horse frame** |
| **Karma** | Tribal areas (Kaimur, Jamui, Banka) | Around the karam tree, after the harvest |
| **Jharni** | Muslim women | Sung/danced during Muharram |
| **Kirtaniya** | Mithila | Devotional drama (medieval Maithili) |
| **Natua naach** | Mithila | Male folk dancers |

## Festivals of Bihar

### Chhath Puja

The most important festival of Bihar.

- **When:** **Kartik Shukla Shashthi** (sixth day of the bright half of Kartik, October–November), six days after Diwali. A second Chhath — **Chaiti Chhath** — falls in Chaitra (March–April).
- **Worship of:** **Surya (the Sun)** and **Chhathi Maiya** (identified with **Usha**, the sun's consort/goddess, or Shashthi Devi).
- **Four days:**

| Day | Ritual |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Nahay-Khay** — bath in a river and a pure meal (rice, chana dal, lauki) |
| 2 | **Kharna (Lohanda)** — day-long fast broken in the evening with gur-kheer and roti |
| 3 | **Sandhya Arghya** — offering to the **setting sun** standing in water |
| 4 | **Usha Arghya** — offering to the **rising sun**, then the fast ends |

- Offerings: **thekua**, fruits, sugarcane in bamboo baskets (**soop, daura**).
- Special features: **no priest is needed**; it is eco-friendly; women and men both observe the fast (the observer is called a **vrati**).
- **Sun temples** associated: **Deo** (Aurangabad), **Ulaar** (Patna), **Baragaon** (Nalanda), **Kandaha** (Saharsa).

| Festival | Region | When | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sama-Chakeva** | Mithila | Kartik (after Chhath to Kartik Purnima) | Brother–sister festival; clay figures of birds; the backbiter **Chugla** is burnt; based on the legend of **Shyama (Sama)**, daughter of Krishna, and her brother **Samba** |

| **Chaurchan (Chauth Chandra)** | Mithila | Bhadra | Moon worship |
| **Bishahari (Bihula) Puja** | Anga (Bhagalpur) | Shravan–Bhadra (August) | Snake goddess Mansa; **Manjusha** boxes |

**Litti-chokha** (wheat balls stuffed with sattu, roasted, with mashed brinjal-tomato-potato), **sattu** (roasted gram flour), **khaja** (Silao), **tilkut** (Gaya), **anarsa**, **thekua** (Chhath), **makhana**, **dahi-chura**, **balushahi**, **pua** (Holi), **khurma**, **malpua**, **chandrakala**.

## Languages of Bihar

| Language | Status | Area | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hindi** | **Official language** (Bihar Official Language Act, 1950) | Whole state | In **1881**, Hindi in Devanagari replaced Urdu in courts in Bihar |
| **Urdu** | **Second official language** (from **1981**, in notified areas/purposes) | Across Bihar, especially Seemanchal and cities | — |
| **Maithili** | **Eighth Schedule** — added by the **92nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** (with **Bodo, Dogri, Santali**) | Mithila; also Nepal's Terai | Old script **Tirhuta (Mithilakshar)**; now usually Devanagari; Sahitya Akademi recognised it in **1965** |
| **Bhojpuri** | Not in the Eighth Schedule (demand pending) | West Bihar and east UP | Spoken widely by the diaspora in **Mauritius, Fiji, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana** |
| **Magahi (Magadhi)** | Not in the Eighth Schedule | Magadh | Descends from **Magadhi Prakrit** |
| **Angika** | Not in the Eighth Schedule | Anga (Bhagalpur–Munger–Banka) | — |
| **Bajjika** | Not in the Eighth Schedule | Vaishali–Muzaffarpur–Sitamarhi | — |
| **Surjapuri** | — | Kishanganj–Purnia | Mixed with Bengali |
| **Bengali, Santhali** | Eighth Schedule languages spoken in parts | Border districts / tribal areas | — |

- **G. A. Grierson** in the ***Linguistic Survey of India*** (early 1900s) grouped **Maithili, Magahi and Bhojpuri** as "**Bihari**" languages.
- **Kaithi** was a script widely used in Bihar for court and land records until the early 20th century.
- **Pali** — the language of the Buddhist canon — is closely linked to the old speech of Magadha.

> **Common mistake:** Only **Maithili** among Bihar's regional languages is in the Eighth Schedule. Bhojpuri, Magahi, Angika and Bajjika are **not** (as of 2026 — check for any new amendment).

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the folk art traditions of Bihar and suggest measures for their promotion. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar's folk arts are living traditions tied to rituals and women's creativity.
- **Body:** Madhubani (styles, Kohbar, Padma awards, GI 2007); Manjusha (Bihula legend); Patna Kalam (common life); Tikuli; Sikki, Sujini, Applique, Bawan Buti; challenges — middlemen, low prices, machine copies, loss of natural colours; measures — GI enforcement, Upendra Maharathi Shilp Anusandhan Sansthan training, e-commerce, design schools, tourism links, ODOP-type branding, Bihar Museum exhibitions.
- **Conclusion:** Folk art can be both cultural identity and rural livelihood, especially for women.

**2. "Chhath is not just a festival but an expression of Bihar's social and ecological values." Explain. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Kartik Shukla Shashthi; worship of the sun.
- **Body:** Four-day rituals; no priest — direct worship; equality across castes at ghats; women's central role; cleanliness drives; use of local, seasonal produce (sugarcane, thekua, bamboo); river and water respect; community volunteering; diaspora spread; songs (Sharda Sinha) as identity.
- **Conclusion:** Chhath combines faith, discipline, equality and nature — a model for cultural ecology.

**3. Assess the contribution of Bihar's writers to Hindi and regional literature. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** From Vidyapati to Renu, Bihar has shaped Indian literature.
- **Body:** Vidyapati and Bengal Vaishnavism; Dinkar's nationalism and *Sanskriti ke Char Adhyay*; Renu and the regional novel; Nagarjuna's people's poetry and Maithili; Bhikhari Thakur's social theatre on migration and dowry; Benipuri, Shivpujan Sahay; Urdu tradition of Azimabad.
- **Conclusion:** Their themes — rural life, justice, migration, nationalism — remain relevant to Bihar today.
