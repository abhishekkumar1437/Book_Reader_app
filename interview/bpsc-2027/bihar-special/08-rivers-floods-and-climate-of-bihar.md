Rivers decide almost everything in Bihar — where people live, what they grow, and which disasters they face. BPSC Prelims asks river origin and confluence questions ("Where does the Son meet the Ganga?"), barrage locations and the "Sorrow of Bihar". Mains asks about floods, droughts and water management — among the most repeated Bihar questions in GS Paper II.

> **In simple words:** The Ganga flows west to east across Bihar. Rivers from the north come down from the Himalaya in Nepal — they carry huge amounts of water and silt and cause floods. Rivers from the south come from the plateau — they are smaller, seasonal and often dry. So north Bihar fights floods while south Bihar fights drought. The climate is hot and humid, and most rain falls in the four monsoon months.

All-India background: [Rivers and Drainage of India](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/rivers-and-drainage-of-india), [Climate and Monsoon of India](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/climate-and-monsoon-of-india).

## The Ganga in Bihar

- Enters Bihar near **Chausa (Buxar)**, after being joined by the **Karmanasa**, from Uttar Pradesh. For a stretch near Buxar it forms the **Bihar–UP border**.
- Flows about **445 km** across Bihar (commonly cited figure), west to east, then leaves Bihar beyond **Bhagalpur (near Pirpainti)** towards **Sahebganj–Rajmahal (Jharkhand)** and on to West Bengal.
- Main towns on its banks: **Buxar, Patna, Hajipur (opposite, on the Gandak), Barh, Mokama, Munger, Sultanganj, Bhagalpur, Kahalgaon**.
- It divides Bihar into **North Bihar** (about 21 districts) and **South Bihar**.
- The stretch from **Sultanganj to Kahalgaon** is the **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary** (see next chapter). The Gangetic dolphin is India's **national aquatic animal** (2009).
- **National Waterway 1 (NW-1)** — **Haldia to Prayagraj** on the Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly — passes through Bihar; Patna has an inland water terminal.

## Northern tributaries (west to east)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ghaghara (Saryu)** | Tibet–Nepal Himalaya (Mapchachungo glacier region) | Forms part of the Bihar–UP border along Siwan–Saran | **Ganga near Chhapra (Saran)** (Doriganj/Revelganj area) | Big flood river of UP |
| **Gandak (Narayani; ancient Sadanira)** | Nepal Himalaya (Kali Gandaki and Trishuli streams; near the Dhaulagiri–Annapurna region) | Enters Bihar at **Triveni / Valmikinagar** (West Champaran); forms the boundary of Saran–Gopalganj with Champaran–Muzaffarpur–Vaishali | **Ganga at Hajipur–Sonepur** (opposite Patna) | **Gandak barrage at Valmikinagar** (Indo-Nepal Gandak agreement, **1959**); Sonepur fair at the confluence; **Shaligram** stones found in its upper course |
| **Burhi Gandak** | **Someshwar hills**, West Champaran (Chautarwa chaur area) | Flows south-east, parallel to the Gandak, through Muzaffarpur, Samastipur, Begusarai | **Ganga near Khagaria** (opposite Munger) | An old channel of the Gandak; **Muzaffarpur** stands on it |
| **Bagmati** | Shivpuri hills near **Kathmandu** (Nepal) | Enters Bihar in **Sitamarhi**; flows through Sheohar, Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Samastipur | Joins the **Kosi** system (near Khagaria) | Pashupatinath temple (Kathmandu) is on its bank in Nepal; changes course often |
| **Kamla–Balan** | **Mahabharat range** (Nepal) | Madhubani, Darbhanga | Joins the Kosi system | Called the "**Ganga of Mithila**" |
| **Kosi (Saptakoshi)** | Formed in **Nepal** by **seven streams** — main ones **Sun Koshi, Arun (from Tibet) and Tamur** — from the Everest–Kanchenjunga region | Enters Bihar at **Bhimnagar (Supaul)**; flows through Supaul, Saharsa, Madhepura, Purnia, Khagaria, Katihar | **Ganga near Kursela (Katihar)** | "**Sorrow of Bihar**"; **Kosi barrage** at **Bhimnagar/Hanumannagar** on the Nepal side (Kosi agreement **1954**; barrage 1960s) |
| **Mahananda** | Darjeeling hills (West Bengal) | Kishanganj, Purnia, Katihar | Ganga in **West Bengal / Bangladesh** (not in Bihar) | Tributary **Kankai**; drains Seemanchal |

> **Memory trick:** West to east, north side — "**Go, Get Big Bags, Kamla Keeps Mangoes**" — **G**haghara, **G**andak, **B**urhi Gandak, **B**agmati, **K**amla, **K**osi, **M**ahananda.

> **Prelims angle:** Where do rivers meet the Ganga? **Ghaghara — near Chhapra**; **Gandak — Hajipur/Sonepur**; **Burhi Gandak — near Khagaria (Munger)**; **Kosi — Kursela (Katihar)**; **Son — near Maner/Danapur (Patna)**; **Punpun — Fatuha (Patna)**; **Kiul — near Surajgarha (Lakhisarai)**; **Karmanasa — near Chausa (Buxar)**.

| **Son** | **Amarkantak plateau** (Madhya Pradesh) | Enters Bihar on the Rohtas–Aurangabad border; flows north-east past **Dehri** | **Ganga near Maner/Danapur, west of Patna** | Longest southern tributary; tributaries **North Koel** (Jharkhand), **Rihand** (UP); **Indrapuri barrage** near Dehri; old **Dehri anicut** (1870s) fed the Son canals; the Son's sand is famous for construction |

| **Sakri** | Chotanagpur hills | Nawada, Nalanda | Tal area / Kiul system | — |
| **Kiul** | Hills of **Jharkhand / Jamui** | Jamui, Lakhisarai | **Ganga near Surajgarha** | Tributaries **Harohar**, Ulai |
| **Chandan** | Hills of **Jharkhand / Banka** | Banka, Bhagalpur | Ganga near Bhagalpur | Flashy seasonal river |
| **Badua** | Banka hills | Banka, Bhagalpur | Ganga | — |

> **Common mistake:** The **Son** joins the Ganga **west of Patna** (near Maner/Danapur), **not** at Patna city itself. In ancient times the confluence was closer to Pataliputra — that is why Pataliputra was a "water fort".

**Ahar–pyne system**: the traditional irrigation of south Bihar (Gaya, Jehanabad, Nalanda, Nawada, Aurangabad). An **ahar** is a catchment basin with embankments on three sides that stores rainwater; a **pyne** is a channel that carries water from a river to ahars and fields. Reviving ahar–pynes is part of **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali**.

## Floods in Bihar

Bihar is **India's most flood-prone state**. According to the **Water Resources Department, Bihar**, about **68,800 km² — around 73% of the state's area — is flood-affected**. North Bihar is the worst hit.

### Why north Bihar floods

1. **Himalayan rivers from Nepal** (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla, Mahananda) bring very high monsoon discharge.
2. **Heavy silt load**: Himalayan rocks are young and erode easily; the Kosi carries one of the highest silt loads of any river. Silt raises the riverbed, so water spills over.
3. **Sudden fall in slope**: rivers drop steeply in Nepal and then spread over the flat plain, losing speed.
4. **Shifting channels**: the **Kosi has shifted westward by about 100–150 km** over two centuries; rivers break into many channels.
5. **Heavy monsoon rain** in the Nepal catchment and in north Bihar itself; cloudbursts.
6. **Deforestation** and land use change in the hills.
7. **Embankments**: they protect some areas but trap silt inside, raise the riverbed, and **breach** suddenly; they also block drainage, creating **waterlogging** outside.
8. **Poor drainage**: chaurs, flat land, roads and rail lines that block natural flow.
9. **Ganga backwater effect**: when the Ganga is in high flood, tributaries cannot drain into it; silting of the Ganga (some link it to the Farakka barrage downstream) adds to this.
10. **Trans-boundary factor**: catchments are in Nepal (and Tibet), so Bihar cannot manage them alone.

### The Kosi — "Sorrow of Bihar"

- Most dangerous river of Bihar because of silt, steep slope and shifting course.
- Embankments were built on both sides in the **1950s–60s** under the Kosi project.
- **18 August 2008 — the Kusaha breach**: the eastern embankment broke at **Kusaha (in Nepal, upstream of the barrage)**; the Kosi moved into an old channel and flooded **Supaul, Madhepura, Araria, Saharsa and Purnia** — declared a **national calamity**. Lakhs of people were displaced and farmland was covered with sand.
- Response: the **Kosi Flood Recovery Project** and later the **Bihar Kosi Basin Development Project** (World Bank support).
- Long-term proposal: the **Sapta Kosi High Dam** in Nepal (multi-purpose; pending for decades).

### Flood management in Bihar

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| Embankments | Several thousand km along north Bihar rivers (WRD Bihar data — check latest figure) |
| Forecasting | Central Water Commission and **FMISC** (Flood Management Improvement Support Centre, WRD Bihar) — modelling and early warning |
| Disaster management | **Bihar State Disaster Management Authority (BSDMA)**; SDRF; flood shelters; community kitchens; "Disaster Management Roadmap" |
| Cash relief | **Gratuitous relief** transferred by DBT to flood-hit families |
| River linking | Kosi–Mechi; Burhi Gandak–Noon–Baya–Ganga link (proposed) |
| Non-structural | Flood plain zoning, raised houses and hand pumps, flood-tolerant paddy, insurance |

> **Mains angle:** For "Floods are a recurring tragedy of Bihar. Suggest a long-term strategy", use **4 Rs**: **R**educe (catchment treatment in Nepal, Sapta Kosi dam with Nepal), **R**oom for the river (flood-plain zoning, fewer new embankments, drainage of chaurs), **R**eady (forecasting, early warning, shelters, raised hand pumps), **R**ecover (DBT relief, crop insurance, desilting, sand removal). Add **Indo-Nepal cooperation** and **climate change** (more intense rain events).

## Drought and water scarcity

- **South and south-west Bihar** (Gaya, Aurangabad, Nawada, Jamui, Banka, Kaimur, Rohtas, Jehanabad, parts of Munger) are **drought-prone**.
- Causes: lower and erratic rainfall, rocky plateau soil that holds little water, seasonal rivers, decline of ahar–pynes, over-use of groundwater.
- In several recent years (for example 2022 and 2023) many districts were declared drought-affected after weak monsoons (check current-year declarations).
- Paradox: in some years north Bihar has floods while south Bihar has drought **at the same time**.

### Groundwater quality problems

| Contaminant | Where | Health effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Arsenic** | Districts along the **Ganga** (e.g. Buxar, Bhojpur, Patna, Vaishali, Samastipur, Begusarai, Bhagalpur, Khagaria) | Skin lesions, cancer |
| **Fluoride** | **South Bihar** (e.g. Gaya, Nawada, Aurangabad, Jamui, Banka, Kaimur, Rohtas) | Dental and skeletal fluorosis |
| **Iron** | **North-east** (e.g. Purnia, Kishanganj, Araria, Katihar, Supaul) | Taste, staining, health issues |

The **Har Ghar Nal ka Jal** scheme (Saat Nischay) and water treatment plants aim at safe piped water in these areas.

## Climate of Bihar

### Type

Bihar has a **humid subtropical, monsoon-type climate** (Köppen **Cwa/Cwg** — dry winter, hot summer). Being far from the sea and close to the Himalaya, it has a **continental** touch — hot summers and cool winters.

### Seasons

| Season | Months | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Summer** | March – mid-June | Temperatures above 40 °C in May in south-west Bihar (Gaya is among the hottest); **loo** — hot, dry westerly winds in April–May; **Kalbaisakhi (nor'westers)** — thunderstorms with hail in east and north-east Bihar in April–May, useful for jute and early crops; frequent **lightning** |
| **Monsoon (rainy)** | Mid-June – September | **Bay of Bengal branch** of the south-west monsoon arrives from the **south-east** around **mid-June** (first in Purnia–Kishanganj); about **80–85% of annual rain** falls in these months |
| **Retreating monsoon / autumn** | October – November | The "**Hathiya (Hathia)**" rain (under the Hasta nakshatra, late September–early October) is critical for kharif paddy and rabi sowing moisture |
| **Winter** | December – February | Cool and dry; **western disturbances** bring light rain useful for **wheat and rabi crops**; **cold waves** and dense **fog** in December–January |

### Rainfall pattern

- Average annual rainfall is roughly **1,000–1,200 mm** (IMD normals; check the latest).
- Rainfall **increases from west to east and from south to north**:
  - **Highest** in the north-east — **Kishanganj** and neighbouring districts (often around **2,000 mm** or more).
  - **Lowest** in the south-west — **Kaimur, Rohtas, Aurangabad, Gaya, Buxar** (around 1,000 mm or less).
- The monsoon is **erratic**: late onset, long dry spells and early withdrawal are common, and recent years show more intense rain on fewer days.

### Climate hazards

| Hazard | Note |
|---|---|
| Floods | North Bihar, July–September |
| Drought | South-west Bihar, weak monsoon years |
| Heat wave | April–June; school timings changed in recent heat waves |
| **Lightning** | Bihar records many lightning deaths each year; the state's **Indra Vajra** mobile app gives lightning warnings |
| Cold wave and fog | December–January; transport and health impact |
| Earthquake | North-eastern border districts (Zone V) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar has prepared a **State Action Plan on Climate Change** and, with UNEP support, a roadmap for **climate-resilient, low-carbon development** (announced in 2021 — check latest). Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali is the state's flagship climate mission. Link these in any Mains answer on climate change.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Tributary | A river that flows into a bigger river |
| Confluence | Place where two rivers meet |
| Barrage | A low dam with gates that raises water level to divert it into canals |
| Anicut | An old-style masonry weir across a river for irrigation |
| Embankment | A raised earth wall along a river to stop floods |
| Silt load | Amount of fine sediment a river carries |
| Breach | A break in an embankment |
| Backwater effect | When a high main river stops a tributary from draining into it |
| Ahar / pyne | Rainwater catchment basin / feeder channel (south Bihar) |

| Kalbaisakhi | Pre-monsoon thunderstorm (nor'wester) in eastern India |
| Hathiya rain | Late monsoon rain under the Hasta star, important for paddy |
| Western disturbance | Winter weather system from the Mediterranean region bringing light rain |

### Mains questions

**1. "Floods in north Bihar are as much a man-made problem as a natural one." Critically examine and suggest a long-term strategy. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** About 73% of Bihar's area is flood-prone; north Bihar faces floods almost every year.
- **Body (natural):** Himalayan rivers, silt, slope, heavy monsoon, shifting courses, trans-boundary catchment.
- **Body (man-made):** embankments trapping silt and breaching (Kusaha 2008), blocked drainage, encroachment of floodplains and chaurs, deforestation, poorly planned roads, weak maintenance.
- **Strategy:** catchment treatment and Sapta Kosi dam with Nepal; flood-plain zoning; drainage of chaurs; desilting; river linking (Kosi–Mechi); forecasting (FMISC); disaster preparedness; crop insurance; flood-tolerant crops; making chaurs productive (fish, makhana).
- **Conclusion:** Move from "flood control" to "flood management and living with floods".

**2. Discuss the problem of drought and groundwater in south Bihar and the role of traditional water systems. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** South Bihar is rain-shadowed and rocky.
- **Body:** Low rainfall; seasonal rivers; falling water tables; fluoride; ahar–pyne system and its decline (zamindari end, neglect, encroachment); Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali revival of ahars, pynes, ponds and wells; check dams; micro-irrigation; crop diversification to pulses and millets.
- **Conclusion:** Community-based revival of traditional systems with modern science can drought-proof south Bihar.

**3. Explain the main features of Bihar's climate and their impact on agriculture. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Humid subtropical monsoon climate.
- **Body:** Seasons; monsoon dependence; west–east and south–north rainfall gradient; Hathiya rain and paddy; western disturbances and wheat; heat waves and terminal heat stress on wheat; cold waves; floods and droughts; climate change signals.
- **Conclusion:** Climate-resilient agriculture (crop calendars, tolerant varieties, water harvesting) is the answer — link to the Agriculture Roadmap.
