GS Paper II asks Indian Polity "with Bihar focus: administration, Panchayati Raj, etc." So you must know Bihar's institutions in detail — seat numbers, the Legislative Council's composition, the Gram Kachahari, the 2006 Panchayat Act, the Right to Public Services and Grievance Redressal Acts. Prelims asks numbers and "first state" facts; Mains asks you to evaluate local governance and administrative reforms in Bihar.

> **In simple words:** Bihar's government works like every Indian state: the Governor is the formal head, the Chief Minister and ministers run the government, and the legislature makes laws. Bihar is one of the few states with two houses. Below the state are 9 divisions, 38 districts, 101 sub-divisions and 534 blocks. Villages are governed by three-tier panchayats with a special village court called the Gram Kachahari, and half of all panchayat seats are reserved for women.

## The executive

### Governor

- Appointed by the **President** (Article 155); holds office during the President's pleasure; normal term five years.
- **Current Governor: Syed Ata Hasnain** (retired Lieutenant General), sworn in on **14 March 2026**, succeeding **Arif Mohammed Khan** (as of 2026 — check the latest).
- The Governor is also **Chancellor (Kuladhipati)** of most state universities.
- First Governor of Bihar after independence: **Jairamdas Daulatram** (1947–48).

### Chief Minister and Council of Ministers

- The CM is appointed by the Governor (Article 164) and must enjoy the confidence of the **Vidhan Sabha**.
- **Current CM: Samrat Choudhary (BJP)**, sworn in on **15 April 2026** as Bihar's **24th Chief Minister** and the **first from the BJP**. Deputy CMs: **Vijay Kumar Chaudhary** and **Bijendra Prasad Yadav** (JD(U)) (as of 2026 — check the latest).
- The size of the Council of Ministers cannot exceed **15% of the Assembly** (91st Amendment, 2003) → maximum **36** ministers in Bihar (15% of 243 = 36.45).
- **Article 164(1) proviso**: Bihar was one of three states (with MP and Odisha) required to have a **Minister for Tribal Welfare**. The **94th Amendment (2006)** replaced **Bihar** with **Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh**, because most tribal areas went to Jharkhand. Prelims asks this.

### Bureaucracy

| Post | Role |
|---|---|
| **Chief Secretary** | Head of the state civil service; coordinates all departments |
| **Development Commissioner** | Coordinates development and planning (released the caste survey data in 2023) |
| Additional Chief Secretary / Principal Secretary / Secretary | Head of a department |
| **Divisional Commissioner** | Head of a division (9) — supervises districts, revenue appeals |
| **District Magistrate (DM) / Collector** | Head of district administration — law and order, revenue, development, elections (District Election Officer) |
| **Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO)** | Head of a sub-division (101) — one of the posts filled through BPSC CCE (as SDM/SDO after promotion) |
| **Block Development Officer (BDO)** | Head of a block (534) — development schemes; a typical CCE entry post (Bihar Administrative Service / Rural Development Service) |
| **Circle Officer (CO)** | Head of a revenue circle — land records, mutation |
| **Superintendent of Police (SP)** | Head of district police; **DSP** posts are filled through CCE |

> **Bihar connection:** Many posts you are applying for through the CCE — **Deputy Collector, DSP, BDO, Revenue Officer, Supply Inspector, Labour Enforcement Officer** — sit in this structure. Interview boards often ask how you would handle floods, land disputes or prohibition enforcement in such a post.

## The legislature

Bihar has a **bicameral legislature** (two houses) — one of only six states with a Legislative Council (the others: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana; check for changes).

### Bihar Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seats | **243** (after 2000; earlier **324** in undivided Bihar) |
| Reserved | **38 for SC**, **2 for ST** (Delimitation 2008) |
| Term | 5 years |
| Speaker | **Prem Kumar** (elected 2 December 2025; as of 2026 — check the latest) |
| Last election | **November 2025** — NDA **202** (BJP 89, JD(U) 85, LJP(RV) 19, HAM 5, RLM 4); RJD 25; Congress 6 |
| Building | Built in **1920**; the first sitting of the Bihar and Orissa Legislative Council was held there on **7 February 1921**; centenary celebrated in 2021 |
| Article | Art. 170 (composition) |

### Bihar Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total members | **75** |
| Elected by MLAs | **27** |
| Elected by local authorities | **24** |
| Elected by graduates | **6** |
| Elected by teachers | **6** |
| Nominated by Governor | **12** (from literature, science, art, cooperation, social service) |
| Term | 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years; it is a **permanent house** |
| Article | Art. 169 (creation/abolition by Parliament on the Assembly's resolution), Art. 171 (composition) |

Check the arithmetic: 27 + 24 + 6 + 6 + 12 = **75**.

> **Memory trick:** "**27-24-6-6-12**" — read it as "**twenty-seven MLAs, twenty-four locals, six-six graduates and teachers, a dozen nominated**."

## Judiciary — Patna High Court

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Established | **1916** (foundation stone laid by Viceroy **Lord Hardinge** in **1913**; Letters Patent dated **9 February 1916**; formally inaugurated in early 1916) |
| First Chief Justice | **Sir Edward Maynard Des Champs Chamier** |
| Jurisdiction | State of Bihar only (its Ranchi bench became the **Jharkhand High Court** in 2000) |
| Seat | Patna (no permanent bench elsewhere) |
| Important cases | *Kameshwar Singh* (1951) — land reforms law struck down → First Amendment; **20 June 2024** — struck down the 65% reservation laws; earlier, the 2023 caste survey was **upheld** by the High Court (August 2023) |
| Subordinate courts | District and Sessions Courts in all districts; **Gram Kachaharis** at village level for petty cases |

The **Patna High Court building** (Indo-Saracenic / neo-classical) and the **Patna Secretariat** were built in the 1910s when Patna became the capital.

## Panchayati Raj in Bihar

### History

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1947 | **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 1947** — early panchayat law |
| 1961 | Bihar Panchayat Samiti and Zila Parishad Act — three tiers on paper |
| 1993 | **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 1993** — after the **73rd Amendment (1992)** |
| 2001 | First elections under the new system (after a long gap since 1978) |
| **2006** | **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006** — present law; **50% reservation for women** (first state in India); reservation for **EBCs** |
| 2016, 2021 | Regular elections; **2021** elections held in **11 phases**, with **EVMs used for the first time** in Bihar's panchayat polls |
| 2026 | Next panchayat elections due (check the State Election Commission's schedule) |

### Structure

| Tier | Body | Head (elected) | Officer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village | **Gram Panchayat** (with Gram Sabha of all voters) | **Mukhiya** | Panchayat Secretary |
| Village (justice) | **Gram Kachahari** | **Sarpanch** | Nyaya Mitra (legal assistant) |
| Block | **Panchayat Samiti** | **Pramukh** | BDO (executive officer) |
| District | **Zila Parishad** | **Adhyaksh** | Deputy Development Commissioner (DDC) as CEO |

### Gram Kachahari — Bihar's special feature

- A **village court** elected alongside the gram panchayat, headed by the **Sarpanch** with elected **panches**.
- Hears **petty civil cases** (e.g. small money or property disputes up to a set value) and **minor criminal cases** (e.g. small thefts, quarrels) listed in the Act.
- Aims at **quick, cheap justice and reconciliation** in the village; a **Nyaya Mitra** (law graduate) and **Nyaya Sachiv** help it.
- Cannot award imprisonment; it can impose small fines.

### Reservation in panchayats (Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006)

| Group | Reservation |
|---|---|
| **Women** | **50%** of seats and posts in all categories (**first state** to do this) |
| **SC and ST** | In proportion to their population |
| **EBC (Extremely Backward Classes)** | Up to **20%** (Bihar specific) |
| Total cap | Within 50% for SC + ST + EBC combined |

The reservation **rotates** (changes from seat to seat) after every two terms.

> **Mains angle:** For "Evaluate Panchayati Raj in Bihar": positives — 50% women (lakhs of women representatives), EBC reservation, Gram Kachahari, regular elections, devolution of some schemes (Saat Nischay's Har Ghar Nal and Gali-Nali implemented through ward committees); problems — "**Mukhiya-pati**" (husbands acting for elected women), weak finances, limited transfer of the 29 subjects of the Eleventh Schedule, staff shortage, corruption in schemes. Suggest: training (Panchayat Sarkar Bhawans), own revenue, social audit, e-Gram Swaraj, capacity building of women leaders.

## Governance laws and reforms of Bihar

| Law / reform | Year | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Bihar Right to Public Services Act** | **2011** (in force **15 August 2011**) | Time-bound delivery of notified services (caste, income, residence certificates, licences); officials penalised for delay; RTPS counters in every block |
| **Bihar Lok Shikayat Nivaran Adhikar Adhiniyam** (Right to Public Grievance Redressal) | **2015** (in force **5 June 2016**) | Legal right to have a grievance heard and decided within **60 working days**; Lok Shikayat Nivaran Padadhikari at sub-division/district level — **Bihar was the first state** with such a law |
| **Bihar Special Courts Act** | **2009** | Special courts for corruption cases; **confiscation of disproportionate property** of public servants |
| **Bihar Lokayukta Act** | **2011** | Ombudsman |
| **Bihar Police Act** | **2007** | Police reforms after the Prakash Singh judgment |
| **Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act** | **2016** (amended 2018, 2022) | Total prohibition (see the schemes chapter) |
| **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act** | 2006 | 50% women |
| **Bihar Municipal Act** | 2007 | Urban governance |
| **Bihar Caste-Based Survey** | 2022–23 | State survey of caste and economic status |
| **Reservation Amendment Acts** | 2023 | 65% quota (struck down by Patna HC, 2024; appeal pending) |
| **e-Governance** | — | **e-Labharthi**, **DBT** portals, **Bihar Bhumi** (online land records, mutation), **RTPS online**, **e-Kalyan**; a **state-wide land survey** (Bihar Special Survey and Settlement) is under way |

> **Prelims angle:** "**First state** to give a legal right to grievance redressal" — **Bihar (2015 Act, in force 5 June 2016)**. "Right to Public Services Act in force" — **15 August 2011**. "50% women in panchayats" — **2006**.

### Elections in Bihar — recent points

- The **Election Commission of India** carried out a **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of Bihar's electoral rolls in **mid-2025**, before the Assembly election — the first such exercise in decades; it was challenged in the Supreme Court and became national news (check the outcome and later SIRs in other states).
- The **2025 Assembly election** was held in two phases in November 2025.

### Mains questions

**1. Critically evaluate the working of Panchayati Raj institutions in Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 73rd Amendment + Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006.
- **Body:** Three tiers + Gram Kachahari; 50% women and EBC reservation — social inclusion; implementation of Saat Nischay schemes via wards; problems — proxy leadership, limited functions/funds/functionaries (3Fs), dependence on grants, corruption, weak Gram Sabhas; steps — Panchayat Sarkar Bhawans, training, e-Gram Swaraj, 15th FC grants, social audit.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar leads in representation; it must now lead in real devolution.

**2. Discuss the innovations in governance introduced by Bihar since 2005 to improve service delivery and accountability. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar's shift towards a rights-based, time-bound administration.
- **Body:** RTPS Act 2011; Lok Shikayat Act 2015 (first in India); Special Courts Act 2009; Lokayukta Act 2011; e-governance (DBT, e-Labharthi, Bihar Bhumi); 50% women in local bodies and 35% in jobs; JEEViKA for last-mile delivery; land survey; limitations — staff shortage, digital divide, implementation gaps, prohibition enforcement burden on police and courts.
- **Conclusion:** These laws convert citizens from "beneficiaries" into "rights-holders"; capacity building is the next step.

**3. What is the role of the Legislative Council in Bihar? Should it continue? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Art. 169, 171; Bihar's Council of 75.
- **Body (for):** revising chamber, representation of teachers, graduates, local bodies, experts; continuity as a permanent house. **(against):** cost; can only delay; used to accommodate defeated leaders; most states manage without one.
- **Conclusion:** Reform (better use of expert nominations and committee work) rather than abolition.
