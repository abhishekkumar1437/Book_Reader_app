BPSC loves state schemes: Prelims asks launch years, components and target groups; Mains asks you to evaluate them ("Assess the impact of prohibition in Bihar", "Discuss the role of JEEViKA in women's empowerment"). Schemes change often, so every item here carries **"as of 2026 — check the latest"**. Update this chapter from the newspaper, the Bihar Budget speech and the Economic Survey.

> **In simple words:** Since 2005 Bihar's big policy themes have been: girls' education (cycles, uniforms, cash incentives), women's empowerment (50% in panchayats, 35% in jobs, JEEViKA self-help groups), basic services to every home (Saat Nischay — electricity, piped water, lanes, toilets), environment (Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali), social reform (prohibition) and social justice (caste survey and reservation). In 2025–26 the focus shifted to jobs, women's enterprises, pensions and free electricity.

All-India schemes: [Social Sector and Flagship Schemes](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/social-sector-and-flagship-schemes) and [National Schemes and Missions Tracker](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/national-schemes-and-missions-tracker).

## Saat Nischay (Seven Resolves)

### Saat Nischay-1 (2015–20)

Launched after the **2015** election as the state's main development agenda.

| # | Nischay (Hindi name) | What it gave |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aarthik Hal, Yuvaon ko Bal** (economic solution, strength to youth) | **Bihar Student Credit Card** (education loan up to **₹4 lakh**), **Mukhyamantri Nishchay Swayam Sahayata Bhatta** (₹1,000 a month for up to 2 years to job-seeking youth aged 20–25), **Kushal Yuva Programme** (soft skills, computer, communication) — all launched on **2 October 2016**; free Wi-Fi in universities and colleges |
| 2 | **Aarakshit Rozgar, Mahilaon ka Adhikar** | **35% reservation for women** in all state government jobs (2016) |
| 3 | **Har Ghar Bijli** | Electricity connection to every household |
| 4 | **Har Ghar Nal ka Jal** | Piped drinking water to every household |
| 5 | **Ghar tak Pakki Gali–Naliyan** | Paved lanes and drains up to every house |
| 6 | **Shauchalay Nirman, Ghar ka Samman** | Toilets in every home (with Lohiya Swachh Bihar Abhiyan) |
| 7 | **Avsar Badhe, Aage Padhe** | New engineering colleges, polytechnics, ITIs, nursing schools, medical colleges in districts |

### Saat Nischay-2 (2020–25)

| # | Nischay | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Yuva Shakti, Bihar ki Pragati** | Skills, IT, employment, new technical training |
| 2 | **Sashakt Mahila, Saksham Mahila** | Women entrepreneurs — grants and loans (Mukhyamantri Mahila Udyami Yojana), higher incentives for girls' graduation |
| 3 | **Har Khet tak Sinchai ka Pani** | Irrigation to every farm |
| 4 | **Swachh Gaon, Samriddh Gaon** | Solar street lights, solid waste management in villages |
| 5 | **Swachh Shahar, Viksit Shahar** | Urban infrastructure, housing for the urban poor |

| 7 | **Sabke liye Atirikt Swasthya Suvidha** | More health facilities, telemedicine, animal health |

### Saat Nischay-3 (2025–30)

Approved by the state cabinet on **16 December 2025** (as reported).

| # | Nischay (English meaning) | Goals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Double employment, double income** | **Double the per capita income**; priority to about **94 lakh poor families** identified in the 2023 caste survey |
| 2 | **Prosperous industries, empowered Bihar** | Make Bihar a tech hub of eastern India; attract about **₹50 lakh crore** private investment; **1 crore jobs** for youth over five years |
| 3 | **Prosperity through progress in agriculture** | Fourth Agriculture Roadmap, **Makhana roadmap** for production and processing |
| 4 | **Bright future through quality education** | Upgrade old institutions as centres of excellence; an **Education City** |
| 5 | **Accessible healthcare, secure life** | Incentives for doctors in rural areas, ban on private practice by government doctors, PPP for specialty hospitals |
| 6 | **Strong foundation for modern expansion** | Planned modern cities, affordable urban housing, connectivity |
| 7 | **Ease of living with dignity for all** | Technology-based, responsive governance |

Source: The Daily Pioneer, 17 December 2025 report on cabinet approval (check official Hindi names of each nischay).

> **Mains angle:** Compare the three: **SN-1 = basic household services**, **SN-2 = quality and coverage (irrigation, health, cities)**, **SN-3 = income, jobs and investment**. This shows Bihar moving from "basic needs" to "growth and prosperity" — a good frame for any Mains answer on Bihar's development model.

### JEEViKA (Bihar Rural Livelihoods Promotion Society)

- Started in **2006** as the **Bihar Rural Livelihoods Project (BRLP)** with **World Bank** support; later became the state mission under the national **NRLM (Aajeevika)**. The women members are called "**Jeevika Didis**".
- Scale: about **1.40 crore families** in **11.03 lakh self-help groups (SHGs)** (Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26).
- Structure: **SHG (10–12 women) → Village Organisation (VO) → Cluster Level Federation (CLF)**.
- Activities: savings and credit, bank linkage, farm and non-farm livelihoods, **Didi ki Rasoi** (canteens in hospitals and offices), dairy, poultry, **Satat Jeevikoparjan Yojana** (2018, for ultra-poor families including those once dependent on liquor and toddy trade), anti-liquor campaigns.
- JEEViKA Didis played a big role in **prohibition** (they demanded it) and in campaigns like human chains.

> **Prelims angle:** JEEViKA — **2006**, **World Bank**, **BRLPS**, SHG → VO → CLF. Kushal Yuva, Student Credit Card, Swayam Sahayata Bhatta — **2 October 2016**.

## Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan

- Launched on **26 October 2019**. Theme: "**Jal aur Hariyali hain, tabhi Jeevan hai**" (life exists only when there is water and greenery).
- Aim: fight **climate change**, falling groundwater, drought and floods through water conservation and tree cover.
- **Eleven components**:

| # | Component |
|---|---|
| 1 | Survey and **removal of encroachment** from public water bodies |
| 2 | **Renovation of public ponds, ahars, pynes** |
| 3 | Renovation of **public wells** |
| 4 | **Soak pits / recharge structures** near hand pumps, wells and taps |
| 5 | **Check dams** and water harvesting in hilly areas |
| 6 | **New water sources** (ponds) |
| 7 | **Rooftop rainwater harvesting** in government buildings |
| 8 | **Tree plantation** (green cover target) |
| 9 | **Crop diversification**, drip irrigation, organic farming, new technology |
| 10 | **Solar energy** — rooftop solar and energy saving |
| 11 | **Awareness** campaigns |

- A **human chain** of crores of people was formed on **19 January 2020** in support (human chains were also formed for prohibition in 2017 and against dowry and child marriage in 2018).
- Implemented by **15 departments** with the Rural Development Department as the nodal department.

> **Bihar connection:** Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali is Bihar's main climate programme. Use it in Mains answers on climate change, groundwater, drought in south Bihar and the Ramsar wetlands.

## Prohibition (liquor ban)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| Nov 2015 | Promise to ban liquor, demanded by women (Jeevika Didis) |
| **1 April 2016** | Ban on **country liquor** |
| **5 April 2016** | **Total prohibition** — ban on Indian-made foreign liquor too |
| 30 Sept 2016 | Patna High Court struck down the notification under the old excise law |
| **2 October 2016** | New **Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016** came into force — strict penalties |
| 2017 | Human chain in support of prohibition (21 January 2017) |
| **2018** | Amendment softened some provisions (e.g. collective fines removed) |
| **2022** | Amendment — **first-time drinkers** can be released on paying a **fine**, instead of jail |
| 2021–22 | Several **hooch (spurious liquor) tragedies** (e.g. Saran, December 2022) |

**Positive effects claimed:** fall in domestic violence and household spending on liquor, more spending on food and education, women's support, fewer road accidents (studies differ on size).

**Problems:** loss of **excise revenue** (Bihar earned several thousand crore rupees a year from liquor before 2016); **illegal trade** and smuggling from neighbouring states and Nepal; **hooch deaths**; burden on **courts and jails** — the Supreme Court commented on the large number of bail cases; police resources diverted.

> **Mains angle:** "Critically assess prohibition in Bihar" — Intro (2016, women's demand, Article 47 DPSP); social gains; economic costs (revenue loss; Satat Jeevikoparjan for those who lost livelihoods); enforcement problems; data from surveys (cite carefully, "studies suggest"); suggestions — focus on suppliers not consumers, de-addiction centres, community vigilance, technology (drones, scanners), periodic review. Conclusion: social goal is valid; enforcement must be humane and effective.

## Caste-Based Survey 2023 and reservation

### The survey

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | **Bihar Jaati Adharit Ganana** (Caste-Based Survey) |
| Why a "survey" | Only the Centre can conduct a **census** (Union List, entry 69); a state can conduct a survey |
| Phases | Phase 1 (house listing) from **7 January 2023**; Phase 2 (caste and socio-economic data) from April 2023 |
| Court | **Patna HC** stayed it in May 2023, then **upheld** it on **1 August 2023**; a challenge remains pending in the Supreme Court (check) |
| Results | Released on **2 October 2023** by the Development Commissioner; socio-economic data placed in the Assembly in **November 2023** |
| Total population counted | **about 13.07 crore** |

### Results

| Category | Share of population |
|---|---|
| **Extremely Backward Classes (EBC)** | **36.01%** (largest group) |
| **Backward Classes (BC)** | **27.12%** (some reports: 27.13%) |
| **Scheduled Castes (SC)** | **19.65%** |
| **Scheduled Tribes (ST)** | **1.68%** |
| **Unreserved (General)** | **15.52%** |

- **BC + EBC together: about 63%**.
- **Largest single caste: Yadav, about 14.3%** (reported as 14.26–14.27%).
- **Religion**: **Hindu 81.99%**, **Muslim 17.70%**, others small.
- **Economic data**: about **34%** of families (about **94 lakh families**) earned **₹6,000 or less per month**.

### Reservation changes (November 2023) and the court verdict

| Category | Before | After the 2023 laws |
|---|---|---|
| SC | 16% | **20%** |
| ST | 1% | **2%** |
| EBC | 18% | **25%** |
| BC | 12% | **18%** |
| **Total (caste-based)** | **50%** | **65%** |
| EWS (central law, 103rd Amendment) | 10% | 10% |
| **Grand total** | 60% | **75%** |

- The **Bihar Reservation of Vacancies in Posts and Services (Amendment) Act, 2023** and the education counterpart were passed in **November 2023**.
- **20 June 2024**: the **Patna High Court struck down** both laws as violating **Articles 14, 15 and 16** (equality), beyond the **50% ceiling** of the *Indra Sawhney* (1992) judgment, and without proper study of adequacy of representation.
- **29 July 2024**: the Supreme Court **refused to stay** the High Court verdict but agreed to hear the appeal; the state asked for the laws to be put in the **Ninth Schedule** (check the latest status).
- **Bihar Laghu Udyami Yojana** (2023): up to **₹2 lakh** in instalments to one member of each poor family identified by the survey to start a small business.

> **Prelims angle:** EBC **36.01%** is the **largest** group; SC **19.65%**; ST **1.68%**; unreserved **15.52%**. Released **2 October 2023**. Raised quota **65%** (75% with EWS). Struck down by Patna HC **20 June 2024**.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Nischay | Resolve or commitment |
| SHG | Self-help group — small group of women who save and borrow together |
| VO / CLF | Village Organisation / Cluster Level Federation of SHGs |
| DBT | Direct Benefit Transfer — money sent straight to the bank account |
| Prohibition | Legal ban on making, selling and drinking alcohol |
| Hooch | Illegal, often poisonous, liquor |

| EWS | Economically Weaker Sections (10% quota, 103rd Amendment 2019) |
| Domicile | Permanent residence in the state |
| Human chain | Mass event where people hold hands across long distances to support a cause |

### Mains questions

**1. Assess the impact of the Saat Nischay programme on development in Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 2015 agenda, continued as SN-2 and SN-3.
- **Body:** Household services — electricity, piped water, lanes, toilets (near-universal coverage claims); youth — credit card, skills, allowance; women — 35% jobs; institutions — colleges, polytechnics; SN-2 — irrigation, health, cities; issues — quality of water supply and maintenance, slow loan disbursal under the credit card, employment gaps; SN-3's shift to income and jobs.
- **Conclusion:** SN built the basic floor; SN-3 must deliver jobs and productivity.

**2. "JEEViKA has transformed the lives of rural women in Bihar." Examine. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 2006, World Bank; 1.40 crore families (ES 2025–26).
- **Body:** Savings and credit; bank linkage; livelihoods; voice in family and village; role in prohibition; Didi ki Rasoi; health and nutrition behaviour; limits — small loan size, market linkage, elite capture, dependence on the state.
- **Conclusion:** A national model of women-led development; next step is enterprise and market scale (Mahila Rozgar Yojana).

**3. Discuss the significance of the Bihar Caste-Based Survey 2023 and the legal issues around the reservation increase. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** First state-level caste-and-economic survey in decades.
- **Body:** Data — EBC 36%, BC 27%, SC 19.65%; poverty data (94 lakh poor families); use for targeted welfare (Laghu Udyami Yojana); national debate leading to caste enumeration in the next Census; legal issues — state survey vs census (Union List), privacy, Patna HC upholding the survey; 65% quota struck down (Arts. 14–16, Indra Sawhney); Ninth Schedule demand; SC appeal pending.
- **Conclusion:** Data-based social justice is valuable, but reservation beyond 50% needs strong, quantifiable justification.
