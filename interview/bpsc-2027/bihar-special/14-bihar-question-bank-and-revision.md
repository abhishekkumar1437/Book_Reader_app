There are no multiple-choice questions here; MCQ practice is in the Quiz tab.

> **In simple words:** Recall first, then write. If you can answer 90 of these 100 questions and outline the ⭐ Mains questions from memory, your Bihar preparation is in good shape.

## Mains questions with outlines

Write each in about 300 words (38-mark style) unless stated. Every outline has an intro, body points and a conclusion. Always add **one Bihar figure with its source** (for example "Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26").

### History

**1. ⭐ Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement.**
- Intro: 8 August 1942 resolution; Bihar a main centre.
- Body: arrests at Sadaquat Ashram; Patna Secretariat martyrs (11 August); attacks on rail, telegraph, thanas; collapse of authority in districts; aerial firing and fines; JP's escape and Azad Dasta; students, women (Tara Rani Srivastava), peasants and socialists.
- Conclusion: proved mass readiness for freedom; Linlithgow's "most serious since 1857".

**2. ⭐ "Champaran gave Gandhi to India and India to Gandhi." Discuss.**
- Intro: 1917, first satyagraha in India.
- Body: tinkathia; Raj Kumar Shukla; defiance at Motihari; inquiry method; Agrarian Committee and 1918 Act; constructive work; rise of Bihari leaders.
- Conclusion: the template for later mass movements.

**3. ⭐ Assess the contribution of Bihar to the Revolt of 1857.**
- Intro: Bihar as the eastern centre.
- Body: Rohini; Patna (Peer Ali, Wahabis); Danapur; Arrah siege; Kunwar Singh's campaign and victory; Amar Singh; Waris Ali; reasons for failure.
- Conclusion: Kunwar Singh as the symbol of popular resistance.

**4. Evaluate Sher Shah's administration as a precursor of Mughal and British systems.**
- Intro: five-year reign, Sasaram roots.
- Body: sarkar–pargana; land measurement, patta–qabuliyat; rupiya; roads and sarais; dak; army reforms; justice.
- Conclusion: Akbar–Todar Mal and the British built on it.

**5. ⭐ "Ancient Bihar was the cradle of Indian civilisation." Justify.**
- Intro: Magadha to the Palas.
- Body: first empire; Vaishali republic; Buddhism and Jainism; councils; Kautilya and Aryabhata; Nalanda, Vikramshila; Mauryan and Pala art.
- Conclusion: link to Nalanda University revival and Buddhist circuit.

**6. Assess the significance of the JP movement.**
- Intro: March 1974 student protest.
- Body: causes; Total Revolution; Emergency; Janata victory; new leadership; civil liberties.
- Conclusion: moral politics and citizen accountability.

### Culture

**7. ⭐ Discuss the folk arts of Bihar and the role of GI tags in protecting them.**
- Intro: living, women-led traditions.
- Body: Madhubani, Manjusha, Patna Kalam, Tikuli, Sikki, Sujini, Applique, Bawan Buti; GI list and years; problems; promotion — Upendra Maharathi institute, e-commerce, tourism.
- Conclusion: culture as livelihood.

**8. Explain the importance of Chhath and other festivals in Bihar's social life.**
- Intro: Chhath as Bihar's identity.
- Body: rituals; equality; ecology; women; Sama-Chakeva, Jitiya, Madhushravani; fairs (Sonepur, Pitripaksha, Shravani).
- Conclusion: festivals as social cement and tourism assets.

### Geography and environment

**9. ⭐ Floods are an annual tragedy in north Bihar. Analyse the causes and suggest a long-term strategy.**
- Intro: ~73% area flood-prone (WRD Bihar).
- Body: Himalayan rivers, silt, slope, shifting Kosi, embankment breaches (Kusaha 2008), drainage blockage, trans-boundary catchment; strategy — Indo-Nepal cooperation (Sapta Kosi), flood-plain zoning, desilting, Kosi–Mechi link, FMISC forecasting, BSDMA preparedness, crop insurance, chaur development.
- Conclusion: from flood control to flood management.

**10. Describe the physiographic divisions of Bihar and their economic significance.**
- Intro: plain with hilly margins.
- Body: Shivalik; Terai; north plain; south plain; plateau fringe; resources and problems of each.
- Conclusion: region-specific planning.

**11. Discuss the importance of wetlands in Bihar with reference to its Ramsar sites.**
- Intro: chaurs, mauns, tals.
- Body: six Ramsar sites; ecological services; fisheries and makhana; threats; conservation steps (wetland authority, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali, community reserve at Gogabeel).
- Conclusion: wetlands as natural flood insurance.

### Economy, polity and schemes

**12. ⭐ "Bihar's economy is growing fast but remains poor." Examine with recent data.**
- Intro: ES 2025–26 — real growth 8.6% vs 6.5%.
- Body: low base; per capita NSDP 31.7% of national; agriculture employs ~54% but gives 18.3% of GSVA; MPI 33.76% (2019–21); district gaps; fiscal dependence (~74% central transfers); improvements in roads, power, SHGs.
- Conclusion: jobs, skills and industry — Saat Nischay-3 goals.

**13. ⭐ Critically evaluate prohibition in Bihar.**
- Intro: 2016, women's demand, Article 47.
- Body: social gains; revenue loss; illegal trade and hooch deaths; burden on courts; 2018 and 2022 amendments; livelihoods (Satat Jeevikoparjan).
- Conclusion: keep the goal, reform enforcement.

**14. ⭐ Evaluate the working of Panchayati Raj institutions in Bihar.**
- Intro: 2006 Act.
- Body: three tiers + Gram Kachahari; 50% women, EBC quota; scheme delivery; 3F gaps; proxy leaders; remedies.
- Conclusion: representation achieved, devolution pending.

**15. ⭐ Discuss the Bihar Caste-Based Survey 2023 and its implications for social justice.**
- Intro: released 2 October 2023.
- Body: findings; poverty data; welfare targeting; 65% quota and Patna HC verdict (Arts. 14–16, Indra Sawhney); SC appeal; national caste-census debate.
- Conclusion: data-driven policy within constitutional limits.

**16. Assess the role of JEEViKA and women-centric policies in Bihar's development.**
- Intro: 1.40 crore families in SHGs (ES 2025–26).
- Body: JEEViKA; 50% local bodies; 35% jobs; cycle and Kanya Utthan; Mahila Rozgar Yojana; outcomes and limits (low female LFPR).
- Conclusion: women as the engine of Bihar's change.
