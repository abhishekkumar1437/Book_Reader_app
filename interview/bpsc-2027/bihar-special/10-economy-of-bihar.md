"Indian Economy including Bihar" is a Prelims head, and Bihar's economy is named in Mains GS Paper II. Examiners reward answers that quote the **latest Bihar Economic Survey**. This chapter gives the figures from the **Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26** (tabled on **2 February 2026**, the 20th survey) and the **Bihar Budget 2026–27**, with the source and year next to every number. Update them when the 2026–27 survey comes out in early 2027.

> **In simple words:** Bihar's economy is growing faster than India's for several years, but it started from a very low base, so its per capita income is still the lowest among states — about one-third of the national average. Services are the biggest part of the economy, but most people still work in agriculture. The state depends heavily on money from the Centre.

All-India background: [Basic Concepts and National Income](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/basic-concepts-and-national-income), [Public Finance, Budget and Taxation](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/public-finance-budget-and-taxation), [Poverty, Unemployment and Inclusive Growth](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/poverty-unemployment-and-inclusive-growth).

## Key terms first

- **GSDP (Gross State Domestic Product)**: the value of all final goods and services produced within the state in a year — the state version of GDP.
- **NSDP (Net State Domestic Product)**: GSDP minus depreciation (wear and tear of capital).
- **Current prices** include inflation; **constant prices** (base year **2011–12**) remove inflation and show **real growth**.
- **Per capita income**: usually per capita NSDP — NSDP divided by population.
- **GSVA (Gross State Value Added)**: value added by sectors (GSDP minus product taxes plus subsidies); sector shares are given in GSVA.

### Finance Commission share

| Finance Commission | Period | Bihar's share of the divisible pool |
|---|---|---|
| 15th FC (N. K. Singh) | 2021–26 | **10.06%** — second highest after UP |
| **16th FC (Arvind Panagariya)** | **2026–31** | **9.95%** (states' total share kept at **41%**) |

Source: PRS summary of the 16th Finance Commission report. The 16th FC changed weights: income distance 42.5%, population (2011) 17.5%, demographic performance 10%, area 10%, forest 10%, and a new **contribution to GDP** criterion 10%. Bihar had asked for the states' share to be raised to 50%.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar depends on the Centre for about **three-fourths** of its revenue receipts. Its **own tax** base is small (about 5% of GSDP) partly because of **prohibition** (no liquor excise since 2016) and low urbanisation. This is a standard Mains point on "fiscal federalism and Bihar".

### Special category status (SCS) debate

- **Demand**: Bihar has asked for SCS since the 2000 bifurcation (loss of minerals and industry, floods, low per capita income, high population density). The **Raghuram Rajan Committee (2013)** placed Bihar among the "**least developed**" states on its composite index.
- **Central position**: after the **14th Finance Commission (2015)**, the Centre says no new SCS is possible; higher tax devolution was given to all states instead.
- **Bihar's case**: the state government resolution of 2023 (after the caste survey) repeated the demand.
- **Packages**: the **Prime Minister's package for Bihar (2015)** of ₹1.25 lakh crore; the **Union Budget 2024–25** announced projects for Bihar (expressways, power plant at Pirpainti, flood control including Kosi–Mechi, tourism corridors at Gaya and Rajgir); the **Union Budget 2025–26** announced the **Makhana Board**, a greenfield airport and the Western Kosi canal (check details).

## Industry and investment

### Why industry is weak

- Loss of mineral-based industry to Jharkhand (2000).
- Landlocked, flood-prone, high land prices due to density and small plots.
- Poor power supply in the past (much better after 2015), weak urban infrastructure, low credit–deposit ratio of banks.
- Image problems in the 1990s; skilled workers migrate out.

### Infrastructure

- **Roads**: big growth in rural roads and state highways after 2005; new expressways announced (Patna–Purnia, Gorakhpur–Siliguri, Raxaul–Haldia, Varanasi–Kolkata — check status).
- **Bridges** over the Ganga: Mahatma Gandhi Setu, J. P. Setu, Vikramshila Setu, Munger bridge, new six-lane bridges (Kacchi Dargah–Bidupur, Aunta–Simaria).
- **Airports**: Patna, Gaya (international), **Darbhanga** (UDAN, 2020), Purnia (opened 2025 — check), new greenfield airport announced.
- **Waterway**: NW-1 terminals at Patna (Gaighat) and Kalughat (Saran).
- **Metro**: Patna Metro (first stretch opened 2025 — check).
- **Power**: universal household electrification (Har Ghar Bijli, by 2018), 125 free units from August 2025.

## Migration and remittances

- Bihar is one of India's largest sources of **out-migrant workers** — to Delhi, Punjab, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Kerala and the Gulf.
- **Remittances** (money sent home) support rural consumption and construction.
- The Bhojpuri song tradition of **Bidesiya** and **Purbi** reflects a long history of migration (19th-century indentured labour to Mauritius, Fiji and the Caribbean).
- Issues: distress migration, lack of local jobs, family separation; the **COVID-19 lockdown (2020)** showed the vulnerability of migrant workers.

## Strengths and weaknesses — summary

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| High growth for several years | Lowest per capita income |
| Young, large workforce | Low female labour force participation |
| Fertile land, water, makhana–litchi–maize | Tiny landholdings, floods, drought |
| Improved roads, power, women's SHGs (JEEViKA) | Low industrialisation and urbanisation |
| Strong social sector spending (education is the biggest budget head) | High dependence on central transfers |
| Tourism potential (Buddhist, Jain, Sikh circuits) | Low literacy, high child stunting |

## Key terms

### Mains questions

**1. "Bihar has achieved high growth but not yet high development." Discuss with data. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Quote ES 2025–26 — 8.6% real growth vs India's 6.5%; three years above national growth.
- **Body:** Low base; per capita NSDP only 31.7% of national; output–employment mismatch (54% workers in primary, 18.3% output); low literacy, high stunting (42.9%); MPI fall from 51.89% to 33.76%; district disparities (Patna vs Sheohar); improvements in roads, power, SHGs; fiscal dependence on the Centre.
- **Conclusion:** Sustained growth with human capital, industry and urbanisation can convert growth into development — the aim of Saat Nischay-3.

**2. Examine the case for special category status for Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** A long-standing demand since 2000.
- **Body (for):** loss of resources in 2000; lowest per capita income; floods; high density; low urbanisation; Rajan Committee; caste survey poverty data. **(against / limits):** 14th FC ended the category; NITI–Centre view; higher devolution; packages instead; better use of funds needed.
- **Conclusion:** A special development package plus capacity building and own-revenue growth may be more realistic.

**3. Suggest a strategy to industrialise Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Secondary share has risen to 26.8% but manufacturing is still small.
- **Body:** Agro-processing (makhana, litchi, maize, dairy, fish); ethanol; textiles and leather (labour-intensive); logistics using NW-1 and corridors; IMC Gaya; skill training; land bank; ease of doing business; power reliability; MSME credit; start-ups; return migrants' skills.
- **Conclusion:** Labour-intensive, agro-linked manufacturing is Bihar's comparative advantage.
