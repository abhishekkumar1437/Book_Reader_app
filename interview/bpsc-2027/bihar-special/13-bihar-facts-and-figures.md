This chapter is for **fast revision**. It pulls together the facts BPSC asks again and again — "Which district has the lowest literacy?", "What is the state bird?", "Which is the first state to…?", "Match the place with its district". Read the full chapters first; then use these tables in the last weeks before Prelims and again before the interview (boards ask Bihar facts about your home district).

> **In simple words:** Learn this chapter like a pocket diary of Bihar. Every row is a possible one-line question. Where a figure can change (office holders, new GI tags, new Ramsar sites), it is marked "check latest".

### Mains questions

**1. Analyse the demographic profile of Bihar as per Census 2011 and its development implications. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Third most populous, highest density, lowest literacy.
- **Body:** High density (1,106) and pressure on land; high decadal growth (25.42%) and young population — demographic dividend or burden; low literacy especially female (51.5%); sex ratio 918; low urbanisation (11.3%); district disparities (Patna vs Sheohar/Purnia); TFR decline expected (replacement by ~2039, ES 2025–26); migration.
- **Conclusion:** Investing in girls' education, health and skills can turn Bihar's young population into a dividend.

**2. "Bihar's heritage is its greatest soft power." Discuss with examples. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar is the land of Buddha, Mahavira, Guru Gobind Singh, Ashoka and Nalanda.
- **Body:** UNESCO sites (Mahabodhi, Nalanda); Buddhist circuit and Asian diplomacy (Nalanda University with East Asia Summit support); Jain and Sikh pilgrim sites; folk arts with GI tags; literature and music; tourism economy and jobs; challenges — infrastructure, cleanliness, promotion.
- **Conclusion:** Heritage-led tourism and cultural diplomacy can be a growth engine for Bihar.
