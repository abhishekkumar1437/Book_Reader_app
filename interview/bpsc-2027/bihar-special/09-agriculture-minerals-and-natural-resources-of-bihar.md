Agriculture is the backbone of Bihar: it employs more than half the workforce. This chapter covers what Bihar grows and where, its unique products (makhana, litchi, Magahi paan), its forests and wildlife, and the minerals it still has after Jharkhand separated. Prelims asks product–district pairs, sanctuary–district pairs and Ramsar sites; Mains asks about agricultural development, the Agriculture Roadmap and natural resource management.

> **In simple words:** Bihar has fertile alluvial soil, plenty of water and very small farms. Rice, wheat and maize are the main crops. Bihar leads India in makhana and litchi. Forests cover only about 8% of the state, mostly in West Champaran and the southern hills. Most minerals went to Jharkhand in 2000; Bihar still has limestone, pyrite, mica and a large gold deposit in Jamui.

All-India background: [Agriculture and Irrigation](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/agriculture-and-irrigation), [Minerals, Energy, Industries and Transport](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/minerals-energy-industries-and-transport), [Agriculture and Food Security](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/agriculture-and-food-security).

**Cropping intensity** = gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100. A value of about 152% means that on average each field is cropped about one and a half times a year.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar **repealed its APMC Act in 2006** — the first state to abolish the regulated agricultural market (mandi) system. Supporters said it would free farmers; critics say it left farmers without proper markets and price discovery. This is a frequent Mains discussion point, especially after the national farm-law debate of 2020–21.

### Bihar's star products

**Makhana (fox nut, *Euryale ferox*)**

- An aquatic crop grown in ponds, chaurs and wetlands; the seeds are popped.
- Main districts: **Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia, Katihar, Saharsa, Supaul, Araria, Kishanganj**.
- Bihar produces the **bulk of India's makhana** — a PIB document (2026) says nearly **three-fourths**, and a Parliament reply put it around **85%** (check the latest figure). India produces most of the world's makhana.
- **Mithila Makhana** received a **GI tag in 2022**.
- **National Makhana Board**: announced in the **Union Budget 2025–26**; launched by the Prime Minister at **Purnia** in **September 2025**.
- Research: the **ICAR National Research Centre for Makhana** at **Darbhanga** (now part of the ICAR Research Complex for Eastern Region).

**Litchi**

- Bihar is **India's largest litchi producer** (over 40% of national output by National Horticulture Board data — check latest).
- **Shahi litchi** of **Muzaffarpur** (also Samastipur, Vaishali, East Champaran, Sitamarhi) — **GI tag 2018**.
- **ICAR National Research Centre on Litchi**, **Muzaffarpur** (est. 2001).

**Mango**

- Varieties: **Zardalu** (Bhagalpur, GI 2018), **Malda/Dudhiya Malda** (Patna – Digha), **Bombay, Gulabkhas, Kishanbhog, Bathua**.
- **Banana**: **Hajipur (Vaishali)** is famous; **guava** from Hajipur and Bettiah.

### Agriculture Roadmaps

Bihar plans agriculture through five-year **Agriculture Roadmaps (Krishi Road Map)** that combine many departments (agriculture, animal husbandry, fisheries, cooperatives, water, energy, forests, food processing).

| Roadmap | Period | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | 2008–12 | Seeds, mechanisation, SRI paddy, food grain production |
| **Second** | 2012–17 | "**Rainbow revolution**" — foodgrains, horticulture, dairy, fisheries, organic farming |
| **Third** | 2017–22 (extended) | Organic corridor along the Ganga, crop diversification, climate-resilient agriculture |
| **Fourth** | **2023–28**; launched **18 October 2023** at Patna by **President Droupadi Murmu** | Crop diversification, millets, horticulture, makhana, dairy and fish, food processing, climate resilience, farm income |

**Krishi Karman awards**: Bihar received national awards for record rice (2011–12) and wheat production in the early 2010s.

> **Mains angle:** Use the Agriculture Roadmap as the "Bihar angle" in any answer on farm income, food processing or climate-smart farming: mention SRI paddy, zero-tillage wheat, seed replacement, Jaivik (organic) corridor, DBT input subsidies, and the target in Saat Nischay-3 of raising farm incomes.

## Forests of Bihar

- Bihar's **forest cover is about 7,500 km² — roughly 8% of its area** (India State of Forest Report 2023; check the exact figure). The national average is about **21.8%**.
- Bihar lost most of its forests to Jharkhand in 2000.
- Forest cover rose by about **689 km² over 12 years** (Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26).
- Targets: the **Hariyali Mission** (2012) and **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan** (2019) aim to raise **green (tree + forest) cover to about 17%** through mass plantation (check the current target).

| Forest type | Where | Main trees |
|---|---|---|
| **Tropical moist deciduous** | **Valmiki area (West Champaran)**, Terai, parts of Kishanganj | **Sal**, shisham, khair, semal, bamboo |
| **Tropical dry deciduous** | **Southern hills** — Kaimur, Rohtas, Gaya, Nawada, Jamui, Banka, Munger | Sal, **mahua**, palash, kendu (tendu), bamboo, khair |
| Riverine / grassland | Diara lands, Valmiki grasslands | Tall grasses, tamarisk |

**Districts with most forest:** **West Champaran, Kaimur, Rohtas, Jamui, Gaya, Banka, Munger, Nawada**. Most districts of the north and central plain have very little forest.

## Wildlife: national park, tiger reserve and sanctuaries

### Valmiki — Bihar's only national park and tiger reserve

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Location | **West Champaran**, on the **Nepal border**; continuous with **Chitwan National Park (Nepal)** and close to UP's Sohagi Barwa |
| Status | **Only national park** of Bihar; **Valmiki Tiger Reserve (VTR)** — India's **18th tiger reserve** (sanctuary 1978; national park c. 1989–90; added to Project Tiger in the early 1990s — dates differ in sources) |
| Area | About **900 km²** for the tiger reserve (core includes the national park) |
| Tigers | **54** in the All India Tiger Estimation **2022** (up from 31 in 2018); check the 2026 estimation results |
| Habitat | Sal forest, grasslands, Gandak riverine forest; Shivalik hills |
| Animals | Tiger, leopard, sloth bear, Indian bison (gaur), sambar, chital, one-horned rhino (occasional, from Chitwan), hornbills |

> **Common mistake:** Valmiki is a national park, wildlife sanctuary and tiger reserve — **not** a biosphere reserve. **Bihar has no biosphere reserve** (as of 2026 — check the latest).

### Kaimur — the proposed second tiger reserve

- **Kaimur Wildlife Sanctuary** (Kaimur and Rohtas) is **Bihar's largest sanctuary**.
- The **NTCA** gave **in-principle approval** (October 2024) to develop it as a tiger reserve; the **state cabinet approved** the proposal; **final NTCA notification was awaited** in early 2026 — check the latest.
- Habitat: dry deciduous forest on the **Vindhyan Kaimur plateau**, with waterfalls and Shergarh fort; it links with forests of Madhya Pradesh and UP.

Other wildlife facts:

- **Gangetic dolphin** (Platanista gangetica, locally "**soons**") — national aquatic animal (2009). The **National Dolphin Research Centre** has been set up at **Patna** (opened 2024 — check). Bihar has the largest share of India's Gangetic dolphins.
- **Greater adjutant stork** ("garuda") breeds in the **Kadwa diara of Bhagalpur** — one of very few breeding sites in the world (others in Assam and Cambodia).
- **Sanjay Gandhi Biological Park**, Patna (1973), and the **Rajgir Zoo Safari** and **Nature Safari** (with a glass bridge) at Rajgir.

## Ramsar sites of Bihar

A **Ramsar site** is a wetland of international importance listed under the **Ramsar Convention (1971, Ramsar, Iran)**. Bihar had **six** Ramsar sites by the end of 2025 (as of 2026 — check for new additions).

| # | Ramsar site | District | Year designated | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kanwar Jheel (Kabartal)** | **Begusarai** | **2020** | Oxbow lake (maun) |
| 2 | **Nagi Bird Sanctuary** | **Jamui** | **2024** (announced 5 June 2024; Ramsar database date differs) | Man-made reservoir |
| 3 | **Nakti Bird Sanctuary** | **Jamui** | **2024** | Man-made reservoir |
| 4 | **Gokul Jalashay** | **Buxar** | **2025** (September) | Oxbow lake on the south bank of the Ganga; about 448 ha |
| 5 | **Udaipur Jheel** | **West Champaran** | **2025** (September) | Oxbow lake of the Gandak system; about 319 ha |
| 6 | **Gogabeel** | **Katihar** | **2025** (announced early November) | Oxbow lake between the Ganga and Mahananda; India's **94th** Ramsar site |

Sources: MoEFCC announcements reported by Drishti IAS (29 September 2025; 4 November 2025), India Water Portal and Ramsar Sites Information Service. India's total has kept growing (counts in 2026 sources vary from about 94 to 100+) — check the latest number.

> **Memory trick:** "**K-N-N-G-U-G**" — **K**anwar (2020); **N**agi, **N**akti (2024, both Jamui); **G**okul, **U**daipur, **G**ogabeel (2025). Three are **oxbow lakes from 2025**, two are **Jamui dams from 2024**, one is **Begusarai's maun from 2020**.

More on wetland conventions: [Environment Conventions and Protected Areas](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/environment-conventions-and-protected-areas).

## Minerals of Bihar after 2000

When Jharkhand was created, about **90% of undivided Bihar's mineral wealth** went with it (coal, iron ore, copper, bauxite, uranium, most mica). What Bihar still has:

| Mineral | Where | Use / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Limestone** | **Rohtas, Kaimur** (Banjari, Kalyanpur area) | Cement; the **Kalyanpur Lime** works; Vindhyan limestone |
| **Pyrite** | **Amjhore (Rohtas)** | Sulphur and sulphuric acid; the **Pyrites, Phosphates and Chemicals Ltd (PPCL)** unit (now closed) |
| **Potash / glauconite** | Rohtas, Kaimur | Fertiliser potential; blocks offered for auction recently |
| **Mica** | **Nawada, Jamui, Gaya** | Edge of the Koderma mica belt |
| **Gold** | **Jamui** (Sono – **Karmatiya** area) | A Union Mines Ministry reply in the Lok Sabha (2021, citing the National Mineral Inventory of 1 April 2015) said Bihar has about **222.885 million tonnes of gold ore (about 44% of India's)** containing about **37.6 tonnes** of gold metal; Bihar allowed exploration in Jamui in 2022. (Media reports attributing a Jamui survey to GSI were denied by GSI — note the figure is **ore**, not metal.) Check status |
| **Quartz, silica sand** | Munger, Jamui, Bhagalpur | Glass |
| **China clay (kaolin)** | Bhagalpur, Munger | Ceramics |
| **Steatite (soapstone)** | Munger | — |
| **Saltpetre** | Saran, Muzaffarpur, Vaishali, Champaran | Historically exported (gunpowder) |
| **Stone and granite** | Gaya, Sheikhpura, Jamui, Nawada, Banka | Construction; stone crushers |
| **Coal** | **Pirpainti–Barahat (Bhagalpur)** area | Small reserves; a new **thermal power project at Pirpainti** was announced in 2025 (check) |
| **Petroleum and gas** | Ganga basin (Buxar, Patna area) | ONGC exploration; no commercial production yet (check) |
| **Sand (river sand)** | Son, Phalgu, Kiul and others | Major revenue source; illegal sand mining is a problem |

> **Prelims angle:** Gold — **Jamui (Karmatiya/Sono)**; Pyrite — **Amjhore, Rohtas**; Limestone — **Rohtas–Kaimur**; Mica — **Nawada–Jamui–Gaya**. Bihar is the only state with significant pyrite reserves at Amjhore.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Kharif / Rabi / Zaid | Monsoon / winter / summer crop seasons |
| Cropping intensity | How many times, on average, land is cropped in a year |
| APMC | Agricultural Produce Market Committee — the regulated mandi system |
| SRI | System of Rice Intensification — fewer seeds, less water, more yield |
| Zero tillage | Sowing wheat without ploughing after rice, saving time and cost |
| GI tag | Legal label that a product comes from a specific place |
| Tiger reserve | Protected area under Project Tiger with a core and buffer |
| Community reserve | Protected area managed with local people on community/private land |
| Ramsar site | Wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention |
| Oxbow lake | Curved lake left when a river changes course |
| Glauconite | A green mineral containing potash |

### Mains questions

**1. "Bihar's agriculture has high potential but low productivity." Analyse the causes and suggest measures. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Fertile soil, abundant water, 54% of workers — yet low farm incomes.
- **Body (causes):** tiny holdings (~0.39 ha); floods and droughts; weak irrigation in the south; low seed replacement and mechanisation; poor markets after APMC repeal; weak credit; low processing; land record problems; migration of young men.
- **Body (measures):** Agriculture Roadmap; crop diversification (maize, pulses, vegetables, makhana, litchi); FPOs; cold chains and food parks; DBT for inputs; Kisan Samman top-ups; crop insurance (Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata Yojana); market yards and e-NAM; climate-resilient varieties.
- **Conclusion:** Moving from food security to income security needs markets and processing, not only production.

**2. Discuss the importance of wetlands in Bihar and the steps taken for their conservation. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Chaurs, mauns and tals cover large parts of north Bihar.
- **Body:** Ecological roles — flood buffer, groundwater recharge, migratory birds (Central Asian Flyway), fisheries, makhana; six Ramsar sites; threats — encroachment, siltation, pesticides, illegal fishing; steps — Bihar State Wetland Authority, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali, Amrit Dharohar, community reserves (Gogabeel), eco-tourism.
- **Conclusion:** Wetlands are Bihar's natural flood insurance and livelihood base.

**3. How did the bifurcation of 2000 change Bihar's resource base? What new opportunities exist? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar lost most minerals, forests and industries to Jharkhand.
- **Body:** Losses; remaining resources — limestone, pyrite, mica, gold (Jamui), sand, water, fertile soil, human resources; opportunities — agro-processing (makhana, litchi, maize ethanol), fisheries, tourism, renewable energy, mineral auction under MMDR reforms, logistics on NW-1.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's future lies in agro-based industry, services and human capital rather than heavy mining.
