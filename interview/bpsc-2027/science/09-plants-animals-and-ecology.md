This chapter completes biology: how plants make food and grow, which plant hormone does what, how animals are grouped (with the "odd-one-out" facts BPSC loves — bat is a mammal, whale is a mammal, starfish is not a fish), and ecology — food chains, energy pyramids, nutrient cycles and biodiversity. Ecology overlaps with geography and environment; here we focus on the science, with Bihar's tiger reserve, dolphin sanctuary and wetlands.

> **In simple words:** Plants catch sunlight and turn it into food — every other living thing depends on that. Animals are grouped by their body plan, from simple sponges to mammals. In nature, energy flows in one direction (sun → plants → animals) and gets smaller at every step, while materials like carbon and nitrogen go round and round in cycles.

## Plant structure and functions

| Part | Main function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Root** | Absorbs water and minerals, anchors the plant | **Modified roots store food**: **carrot, radish, beetroot, turnip, sweet potato**; **pneumatophores** (breathing roots) in mangroves; prop roots in **banyan**; root nodules in legumes fix nitrogen |
| **Stem** | Supports, transports water and food | **Modified stems**: **potato (tuber — has "eyes" or buds)**, **ginger, turmeric** (rhizome), **onion, garlic** (bulb), **sugarcane**, colocasia (corm), cactus (green, photosynthetic stem) |
| **Leaf** | **Photosynthesis**, transpiration, gas exchange through **stomata** | Modified leaves: tendrils (pea), spines (cactus), pitcher (insect-eating plants) |
| **Flower** | Reproduction | Stamen (male — anther, pollen); pistil/carpel (female — stigma, style, ovary) |
| **Fruit** | Protects seeds; helps dispersal | Develops from the **ovary**; seeds develop from **ovules** |

- **Xylem** carries **water and minerals upward** (one-way) from roots; **phloem** carries **food (sugar)** from leaves to all parts (both ways). Age of a tree is found by counting **annual rings** (xylem rings) — **dendrochronology**.
- **Transpiration**: loss of water vapour through **stomata**; it creates the "pull" that lifts water up tall trees and cools the plant. **Guttation**: water drops at leaf edges in the morning (grass).
- **Insectivorous plants** (e.g. **pitcher plant** *Nepenthes*, **Venus flytrap**, sundew, bladderwort) grow in **nitrogen-poor** soils and trap insects to get nitrogen.
- **Parasitic plants**: **Cuscuta** (amarbel — no chlorophyll), mistletoe (partial).
- Some odd facts: **coconut** is the largest seed (Lodoicea, "double coconut", is the largest in the world); **Rafflesia** has the largest single flower; **Wolffia** is the smallest flowering plant; **Sequoia** (redwood) is among the tallest trees; **eucalyptus** is called an "ecological terrorist" because it uses a lot of groundwater.

## Photosynthesis

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O + sunlight → (chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + 6O₂**

- Takes place in **chloroplasts**, using the green pigment **chlorophyll** (contains **magnesium**). Other pigments: carotenoids (yellow-orange), xanthophylls.
- Two stages: **light reaction** (in grana/thylakoids — water is split, **oxygen is released**, ATP and NADPH are made) and **dark reaction / Calvin cycle** (in stroma — CO₂ is fixed into sugar; does not need light directly).
- **The oxygen released comes from water**, not from CO₂ (proved with isotope studies by **Ruben and Kamen**).
- Most photosynthesis happens in **red and blue light**; least in **green** (which is reflected — that is why leaves look green).
- **C₃ plants** (rice, wheat, most plants) vs **C₄ plants** (**maize, sugarcane, sorghum** — more efficient in hot, bright conditions) vs **CAM plants** (cactus, pineapple — open stomata at night to save water).
- Photosynthesis is an **endothermic** process (stores energy); respiration is the reverse and releases energy.
- Scientists: **Jan Ingenhousz** (light is needed), **Joseph Priestley** (plants "restore" air), **Melvin Calvin** (Calvin cycle, Nobel 1961).
- About **half of Earth's oxygen** is produced by **marine phytoplankton**, not by forests.

## Plant hormones

| Hormone | Main role | Uses / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Auxin** (IAA) | **Cell elongation**, growth towards light (**phototropism**), root formation | **Rooting of cuttings**; synthetic auxin **2,4-D** is a **weedicide**; first plant hormone discovered (Darwin's observations; **F. W. Went** isolated it) |
| **Gibberellin** | **Stem elongation**, breaks seed dormancy | **Increases fruit size** (grapes), malting of barley, makes sugarcane taller |
| **Cytokinin** | **Cell division**, delays ageing of leaves | Keeps flowers and vegetables fresh |
| **Abscisic acid (ABA)** | **Stress hormone**; **closes stomata**, causes dormancy and **leaf fall** | "Dormin" |
| **Ethylene** (gas) | **Fruit ripening** | Only **gaseous** plant hormone; **ethephon** releases ethylene (safe ripening) — unlike harmful **calcium carbide** |
| Florigen | Flowering | Hypothetical/flowering signal |

**Plant movements**: **phototropism** (towards light — stem), **geotropism** (towards gravity — root), **hydrotropism** (towards water — root), **thigmotropism** (touch — tendrils), and **nastic movements** like the folding of **touch-me-not (*Mimosa pudica*, lajwanti)** leaves.

**Photoperiodism**: response of plants to day length — short-day plants (rice, soybean, tobacco), long-day plants (wheat, radish), day-neutral (tomato, maize).

> **Bihar connection:** **J. C. Bose** showed with his **crescograph** that plants respond to stimuli and have something like feelings — a well-loved Indian science story.

## Plant reproduction

- **Asexual / vegetative propagation**: by stems (potato, sugarcane, rose cuttings), roots (sweet potato, dahlia), leaves (**Bryophyllum** — buds on leaf margins). Artificial methods: **cutting, layering, grafting** (mango, citrus, rose), **tissue culture** (micropropagation — banana, orchids, potato; thousands of identical plants from a tiny piece of tissue). Bihar has tissue-culture labs for banana and potato.
- **Sexual reproduction** in flowering plants: **pollination** (transfer of pollen to stigma) → **fertilisation**. Flowering plants show **double fertilisation** (one sperm + egg → embryo; another sperm + polar nuclei → **endosperm**, the food store).
- **Self-pollination** (same flower/plant — pea, wheat, rice) and **cross-pollination** by **wind** (maize, grasses — light, abundant pollen), **insects** (bright, scented flowers), water (Vallisneria), birds, bats.
- **Honeybees** are the most important pollinators; their decline threatens crops. India's **National Beekeeping and Honey Mission** (Sweet Revolution) promotes beekeeping — **Muzaffarpur's litchi honey** is well known.
- **Parthenocarpy**: fruit without fertilisation — **seedless** banana, grapes, watermelon.
- **Apomixis**: seeds without fertilisation.
- Seed dispersal: **wind** (cotton, dandelion, drumstick — winged), **water** (coconut, lotus), **animals** (burrs, fleshy fruits), **explosion** (balsam, pea pods).

## Ecology and ecosystems

**Ecology** is the study of relationships between living things and their environment. The term was coined by **Ernst Haeckel (1866)**. The **ecosystem** concept was given by **A. G. Tansley (1935)**. **Ramdeo Misra** is called the **father of Indian ecology**.

An **ecosystem** has:
- **Biotic** (living) components: **producers** (green plants, phytoplankton — **autotrophs**), **consumers** (herbivores = primary, carnivores = secondary/tertiary, omnivores), **decomposers** (bacteria and fungi — break down dead matter and return nutrients).
- **Abiotic** (non-living) components: sunlight, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals.

- **Food chain**: a single line of "who eats whom". **Grazing food chain** starts with green plants; **detritus food chain** starts with dead matter.
- **Food web**: many interconnected food chains — more stable than a single chain.
- **Trophic levels**: producer (T1) → herbivore (T2) → carnivore (T3)…
- **Energy flow is one-way** (unidirectional) — it cannot be recycled; it is lost as heat at each level. **Lindeman's 10% law (1942)**: only about **10%** of energy passes to the next trophic level.
- **Ecological pyramids**: of **numbers**, **biomass** and **energy**. The **pyramid of energy is always upright**. The pyramid of numbers can be **inverted** (one tree supporting many insects); the pyramid of biomass is **inverted in the sea** (small phytoplankton biomass supports larger zooplankton/fish).
- **Biomagnification**: non-biodegradable chemicals (**DDT**, mercury, lead) become **more concentrated** at higher levels — fish-eating birds' **eggshells became thin** due to DDT (described in **Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring*, 1962**).
- **Bioaccumulation**: build-up of such chemicals inside one organism over time.

### Interactions between species

| Interaction | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutualism** | Both benefit (+/+) | **Lichens** (alga + fungus), *Rhizobium* in legume roots, bees and flowers, **mycorrhiza** (fungus + plant roots) |
| **Commensalism** | One benefits, other unaffected (+/0) | Orchid (epiphyte) on a mango tree, cattle egret with cattle, barnacles on a whale |
| **Parasitism** | One benefits, other harmed (+/−) | Cuscuta on plants, lice, tapeworm, *Plasmodium* |
| **Predation** | One eats the other (+/−) | Tiger and deer |
| **Competition** | Both harmed (−/−) | Weeds and crops |
| **Amensalism** | One harmed, other unaffected (−/0) | *Penicillium* inhibits bacteria |

## Nutrient (biogeochemical) cycles

| Cycle | Key steps |
|---|---|
| **Carbon cycle** | Photosynthesis takes CO₂ in; respiration, decomposition, **burning of fossil fuels** and forest fires release it; oceans are a huge **carbon sink**; extra CO₂ → **global warming** |
| **Nitrogen cycle** | Air is ~78% N₂ but plants cannot use it directly. **Nitrogen fixation** by **lightning** and by bacteria (***Rhizobium*** in root nodules of **legumes** — pulses, groundnut; ***Azotobacter***, **blue-green algae** like *Anabaena*, *Nostoc* in paddy fields) → **ammonification** (decomposers) → **nitrification** (*Nitrosomonas* → nitrite; *Nitrobacter* → nitrate) → plants absorb nitrates → **denitrification** (*Pseudomonas*) returns N₂ to air |
| Water cycle | Evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, run-off, infiltration |
| Phosphorus cycle | **Sedimentary** cycle (no gaseous phase) — from rocks to soil to organisms |
| Oxygen cycle | Photosynthesis releases it; respiration and burning use it |

> **Prelims angle:** "Why do farmers grow pulses between cereal crops?" — **legumes fix nitrogen** through *Rhizobium*, restoring soil fertility (**crop rotation**). "Which organism is used as a biofertiliser in paddy fields?" — **blue-green algae / Azolla** (a water fern that hosts *Anabaena*).

## Biomes and ecological succession

- **Biomes** — large regions with similar climate and vegetation: tropical rainforest (most biodiversity), savanna, desert, temperate grassland, deciduous forest, taiga (coniferous), tundra. Details in [The Universe and the Earth](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/the-universe-and-the-earth) and the geography book.
- **Ecological succession**: gradual change of communities in an area — **pioneer species** (lichens on bare rock, phytoplankton in water) → intermediate stages → stable **climax community** (e.g. forest).
- **Ecotone**: transition zone between two ecosystems (e.g. mangroves between land and sea; wetlands); often richer in species (**edge effect**).
- **Ecological niche**: the role and "profession" of a species in its habitat.
- **Keystone species**: has a large effect on its ecosystem relative to its numbers (tiger, elephant, sea otter, fig trees). **Flagship species**: popular species used for conservation campaigns (tiger, panda, **Gangetic dolphin**). **Indicator species**: show environmental health (**lichens** for SO₂, frogs, mayflies, *E. coli* in water for faecal contamination).

## Biodiversity and conservation

**Biodiversity** = variety of life at three levels: **genetic, species and ecosystem** diversity. The term was popularised by **E. O. Wilson**. **India is a megadiverse country** with about **7–8% of recorded species** on about **2.4% of world land area**. India has **4 of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots**: **Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar Islands)**. Hotspot concept: **Norman Myers (1988)**.

### Threats ("HIPPO")

**H**abitat loss, **I**nvasive species (water hyacinth — "terror of Bengal", *Lantana*, *Parthenium* — congress grass, African catfish), **P**ollution, **P**opulation (human), **O**ver-exploitation (poaching, overfishing). Plus **climate change**.

### Conservation methods

| In-situ (on-site) | Ex-situ (off-site) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's key wildlife sites — **Valmiki Tiger Reserve** (West Champaran; Bihar's **first tiger reserve and only national park**, bordering Nepal's Chitwan; part of the **Terai Arc Landscape**), **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary** (Bhagalpur), **Kaimur Wildlife Sanctuary** (largest sanctuary in Bihar; being developed as Bihar's **second tiger reserve** — status as of 2026, check latest), **Bhimbandh** (Munger), **Gautam Buddha Sanctuary** (Gaya), **Pant (Rajgir)**, **Udaipur** (West Champaran), **Kanwar Jheel/Kabartal** (Begusarai — Bihar's **first Ramsar site, 2020**), **Nagi and Nakti dams** (Jamui — Ramsar sites, 2024), plus more Ramsar wetlands added later (check the latest count). Kanwar is an **oxbow lake** of the Burhi Gandak and a major winter bird habitat.

## Pollution and environment (science basics)

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **CO₂, CH₄, N₂O**, CFCs/HFCs | Fossil fuels, paddy fields, cattle, fertilisers, ACs | **Greenhouse effect** → global warming; methane is ~28 times stronger than CO₂ over 100 years |
| CFCs, halons | Old fridges, ACs, fire extinguishers | **Ozone depletion** (ozone hole over **Antarctica**); Montreal Protocol (1987) |
| SO₂, NOx | Coal plants, vehicles | **Acid rain**, smog |
| **PM2.5 / PM10** | Dust, vehicles, stubble burning, brick kilns, biomass | Lung/heart disease; measured in **AQI** |
| Ground-level ozone | Formed from NOx + VOCs in sunlight | **Photochemical smog** |
| Sewage, fertiliser run-off | Cities, farms | **Eutrophication** — algal bloom, low oxygen, fish death; high **BOD** (biological oxygen demand) means polluted water |
| Plastics, microplastics | Single-use plastic | Choke drains and animals, enter food chain |
| Noise | Traffic, loudspeakers | Measured in decibels; hearing loss, stress |
| E-waste | Old phones, computers | Lead, mercury, cadmium; **E-Waste Management Rules** |

**BOD**: amount of oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter in water; **clean water has low BOD**. The **Ganga** is cleaned under **Namami Gange** (2014). The **ozone layer** is in the **stratosphere** (about 15–35 km); ozone is measured in **Dobson units**. **World Environment Day: 5 June**; **Ozone Day: 16 September**; **Earth Day: 22 April**; **Biodiversity Day: 22 May**; **Wetlands Day: 2 February**; **Wildlife Day: 3 March**.

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the ecological and economic importance of wetlands in Bihar. What measures are needed for their conservation?** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar's floodplains have thousands of wetlands (chaurs, mauns, oxbow lakes); Kanwar Jheel and other Ramsar sites.
- **Ecological value:** flood buffering, groundwater recharge, migratory bird habitat (Central Asian Flyway), fish breeding, carbon storage.
- **Economic value:** makhana and fisheries (livelihood for Mallah/Sahni communities), water chestnut, fodder, eco-tourism.
- **Threats:** encroachment for agriculture, siltation, fertiliser run-off (eutrophication), invasive water hyacinth, drainage, climate change.
- **Measures:** Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017, State Wetland Authority, Amrit Dharohar scheme, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali (renovation of ponds and ahars-pynes), GIS mapping of wetlands, community management, scientific makhana farming.
- **Conclusion:** wetlands are Bihar's natural infrastructure; conserving them supports both ecology and the rural economy.

**2. "The Gangetic dolphin is an indicator of the health of the Ganga." Explain, with reference to threats and conservation efforts.** (about 250 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** national aquatic animal (2009), endangered (IUCN), nearly blind, echolocation; Vikramshila sanctuary in Bihar.
- **Indicator role:** needs deep, clean, flowing water and fish — its presence shows a healthy river.
- **Threats:** pollution, low flows due to dams and barrages (Farakka), sand mining, accidental entanglement in fishing nets, oil-based poaching (historically), river traffic.
- **Conservation:** Project Dolphin (announced 2020), Namami Gange, dolphin surveys (first all-India estimate released around 2025 — check latest), National Dolphin Research Centre in Patna, community "dolphin mitras".
- **Conclusion:** saving the dolphin means saving the river and the livelihoods it supports.
