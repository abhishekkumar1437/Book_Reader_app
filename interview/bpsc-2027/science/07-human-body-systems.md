Human body questions are the most frequent biology questions in state PSC Prelims: "largest gland", "universal donor", "where is bile made", "which hormone controls blood sugar", "how many bones in an adult", "normal blood pressure". This chapter goes system by system, with tables for quick learning and a heart diagram. Diseases, nutrition and vaccines are in the next chapter.

> **In simple words:** The body is a team of systems. The digestive system breaks food into small pieces; the lungs bring in oxygen; the heart pumps blood to carry food and oxygen everywhere; the kidneys clean the blood; nerves and hormones send messages; bones and muscles give shape and movement; the immune system fights germs.

## Levels of organisation

**Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.**

Animal tissues: **epithelial** (covering — skin, lining of gut), **connective** (bone, cartilage, blood, fat, tendons and ligaments), **muscular** (movement), **nervous** (messages). **Blood is a fluid connective tissue.** Tendons join **muscle to bone**; ligaments join **bone to bone**.

## Digestive system

**Path of food**: mouth → pharynx → **oesophagus** (food pipe) → **stomach** → **small intestine** (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → **large intestine** (caecum, colon, rectum) → anus. Food moves by wave-like muscle contractions called **peristalsis**. The total length of the alimentary canal is about **8–9 metres**; the **small intestine is about 6–7 m** long and the large intestine about 1.5 m.

### Digestion step by step

| Part | Juice / enzyme | Acts on → product |
|---|---|---|
| **Mouth** | Saliva: **salivary amylase (ptyalin)** | Starch → maltose (digestion of **carbohydrates starts in the mouth**) |
| **Stomach** | Gastric juice: **HCl** (kills germs, makes medium acidic, activates pepsin), **pepsin**, **rennin** (in infants, curdles milk), mucus (protects stomach wall) | Proteins → peptides (**protein digestion starts in the stomach**) |
| **Duodenum** | **Bile** from **liver** (stored in gall bladder) — **no enzymes**; **emulsifies fats** and makes medium alkaline | Large fat drops → tiny droplets |
| | **Pancreatic juice**: **trypsin** (proteins), **amylase** (starch), **lipase** (fats) | Proteins → peptides; starch → maltose; fats → fatty acids + glycerol |
| **Small intestine** | Intestinal juice (**succus entericus**): maltase, sucrase, lactase, peptidases, lipase | Final products: **glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol** |
| **Small intestine (ileum)** | **Villi** (finger-like folds) greatly increase surface area | **Absorption** of digested food into blood and lymph |
| **Large intestine** | — | **Absorption of water** and salts; forms faeces; bacteria here make **vitamin K** and some B vitamins |

- **Fat digestion starts and ends mainly in the small intestine.**
- **Liver** — the **largest gland** (and largest internal organ), ~1.5 kg. Functions: makes **bile**, stores **glycogen**, iron and vitamins A, D, B₁₂, **detoxifies** alcohol and drugs, makes urea, makes plasma proteins and clotting factors. The liver can **regenerate**. **Jaundice** (yellow skin and eyes) shows high **bilirubin** — often due to liver infection (hepatitis) or blocked bile duct.
- **Pancreas** is both an **exocrine** gland (digestive juice) and an **endocrine** gland (insulin, glucagon) — a **mixed gland**.
- **Appendix** is a vestigial organ attached to the caecum; inflammation = appendicitis.
- **Teeth**: adults have **32 permanent teeth** (incisors 8, canines 4, premolars 8, molars 12). Children have **20 milk teeth**. Dental formula (half jaw): **2-1-2-3**. The hardest substance in the body is **tooth enamel**. Humans have two sets of teeth in life (**diphyodont**).
- **Ruminants** (cow, buffalo, goat) have a four-chambered stomach (rumen is the largest part) and digest **cellulose** with the help of bacteria. Humans cannot digest cellulose, but it is useful as **fibre (roughage)**.

## Respiratory system

**Path of air**: nose → pharynx → **larynx** (voice box — "Adam's apple" in men) → **trachea** (windpipe, held open by C-shaped cartilage rings) → **bronchi** → bronchioles → **alveoli** (tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens; about **30 crore — 300 million — in both lungs**).

- The **epiglottis** is a flap that closes the windpipe while swallowing, so food goes into the food pipe.
- **Diaphragm** (dome-shaped muscle below the lungs) and rib muscles cause breathing. Inhaling: diaphragm **contracts and flattens**, chest expands.
- Normal breathing rate: **12–20 times per minute** in adults (often quoted 15–18). Newborns breathe faster.
- **Inhaled air**: ~21% oxygen, 0.04% CO₂. **Exhaled air**: ~16% oxygen, ~4% CO₂.
- **Oxygen is carried by haemoglobin** in RBCs (as oxyhaemoglobin). CO₂ is carried mostly as **bicarbonate** in plasma.
- **Vital capacity** — maximum air you can breathe out after the deepest breath — about 3.5–4.5 litres; higher in athletes.
- The right lung has **3 lobes**, the left lung has **2** (space for the heart).

### Respiration (cell level)

| Aerobic respiration | Anaerobic respiration |
|---|---|
| Uses oxygen | Without oxygen |
| Glucose → CO₂ + water + **lots of energy (≈ 36–38 ATP)** | Glucose → **lactic acid** (in muscles) or **ethanol + CO₂** (in **yeast**) + little energy (**2 ATP**) |
| In **mitochondria** | In cytoplasm |
| Most living things | **Muscle cramps** after heavy exercise; **fermentation** for bread, idli, dosa, alcohol |

## Circulatory system

The circulatory system was explained by **William Harvey (1628)**. Humans have a **closed circulatory system** with **double circulation** — blood passes through the heart **twice** in one complete round (once to the lungs, once to the body).

### The heart

- Size of a **fist**, weighs about **300 g**, lies in the chest slightly to the **left**, protected by a double membrane called the **pericardium**.
- Human heart has **4 chambers**: 2 upper **atria** (receive blood) and 2 lower **ventricles** (pump blood). The **left ventricle has the thickest wall** because it pumps blood to the whole body.
- **Fish**: 2 chambers; **amphibians** (frog) and most **reptiles**: 3 chambers; **crocodile, birds and mammals**: 4 chambers.
- Valves stop backflow: **tricuspid** (between right atrium and right ventricle), **bicuspid or mitral** (left atrium – left ventricle), **semilunar valves** (at the bases of aorta and pulmonary artery). The "**lub-dub**" sound is the closing of valves.

> **Prelims angle:** Two exceptions are asked every year: the **pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood** (all other arteries carry oxygenated blood) and the **pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood** (all other veins carry deoxygenated blood).

- **Arteries** carry blood **away from** the heart (thick, elastic walls, high pressure). The **aorta** is the largest artery. **Veins** carry blood **to** the heart (thin walls, valves). **Capillaries** are tiny vessels where exchange with tissues happens.
- **Pacemaker**: the **SA node** (sino-atrial node) in the right atrium starts each heartbeat. An artificial pacemaker is fitted when it fails.
- **Heart rate**: about **72 beats per minute** (60–100 normal); one beat ≈ 0.8 s. The heart pumps about **5 litres per minute** at rest.
- **Blood pressure** (measured by a **sphygmomanometer**, invented by Riva-Rocci): normal about **120/80 mm Hg** (systolic/diastolic). High BP = **hypertension**, the "silent killer".
- **Coronary arteries** supply the heart muscle itself; their blockage (cholesterol plaque) causes a **heart attack (myocardial infarction)**. Treatments: **angioplasty and stents**, bypass surgery. **ECG** records the heart's electrical activity (Willem Einthoven). The first human heart transplant was done by **Christiaan Barnard (South Africa, 1967)**; in India, by **Dr P. Venugopal at AIIMS Delhi (1994)**.

### Blood

An adult has about **5–6 litres** of blood (about **7–8% of body weight**). Blood is slightly alkaline (**pH ≈ 7.4**).

| Component | Share / count | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plasma** | ~55% of blood; ~90% water | Carries nutrients, hormones, CO₂, proteins (albumin, globulin, fibrinogen) | Straw-coloured |
| **RBCs (erythrocytes)** | ~4.5–5.5 million per mm³ (men higher) | Carry **oxygen** using **haemoglobin** (iron-containing) | **No nucleus** in mammals (more room for haemoglobin); life ~**120 days**; made in **red bone marrow**; destroyed in **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs") and liver |
| **WBCs (leucocytes)** | ~5,000–10,000 per mm³ | **Defence** against germs | Neutrophils (most common; engulf germs), lymphocytes (antibodies), monocytes, eosinophils (allergy), basophils; **leukaemia** = blood cancer with abnormal WBCs |
| **Platelets (thrombocytes)** | ~1.5–4.5 lakh per mm³ | **Blood clotting** | Life ~7–10 days; their count falls sharply in **dengue** |

- **Clotting** needs **calcium**, **vitamin K**, platelets and fibrinogen → fibrin threads. **Heparin** (anticoagulant) prevents clotting inside vessels; **sodium citrate/oxalate** keeps stored blood from clotting.
- **Haemoglobin** normal: about **13–17 g/dL in men** and **12–15 g/dL in women**. Low haemoglobin = **anaemia** — very common among women and children in Bihar (NFHS-5 data — check latest).
- **Lymph**: colourless fluid without RBCs; lymph nodes filter germs. The **spleen** is the largest lymphatic organ.

### Blood groups

The **ABO blood group system** was discovered by **Karl Landsteiner (1900–01)** (Nobel 1930). He also co-discovered the **Rh factor (1940, with Wiener)**, named after the **Rhesus monkey**.

| Blood group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Can donate to | Can receive from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | A | anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| B | B | anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| **AB** | A and B | **None** | AB only | **All** → **universal recipient** |
| **O** | **None** | anti-A and anti-B | **All** → **universal donor** | O only |

- Strictly: **O negative** is the universal donor of red cells; **AB positive** is the universal recipient.
- **Rh factor**: Rh+ (has the antigen; ~90%+ of Indians) or Rh−. If an **Rh− mother** carries an **Rh+ baby**, her antibodies can attack the baby's RBCs in a later pregnancy — **erythroblastosis fetalis**. It is prevented by giving the mother an **anti-D injection** after the first delivery.
- The **Bombay blood group** (hh, no H antigen) was first found in **Mumbai (1952)** by Dr Y. M. Bhende; such people can receive blood only from Bombay-group donors.
- Most common blood group in India: **B+ and O+** (roughly equal; varies by region).
- **World Blood Donor Day**: **14 June** (Landsteiner's birthday). National Voluntary Blood Donation Day: 1 October.
- A healthy adult can donate blood about every **3 months** (men) / 4 months (women).

> **Memory trick:** "**O** gives to all (O = Open-hearted donor); **AB** takes from all (AB = Accepts Blood)."

## Excretory system

**Excretion** is removal of metabolic wastes, mainly **urea**. **Urea is made in the liver** (ornithine cycle) and **filtered out by the kidneys**.

- Two bean-shaped **kidneys**, each with about **10 lakh (1 million) nephrons** — the functional units. Filtering happens in the **glomerulus** inside the **Bowman's capsule**.
- Kidneys filter about **180 litres** of fluid a day but produce only **1–1.5 litres** of urine (most water is reabsorbed). **ADH (vasopressin)** from the pituitary controls water reabsorption.
- Urine is yellow due to **urochrome** (from broken-down haemoglobin).
- Path: kidney → **ureters** → **urinary bladder** → **urethra**.
- **Kidney stones** are mostly **calcium oxalate**. **Dialysis** (artificial kidney, Willem Kolff) removes waste when kidneys fail; **kidney transplant** is the permanent option (the first successful one was in 1954, USA).
- Other excretory organs: **skin** (sweat), **lungs** (CO₂, water vapour), **liver** (bile pigments).
- **Animals and their main nitrogenous waste**: fish — **ammonia** (ammonotelic); humans and mammals — **urea** (ureotelic); birds, reptiles and insects — **uric acid** (uricotelic; saves water).

## Nervous system

The **nervous system** controls and coordinates the body using electrical signals. Its unit is the **neuron** (nerve cell) — **dendrites** receive signals, the **axon** carries them away; the gap between neurons is a **synapse**, crossed by chemicals (**neurotransmitters** like acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin).

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cerebrum** (largest part, two hemispheres) | **Intelligence, memory, thinking, will, speech**, senses, voluntary actions |
| **Cerebellum** | **Balance, posture and coordination** of voluntary movements (affected by alcohol — staggering walk) |
| **Medulla oblongata** | **Involuntary actions**: heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, swallowing, vomiting, sneezing, coughing |
| **Hypothalamus** | Body temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep; controls the pituitary ("**master of the master gland**") |
| Thalamus | Relay centre for sensory signals |
| Pons | Links parts of the brain; helps control breathing |
| **Spinal cord** | Carries messages to and from the brain; centre of **reflex actions** |

- The brain weighs about **1.3–1.4 kg** in adults; it is protected by the skull (cranium), **meninges** (three membranes — their inflammation is **meningitis**) and **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)**.
- **Reflex action**: quick, automatic response without thinking — e.g. pulling your hand away from a hot object, knee jerk, blinking. Controlled by the **spinal cord** (the path is called the **reflex arc**).
- **Peripheral nervous system**: 12 pairs of cranial nerves + 31 pairs of spinal nerves. The **autonomic nervous system** controls involuntary organs: **sympathetic** ("fight or flight" — speeds heart) and **parasympathetic** ("rest and digest").
- **EEG** records brain waves. Disorders: **Alzheimer's** (memory loss), **Parkinson's** (low **dopamine**; tremors), epilepsy (seizures), stroke (blocked or burst brain blood vessel).

## Endocrine system — glands and hormones

**Hormones** are chemical messengers made by **ductless (endocrine) glands** and carried by blood. The word "hormone" was coined by **Bayliss and Starling (1905)**; the first hormone discovered was **secretin**.

<div class="formula">

| Gland | Hormone(s) | Function | Disorder |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pituitary** (at base of brain; "**master gland**") | **Growth hormone (GH)**, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, **prolactin** (milk production), **ADH/vasopressin** (water balance), **oxytocin** (childbirth contractions, milk release) | Controls other glands | Excess GH in children → **gigantism**; in adults → **acromegaly**; deficiency → **dwarfism**; lack of ADH → **diabetes insipidus** (lots of dilute urine) |
| **Thyroid** (neck; largest endocrine gland) | **Thyroxine (T4)**, T3 (need **iodine**); calcitonin | Metabolism, growth, brain development | Iodine deficiency → **goitre**; low thyroxine in children → **cretinism**; in adults → **myxoedema**; excess → **hyperthyroidism / Graves' disease** (bulging eyes) |
| Parathyroid (4, behind thyroid) | **Parathormone** | Raises blood **calcium** | Low → tetany (muscle spasms) |
| **Adrenal** (on top of kidneys) | **Adrenaline (epinephrine)** — "**fight or flight / emergency hormone**"; cortisol (stress), aldosterone (salt balance) | Raises heart rate, BP and blood sugar in emergencies | Addison's disease, Cushing's syndrome |
| **Pancreas** (islets of Langerhans) | **Insulin** (β-cells) — **lowers blood sugar**; **glucagon** (α-cells) — raises blood sugar | Glucose balance | Lack/resistance of insulin → **diabetes mellitus** (sugar in urine) |
| **Thymus** | Thymosin | Matures **T-lymphocytes** (immunity) | Shrinks after puberty |
| Pineal | **Melatonin** | Sleep–wake cycle (body clock) | — |
| **Testes** | **Testosterone** | Male characteristics | — |
| **Ovaries** | **Oestrogen, progesterone** | Female characteristics, menstrual cycle, pregnancy ("pregnancy hormone" = progesterone) | — |
| Placenta | hCG (detected in **pregnancy tests**) | Maintains pregnancy | — |
| Stomach/intestine | Gastrin, secretin | Digestion | — |

</div>

- **Insulin** was discovered by **Frederick Banting and Charles Best (1921)**; Banting and Macleod got the 1923 Nobel. Insulin is a protein, so it is **injected** (it would be digested if eaten). Today, **human insulin is made by genetically modified bacteria** (*E. coli*) — the first recombinant DNA medicine (1982).
- India is often called the **"diabetes capital of the world"** — it has one of the largest numbers of diabetics (ICMR-INDIAB study 2023 estimated about **10 crore (101 million)** diabetics — check latest).

## Skeletal and muscular system

- **An adult has 206 bones**; a newborn has about **270–300** (some fuse later).
- **Largest/longest bone**: **femur** (thigh bone). **Smallest bone**: **stapes** (stirrup) in the **middle ear**. The three ear ossicles are **malleus (hammer), incus (anvil), stapes (stirrup)**.
- Skull: 22 bones (8 cranial + 14 facial; with ear bones 28–29). Only movable skull bone: **lower jaw (mandible)**. **Ribs: 12 pairs (24)** — 7 true, 3 false, 2 floating. **Vertebral column**: **33 vertebrae** in a child (26 bones in adults after fusion); the neck has **7 cervical vertebrae** — the same number in a giraffe and a human.
- Bones are made mostly of **calcium phosphate** (hydroxyapatite) and the protein **collagen**. **Bone marrow** makes blood cells.
- **Joints**: **ball-and-socket** (shoulder, hip — move in all directions), **hinge** (elbow, knee — one direction), **pivot** (neck — between atlas and axis; lets you turn your head), **gliding** (wrist, ankle), **fixed/immovable** (skull sutures). Joints are lubricated by **synovial fluid**.
- **Muscles**: about **600+** muscles; **largest — gluteus maximus** (buttock); **smallest — stapedius** (in the ear); **strongest (by force) — masseter** (jaw); hardest-working — the heart. Three types: **skeletal (voluntary, striped)**, **smooth (involuntary, e.g. stomach, intestine)**, **cardiac (involuntary, striped, never tires)**.
- Disorders: **osteoporosis** (porous bones in old age, especially women after menopause), **arthritis** (joint inflammation), **gout** (uric acid crystals in joints), **rickets/osteomalacia** (vitamin D deficiency), **tetanus** (spasm), **muscular dystrophy** (genetic).

## Skin and sense organs

- **Skin is the largest organ** of the body (about 1.5–2 m², 15% of body weight). Its colour comes from **melanin**. It makes **vitamin D** in sunlight, controls temperature by sweating, and protects against germs.
- **Eye**: retina has rods (dim light) and cones (colour); see the optics in [Heat, Light and Sound](/interview/bpsc-2027/science/heat-light-and-sound). The **cornea** is donated in eye donation. Tears contain **lysozyme** (kills bacteria). **Glaucoma**: raised pressure in the eye damages the optic nerve. **Conjunctivitis** ("pink eye", "Madras eye").
- **Ear**: outer ear → **eardrum (tympanum)** → middle ear ossicles → inner ear (**cochlea** for hearing; **semicircular canals** for **balance**). The **Eustachian tube** connects the middle ear to the throat and equalises pressure (yawning on an aeroplane helps).
- **Tongue**: taste buds detect sweet, sour, salty, bitter and **umami** (savoury). Most of what we call taste is actually smell.
- **Nose**: olfactory receptors for smell.

## Reproductive system (basics)

- **Male**: testes make **sperm** and testosterone; testes lie in the **scrotum** outside the body because sperm need a temperature **2–3 °C lower** than body temperature.
- **Female**: ovaries release one **ovum** (egg) roughly every **28 days** (menstrual cycle). **Fertilisation** takes place in the **fallopian tube (oviduct)**. The embryo implants in the **uterus**; the **placenta** connects mother and foetus. Pregnancy lasts about **280 days (~40 weeks, ~9 months)**.
- **Twins**: identical (monozygotic — one egg splits; always same sex) and fraternal (dizygotic — two eggs).
- **IVF ("test-tube baby")**: fertilisation outside the body. **Louise Brown** (UK, 1978) was the first; India's first IVF baby **"Durga" (Kanupriya Agarwal)** was born in **Kolkata in 1978** through the work of **Dr Subhash Mukhopadhyay**.
- **Contraception**: condoms (also protect against STDs), oral pills, copper-T (IUD), surgical methods — **vasectomy** (men) and **tubectomy** (women).
- Sexually transmitted infections: **HIV/AIDS**, syphilis, gonorrhoea, hepatitis B, HPV (cause of **cervical cancer**).

## Immune system

**Immunity** is the body's ability to resist disease.

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Innate (natural, non-specific)** | Present from birth | Skin, mucus, stomach acid, tears (lysozyme), WBCs that eat germs (phagocytes), fever, inflammation |
| **Acquired (adaptive, specific)** | Develops after exposure | Antibodies after infection or vaccination; has **memory** |
| **Active immunity** | Body makes its own antibodies | After infection or **vaccination**; long-lasting |
| **Passive immunity** | Ready-made antibodies given | **Mother's milk (colostrum — rich in IgA antibodies)**, antibodies across the placenta, **anti-venom**, anti-rabies serum, tetanus antitoxin; quick but short-lived |

- **B-lymphocytes** make **antibodies** (proteins called **immunoglobulins** — IgG is the most common and crosses the placenta; IgA in milk; IgE in allergy). **T-lymphocytes** (matured in the **thymus**) kill infected cells and help B cells. **HIV attacks helper T-cells (CD4 cells)**, weakening immunity.
- **Antigen**: foreign substance that triggers antibody production.
- **Allergy**: over-reaction to harmless substances (pollen, dust) — **histamine** is released; treated with antihistamines.
- **Autoimmune disease**: the body attacks itself — **rheumatoid arthritis**, type-1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis.
- **Organ transplant**: the body may **reject** a foreign organ; **cyclosporin** suppresses immunity to prevent rejection.
- **Edward Jenner (1796)** made the first vaccine (smallpox, from cowpox) — "father of immunology". **Louis Pasteur** made vaccines for **rabies** and anthrax.

## Quick revision

- Digestion of carbohydrates starts in the **mouth**, proteins in the **stomach**, fats in the **small intestine**.
- **Bile** is made by the **liver**, stored in the **gall bladder**, emulsifies fats, has **no enzymes**.
- **Villi** absorb food; **large intestine** absorbs water.
- **Left ventricle** has the thickest wall; **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood.
- **SA node** is the natural pacemaker.
- Blood groups: **Landsteiner**; **O** universal donor, **AB** universal recipient; Rh from Rhesus monkey.
- **RBCs** have no nucleus, live 120 days; **platelets** fall in dengue.
- **Urea** is made in the **liver**, removed by the **kidneys** (nephrons).
- **Cerebellum** = balance; **medulla** = involuntary actions; **cerebrum** = intelligence.
- **Reflex actions** are controlled by the spinal cord.
- **Insulin** lowers blood sugar (Banting & Best 1921); **adrenaline** = emergency hormone.
- **Iodine** → thyroxine → goitre if deficient.
- **206** bones; femur largest, stapes smallest.

### Mains questions

**1. Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) like diabetes, hypertension and heart disease are rising in India, including in Bihar. Discuss the causes and the role of technology in prevention and management.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** NCDs now cause the majority of deaths in India; ICMR-INDIAB estimates very large numbers of diabetics and pre-diabetics (check latest).
- **Body science:** insulin and blood sugar; blood pressure and arteries; cholesterol and coronary blockage.
- **Causes:** sedentary life, high salt/sugar/fat diets, tobacco and alcohol, stress, air pollution; rising urbanisation in Patna and other cities.
- **Technology for screening:** population-based NCD screening at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (health & wellness centres), glucometers, digital BP machines, NCD portal, ABHA health IDs.
- **Management:** telemedicine (eSanjeevani), mobile reminders, low-cost generics (Jan Aushadhi), wearables, AI-assisted retinal screening for diabetic eye disease.
- **Bihar angle:** strengthening district hospitals and AIIMS Patna/IGIMS; ASHA-led screening; awareness on tobacco (Bihar's khaini use).
- **Conclusion:** prevention through lifestyle plus tech-enabled primary care is the cost-effective path.

**2. "Blood donation is the gift of life." Explain the science of blood groups and transfusion, and suggest measures to improve blood availability in Bihar.** (about 250 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** blood cannot be manufactured; demand from accidents, surgery, childbirth, thalassaemia, dengue.
- **Science:** ABO and Rh systems, compatibility, component separation (RBCs, platelets, plasma) — one unit can help several patients.
- **Issues:** shortages in rural districts, reliance on replacement donors, lack of blood storage units in sub-divisional hospitals, myths about donation.
- **Technology:** e-RaktKosh portal for real-time stock, blood component separators, cold-chain storage, drones for delivery (trials in some states).
- **Measures:** voluntary donation camps in colleges, recognition for donors, blood storage centres at FRUs, thalassaemia registries.
- **Conclusion:** science-based awareness + digital stock management can end avoidable deaths.
