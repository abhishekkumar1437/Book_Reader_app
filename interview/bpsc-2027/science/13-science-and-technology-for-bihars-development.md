GS Paper II of BPSC Mains has a full section on the **"role and impact of science and technology in the development of India and Bihar"**. Examiners expect answers that link a technology to a **Bihar problem** — floods, low farm incomes, malnutrition, groundwater contamination, migration, weak services — and show **examples, schemes and institutions** from Bihar. This chapter gives you that content in one place. Prelims also asks Bihar S&T facts (where is the litchi research centre? which state first made an ethanol policy? where was the Imperial Agricultural Research Institute first set up?). Figures and scheme details change, so everything recent is **"as of 2026 — check the latest"** against the Bihar Economic Survey, the state Budget and department websites (see [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar) and [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar)).

> **In simple words:** Bihar has fertile land, plenty of water, young people and a rich tradition of learning (Nalanda, Vikramshila, Aryabhata). Its problems — floods in the north, drought in the south, small farms, weak industry and poor services — are exactly the kind that science and technology can help solve: better seeds, flood forecasting, safe drinking water, telemedicine, online services and clean energy. The key is to use technology for ordinary people, not only for show.

## Why S&T matters for Bihar — the development context

| Bihar's challenge | Technology that helps |
|---|---|
| **Agriculture-dependent** economy with **small and marginal holdings**; low productivity and low farm income | Improved seeds, mechanisation (custom hiring), micro-irrigation, soil testing, weather-based advisories, post-harvest processing, digital markets |
| **North Bihar floods** (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Burhi Gandak, Mahananda, Kamla) — about three-fourths of the state's area is described as flood-prone in official documents (check latest) | Flood forecasting, satellite inundation mapping, embankment monitoring, flood-tolerant crops, early warning |
| **South Bihar droughts** and falling groundwater | Watershed technology, ahar–pyne revival, rainwater harvesting, Ganga water lift, drip irrigation, drought-tolerant varieties |
| **Groundwater contamination** — arsenic, fluoride, iron | Testing kits, treatment plants, surface-water supply |
| **Lightning, heat waves, cold waves, earthquakes** (Bihar lies in seismic zones III, IV and V) | Early-warning apps, lightning arresters, earthquake-resistant building codes |
| **Health**: high child malnutrition and anaemia, kala-azar, AES, TB | Diagnostics, telemedicine, fortification, digital health records |
| **Education and skills**: large young population, learning gaps | EdTech, digital classrooms, skill programmes, higher-education institutions |
| **Low industrialisation, migration** | Food-processing tech, start-ups, IT services, ethanol and agro-industry, logistics |
| **Governance**: leakages, delays | DBT, online services (RTPS), land-record digitisation, grievance portals |
| **Low per-capita energy use** | Grid strengthening, smart meters, rooftop/floating solar, bioenergy |

> **Mains angle:** A strong GS II answer on this section usually follows **Problem → Technology → Bihar example/scheme → Impact → Challenges → Way forward**. Learn 2–3 examples for each sector below so you can fit them into any question.

## Bihar's science heritage and institutions

### Heritage (use in introductions)

- **Aryabhata** (born 476 CE), linked with **Kusumapura (Pataliputra)** — *Aryabhatiya* (499 CE): Earth's rotation, value of π (≈ 3.1416), place-value system. India's first satellite carried his name.
- **Nalanda** (5th century CE onwards, Gupta period) and **Vikramshila** (founded by Pala king **Dharmapala**, 8th–9th century) — great centres of learning including medicine, logic and astronomy.
- Ancient **Tarégna** (near Masaurhi, Patna) is traditionally linked with Aryabhata's observatory — the name is said to mean "counting stars"; the **2009 total solar eclipse** was famously observed there.
- **Imperial Agricultural Research Institute** was first set up at **Pusa (Samastipur, Bihar) in 1905** — after the **1934 Bihar–Nepal earthquake** damaged it, it moved to **New Delhi (1936)**, where it is still called "**Pusa Institute**" (IARI). This is a classic Bihar S&T fact.

## Agriculture and allied sectors

Agriculture employs the largest share of Bihar's workforce, so this is the most important sector for any S&T answer.

### Agriculture Road Maps

Bihar is known for its five-year **Agriculture Road Maps** (Krishi Road Map):

| Road Map | Period | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| First | **2008–12** | Seeds (**Mukhyamantri Tivra Beej Vistar Yojana**), productivity — "Rainbow revolution" idea |
| Second | **2012–17** | Food and nutrition security, horticulture, SRI and zero tillage |
| Third | **2017–22** | Organic farming, **organic corridor** along the Ganga, climate change |
| **Fourth** | **2023–28** | Mechanisation, crop diversification, dairy/fisheries, storage and processing, markets, climate resilience (check details) |

### Key farm technologies used in Bihar

| Technology | What it is | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Improved and hybrid seeds**, seed replacement | Certified seeds of rice, wheat, maize, pulses | Higher yield |
| **Flood-tolerant rice (Swarna Sub-1, Sambha Mahsuri Sub-1)** | Carries the **SUB1 gene**, survives about two weeks of submergence | Crucial for **north Bihar's flood-prone fields** |
| **Drought-tolerant rice (Sahbhagi Dhan)** | Short-duration, tolerant | South Bihar's dry years |
| **Zinc-rich and heat-tolerant wheat** | Biofortified, early-sowing varieties | Nutrition and terminal-heat protection |
| **SRI (System of Rice Intensification)** | Young seedlings, wider spacing, less water | Higher yield with less seed and water (Nalanda farmers became famous for record yields reported around 2011–12; figures are disputed — avoid numbers) |
| **Zero tillage / Happy Seeder** | Sowing wheat directly after rice harvest without ploughing | Saves time, diesel and water; timely sowing beats heat; avoids stubble burning |
| **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)** | Sowing rice seeds without nursery and puddling | Saves water and labour; less methane |
| **Laser land levelling** | Even fields | 20–30% water saving (commonly cited) |
| **Micro-irrigation** (drip, sprinkler) | Water to roots | "More crop per drop" |
| **Solar pumps** (PM-KUSUM) | Replace diesel pumps | Cheaper irrigation, cleaner |
| **Soil Health Card**, soil testing labs | Fertiliser according to soil need | Balanced fertiliser use |
| **Drones** | Spraying and crop monitoring; **Namo Drone Didi** (drones to women's SHGs) | Precision, lower chemical use |
| **Custom Hiring Centres** | Rent tractors, harvesters, seeders | Mechanisation for small farmers |
| **Weather-based agro-advisories** | IMD + KVKs via SMS, apps (Meghdoot, Mausam) | Better sowing and spraying decisions |
| **DBT agriculture portal** | Online registration for subsidies | Transparency |
| **Climate-Resilient Agriculture Programme** (Jalvayu Anukool Krishi, from **2019**) | Package of zero tillage, DSR, crop diversification in all districts — with **BISA, BAU Sabour, RPCAU Pusa, ICAR-RCER** | Adapting to rising heat and erratic rain |
| **Organic farming** | Organic corridor in Ganga districts | Premium prices, soil health |

### Livestock, dairy and fisheries

- **COMFED (Sudha)** — the Bihar State Milk Co-operative Federation (1983), on the **Anand model**; milk testing, chilling centres, processing plants.
- **Animal health tech**: vaccination (FMD, brucellosis) under the National Animal Disease Control Programme, **sex-sorted semen** and **IVF** under the Rashtriya Gokul Mission, **mobile veterinary units** (call centre "1962"), ear-tagging and the **Bharat Pashudhan** digital database.
- **Fisheries**: Bihar has many ponds, chaurs (low-lying wetlands) and rivers; technology — **biofloc** and **RAS (recirculatory aquaculture)**, cage culture in reservoirs, fish seed hatcheries, **chaur development** schemes; the state has pushed towards fish self-sufficiency (check latest production).
- **Poultry and goatry**: Jeevika-linked backyard poultry and goat rearing for women.

### Post-harvest technology and markets

- **Cold storage and warehousing** gaps cause large losses of fruits and vegetables.
- **Food processing**: Bihar's food-processing policies (e.g. **Bihar Agri Investment Promotion Policy, 2020** for makhana, fruits, vegetables, honey, medicinal plants, tea, seeds — check latest), **PMFME** (micro food enterprises — "one district one product").
- **e-NAM** mandis; Bihar abolished the APMC Act in **2006**, which ended regulated mandis — a debated reform (private trade grew, but price discovery and infrastructure remain issues).
- **Food irradiation** can extend the shelf life of litchi and mango (BARC technology).

> **Bihar connection:** **Jeevika** (Bihar Rural Livelihoods Promotion Society, started **2006** with World Bank support; later merged with the national NRLM framework) organises over a crore of rural women into **self-help groups**. It spreads technology at village level — **SRI**, kitchen gardens, poultry, solar lamps, digital bank sakhis (**BC Sakhi**), **Didi ki Rasoi** canteens in hospitals, and community health and nutrition messages. It is the best "technology through people" example for Bihar.

### The problem

- North Bihar's rivers come from the **Himalaya in Nepal**, carry huge **silt** loads and shift course. The **Kosi** — "**Sorrow of Bihar**" — has moved over 100 km westward over about two centuries (commonly cited).
- **Embankments** (over 3,000 km along Bihar's rivers — check latest) protect some areas but also trap water and raise riverbeds; **breaches** can be disastrous.
- **Kosi flood, 2008**: the embankment breached at **Kusaha (Nepal) on 18 August 2008**; the river switched course and affected Supaul, Madhepura, Araria, Saharsa and Purnia — over 30 lakh people affected.
- Other hazards: **lightning** (among India's highest death tolls), **heat waves**, **cold waves**, **earthquakes** (**1934 Bihar–Nepal earthquake**, **1988**), **fires** in thatched villages in summer, **boat accidents**, drought in south Bihar.

### Technology-based responses

| Area | Bihar / national examples (as of 2026 — check latest) |
|---|---|
| **Flood forecasting** | **Flood Management Improvement Support Centre (FMISC)** of the Water Resources Department — mathematical models, satellite data, river-gauge networks; **Central Water Commission** forecasts; data sharing with Nepal; **Google Flood Hub** AI forecasts |
| **Satellite mapping** | **NRSC/ISRO** inundation maps, **Bhuvan**, embankment vulnerability mapping, river-course change studies; **NISAR** radar data (2025 launch) will help through clouds |
| **Early warning to people** | SMS alerts, sirens, community radio, **Common Alerting Protocol (Sachet)**; **Indra Vajra** app for **lightning**; **heat-wave action plans** |
| **Structural measures** | Embankment strengthening with **geo-bags**, porcupine and spur structures, anti-erosion works; **Kosi barrage (Bhimnagar, 1959–63, in Nepal)**; proposed **Kosi High Dam** (Nepal, long-pending); river interlinking proposals (**Kosi–Mechi link** — approved in principle; check) |
| **Non-structural** | Flood-plain zoning, raised platforms and **flood shelters**, safer house designs on plinths, flood-tolerant crops, crop insurance, boat ambulances |
| **Institutions** | **Bihar State Disaster Management Authority (BSDMA)**; **SDRF** (State Disaster Response Force); **Bihar Disaster Risk Reduction Roadmap (2015–2030)** aligned with the **Sendai Framework** — Bihar was among the first states to adopt one |
| **Earthquake safety** | Bihar is in seismic zones **III, IV and V** (the Kosi–Nepal border belt is zone V); **BIS codes** for earthquake-resistant buildings; masons' training; school safety programmes |
| **Lightning safety** | Lightning arresters on public buildings, awareness on the crouch position, compensation |
| **Relief delivery** | **DBT of Gratuitous Relief** directly to flood-affected families' bank accounts (Bihar uses this widely); online relief camps data |

> **Mains angle:** Write that Bihar needs to move from "**flood control**" (only embankments) to "**flood management and living with floods**" — combining forecasting, warning, flood-resilient agriculture, safe housing, drainage of waterlogged areas, wetland restoration and cooperation with Nepal. Cite the **2008 Kosi breach** as the turning point.

| Programme / technology | Facts (as of 2026 — check latest) |
|---|---|
| **Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal** (Saat Nischay, 2016) | Piped water to every household; in quality-affected areas — **arsenic, fluoride and iron removal plants**; aligned with **Jal Jeevan Mission (2019)** |

> **Common mistake:** Do not list schemes only. Examiners want **impact and problems** too — e.g. RTPS improved time-bound delivery but faces server problems, middlemen and low awareness; DBT reduced leakages but some beneficiaries face Aadhaar-seeding and bank-access issues.

## Energy

- **Har Ghar Bijli** (Saat Nischay): near-universal household electrification; Bihar's **per-capita power consumption has risen many times** since the mid-2000s but remains below the national average (check latest figures).
- **Smart prepaid meters**: Bihar was among the **first states to install them at a very large scale** (from 2019; check latest numbers) — better billing and lower losses, but consumer complaints initially.
- **Generation**: mostly from central coal plants (NTPC **Barh, Kahalgaon, Nabinagar, Kanti**); Bihar has limited own generation and depends on long-term purchase.
- **Renewables**: rooftop solar (PM Surya Ghar), **floating solar** on reservoirs and ponds, solar on government buildings (Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali), solar pumps, **solar street lights in villages** (Saat Nischay-2), battery storage projects.
- **Bioenergy**: **Bihar Ethanol Production Promotion Policy, 2021** (first such state policy); **Purnia** grain-based ethanol plant (2022); maize and broken-rice based plants; **biogas/CBG** (GOBARdhan) for dung and farm waste; **rice-husk mini-grids** (Husk Power Systems, West Champaran).
- **Oil and gas**: **Barauni refinery** expansion; city gas distribution (**Patna CNG and piped natural gas** under the **Pradhan Mantri Urja Ganga** pipeline — Jagdishpur–Haldia–Bokaro–Dhamra).
- See [Energy and Environment Technology](/interview/bpsc-2027/science/energy-and-environment-technology) for the national picture.

## Challenges in using S&T for Bihar's development

1. **Low R&D spending and few research institutions** relative to population; dependence on central institutions.
2. **Brain drain**: talented students migrate to Kota, Delhi, Bengaluru and abroad; few local high-tech jobs.
3. **Digital divide**: low internet use among rural women, poor connectivity in flood-prone areas, language barriers.
4. **Infrastructure gaps**: cold chains, labs, hospital equipment, reliable power in rural areas.
5. **Small farm sizes** limit mechanisation and investment.
6. **Extension gap**: research does not reach farmers fast; too few extension workers.
7. **Disaster vulnerability** keeps destroying assets.
8. **Skill mismatch**: degrees without employable technical skills.
9. **Implementation**: schemes announced but maintenance weak (e.g. water plants not maintained, ICT labs unused).
10. **Data**: weak district-level data for planning; privacy concerns as data grows.

## Way forward

- **Raise state R&D and innovation funding**; set up a **State S&T Mission** with clear goals (water, floods, agriculture, health).
- **Link universities to local problems**: IIT Patna, BAU, RPCAU, AKU to work on flood modelling, makhana mechanisation, arsenic removal, low-cost housing.
- **Strengthen extension**: KVKs + Jeevika + digital advisories in **Bhojpuri, Maithili, Magahi, Angika, Bajjika** (use **Bhashini**).
- **Climate-resilient agriculture** in every block; crop diversification; protected cultivation.
- **Living with floods**: forecasting, early warning, flood-resilient housing and agriculture, wetland restoration, cooperation with Nepal.
- **Health**: telemedicine to every PHC, diagnostics at block level, nutrition technology (fortification, biofortified seeds).
- **Digital inclusion**: BharatNet last mile, CSCs, digital literacy for women, cyber safety.
- **Industry**: agro- and food-processing parks, ethanol, start-up ecosystem, IT parks in tier-2 cities (Bhagalpur, Darbhanga, Muzaffarpur, Gaya).
- **Bring back talent**: incentives for returning professionals and diaspora engagement.
- **Monitoring and evaluation**: outcome-based tracking with dashboards.

### Mains questions

**1. "Science and technology can turn Bihar's floods from a disaster into a manageable event." Discuss.** (38 marks / about 500 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** north Bihar's flood-prone geography; Kosi as "Sorrow of Bihar"; 2008 Kusaha breach; recurring losses to life, crops and assets.
- **Forecasting:** FMISC models, CWC, rain gauges, satellite rainfall, data sharing with Nepal, AI-based forecasts (Flood Hub).
- **Mapping and monitoring:** NRSC inundation maps, Bhuvan, embankment health monitoring, river-course change studies, NISAR radar.
- **Warning and response:** SMS, Sachet alerts, community radio, SDRF with boats and drones, flood shelters, DBT of gratuitous relief.
- **Resilient livelihoods:** Swarna Sub-1 rice, crop calendars, makhana and fisheries in waterlogged land, crop insurance.
- **Infrastructure:** geo-bag protection, raised houses and hand pumps, embankment maintenance, drainage of waterlogging, wetland restoration; Kosi High Dam and Kosi–Mechi link as long-term options.
- **Institutions:** BSDMA, DRR Roadmap 2015–30, Sendai Framework.
- **Limits:** embankments' side effects, siltation, Nepal cooperation, funding, maintenance.
- **Conclusion:** technology plus community preparedness can save lives and livelihoods — "living with floods".

**2. Examine the role of technology in transforming agriculture in Bihar. What are the barriers to its adoption by small farmers?** (38 marks / about 500 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** majority of workers depend on agriculture; small holdings; Agriculture Road Maps since 2008.
- **Seeds and varieties:** hybrid maize, flood- and drought-tolerant rice, zinc wheat, tissue-culture banana.
- **Resource-saving tech:** zero tillage, DSR, laser levelling, drip, solar pumps.
- **Climate resilience:** Climate-Resilient Agriculture Programme (2019) with BISA and agricultural universities.
- **Digital:** weather advisories, DBT portal, soil health cards, drones (Namo Drone Didi), e-NAM, Kisan e-Mitra.
- **Allied:** COMFED dairy tech, fisheries (biofloc, chaur development), makhana mechanisation, litchi cold chain, honey, mushroom.
- **Barriers:** small and fragmented land, tenancy, low credit, low awareness, poor extension, weak markets after APMC repeal, power supply, cost of machines, gender gaps.
- **Solutions:** custom hiring, FPOs, Jeevika, KVKs, credit and insurance, local-language advisories, processing units.
- **Conclusion:** technology plus institutions (FPOs, SHGs) can raise income and resilience.

**3. Discuss how digital technology has improved public service delivery in Bihar. Suggest measures to make it more inclusive.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** shift from office visits to online services; RTPS Act 2011.
- **Examples:** RTPS certificates, Lok Shikayat Nivaran (grievances in 60 days), DBT for scholarships/pensions/relief, Bihar Bhumi online records and mutation, e-registration, Dial 112, OFSS admissions, Student Credit Card portal.
- **Impact:** time-bound service, transparency, lower corruption and middlemen, better data.
- **Gaps:** digital divide, server downtime, language, women's access, cyber fraud, land survey disputes.
- **Measures:** assisted access at panchayat RTPS counters and CSCs, local-language interfaces, offline options, digital literacy (PMGDISHA), cyber police, feedback and social audit.
- **Conclusion:** e-governance must remain "citizen-first" to reach the last person.

**4. "Bihar's demographic dividend needs a science and technology push." Comment.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar has one of India's youngest populations; risk of unemployment and migration.
- **Education:** new engineering colleges and polytechnics, IIT/NIT/AIIMS, Unnayan, digital classrooms, Student Credit Card.
- **Skills:** Kushal Yuva, ITIs, industry-linked courses in food processing, electronics repair, solar installation, drones.
- **Jobs and enterprise:** start-up policy, IT policy, agro-processing, ethanol, logistics.
- **Women:** Jeevika, digital sakhis, reservation in jobs.
- **Challenges:** quality of education, faculty shortage, brain drain, low private investment.
- **Conclusion:** investing in STEM education, research and local industry can turn Bihar's youth into an engine of growth.
