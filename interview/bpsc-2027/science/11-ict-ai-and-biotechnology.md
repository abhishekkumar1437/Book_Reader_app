This chapter covers the two technologies that are changing governance and the economy fastest: **information and communication technology (ICT) including artificial intelligence**, and **biotechnology**. Prelims asks terms ("What is phishing?", "Which is the only GM crop approved for cultivation in India?", "Who invented the World Wide Web?") and recent missions; Mains GS II asks their **role and impact on India's and Bihar's development** — e-governance, digital payments, telemedicine, AI in agriculture, biotech crops and ethical issues. Fast-changing facts are marked "as of 2026 — check the latest".

> **In simple words:** ICT means using computers, the internet and mobiles to store and share information. AI is software that learns from data to recognise patterns, understand language or make predictions. Biotechnology means using living cells or their parts (like DNA and enzymes) to make useful products — from curd and insulin to pest-resistant crops and vaccines.

## Computers — basics

| Generation | Period (approx.) | Technology | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 1940s–1956 | **Vacuum tubes** | ENIAC, UNIVAC |
| Second | 1956–1963 | **Transistors** | IBM 1401 |
| Third | 1964–1971 | **Integrated circuits (ICs)** | IBM 360 |
| Fourth | 1971–present | **Microprocessors** (VLSI) | Personal computers (Intel 4004 was the first microprocessor, 1971) |
| Fifth | Present and future | **AI**, parallel processing, quantum | — |

- **Charles Babbage** — "**father of the computer**" (Analytical Engine design). **Ada Lovelace** — first computer programmer. **Alan Turing** — theory of computation, the **Turing test** for machine intelligence. **John von Neumann** — stored-program architecture.
- **Hardware**: CPU (ALU + control unit), memory, input/output devices. **Software**: system software (operating systems — Windows, Linux, Android, **BOSS Linux** made by C-DAC in India; **Maya OS** for defence) and application software.
- **Memory**: **RAM** (temporary, volatile — lost when power goes off), **ROM** (permanent, non-volatile), **cache** (fastest), storage (HDD, **SSD**, pen drive — flash memory).
- **Units**: 1 byte = 8 bits; 1 KB = 1,024 bytes; then MB, GB, **TB**, PB, EB, ZB, YB.
- **Binary system** (0 and 1) is the language of computers.
- **Open-source software**: source code freely available (Linux, Android core, LibreOffice). India's policy promotes open source in e-governance.

## Internet and telecommunication

| Milestone | Fact |
|---|---|
| **ARPANET** (USA) | **1969** — forerunner of the internet |
| TCP/IP | Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn ("fathers of the internet") |
| Email | Ray Tomlinson (1971) — chose the **@** symbol |
| **World Wide Web (WWW)** | **Tim Berners-Lee**, **1989** at **CERN** (Geneva); HTML, HTTP, URL |
| **Internet in India** | Public internet launched by **VSNL** on **15 August 1995** |
| First mobile call in India | **31 July 1995** — **Jyoti Basu** (West Bengal CM) called Union Telecom Minister Sukh Ram |

**Key terms**:
- **IP address**: unique number of a device on a network (**IPv4** 32-bit; **IPv6** 128-bit — vastly more addresses).
- **DNS**: "phone book of the internet" that turns names (bpsc.bihar.gov.in) into IP addresses.
- **Browser** (Chrome, Firefox), **search engine** (Google), **URL**, **HTTP/HTTPS** (S = secure, encrypted).
- **Bandwidth**: data-carrying capacity (bits per second).
- **LAN / MAN / WAN**: local / metropolitan / wide-area networks. **Wi-Fi** (wireless LAN), **Bluetooth** (short-range), **NFC** (very short-range, contactless cards), **Li-Fi** (data through light).
- **Optical fibre** carries data as light (total internal reflection) — the backbone of the internet; undersea cables carry most international traffic.
- **Cloud computing**: renting storage and computing over the internet — **IaaS, PaaS, SaaS**. India's government cloud: **MeghRaj** (GI Cloud).
- **Internet of Things (IoT)**: everyday devices connected to the internet (smart meters, sensors in fields, smart city cameras).
- **Big data**: very large datasets analysed for patterns.
- **Blockchain**: a tamper-proof shared digital ledger; basis of **cryptocurrencies** (Bitcoin, 2009, by "Satoshi Nakamoto"). India's **RBI digital rupee (e₹, CBDC)** pilots began in **2022**. Blockchain is also tried for land records and supply chains.
- **Edge computing**: processing data near where it is produced (e.g. in a factory) instead of in a distant cloud.
- **Metaverse / AR / VR**: immersive virtual worlds; augmented reality overlays digital images on the real world.

### Bridging the digital divide

- **BharatNet**: optical fibre to all **~2.5 lakh gram panchayats** (began 2011 as NOFN); amended BharatNet (2023) for last-mile connectivity — check progress.
- **PM-WANI** (2020): public Wi-Fi hotspots by small shopkeepers.
- **Common Service Centres (CSCs)**: village-level e-service kiosks run by **Village Level Entrepreneurs (VLEs)**.
- **PMGDISHA**: digital literacy for rural households.
- **Telecommunications Act, 2023** replaced the Indian Telegraph Act (1885).

## Cyber security and data protection

### Common cyber threats

| Threat | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Malware** | Malicious software — virus, worm, trojan, spyware |
| **Virus** | Attaches to files, spreads when files are opened |
| **Worm** | Spreads by itself across networks |
| **Trojan horse** | Looks harmless but hides malicious code |
| **Ransomware** | **Encrypts your data and demands ransom** (e.g. WannaCry 2017; AIIMS Delhi server attack, 2022) |
| **Spyware** | Secretly watches activities (e.g. **Pegasus** controversy) |
| **Phishing** | Fake emails/sites to steal passwords and bank details; **vishing** (by voice call), **smishing** (by SMS) |
| **DoS / DDoS** | Flooding a website so it crashes |
| **Identity theft, SIM swap, "digital arrest" scams** | Fraudsters pretend to be police/CBI and demand money — a fast-growing scam (2024–25) |
| **Deepfake** | AI-made fake video/audio of real people |
| **Botnet** | Network of infected computers controlled by an attacker |
| Cookies | Small files saved by websites (can track you) |
| Firewall | Barrier that filters network traffic |
| Encryption | Converting data into a secret code |
| Two-factor authentication (2FA) | Password + OTP or device |

> **Bihar connection:** **Jamtara** (in Jharkhand, but bordering Bihar's region) and some Bihar districts like **Nawada** have been in news as hubs of phone-based cyber fraud. Bihar Police set up an **Economic Offences Unit (EOU)** cyber wing and cyber police stations in districts. National helpline: **1930** and portal **cybercrime.gov.in**.

| **NCIIPC** | Protects critical information infrastructure (power grid, banks) |

| **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** | Consent-based processing of personal data; rights of "data principals"; duties of "data fiduciaries"; **Data Protection Board of India**; penalties up to ₹250 crore; DPDP Rules notified in **2025** (check latest) |
| **IT Rules 2021** (amended) | Duties of social media intermediaries; grievance officers; rules on deepfakes and AI-labelling (amendments — check latest) |

## Supercomputers, semiconductors and quantum technology

### Supercomputers

- Speed is measured in **FLOPS** (floating-point operations per second); petaflop = 10¹⁵.
- India's first supercomputer: **PARAM 8000 (1991)** by **C-DAC, Pune** under **Vijay Bhatkar** ("father of Indian supercomputing") — built after the USA refused to sell a Cray supercomputer.
- **National Supercomputing Mission (NSM, 2015)** — C-DAC and IISc; PARAM Shivay, PARAM Siddhi-AI, **PARAM Rudra** (2024, indigenous servers).
- Weather-forecasting supercomputers: **Pratyush** (IITM Pune) and **Mihir** (NCMRWF Noida); **Arka and Arunika** (2024) upgraded these.
- **AIRAWAT** — AI supercomputer (C-DAC).

### Semiconductors

- Chips are made of **silicon**; modern chips have billions of **transistors**. Making a chip has stages: **design → fabrication (fab) → assembly, testing, marking and packaging (ATMP/OSAT)**.
- **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)** — **December 2021**, ₹76,000 crore incentive programme (Semicon India).
- Approved projects (as of 2026 — check latest): **Tata Electronics–PSMC fab at Dholera (Gujarat)** — India's first commercial fab; **Micron's ATMP unit at Sanand (Gujarat)**; Tata's ATMP at **Jagiroad (Assam)**; CG Power–Renesas (Sanand), Kaynes (Sanand), HCL–Foxconn and others.
- **Vikram 3201** — a 32-bit processor developed by ISRO's **Semiconductor Laboratory (SCL), Chandigarh** — was presented as a "made-in-India" chip at **Semicon India 2025** (check latest).
- **Design Linked Incentive (DLI)** scheme supports chip design start-ups.

### Quantum technology

- **Quantum computers** use **qubits**, which can be 0 and 1 at the same time (**superposition**) and linked with each other (**entanglement**). They may solve some problems far faster than ordinary computers (drug design, materials, cryptography).
- **Quantum communication / Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)**: communication that cannot be secretly intercepted.
- **National Quantum Mission (NQM)** — approved **April 2023**, about **₹6,003 crore** for **2023–24 to 2030–31**; goal: intermediate-scale quantum computers (**50–1,000 physical qubits**), satellite-based secure quantum communication over 2,000 km, quantum sensors and materials; **four thematic hubs** (IISc Bengaluru, IIT Madras, IIT Bombay, IIT Delhi).
- **Nobel Prize in Physics 2022**: Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger — quantum entanglement experiments. (2025 Physics Nobel was also for macroscopic quantum effects in electrical circuits — Clarke, Devoret, Martinis.)

## Artificial intelligence

### Basic ideas

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Artificial intelligence (AI)** | Machines doing tasks that need human-like intelligence — understanding language, recognising images, making decisions. Term coined by **John McCarthy (1956, Dartmouth conference)** |
| **Machine learning (ML)** | AI that **learns patterns from data** instead of following fixed rules |
| **Deep learning** | ML using many-layered **artificial neural networks** (inspired by the brain); 2024 Physics Nobel to **Hopfield and Hinton** for foundations of neural networks |
| **Generative AI** | AI that **creates** new text, images, audio, video (e.g. ChatGPT, image generators) |
| **Large language model (LLM)** | Huge neural network trained on massive text to understand and generate language |
| **Natural language processing (NLP)** | Computers understanding human language (translation, chatbots, speech-to-text) |
| **Computer vision** | Machines "seeing" — face recognition, crop disease detection from photos, number-plate reading |
| **Narrow vs general AI** | Narrow AI does one task well (all current AI); **artificial general intelligence (AGI)** would match humans across tasks — not yet achieved |
| **Robotics** | Machines that sense and act; with AI → autonomous robots, drones |
| **Chatbot** | Program that converses (e.g. **Kisan e-Mitra** for PM-KISAN queries) |
| **Bias, hallucination** | AI can reproduce unfair patterns from data, and can confidently give **false** answers |
| **Deepfake** | Realistic fake media made by AI |

| **Principles for Responsible AI** (NITI, 2021) | Safety, equality, inclusivity, privacy, transparency, accountability |

| **Global role** | India was **lead chair of GPAI** (Global Partnership on AI, 2024); co-chaired the Paris AI Action Summit (Feb 2025) and hosted the **India AI Impact Summit in New Delhi (February 2026)** (check outcomes) |

### AI applications for development

| Sector | Applications |
|---|---|
| **Agriculture** | Pest and disease detection from phone photos, weather-based advisories, yield prediction, soil analysis, **Kisan e-Mitra** chatbot, drone spraying, price forecasting, **AI-based crop surveys** (Digital Crop Survey) |
| **Health** | Reading X-rays and TB screening (AI-assisted chest X-ray), diabetic retinopathy detection, **eSanjeevani** clinical decision support, outbreak prediction, drug discovery |
| **Education** | Personalised learning in Indian languages, reading-fluency assessment tools, automated evaluation |
| **Governance** | Grievance analysis, fraud detection in welfare schemes, traffic management, predictive policing (with caution), translation of court judgments (**SUVAS** in the Supreme Court) |
| **Disaster management** | Flood forecasting (e.g. Google's Flood Hub covers India), lightning prediction, cyclone track models |

### Concerns and ethics

- **Jobs**: automation may replace routine work; need for reskilling (FutureSkills Prime, Skill India).
- **Bias and fairness**: data from cities and English may ignore rural and Indian-language users.
- **Privacy and surveillance**: facial recognition; DPDP Act compliance.
- **Misinformation**: deepfakes during elections; labelling and detection tools.
- **Accountability**: who is responsible for an AI's mistake?
- **Energy and water use** of large data centres.
- **Digital divide**: benefits may go to those already connected.
- **Global governance**: EU AI Act (2024) takes a risk-based approach; India emphasises inclusive, development-focused AI.

## Biotechnology — basics

**Biotechnology** = using living organisms, cells or their parts to make products or solve problems. The word was coined by **Karl Ereky (1919)**.

| Traditional biotech | Modern biotech |
|---|---|
| Curd (*Lactobacillus*), bread and idli (yeast), alcohol, vinegar, cheese, selective breeding | **Genetic engineering**, recombinant DNA, cloning, PCR, CRISPR, monoclonal antibodies, stem cells |

### Key tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **Restriction enzymes** ("molecular scissors") | Cut DNA at specific sequences; first — **EcoRI** from *E. coli* |
| **DNA ligase** ("molecular glue") | Joins DNA pieces |
| **Vectors** | Carriers — **plasmids** (small circular DNA in bacteria), viruses (bacteriophages); ***Agrobacterium tumefaciens*** (Ti plasmid) — "**natural genetic engineer**" of plants |
| **PCR** (Polymerase Chain Reaction) | Makes **millions of copies** of a DNA piece in hours; invented by **Kary Mullis (1983)**, Nobel 1993; uses heat-stable **Taq polymerase** (from the hot-spring bacterium *Thermus aquaticus*); **RT-PCR** was the main COVID-19 test |
| **Gel electrophoresis** | Separates DNA pieces by size |
| **DNA sequencing** | Reading the order of bases (Sanger method; now next-generation sequencing) |
| **CRISPR-Cas9** | "**Genetic scissors**" — precise **gene editing**; **Jennifer Doudna & Emmanuelle Charpentier**, Nobel Chemistry **2020**; first approved CRISPR therapy for sickle-cell disease (**Casgevy**, UK/USA 2023) |
| **Monoclonal antibodies** | Identical antibodies from one clone (hybridoma technology — Köhler and Milstein); used in cancer therapy, diagnostics, pregnancy test kits |
| **ELISA** | Antibody-based test (HIV, dengue) |
| **Bioreactor** | Large vessel to grow cells and make products |

### Cloning and stem cells

- **Cloning**: making a genetically identical copy. **Dolly the sheep** (1996, **Roslin Institute, Scotland**; Ian Wilmut) — first mammal cloned from an adult cell. India's **first cloned buffalo calf — "Samrupa" (2009)** and **Garima** at **NDRI Karnal**; first cloned Pashmina goat **"Noori" (2012)** at SKUAST Kashmir.
- **Stem cells**: unspecialised cells that can become many cell types. **Embryonic** (can become any cell — pluripotent), **adult** (bone marrow — limited), **iPSCs** (induced pluripotent stem cells from skin cells — **Shinya Yamanaka**, Nobel 2012). **Umbilical cord blood banking**. Use: bone-marrow transplant for leukaemia and thalassaemia; research in regenerative medicine.
- **Gene therapy**: correcting a faulty gene (e.g. for SCID, sickle-cell, haemophilia). India's first gene therapy trial for **haemophilia A** at CMC Vellore showed success (2024–25 reports).
- **CAR-T cell therapy**: patient's own T-cells re-engineered to attack cancer; **NexCAR19** (IIT Bombay–Tata Memorial, ImmunoACT) — India's first indigenous CAR-T therapy, approved **2023**, at a fraction of global cost.
- **Three-parent baby / mitochondrial replacement**, **designer babies** — ethical debates.

## Biotechnology in agriculture

### Genetically modified (GM) crops

A **GM crop** has a gene from another organism inserted to give a new trait.

| Crop | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Bt cotton** | Contains a gene from the soil bacterium ***Bacillus thuringiensis*** that produces **Cry protein** toxic to **bollworms**. Approved in **2002** — **the only GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India** (as of 2026 — check). Covers over 90% of India's cotton area. Problem: **pink bollworm resistance**, illegal herbicide-tolerant (HTBt) cotton |
| **Bt brinjal** | Approved by GEAC in 2009 but **moratorium in 2010** (Jairam Ramesh); grown in **Bangladesh** |
| **GM mustard — DMH-11** | Developed by **Delhi University (Deepak Pental)** using **barnase–barstar** system for hybrid seed; GEAC approved **environmental release in October 2022**; matter **pending in the Supreme Court** (split verdict in 2024 — check latest) |
| **Golden rice** | Rice with **β-carotene** (vitamin A) — fights vitamin A deficiency; approved in the Philippines |
| Flavr Savr tomato | First commercial GM food (USA, 1994) — slower ripening |

- **Regulator**: **GEAC — Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee**, under the **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change**, under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** rules (1989).
- **International**: **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2000)** under the CBD — India is a party.
- **Arguments for**: higher yields, less pesticide, nutrition (biofortification), drought/flood tolerance, climate resilience. **Against**: biosafety and health concerns, gene flow to wild relatives, seed monopolies and costs, loss of biodiversity, resistance in pests.

### Genome-edited crops (recent)

- In **2022**, India **exempted SDN-1 and SDN-2 genome-edited plants** (no foreign DNA added) from strict GM rules.
- In **May 2025**, ICAR released the **world's first genome-edited rice varieties** — **DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala)** (higher yield, earlier maturity) and **Pusa DST Rice 1** (salinity and alkalinity tolerance) (check latest).

### Other agri-biotech

- **Biofertilisers**: *Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*, *Azospirillum*, blue-green algae, *Azolla*, **mycorrhiza**, phosphate-solubilising bacteria.
- **Biopesticides**: ***Bacillus thuringiensis*** sprays, ***Trichoderma*** (fungus against soil diseases), **NPV** (nuclear polyhedrosis virus), **neem** products; **Trichogramma** wasps (biocontrol).
- **Tissue culture**: disease-free banana, potato, sugarcane plantlets; **somatic hybrids** (pomato).
- **Marker-assisted breeding**: faster conventional breeding using DNA markers (e.g. flood-tolerant **Swarna Sub-1** rice, which survives up to two weeks under water — very useful in **flood-prone Bihar**).
- **Biofortified crops** (by conventional breeding): zinc-rich wheat and rice, iron-rich pearl millet (**Dhanashakti**), vitamin-A maize; ICAR has released many such varieties.
- **Animal biotech**: embryo transfer, **IVF in cattle**, sex-sorted semen (more female calves) under **Rashtriya Gokul Mission**; transgenic animals (research).

> **Bihar connection:** **Bihar Agricultural University (Sabour, Bhagalpur)** and **Dr Rajendra Prasad Central Agricultural University (Pusa, Samastipur)** work on tissue-culture banana, makhana and litchi improvement, flood- and drought-tolerant rice (Sub-1 types), and zinc-rich wheat. Tissue-culture banana and potato labs, and biofertiliser production units, support farmers. Details in [Science and Technology for Bihar's Development](/interview/bpsc-2027/science/science-and-technology-for-bihars-development).

## Biotechnology in medicine, industry and environment

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Medicines** | **Human insulin** (first rDNA drug, 1982, made in *E. coli*), human growth hormone, interferons, erythropoietin, clotting factors, **biosimilars** (India is a major producer) |
| **Vaccines** | Recombinant **hepatitis B** vaccine (Shantha Biotechnics, Hyderabad — India's first, 1997), **Rotavac**, Covaxin, ZyCoV-D (DNA), GEMCOVAC (mRNA), **Cervavac** (HPV); Serum Institute (Pune) is the world's largest vaccine maker by doses |
| **Diagnostics** | PCR, ELISA, rapid test kits (rK39 for kala-azar, dengue NS1), **TrueNat** (Indian molecular TB/COVID test) |
| **Forensics** | **DNA fingerprinting** (Alec Jeffreys 1984; Lalji Singh in India); **DNA Technology (Use and Application) Regulation Bill** (withdrawn in 2023 — check) |
| **Industry** | Enzymes in detergents, textiles and food; **biofuels** (ethanol from sugarcane, maize; **2G ethanol from crop residue** — Panipat plant, 2022); **bioplastics** (PLA) |
| **Environment** | **Bioremediation** — microbes clean pollution; "**superbug**" (*Pseudomonas*) engineered by **Ananda Mohan Chakrabarty** to eat oil — first GM organism to be **patented (Diamond v. Chakrabarty, USA, 1980)**; TERI's **Oilzapper** for oil spills; **phytoremediation** (plants removing heavy metals); biogas from waste |
| **Genomics** | **Genome India Project** (2020, DBT) — 10,000 genomes; **IndiGen**; Human Genome Project (2003); precision medicine |

### India's bio-economy policy (as of 2026 — check latest)

- **Department of Biotechnology (DBT)** — set up **1986** (under the Ministry of Science and Technology). **BIRAC** (Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council, 2012) supports start-ups. **National Biotechnology Development Strategy** (2015–20, 2021–25).
- **BioE3 Policy** — "**Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment**", approved **August 2024**: bio-manufacturing hubs, **Bio-AI hubs**, biofoundries; focus areas include bio-based chemicals and enzymes, functional foods and smart proteins, precision biotherapeutics, climate-resilient agriculture, carbon capture, marine and space research.
- India's **bio-economy** has grown fast (to well over **$150 billion** by 2024 as per DBT/BIRAC reports); target about **$300 billion by 2030** (check latest).
- **Biosafety** labs: BSL-3 and BSL-4 (e.g. NIV Pune — first BSL-4 lab in India).

### Intellectual property and biopiracy

**Biopiracy** = patenting traditional knowledge or biological resources of a community without permission or benefit sharing.

| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Turmeric** (wound healing) | US patent (1995) **revoked in 1997** after CSIR challenged it with old Indian texts |
| **Neem** (fungicide) | European patent **revoked** (2000, final 2005) |
| **Basmati** | US firm RiceTec's patent (1997) largely struck down; **Basmati has a GI tag in India** |

- India's response: **TKDL — Traditional Knowledge Digital Library** (2001, CSIR + AYUSH) — gives patent offices access to traditional knowledge in their languages to stop wrong patents.
- **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (amended 2023): access and benefit sharing; **National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai)**, **State Biodiversity Boards** (Bihar has one), **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** and **People's Biodiversity Registers** at local level.
- **Patents Act, 1970** (amended 2005 for product patents in pharmaceuticals; **Section 3(d)** prevents "evergreening" — upheld in the **Novartis (Glivec) case, 2013**).
- **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001** — protects farmers' rights to save and sell seeds (PepsiCo potato case, 2019).

### Mains questions

**1. "Digital public infrastructure has transformed governance in India." Discuss its benefits and limitations, with examples from Bihar.** (38 marks / about 500 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India Stack — Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, Account Aggregator; Digital India since 2015.
- **Benefits:** Direct Benefit Transfer (pensions, scholarships, PM-KISAN) with reduced leakages; financial inclusion (Jan Dhan accounts, UPI payments by small vendors); faster services (DigiLocker certificates, RTPS-type online services); transparency (GeM procurement, e-NAM); health (ABHA, CoWIN, eSanjeevani); education (DIKSHA, SWAYAM).
- **Bihar examples:** RTPS online services, e-Kalyan/DBT portals for scholarships and cycle/poshak schemes, Bihar Student Credit Card portal, e-Labharthi for pensions, Jeevika digital records, online land records (Bhumi) and land mutation, BPSC online applications.
- **Limitations:** digital divide (low internet and smartphone use among rural women), biometric authentication failures, cyber fraud (phishing, digital arrest scams), data privacy (DPDP Act implementation), poor connectivity, language barriers.
- **Way forward:** BharatNet last mile, CSCs and assisted access, Bhashini for Bihar's languages, cyber police stations and awareness (1930 helpline), grievance redress, offline fallbacks.
- **Conclusion:** DPI is powerful if inclusion, privacy and security grow with it.

**2. Discuss the opportunities and risks of artificial intelligence for a developing state like Bihar.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** AI as a general-purpose technology; IndiaAI Mission; #AIforAll.
- **Opportunities:** agriculture advisories in Bhojpuri/Maithili/Magahi via Bhashini; crop disease detection; flood and lightning prediction; AI-assisted TB and diabetic eye screening at PHCs; personalised learning; welfare fraud detection; grievance analysis; traffic management in Patna.
- **Risks:** job loss in routine work, low digital skills, data gaps (bias against rural users), deepfakes in elections, privacy, high cost of compute.
- **Enablers:** IIT Patna, NIT Patna, Bihar start-up policy, CSCs, skilling (FutureSkills), open datasets (AIKosh), public-private partnerships.
- **Conclusion:** human-centred, language-inclusive AI can help Bihar leapfrog, if skills and safeguards come first.

**3. Examine the role of biotechnology in ensuring food and health security in India. What ethical and regulatory challenges does it raise?** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** biotech — rDNA, PCR, CRISPR; DBT, BioE3 policy.
- **Food security:** Bt cotton experience; flood-tolerant Swarna Sub-1 rice for Bihar; genome-edited rice varieties (2025); biofortified crops against hidden hunger; biofertilisers and biopesticides; tissue culture.
- **Health security:** indigenous vaccines (Covaxin, ZyCoV-D, Rotavac, Cervavac), diagnostics (TrueNat, rK39), CAR-T (NexCAR19), insulin and biosimilars, genome sequencing for surveillance.
- **Challenges:** GM debates (Bt brinjal, GM mustard in court), biosafety, seed sovereignty, pest resistance, gene-editing ethics, data privacy in genomics, biopiracy, slow regulation.
- **Way forward:** science-based, transparent regulation (GEAC reforms), public-sector R&D, farmer participation, labelling, benefit sharing under the Biological Diversity Act.
- **Conclusion:** biotech is essential for a growing population; trust needs transparency and strong regulation.
