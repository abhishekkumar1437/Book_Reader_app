Energy and environment technology is a favourite meeting point of Prelims current affairs ("India's target for non-fossil capacity by 2030?", "Where is the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor?", "What is green hydrogen?") and Mains GS II section 3 ("role of science and technology in sustainable development"). This chapter explains each energy source simply, lists India's missions and targets, and shows how Bihar fits in. **Capacity numbers and targets change every year — treat them as "as of 2026, check the latest" and update from the Economic Survey, PIB and the MNRE website.**

> **In simple words:** Most of India's electricity still comes from coal, which pollutes the air and warms the climate. India is adding solar and wind power fast, building nuclear plants, mixing ethanol into petrol, and planning to make "green" hydrogen. At the same time, technology is used to clean polluted air, water and waste. The goal is to grow the economy while reaching net-zero emissions by 2070.

## Energy basics

- **Primary energy**: energy in its natural form — coal, crude oil, natural gas, uranium, sunlight, wind, flowing water, biomass.
- **Secondary energy**: converted forms — **electricity**, petrol, LPG, hydrogen.
- **Conventional (non-renewable)**: coal, oil, gas, (nuclear fuel) — limited stocks.
- **Non-conventional (renewable)**: solar, wind, hydro, biomass, geothermal, tidal, wave — naturally replenished.
- **Energy security**: reliable, affordable supply. India imports about **85% of its crude oil** and about half of its natural gas (check latest), so price shocks hit its economy.
- **Units**: power in **watt (W)** — kW, **MW, GW** (1 GW = 1,000 MW); energy in **kWh ("units")**, MWh, **TWh**. **Capacity factor**: actual output ÷ maximum possible output. Solar plants in India run at about **18–22%** capacity factor; coal plants much higher — so 1 GW of solar gives much less energy than 1 GW of coal.

### India's power mix (approximate, as of 2025–26 — check latest)

| Source | Share of installed capacity | Share of actual generation |
|---|---|---|
| **Coal and lignite** | Under half | **About 70–75%** (still dominant) |
| **Solar** | Fastest growing; largest renewable source | Rising |
| **Wind** | Large | — |
| **Large hydro** | Moderate | — |
| **Nuclear** | Small (~2%) | ~3% |
| Gas, biomass, small hydro | Small | — |

> **Prelims angle:** In **2025**, India's **non-fossil fuel sources crossed 50% of installed electricity capacity** — the 2030 NDC target reached about five years early (reported mid-2025; check latest). But in **generation**, coal still supplies most electricity. Examiners like this difference between **capacity** and **generation**.

## Solar energy

**How it works**: a **solar photovoltaic (PV) cell** is a large **p–n junction** of **silicon**; sunlight knocks electrons free and produces **direct current (DC)**; an **inverter** converts it to AC. **Solar thermal** systems use sunlight for heat (solar water heaters, solar cookers, concentrated solar power with mirrors).

| Initiative | Key facts (as of 2026 — check latest) |
|---|---|
| **Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission** (JNNSM, **2010**) | Original target 20 GW by 2022, raised to **100 GW** (2015); part of the **National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008)** |
| **Solar parks** | **Bhadla (Rajasthan)** — among the world's largest; Pavagada (Karnataka); Rewa (MP, supplied Delhi Metro); **Khavda (Gujarat)** — giant hybrid renewable park in the Rann of Kutch |
| **PM-KUSUM** (2019) | **Solar pumps** for farmers, solar plants on barren land, solarisation of grid-connected pumps — income plus irrigation |
| **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana** (**February 2024**) | **Rooftop solar for 1 crore households**, up to **300 units free electricity a month**, subsidy for 1–3 kW systems |
| **PLI for solar modules** | To make high-efficiency modules in India and cut Chinese imports; **ALMM** list of approved manufacturers |
| **International Solar Alliance (ISA)** | Launched by **India and France** at **COP21 Paris (30 November 2015)**; **HQ Gurugram (Haryana)** — first international treaty-based organisation headquartered in India; "**One Sun One World One Grid (OSOWOG)**" idea |
| **Solar cities and villages** | **Modhera (Gujarat)** — India's first 24×7 solar-powered village (2022); **Konark** solar city; **Diu** ran on 100% solar by day |

**Solar advantages**: free fuel, no emissions while running, modular (from 1 kW rooftop to GW parks), works in remote villages. **Limits**: only by day, low capacity factor, land needs, dust, storage cost, Chinese supply-chain dependence, panel waste at end of life.

> **Bihar connection:** **Dharnai village (Jehanabad district)** became famous as a **solar micro-grid village (2014)** — an early experiment showing both promise and the problem of grid competition. Bihar has also built **floating solar** projects on reservoirs/ponds (e.g. at **Durgawati reservoir in Kaimur** and in **Darbhanga** — check latest), large solar with **battery storage at Kajra (Lakhisarai)** (check latest status), and rooftop solar on government buildings under **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali**. Bihar's land is mostly fertile, densely populated farmland, so **rooftop, floating and agri-voltaic** (solar above crops) models suit it better than giant land-based parks.

## Wind energy

- **Wind turbines** convert kinetic energy of wind into electricity (electromagnetic induction in a generator). Good sites need steady winds — coasts and high plateaus.
- Leading states: **Gujarat, Tamil Nadu**, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Rajasthan. **Muppandal (Tamil Nadu)** is one of India's largest wind farm clusters.
- **Offshore wind**: potential off **Gujarat and Tamil Nadu**; **Viability Gap Funding** approved (2024) for the first projects (check latest).
- **Hybrid** solar-wind parks smooth the supply (wind often blows at night).
- **National Institute of Wind Energy (NIWE)**: Chennai.

## Hydropower

- **Large hydro** (above 25 MW) is a **renewable** source in India's classification since 2019; **small hydro** (up to 25 MW) is promoted for hilly states.
- Uses: base and peak power, irrigation, flood control. **Pumped storage** stores energy by pumping water uphill when power is cheap and releasing it when needed — **"water batteries"** for solar-heavy grids.
- Concerns: displacement of people, ecological damage, earthquakes and landslides in the Himalaya, siltation, disputes.
- Big projects: Tehri (Uttarakhand), Bhakra Nangal, Sardar Sarovar, **Subansiri Lower** (Assam–Arunachal, largest under construction — check latest).

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar has limited hydro potential (flat plains). The **Kosi** and **Gandak** projects (with Nepal) have small power houses; the **Kosi High Dam** proposal in Nepal is discussed for both **flood control and power** (long-pending — check latest). Bihar buys hydro power from other states and Nepal.

## Bioenergy and biofuels

| Type | What it is | Indian programme |
|---|---|---|
| **Biomass power** | Burning crop residue, rice husk, bagasse for electricity | Bagasse **cogeneration** in sugar mills |
| **Biogas** | **Methane (~55–75%)** + CO₂ from **anaerobic digestion** of cattle dung, kitchen and farm waste; slurry is good manure | **GOBARdhan** (2018) |
| **Compressed biogas (CBG)** | Purified biogas (like CNG) for vehicles | **SATAT** (2018) — CBG plants and sale through oil companies |
| **1G ethanol** | From **sugarcane juice, molasses, grains (maize, broken rice)** by **fermentation** (yeast) | **Ethanol Blending Programme** |
| **2G ethanol** | From **crop residue** (rice straw, stubble), cellulose | **PM JI-VAN Yojana** (2019); **Panipat 2G plant** (IOCL, 2022) — also reduces stubble burning |
| **Biodiesel** | From non-edible oils (**jatropha**, karanja), used cooking oil | **RUCO** (Repurpose Used Cooking Oil) initiative |
| **Sustainable aviation fuel (SAF)** | Bio-based jet fuel | Blending targets announced for international flights (1% by 2027, check latest) |

- **National Policy on Biofuels, 2018** (amended **2022**): **20% ethanol blending in petrol (E20) target advanced to 2025-26** (from 2030). India reached **about 20% blending in 2025** (check latest). Benefits: lower oil imports, income for farmers, lower emissions. Concerns: food-vs-fuel (rice/maize diverted), water use of sugarcane, older vehicles' compatibility.
- **Global Biofuels Alliance** launched by India at the **G20 New Delhi summit (September 2023)**.

> **Bihar connection:** **Bihar was the first state to bring out an Ethanol Production Promotion Policy (2021)** after the Centre allowed ethanol from grains and sugarcane juice. **India's first greenfield grain-based ethanol plant was inaugurated at Purnia (2022)**, using **maize** — Bihar is a major **rabi maize** producer (Kosi–Seemanchal belt). Several more ethanol units have come up (Gopalganj, Begusarai, Bhojpur, Nalanda, etc. — check latest). Bihar is also the birthplace of **Husk Power Systems** (started around **2008 in West Champaran**), which used **rice-husk gasifiers** to run **village mini-grids** — a famous rural energy innovation. And the **Barauni refinery** (IOCL) is being expanded, with a **gas-based Barauni fertiliser plant** (HURL) revived.

## Green hydrogen

- **Hydrogen** burns or reacts in a **fuel cell** to give energy with **only water** as by-product. It has the **highest energy per kg** of any fuel.
- **Colours of hydrogen**: **grey** (from natural gas, CO₂ released — most hydrogen today), **blue** (from gas with carbon capture), **green** (by **electrolysis of water** using **renewable electricity** — no emissions), **brown/black** (from coal), **pink** (nuclear-powered electrolysis), **turquoise** (methane pyrolysis).
- **National Green Hydrogen Mission** — approved **January 2023**, outlay about **₹19,744 crore**; target **5 million tonnes (MMT) green hydrogen per year by 2030**, with ~125 GW of new renewable capacity; **SIGHT** programme (incentives for electrolysers and green hydrogen production); green hydrogen hubs; uses — fertiliser (green ammonia), refineries, steel, shipping, heavy transport (check latest).
- **Green Hydrogen Standard** (2023): defines "green" as ≤ 2 kg CO₂ per kg H₂.
- Challenges: high cost of electrolysers and renewable power, water needs, storage and transport (hydrogen is very light and leaks easily), safety.

| Stage | Reactor | Status (as of 2026 — check latest) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs)** — natural uranium fuel, **heavy water** as moderator and coolant | Mature; India's indigenous **700 MWe PHWRs** (first at **Kakrapar, Gujarat — KAPP-3**, 2021) are the "fleet mode" workhorse |
| **2** | **Fast Breeder Reactors** — plutonium-based fuel (MOX), **liquid sodium** coolant; "breed" more fissile material than they use, and convert thorium to **U-233** | **Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR, 1985)** at Kalpakkam; **Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR, 500 MWe), Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu)** — **core loading began March 2024**; first criticality reported later (check latest); built by **BHAVINI** |
| **3** | **Thorium-based reactors** (U-233 + Th-232), e.g. **Advanced Heavy Water Reactor (AHWR)** | Design/research stage at **BARC** |

### India's nuclear power facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Atomic Energy Commission | **1948** (Bhabha first chairman); **Department of Atomic Energy (DAE)** 1954, under the PM |
| First research reactor | **Apsara** (1956, Trombay) — first in Asia |
| **First nuclear power station** | **Tarapur (Maharashtra), 1969** — boiling water reactors built with US help |
| **Largest nuclear power plant** | **Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu)** — **with Russia**, VVER-1000 reactors; more units under construction |
| Other plants | Rawatbhata (Rajasthan), Kaiga (Karnataka), Kakrapar (Gujarat), Narora (UP), Kalpakkam (Madras Atomic Power Station, TN); new — Gorakhpur (Haryana), Mahi Banswara (Rajasthan), Kovvada and Jaitapur (planned with foreign partners) |
| Operator | **NPCIL** (Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd); **BHAVINI** for fast breeders; NTPC joint venture **ASHVINI** (2024) |
| Regulator | **AERB** (Atomic Energy Regulatory Board, 1983) |
| Uranium mining | **UCIL** — **Jaduguda (Jharkhand)**, Tummalapalle (Andhra Pradesh — among the largest reserves) |
| Liability law | **Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010** |
| **Nuclear Energy Mission** (Budget 2025–26) | Target **100 GW nuclear capacity by 2047**; **₹20,000 crore** for **Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)** — at least 5 indigenous SMRs by 2033; **Bharat Small Reactors** (220 MW PHWR-based) with private participation |
| Reform law | A new law in **December 2025** (the **SHANTI Act** — Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India) opened nuclear power to private companies and revised liability (as of 2026 — check details) |
| Fusion | India is a member of **ITER** (France); **Institute for Plasma Research (Gandhinagar)** — Aditya and SST-1 tokamaks |

**Pros of nuclear**: very low carbon, steady base-load power, small land need. **Cons**: high cost and long construction, radioactive waste, accident risk (**Chernobyl 1986**, **Fukushima 2011**), public opposition (Kudankulam protests), liability concerns.

## Electric mobility and energy storage

- **Electric vehicles (EVs)**: use **lithium-ion batteries**; no tailpipe emissions (but grid electricity may be coal-based). **Two- and three-wheelers** (e-rickshaws in Bihar's towns) lead adoption.
- Schemes: **FAME-I (2015), FAME-II (2019)**, **PM E-DRIVE (2024)** (subsidies for e-2W, e-3W, e-buses, charging stations), **PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) batteries** (2021), **PLI for automobiles**, **PM e-Bus Sewa** (10,000 e-buses for cities — Bihar's cities included; check latest). EV target often cited: **30% of new vehicle sales by 2030**.
- **Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)**: store solar power for night use; **Viability Gap Funding for BESS** (2024); mandatory storage with new solar bids in many tenders.
- Battery chemistries: lithium-ion (lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite), **sodium-ion** (cheaper, no lithium — emerging), solid-state. India's **critical minerals**: 30 critical minerals identified (2023); **National Critical Mineral Mission** (2025); lithium finds in **Reasi (J&K)** (2023) and Karnataka; **KABIL** acquires mines abroad (Argentina lithium blocks).
- **Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022** — extended producer responsibility (EPR) for battery makers.
- **Hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles**, **CNG/LNG** trucks, **flex-fuel** vehicles (run on any petrol–ethanol mix) — under trials.
- Railways: **100% electrification** of broad-gauge network nearly complete (as of 2025–26 — check); **Vande Bharat** trains; first **hydrogen train** trials (Jind–Sonipat, Haryana — check latest).

## India's climate and energy commitments

| Commitment | Details (as of 2026 — check latest) |
|---|---|
| **National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)** (2008) | **8 national missions**: Solar, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture, Strategic Knowledge on Climate Change; states have **State Action Plans** (Bihar's SAPCC) |

| **Green Credit Programme** (2023) | Market-based credits for actions like tree planting, water conservation |
| **Long-term low-emission strategy** | Submitted at COP27 (2022) |
| **Climate finance** | COP29 (Baku, 2024) agreed a new goal (US$300 billion a year by 2035 from developed countries) — India criticised it as too low; COP30 in **Belém, Brazil (November 2025)** (check outcomes) |

> **Mains angle:** India argues for **"common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities" (CBDR-RC)** and **climate justice** — its **per-capita emissions are far below the world average** even though it is the third-largest total emitter. Mention this balance in any climate answer.

## Environment technologies

### Clean air

| Technology / policy | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Electrostatic precipitator (ESP)** | Removes fly ash/dust from power-plant chimneys |
| **Flue-gas desulphurisation (FGD)** | Removes **SO₂** from coal plant exhaust (deadlines repeatedly extended — check latest) |
| **Catalytic converter** | Turns CO, NOx and hydrocarbons into CO₂, N₂ and water (platinum, palladium, rhodium) |
| **Bharat Stage VI (BS-VI)** | Vehicle emission norms equal to Euro-VI; India **leapfrogged from BS-IV to BS-VI on 1 April 2020** |
| **National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)** (**2019**) | Target **20–30% reduction in PM** by 2024 (revised to **40% by 2025–26** vs 2017 levels) in 130+ non-attainment cities — **Patna, Gaya, Muzaffarpur** are included |
| **AQI** (Air Quality Index) | Launched 2014; 8 pollutants; categories Good → Severe; CPCB's **SAMEER** app |
| **Smog towers, anti-smog guns** | Local measures (limited effect) |
| **Zig-zag brick kiln technology** | Cuts fuel use and emissions — important for **Bihar, which has thousands of brick kilns** |
| **Ujjwala (PMUY)** (2016) | LPG connections to poor households — reduces indoor smoke from chulhas (launched at **Ballia, UP**) |
| **Happy Seeder / bio-decomposer (Pusa decomposer)** | Alternatives to **stubble burning** |
| **CAQM** | Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR (2021) |

### Clean water

- **Sewage treatment plants (STPs)**, **common effluent treatment plants (CETPs)**, **zero liquid discharge (ZLD)** in industries.
- **Namami Gange** (2014) — STPs in Ganga towns, including **Patna, Bhagalpur, Munger, Buxar, Begusarai, Hajipur** (check progress); river-front development; **Ganga Gram**.
- Drinking water: **RO, UV, ultrafiltration**, **arsenic and fluoride removal plants**, **chlorination**, solar-powered water purification, **Jal Jeevan Mission (2019)** — tap water to every rural home, with **Field Test Kits** used by trained village women.
- **Desalination** for coastal cities (Chennai).
- **Rainwater harvesting**, **wastewater reuse**, **micro-irrigation** (drip and sprinkler — "Per Drop More Crop").

### Waste management

| Rule / technique | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016** | Segregation at source (wet/dry/hazardous), user fees |
| **Plastic Waste Management Rules** (2016, amended 2021–24) | **Ban on identified single-use plastics from 1 July 2022**; carry bags < 120 microns banned; **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** |
| **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022** | EPR targets for producers |
| **Biomedical Waste Rules, 2016** | Colour-coded bins (yellow, red, white, blue) |
| **Composting, vermicomposting, biomethanation** | Wet waste → manure, biogas |
| **Waste-to-energy, RDF** (refuse-derived fuel) | For non-recyclable dry waste, used in cement kilns |
| **Swachh Bharat Mission** (2 October 2014) | Toilets (ODF), Urban 2.0 for "garbage-free" cities; **Swachh Survekshan** rankings |
| **Circular economy** | Reduce, reuse, recycle, repair; **Vehicle scrappage policy** (2021) |

### Carbon capture and climate technology

- **CCUS** (Carbon Capture, Utilisation and Storage): capturing CO₂ from power plants or industry and storing it underground or using it (in concrete, chemicals). India released a CCUS policy framework/roadmap (NITI Aayog, 2022).
- **Direct air capture**, **biochar** (charcoal from biomass added to soil — stores carbon and improves fertility).
- **Green steel** (hydrogen-based), **green cement**.
- **Climate-resilient agriculture** (see the Bihar chapter).
- **Early-warning systems**: IMD's Doppler weather radars, impact-based forecasting, **Mission Mausam** (2024) to make India "weather-ready and climate-smart".

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacity factor | Actual output as a share of the maximum possible |
| Photovoltaic | Turning light directly into electricity |
| Inverter | Device converting DC to AC |
| Pumped storage | Storing energy by pumping water uphill |
| Cogeneration | Producing electricity and heat together (e.g. bagasse in sugar mills) |

### Mains questions

**1. "Energy transition must be just and inclusive." Discuss India's renewable energy strategy and its relevance for Bihar.** (38 marks / about 500 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India's Panchamrit and NDC; non-fossil capacity crossing 50% in 2025; coal still dominant in generation.
- **Strategy:** solar parks and rooftop (PM Surya Ghar), PM-KUSUM, wind and hybrid, storage (BESS, pumped hydro), green hydrogen, nuclear expansion (SMRs), ethanol and CBG, energy efficiency (UJALA, BEE).
- **Just transition:** coal-dependent regions and workers (Jharkhand next door), affordable power for the poor, local manufacturing jobs, skill development (Suryamitra).
- **Bihar relevance:** high dependence on central coal plants; low per-capita consumption; suited to rooftop, floating and agri-voltaic solar; maize-based ethanol and biogas (GOBARdhan, Jeevika); rice-husk mini-grids (Husk Power); solar pumps for irrigation reduce diesel use; e-rickshaws.
- **Challenges:** land scarcity, DISCOM finances, grid integration, storage costs, finance.
- **Way forward:** state renewable policy targets, rooftop on all public buildings, net metering, women-led solar enterprises, research at IIT Patna and agricultural universities.
- **Conclusion:** a green transition can give Bihar cleaner air, farmer income and jobs if it is planned locally.

**2. Explain India's three-stage nuclear power programme. Can nuclear energy help India reach net zero by 2070?** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bhabha's plan due to limited uranium and abundant thorium.
- **Stages:** PHWR → FBR (PFBR Kalpakkam) → thorium reactors (AHWR).
- **Current status:** ~2% of capacity; 700 MWe PHWR fleet; Kudankulam; Nuclear Energy Mission (100 GW by 2047, SMRs); legal reforms opening to private participation (check details).
- **For net zero:** steady low-carbon base load to balance solar/wind; industrial heat and hydrogen production.
- **Challenges:** cost, delays, liability, waste, safety perception, uranium imports, skilled manpower.
- **Conclusion:** nuclear is a necessary complement to renewables; success depends on timely execution and public trust.

**3. Discuss the role of technology in tackling air pollution in Indian cities, with special reference to cities of Bihar.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Patna, Gaya and Muzaffarpur under NCAP; winter PM2.5 spikes.
- **Sources:** road dust, vehicles, construction, brick kilns, biomass and waste burning, regional pollution in the Indo-Gangetic plain.
- **Technology:** real-time monitors and AQI apps, satellite-based pollution mapping, BS-VI vehicles and e-buses/e-rickshaws, CNG, zig-zag kilns, mechanical road sweepers and water sprinklers, Ujjwala LPG, waste processing, green belts.
- **Governance:** city clean-air action plans, Bihar State Pollution Control Board, public awareness, health advisories.
- **Conclusion:** technology works only with enforcement and regional (airshed) cooperation.
