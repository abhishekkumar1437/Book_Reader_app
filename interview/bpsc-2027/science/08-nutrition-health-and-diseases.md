It also has a strong Bihar angle — **kala-azar**, **AES (chamki bukhar)**, **Japanese encephalitis**, **anaemia** and **child malnutrition** — useful for Mains GS II.

> **In simple words:** Food gives us energy (carbohydrates, fats), body-building material (proteins) and small "protective" items (vitamins, minerals). Missing any one causes a deficiency disease. Many other diseases are caused by germs — bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi and worms — often carried by mosquitoes, flies, water or air. Vaccines train the body to fight germs before they attack.

## Nutrients and a balanced diet

| Nutrient | Main job | Sources | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | Main energy source | Rice, wheat, maize, potato, sugar | ~4 kcal/g |
| **Fats** | Concentrated energy, insulation, absorption of vitamins A, D, E, K | Oil, ghee, butter, nuts | **~9 kcal/g** (highest) |
| **Proteins** | **Body building**, repair, enzymes, hormones, antibodies | Pulses (dal), milk, eggs, meat, fish, soybean (~40% protein — richest plant source) | ~4 kcal/g |
| **Vitamins** | Protective; regulate body processes | Fruits, vegetables, milk | 0 |
| **Minerals** | Bones, blood, nerves, enzymes | Milk, green vegetables, salt | 0 |
| **Water** | ~60–70% of body; transport, temperature control | — | 0 |
| **Roughage (dietary fibre)** | Prevents **constipation**; helps digestion | Whole grains, vegetables, fruits, salad (cellulose) | 0 |

- A **balanced diet** has all nutrients in the right amounts. The ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (**NIN, Hyderabad**) publishes India's dietary guidelines (latest 2024 edition — "My Plate for the Day").
- **Calorie** in nutrition means **kilocalorie** (kcal). A moderately active adult needs roughly **2,000–2,700 kcal a day** (varies by sex, age, work).
- **Tests**: starch turns **blue-black with iodine**; protein gives **violet** with copper sulphate + caustic soda (biuret test); fat leaves a **translucent spot on paper**.
- **Essential amino acids** (9 for adults) must come from food. Cereal + pulse together (**dal-chawal, khichdi, roti-dal**) give a complete protein — a scientific basis for traditional Indian meals.

## Vitamins

The word "**vitamine**" was coined by **Casimir Funk (1912)**. Vitamins are of two types:
- **Fat-soluble — A, D, E, K**: stored in the body (liver, fat); excess can be toxic.
- **Water-soluble — B-complex and C**: not stored much; excess is passed out in urine, so they are needed daily. They are **easily lost in cooking** (washing cut vegetables, throwing away rice water, overcooking).

> **Memory trick:** "**ADEK** is fat; **B and C** go swimming" (water-soluble).

<div class="formula">

| Vitamin | Chemical name | Main sources | Deficiency disease / effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Retinol** | Carrot, papaya, mango, green leafy vegetables, milk, egg yolk, **fish-liver oil**; **β-carotene** in plants is converted to vitamin A | **Night blindness**, **xerophthalmia** (dry eyes), Bitot's spots, dry skin; leading cause of preventable childhood blindness |
| **B₁** | **Thiamine** | Whole grains, unpolished rice, pulses, yeast | **Beriberi** (nerve and heart problems; common where **polished rice** is the staple) |
| **B₂** | **Riboflavin** | Milk, eggs, green vegetables | **Cracked lips and corners of mouth (cheilosis)**, sore tongue (glossitis), skin problems |
| **B₃** | **Niacin** (nicotinic acid) | Groundnut, meat, whole grains | **Pellagra** — the **"3 Ds": dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia** (common where maize is the staple) |
| **B₅** | Pantothenic acid | Most foods | Burning feet (rare) |
| **B₆** | **Pyridoxine** | Cereals, meat, banana | Anaemia, convulsions, nerve problems |
| **B₇** | **Biotin** (vitamin H) | Eggs, liver | Hair loss, skin problems |
| **B₉** | **Folic acid** (folate) | Green leafy vegetables, pulses, liver | **Megaloblastic anaemia**; in pregnancy → **neural tube defects** in the baby (so pregnant women get **iron-folic acid (IFA) tablets**) |
| **B₁₂** | **Cyanocobalamin** (contains **cobalt**) | **Only animal foods** — milk, meat, eggs, fish (and fortified foods) | **Pernicious anaemia**, nerve damage; common in strict vegetarians |
| **C** | **Ascorbic acid** | **Amla** (Indian gooseberry — one of the richest sources), citrus fruits, guava, tomato, sprouts | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums, slow wound healing); **destroyed by heat**; helps iron absorption |
| **D** | **Calciferol** (D₃ cholecalciferol) | **Sunlight on skin** ("sunshine vitamin"), fish-liver oil, egg yolk, fortified milk | **Rickets** in children (bow legs), **osteomalacia** in adults (soft bones) |
| **E** | **Tocopherol** | Vegetable oils, nuts, wheat germ | Reduced fertility (in animals), nerve and muscle problems; an **antioxidant** ("anti-sterility / beauty vitamin") |
| **K** | **Phylloquinone** | Green leafy vegetables; **made by gut bacteria** | **Delayed blood clotting** (haemorrhage); newborns get a vitamin K injection at birth |

</div>

> **Prelims angle:** Favourite pairs — **A → night blindness**, **B₁ → beriberi**, **B₃ → pellagra**, **C → scurvy**, **D → rickets**, **K → clotting**. Also: vitamin **B₁₂ contains cobalt**; vitamin **D is a hormone-like vitamin made in skin**; **vitamin C is destroyed by cooking**; **vitamin K is made by intestinal bacteria**.

## Minerals

| Mineral | Function | Sources | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium** | Bones, teeth, clotting, muscle contraction | Milk, curd, ragi, green leaves | Rickets, osteoporosis, weak teeth; tetany |
| Phosphorus | Bones, teeth, ATP, DNA | Milk, pulses, meat | Weak bones |
| **Iron** | Part of **haemoglobin** (oxygen transport) | Green leafy vegetables, jaggery (gur), dates, liver, meat, pulses | **Iron-deficiency anaemia** — tiredness, pale skin, breathlessness |
| **Iodine** | Makes **thyroxine** | **Iodised salt**, sea food | **Goitre**, cretinism in children, mental retardation |
| Sodium | Water balance, nerve impulses | Common salt | Muscle cramps; excess → high BP |
| Potassium | Nerve and muscle function | Banana, potato, coconut water | Muscle weakness |
| **Fluorine** | Strong teeth enamel | Water, tea | Too little → tooth decay; **too much → fluorosis** |
| Zinc | Immunity, growth, wound healing | Meat, pulses, nuts | Stunted growth, diarrhoea (zinc + ORS treats childhood diarrhoea) |
| Magnesium | Enzymes; part of chlorophyll | Green vegetables, nuts | Muscle cramps |
| Cobalt | Part of vitamin B₁₂ | Animal foods | Anaemia |
| Copper | Iron use, enzymes | Nuts, liver | Anaemia |
| Selenium | Antioxidant | Grains, seafood | Heart muscle disease (Keshan disease) |

## Malnutrition

**Malnutrition** means wrong nutrition — both **undernutrition** (too little) and **overnutrition** (too much, obesity).

| Condition | Cause | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Kwashiorkor** | **Protein deficiency** (enough calories) — typically after weaning | **Swollen belly and legs (oedema)**, thin limbs, discoloured hair, "moon face" |
| **Marasmus** | Deficiency of **both protein and calories** (energy) — usually in infants | **Extremely thin**, "skin and bones", wrinkled skin, no oedema |
| **Stunting** | Long-term (chronic) undernutrition | **Low height for age** |
| **Wasting** | Acute (recent) undernutrition | **Low weight for height** |
| **Underweight** | Combined | **Low weight for age** |
| Obesity | Excess energy intake, little activity | High BMI (≥ 25 overweight, ≥ 30 obese by WHO; India uses lower Asian cut-offs) |

**BMI (Body Mass Index)** = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]².

### Worked example — BMI

A man weighs 72 kg and is 1.70 m tall. BMI = 72 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 72 ÷ 2.89 ≈ **24.9** — at the upper edge of "normal" by WHO (18.5–24.9), but "overweight" by the lower Asian Indian cut-off (≥ 23).

### "Hidden hunger"

**Micronutrient deficiency** (vitamin A, iron, iodine, zinc, folate) is called **hidden hunger** because the person may get enough calories. India addresses it with **food fortification** — **fortified rice** (iron, folic acid, B₁₂) distributed through PDS, ICDS and PM POSHAN (supplying fortified rice across government schemes was completed in phases by **2024** and extended up to 2028 — check latest), iodised salt, double-fortified salt (iodine + iron), and fortified oil and milk ("+F" logo by FSSAI).

> **Bihar connection:** **NFHS-5 (2019–21)** showed Bihar with among the **highest child stunting (about 43%)** and **underweight (about 41%)** rates in India, and very high **anaemia among women and children** (well over 60% of women aged 15–49 — figures as reported in NFHS-5; check the NFHS-6 release for updates). Programmes: **ICDS / anganwadi** (Poshan Tracker), **PM POSHAN** midday meals, **POSHAN Abhiyaan** (launched March 2018 at **Jhunjhunu, Rajasthan**), **Anaemia Mukt Bharat** (2018, "6×6×6" strategy), **Mission Indradhanush**, **Jeevika**-led nutrition and kitchen-garden initiatives, and **Bihar's poshak/cycle schemes** for school girls (indirectly improving schooling and nutrition). These make excellent Mains content.

## Health and disease — basic ideas

- WHO defines health as "**a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity**". **World Health Day: 7 April** (WHO founded **1948**, HQ **Geneva**).
- **Communicable (infectious) diseases** spread from person to person through germs (**pathogens**). **Non-communicable diseases (NCDs)** do not spread (diabetes, cancer, heart disease).
- **Acute** diseases are short (cold, typhoid); **chronic** ones last long (TB, diabetes).
- **Epidemic**: sudden spread in a region (e.g. dengue outbreak in Patna). **Pandemic**: spread across countries/continents (**COVID-19**, declared by WHO on **11 March 2020**; the **Spanish flu** of 1918). **Endemic**: always present in a region (**kala-azar in Bihar**, malaria in tribal belts).
- **Incubation period**: time between infection and the first symptoms.
- **Germ theory of disease**: **Louis Pasteur** and **Robert Koch**. Koch discovered the bacteria of **anthrax**, **tuberculosis (1882)** and **cholera (1883)**. **World TB Day: 24 March** (the day Koch announced his TB discovery).

## Viral diseases

<div class="formula">

| Disease | Virus / note | Spread | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polio (poliomyelitis)** | Poliovirus (enterovirus) | Faecal-oral (water, food) | Attacks the **nervous system** → paralysis; vaccines **OPV (Sabin, oral)** and **IPV (Salk, injected)**; India's last wild polio case: **Howrah, West Bengal, 13 January 2011**; WHO certified India **polio-free on 27 March 2014**; **Pulse Polio** drives since 1995 |
| **Measles** | Morbillivirus | Air | Rash, fever; **MR (measles–rubella) vaccine** |
| Rubella (German measles) | Rubella virus | Air | Dangerous in pregnancy (birth defects) |
| Mumps | Paramyxovirus | Saliva | **Parotid salivary glands** swell |

| **Smallpox** | Variola | Air, contact | **Eradicated** — WHO declared global eradication **8 May 1980**; India free in 1977 (last case 1975); first vaccine by Jenner (1796) |
| **Rabies (hydrophobia)** | Rabies virus (lyssavirus) | **Bite of infected dogs**, cats, monkeys, bats | Nervous system; **almost 100% fatal once symptoms appear**; post-exposure vaccine; **Pasteur** made the first rabies vaccine (1885); **World Rabies Day: 28 September** (Pasteur's death anniversary) |

| **Hepatitis A, E** | Hepatitis viruses | **Contaminated water/food** | **Liver** (jaundice); hep E dangerous in pregnancy |
| **Hepatitis B, C** | Hepatitis viruses | **Blood**, sex, mother to child | Can cause liver cirrhosis and cancer; **Hep B vaccine** at birth; hep C curable by new drugs |
| **Dengue** ("**breakbone fever**") | Dengue virus (4 serotypes) | ***Aedes aegypti*** mosquito (bites **by day**, breeds in **clean stagnant water** — coolers, tyres, pots) | High fever, severe joint pain, **fall in platelets**, haemorrhagic fever; no specific drug; vaccine development in India (check latest); **National Dengue Day: 16 May** |
| **Chikungunya** | Chikungunya virus | ***Aedes*** mosquito | Severe, long-lasting joint pain |
| **Zika** | Zika virus | *Aedes* mosquito | **Microcephaly** (small head) in babies of infected mothers |
| **Japanese encephalitis (JE)** | JE virus (flavivirus) | ***Culex*** mosquito; **pigs** and water birds are reservoir hosts | **Brain inflammation**; affects eastern UP (Gorakhpur) and north Bihar; **JE vaccine** in UIP for endemic districts |
| **Influenza (flu)** | Influenza viruses — **H1N1 ("swine flu")**, **H5N1 ("bird flu")** | Air | Fever, cough |
| **COVID-19** | **SARS-CoV-2** (coronavirus) | Droplets/air | First identified in **Wuhan, China (Dec 2019)**; India's first case **30 January 2020 (Kerala)**; vaccines **Covishield** (Oxford–AstraZeneca, made by Serum Institute) and **Covaxin** (indigenous, **Bharat Biotech + ICMR**); **ZyCoV-D** (Zydus) — **world's first DNA vaccine** for humans; **CoWIN** platform; over 220 crore doses given |
| Common cold | Rhinovirus | Air | Mild |
| Ebola, Nipah | Ebola virus; **Nipah** (from **fruit bats**) | Body fluids; bats/pigs | Very high fatality; Nipah outbreaks in **Kerala** |
| Mpox (monkeypox) | Mpox virus | Close contact | WHO declared public health emergency (2022, again 2024) |
| HPV infection | Human papillomavirus | Sexual contact | Causes **cervical cancer**; **Cervavac** — India's first indigenous HPV vaccine (Serum Institute, 2022) |
| Rotavirus diarrhoea | Rotavirus | Faecal-oral | Leading cause of severe diarrhoea in infants; **Rotavac** — indigenous vaccine (Bharat Biotech) in UIP |

</div>

> **Common mistake:** **Antibiotics do not work against viruses.** Taking antibiotics for a cold or flu is useless and creates antibiotic resistance. Viral diseases are treated with antivirals (for a few), supportive care and prevented by vaccines.

## Protozoan diseases

| Disease | Protozoan | Vector / spread | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaria** | ***Plasmodium*** (*P. vivax* most common in India, ***P. falciparum*** most dangerous — cerebral malaria) | Bite of **female *Anopheles*** mosquito (bites at night; breeds in clean water) | Fever with **chills at regular intervals**; parasite multiplies in **liver** and **RBCs**; **Ronald Ross** proved mosquito transmission working in **Secunderabad (1897)** — Nobel 1902; drugs: quinine, chloroquine, **artemisinin**; India aims to **eliminate malaria by 2030**; vaccines RTS,S and R21 (WHO-approved); **World Malaria Day: 25 April** |
| **Kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis)** | ***Leishmania donovani*** | Bite of **sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*) | "**Black fever**" — fever, weight loss, **enlarged spleen and liver**, darkened skin; endemic in **Bihar**, Jharkhand, West Bengal, UP; treatment **single-dose liposomal amphotericin B**, miltefosine; spraying of insecticide; post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL) |
| **Amoebic dysentery** (amoebiasis) | ***Entamoeba histolytica*** | Contaminated food/water; **house flies** carry cysts | Blood and mucus in stool |
| Giardiasis | *Giardia* | Water | Diarrhoea |
| **African sleeping sickness** | *Trypanosoma* | **Tsetse fly** | Africa |
| Oriental sore (cutaneous leishmaniasis) | *Leishmania tropica* | Sandfly | Skin |
| Toxoplasmosis | *Toxoplasma* | Cats, undercooked meat | Dangerous in pregnancy |

> **Bihar connection:** **Kala-azar** was historically concentrated in Bihar — the state accounted for the large majority of India's cases. India achieved the WHO **elimination target (fewer than 1 case per 10,000 population at block level)** in all endemic blocks around **2023**, and needs to sustain it for three years to be certified (check latest status). Tools used: **rapid rK39 test kits**, single-dose **liposomal amphotericin B**, **indoor residual spraying** (shift from DDT to synthetic pyrethroids), pucca houses that leave fewer cracks for sandflies, active case search by **ASHAs**, and cash incentives for patients. This is a model Mains example of S&T + public health in Bihar.

### Acute Encephalitis Syndrome (AES) and Muzaffarpur

**AES** (locally called **"chamki bukhar"**) is a group of symptoms — high fever with brain involvement (confusion, seizures, coma). In **Muzaffarpur** and nearby north Bihar districts, outbreaks among young children occur in **May–June** (litchi season), with a major outbreak in **2019**. Studies (e.g. by CDC and Indian researchers, 2014–2017) linked many cases to **low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)** in **undernourished children** who **skipped the evening meal** and ate **unripe litchis**, which contain the toxins **hypoglycin A** and **MCPG** (methylene cyclopropyl glycine) that block glucose production. Other cases are caused by **Japanese encephalitis** and other infections. Prevention: **feeding children a night meal**, early glucose correction (10% dextrose) at PHCs, awareness through ASHAs and anganwadis, **JE vaccination**, paediatric ICUs (SKMCH Muzaffarpur), and the state's **SOP for AES**.

> **Mains angle:** AES shows how **nutrition, agriculture (litchi), poverty and health infrastructure** interact — a perfect GS II S&T + Bihar answer. Never write that "litchi causes AES" alone; write "toxins in unripe litchi plus missed meals and malnutrition cause hypoglycaemic encephalopathy in vulnerable children".

## Vaccines and immunisation

A **vaccine** contains a killed or weakened germ, or a part of it (protein, toxoid, mRNA), that trains the immune system to make **antibodies and memory cells** without causing the disease — **active immunity**.

| Vaccine type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Live attenuated (weakened) | **BCG**, **OPV**, measles, MR, rotavirus, yellow fever, chickenpox |
| Inactivated (killed) | **IPV** (Salk), rabies (cell-culture), **Covaxin**, hepatitis A |
| Toxoid (inactivated toxin) | **Tetanus**, **diphtheria** |
| Subunit / recombinant | **Hepatitis B**, HPV, Corbevax (protein subunit COVID vaccine) |
| Conjugate | **Hib**, **PCV**, typhoid conjugate |
| Viral vector | **Covishield** (adenovirus vector) |
| **mRNA** | Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna (COVID-19); **Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman — Nobel Medicine 2023**; India's indigenous mRNA vaccine **GEMCOVAC** (Gennova, Pune) |
| DNA | **ZyCoV-D** (world's first DNA vaccine for humans, India 2021) |

### Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP)

- India's **Expanded Programme on Immunisation** began in **1978**; it became the **Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP) in 1985**. It is one of the world's largest public health programmes.
- **Mission Indradhanush** (launched **25 December 2014**) aims to reach unvaccinated and partially vaccinated children and pregnant women; **Intensified Mission Indradhanush (IMI)** rounds followed.
- **U-WIN** portal (2023–24) digitally records every vaccination (like CoWIN for routine immunisation).
- UIP covers vaccines against about **12 diseases** nationally (some only in endemic areas): **TB (BCG), polio (OPV + fIPV), hepatitis B, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus (pentavalent: DPT + Hep B + Hib), Hib, measles, rubella (MR), rotavirus, pneumococcal pneumonia (PCV), Japanese encephalitis** (endemic districts) — check for additions such as HPV for girls (announced for rollout — check latest).

| Age | Main vaccines (simplified) |
|---|---|
| At birth | **BCG**, **OPV-0**, **Hepatitis B birth dose** |
| 6, 10, 14 weeks | OPV 1-2-3, **Pentavalent** 1-2-3, **Rotavirus**, **fIPV** (6 & 14 weeks), **PCV** (6 & 14 weeks) |
| 9–12 months | **MR-1**, JE-1 (endemic), PCV booster, vitamin A (1st dose) |
| 16–24 months | MR-2, JE-2, DPT booster-1, OPV booster |
| 5–6 years | DPT booster-2 |
| 10 and 16 years | Td (tetanus–diphtheria) |
| Pregnant women | Td (2 doses or booster) |

### Mains questions

**1. Bihar has made major progress against kala-azar. Explain the role of science and technology in this achievement and the challenges in sustaining it.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis), *Leishmania donovani*, sandfly vector; Bihar historically had most of India's cases.
- **Diagnosis:** rK39 rapid diagnostic kits replaced painful spleen/bone-marrow tests; early detection in PHCs.
- **Treatment:** move from long, toxic antimonial injections to single-dose liposomal amphotericin B and oral miltefosine; free drugs.
- **Vector control:** indoor residual spraying with synthetic pyrethroids; insecticide-resistance monitoring; better housing (PMAY) reduces sandfly habitat.
- **Research and monitoring:** ICMR-RMRIMS Patna, KalaCORE/partners, GIS mapping of hotspots, real-time case data.
- **Community:** ASHA incentives, wage compensation for patients, awareness.
- **Challenges:** PKDL cases as hidden reservoirs, HIV co-infection, migrant workers, sustaining surveillance for certification, funding fatigue.
- **Conclusion:** a model of evidence-based public health; must be sustained until WHO certification and beyond.

**2. "Malnutrition in Bihar is a development problem, not just a health problem." Discuss and suggest science-based solutions.** (about 300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** NFHS-5 shows high stunting, underweight and anaemia in Bihar (cite as "about 43% stunting" with "check NFHS-6").
- **Causes:** poverty, low dietary diversity, poor sanitation and repeated infections (diarrhoea, worms), early marriage, low maternal education, flood-related food insecurity.
- **Consequences:** lower learning, productivity and income; intergenerational cycle; demographic dividend at risk.
- **Science-based solutions:** fortified rice and double-fortified salt; biofortified crops (zinc wheat, iron-rich pearl millet — varieties by ICAR); IFA supplementation and deworming; growth monitoring through Poshan Tracker; WASH (Swachh Bharat, Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal).
- **Local food systems:** millets, pulses, makhana, kitchen gardens via Jeevika; egg in midday meals (debate), dairy and fisheries.
- **Governance:** convergence of ICDS, health, agriculture and water departments; data-driven targeting of districts.
- **Conclusion:** multi-sector, evidence-based action can break the malnutrition cycle.

**3. Explain how vaccines work. Assess India's achievements in immunisation and the remaining gaps.** (about 250 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** vaccines create active immunity and memory; herd immunity.
- **Types:** live, inactivated, toxoid, subunit, vector, mRNA, DNA (ZyCoV-D).
- **Achievements:** smallpox (1977/1980), polio-free 2014, MNT elimination 2015, UIP expansion (rotavirus, PCV, MR), COVID-19 vaccination with CoWIN, U-WIN; India as a major vaccine maker ("pharmacy of the world").
- **Gaps:** zero-dose children, urban slums, migrant families, vaccine hesitancy, cold-chain issues in remote and flood-hit areas.
- **Technology:** eVIN (electronic vaccine intelligence network), drones for delivery (ICMR trials), solar fridges.
- **Conclusion:** last-mile coverage and digital tracking are the next frontier.
