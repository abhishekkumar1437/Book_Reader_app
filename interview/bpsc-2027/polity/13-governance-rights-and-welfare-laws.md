GS Paper II Polity in BPSC is not only about the Constitution; it also asks how governance reaches citizens. This chapter covers the laws that give citizens practical rights — to **information**, to **education**, to **time-bound public services** and to **grievance redressal** — and the e-governance systems that deliver them. Bihar has strong examples here: it was among the first states with a **Right to Public Services Act (2011)**, the first with a **legal right to grievance redressal (2015)**, and an early adopter of **RTI by telephone (Jaankari, 2007)**.

> **In simple words:** Good governance means the government is transparent, accountable, quick and fair. RTI lets you ask for government information. RTE makes schooling a right for children aged 6–14. Citizen's charters and "right to service" laws promise services within fixed time limits, with penalties for late officers. E-governance puts services online so people do not have to visit offices or pay bribes.

## Good governance — the idea

- **Good governance** (World Bank, UNDP): **participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus orientation, equity and inclusiveness, effectiveness and efficiency, accountability**.
- In India: **Good Governance Day** is observed on **25 December** (birth anniversary of **Atal Bihari Vajpayee**, since 2014). The **Good Governance Index** is prepared by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG).
- **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2005–09)** — chaired first by **M. Veerappa Moily**, later **V. Ramachandran**; **15 reports**, including *Right to Information: Master Key to Good Governance* (1st), *Ethics in Governance* (4th), *Local Governance* (6th), *Promoting e-Governance — The SMART Way Forward* (11th), *Citizen Centric Administration* (12th). It recommended the **Sevottam** model for citizen's charters.
- Related ideas: **"Minimum government, maximum governance"**, **Mission Karmayogi** (2020, capacity building of civil servants), **lateral entry**, **social audit**, **e-office**.

## Right to Information Act, 2005

### Background

- Courts had already read the right to know into **Article 19(1)(a)** (*State of UP v Raj Narain, 1975*; *S.P. Gupta, 1981*).
- The **Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)** led by **Aruna Roy** in Rajasthan (1990s) pushed for transparency through **public hearings (jan sunwai)**.
- States went first: **Tamil Nadu and Goa (1997)**, Rajasthan, Karnataka, Delhi, Maharashtra and others. The central **Freedom of Information Act, 2002** was never notified.
- The **RTI Act** was passed in **May 2005**, notified on **15 June 2005**, and came **fully into force on 12 October 2005**.

### Key provisions

<div class="formula">

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who can ask | Any **citizen** of India |
| From whom | **Public authorities** — bodies under the Constitution or a law, or owned, controlled or substantially financed by the government, including NGOs substantially financed |
| Officer | **Public Information Officer (PIO)**; Assistant PIO at sub-division level |
| Fee | **₹10** (Central rules); **no fee for BPL** applicants |
| Time limit | **30 days**; **48 hours** if the matter concerns **life or liberty**; **35 days** if filed with an APIO; **40 days** if third-party information |
| Suo motu disclosure | **Section 4** — authorities must publish information proactively |
| Exemptions | **Section 8** — sovereignty, security, strategic and economic interests, foreign relations, contempt of court, breach of parliamentary privilege, commercial confidence, fiduciary relationship, foreign government information, danger to life, impeding investigation, cabinet papers (until decision), **personal information** (8(1)(j)). Information older than **20 years** is generally to be given (with some exceptions) |
| Excluded organisations | **Section 24** + Second Schedule — intelligence and security organisations (e.g. IB, RAW, CBI was added in 2011), **except** for information on **corruption and human rights violations** |
| First appeal | To an officer senior to the PIO within **30 days**; decided in 30 (max 45) days |
| Second appeal | To the **Central / State Information Commission** within **90 days** |
| Penalty | **₹250 per day** of delay, up to **₹25,000**, on the PIO (imposed by the Information Commission) |

</div>

### Information Commissions

- **Central Information Commission (CIC)** — **Chief Information Commissioner + up to 10 Information Commissioners**; appointed by the **President** on the recommendation of a committee of the **PM**, the **Leader of Opposition** in the Lok Sabha and a **Union Cabinet Minister** nominated by the PM.
- **State Information Commissions** — appointed by the **Governor** on a similar committee (CM, LoP, a minister). **Bihar State Information Commission** at Patna.
- **RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019** — the **term, salary and conditions** of the CIC and ICs (Centre and states) are now **fixed by the Central Government** (the rules set a **3-year term**); critics say this reduced independence.
- **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** — amended **Section 8(1)(j)** to exempt **all personal information** (removing the "larger public interest" test) — debated as a weakening of RTI.
- ***CPIO, Supreme Court v Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019)*** — the **office of the CJI** is a public authority under RTI (with safeguards for privacy and judicial independence).
- In **2013**, the CIC declared six national parties to be public authorities, but parties did not comply; the matter is before the SC.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar launched "**Jaankari**" in **January 2007** — a call centre through which citizens could **file RTI applications by telephone**, a first-of-its-kind innovation in India. The fee is added to the phone bill. Bihar also faced cases of **violence against RTI activists**, which is why the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014** and its implementation are relevant in Mains.

## Right to Education — Article 21A and the RTE Act, 2009

- **86th Amendment (2002)** — Article **21A**: free and compulsory education for children aged **6–14**.
- **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009** — in force from **1 April 2010**.

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Free and compulsory | Admission, attendance and completion of elementary education (classes **1–8**) in a neighbourhood school |
| **25% reservation** | Private **unaided** schools must admit **25%** of class 1 (or pre-primary) students from **disadvantaged groups and weaker sections**; the government reimburses (Section 12(1)(c)) |
| Banned | **Capitation fees**, **screening** of children or parents for admission, **physical punishment and mental harassment**, private tuition by teachers, running schools without recognition |
| Pupil–Teacher Ratio | **30:1** (primary), **35:1** (upper primary) |
| Teachers | Minimum qualifications — **Teacher Eligibility Test (TET)** |
| School Management Committees | 75% members parents/guardians; 50% women |
| **No-detention** | Originally no child held back till class 8; the **2019 amendment** allows states to hold **regular exams in classes 5 and 8** and detain after a re-exam (the Centre ended no-detention in its schools in December 2024) |
| Monitoring | **NCPCR** and SCPCRs |

- ***Society for Unaided Private Schools of Rajasthan (2012)*** upheld the 25% quota; ***Pramati Educational Trust (2014)*** exempted **minority schools** (aided and unaided) from the RTE Act.
- **National Education Policy 2020** — 5+3+3+4 structure, foundational literacy and numeracy (NIPUN Bharat), mother tongue up to class 5 where possible, and a target of universal access from age 3 to 18.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar has expanded school access with schemes like the **Mukhyamantri Balika Cycle Yojana (2006)** and **Poshak Yojana** (uniforms), the **mid-day meal (PM POSHAN)**, and massive **teacher recruitment by BPSC (TRE, 2023 onward)** to improve pupil–teacher ratios. Learning outcomes remain a challenge (see ASER reports). These are strong Mains examples of the right to education in practice (see [Bihar schemes](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar)).

## Citizen's charters and Sevottam

- **Citizen's Charter** — a public document in which a department states its **services, standards, time limits, and grievance mechanism**. Started in the **UK in 1991** (John Major government).
- In **India**, adopted at a **Conference of Chief Ministers in 1997**; DARPG coordinates. Charters were often not followed because they were **not legally enforceable**.
- **Sevottam** (2nd ARC) — a quality model with three modules: **Citizen's Charter**, **Public Grievance Redress** and **Service Delivery Capability**.
- The **Citizens' Right to Grievance Redress Bill, 2011** lapsed. Grievances at the Centre are handled through **CPGRAMS** (Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System).
- **Right to Service laws** gave charters legal force — the first was **Madhya Pradesh (2010)**; **Bihar** followed in **2011**.

## Bihar Right to Public Services Act, 2011

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | **Bihar Lok Sewaon ka Adhikar Adhiniyam, 2011** (Bihar Right to Public Services Act) |
| Enactment | Bihar Act 4 of **2011** |
| In force | **August 2011** (commonly given as **15 August 2011**) |
| Position | Bihar was the **second state** after **Madhya Pradesh** (2010) to pass such a law |
| Aim | Delivery of notified public services **within a fixed time limit** |
| Services | Notified services such as **caste, income and residence certificates**, ration cards, **land mutation**, licences, pension and scholarship approvals, and many more (the list has been expanded over time) |
| Key officer | **Designated Public Servant** for each service, who must deliver within the time limit or give reasons for rejection |
| Appeal | **First appeal** to the **Appellate Authority**; **second appeal** to the **Reviewing Authority** |
| Penalty | Fine on the officer for delay or deficiency without good reason (per-day fine with an upper limit), plus disciplinary action |
| Delivery | **RTPS counters** at block, sub-division and district offices; online through the state's service portal (**serviceonline.bihar.gov.in**) |

</div>

- **Significance:** turned promises into **legal rights**; reduced middlemen and bribes for routine certificates; created a monitoring system with receipts and tracking numbers.
- **Limitations:** depends on staff and digital infrastructure; some services remain slow; awareness gaps in rural areas; penalty use is limited.

## Bihar Public Grievance Redressal Act, 2015

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | **Bihar Lok Shikayat Nivaran Adhikar Adhiniyam, 2015** (Bihar Act 19 of 2015) |
| In force | **5 June 2016** |
| Uniqueness | Widely described as the **first law in India** giving citizens a **legal right to have their grievances heard and redressed** within a time limit |
| Time limit | Hearing and decision within **60 working days** |
| Officers | Independent, quasi-judicial **Public Grievance Redressal Officers (PGROs)** at **sub-division, district and state** levels; the complainant and the officer of the public authority are heard on an equal footing |
| Appeal | First appeal to the appellate authority; then revision |
| Penalty | Officials who fail to comply can be fined (about **₹500 to ₹5,000**) and face disciplinary action |
| Access | Online portal (**lokshikayat.bihar.gov.in**), call centre, and offices |

</div>

> **Bihar connection:** Together, **RTPS 2011** (time-bound services), **Lok Shikayat 2015** (grievance redressal), **RTI** (information), **Bihar Special Courts Act 2009** (confiscation of property of corrupt officials) and the **Bihar Lokayukta Act 2011** form Bihar's **good-governance framework**. Earlier, the CM's weekly public hearing "**Janata ke Darbar mein Mukhyamantri**" (from 2006) gave direct access. Use this set as a ready paragraph in GS Paper II answers on "citizen-centric administration in Bihar".

## E-governance

| Initiative | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Information Technology Act** | **2000** | Legal recognition of electronic records and signatures; cyber offences (Section 66A struck down in 2015) |
| **National e-Governance Plan (NeGP)** | **2006** | 27 (later 31) **Mission Mode Projects**; **Common Service Centres (CSCs)** |
| **Aadhaar** | UIDAI **2009**; **Aadhaar Act 2016** | 12-digit biometric identity; upheld with limits in *Puttaswamy (Aadhaar), 2018* |
| **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)** | **2013** | Subsidies directly to bank accounts; **JAM trinity** (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) |
| **Digital India** | **1 July 2015** | Digital infrastructure, services on demand, digital literacy |
| **DigiLocker, UMANG, e-Sign** | 2015–17 | Digital documents and one-app services |
| **Government e-Marketplace (GeM)** | **2016** | Online public procurement |
| **e-Courts** | 2007 onward (Phase III 2023) | Case status online, virtual courts |
| **Digital Personal Data Protection Act** | **2023** | Consent-based processing of personal data; Data Protection Board |
| **Mission Karmayogi** | **2020** | Online training of civil servants (iGOT platform) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar uses e-governance in **RTPS online services**, **land records** (Bihar Bhumi portal, online mutation and the **special land survey**), **e-Kalyan** (scholarships and welfare payments through DBT), **e-Shikshakosh** (teacher and student attendance monitoring), the **CFMS** (Comprehensive Financial Management System) for treasury, and **JEEViKA** (self-help groups) data systems. Mains answers can mention both the gains (transparency, speed) and the **digital divide** (low internet use, especially among women, in rural Bihar).

Use these with the flagship **Saat Nischay** programme (2015; Part 2 in 2020) — details in the [Bihar schemes chapter](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar).

## Social audit and accountability

- **Social audit** — the community checks records of a scheme (muster rolls, bills) in a **Gram Sabha**. Made mandatory under **MGNREGA (Section 17)**; Meghalaya passed the first social audit law (**2017**).
- Other tools: **public hearings (jan sunwai)**, **community score cards**, **citizen report cards** (Bangalore, 1993), **participatory budgeting**, **Lokpal/Lokayukta**, **CAG performance audits**.

> **Mains angle:** A typical GS Paper II question: "Discuss the role of right-based legislation in improving governance in Bihar." Structure: (1) shift from welfare to **rights** (RTI, RTE, NFSA, MGNREGA/VB-G RAM G); (2) **Bihar's laws** — RTPS 2011, Lok Shikayat 2015, Special Courts 2009, Lokayukta 2011; (3) **e-governance** enablers; (4) **outcomes** — certificates on time, fewer middlemen, better grievance tracking; (5) **challenges** — staff shortage, digital divide, awareness, weak penalties, corruption; (6) **way forward** — Sevottam, social audit, capacity building (Mission Karmayogi, BIPARD — Bihar Institute of Public Administration and Rural Development, Gaya), citizen feedback.

### Mains questions

**1. "The Right to Information Act has been a master key to good governance." Examine its achievements and challenges. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 19(1)(a); RTI Act 2005; 2nd ARC's first report title.
- **Body:**
  - Key provisions (time limits, appeals, penalties, Section 4).
  - Achievements: exposed scams, empowered the poor (ration, pensions), increased accountability; Bihar's Jaankari innovation.
  - Challenges: pendency in Information Commissions, vacancies, weak suo motu disclosure, attacks on RTI activists, 2019 amendment, DPDP 2023 change to Section 8(1)(j), political parties outside RTI.
  - Way forward: fill vacancies, strengthen independence, whistle-blower protection, proactive disclosure.
- **Conclusion:** RTI turned citizens from subjects into participants; it must be protected.

**2. Discuss the role of the Bihar Right to Public Services Act 2011 and the Bihar Public Grievance Redressal Act 2015 in citizen-centric governance. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** From citizen's charters (1997) to legal rights; MP 2010, Bihar 2011.
- **Body:** RTPS provisions (notified services, designated public servant, appeals, penalties, counters and online services); Lok Shikayat provisions (60 working days, PGROs, penalties); impact (time-bound certificates, fewer middlemen, data tracking); problems (staff and digital gaps, awareness, quality of redressal, low penalty use); suggestions (Sevottam, social audit, more online services, feedback).
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's laws show how legal rights can improve everyday governance.

**3. Examine the role of e-governance in improving public service delivery in Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Digital India and NeGP; "maximum governance".
- **Body:** National tools (Aadhaar, DBT, DigiLocker, CSCs); Bihar tools (RTPS online, land records and survey, e-Kalyan, e-Shikshakosh, CFMS, JEEViKA); benefits (transparency, speed, leakage reduction); challenges (digital divide, connectivity, cyber security, data privacy under DPDP Act, exclusion due to biometric failures); way forward.
- **Conclusion:** Technology must be paired with human capacity and inclusion.
