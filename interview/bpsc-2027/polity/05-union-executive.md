The Union Executive is the part of the government that runs the country day to day: the **President**, the **Vice-President**, the **Prime Minister**, the **Council of Ministers** and the **Attorney General** (Part V, Articles 52–78). BPSC asks about the method of electing the President, types of veto, the pardoning power, ordinances, and the 91st Amendment limit on ministers. Mains asks "Is the President a rubber stamp?" or "Explain the role of the PM in a coalition era".

> **In simple words:** The President is the head of the state, but works on the advice of the Prime Minister and the ministers. The real power is with the PM and the Council of Ministers, who answer to the Lok Sabha. The Vice-President chairs the Rajya Sabha and fills in if the President's post is vacant. The Attorney General is the government's top lawyer.

## The President (Articles 52–62)

### Basic articles

| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| **52** | There shall be a President of India |
| **53** | **Executive power of the Union** is vested in the President; exercised directly or through subordinate officers. Supreme commander of the defence forces |
| **54** | **Election** of the President (electoral college) |
| **55** | **Manner of election** — uniformity among states, parity between states and Union |
| **56** | **Term** — 5 years; resignation addressed to the **Vice-President** |
| **57** | Eligible for **re-election** (no limit in India; in the US two terms) |
| **58** | **Qualifications** |
| **59** | Conditions of office (not a member of Parliament or a state legislature; no office of profit; official residence; emoluments not reduced during term) |
| **60** | **Oath** — administered by the **Chief Justice of India** (in absence, the senior-most SC judge) |
| **61** | **Impeachment** |
| **62** | Election to fill a vacancy — within **6 months**; the new President gets a full 5-year term |

### Election (Article 54) — the electoral college

The President is **elected indirectly** by an **electoral college** of:
1. **Elected** members of **both Houses of Parliament**;
2. **Elected** members of the **Legislative Assemblies of the states**;
3. **Elected** members of the Legislative Assemblies of the **Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry** (added by the **70th Amendment, 1992**; effective 1995). (J&K's assembly, as a UT with a legislature, is also included under the 2019 reorganisation.)

**Not included:** **nominated** members of Parliament and of state assemblies; members of **Legislative Councils** (e.g. Bihar Vidhan Parishad); elected members of other UTs without a legislature.

- Method: **proportional representation** by means of the **single transferable vote**, with **secret ballot**. (Experts point out it is really a "single transferable vote" system — the term "proportional representation" is not quite exact since only one seat is filled.)
- To win, a candidate needs a **quota** = (total valid votes ÷ 2) + 1.

**Value of votes (Article 55):**
- Value of an **MLA's vote** = (Population of the state ÷ Total elected MLAs) × 1/1000. Population used is of the **1971 Census** (frozen until the first census after 2026 by the 84th Amendment).
- Value of an **MP's vote** = (Total value of votes of all MLAs ÷ Total number of elected MPs).

> **Bihar connection:** The value of one Bihar MLA's vote is about **173** (1971 population about 4.21 crore ÷ 243 MLAs ÷ 1000). Uttar Pradesh has the highest value per MLA (208) and Sikkim the lowest (7). Bihar's 243 MLAs together carry about 42,000 votes in the electoral college — one of the largest blocks after UP and Maharashtra. Bihar's **75 MLCs do not vote**.

### Qualifications and nomination

- Citizen of India; completed **35 years**; qualified for election as a member of the **Lok Sabha**; not holding an **office of profit** (the President, VP, Governor and ministers are not treated as holding offices of profit for this).
- Nomination paper must be signed by **50 proposers and 50 seconders** from the electoral college; security deposit **₹15,000** in the Reserve Bank of India.
- All disputes about the election are decided by the **Supreme Court** (Article 71); its decision is final.

### Impeachment (Article 61)

- Only ground: "**violation of the Constitution**" (not defined).
- Charges can be started in **either House**, signed by **one-fourth** of the members of that House, after **14 days' notice**.
- Must be passed by a majority of **two-thirds of the total membership** of the House.
- The other House **investigates** the charges; the President can appear and be represented.
- If the other House also passes it by **two-thirds of total membership**, the President is removed from the date of the resolution.
- It is a **quasi-judicial** procedure. **Nominated** members of Parliament **can participate** in impeachment (though they do not vote in the election); **MLAs do not participate**.
- No President has been impeached so far.

### Powers of the President

**Executive powers**
- All executive actions are taken in the President's name (Article 77).
- Appoints the **PM and other ministers**, the **Attorney General**, the **CAG**, the **Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners**, the **Chairman and members of UPSC**, the **Governors of states**, the **Finance Commission**, judges of the SC and HCs, etc.
- Can seek information from the PM (Article 78).
- Can declare any area a **Scheduled Area**; administers **UTs** through administrators.
- Can appoint an **Inter-State Council** (Article 263).

**Legislative powers**
- **Summons and prorogues** Parliament; **dissolves the Lok Sabha** (Article 85).
- Calls a **joint sitting** (Article 108) in case of deadlock.
- **Addresses** Parliament at the start of the first session after each general election and at the start of the first session of each year (Article 87).
- **Nominates 12 members** to the Rajya Sabha (literature, science, art, social service — Article 80). (The power to nominate **2 Anglo-Indians** to the Lok Sabha under Article 331 **ended** with the **104th Amendment, 2019/2020**.)
- **Prior recommendation** needed for certain bills — money bills, new states (Article 3), bills affecting taxes in which states are interested.
- **Assent to bills** (Article 111) — and the vetoes (below).
- **Ordinances** (Article 123).
- Lays reports of CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission before Parliament.

**Financial powers**
- Money bills only with the President's recommendation.
- Causes the **Union Budget** (Annual Financial Statement, Article 112) to be laid before Parliament.
- No demand for a grant without the President's recommendation.
- Can make advances from the **Contingency Fund of India**.
- Constitutes a **Finance Commission every five years** (Article 280).

**Judicial powers**
- Appoints the CJI and judges of SC and HCs.
- Can seek the **advice of the Supreme Court** on a question of law or fact (**Article 143**) — the advice is **not binding** on the President.
- **Pardoning power (Article 72).**

**Diplomatic and military powers**
- International treaties are negotiated and concluded in the President's name (subject to Parliament's approval).
- Receives and sends ambassadors.
- **Supreme commander of the defence forces**; appoints the chiefs of the Army, Navy and Air Force; declares war and peace (subject to Parliament).

**Emergency powers** — National emergency (352), President's Rule (356), Financial emergency (360). See [Centre–State Relations and Emergency](/interview/bpsc-2027/polity/centre-state-relations-and-emergency).

### Veto powers (Article 111)

| Veto | Meaning | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute veto** | President **withholds assent**; the bill ends | Usually private members' bills; government bills when the cabinet resigns before assent (e.g. **Dr Rajendra Prasad, 1954 — PEPSU Appropriation Bill**; **R. Venkataraman, 1991 — Salary, Allowances and Pension of MPs (Amendment) Bill**) |
| **Suspensive veto** | President **returns the bill for reconsideration**; if Parliament passes it again (with or without changes) by a **simple majority**, the President **must** give assent | Ordinary bills (**not** money bills) |
| **Pocket veto** | President **neither** gives nor withholds assent nor returns — simply **keeps the bill pending indefinitely** (the Constitution sets no time limit) | Ordinary bills. Used by **Giani Zail Singh (1986)** on the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill** |
| **Qualified veto** | Overridden only by a higher majority | **Not available** to the Indian President (the US President has it) |

- **Money bills:** the President can give or withhold assent but **cannot return** them (they are introduced with his prior recommendation).
- **Constitutional amendment bills:** the President **must give assent** (24th Amendment, 1971) — **no veto**.

> **Memory trick:** "**A**ll **S**uspended **P**ockets" — **A**bsolute, **S**uspensive, **P**ocket — the three vetoes the Indian President has. The **Q**ualified veto is with the US President ("Q for Quite American").

### Ordinance-making power (Article 123)

- The President can issue an ordinance only when **both Houses** (or either House) are **not in session**, and he is **satisfied** that immediate action is needed.
- An ordinance has the **same force as an Act** of Parliament. It can be made only on subjects on which Parliament can make laws. It **cannot amend the Constitution**.
- It must be **laid before Parliament** and **lapses six weeks after Parliament reassembles** (unless approved earlier). So the **maximum life** is **6 months + 6 weeks** (since the gap between two sessions cannot exceed 6 months).
- The President can **withdraw** it at any time.
- He acts on the **advice of the Council of Ministers**.
- **38th Amendment (1975)** made the President's satisfaction final; the **44th Amendment (1978)** removed this, so the satisfaction **can be challenged** in court for mala fide (*R.C. Cooper, 1970*, also said this).

> **Bihar connection:** Two landmark cases on ordinances came from **Bihar**. In **D.C. Wadhwa v State of Bihar (1987)**, the Supreme Court condemned Bihar for **re-promulgating ordinances again and again** (hundreds of ordinances kept alive for years without the legislature passing them) and called it a "**fraud on the Constitution**". In **Krishna Kumar Singh v State of Bihar (2017)**, a 7-judge bench held that **re-promulgation** of ordinances without placing them before the legislature is a fraud on the Constitution, and that an ordinance's satisfaction is subject to **judicial review**; laying before the legislature is mandatory. The Governor's parallel power is **Article 213**.

### Pardoning power (Article 72)

The President can grant pardons etc. to persons convicted of any offence:
1. In all cases where the punishment is by a **court martial**;
2. In all cases of offences against a **Union law**;
3. In all cases where the sentence is a **sentence of death**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pardon** | Removes both the sentence and the conviction completely |
| **Commutation** | Substitutes a lighter form of punishment (e.g. death → life imprisonment) |
| **Remission** | Reduces the **period** of sentence without changing its nature (2 years → 1 year) |
| **Respite** | A lesser sentence for special reasons (e.g. pregnancy of a woman convict, physical disability) |
| **Reprieve** | **Stays** the execution of a sentence (especially death) temporarily, to allow time to seek pardon or commutation |

- The power is exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers (*Maru Ram, 1980*). It is **subject to limited judicial review** — on grounds of arbitrariness, mala fide, irrelevant considerations (*Epuru Sudhakar, 2006*; *Kehar Singh, 1989*).
- The **Governor (Article 161)** can pardon etc. for offences against **state law**, but **cannot pardon a death sentence** (he can suspend, remit or commute it) and **has no power over court-martial** sentences. Only the President can pardon a death sentence.

### Position of the President — nominal head

- India follows the parliamentary system, so the President is the **nominal (constitutional) executive**; the PM-led Council of Ministers is the **real executive**.
- **Article 74(1)** — "There shall be a Council of Ministers with the PM at the head to aid and advise the President." The **42nd Amendment (1976)** made the advice **binding**. The **44th Amendment (1978)** allowed the President to **return the advice once** for reconsideration; he must act on the reconsidered advice.
- **Article 74(2)** — the question whether any advice was given and what it was **cannot be inquired into** by any court.
- **Situational discretion** — the President has some room in: (1) appointing a PM when no party has a clear majority or the PM dies suddenly (e.g. 1979 Charan Singh, 1989 onward hung Parliaments); (2) dismissing a Council of Ministers that has lost the confidence of the Lok Sabha but refuses to resign; (3) dissolving the Lok Sabha if the Council of Ministers has lost majority; (4) the pocket veto; (5) returning advice once.

## The Vice-President (Articles 63–71)

| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Office | **Article 63** — there shall be a Vice-President. Second-highest office. Modelled on the **US Vice-President** |
| Role | **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (Article 64); acts as President during a vacancy (**up to 6 months**) or the President's absence (Article 65) |
| Election (Article 66) | Electoral college of **all members of both Houses of Parliament** — **elected and nominated** — **not** state MLAs. Single transferable vote, secret ballot |
| Qualifications | Citizen, **35 years**, qualified for election to the **Rajya Sabha**, no office of profit |
| Term (Article 67) | **5 years**; resigns to the **President** |
| Removal | By a resolution of the **Rajya Sabha** passed by a **majority of all the then members** (effective majority) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha** (simple majority). **14 days' notice**. **No ground** is mentioned; no formal impeachment |
| Oath | Administered by the **President** (Article 69) |
| Disputes | Decided by the **Supreme Court** (Article 71) |
| Salary | Gets the salary of the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (not as VP); while acting as President, gets the President's emoluments |

- **Dr S. Radhakrishnan** was the first VP (two terms, 1952–62). V.V. Giri, R. Venkataraman, S.D. Sharma, K.R. Narayanan and others later became President.
- **Jagdeep Dhankhar** resigned as VP on **21 July 2025** (citing health). **C.P. Radhakrishnan** was elected Vice-President in **September 2025** (as of 2026 — check the latest).

> **Common mistake:** The VP is elected by **both Houses including nominated members**; the President by **elected MPs and MLAs only**. The VP's removal needs **no impeachment** and starts **only in the Rajya Sabha**.

## Prime Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 74–75, 77–78)

### Appointment

- **Article 75(1)** — the PM is appointed by the President; other ministers by the President **on the PM's advice**.
- By convention the President appoints the leader of the **majority party** in the Lok Sabha. If no party has a majority, the President uses discretion and asks the PM to **prove majority** within a time limit.
- A person who is **not a member of Parliament** can be PM/minister for **six months**, within which he must become an MP (Article 75(5)).
- The PM can be a member of **either House** (e.g. Indira Gandhi in 1966, H.D. Deve Gowda, I.K. Gujral and Manmohan Singh were Rajya Sabha members when they became PM).
- **Oath** administered by the **President**.

### Key constitutional rules

| Article | Rule |
|---|---|
| **74(1)** | Council of Ministers headed by the PM to aid and advise the President |
| **75(1A)** | Total number of ministers including the PM shall **not exceed 15%** of the total strength of the **Lok Sabha** (**91st Amendment, 2003**). Same rule for states, Article 164(1A): **15% of the Assembly, minimum 12** |
| **75(1B)** | A member **disqualified for defection** cannot be a minister until re-elected |
| **75(2)** | Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President** (individual responsibility) |
| **75(3)** | Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** |
| **75(4)** | Oath of office and secrecy by the President |
| **77** | Conduct of business — all executive action in the name of the President; **allocation of business** rules |
| **78** | Duties of the PM — to **communicate** all decisions of the Council of Ministers to the President, furnish information the President calls for, and submit any matter for consideration of the Council if the President requires |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's Assembly has **243** members, so the state Council of Ministers can have at most **36** members including the CM (15% of 243 = 36.45 → 36).

### Council of Ministers vs Cabinet

| Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|---|---|
| Wider body — **60–70** ministers of three ranks | Smaller body — **15–20** senior ministers |
| **Cabinet ministers, Ministers of State** (with independent charge or attached), **Deputy Ministers** | Only **cabinet ministers** |
| Does not meet as a body | Meets regularly; takes policy decisions |
| Mentioned in Article 74, 75 | Word "cabinet" appears only once — **Article 352**, inserted by the **44th Amendment** |
| Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha | Enforces collective responsibility |

**Cabinet committees** (extra-constitutional): **Political Affairs**, **Economic Affairs**, **Appointments Committee of the Cabinet**, **Cabinet Committee on Security**, **Parliamentary Affairs**, **Accommodation**, plus others. Most are chaired by the PM.

### Powers and role of the PM

- **In relation to the Council:** recommends ministers, allocates portfolios, can ask a minister to resign, presides over Cabinet meetings; the PM's resignation or death **dissolves** the whole Council of Ministers.
- **In relation to the President:** the principal channel of communication (Article 78); advises appointments of key officers.
- **In relation to Parliament:** **Leader of the Lok Sabha** (if a member), advises summoning and prorogation, can recommend dissolution, announces government policy.
- **Other roles:** chairman of **NITI Aayog**, **National Integration Council**, **Inter-State Council**, **National Water Resources Council**, **National Disaster Management Authority**; leader of the nation and the party; chief spokesperson.
- Descriptions: "first among equals" (*primus inter pares*), "keystone of the cabinet arch" (Morley), "sun around which planets revolve".

### Collective and individual responsibility

- **Collective** — all ministers own every cabinet decision; if the Lok Sabha passes a **no-confidence motion**, the **entire Council resigns**. (The no-confidence motion is not in the Constitution; it is in the Lok Sabha Rules, needing support of **50 members** to be admitted.) Governments that fell on a confidence/no-confidence vote: **Morarji Desai (1979**, resigned before vote), **V.P. Singh (1990)**, **H.D. Deve Gowda (1997)**, **A.B. Vajpayee (1999** — lost by one vote).
- **Individual** — a minister holds office at the President's pleasure, i.e. the PM can get a minister dismissed.
- **No legal responsibility** — the Constitution does not require a minister's counter-signature on the President's orders (unlike Britain).

> **Mains angle:** Use the ideas of "**cabinet government → prime ministerial government**" and "**coalition dharma**" when discussing the PM's role. Mention checks — collective responsibility, Lok Sabha's confidence, the media, federalism (strong regional parties in Bihar shaped central coalitions in 1989–2014 and again from 2024).

## Attorney General (Article 76)

- The **highest law officer** of the Government of India.
- Appointed by the **President**; must be **qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court**.
- **No fixed term** in the Constitution; holds office during the **pleasure of the President**; by convention resigns when the government changes. **Remuneration** is fixed by the President.
- **Duties:** gives legal advice to the Government of India; appears for the government in the SC and HCs; represents the government in Presidential references (Article 143).
- **Rights:** right of audience in **all courts** in India; **right to speak and take part in proceedings of both Houses** of Parliament and their joint sittings and committees, but **no right to vote** (**Article 88**); enjoys the privileges of an MP (Article 105).
- **Not** a member of the Cabinet (the Law Minister handles legal matters in the government). Not a full-time government servant; can do private practice but not against the Government of India.
- Assisted by the **Solicitor General** and **Additional Solicitors General** — these are **not constitutional** posts.
- The first AG was **M.C. Setalvad** (1950–63).
- **R. Venkataramani** is the AG (since 1 October 2022; term extended to 30 September 2027, as of 2026 — check the latest).
- The state counterpart is the **Advocate General** (**Article 165**), appointed by the Governor.

### Mains questions

**1. "The President of India is a constitutional head, but not a rubber stamp." Discuss. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Parliamentary system; Article 74 as amended by the 42nd and 44th Amendments.
- **Body:**
  - Formal powers (executive, legislative, financial, judicial, emergency) are exercised on ministerial advice.
  - Real influence: right to be informed (Art 78), return advice once, suspensive and pocket vetoes, Article 143 references.
  - Situational discretion: hung Parliament, loss of majority, dissolution.
  - Examples: Rajendra Prasad's views on the Hindu Code Bill; Zail Singh's pocket veto; K.R. Narayanan returning advice on UP (1997) and Bihar (1998); Kalam returning the Office of Profit Bill (2006).
  - Role as guardian of the Constitution (oath under Art 60).
- **Conclusion:** A "moral authority" and a constitutional safety valve — not a ruler, not a rubber stamp.

**2. Critically examine the ordinance-making power of the executive in India, with reference to judicial pronouncements. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Articles 123 and 213 — for urgent situations when the legislature is not in session.
- **Body:** Conditions and limits; 38th and 44th Amendments; R.C. Cooper (1970); D.C. Wadhwa (1987) — Bihar's re-promulgation; Krishna Kumar Singh (2017) — 7 judges; concerns: bypassing Parliament, frequent ordinances; suggestions: limit numbers, quicker legislation, longer sessions.
- **Conclusion:** A necessary emergency tool, but it must remain an exception, not a parallel law-making route.

**3. Discuss the position and role of the Prime Minister in the Indian parliamentary system. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 75; real executive; "keystone of the cabinet arch".
- **Body:** Relations with Council of Ministers, President, Parliament; chair of NITI Aayog, NDMA etc.; coalition era (1989–2014) vs single-party majority; checks (collective responsibility, federal parties, courts, media).
- **Conclusion:** The PM's strength depends on the party system; constitutional morality must keep it accountable.
