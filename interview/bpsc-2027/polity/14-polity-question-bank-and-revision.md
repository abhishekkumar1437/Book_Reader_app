This is your revision chapter for the whole Polity book. Use it in three ways: (1) at the end of the Polity phase (**Days 50–77** of the roadmap) to test yourself; (2) in **Phase 4 (Days 246–300)** during the two revision rounds; and (3) in the **Mains sprint** for answer-writing practice. The questions are **not** multiple-choice — cover the answer, say it aloud or write it, then check. Themes that come up again and again in state PSC papers are marked ⭐.

> **In simple words:** Read the question, answer from memory, then check. Mark the ones you got wrong and redo them after 1 day, 7 days and 30 days. For Mains, write one outline a day in 15 minutes and one full answer a week.

## Mains questions with answer outlines

Write each in about **300 words (38-mark style)** unless marked otherwise. Always add a **Bihar example** and, where useful, a small diagram (e.g. a flowchart of the three tiers, or the three emergencies in a table).

### Constitution and rights

**1. ⭐ "The Indian Constitution is a living document." Discuss with reference to amendments and judicial interpretation.**
- **Intro:** Adopted 1949; 106 amendment Acts by 2026; basic structure keeps its core.
- **Body:** Flexibility via Article 368; key amendments (1st, 42nd, 44th, 73rd/74th, 101st, 103rd, 106th); judicial expansion (Article 21 — Maneka, Puttaswamy); basic structure (Kesavananda, Minerva); Bihar-linked cases (Hussainara, Rameshwar Prasad, Krishna Kumar Singh).
- **Conclusion:** It adapts without losing its identity.

**2. ⭐ Discuss the significance of Fundamental Rights and the role of the judiciary in protecting them.**
- **Intro:** Part III, Magna Carta of India.
- **Body:** Six rights; Article 32 and 226; writs; PIL; landmark cases; suspension in emergency and the 44th Amendment; Bihar PILs.
- **Conclusion:** Rights are real only when courts and citizens defend them.

**3. Examine the relationship between Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties.**
- **Intro:** Three pillars of a democratic welfare state.
- **Body:** Nature of each; FR–DPSP conflict and harmony (Champakam → Minerva → 2024 Property Owners case); duties as the other side of rights; Bihar examples — Article 40 (panchayats), 47 (prohibition), 48A (Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali), 51A(k).
- **Conclusion:** Rights, goals and duties together build a just society.

### Union and state government

**4. ⭐ "The President is the guardian of the Constitution." Comment.**
- **Intro:** Oath under Article 60.
- **Body:** Powers on advice; Article 74; situational discretion; vetoes; Article 143; examples (Zail Singh, K.R. Narayanan on Bihar 1998).
- **Conclusion:** A moral check rather than a ruler.

**5. ⭐ Critically examine the role of the Governor in Indian federalism with examples from Bihar.**
- **Intro:** Article 153–163; agent of Centre vs constitutional head.
- **Body:** Discretion; Article 356 reports; assent delays (2025 TN case and reference); Bihar's 2005 dissolution and Rameshwar Prasad; commissions' recommendations.
- **Conclusion:** Codify conventions and time limits.

**6. ⭐ "Parliament is losing its centrality in Indian democracy." Discuss.**
- **Intro:** Parliament as the voice of the people.
- **Body:** Falling sitting days, disruptions, less committee scrutiny, ordinances, money-bill route; positive signs (DRSCs, PRS data, live telecasts); comparison with the Bihar Legislature's short sessions.
- **Conclusion:** Reforms: fixed calendar, mandatory committee review, opposition days.

**7. Discuss the structure and role of the Bihar Legislative Council. Should it continue?**
- **Intro:** One of six bicameral states; 75 members.
- **Body:** Composition (27 + 24 + 6 + 6 + 12); powers (delay up to 4 months); merits and demerits; Article 169 procedure.
- **Conclusion:** Keep it if it adds expertise; reform it to avoid patronage.

### Judiciary

**8. ⭐ Evaluate judicial activism in India. Where does activism end and overreach begin?**
- **Intro:** PIL and Article 32/226.
- **Body:** Positive role (Hussainara, Vishaka, M.C. Mehta, Right to Food); overreach concerns (policy domains, separation of powers); restraint principles.
- **Conclusion:** Activism must be principled and temporary, not a substitute for governance.

**9. Discuss the role of the Patna High Court in the governance of Bihar.**
- **Intro:** Established 1916; history (Orissa 1948, Jharkhand 2000).
- **Body:** Writ jurisdiction; PILs on floods, health, encroachment; key judgments (caste survey 2023, reservation 2024, prohibition bail matters, fodder scam CBI order); pendency and vacancies; e-courts and Hindi translations.
- **Conclusion:** A key guardian of rights and good governance in Bihar.

### Federalism and emergency

**10. ⭐ "Cooperative federalism is the need of the hour." Discuss with reference to GST and the Finance Commission, and Bihar's concerns.**
- **Intro:** Union of States; quasi-federal.
- **Body:** GST Council (279A, Mohit Minerals); Finance Commissions (41% devolution; horizontal criteria; Bihar's share ~10%); cesses and surcharges; special category demand; NITI Aayog; Inter-State Council; zonal councils.
- **Conclusion:** Trust-based fiscal partnership helps poorer states like Bihar.

**11. ⭐ Analyse the misuse of Article 356 and the safeguards evolved against it.**
- **Intro:** Ambedkar's "dead letter".
- **Body:** Over 100 uses; Bihar 8 times; 44th Amendment; Bommai; Rameshwar Prasad; Sarkaria and Punchhi.
- **Conclusion:** Article 356 is now a last resort.

### Local government

**12. ⭐ Evaluate the impact of 50% reservation for women in Panchayati Raj Institutions in Bihar.**
- **Intro:** Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006 — first in India.
- **Body:** Political participation; leadership pipeline; changes in priorities (water, schools, sanitation); JEEViKA links; problems (proxy "Mukhiya-pati", low literacy, caste barriers); remedies (training, SHGs, penalties for proxies).
- **Conclusion:** A big step from representation to empowerment; needs capacity building.

**13. Discuss the challenges of urban local governance in Bihar.**
- **Intro:** 74th Amendment; Bihar's low urbanisation (11.3%).
- **Body:** Bihar Municipal Act 2007; directly elected mayors (2022); problems — waterlogging in Patna, solid waste, weak finances, staff shortage, parastatals; Smart Cities, AMRUT, Swachh Bharat.
- **Conclusion:** Empower ULBs with funds, functions and functionaries.

### Bodies, elections and governance

**14. ⭐ Discuss the constitutional position and functions of State Public Service Commissions. Suggest reforms for BPSC.**
- **Intro:** Article 315; BPSC 1949.
- **Body:** Appointment, removal, independence; functions; issues (paper leaks, delays, litigation); 2024 anti-unfair-means laws; reforms (calendar, technology, transparency).
- **Conclusion:** Merit-based recruitment is the base of good administration.

**15. ⭐ Discuss the need for electoral reforms in India in the light of recent judgments.**
- **Intro:** Free and fair elections — basic structure.
- **Body:** Criminalisation (ADR 2002, Lily Thomas 2013, PIF 2018); electoral bonds (2024); ECI appointments (Anoop Baranwal 2023, Act 2023); rolls (Bihar SIR 2025); ONOE; anti-defection.
- **Conclusion:** Reform for transparency, fairness and trust.

**16. ⭐ "Rights-based laws have changed the relationship between citizens and the state." Discuss with reference to Bihar.**
- **Intro:** From welfare as charity to welfare as right.
- **Body:** RTI, RTE, NFSA, rural employment guarantee (MGNREGA → VB-G RAM G 2025); Bihar RTPS 2011, Lok Shikayat 2015, Jaankari 2007; e-governance; challenges and way forward.
- **Conclusion:** Laws give rights; capacity and awareness make them work.
