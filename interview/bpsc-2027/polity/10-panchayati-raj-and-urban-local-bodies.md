Local self-government is named directly in the BPSC GS Paper II syllabus ("Panchayati Raj"), so this chapter matters for both Prelims and Mains. Prelims asks about the 73rd and 74th Amendments, the Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules, committees (Balwant Rai Mehta, Ashok Mehta) and Bihar's "firsts". Mains asks you to evaluate Panchayati Raj in Bihar — women's empowerment through the 50% quota, the Gram Kachahari, and problems like "Mukhiya-pati" (proxy rule by husbands) and weak finances.

> **In simple words:** Panchayati Raj means villages governing themselves through elected bodies. India has three levels in rural areas: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti (block), and Zila Parishad (district). In towns and cities, there are Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils and Municipal Corporations. The 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) gave these bodies constitutional status. Bihar went further: in 2006 it became the first state to reserve half of all panchayat seats for women.

## Evolution of Panchayati Raj

### Before and after independence

- **Lord Ripon's resolution (1882)** — the "**Magna Carta of local self-government**"; Ripon is called the **father of local self-government** in India.
- **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1907)** and the GoI Act 1919 (local government became a **transferred** subject).
- **Gandhi** dreamed of "**Gram Swaraj**" — self-sufficient village republics.
- **Article 40** (DPSP) — the state shall organise village panchayats.
- **Community Development Programme (1952)** and **National Extension Service (1953)** — did not get people's participation, so a committee was appointed.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar was an early mover — the **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 1947** was passed soon after independence, providing for village panchayats and **Gram Kachaharis** (village courts). Later Acts came in **1993** (after the 73rd Amendment) and the present **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006**. However, Bihar did not hold panchayat elections for a long time — after 1978, the next were held only in **2001**.

### Committees

| Committee | Year | Key recommendations |
|---|---|---|
| **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee** | Appointed **1957** | **Three-tier** system — **Gram Panchayat** (village), **Panchayat Samiti** (block), **Zila Parishad** (district); "**democratic decentralisation**"; direct elections for the village level, indirect for the upper levels; the **Panchayat Samiti** as the executive body and the **Zila Parishad** as an advisory/coordinating body chaired by the **Collector**; transfer of resources |
| **Ashok Mehta Committee** | **1977** (Janata government) | **Two-tier** system — **Zila Parishad** and **Mandal Panchayat** (a group of villages, about 15,000–20,000 population); the **district** as the first point of decentralisation below the state; official participation of political parties; compulsory powers of taxation; Nyaya Panchayats separate; constitutional recognition |
| **G.V.K. Rao Committee** | **1985** | Panchayats had become "**grass without roots**" due to **bureaucratisation**; the **district** should be the basic unit for planning; District Development Commissioner |
| **L.M. Singhvi Committee** | **1986** | **Constitutional recognition** and protection for PRIs; Nyaya Panchayats for a cluster of villages; more financial resources; judicial tribunals for election disputes |
| **Thungon Committee** | 1988 | Constitutional status; three tiers; Zila Parishad as the pivot; 5-year term |
| **Gadgil Committee** | 1988 | Constitutional status; three tiers; reservation for SC, ST and women; State Finance Commission; State Election Commission — many of these went into the 73rd Amendment |

- **First** Panchayati Raj was inaugurated by **Jawaharlal Nehru** at **Nagaur (Rajasthan)** on **2 October 1959**, followed by **Andhra Pradesh** (1959).
- The **64th Amendment Bill (1989)** (Rajiv Gandhi government) was passed by the Lok Sabha but **defeated in the Rajya Sabha**.
- The **73rd Amendment** was passed under the **P.V. Narasimha Rao** government in **December 1992** and came into force on **24 April 1993** — celebrated as **National Panchayati Raj Day**.

## The 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IX, Articles 243–243O)

### Article by article

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **243** | Definitions (Gram Sabha, Panchayat, village, intermediate level, etc.) |
| **243A** | **Gram Sabha** — all persons on the electoral rolls of a village; powers as the state legislature decides |
| **243B** | **Three-tier** Panchayati Raj — village, intermediate and district levels. States with population **not exceeding 20 lakh** may **skip the intermediate** level |
| **243C** | **Composition** — all members at all three levels **directly elected**; **chairpersons** of intermediate and district levels **elected indirectly** (by and from elected members); the village chairperson's election as per state law. MPs, MLAs can be members of intermediate/district panchayats if the state law provides |
| **243D** | **Reservation** — **SCs and STs** in proportion to their population (seats and chairpersons); **not less than one-third** of seats (including SC/ST seats) and chairpersons for **women**; states **may** reserve for **backward classes** |
| **243E** | **Duration — 5 years**; fresh election **before the expiry**, or **within 6 months** of dissolution |
| **243F** | **Disqualifications** — as per laws for the state legislature, but a person aged **21** is eligible (not 25); decided by an authority under state law |
| **243G** | **Powers and responsibilities** — preparation and implementation of plans for **economic development and social justice**, including the **29 subjects** in the **Eleventh Schedule** |
| **243H** | **Taxes and funds** — state may authorise panchayats to levy taxes, duties, tolls, fees; grants-in-aid |
| **243I** | **State Finance Commission** — constituted by the **Governor every 5 years** to review finances of panchayats and recommend sharing of state taxes and grants |
| **243J** | **Audit** of accounts |
| **243K** | **State Election Commission** — superintendence of elections to panchayats; **State Election Commissioner** appointed by the **Governor**; removed only like a **HC judge** |
| **243L** | Application to **Union Territories** |
| **243M** | **Exempted areas** — **Scheduled Areas** and tribal areas (Fifth and Sixth Schedules), **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram**, **hill areas of Manipur** with district councils, and **Darjeeling** (where the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council exists). Parliament can extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas — done by **PESA 1996** |
| **243N** | Continuance of existing laws for **one year** |
| **243O** | **Bar on interference by courts** in electoral matters (delimitation, allotment of seats); election disputes only by an election petition |

### Compulsory vs voluntary provisions

| Compulsory (states **must**) | Voluntary (states **may**) |
|---|---|
| Gram Sabha in each village | Give MPs and MLAs representation in panchayats |
| Three-tier system (except small states) | Reservation for **backward classes** |
| Direct election of all members | Powers to panchayats as **units of self-government** |
| Indirect election of chairpersons of intermediate and district levels | Devolution of the **29 subjects** (in practice, states decide how much) |
| Minimum age **21** | Financial powers — authorising taxes |
| Reservation for **SCs, STs** (proportional) and **one-third for women** | Grants-in-aid |
| 5-year term; elections within 6 months of dissolution | |
| **State Election Commission** and **State Finance Commission** | |

> **Prelims angle:** Common traps: (1) Chairpersons of the **intermediate and district** levels are elected **indirectly**; the **Constitution leaves the village head's election** to the state (in Bihar the **Mukhiya** is **directly** elected). (2) The **minimum age is 21**. (3) Reservation for women is "**not less than one-third**", so states can give **50%** (Bihar did). (4) The **SEC** is appointed by the **Governor**, not the ECI. (5) The 73rd Amendment added **Part IX** and the **Eleventh Schedule** (29 subjects); the 74th added **Part IXA** and the **Twelfth Schedule** (18 subjects).

### The Eleventh Schedule — 29 subjects (selected)

Agriculture and extension; land improvement and soil conservation; **minor irrigation** and watershed development; animal husbandry, dairying, poultry; **fisheries**; social forestry and farm forestry; minor forest produce; small-scale industries including food processing; **khadi, village and cottage industries**; rural housing; **drinking water**; fuel and fodder; roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways; **rural electrification**; non-conventional energy sources; **poverty alleviation** programmes; **education** including primary and secondary schools; technical training and vocational education; adult and non-formal education; libraries; cultural activities; markets and fairs; **health and sanitation** (hospitals, PHCs, dispensaries); family welfare; **women and child development**; social welfare including welfare of the handicapped and mentally retarded; welfare of the weaker sections, especially SCs and STs; **public distribution system**; maintenance of community assets.

> **Memory trick:** "**29** in the **11th**, **18** in the **12th**" — the **village has more work** (29) than the **city list** (18). The smaller numbers go together (73rd → 11th Schedule → villages) and the bigger ones go together (74th → 12th Schedule → cities).

## PESA — Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996

- Recommended by the **Bhuria Committee (1995)**. Extends Part IX to **Fifth Schedule areas** with modifications.
- Applies to **10 states** with Fifth Schedule areas: **Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana**.
- Gives the **Gram Sabha** powers over **minor forest produce**, **minor water bodies**, **land acquisition consultation**, **mining leases for minor minerals**, sale of intoxicants, money lending, village markets, and approval of development plans; protects customs and traditional dispute resolution.
- Reservation for STs at least **50%** of seats in Scheduled Areas; all chairpersons to be STs.

> **Bihar connection:** **Bihar has no Fifth Schedule (Scheduled) Areas** after the creation of **Jharkhand** in 2000 (the tribal belts of Chotanagpur and Santhal Pargana went to Jharkhand), so **PESA does not apply to Bihar**. Bihar's ST population is small (about **1.3%** in Census 2011), concentrated in districts like West Champaran (Tharu), Katihar, Purnia, Banka and Jamui.

| Tier | Head | How chosen | Deputy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gram Panchayat** (village/cluster, about 7,000 population) | **Mukhiya** | **Directly** elected by voters | **Up-Mukhiya** (elected by members) |
| **Panchayat Samiti** (block) | **Pramukh** | Indirectly, by elected members | **Up-Pramukh** |
| **Zila Parishad** (district) | **Adhyaksh** (Chairperson) | Indirectly, by elected members | **Upadhyaksh** |
| **Gram Kachahari** (one for each Gram Panchayat) | **Sarpanch** | **Directly** elected | **Up-Sarpanch**; members called **Panch** |

- Members elected at each level: **Ward members** (Gram Panchayat), **Panchayat Samiti members**, **Zila Parishad members**, and **Panchs** (Gram Kachahari) — so a Bihar voter casts **six votes** in a panchayat election (Mukhiya, Ward member, Panchayat Samiti member, Zila Parishad member, Sarpanch, Panch).
- Bihar has roughly **8,000 Gram Panchayats**, about **530 Panchayat Samitis** and **38 Zila Parishads** (check the latest numbers; the count changes when urban bodies expand).
- **Executive officers:** **Panchayat Secretary** at the village level; **Block Development Officer (BDO)** as executive officer of the Panchayat Samiti; **District Development Commissioner (DDC)** as **Chief Executive Officer** of the Zila Parishad.
- **Standing committees** at each level (e.g. for planning, finance, social justice, education, health, agriculture, public works).

### Reservation — Bihar's landmark

<div class="formula">

| Category | Reservation under the Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006 |
|---|---|
| **Women** | **50%** of seats and posts at **all three tiers and the Gram Kachahari**, **horizontally** within every category (SC, ST, EBC and unreserved) |
| **SC / ST** | In proportion to their population in that area |
| **Extremely Backward Classes (EBC)** | Up to about **20%**, so that total vertical reservation does not exceed **50%** (check the exact rule) |
| **Rotation** | Reserved seats rotate after a fixed number of general elections (the Act provides rotation after **two** elections — check the latest amendment) |

</div>

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar was the **first state in India to give 50% reservation to women in Panchayati Raj Institutions (2006)**, under Chief Minister **Nitish Kumar**. It was also an early state to give a separate quota to **Extremely Backward Classes** in panchayats. Since then about 20 states have adopted 50% for women. In **2007** Bihar extended **50% reservation for women to urban local bodies** too. The Supreme Court (in *Krishna Murthy v Union of India, 2010*) upheld reservation for backward classes in local bodies but said it must be based on **empirical data** and the total should not ordinarily exceed **50%** (the "triple test" was later laid down in *Vikas Kishanrao Gawali, 2021*).

### Gram Kachahari — village justice

- A **village court** in each Gram Panchayat area — a special feature of Bihar (it existed even under the 1947 Act).
- Headed by the directly elected **Sarpanch**; the bench includes **Panchs**; assisted by a law-graduate **Nyay Mitra** and a **Nyay Sachiv**.
- Tries **minor civil disputes** (e.g. small money or property disputes) and **petty criminal cases** (listed minor offences), primarily through **conciliation**; can impose small **fines** (up to about ₹1,000 — check the current limit) but **cannot send anyone to jail**.
- No lawyers are allowed to appear (to keep it simple and cheap).
- Appeals go to a full bench of the Gram Kachahari and then to the regular courts.

### Elections and the State Election Commission (Panchayat), Bihar

- Panchayat elections in Bihar after 2001 were held in **2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021**. The **2021** elections were held in **11 phases** (September–December 2021), with **EVMs** used for some posts for the first time. The next general panchayat elections are due around **late 2026** (check the latest schedule and the new rotation of reserved seats).
- Conducted by the **State Election Commission, Bihar** (Article 243K), not the Election Commission of India.

### Functions and finances

- **Gram Panchayat** functions include sanitation, drinking water (Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal under **Saat Nischay**), village lanes and drains (Har Ghar Pakki Gali-Naliyan), street lights, birth and death registration, primary education support, implementation of rural employment and housing schemes, and preparation of the **Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP)**.
- **Finances:** grants from the **Union Finance Commission** (tied and untied grants for local bodies), the **State Finance Commission** (Bihar has set up several SFCs), state schemes, and a small amount of own revenue (fees, taxes allowed by the Act). Own-source revenue is very low in Bihar's panchayats — a major weakness.
- **Panchayat Sarkar Bhawans** are being built in panchayats to give them offices.

## Problems and reforms (for Mains)

| Problem | Explanation | Reform |
|---|---|---|
| **Proxy representation — "Mukhiya-pati" / "Pradhan-pati"** | Husbands or male relatives of elected women act in their place | Training and capacity building, women's collectives (**JEEViKA**), penalties for proxy work (a Ministry of Panchayati Raj advisory committee in 2025 recommended steps — check the latest) |
| **Inadequate devolution — "3 Fs"** | Functions, **Funds** and **Functionaries** not fully transferred | Activity mapping, untied funds, cadre of panchayat staff |
| **Weak own revenue** | Little tax collection; dependence on grants | Property tax, user fees, better SFC transfers |
| **Bureaucratic dominance** | BDO, DDC control decisions | Make panchayats the implementing agency |
| **Weak Gram Sabha** | Meetings poorly attended | Social audit, mandatory quorum, ward sabhas |
| **Elite and caste capture, corruption** | Local power groups dominate | Transparency through **e-Gram Swaraj**, social audits, RTI |
| **Delayed elections** (in many states) | Violates Article 243E | Strong SECs (SC in *Suresh Mahajan, 2022* — elections cannot be delayed) |

> **Mains angle:** For "Evaluate Panchayati Raj in Bihar", use: the 50% quota produced lakhs of women representatives and new women leaders; Gram Kachaharis reduce litigation; panchayats implement Saat Nischay schemes; but proxy rule, low capacity, weak finances and corruption remain. Quote the idea of "**deepening democracy**" and Gandhi's "Gram Swaraj". National initiatives: **SVAMITVA** (property cards, launched on 24 April 2020), **e-Gram Swaraj**, **Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan**, Panchayat Development Index.

## Urban Local Bodies — 74th Amendment Act, 1992 (Part IXA, Articles 243P–243ZG)

- Came into force on **1 June 1993**. Added the **Twelfth Schedule** (**18 subjects**).

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **243Q** | Three types of municipalities: **Nagar Panchayat** (area in **transition** from rural to urban), **Municipal Council** (smaller urban area), **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area). Industrial townships can be exempted |
| **243R** | Composition — members **directly elected** from **wards**; legislature may provide for nominated persons with special knowledge (without vote), MPs, MLAs |
| **243S** | **Wards Committees** for municipalities with population of **3 lakh or more** |
| **243T** | **Reservation** — SC/ST proportional; **not less than one-third for women**; backward classes optional; chairpersons as per state law |
| **243U** | **5-year term**; elections within 6 months of dissolution |
| **243V** | Disqualifications (minimum age **21**) |
| **243W** | Powers — including Twelfth Schedule subjects |
| **243X** | Taxes and funds |
| **243Y** | **State Finance Commission** (same SFC as for panchayats) |
| **243ZA** | Elections by the **State Election Commission** |
| **243ZD** | **District Planning Committee** — consolidates plans of panchayats and municipalities; at least **four-fifths** of members elected by and from elected members of the district panchayat and municipalities, in proportion to the rural and urban population |
| **243ZE** | **Metropolitan Planning Committee** — for metropolitan areas (**population 10 lakh or more**); two-thirds elected |
| **243ZG** | Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters |

**Twelfth Schedule — 18 subjects:** urban planning including town planning; regulation of land use and construction of buildings; planning for economic and social development; roads and bridges; **water supply**; public health, sanitation, conservancy and **solid waste management**; fire services; urban forestry, environment; safeguarding weaker sections including the handicapped; slum improvement and upgradation; **urban poverty alleviation**; urban amenities (parks, gardens, playgrounds); promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects; burials, cremation grounds, electric crematoriums; cattle pounds, prevention of cruelty to animals; **vital statistics (births and deaths registration)**; public amenities (street lighting, parking, bus stops, public conveniences); regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries.

**Other types of urban bodies:** Cantonment Boards (Cantonments Act 2006; under the Ministry of Defence), Township, Port Trust, Special Purpose Agencies, Notified Area Committees, Town Area Committees.

> **Bihar connection:** Urban bodies in Bihar are governed by the **Bihar Municipal Act, 2007**, which gave **50% reservation to women** (2007). Bihar has **Nagar Panchayats, Nagar Parishads (Municipal Councils) and Nagar Nigams (Municipal Corporations)** — e.g. **Patna Municipal Corporation** (the largest), Gaya, Bhagalpur, Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga and others. Many new urban bodies were created and some upgraded in **2021–22**. By the **Bihar Municipal (Amendment) Act, 2022**, the **Mayor and Deputy Mayor** (and chairpersons of councils) are **directly elected** by voters — first done in the **December 2022** urban elections. Bihar is one of the **least urbanised** states (**11.3%** urban population in Census 2011), so urban governance is a growing Mains theme (Patna's waterlogging, solid waste, Smart City projects in Patna, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur and Biharsharif).

### Mains questions

**1. Evaluate the working of Panchayati Raj Institutions in Bihar after the Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 73rd Amendment; Bihar's Acts of 1947, 1993, 2006; Article 40.
- **Body:**
  - Structure: three tiers + Gram Kachahari; directly elected Mukhiya and Sarpanch.
  - Reservation: 50% women (first in India), SC/ST, EBC; rotation.
  - Achievements: women's political participation, new leadership from weaker sections, scheme delivery (Saat Nischay, housing, sanitation), Gram Kachahari justice.
  - Problems: proxy rule, weak devolution of 3Fs, low own revenue, capacity gaps, corruption, bureaucratic control.
  - Reforms: training (Bihar Institute of Panchayat and Rural Development), JEEViKA linkages, e-Gram Swaraj, social audit, stronger SFC.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's model widened democracy; deepening it needs real powers and money at the village level.

**2. "The 73rd and 74th Amendments were a silent revolution in Indian democracy." Discuss. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Constitutional status in 1992; third tier of government.
- **Body:** Key provisions (Gram Sabha, elections, SEC, SFC, reservation, schedules); impact (lakhs of elected representatives, about half women; inclusion of SC/ST/OBC); urban side (wards, DPC, MPC); limits (voluntary devolution, weak finances, delayed elections, parastatals in cities); Bihar's lead in women's reservation and direct election of mayors.
- **Conclusion:** A revolution in representation; the next step is a revolution in powers.

**3. Discuss the role of the Gram Kachahari in delivering justice in rural Bihar. (15–20 marks, short answer)**

- **Intro:** Village court under the Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006.
- **Body:** Composition (Sarpanch, Panchs, Nyay Mitra, Nyay Sachiv); jurisdiction (petty civil and criminal cases); conciliation; no lawyers; benefits (cheap, quick, local language, reduces burden on courts); problems (limited training, elite influence, enforcement of orders).
- **Conclusion:** A low-cost model of access to justice that needs training and support.
