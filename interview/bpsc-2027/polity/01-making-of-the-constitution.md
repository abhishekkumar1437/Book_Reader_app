This chapter tells the story of how India got its Constitution. First come the British Acts (1773 to 1947) that slowly built the machinery of government. Then comes the Constituent Assembly (1946–1949) that wrote the Constitution. BPSC asks direct fact questions here ("Who was the interim President of the Constituent Assembly?", "Which Act introduced dyarchy?") and the Bihar link is strong: two of the Assembly's Presidents came from Bihar.

> **In simple words:** The British passed many laws to run India. Each law added something new — a Governor-General, councils, elections, Indian members, provincial self-rule. The Government of India Act 1935 was the biggest of these and became the skeleton of our Constitution. After 1946, Indians themselves sat in a Constituent Assembly, debated for almost three years, and produced the Constitution that started on 26 January 1950.

## Why this chapter matters

- **Prelims:** Acts and their "firsts" (first Governor-General of India, first separate electorate, first dyarchy) are standard one-liners. Dates of the Constituent Assembly and names of committee chairmen are tested every few years.
- **Mains (GS Paper II):** questions like "Trace the evolution of the Indian Constitution" or "The Government of India Act 1935 was the blueprint of the Constitution — discuss" need this content.
- **Bihar angle:** Dr Sachchidananda Sinha (interim President), Dr Rajendra Prasad (permanent President) and Bihar's members of the Assembly.

## Part 1 — Company rule (1773–1858)

The East India Company came as traders in 1600. After Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764), and the grant of the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (1765), it became a ruling power. The British Parliament then began to control the Company through Acts.

### Regulating Act, 1773

- First step by the British Parliament to **control and regulate** the Company in India.
- The Governor of Bengal became the **Governor-General of Bengal**. The first was **Warren Hastings**.
- Created an **Executive Council of four** members to assist him.
- Governors of Bombay and Madras were made subordinate to the Governor-General of Bengal.
- Provided for a **Supreme Court at Calcutta (Fort William), set up in 1774**, with Sir Elijah Impey as the first Chief Justice.
- Company servants were barred from private trade and from taking bribes.

### Pitt's India Act, 1784

- Separated the **commercial** and **political** functions of the Company.
- Created a **Board of Control** (political affairs) alongside the **Court of Directors** (commercial affairs). This is called **dual government** (double government).
- For the first time the Company's territories were called "**British possessions in India**".

### Charter Act, 1813

- Ended the Company's **trade monopoly** in India, except for trade in **tea** and trade with **China**.
- Set aside **₹1 lakh a year** for the education of Indians.
- Allowed Christian missionaries to come to India.

### Charter Act, 1833

- Made the Governor-General of Bengal the **Governor-General of India**. The first was **Lord William Bentinck**.
- This was the **final step towards centralisation**: legislative powers were given only to the Governor-General of India.
- Ended the Company's commercial activities completely; it became a purely administrative body.
- Tried to introduce open competition for civil services (not put into practice).

### Charter Act, 1853

- **Separated the legislative and executive functions** of the Governor-General's Council. A separate Legislative Council (the "Indian Legislative Council") was created — a mini-Parliament.
- Introduced **open competition** for the civil services, open to Indians also. (The Macaulay Committee of 1854 followed.)
- Introduced local representation in the Legislative Council.
- The last Charter Act — the Company's rule was not renewed for a fixed period after this.

> **Memory trick:** "**R**eally **P**oor **C**ompany **C**an't **C**ontinue" — **R**egulating 1773, **P**itt's 1784, **C**harter 1813, **C**harter 1833, **C**harter 1853. The last "C" (1853) ends the Charter series.

## Part 2 — Crown rule (1858–1947)

### Government of India Act, 1858

Passed after the Revolt of 1857 (in which **Kunwar Singh of Jagdishpur** led the rising in Bihar).

- Ended Company rule. India was governed **directly by the British Crown**.
- The Governor-General became the **Viceroy** of India (the Crown's representative). The first Viceroy was **Lord Canning**.
- Created the post of **Secretary of State for India**, a member of the British cabinet, with a **15-member Council of India**.
- Abolished the Board of Control and Court of Directors (ended dual government).

### Indian Councils Act, 1861

- Began **association of Indians** with law-making: the Viceroy could nominate some Indians as non-official members. (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala and Sir Dinkar Rao were nominated in 1862.)
- Started **decentralisation** — restored legislative powers to Bombay and Madras.
- Gave **statutory recognition to the portfolio system** introduced by Lord Canning in 1859.
- Empowered the Viceroy to issue **ordinances** in an emergency (valid six months).

### Indian Councils Act, 1892

- Increased non-official members.
- Allowed members to **discuss the budget** and **ask questions** (but no supplementary questions and no vote).
- Introduced a limited and **indirect** method of election (the word "election" was not used; it was "nomination on recommendation").

### Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley–Minto Reforms)

- **Lord Morley** was Secretary of State; **Lord Minto** was Viceroy.
- Introduced **separate electorates for Muslims** — Muslim members were to be elected only by Muslim voters. Lord Minto is called the "**Father of Communal Electorate**".
- Enlarged councils; allowed **supplementary questions** and resolutions on the budget.
- **Satyendra Prasanna Sinha** became the first Indian member of the Viceroy's Executive Council (as Law Member).

### Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms)

- **Montagu** was Secretary of State; **Chelmsford** was Viceroy.
- Divided subjects into **central** and **provincial** lists.
- Introduced **dyarchy (dual rule) in the provinces**. Provincial subjects were split into **transferred** subjects (run by the Governor with Indian ministers responsible to the legislature — e.g. education, health, local government) and **reserved** subjects (run by the Governor and his executive council, not responsible — e.g. law and order, finance).
- Introduced **bicameralism** (two houses) and **direct elections** at the Centre: the Indian Legislative Assembly and the Council of State.
- Extended communal representation to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans**.
- Granted franchise on the basis of property, tax or education (a limited number of people).
- Provided for a **Public Service Commission** — the Central Public Service Commission was set up in **1926**.
- Separated provincial budgets from the central budget.
- Created the office of **High Commissioner for India** in London.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar and Orissa became a separate province from Bengal in **1912** (proclamation of 22 March 1912). Under the 1919 Act, the province of Bihar and Orissa got its own legislative council with dyarchy. In **1936** Orissa was separated, and Bihar became a province on its own. Under the 1935 Act, Bihar got a two-house (bicameral) legislature in **1937**.

### Simon Commission (1927) and after

- A seven-member statutory commission under **Sir John Simon**, appointed in November 1927 to review the 1919 Act. All members were British, so Indians boycotted it ("Simon Go Back"). It reached India in 1928. (In Patna too, large protests were held.)
- Its 1930 report led to three **Round Table Conferences** (1930–32).
- The **Communal Award (1932)** by British PM Ramsay MacDonald extended separate electorates to the Depressed Classes. Gandhi's fast in Yerawada jail led to the **Poona Pact (1932)**: reserved seats for Depressed Classes within the joint Hindu electorate.

### Government of India Act, 1935

The longest and most important British Act. **321 sections and 10 schedules.** It is called the "blueprint" of the Constitution — about **250 provisions** of our Constitution are taken from it, either word for word or with changes.

- Proposed an **All-India Federation** of provinces and princely states. This **never came into being** because princely states did not join.
- Divided powers into three lists: **Federal List (59 items)**, **Provincial List (54 items)** and **Concurrent List (36 items)**. **Residuary powers** went to the Viceroy.
- **Abolished dyarchy in the provinces** and introduced **provincial autonomy** — provinces got responsible government. This came into effect in **1937** (elections of 1937; Congress formed ministries in most provinces including Bihar, with **Sri Krishna Sinha** as Premier of Bihar).
- Introduced **dyarchy at the Centre** (this was never implemented).
- Introduced **bicameralism in six of the eleven provinces** — Bengal, Bombay, Madras, **Bihar**, Assam and the United Provinces.
- Extended separate electorates to Depressed Classes, women and labour.
- Abolished the Council of India (Secretary of State got advisers instead).
- Provided for a **Federal Public Service Commission**, **Provincial Public Service Commissions** and **Joint Public Service Commissions**.
- Established the **Reserve Bank of India** (1935) to control currency and credit.
- Established a **Federal Court** — set up in **1937**.
- Extended the franchise — about **10% of the population** got the right to vote.

> **Prelims angle:** Classic traps: (1) Dyarchy in provinces = 1919; dyarchy at the Centre = 1935 (never implemented). (2) Provincial autonomy = 1935. (3) Bicameral Centre = 1919; bicameral provinces = 1935. (4) Federal Court = 1937 under the 1935 Act. (5) Portfolio system = Lord Canning, recognised in 1861.

### Indian Independence Act, 1947

- Based on the **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)**.
- Ended British rule; India became an **independent and sovereign state** from **15 August 1947**.
- Partitioned India into two dominions — **India and Pakistan** — with the right to leave the British Commonwealth.
- Abolished the office of Viceroy; created a **Governor-General** for each dominion, appointed on the advice of the dominion cabinet.
- Made the **Constituent Assembly** of each dominion sovereign — free to frame any constitution and to repeal any British Act, including the Independence Act itself.
- Gave the Constituent Assembly a second role as the **Dominion Legislature** (it made ordinary laws also).
- Princely states were free to join India or Pakistan or remain independent. British paramountcy lapsed.
- **Lord Mountbatten** became the first Governor-General of free India. **C. Rajagopalachari** became the **first and last Indian Governor-General** (1948–50).

## Part 3 — The Constituent Assembly

### The idea

- **M.N. Roy** first put forward the idea of a Constituent Assembly for India in **1934**.
- The **Indian National Congress** officially demanded it in **1935**.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** said in **1938** that the Constitution of free India must be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected by adult franchise.
- The British accepted the demand in principle in the **August Offer of 1940**.
- The **Cripps Mission (1942)** proposed a constituent body after the war — rejected by the Muslim League.
- The **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)** finally set up the Constituent Assembly.

### Composition under the Cabinet Mission Plan

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Total strength | **389** |
| From British India (provinces) | **296** (292 from the 11 governors' provinces + 4 from the chief commissioners' provinces) |
| From princely states | **93** |
| Ratio | Roughly **1 seat per 10 lakh (1 million) population** |
| Seats divided among | Muslims, Sikhs and General (others), in proportion to their population |
| Method | **Indirect election** by members of provincial legislative assemblies, using **proportional representation by single transferable vote** |
| Princely states' members | Nominated by the heads of the princely states |
| Elections held | **July–August 1946** — Congress won 208 seats, Muslim League 73 |
| After partition (1947) | Strength fell to **299** (229 provinces + 70 princely states) |

> **Remember:** The Constituent Assembly was **not directly elected** by the people. It was partly elected (indirectly, by provincial assemblies) and partly nominated (princely states). Yet it represented almost every community — Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Christians, SCs, STs and women.

### Working of the Assembly — key dates

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **9 December 1946** | First meeting. The Muslim League boycotted it. 211 members attended. **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha**, the oldest member, was elected **temporary (interim) President**, following the French practice. |
| **11 December 1946** | **Dr Rajendra Prasad** elected **President**. **H.C. Mukherjee** and **V.T. Krishnamachari** later became Vice-Presidents. **Sir B.N. Rau** was the **Constitutional Adviser**. |
| **13 December 1946** | **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the **Objectives Resolution** — the philosophy of the Constitution. |
| **22 January 1947** | Objectives Resolution **adopted** unanimously. (It later became the **Preamble**.) |
| **22 July 1947** | **National Flag** adopted. |
| **29 August 1947** | **Drafting Committee** set up under **Dr B.R. Ambedkar**. |
| **February 1948** | First Draft Constitution published. People got 8 months to discuss it. |
| **May 1949** | Ratified India's membership of the Commonwealth. |
| **26 November 1949** | Constitution **adopted** ("We, the people… adopt, enact and give to ourselves"). Celebrated as **Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas** since 2015 (earlier "National Law Day"). |
| **24 January 1950** | Last session. Members **signed** the Constitution (284 members signed). **National Anthem** and **National Song** adopted. **Dr Rajendra Prasad elected first President of India**. |
| **26 January 1950** | Constitution **came into force** — Republic Day. This date was chosen because on 26 January 1930 the Congress had celebrated **Purna Swaraj Day** (after the Lahore session, December 1929). |

- On 26 November 1949, some provisions came into force at once: **citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, temporary and transitional provisions, and the short title** (Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393). The rest came into force on 26 January 1950.
- Time taken: **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**. **11 sessions**, sitting for **165 days**. The draft was considered for 114 days.
- Total expenditure: about **₹64 lakh**.
- About **7,600 amendments** were proposed to the draft; about **2,400** were actually moved/discussed.
- The original Constitution had **395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules**.
- The symbol (seal) of the Constituent Assembly was the **elephant**.
- **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** hand-wrote (calligraphy) the original English copy; artists of Shantiniketan under **Nandalal Bose** decorated it. The Hindi copy was hand-written by **Vasant Krishan Vaidya**.

> **Bihar connection:** **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** (born 1871, Murar village, now in Buxar district) was a lawyer, journalist and the leading campaigner for the **separation of Bihar from Bengal (1912)**. He founded the journal *The Hindustan Review* and was later Vice-Chancellor of Patna University. The **Sinha Library** in Patna is named after him. **Dr Rajendra Prasad** (born 1884, Ziradei, Siwan) was a hero of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), Congress President (1934, and again in 1939 and 1947), President of the Constituent Assembly, the **first President of India (1950–62)**, the only President elected **twice**, and received the **Bharat Ratna in 1962**. He spent his last days at Sadaquat Ashram, Patna. Other members elected from Bihar included **Jagjivan Ram** and **Jaipal Singh Munda**, who spoke powerfully for the Adivasis.

### Dual role and other work of the Assembly

- After independence the Assembly also worked as the **Dominion Legislature** (Parliament). When it met as a law-making body, **G.V. Mavalankar** presided; when it met as the constitution-making body, **Rajendra Prasad** presided.
- It continued as the **Provisional Parliament** from 26 January 1950 until the first Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha were formed after the **first general elections (1951–52)**.
- It also adopted the national flag, anthem and song, ratified Commonwealth membership, and elected the first President.

### The Drafting Committee (seven members)

1. **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** (Chairman)
2. **N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar**
3. **Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar**
4. **Dr K.M. Munshi**
5. **Syed Mohammad Saadullah**
6. **N. Madhava Rau** (replaced B.L. Mitter, who resigned due to ill health)
7. **T.T. Krishnamachari** (replaced D.P. Khaitan, who died in 1948)

Ambedkar is called the "**Father of the Indian Constitution**" and the "**chief architect**" of the Constitution. He was the first Law Minister of India.

> **Common mistake:** B.N. Rau was the **Constitutional Adviser**, not a member of the Drafting Committee. He prepared the first rough draft (October 1947) that the Drafting Committee worked on. He later became a judge of the International Court of Justice.

## Criticism of the Constituent Assembly

| Criticism | Reply usually given |
|---|---|
| **Not a representative body** — not directly elected by universal adult franchise | Members came from every community; the Congress was itself a broad national movement; direct elections in 1946 were not practical |
| **Not a sovereign body** — created by the British Cabinet Mission | The Indian Independence Act 1947 made it fully sovereign |
| **Time-consuming** — the US Constitution was made in 4 months | India's size, diversity and the partition demanded careful debate |
| **Dominated by Congress** — "the Assembly is the Congress and the Congress is India" (Granville Austin) | Congress gave seats to independent experts like Ambedkar, Alladi, Gopalaswamy Ayyangar |
| **Lawyer-politician domination** — Ivor Jennings called the Constitution a "**lawyers' paradise**" | Lawyers were needed for precise drafting |
| **Dominated by Hindus** | Members of all religions were present; decisions were by consensus |
| **"Borrowed Constitution"** / "a patchwork" / "a slavish copy of the 1935 Act" | Borrowed features were adapted to Indian needs; Ambedkar replied that there is nothing to be ashamed of in borrowing |

> **Mains angle:** When asked to "evaluate the Constituent Assembly", give both sides. Use quotes you are sure of: Granville Austin called the Constitution "first and foremost a social document". Ambedkar warned on 25 November 1949 that political democracy must be made a social democracy, and that we must give up "the grammar of anarchy" (unconstitutional methods). Mention the Bihar contribution (Sinha, Prasad) for BPSC.

## Enactment and commencement — the Hindi text

- The **58th Amendment Act (1987)** gave authority to the President to publish an **authoritative Hindi text** of the Constitution (Article 394A).
- **Article 393** — short title "Constitution of India". **Article 394** — commencement. **Article 395** — repealed the Indian Independence Act 1947 and the GoI Act 1935 (but not the Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act 1949).

## Integration of princely states (brief)

- About **552 princely states** were within the territory of India in 1947. All but three (Hyderabad, Junagarh, Kashmir) joined India through the **Instrument of Accession** before 15 August 1947.
- **Junagarh** — by referendum (1948); **Hyderabad** — by police action ("Operation Polo", September 1948); **Kashmir** — by Instrument of Accession (October 1947).
- **Sardar Patel** as Home Minister and **V.P. Menon** as Secretary led the integration.
- The Constitution in 1950 classified states into **Part A, B, C and D**. The **7th Amendment (1956)** abolished this classification after the **States Reorganisation Act 1956** (based on the Fazl Ali Commission). Andhra (1953) was the first linguistic state.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar was a **Part A** state in 1950. In **1956** the States Reorganisation brought small boundary changes (some areas went to West Bengal, e.g. Purulia). On **15 November 2000**, the **Bihar Reorganisation Act 2000** created **Jharkhand** from southern Bihar. Bihar's Legislative Assembly seats fell from **324 to 243**.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Dyarchy | "Double rule" — subjects divided between two authorities, one responsible to the legislature and one not |
| Provincial autonomy | Provinces run by elected ministers, free from central control in their own subjects |
| Separate electorate | Voters of one community elect their own representatives separately |
| Constituent Assembly | A body elected to write a constitution |
| Objectives Resolution | Nehru's 1946 resolution stating the aims of the Constitution; basis of the Preamble |
| Dominion | A self-governing country that still recognised the British Crown (India 1947–50) |
| Instrument of Accession | Legal document by which a princely state joined India |
| Single transferable vote | A method where voters rank candidates; surplus votes move to the next choice |
| Portfolio system | Each member of the executive council handles one department |
| Residuary powers | Powers over subjects not listed in any list |

### Answers

1. Regulating Act, 1773.
2. Charter Act, 1813.
3. Lord William Bentinck (Charter Act 1833).
4. Government of India Act, 1919.
5. Lord Minto (Indian Councils Act 1909).
6. Government of India Act 1935; the Federal Court started in 1937.
7. M.N. Roy (1934).
8. 389 (296 British India + 93 princely states); 299 after partition.
9. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha of Bihar, chosen as the oldest member, following French practice.
10. 22 January 1947.
11. Union Powers — Jawaharlal Nehru; Provincial Constitution — Sardar Patel.
12. Sir B.N. Rau.
13. To honour Purna Swaraj Day, first celebrated on 26 January 1930.
14. 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules.
15. 1937 (elections of 1937), under the Government of India Act 1935.

### Mains questions

**1. "The Government of India Act 1935 was the blueprint of the Indian Constitution." Discuss. (38 marks, about 300 words)**

- **Intro:** The 1935 Act (321 sections, 10 schedules) was the longest British Act for India; about 250 of our provisions draw on it.
- **Body:**
  - Federal scheme and three lists → Seventh Schedule (Union, State, Concurrent).
  - Office of Governor, emergency powers (Sections 45, 93) → Articles 352, 356.
  - Federal Court → Supreme Court; Public Service Commissions → UPSC, SPSCs (BPSC).
  - Administrative details, RBI, bicameral provincial legislatures (Bihar's council since 1937).
  - Differences: the Constitution added Fundamental Rights, DPSP, universal adult franchise, a sovereign people, a republic, parliamentary responsibility at the Centre.
  - Ambedkar's defence of borrowing.
- **Conclusion:** The 1935 Act gave the framework; the Constituent Assembly gave it a democratic soul.

**2. Evaluate the role of the Constituent Assembly in making the Indian Constitution, and highlight Bihar's contribution. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan; first sat on 9 December 1946.
- **Body:**
  - Composition and representativeness (389 → 299; all communities, 15 women members).
  - Method: committees, Objectives Resolution, consensus and accommodation.
  - Main achievements: a long, written, democratic constitution in under three years; adult franchise from day one.
  - Criticisms and replies (not directly elected, Congress dominance, lawyers' paradise).
  - Bihar's role: Sachchidananda Sinha as interim President; Rajendra Prasad as President, steering and procedure committees; members like Jagjivan Ram and Jaipal Singh Munda raising Dalit and Adivasi concerns.
- **Conclusion:** The Assembly turned a freedom struggle's values into a working legal document; Bihar's leaders presided over it.

**3. Trace the constitutional development of India from 1858 to 1947. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** After 1857, the Crown took over; each Act widened Indian participation.
- **Body:** 1858 (Crown, Viceroy); 1861 (nominated Indians, portfolios); 1892 (budget discussion); 1909 (separate electorate); 1919 (dyarchy, bicameral Centre); 1935 (provincial autonomy, federation); 1947 (independence, sovereign CA). Add the national movement's pressure behind each step (Congress 1885, Swadeshi, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Quit India).
- **Conclusion:** A slow move from autocratic rule to self-government, driven by Indian demands.
