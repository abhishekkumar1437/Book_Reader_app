This chapter covers how power and money are divided between the Union and the states (Part XI and XII), and the emergency provisions (Part XVIII). It is a favourite of BPSC because Bihar is a poorer state that depends heavily on central transfers, has demanded **special category status** for years, and has seen **President's Rule eight times**. Expect Prelims questions on Article 249, 252, 356 and the GST Council, and Mains questions on cooperative federalism and the misuse of Article 356.

> **In simple words:** The Constitution gives the Union and the states their own lists of subjects and their own taxes. But the Union is stronger — it collects most of the big taxes and shares them with states through the Finance Commission. In an emergency, the system can become almost unitary: the Centre can take over a state's government (President's Rule) or even direct states' finances.

## Legislative relations (Articles 245–255)

### Territorial extent

- **Article 245** — Parliament can make laws for the **whole or any part of India**, including **extra-territorial** laws (applying to Indians and property abroad). State legislatures make laws for the whole or part of the **state**.
- Exceptions: the President can make regulations for some UTs (Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Ladakh); Governors can modify laws for **Scheduled Areas** (Fifth Schedule); Sixth Schedule areas have special rules.

### The three lists (Article 246, Seventh Schedule)

| List | Who makes laws | Number of items (original → as commonly given now) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **List I — Union List** | **Parliament** only | **97 → about 98** | Defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, atomic energy, railways, posts, census, citizenship, insurance, corporation tax, income tax (non-agricultural) |
| **List II — State List** | **State legislatures** (normally) | **66 → about 59** | **Public order, police**, public health and sanitation, hospitals, agriculture, land, local government, **liquor**, betting and gambling, fisheries, markets, **agricultural income tax**, land revenue |
| **List III — Concurrent List** | **Both**; Union law prevails in conflict | **47 → about 52** | Criminal law and procedure, marriage and divorce, forests, **education**, population control, electricity, trade unions, economic and social planning, newspapers, **weights and measures**, wildlife protection, **administration of justice** |

- The **42nd Amendment (1976)** moved **five subjects** from the State List to the Concurrent List: **education**, **forests**, **weights and measures**, **protection of wild animals and birds**, and **administration of justice** (constitution and organisation of all courts except the SC and HCs).
- **Residuary powers** (subjects in none of the lists) belong to **Parliament** (**Article 248**, Entry 97 of List I) — the Canadian model. E.g. cyber laws. (In the USA and Australia, residuary powers lie with the states.)
- **Article 246A** (**101st Amendment, 2016**) — Parliament **and** state legislatures both have power to make laws on **GST**; only Parliament for inter-state supplies.

> **Memory trick:** The five subjects shifted in 1976: "**E**very **F**orest **W**ants **W**ild **J**ustice" — **E**ducation, **F**orests, **W**eights & measures, **W**ild animals & birds, **J**ustice (administration of).

### When Parliament can make laws on State List subjects

| Article | Situation | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **249** | **National interest** | **Rajya Sabha** resolution supported by **two-thirds of members present and voting**; the law is valid for **1 year**, renewable any number of times; ceases **6 months** after the resolution ends |
| **250** | **National emergency** in operation | Law ceases 6 months after the emergency ends |
| **252** | **Two or more states request** by resolution | Applies only to those states (others can adopt later); **only Parliament** can amend/repeal it. Examples: **Prize Competition Act 1955**, **Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972**, **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974**, **Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) Act 1976**, **Transplantation of Human Organs Act 1994** |
| **253** | To implement **international treaties**, agreements or decisions | E.g. **United Nations (Privileges and Immunities) Act 1947**, **Geneva Convention Act 1960**, **Anti-Hijacking Act 1982**, environment and TRIPS-related laws |
| **356** | **President's Rule** in a state | Parliament makes the state's laws (or delegates to the President) |

### Centre's control over state legislation

- **Article 200/201** — the Governor can **reserve** certain bills for the President; the President has an **absolute veto** over them.
- Some bills need the **President's prior sanction** — e.g. bills imposing restrictions on freedom of trade (**Article 304(b)**).
- During a **financial emergency**, the President can direct that **money bills** be reserved.
- **Article 254 — repugnancy**: if a state law on a Concurrent subject conflicts with a central law, the **central law prevails**; but if the state law was **reserved and received the President's assent**, the **state law prevails** in that state (Article 254(2)) — Parliament can still override it later.

> **Bihar connection:** **Liquor** is a **State List** subject (Entry 8), which is why **Bihar** could enforce total **prohibition** (Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016) on its own. **Police and public order** are also State subjects, so law and order in Bihar is the state government's responsibility, though the Centre helps with central armed police forces.

## Administrative relations (Articles 256–263)

- **Article 256** — states must exercise executive power so as to **comply with laws made by Parliament**; the Centre can give directions.
- **Article 257** — the Centre can give **directions to states** on: construction and maintenance of means of communication of national/military importance, protection of **railways** in the state, and other matters. **Article 365** — if a state fails to follow Union directions, the President may hold that its constitutional machinery has failed (leading to Article 356).
- **Article 258** — the President can **entrust** Union functions to a state with its consent; **258A** — a state can entrust its functions to the Union.
- **Article 261** — **full faith and credit** to public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and every state.
- **Article 262** — Parliament can provide for adjudication of **inter-state river water disputes** and exclude the jurisdiction of the SC. Laws: **Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956**, **River Boards Act 1956**. Tribunals: Krishna, Godavari, Narmada, Cauvery, Ravi-Beas, Vansadhara, Mahadayi, Mahanadi.
- **Article 263** — the President can set up an **Inter-State Council**. It was set up in **1990** (on the **Sarkaria Commission**'s recommendation). **Chairman: PM**; members: CMs of all states, CMs of UTs with legislatures, administrators of other UTs, six Union cabinet ministers (including the Home Minister). Its **Standing Committee** is chaired by the Union Home Minister.
- **All-India Services** (**Article 312**) — **IAS, IPS** (created 1947 as successors of ICS and IP) and **Indian Forest Service (1966)**. Recruited by the Centre (UPSC), serve in states, controlled jointly. New AIS can be created only if the **Rajya Sabha** passes a resolution by **two-thirds of present and voting**. Sardar Patel called them the "**steel frame**" of India.
- **Article 355** — duty of the Union to **protect every state** against external aggression and internal disturbance, and to ensure that every state is governed according to the Constitution. The Centre can deploy **central armed police forces** in a state (even without the state's consent, per the 1990s debate; Sarkaria recommended consultation).

### Zonal Councils (statutory, not constitutional)

- Set up under the **States Reorganisation Act 1956** — **five** zonal councils: **Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern**. Chaired by the **Union Home Minister**; vice-chairman rotates among the CMs.
- The **Eastern Zonal Council** = **Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal** (secretariat at Kolkata).
- The **North Eastern Council** was created separately by the **North Eastern Council Act 1971** (Sikkim added in 2002).

## Financial relations (Articles 264–293)

### Distribution of taxes

| Article | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **268** | Levied by the **Union** but **collected and appropriated by states** | Stamp duties on bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes |
| **269** | Levied and collected by the **Union** but **assigned to the states** | Taxes on inter-state sale/purchase of goods (other than newspapers) and inter-state consignment of goods |
| **269A** | **GST on inter-state supplies (IGST)** levied and collected by the Union, apportioned between Union and states as per GST Council recommendations | IGST |
| **270** | Levied and collected by the Union, **distributed between Union and states** — the **divisible pool** | **Income tax, corporation tax, Union excise, CGST**, etc. (except cesses, surcharges and Art 268, 269) |
| **271** | **Surcharges** on taxes — **exclusively for the Union** (not shared) | Surcharges on income tax |
| **275** | **Statutory grants-in-aid** to states in need (on Finance Commission's recommendation); special grants for **tribal welfare** and Scheduled Areas | Revenue deficit grants |
| **282** | **Discretionary grants** by the Union or a state for any **public purpose** | Grants for centrally sponsored schemes |
| **293** | State borrowing — within India only; needs the Centre's consent if the state owes money to the Centre | |

### Goods and Services Tax (GST)

- **101st Amendment Act, 2016** — introduced GST from **1 July 2017**. It subsumed central excise, service tax, VAT, entry tax, entertainment tax (except by local bodies), etc.
- **Article 246A** — concurrent power over GST. **Article 269A** — IGST. **Article 279A** — **GST Council**.
- **GST Council** — **Chairperson: Union Finance Minister**; members: Union MoS (Finance/Revenue) and the **Finance (or nominated) Ministers of all states**. Voting: the **Centre has one-third** weight and the **states together two-thirds**; decisions need **three-fourths** of weighted votes; quorum one-half.
- **Compensation** to states for revenue loss for **5 years** (till **June 2022**) under the GST (Compensation to States) Act 2017; the compensation cess was extended to repay loans.
- ***Union of India v Mohit Minerals (2022)*** — GST Council recommendations are **persuasive, not binding**; Parliament and states have simultaneous powers — an expression of cooperative federalism.
- **"GST 2.0" (September 2025)** — the rate structure was simplified mainly to two slabs (**5%** and **18%**) with a special higher rate for luxury/sin goods (check the latest rates).
- Excluded from GST: **alcohol for human consumption** (state excise), **petroleum products** (crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas — "zero-rated" until the Council decides), and electricity.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar is a **consuming state** (it buys more goods from outside than it sells), so a **destination-based** tax like GST was expected to help it. Its own tax base is small; Bihar depends on central transfers (share in central taxes plus grants) for a very large part of its revenue — this is a key Mains point. Check the latest figures in the [Bihar economy chapter](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

### Finance Commission (Article 280)

- Constituted by the **President every 5 years** (or earlier). **Chairman + 4 members**; qualifications fixed by the **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1951**. It is a **quasi-judicial** body.
- Recommends: (a) **vertical devolution** — share of states in the divisible pool; (b) **horizontal distribution** — share of each state; (c) principles for **grants-in-aid** (Article 275); (d) measures to augment **Consolidated Funds of states** to supplement **panchayats and municipalities** (added by the 73rd/74th Amendments); (e) any other matter referred.
- Recommendations are **advisory**; laid before Parliament with an explanatory memorandum.
- **First FC: K.C. Neogy (1951)**.

| Finance Commission | Chairman | Period | States' share (vertical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13th | Vijay Kelkar | 2010–15 | 32% |
| **14th** | **Y.V. Reddy** | 2015–20 | **42%** (big jump; general distinction between special and general category states effectively ended) |
| **15th** | **N.K. Singh** | 2020–21 and 2021–26 | **41%** (1% less because J&K became a UT) |
| **16th** | **Arvind Panagariya** | **2026–31** | **41%** (retained; report tabled 1 February 2026 — check the latest details) |

- **Horizontal criteria** of the 15th FC: income distance **45%**, population (2011) **15%**, area **15%**, forest and ecology **10%**, demographic performance **12.5%**, tax effort **2.5%**. The **16th FC** reduced the weight for income distance (to **42.5%**) and introduced a new criterion for a state's **contribution to GDP** (about **10%**) — check the final weights.
- **Bihar's share** under the 15th FC was about **10.06%** — the **second largest after Uttar Pradesh** (about 17.9%), mainly due to high population and low per-capita income (check the 16th FC share for 2026–31).

> **Prelims angle:** Traps: the **Finance Commission** is **constitutional** (Art 280); the **Planning Commission** and **NITI Aayog** are **not** (executive resolutions of 1950 and 2015). The **State Finance Commission** is under **Article 243I** (panchayats) and **243Y** (municipalities). **Surcharges and cesses are not shared** with states (Article 271).

### Special category status

- Not mentioned in the Constitution. Created by the **National Development Council (NDC) in 1969** (Gadgil formula) for states with **hilly terrain, low population density or sizeable tribal population, strategic border location, economic and infrastructural backwardness, and non-viable finances**. Eleven states had it (the NE states, J&K, HP, Uttarakhand).
- After the **14th FC** (2015), the distinction was effectively ended for tax devolution; special category states still get a 90:10 central share in centrally sponsored schemes.
- **Bihar's demand:** Bihar has demanded **special category status** for many years, citing low per-capita income, floods, lack of minerals after 2000, and poor infrastructure. The **Raghuram Rajan Committee (2013)** on a composite development index placed Bihar among the "**least developed**" states. The Centre has instead offered **special packages** (e.g. the **2015 Bihar package**, and **special assistance** for roads, power, flood control and other projects in the **2024–25 Union Budget**) — check the latest.

> **Mains angle:** Use these terms in federalism answers: **cooperative federalism** (GST Council, NITI Aayog Governing Council, Inter-State Council), **competitive federalism** (states competing for investment, rankings like Ease of Doing Business), **fiscal federalism** (Finance Commission, cesses and surcharges reducing the divisible pool), and **asymmetric federalism** (Article 371, Fifth/Sixth Schedule). Bihar's view: higher devolution, more weight to backwardness, special category or special packages, flood-control funding for rivers originating in Nepal.

## Emergency provisions (Part XVIII, Articles 352–360)

Borrowed (suspension of FRs) from the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**. Three types:

### 1. National Emergency (Article 352)

- **Grounds:** **war**, **external aggression** or **armed rebellion** (the words "**internal disturbance**" were replaced by "**armed rebellion**" by the **44th Amendment, 1978**). Can be declared even **before** the actual occurrence if there is imminent danger. Can cover the whole of India or a part.
- **Declaration:** by the President only on the **written recommendation of the Union Cabinet** (44th Amendment).
- **Approval:** by **both Houses** within **one month**, by a **special majority** (majority of total membership + two-thirds of present and voting).
- **Duration:** **6 months** at a time, renewable indefinitely every 6 months with special majority approval.
- **Revocation:** by the President at any time; **must** revoke if the **Lok Sabha** passes a resolution **disapproving** it by **simple majority**. **One-tenth** of LS members can give notice for a special sitting (within **14 days**) for this.
- **Effects:**
  - **Centre–state:** the Union can give **executive directions** to states on any matter; Parliament can make laws on **State List** subjects (**Article 250**); the President can modify the distribution of revenues.
  - **Life of the Lok Sabha** and state Assemblies can be extended by **one year at a time**.
  - **Fundamental Rights:** **Article 358** — **Article 19 is suspended automatically** when the emergency is declared on grounds of **war or external aggression** (not armed rebellion). **Article 359** — the President can suspend the right to move courts for other FRs, **except Articles 20 and 21**.
- **Proclaimed three times:**

| Year | Ground | President | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **October 1962** | External aggression — **China** | Dr S. Radhakrishnan | Lasted till January 1968 |
| **December 1971** | External aggression — **Pakistan** war | V.V. Giri | Continued till March 1977 |
| **25 June 1975** | **Internal disturbance** | **Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed** (PM Indira Gandhi) | Revoked **March 1977**; led to the **Shah Commission** and the **44th Amendment** |

> **Bihar connection:** The **1975 Emergency** followed the **JP Movement** ("**Sampoorna Kranti**" / Total Revolution), which began with the **Bihar students' movement of 1974** led by **Jayaprakash Narayan** of Sitab Diara (Saran/Ballia border). JP's rally at the **Ramlila Ground, Delhi, on 25 June 1975** came just before the proclamation. Many Bihar leaders (Lalu Prasad, Nitish Kumar, Sushil Kumar Modi, Ram Vilas Paswan) emerged from this movement. The Janata Party won the **1977** elections.

### 2. President's Rule / State Emergency (Article 356)

- **Ground:** the President is satisfied, on the **Governor's report or otherwise**, that the government of the state **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution** ("failure of constitutional machinery"). Also **Article 365** — failure to comply with Union directions.
- **Approval:** by **both Houses** within **two months**, by a **simple majority**.
- **Duration:** **6 months** at a time; **maximum 3 years** with approval every 6 months. Beyond **1 year** only if (44th Amendment): (a) a **national emergency** is in operation in the state, **and** (b) the **Election Commission certifies** that elections cannot be held.
- **Revocation:** by the President at any time; no parliamentary approval needed.
- **Effects:**
  - The President takes over the **functions of the state government** (through the **Governor** and advisers); the CM and Council of Ministers are dismissed.
  - The state **legislature is suspended** (kept in animation) or **dissolved**.
  - **Parliament** makes laws for the state (or delegates to the President — "**President's Acts**"); passes the **state budget**.
  - The **High Court** continues to function normally.
  - **Fundamental Rights are not affected**.
- Used **more than 100 times** since 1950 — the first time in **Punjab (1951)**. Often misused against opposition-ruled states (Ambedkar had hoped it would remain a "**dead letter**").

**Judicial review of Article 356 — S.R. Bommai v Union of India (1994)** (9-judge bench):
- President's Rule is **subject to judicial review**; the court can examine the material behind it and **restore** a dismissed government.
- The **floor of the Assembly** is the only place to test majority — not the Governor's opinion.
- The state Assembly should **not be dissolved** until Parliament approves the proclamation; until then it can only be **suspended**.
- **Secularism** is part of the basic structure; a state government pursuing anti-secular policies can be dismissed.
- Article 356 must be used only in case of **breakdown of constitutional machinery**, not for political or administrative reasons (e.g. maladministration, corruption, internal party problems).

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar has had **President's Rule eight times — 1968, 1969, 1972, 1977, 1980, 1995, 1999 and 2005**. The **1999** proclamation was **revoked within about a month** because the government did not have a majority in the Rajya Sabha to approve it. In **2005**, the Assembly elected in February 2005 was **dissolved in May 2005** without being convened, on Governor **Buta Singh**'s report. In ***Rameshwar Prasad v Union of India (2006)***, a Constitution Bench held the dissolution **unconstitutional** and mala fide — a direct application of Bommai.

### 3. Financial Emergency (Article 360)

- **Ground:** a situation that threatens the **financial stability or credit** of India or any part.
- **Approval:** by **both Houses** within **two months**, by **simple majority**.
- **Duration:** once approved, it continues **indefinitely until revoked** — no repeated approval needed.
- **Effects:** the Centre can direct states to observe **canons of financial propriety**; **reduce salaries and allowances** of state employees and of **Union employees, including judges of the SC and HCs**; require all **money bills** and financial bills of states to be **reserved for the President**.
- **Never declared** so far (even in the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis).

### Comparison table — Article 352 vs Article 356

| Point | National Emergency (352) | President's Rule (356) |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | War, external aggression, armed rebellion | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state |
| State government | Continues; Centre gets concurrent powers | **Dismissed**; Centre takes over |
| State legislature | Continues; Parliament can also legislate on State List | **Suspended or dissolved**; Parliament legislates |
| Fundamental Rights | Affected (Art 358, 359) | Not affected |
| Approval | Special majority, 1 month | Simple majority, 2 months |
| Maximum period | No limit | 3 years |
| Revocation by LS resolution | Yes (simple majority) | No such provision |
| Relation with citizens | Directly affects citizens' rights | Affects only Centre–state relations |

### 44th Amendment (1978) — safeguards added after the 1975 Emergency

- "**Internal disturbance**" replaced by "**armed rebellion**".
- Proclamation only on **written advice of the Cabinet**.
- Approval within **1 month** (earlier 2) by **special majority**; renewal every **6 months** (earlier indefinite).
- **Lok Sabha** can revoke by **simple majority**; one-tenth of members can call a special sitting.
- **Articles 20 and 21** cannot be suspended.
- Article 358 applies only for **war/external aggression**, not armed rebellion.
- President's Rule beyond **1 year** only with national emergency + EC certificate.
- The **38th Amendment's** bar on judicial review of the President's satisfaction was removed.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Residuary power | Power over subjects not listed in any list |
| Repugnancy | Conflict between a central and a state law |
| Divisible pool | The part of central taxes shared with states |
| Vertical devolution | Share of all states together in central taxes |
| Horizontal distribution | How the states' share is divided among states |
| Grants-in-aid | Money given by the Centre to states, apart from tax share |
| Cooperative federalism | Centre and states working together as partners |
| President's Rule | Central rule in a state under Article 356 |
| Floor test | A vote in the Assembly to test majority support |
| Special category status | Extra central help for states with special difficulties (not in the Constitution) |

### Mains questions

**1. "Indian federalism has a unitary bias, but cooperative federalism is gaining ground." Discuss with reference to Centre–state relations and Bihar's experience. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Union of States; quasi-federal (K.C. Wheare).
- **Body:**
  - Legislative: Seventh Schedule, residuary power, Articles 249–253, 254, 200–201.
  - Administrative: Articles 256, 257, 355, AIS, Governor.
  - Financial: vertical imbalance; Finance Commission; GST Council (Mohit Minerals); cesses and surcharges.
  - Cooperative tools: GST Council, NITI Aayog Governing Council, Inter-State Council, zonal councils.
  - Bihar: dependence on central transfers; special category demand; special packages; Eastern Zonal Council issues; floods from Nepal needing central cooperation.
  - Reforms: Sarkaria, Punchhi.
- **Conclusion:** A flexible federation that is moving towards partnership; fiscal fairness for backward states like Bihar is the next test.

**2. Critically examine the use of Article 356 in India. How has the judiciary limited its misuse? Illustrate with Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Ambedkar's "dead letter" hope; over 100 uses.
- **Body:** Provisions and safeguards (44th Amendment); misuse patterns (1977, 1980 mass dismissals; dismissing opposition governments); Sarkaria's view; *Bommai (1994)* principles; Bihar cases — eight spells, 1999 revocation, *Rameshwar Prasad (2006)*; Punchhi's localised emergency idea; recent decline in use.
- **Conclusion:** Bommai turned Article 356 from a political weapon into a last resort.

**3. Discuss the role of the Finance Commission in India's fiscal federalism. What are Bihar's concerns? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 280; balancing wheel of fiscal federalism.
- **Body:** Functions; vertical devolution trend (32% → 42% → 41%); horizontal criteria (income distance, population, area, forest, demographic performance; GDP contribution in the 16th FC); grants for local bodies and disaster management; Bihar's share (second largest); Bihar's concerns — reduced weight for income distance, use of 2011 population, cesses outside the pool, need for flood and backwardness grants.
- **Conclusion:** Equalisation must remain central so that poorer states can catch up.
