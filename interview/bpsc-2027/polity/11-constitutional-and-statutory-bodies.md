Independent bodies keep the government honest: the **Election Commission** runs free elections, the **CAG** audits public money, the **UPSC and State PSCs** recruit civil servants on merit, and bodies like the **NHRC, CVC and Lokpal** check rights violations and corruption. BPSC asks "Who appoints the members of a State PSC?" and "Who can remove them?", "Under which Article is the CAG?", "When was NITI Aayog set up?" — and, of course, about **BPSC itself**. Learn the appointment–tenure–removal pattern for each body.

> **In simple words:** Some bodies are created by the Constitution itself (constitutional bodies), like the Election Commission and the CAG. Others are created by an Act of Parliament (statutory bodies), like the NHRC and Lokpal. Some are created by a simple government order (executive bodies), like NITI Aayog. Constitutional bodies are the most protected — their members can be removed only through a hard procedure.

## Election Commission of India (Article 324)

- **Article 324** — superintendence, direction and control of elections to **Parliament, state legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President**. (Panchayat and municipal elections are run by **State Election Commissions**.)
- Set up on **25 January 1950** — this day is celebrated as **National Voters' Day** (since 2011).
- Composition: the **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** and such number of Election Commissioners as the President fixes. **Single-member** until **1989**; briefly multi-member in 1989–90; **three-member** since **1993** (CEC + 2 ECs). Decisions by **majority**.
- First CEC: **Sukumar Sen** (conducted the first general elections, 1951–52).
- **Appointment:** by the President. Under the **Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023**, a **Selection Committee** of the **PM**, the **Leader of Opposition** (or leader of the largest opposition party) in the Lok Sabha, and a **Union Cabinet Minister** nominated by the PM recommends names from a panel prepared by a **search committee** headed by the **Law Minister**. (In ***Anoop Baranwal v Union of India, March 2023***, the SC had said that until Parliament made a law, the committee should be PM, LoP and **CJI**; the 2023 Act replaced the CJI with a minister — challenges are pending, check the latest.)
- **Term:** **6 years** or up to **65 years** of age.
- **Removal:** the **CEC** can be removed only like a **judge of the Supreme Court** (by Parliament, special majority, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity). **Other ECs** can be removed by the President on the **CEC's recommendation**.
- **Powers and functions:** delimitation preparation (with the Delimitation Commission), preparing and revising **electoral rolls**, registering voters, notifying election schedules, **recognising political parties** and allotting symbols, enforcing the **Model Code of Conduct** (MCC — not a law, but by consensus), deciding disputes on symbols, advising the President/Governor on disqualification of members (Articles 103, 192), appointing observers, and cancelling polls for malpractice.
- CEC (as of 2026 — check the latest): **Gyanesh Kumar** (since February 2025).

> **Bihar connection:** Before the **2025 Bihar Assembly elections**, the ECI carried out a **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls in Bihar (from June 2025), requiring voters to submit documents; it was challenged in the **Supreme Court**, which allowed the exercise but asked the ECI to accept **Aadhaar** among identity documents and to publish details of deleted names. The ECI later announced SIR in other states. This is an important current-affairs link for questions on electoral rolls, citizenship and voter rights.

## Union Public Service Commission (Articles 315–323)

- **Origin:** the **Public Service Commission (1926)** under the GoI Act 1919 (on the **Lee Commission**'s recommendation, 1924); became the **Federal PSC** under the 1935 Act; renamed **UPSC** in 1950.
- **Composition:** a **Chairman** and members as determined by the President (usually **9–11**); **half** of the members must have held office for at least **10 years** under the Government of India or a state.
- **Appointment:** by the **President**.
- **Term:** **6 years** or up to **65 years**.
- **Removal (Article 317):** by the **President** on grounds of (a) **insolvency**, (b) taking **paid employment** outside the office, (c) **infirmity of mind or body**; and for **misbehaviour** only after the **Supreme Court** inquires and advises removal (the SC's advice is binding).
- **Independence:** expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund; service conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage; after retirement, the Chairman is **not eligible for further government employment**; a member can become Chairman of UPSC or of a SPSC, but nothing else.
- **Functions (Article 320):** conducts examinations for **All-India Services, central services** and services of UTs; advises on methods of recruitment, promotions, transfers and disciplinary matters; helps states (on the Governor's request, with the President's approval) in joint recruitment.
- **Annual report** to the **President** (Article 323), laid before Parliament.
- **Not consulted** on reservation for backward classes and consideration of SC/ST claims (Article 335).
- Its role is **advisory**; the government can disagree, but must explain to Parliament.

## State Public Service Commission — and BPSC

### Constitutional provisions

| Point | State PSC |
|---|---|
| Article | **315** (a PSC for each state); **Joint State PSC (JSPSC)** by an Act of **Parliament** on the **request of the state legislatures** concerned |
| Appointed by | **Governor** |
| Term | **6 years** or up to **62 years** |
| Removed by | **President** (not the Governor) — on the same grounds as UPSC members (Article 317) |
| Suspension | The **Governor** can suspend a member during the SC inquiry |
| Annual report | To the **Governor**; laid before the state legislature with reasons for non-acceptance |
| After retirement | Chairman can become Chairman or member of **UPSC** or Chairman of another SPSC; members can become Chairman of their SPSC, or UPSC/other SPSC member/Chairman |

> **Common mistake:** A **State PSC member is appointed by the Governor but removed by the President**. Many students write "removed by the Governor" — wrong. The JSPSC is created by **Parliament**, not the Constitution directly, and its members are appointed by the **President**.

## Comptroller and Auditor General of India (Articles 148–151)

- **Article 148** — the CAG is appointed by the **President** by warrant under his hand and seal. Oath by the **President** (Third Schedule form).
- **Term:** **6 years** or up to **65 years**.
- **Removal:** **like a judge of the Supreme Court** (Parliament, special majority, proved misbehaviour or incapacity).
- **Independence:** salary equal to a **SC judge**, charged on the Consolidated Fund; **not eligible for further office** under the Government of India or any state after retirement; administrative expenses of his office charged.
- **Functions (Article 149, and the CAG (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971):**
  - Audits accounts of the **Union and states** — receipts and expenditure from the Consolidated Funds, Contingency Funds and Public Accounts.
  - Audits **government companies**, statutory corporations, and bodies substantially financed by the government.
  - Ascertains and certifies the **net proceeds** of taxes (his certificate is final).
  - Three types of audit: **appropriation audit**, **compliance (legality) audit** and **performance audit** ("propriety" audit is discretionary).
- **Reports (Article 151):** Union reports go to the **President**, who lays them before **Parliament**; state reports go to the **Governor**, laid before the **state legislature**. The **PAC** examines them — the CAG is the PAC's "**friend, philosopher and guide**".
- **Separation of accounts from audit (1976):** the CAG no longer compiles accounts of the Centre (Controller General of Accounts does it); still does for most states.
- Ambedkar called the CAG "**probably the most important officer** in the Constitution"; the CAG is the "**guardian of the public purse**".
- CAG (as of 2026 — check the latest): **K. Sanjay Murthy** (since November 2024).

> **Bihar connection:** The CAG's audit reports on Bihar, through the **Accountant General (Audit), Bihar** at Patna, have highlighted issues like pending **utilisation certificates** and unadjusted advances; the famous **fodder scam** of the 1990s (animal husbandry department) was exposed partly through audit findings and later investigated by the CBI on the Patna HC's orders. Use CAG examples to show the value of audit in Mains answers on accountability.

## Non-constitutional bodies

### NITI Aayog

- **National Institution for Transforming India**; set up on **1 January 2015** by a **Union Cabinet resolution** (executive body — **neither constitutional nor statutory**), replacing the **Planning Commission** (1950–2014).
- **Chairperson: PM**. **Governing Council**: **CMs of all states** and UTs with legislatures, and LGs of other UTs. Full-time **Vice-Chairperson**, full-time members, part-time members, ex officio members (up to 4 Union ministers), special invitees, and a **CEO** (Secretary rank).
- A **think tank**: promotes **cooperative federalism** (bottom-up planning) and **competitive federalism** (indices and rankings — SDG India Index, Aspirational Districts Programme, Export Preparedness Index, Fiscal Health Index). It has **no power to allocate funds** to states (unlike the Planning Commission).
- The **Aspirational Districts Programme (2018)** includes many **Bihar districts** (13 of Bihar's 38 districts — check the latest list) and the **Aspirational Blocks Programme (2023)**.
- Office holders (as of 2026 — check the latest): Vice-Chairperson **Suman Bery**; CEO **B.V.R. Subrahmanyam**.

### National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

- Statutory body under the **Protection of Human Rights Act (PHRA), 1993**; set up on **12 October 1993**.
- **Composition:** Chairperson — a **former Chief Justice of India or a former SC judge** (amendment of **2019** allowed SC judges); members — a serving or retired SC judge, a serving or retired HC Chief Justice, three persons with knowledge of human rights (at least one woman); **ex officio members** — chairpersons of national commissions (SC, ST, BC, Women, Minorities, Child Rights) and the Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities.
- **Appointment:** by the **President** on the recommendation of a committee: **PM** (chair), **Speaker** of LS, **Deputy Chairman** of RS, **Leaders of Opposition** in both Houses, and the **Union Home Minister**.
- **Term:** **3 years** or **70 years** (2019 amendment); eligible for reappointment.
- **Removal:** by the President on grounds like insolvency, infirmity, misbehaviour (after a SC inquiry).
- **Functions:** inquire into human rights violations **suo motu** or on petition; intervene in court cases; visit jails; review laws; spread human rights literacy. **Recommendations are not binding**. It cannot inquire into a matter **more than one year old**, or one pending before another commission. Limited powers over the **armed forces** (it can only seek a report).
- Chairperson (as of 2026 — check the latest): **Justice V. Ramasubramanian** (since December 2024).
- **State Human Rights Commissions** — under the same Act; chairperson a retired **Chief Justice or judge of a HC**; appointed by the **Governor** but removed by the **President**. **Bihar** has a **State Human Rights Commission** at Patna.

### Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

- Set up in **February 1964** by an executive resolution on the recommendation of the **Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962–64)**. Statutory status by an ordinance in 1998 and the **CVC Act, 2003**.
- **Composition:** **Central Vigilance Commissioner** + up to **2 Vigilance Commissioners**.
- **Appointment:** by the President on the recommendation of a committee of the **PM**, the **Union Home Minister** and the **Leader of Opposition** in the Lok Sabha.
- **Term:** **4 years** or **65 years**; not eligible for further government jobs.
- Supervises vigilance in central ministries, superintends the **CBI** (for offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act) and the **Enforcement Directorate** (in the selection of directors), and receives **whistle-blower** complaints (Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers Resolution, 2004; Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014).

### Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)

- Set up in **1963** by a **Home Ministry resolution** (on the Santhanam Committee's recommendation); derives powers from the **Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946**. **Not a statutory body** in itself.
- Works under the **Department of Personnel and Training** (Ministry of Personnel).
- **Director** selected by a committee of the **PM**, the **Leader of Opposition** (or largest opposition party leader) and the **CJI** (or an SC judge nominated by him) — under the **Lokpal Act 2013** amendments to the DSPE Act. Term **2 years**, extendable up to **5 years** (2021 amendment).
- Needs **state consent** to investigate in a state (Section 6 of the DSPE Act) — several states have **withdrawn general consent**.

> **Bihar connection:** The CBI investigated the **fodder scam** (Bihar animal husbandry department, exposed in **1996**) on the orders of the **Patna High Court**, which was upheld by the Supreme Court. It also handled the **Srijan scam** (Bhagalpur, 2017). Bihar's own anti-corruption bodies are the **Vigilance Investigation Bureau**, the **Special Vigilance Unit (SVU)** and the **Economic Offences Unit (EOU)** of Bihar Police. The **Bihar Special Courts Act, 2009** allows **confiscation of property** of corrupt public servants even during trial — its validity was upheld by the Patna High Court; several officers' houses were converted into **schools**.

### Lokpal and Lokayuktas

- Idea: the **Administrative Reforms Commission (1966)** under **Morarji Desai** recommended a **Lokpal** (Centre) and **Lokayuktas** (states), on the model of the Scandinavian **Ombudsman** (Sweden, 1809). Lokpal bills were introduced many times from **1968**; the **Anna Hazare movement (2011)** pushed it through.
- **Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013** — assented on **1 January 2014**, in force from **16 January 2014**.
- **Composition:** a **Chairperson** (former CJI, or former SC judge, or an eminent person) + up to **8 members**, of whom **50% judicial**, and **at least 50%** from **SC, ST, OBC, minorities and women**.
- **Selection committee:** **PM** (chair), **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**, **Leader of Opposition** in LS, **CJI** (or an SC judge nominated by him), and an **eminent jurist** nominated by the President on the recommendation of the others.
- **Term:** **5 years** or **70 years**.
- **Jurisdiction:** the **PM** (with exclusions — international relations, security, public order, atomic energy, space; inquiry into the PM needs a **two-thirds** full bench decision and is held **in camera**), **Union ministers**, **MPs** (except for speech/vote in Parliament), **Group A to D** central officials, and bodies financed by the government or receiving foreign donations above a limit.
- First Lokpal: **Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose** (**March 2019**). Chairperson (as of 2026 — check the latest): **Justice A.M. Khanwilkar** (since 2024).
- The Act required states to set up **Lokayuktas within one year**.
- **Lokayukta** — first set up by **Maharashtra (1971)** (Odisha passed an Act in 1970 but the body came in 1983).

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar enacted the **Bihar Lokayukta Act, 1973** — one of the early states — and later replaced it with the **Bihar Lokayukta Act, 2011**, which covers public servants including ministers. Combine it with the **Bihar Special Courts Act 2009**, the **Right to Public Services Act 2011** and the **Public Grievance Redressal Act 2015** to describe Bihar's anti-corruption and good-governance framework in Mains (see [Governance, Rights and Welfare Laws](/interview/bpsc-2027/polity/governance-rights-and-welfare-laws)).

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Constitutional body | A body created directly by the Constitution |
| Statutory body | A body created by an Act of the legislature |
| Executive body | A body created by a government order or resolution |
| Ombudsman | An independent official who looks into public complaints against officials |
| Performance audit | Checking whether public money achieved its intended results |
| Model Code of Conduct | ECI's guidelines for parties and candidates during elections |
| Suo motu | Acting on its own, without a complaint |
| Whistle-blower | A person who exposes wrongdoing inside an organisation |
| Think tank | A body that does research and gives policy advice |

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the role of the Election Commission in ensuring free and fair elections. What reforms are needed? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 324; free and fair elections are part of the basic structure (Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain 1975).
- **Body:**
  - Composition, independence, 2023 Act and Anoop Baranwal.
  - Functions: rolls, MCC, party recognition, EVMs/VVPAT, observers.
  - Achievements: world's largest elections, high turnout, women voters in Bihar outvoting men in recent elections.
  - Issues: appointment process, MCC enforcement, money power, criminalisation, roll accuracy (Bihar SIR debate), trust in EVMs.
  - Reforms: collegium-type selection, statutory MCC, state funding, decriminalisation, transparency in roll revision.
- **Conclusion:** A credible ECI is the foundation of democracy.

**2. "State Public Service Commissions are vital for good governance, but face a crisis of credibility." Examine with reference to BPSC. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 315; merit-based recruitment.
- **Body:** Constitutional safeguards (appointment by Governor, removal by President, charged expenses); functions; BPSC's role (CCE, judiciary, teacher recruitment through TRE); problems (paper leaks, delays, litigation, normalisation disputes, protests in 2024); reforms (anti-leak law 2024, technology, fixed calendar, transparency, independent members).
- **Conclusion:** A fair PSC is the gateway to a competent bureaucracy; trust must be rebuilt through technology and accountability.

**3. Examine the institutional framework against corruption in India and Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Corruption undermines development and trust.
- **Body:** CAG, CVC, CBI, Lokpal, Lokayukta (Bihar Acts 1973, 2011), Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 (2018 amendment), Whistle Blowers Act 2014, RTI; Bihar — Special Courts Act 2009, SVU, EOU, RTPS Act, Lok Shikayat Act; weaknesses (delays, vacancies, political control, low conviction rates); suggestions.
- **Conclusion:** Institutions need independence, capacity and citizen participation to work.
