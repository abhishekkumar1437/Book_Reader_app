Parliament is the law-making body of the Union (Part V, Articles 79–122). It is one of the most-asked Polity topics: the difference between money bills and financial bills, joint sittings, the Speaker's powers, the Public Accounts Committee, and the stages of the budget all come up again and again. In Mains, questions on "decline of Parliament", "role of committees" and "the Rajya Sabha as a federal chamber" are common.

> **In simple words:** Parliament = the **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha** (Article 79). The Lok Sabha is the house of the people, directly elected. The Rajya Sabha is the council of states, elected by state MLAs. Parliament makes laws, controls the government's money (the budget), and keeps the government answerable through questions, debates and committees.

## Composition

### Lok Sabha (House of the People) — Article 81

| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Maximum strength | **550** — up to **530** from states and up to **20** from UTs (the provision for **2 nominated Anglo-Indians**, Article 331, **ended in January 2020** under the **104th Amendment**) |
| Present strength | **543** elected seats |
| Election | **Direct** election by adult citizens (**18 years**, 61st Amendment), **first-past-the-post** in single-member territorial constituencies |
| Seats frozen | Allocation among states based on the **1971 Census**, frozen until the first census after **2026** (**42nd** froze till 2000; **84th Amendment 2001** extended to 2026). Constituency boundaries readjusted on the 2001 Census (**87th Amendment, 2003**; Delimitation 2008) |
| Reservation | Seats reserved for **SCs and STs** (Article 330) — extended up to **January 2030** by the **104th Amendment**. Reservation for **women (one-third)** under the **106th Amendment (2023)** will start after the next census and delimitation |
| Term | **5 years** from the first meeting (Article 83(2)); can be extended by **one year at a time** during a **national emergency** (and not beyond 6 months after it ends). The 42nd Amendment made it 6 years; the 44th restored 5 |
| Qualification | Citizen, **25 years**, registered elector in any constituency (Article 84; RPA 1951) |

### Rajya Sabha (Council of States) — Article 80

| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Maximum strength | **250** — **238** representatives of states and UTs + **12 nominated** by the President |
| Present strength | **245** (233 elected + 12 nominated) |
| Election | Elected by **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies** using **proportional representation by single transferable vote**; **open ballot** since 2003 (party agent can see the vote) |
| Allocation | Fourth Schedule — on the basis of **population** (so not equal representation of states, unlike the US Senate). **Uttar Pradesh 31** (largest), **Bihar 16** |
| Nominated members | 12 persons with special knowledge or practical experience in **literature, science, art and social service** (model: Irish Constitution) |
| Permanent house | **Not subject to dissolution**; **one-third** of members retire **every second year**; each member's term is **6 years** (fixed by the RPA 1951, not the Constitution) |
| Qualification | Citizen, **30 years**; the 2003 amendment of RPA removed the domicile requirement (upheld in *Kuldip Nayar, 2006*) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar sends **40 members to the Lok Sabha** (of which **6 are reserved for SCs**, **none for STs** after Jharkhand's creation) and **16 members to the Rajya Sabha**. Before 2000, undivided Bihar had 54 Lok Sabha and 22 Rajya Sabha seats. **Meira Kumar**, MP from **Sasaram** (Bihar), was the **first woman Speaker of the Lok Sabha (2009–14)**. **Harivansh Narayan Singh**, a Rajya Sabha member from Bihar, became **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha in 2018** (check the current holder as of 2026).

### Disqualification of members

- **Article 102** — office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, not a citizen, disqualified by a law of Parliament, and under the **Tenth Schedule** (defection).
- **RPA 1951** — conviction with imprisonment of **2 years or more** (disqualified from the date of conviction and for 6 years after release — *Lily Thomas, 2013* struck down the 3-month protection for sitting members), corrupt practices, failure to lodge election expenses.
- Questions of disqualification (other than defection) are decided by the **President**, who must act on the **opinion of the Election Commission** (Article 103). Defection cases are decided by the **Speaker/Chairman** (subject to judicial review — *Kihoto Hollohan, 1992*).
- **Double membership:** a person cannot be a member of both Houses, or of Parliament and a state legislature; must resign one seat within the time limit (14 days for state seat; 10 days for choosing between two Houses).
- **Absence:** a seat can be declared vacant if the member is absent for **60 days** without permission.

## Presiding officers

### Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Articles 93–96)

- Elected by the Lok Sabha from among its members. Holds office until **just before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha** (does not vacate on dissolution).
- Resignation addressed to the **Deputy Speaker**.
- **Removal:** resolution passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the Lok Sabha (effective majority) with **14 days' notice**. While the removal resolution is under consideration, the Speaker cannot preside but **can speak and vote** in the first instance (not a casting vote).
- **Powers:** maintains order; final interpreter of rules; **casting vote** in a tie (Article 100); **certifies money bills** (decision final, Article 110(3)); **presides over joint sittings** (Article 118); decides **quorum** (one-tenth); decides **defection** cases (Tenth Schedule); appoints chairmen of LS committees; is the chairman of the Business Advisory Committee, Rules Committee and General Purposes Committee; can allow a member to speak in a regional language.
- Salary charged on the **Consolidated Fund**.
- The **pro tem Speaker** (usually the senior-most member) is appointed by the **President** to administer oaths to new members and preside over the Speaker's election.
- First Speaker: **G.V. Mavalankar** ("Father of the Lok Sabha"). Present Speaker: **Om Birla** (since 2019; re-elected 2024 — as of 2026, check the latest).

### Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

- **Chairman** = the **Vice-President of India** (ex officio, Article 89). Can be removed as Chairman only if removed as VP. Has a casting vote; is **not a member** of the Rajya Sabha.
- **Deputy Chairman** — elected by the RS from among its members.
- Unlike the Speaker, the Chairman **cannot certify money bills** and does **not** preside over joint sittings.

### Leader of the Opposition

- Recognised for the leader of the largest opposition party having at least **one-tenth** of the House's strength. Statutory recognition by the **Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977**. The post was vacant in the 16th and 17th Lok Sabhas (2014–24); **Rahul Gandhi** became LoP in the Lok Sabha in 2024 (as of 2026 — check the latest).

## Sessions of Parliament

- **Article 85** — the President **summons** each House; the gap between two sessions **cannot exceed 6 months**, so Parliament meets **at least twice a year**.
- Usually three sessions: **Budget** (February–May), **Monsoon** (July–September), **Winter** (November–December).

| Term | Who | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | Presiding officer | Suspends a **sitting** for hours/days; session continues |
| **Adjournment sine die** | Presiding officer | Ends a sitting **without fixing a day** to meet again |
| **Prorogation** | **President** | Ends the **session**. Pending bills **do not lapse** |
| **Dissolution** | **President** (only the **Lok Sabha**) | Ends the life of the House; general elections follow |

**Lapse of bills on dissolution of the Lok Sabha**

| Bill | Lapses? |
|---|---|
| Pending in the Lok Sabha (originated there or sent by RS) | **Yes** |
| Passed by Lok Sabha, pending in Rajya Sabha | **Yes** |
| Originated in Rajya Sabha, not yet passed by Lok Sabha, pending in RS | **No** |
| Passed by both Houses, pending the President's assent | **No** |
| Returned by the President for reconsideration | **No** |
| Notified for a joint sitting before dissolution | **No** |

- **Quorum** — **one-tenth** of the total number of members of the House, including the presiding officer (Article 100).
- **Language** — Hindi or English; the presiding officer can allow the mother tongue (Article 120).
- **Ministers and the Attorney General** can speak in either House but vote only in the House of which they are members (Article 88).

## How a bill becomes law

### Types of bills

| Type | Article | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary bill** | 107–108 | Any matter except finance. Can be introduced in **either House**, by a minister (government bill) or private member. Rajya Sabha can delay up to **6 months**; deadlock → **joint sitting** |
| **Money bill** | **110** | Only taxes, borrowing, Consolidated/Contingency Fund, appropriation, etc. Introduced **only in the Lok Sabha** with the **President's recommendation**. **Speaker certifies** (final). RS must return within **14 days**; can only recommend — LS may accept or reject. **No joint sitting**. President cannot return it |
| **Financial bill (I)** | **117(1)** | Contains money-bill matters plus other matters. Introduced only in the **Lok Sabha** on the **President's recommendation**; otherwise like an ordinary bill (RS can reject or amend; joint sitting possible) |
| **Financial bill (II)** | **117(3)** | Involves expenditure from the Consolidated Fund but no money-bill matter. Can be introduced in **either House**; the President's recommendation is needed for **consideration**, not introduction |
| **Constitution amendment bill** | **368** | Either House; special majority in **each House separately**; **no joint sitting**; President **must** give assent |

### Stages of an ordinary bill

1. **First reading** — introduction (leave of the House), title and objectives read; published in the Gazette.
2. **Second reading** — the most important stage: general discussion → may be referred to a **select committee**, **joint committee** or circulated for public opinion → **clause-by-clause consideration** with amendments.
3. **Third reading** — the bill is accepted or rejected as a whole; no amendments.
4. **In the second House** — same three readings. The second House may (a) pass it, (b) pass it with amendments (sent back), (c) reject it, or (d) not act for **6 months** → deadlock.
5. **Joint sitting** (Article 108) if there is deadlock.
6. **Assent of the President** (Article 111) — assent, withhold, or return for reconsideration.

### Joint sitting (Article 108)

- Called by the **President** when: (a) a bill passed by one House is **rejected** by the other; (b) the Houses **disagree on amendments**; (c) **more than 6 months** pass without the other House passing it.
- **Presided over by the Speaker**; in his absence, the Deputy Speaker; then the Deputy Chairman of the RS.
- Decided by a **simple majority** of members **present and voting** of both Houses together — so the larger **Lok Sabha usually prevails**.
- **Not available** for **money bills** and **constitutional amendment bills**.
- Quorum: one-tenth of the total members of both Houses.
- Held only **three times**:

| Year | Bill |
|---|---|
| **1961** | **Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1959** |
| **1978** | **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977** |
| **2002** | **Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002** |

> **Memory trick:** "**D**on't **B**ring **P**roblems" — **D**owry (1961), **B**anking (1978), **P**OTA (2002).

### Money bill vs financial bill — the big trap

| Point | Money bill (Art 110) | Financial bill (Art 117(1)) |
|---|---|---|
| Introduced in | Lok Sabha only | Lok Sabha only |
| President's recommendation | Needed | Needed |
| Rajya Sabha's power | Only recommend within 14 days | Full power — amend or reject |
| Certification by Speaker | Yes | No |
| Joint sitting | Not possible | Possible |
| President can return | No | Yes |

> **Remember:** In **Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar), 2018**, the majority upheld passing the **Aadhaar Act as a money bill**; Justice Chandrachud's dissent called it a "fraud on the Constitution". In **Rojer Mathew (2019)**, a 5-judge bench referred the question of the Speaker's certification and money bills to a **larger 7-judge bench** (pending as of 2026 — check the latest).

## The Budget (Articles 112–117)

- The Constitution calls it the "**Annual Financial Statement**" (**Article 112**); the word "budget" is **not** used.
- Since **2017**: presented on **1 February**; the **Railway Budget merged** with the Union Budget (Bibek Debroy Committee recommendation; the separation dated from **1924**, Acworth Committee).
- The budget separates **charged expenditure** (not voted, only discussed) from **voted expenditure**.

**Expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (not voted): emoluments of the **President**; salaries of the **Chairman and Deputy Chairman** of RS and **Speaker and Deputy Speaker** of LS; salaries, allowances and pensions of **Supreme Court judges**; **pensions of High Court judges**; salary of the **CAG**; salaries of the **Chairman and members of UPSC**; debt charges; sums for court judgments; any other expenditure declared charged by Parliament.

### Six stages of the budget in Parliament

1. **Presentation** — by the Finance Minister in the Lok Sabha (and laid before the RS).
2. **General discussion** — both Houses, 3–4 days; no voting.
3. **Scrutiny by Departmental Standing Committees** — Parliament adjourns for 3–4 weeks.
4. **Voting on demands for grants** — **only in the Lok Sabha**. **Cut motions**:
   - **Policy cut** — reduce the demand to **₹1** (disapproval of policy);
   - **Economy cut** — reduce by a specified amount (economy in expenditure);
   - **Token cut** — reduce by **₹100** (a specific grievance).
   - On the last day, the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote together — the **guillotine**.
5. **Passing of the Appropriation Bill** (**Article 114**) — authorises withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund; "no money shall be withdrawn except under appropriation made by law".
6. **Passing of the Finance Bill** (**Article 117** / money bill) — gives effect to tax proposals. Under the **Provisional Collection of Taxes Act 1931**, it must be enacted within **75 days**.

**Other grants**

| Grant | Article | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Vote on account** | **116** | Advance grant for part of the year (usually 2 months) until the budget is passed. In an election year, an **interim budget** with a vote on account is presented |
| **Supplementary grant** | 115 | When the amount authorised is insufficient |
| **Additional grant** | 115 | For a new service not in the budget |
| **Excess grant** | 115 | When more money was spent than granted; first approved by the **PAC** |
| **Vote of credit** | 116 | A blank cheque for an unexpected demand (e.g. war) |
| **Exceptional grant** | 116 | For a purpose that is not part of the current service of any year |
| **Token grant** | — | ₹100 voted when funds for a new service can be met by re-appropriation |

### Funds

| Fund | Article | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Consolidated Fund of India** | **266(1)** | All revenues, loans raised and loan repayments received; withdrawal only by **parliamentary law** |
| **Public Account of India** | **266(2)** | Money held by the government as a banker — provident funds, small savings, deposits; withdrawals by **executive action** |
| **Contingency Fund of India** | **267** | Imprest at the disposal of the **President** for unforeseen expenditure, pending parliamentary approval; held by the **Finance Secretary**; corpus raised to **₹30,000 crore** (2021) |

> **Bihar connection:** States have the same structure: the **Consolidated Fund of the State** (Article 266), **Contingency Fund of the State** (Article 267(2)), state budget (**Article 202**) passed by the **Bihar Legislative Assembly**. The Bihar Budget is presented in February–March each year; BPSC Mains expects you to know broad headings (education gets the largest share) — see the [Bihar economy chapter](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

## Parliamentary devices — questions, motions and hours

### Question Hour and Zero Hour

- **Question Hour** — usually the **first hour** of a sitting (rules changed in 2014 for the RS to 12 noon).
  - **Starred question** (asterisk) — **oral** answer; **supplementary questions** allowed.
  - **Unstarred question** — **written** answer; no supplementaries.
  - **Short notice question** — asked with **less than 10 days'** notice; oral answer.
  - Questions can also be asked to private members.
- **Zero Hour** — the time right **after Question Hour** (around 12 noon) when members raise matters without prior notice. It is an **Indian innovation** (since **1962**) and is **not mentioned in the Rules** of Procedure.

### Motions

| Motion | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Adjournment motion** | Draws attention to a **definite matter of urgent public importance**; needs support of **50 members**; involves an element of **censure**; **only in the Lok Sabha**; discussion at least 2.5 hours |
| **No-confidence motion** | Under **Rule 198** of LS rules; **only in Lok Sabha**; needs **50 members**; **no reasons needed**; if passed, the whole Council of Ministers resigns |
| **Censure motion** | Must state **reasons**; against a minister/ministers or the whole Council; if passed, the government need not resign (but must seek confidence) |
| **Confidence motion** | Moved by the government to prove majority (not in rules originally; introduced in practice) |
| **Calling attention motion** | A member calls the attention of a minister to an urgent matter; **Indian innovation (1954)**; mentioned in the Rules |
| **Privilege motion** | When a minister is thought to have **misled the House** or breached privilege |
| **Motion of thanks** | On the **President's address**; if defeated, it amounts to defeat of the government |
| **Closure motion** | To cut short a debate — simple, compartment ("guillotine" of clauses), kangaroo closure (only important clauses), guillotine |
| **No-day-yet-named motion** | Admitted by the Speaker but no date fixed |
| **Point of order** | Raised when proceedings break the rules; no debate |

- **Half-an-hour discussion** (on a matter arising from questions), **short duration discussion** (Rule 193 — "2-hour discussion", no vote), **special mention** (in RS), **resolutions** (private members', government, statutory).

## Parliamentary committees

Two kinds: **standing** (permanent) and **ad hoc** (temporary, for a specific task, e.g. select/joint committees on bills, JPCs on scams).

### The three financial committees

| Committee | Set up | Members | Chairman | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** | **1921** (under GoI Act 1919) | **22** — **15 LS + 7 RS**; elected annually by proportional representation; **ministers cannot be members** | Appointed by the Speaker; **by convention since 1967, from the opposition** | Examines the **CAG's audit reports** on appropriation accounts and finance accounts; checks whether money was spent as Parliament granted. The **CAG** is called its "friend, philosopher and guide" |
| **Estimates Committee** | **1950** (on John Mathai's recommendation; origin in the Standing Financial Committee 1921) | **30**, **all from the Lok Sabha** | Appointed by the Speaker from the ruling party usually | Suggests **economies** in expenditure and alternative policies — "**continuous economy committee**" |
| **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)** | **1964** (Krishna Menon Committee) | **22** — **15 LS + 7 RS** | Appointed by Speaker from LS members | Examines reports and accounts of **public sector undertakings** and CAG reports on them |

> **Memory trick:** "**P**AC **P**ast, **E**stimates **E**arly" — PAC looks at **past** spending (post-mortem); Estimates looks at budget estimates (**early**, before spending). Both PAC and COPU have **22** members (15 + 7); Estimates has **30**, all LS.

### Departmentally Related Standing Committees (DRSCs)

- Started in **1993** (on the Rules Committee's recommendation); **24** committees now, each with **31 members (21 LS + 10 RS)**. **16** are under the Lok Sabha and **8** under the Rajya Sabha.
- They examine **demands for grants**, **bills**, **annual reports** and long-term policy of ministries. Ministers cannot be members.
- Called "**mini-Parliaments**".

### Other standing committees

Business Advisory Committee (Speaker chairs), Committee on Petitions, **Committee of Privileges**, **Ethics Committee** (RS 1997, LS 2000), Committee on Government Assurances, Committee on Subordinate Legislation, Committee on Absence of Members (LS), Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs, Committee on Empowerment of Women, Rules Committee, Joint Committee on Offices of Profit, General Purposes Committee, House Committee.

## Parliamentary privileges (Article 105)

- **Collective**: publish debates, exclude strangers, make rules, punish members and outsiders for **contempt**/breach of privilege, receive immediate information of arrest of a member.
- **Individual**: **freedom of speech** in Parliament (no court action for anything said or any vote), **freedom from arrest in civil cases** 40 days before and after a session, exemption from jury service and from giving evidence while Parliament is in session.
- The privileges are **not codified** (left to be defined by law; until then, those of the British House of Commons as on 1950 were followed — the 44th Amendment removed the British reference).
- **Sita Soren v Union of India (2024)** — 7-judge bench **overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998)**: **legislators who take bribes** to vote or speak in a certain way **do not enjoy immunity** under Articles 105/194.

> **Mains angle:** For "decline of Parliament" questions use these points: fewer sitting days (often 60–70 a year vs 120+ in the 1950s), disruptions, bills passed with little debate, fewer bills sent to committees, ordinance use, money-bill route (Aadhaar), weakened Rajya Sabha role. Suggestions: a fixed parliamentary calendar, mandatory committee scrutiny, a stronger opposition role, a code of conduct, live data on productivity (PRS Legislative Research). Add Bihar: the Bihar Assembly also sits for a few weeks a year; strengthening state legislature committees is needed.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Bicameral | Having two Houses |
| Prorogation | Ending of a session by the President |
| Dissolution | Ending the life of the Lok Sabha |
| Quorum | Minimum members needed for a valid sitting |
| Money bill | A bill only about taxes, borrowing and government funds |
| Joint sitting | Combined meeting of both Houses to settle a deadlock |
| Charged expenditure | Spending that Parliament discusses but does not vote on |
| Cut motion | A motion to reduce a budget demand |
| Guillotine | Putting all remaining demands to vote at once without discussion |
| Casting vote | The presiding officer's vote used only to break a tie |
| Privilege | Special rights of members needed to do their work freely |

### Mains questions

**1. Explain the legislative procedure for an ordinary bill in Parliament. How is a deadlock between the two Houses resolved? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Articles 107–111; Parliament's main function.
- **Body:**
  - Three readings in each House; committee stage.
  - Options before the second House; the 6-month rule.
  - Joint sitting (Art 108): who calls, who presides, majority, exclusions; three instances.
  - Assent: assent, withhold, return; pocket veto.
  - Money-bill and amendment-bill differences.
  - Bihar: similar procedure in the bicameral Bihar Legislature (Art 196–197) — but there is **no joint sitting** in states; the Assembly prevails.
- **Conclusion:** A careful, deliberative process; better committee scrutiny would improve law quality.

**2. "Parliamentary committees are the backbone of parliamentary democracy." Discuss. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Parliament's time is limited; committees allow detailed scrutiny.
- **Body:** Financial committees (PAC, Estimates, COPU) — composition and role; DRSCs as mini-Parliaments; ad hoc and JPCs; benefits (expertise, bipartisan work, accountability, public input); problems (low referral of bills, non-binding reports, absenteeism); suggestions.
- **Conclusion:** Strong committees keep the executive answerable between elections.

**3. Is the Rajya Sabha a truly federal chamber? Examine its role and relevance. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Council of States; elected by state MLAs.
- **Body:** Special powers (Art 249, Art 312, Art 67(b)); revising chamber; continuity (permanent house); population-based allocation (Bihar 16 vs UP 31); removal of domicile requirement (Kuldip Nayar); weak in money matters; open ballot; debates on relevance.
- **Conclusion:** A useful "second look" and voice of states, which can be strengthened by restoring its federal character.
