Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are the Constitution's instructions to the government on how to build a welfare state. Fundamental Duties are what citizens owe the nation. BPSC tests article numbers (Article 40 — village panchayats; Article 44 — uniform civil code; Article 47 — prohibition), the 42nd Amendment additions, and Mains questions on how DPSPs have been implemented — Bihar's liquor prohibition (2016) and 50% women in panchayats are direct DPSP examples.

> **In simple words:** DPSPs are goals the government should try to achieve — equal pay, free legal aid, village panchayats, protection of the environment. You cannot go to court if the government ignores them. Fundamental Duties are a list of good-citizen duties — respect the flag, protect the environment, develop a scientific temper. They also cannot be enforced in court by themselves.

## Directive Principles — basics

- **Part IV, Articles 36–51.** Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution (1937)**, which took the idea from the Spanish Constitution.
- Dr Ambedkar called them "**novel features**" of the Constitution. Granville Austin called DPSP and FR together "**the conscience of the Constitution**".
- They are like the "**Instrument of Instructions**" in the GoI Act 1935, but given to the legislature and executive of free India.
- **Article 36** — "State" has the same meaning as in Part III (Article 12).
- **Article 37** — DPSPs are **not enforceable by any court**, but they are "**fundamental in the governance of the country**" and it is the **duty of the state to apply them in making laws**.
- They aim at a **welfare state** and **economic and social democracy** (FRs aim at political democracy).

### Classification of DPSPs

The Constitution does not classify them. Scholars use three groups.

**1. Socialistic principles** (social and economic justice)

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **38** | Promote welfare by securing a **social order** with social, economic and political justice; **minimise inequalities** in income, status, facilities and opportunities (38(2) added by **44th Amendment, 1978**) |
| **39** | (a) adequate **means of livelihood** for all; (b) **ownership and control of material resources** distributed for the common good; (c) prevent **concentration of wealth**; (d) **equal pay for equal work** for men and women; (e) protect health and strength of workers and children; (f) opportunities for healthy development of children (39(f) amended by 42nd) |
| **39A** | **Equal justice and free legal aid** (added by **42nd Amendment, 1976**) |
| **41** | Right to **work, education and public assistance** in unemployment, old age, sickness, disablement |
| **42** | **Just and humane conditions of work** and **maternity relief** |
| **43** | **Living wage**, decent standard of life, social and cultural opportunities |
| **43A** | **Participation of workers in management** of industries (added by **42nd**) |
| **47** | Raise the **level of nutrition** and standard of living; improve **public health** |

**2. Gandhian principles**

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **40** | Organise **village panchayats** as units of self-government |
| **43** | Promote **cottage industries** on an individual or co-operative basis in rural areas |
| **43B** | Promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of **co-operative societies** (added by **97th Amendment, 2011**) |
| **46** | Promote educational and economic interests of **SCs, STs and weaker sections**; protect them from social injustice and exploitation |
| **47** | **Prohibit the consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs** injurious to health (except for medicinal purposes) |
| **48** | Prohibit **slaughter of cows, calves** and other milch and draught cattle; improve their breeds |

**3. Liberal-intellectual principles**

| Article | Directive |
|---|---|
| **44** | **Uniform Civil Code (UCC)** for citizens throughout India |
| **45** | **Early childhood care and education** for children **below 6** (changed by **86th Amendment, 2002**; originally free education up to 14) |
| **48** | Organise **agriculture and animal husbandry** on modern scientific lines |
| **48A** | **Protect and improve the environment** and safeguard **forests and wildlife** (added by **42nd**) |
| **49** | Protect **monuments** and places and objects of artistic or historic interest of national importance |
| **50** | **Separate the judiciary from the executive** in public services of the state |
| **51** | Promote **international peace and security**; just and honourable relations among nations; respect for international law; settle disputes by **arbitration** |

> **Memory trick:** The **42nd Amendment** added "**3 new + 1 changed**": **39A** (free legal aid), **43A** (workers in management), **48A** (environment) — "**A**dded **A**ll three A's" — and changed **39(f)** (children). The **44th** added **38(2)** (reduce inequalities). The **86th** changed **45**. The **97th** added **43B**.

### DPSPs outside Part IV

- **Article 335** — claims of SCs and STs to services to be considered consistent with **efficiency of administration**.
- **Article 350A** — instruction in the **mother tongue** at the **primary stage** for linguistic minority children.
- **Article 351** — promote the spread of **Hindi**.

These are non-justiciable but the courts read them with Part IV.

## DPSP vs Fundamental Rights

| Point | Fundamental Rights | Directive Principles |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Negative** — prohibit the state from doing certain things | **Positive** — require the state to do certain things |
| Enforceability | **Justiciable** | **Non-justiciable** (Article 37) |
| Aim | **Political democracy** | **Social and economic democracy** |
| Legal sanction | Laws violating them are **void** | Laws violating them are not void |
| Beneficiary | Individual | Community / society |
| Implementation | Automatic in most cases | Need legislation |
| Source | USA | Ireland |

### The conflict and its resolution

1. **Champakam Dorairajan (1951)** — FRs prevail over DPSP; DPSPs must "run as subsidiary". Parliament replied with the **1st Amendment (1951)** (Article 15(4), Ninth Schedule) and later amendments.
2. **Golaknath (1967)** — FRs cannot be amended to implement DPSPs.
3. **25th Amendment (1971)** — **Article 31C**: laws implementing **Article 39(b) and (c)** cannot be challenged for violating Articles **14, 19** (and 31).
4. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** — upheld the first part of 31C; struck down the part that excluded judicial review.
5. **42nd Amendment (1976)** — extended 31C protection to **all** DPSPs.
6. **Minerva Mills (1980)** — struck down this extension. The Constitution is founded on the **balance between Part III and Part IV**; to give absolute primacy to one over the other disturbs the harmony, which is a **basic feature**. FRs and DPSPs are "**two wheels of a chariot**".
7. Today: **DPSPs in 39(b) and (c)** have primacy over Articles 14 and 19; for others, the courts **harmonise** both. Courts use DPSPs to judge the "reasonableness" of restrictions on FRs and to widen Article 21.

> **Remember:** In **Property Owners Association v State of Maharashtra (2024)**, a 9-judge bench held (by majority) that **not all private property** is a "material resource of the community" under Article 39(b); only resources with certain features (nature, scarcity, public impact) qualify. This narrowed the earlier wide view of Justice Krishna Iyer.

## Implementation of DPSPs

| Directive | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Art 38, 39 — social order, wealth | **Land reforms** (zamindari abolition — **Bihar Land Reforms Act 1950** was among the first), ceiling laws, Five-Year Plans, NITI Aayog |
| Art 39(d) — equal pay | **Equal Remuneration Act 1976** (now in Code on Wages 2019) |
| Art 39A — free legal aid | **Legal Services Authorities Act 1987**, NALSA (1995), Lok Adalats |
| Art 40 — panchayats | **73rd Amendment 1992**; Bihar Panchayati Raj Act 2006 |
| Art 41, 43 — work, living wage | **MGNREGA 2005** (rural employment guarantee — see the governance chapter on its 2025 replacement), Minimum Wages Act 1948 |
| Art 42 — maternity relief | **Maternity Benefit Act 1961** (amended 2017: 26 weeks) |
| Art 43 — cottage industries | KVIC (1957), handloom boards, **JEEViKA** self-help groups in Bihar |
| Art 43B — co-operatives | 97th Amendment; Ministry of Cooperation (2021) |
| Art 44 — UCC | Not implemented nationally. **Goa** has a common civil code (Portuguese era). **Uttarakhand** passed a UCC Act in 2024 (in force January 2025) — first state after independence |
| Art 45 — education | **RTE Act 2009** (Art 21A); ICDS (1975) for under-6 |
| Art 46 — weaker sections | Reservation, SC/ST Atrocities Act 1989, scholarships |
| Art 47 — nutrition, prohibition | Mid-day meal (**PM POSHAN**), NFSA 2013; **prohibition in Gujarat, Bihar, Mizoram, Nagaland**, Lakshadweep (as of 2026 — check the latest) |
| Art 48 — cow slaughter | Cow protection laws in most states |
| Art 48A — environment | **Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972**, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, Environment (Protection) Act 1986, NGT 2010 |
| Art 49 — monuments | **Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958** |
| Art 50 — separation of judiciary | **Code of Criminal Procedure 1973** separated judicial magistrates from executive; now BNSS 2023 |
| Art 51 — peace | Panchsheel, Non-Aligned Movement, UN peacekeeping |

> **Bihar connection:** **Article 47** in action — Bihar enforced **total prohibition from 5 April 2016** under the Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016 (country liquor was banned from 1 April 2016). The policy is linked to women's demands (JEEViKA groups). It is debated for loss of excise revenue, hooch tragedies and huge pendency of cases; the Act has been amended (e.g. 2022) to ease penalties for first-time drinkers. **Article 40** in action — Bihar was the **first state to give 50% reservation to women** in panchayats (Bihar Panchayati Raj Act 2006). **Article 39A** — Bihar State Legal Services Authority and Lok Adalats. **Article 48A** — **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan (2019)** on water conservation and greenery.

> **Prelims angle:** Standard questions: "Which Article directs the state to organise village panchayats?" (40); "UCC?" (44); "Separation of judiciary from executive?" (50); "Free legal aid?" (39A); "Protection of environment?" (48A); "Prohibition?" (47); "Equal pay for equal work?" (39(d)); "Participation of workers in management?" (43A); "Promotion of international peace?" (51). Trap: **Article 45** is now about children **below 6**, not 6–14.

## Criticism of DPSPs

- **No legal force** — K.T. Shah called them "a cheque on a bank, payable at the convenience of the bank". Nasiruddin called them "New Year's resolutions" that are broken on the second of January.
- **Illogically arranged** — no consistent philosophy.
- **Conservative** — based on 19th-century ideas of Britain (Ivor Jennings).
- **Constitutional conflict** — possible conflict between Centre and states, President and PM, if a law ignores DPSPs.

**Utility:** they are a yardstick to judge government performance (the opposition uses them), they guide courts, they give continuity to policy, and they supplement FRs. Many DPSPs have become law or even FRs (education → 21A).

## Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A)

### Origin

- **Not in the original Constitution.**
- Recommended by the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)** (it suggested 8 duties).
- Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** — **10 duties**. The **11th duty** was added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**.
- Inspired by the **Constitution of the former USSR**. India's are the only ones (among major democracies) listed in a separate part.
- Apply **only to citizens**, not foreigners.
- **Non-justiciable** — no direct legal punishment for violating them, but Parliament can enforce them by law.
- **Verma Committee (1999)** on teaching Fundamental Duties identified laws that already enforce some of them (e.g. Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act 1971, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Forest Conservation Act 1980).

### The eleven duties

It shall be the duty of every citizen of India —

| Clause | Duty |
|---|---|
| (a) | To **abide by the Constitution** and respect its ideals and institutions, the **National Flag** and the **National Anthem** |
| (b) | To cherish and follow the **noble ideals of the national struggle for freedom** |
| (c) | To uphold and protect the **sovereignty, unity and integrity** of India |
| (d) | To **defend the country** and render national service when called upon |
| (e) | To promote **harmony and common brotherhood**; to **renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women** |
| (f) | To value and preserve the **rich heritage of our composite culture** |
| (g) | To protect and improve the **natural environment** including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have **compassion for living creatures** |
| (h) | To develop the **scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform** |
| (i) | To **safeguard public property** and to abjure violence |
| (j) | To strive towards **excellence** in all spheres of individual and collective activity |
| (k) | Who is a **parent or guardian**, to provide **opportunities for education** to his child or ward **between 6 and 14 years** (86th Amendment, 2002) |

> **Memory trick:** The two "environment" articles: **48A** (DPSP — the state protects) and **51A(g)** (FD — the citizen protects). Both came in **1976** (42nd). Duty (k) and Article 21A both came in **2002** (86th).

> **Common mistake:** Fundamental Duties do **not** include "paying taxes" or "voting" (the Swaran Singh Committee had suggested a duty to pay taxes; it was not adopted). Voting is a **constitutional/legal right** (Article 326), not an FR or FD.

### Significance and criticism

- **Significance:** reminder to citizens while enjoying rights; warning against anti-national activities; inspiration for discipline; help courts decide the reasonableness of a law (*AIIMS Students Union, 2001*: FDs are equally important as FRs).
- **Criticism:** list not exhaustive (no duty to vote or pay taxes); some duties are vague ("noble ideals", "composite culture"); non-justiciable; placed as an appendage to Part IV.

> **Mains angle:** Link rights and duties — "rights and duties are two sides of the same coin". Gandhi said the true source of rights is duty. Give examples: Swachh Bharat (cleanliness), voter awareness, environment drives like Bihar's Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali, respecting women (Bihar's campaigns against dowry and child marriage, 2017).

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Directive Principles | Guidelines for the state to build a welfare society |
| Welfare state | A state that takes responsibility for citizens' well-being |
| Non-justiciable | Cannot be enforced by going to court |
| Uniform Civil Code | One set of personal laws (marriage, divorce, inheritance) for all religions |
| Article 31C | Protects laws made for DPSP 39(b),(c) from challenge under Articles 14 and 19 |
| Harmonious construction | Reading two provisions so both can work together |
| Scientific temper | Thinking based on reason, evidence and inquiry |
| Composite culture | A culture formed by mixing many traditions |

### Mains questions

**1. "Directive Principles are non-justiciable, yet they have shaped Indian governance." Discuss with examples from Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Part IV, Article 37; Irish source; aim of a welfare state.
- **Body:**
  - Socialist, Gandhian, liberal groups with key Articles.
  - National implementation: land reforms, MGNREGA/rural employment, NFSA, RTE, Legal Services Act, environment laws.
  - Bihar: Bihar Land Reforms Act 1950; 50% women in panchayats (Art 40, 2006); total prohibition 2016 (Art 47); JEEViKA (Art 43); Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali (Art 48A); mid-day meal and cycle schemes.
  - Courts use DPSPs to expand FRs (education, environment, livelihood).
  - Limits: resource constraints, political will, UCC unresolved.
- **Conclusion:** DPSPs act as the moral compass of policy — "fundamental in governance".

**2. Trace the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles through constitutional amendments and judgments. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** FRs = political democracy; DPSPs = socio-economic democracy.
- **Body:** Champakam (1951) → 1st Amendment → Golaknath (1967) → 24th & 25th (31C) → Kesavananda (1973) → 42nd → Minerva Mills (1980) → Waman Rao → Property Owners Association (2024).
- **Conclusion:** From conflict to harmony; both are "two wheels of a chariot".

**3. Examine the significance of Fundamental Duties in a democracy. Should they be made enforceable? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 42nd Amendment, Swaran Singh Committee, Article 51A.
- **Body:** List and themes; significance (civic discipline, national unity, environment, scientific temper); laws enforcing them (Verma Committee); arguments for enforceability (civic decline) and against (coercion, vagueness); better approach — education, awareness, incentives.
- **Conclusion:** Duties work best through civic culture, not punishment.
