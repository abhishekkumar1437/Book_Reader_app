Part VI of the Constitution (Articles 152–237) sets up the government of each state. The structure is like the Union: a nominal head (**Governor**), a real executive (**Chief Minister and Council of Ministers**) and a **legislature**. BPSC asks about the Governor's discretionary powers, Article 356 reports, the creation of Legislative Councils, and Bihar facts — how many seats in the Bihar Assembly and Council, who was Bihar's first Chief Minister, when Bihar had President's Rule. GS Paper II asks for "the role of the Governor" and "Bihar's legislature" with a Bihar focus.

> **In simple words:** Each state is run like a small version of the Centre. The Governor is the formal head, appointed by the President. The Chief Minister, who leads the majority in the Vidhan Sabha, runs the government with his ministers. Bihar has two Houses — the Vidhan Sabha (243 elected members) and the Vidhan Parishad (75 members). Only six states have two Houses.

## The Governor (Articles 153–162)

### Appointment and office

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **153** | A Governor for each state; the **7th Amendment (1956)** allows **one person as Governor of two or more states** |
| **154** | **Executive power of the state** is vested in the Governor |
| **155** | Appointed by the **President** by warrant under his hand and seal (the Canadian model — **not elected**) |
| **156** | Holds office during the **pleasure of the President**; term **5 years** (can continue until the successor takes charge); resigns to the **President** |
| **157** | Qualifications — **citizen of India**, **35 years** of age |
| **158** | Conditions — not a member of any legislature; no office of profit; free residence (Raj Bhavan — in Bihar now called **Lok Bhavan**); emoluments by Parliament (Second Schedule) |
| **159** | **Oath** — administered by the **Chief Justice of the High Court** concerned (in Bihar, the Chief Justice of the **Patna High Court**) |
| **161** | **Pardoning power** — for offences against state law (cannot pardon death sentences; no court-martial power) |
| **163** | Council of Ministers to aid and advise the Governor **except** where he is required to act in his **discretion**; the Governor's decision on whether a matter is in his discretion is **final** |
| **164** | CM appointed by the Governor; other ministers on CM's advice; collective responsibility to the **Assembly** |
| **165** | **Advocate General** of the state |
| **166** | Conduct of business of the state government |
| **167** | Duties of the CM to inform the Governor |

**Conventions on appointment** (Sarkaria Commission): the Governor should be an **outsider** to the state, an eminent person, not too closely linked with active politics, and the CM should be consulted.

**Removal:** No grounds and no procedure in the Constitution. In ***B.P. Singhal v Union of India (2010)***, the Supreme Court held the "pleasure" doctrine cannot be used arbitrarily — a Governor cannot be removed merely because of a change in the central government or a difference of opinion; removal must have valid reasons and is subject to limited judicial review.

### Powers of the Governor

- **Executive** — all executive actions in his name; appoints the **CM** and ministers, the **Advocate General**, the **State Election Commissioner** (removable only like a HC judge), the **Chairman and members of the State Public Service Commission** (BPSC — but they are **removed by the President**), and the **State Finance Commission**; seeks information from the CM; recommends **President's Rule** (Article 356); acts as **Chancellor of state universities** (under state laws).
- **Legislative** — summons, prorogues the legislature and **dissolves the Assembly**; addresses the legislature at the first session after elections and each year; **nominates one-sixth** of the Legislative Council (literature, science, art, co-operative movement, social service); decides disqualification questions of MLAs (other than defection) on the **Election Commission's opinion** (Article 192); **assents to bills** (Article 200); issues **ordinances** (**Article 213**).
- **Financial** — the state budget is laid by the Governor's direction (Article 202); money bills need his recommendation; **Contingency Fund of the State**; constitutes the **State Finance Commission every 5 years** for panchayats and municipalities (Articles 243I, 243Y).
- **Judicial** — pardons (161); consulted by the President on HC judges' appointments; appoints district judges with the HC (233).

### Assent to state bills (Article 200 and 201)

When a bill passed by the state legislature is presented, the Governor can:
1. **Give assent**;
2. **Withhold assent**;
3. **Return** the bill (not a money bill) for reconsideration — if the legislature passes it again, the Governor **must** give assent;
4. **Reserve** the bill for the **consideration of the President** — **compulsory** if the bill endangers the position of the **High Court**; optional in other cases (e.g. against DPSP, against national interest, ultra vires).

Under **Article 201**, the President can assent, withhold, or (for non-money bills) direct the Governor to return it to the legislature; the legislature must reconsider within **6 months**, but the President is **not bound** to assent even after that.

> **Remember:** In ***State of Tamil Nadu v Governor of Tamil Nadu (April 2025)***, a two-judge bench held that Governors cannot sit on bills indefinitely and set time limits. On a **Presidential reference under Article 143**, a **Constitution Bench (November 2025)** advised that courts cannot impose fixed timelines or "deemed assent", but prolonged, unexplained inaction can be examined by courts in a limited way (check the latest developments). This is a hot Mains topic on Governor–state friction.

### Discretionary powers of the Governor

**Constitutional discretion** (expressly given):
- **Reserving a bill** for the President (Article 200).
- **Recommending President's Rule** (Article 356).
- As **administrator of an adjoining UT** (if given additional charge).
- Determining the amount payable by **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram** to tribal District Councils (Sixth Schedule).
- Seeking information from the CM (Article 167).
- Special responsibilities under **Article 371** (e.g. Maharashtra and Gujarat for development boards, Nagaland for law and order, Sikkim, Manipur etc.).

**Situational discretion** (by circumstances):
- Appointing a CM when **no party has a clear majority** or the CM dies suddenly.
- **Dismissing** a Council of Ministers that cannot prove confidence.
- **Dissolving** the Assembly if the Council of Ministers has lost its majority.

> **Common mistake:** The Governor has **more discretion** than the President. The President has **no constitutional discretion** (only situational), while the Governor has both. Also, the **Article 163(2)** rule — the Governor's decision about whether a matter is discretionary is final — has no counterpart for the President.

### President vs Governor — comparison

| Point | President | Governor |
|---|---|---|
| Office | Head of the Union (Art 52) | Head of the state (Art 153) |
| Selection | **Elected** indirectly by an electoral college (Art 54) | **Appointed** by the President (Art 155) |
| Age | 35 years | 35 years |
| Term | 5 years; removal only by **impeachment** (Art 61) | 5 years, but during the **pleasure of the President**; no impeachment |
| Oath by | Chief Justice of India | Chief Justice of the High Court |
| Resigns to | Vice-President | President |
| Acting head | Vice-President | As provided by the President (often a neighbouring Governor or the HC CJ) |
| Discretion | No constitutional discretion; only situational | **Constitutional + situational** discretion (Art 163) |
| Pardon | Can pardon **death sentences**; **court-martial** cases | **Cannot** pardon death sentences; **no** court-martial power |
| Emergency powers | Can declare emergencies (352, 356, 360) | **None** — can only send a **report** under Art 356 |
| Ordinance | Art 123 | Art 213 (needs President's instructions in some cases) |
| Veto | Absolute, suspensive, pocket; cannot reserve a bill | Can reserve bills for the President; absolute, suspensive; pocket veto debated |
| Nominations | 12 to Rajya Sabha | One-sixth of the Legislative Council (12 in Bihar) |
| Supreme commander | Of the defence forces | No military powers |
| Diplomatic powers | Yes | No |
| Appoints | CJI and judges, CAG, CEC, UPSC members, Governors | CM, Advocate General, SEC, SPSC members (removed by President) |

> **Bihar connection:** Former Governors of Bihar who became President: **Dr Zakir Husain** (Governor 1957–62) and **Ram Nath Kovind** (Governor 2015–17). The Governor of Bihar is also **Chancellor of state universities** (Patna University, etc.); the Governor's residence in Patna, earlier called Raj Bhavan, is now called **Lok Bhavan**. **Lt Gen (Retd) Syed Ata Hasnain** was sworn in as Governor of Bihar on **14 March 2026**, succeeding **Arif Mohammed Khan** (as of 2026 — check the latest).

## Chief Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 163–167)

- **Article 164(1)** — the CM is appointed by the Governor; other ministers by the Governor on the CM's advice. By convention, the leader of the **majority party** in the Assembly. A non-member can be CM for **6 months** (must get elected to either House of the state legislature).
- The CM can be from either House — Bihar CMs have often been **members of the Legislative Council** (e.g. **Nitish Kumar** sat in the Vidhan Parishad for long periods; **Rabri Devi** also).
- **Article 164(1A)** — ministers including the CM **not more than 15%** of the Assembly strength, but **not fewer than 12** (91st Amendment, 2003). **Bihar's maximum = 36**.
- In **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha**, there must be a **Minister for Tribal Welfare** (Article 164(1); **Bihar was removed** from this list by the **94th Amendment, 2006**, after Jharkhand's creation).
- **Article 164(2)** — collective responsibility to the **Legislative Assembly**.
- **Article 167** — the CM must communicate decisions to the Governor and furnish information.
- **Powers of the CM** — recommends ministers, allocates portfolios, presides over the cabinet, advises the Governor on appointments, summoning/prorogation and dissolution; leader of the House; member of **NITI Aayog's Governing Council**, the **Inter-State Council** and the **Eastern Zonal Council** (Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal); chairs state-level bodies like the State Planning Board and the State Disaster Management Authority.

### Advocate General (Article 165)

- Highest law officer of the **state**; appointed by the **Governor**; must be **qualified to be a HC judge**; holds office during the **pleasure of the Governor**.
- Can speak and take part in proceedings of the state legislature but **cannot vote** (**Article 177**).

## The State Legislature (Articles 168–212)

### Unicameral or bicameral

- **Article 168** — each state has a legislature consisting of the **Governor** and one or two Houses.
- **Six states** have **bicameral** legislatures (two Houses): **Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka**. (J&K's council was abolished in 2019.)
- **Article 169** — creation or abolition of a Legislative Council:
  1. The **Legislative Assembly** passes a resolution by a **special majority** — majority of the **total membership** and **two-thirds of members present and voting**;
  2. **Parliament** then passes a law by a **simple majority**.
  Such a law is **not** a constitutional amendment under Article 368.

> **Memory trick:** The six states with councils: "**BUM-KAT**" — **B**ihar, **U**ttar Pradesh, **M**aharashtra, **K**arnataka, **A**ndhra Pradesh, **T**elangana.

### Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) — Article 170

- **Directly elected**; strength **not more than 500 and not less than 60** (exceptions: Arunachal, Sikkim, Goa, Mizoram — fewer; Sikkim has 32, Puducherry UT 30).
- Term **5 years** (can be extended by 1 year at a time during a national emergency).
- Qualification **25 years**.
- Seats reserved for **SCs and STs** (Article 332) up to **2030** (104th Amendment); **one-third for women** after the 106th Amendment comes into effect (after the next census and delimitation).
- **Speaker** — elected by members; resigns to the **Deputy Speaker**; removable by an effective majority with 14 days' notice; decides defection cases (Tenth Schedule) and certifies **money bills** (Article 199).

### Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) — Article 171

- Strength **not more than one-third** of the Assembly's total and **not less than 40** (Jammu & Kashmir was an exception earlier).
- **Composition (Article 171(3))**:

| Electorate | Share |
|---|---|
| Elected by **members of local bodies** (municipalities, district boards etc.) | About **one-third** |
| Elected by **graduates** of 3 years' standing residing in the state | **One-twelfth** |
| Elected by **teachers** of 3 years' standing (in institutions not lower than secondary school) | **One-twelfth** |
| Elected by **MLAs** from persons who are not MLAs | About **one-third** |
| **Nominated by the Governor** (literature, science, art, co-operative movement, social service) | About **one-sixth** |

- **Permanent House** — one-third of members retire **every second year**; term **6 years**. Qualification **30 years**.
- Presiding officer: **Chairman**, elected by members.
- **Powers are weaker than the Rajya Sabha's:** for an ordinary bill passed by the Assembly, the Council can delay it for **3 months** at first and **1 month** on the second passage — a **total of 4 months** — after which the bill is deemed passed in the form passed by the Assembly. There is **no joint sitting** in states. For a **money bill**, the Council can hold it for only **14 days**. A bill originating in the Council and rejected by the Assembly **ends**.
- The Council is called a "**dilatory chamber**" or "**secondary chamber**".

**History of Bihar's legislature**

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **1912** | Bihar and Orissa separated from Bengal (22 March 1912); a **Legislative Council** for the new province formed; first sitting on **20 January 1913** |
| **1920–21** | New legislative building at Patna completed; under the 1919 Act, the council had an elected majority and **dyarchy** |
| **1936** | Orissa separated; Bihar became a province on its own (1 April 1936) |
| **1937** | Under the GoI Act 1935, Bihar got a **bicameral legislature** — Assembly and Council; Congress ministry under **Sri Krishna Sinha** ("Shri Babu") as Premier, with **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** ("Bihar Vibhuti") as the senior colleague (later Deputy Premier/Finance Minister) |
| **1946** | Sri Krishna Sinha again led the ministry; he became Bihar's **first Chief Minister** under the Constitution and served until his death in **1961** |
| **1952** | First general elections under the Constitution |
| **2000** | **Jharkhand** separated (15 November 2000) — Assembly reduced from 324 to **243** |
| **2005** | Assembly dissolved (May 2005) during President's Rule without any government being formed — the Supreme Court held the dissolution unconstitutional in ***Rameshwar Prasad v Union of India (2006)***; fresh elections were held in October–November 2005 |
| **2025** | Assembly elections in **November 2025** (preceded by the **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls, which went to the Supreme Court); the NDA won a large majority (about **202 of 243** seats reported). **Nitish Kumar** was sworn in as CM a record **10th time** (November 2025) |
| **2026** | **Nitish Kumar resigned** (14 April 2026) to move to the Rajya Sabha; **Samrat Choudhary** became Bihar's **first BJP Chief Minister** on **15 April 2026** (as of 2026 — check the latest) |

> **Bihar connection — Chief Ministers to know:** **Sri Krishna Sinha** (first CM; led the government from 1946 until his death in 1961), **Karpoori Thakur** (CM 1970–71 and 1977–79; social justice reforms — the **Karpoori Thakur formula** of 1978; as Education Minister in 1967 he removed English as a compulsory subject for matriculation; **Bharat Ratna 2024**), **Jagannath Mishra**, **Lalu Prasad** (1990–97), **Rabri Devi** (1997–2005; **first woman CM of Bihar**), **Nitish Kumar** (2005 onward with breaks — the **longest-serving CM** of Bihar; sworn in a record ten times), **Jitan Ram Manjhi** (2014–15), and **Samrat Choudhary** (from April 2026 — as of 2026, check the latest).

### President's Rule in Bihar

Bihar has been under **President's Rule eight times** (commonly listed): **1968, 1969, 1972, 1977, 1980, 1995, 1999 and 2005**.
- **1977 and 1980** — after general elections, when the new Union governments dismissed state governments of other parties en masse (challenged in *State of Rajasthan v Union of India, 1977*).
- **1999** — imposed in February but **revoked in March 1999** within about a month because the **Rajya Sabha did not approve** it — showing Parliament's check.
- **2005** — after a hung Assembly (February 2005 elections); the Assembly was dissolved in May 2005 on the Governor's (Buta Singh's) report that horse-trading was taking place. In ***Rameshwar Prasad (2006)***, the SC held the dissolution **unconstitutional and mala fide** but did not restore the Assembly since fresh elections were under way. The judgment strongly criticised the Governor.

## Legislative procedure in states

- **Ordinary bills** can start in **either House** (if bicameral). The Assembly has the final say (Council's delay up to 4 months; no joint sitting).
- **Money bills** (Article 198) — only in the **Assembly**, on the Governor's recommendation; the Council has **14 days**; the **Speaker certifies** (Article 199).
- **Budget** (Article 202) — the **Annual Financial Statement of the state**; demands for grants voted only in the Assembly (Article 203); Appropriation Bill (Article 204); vote on account (Article 206).
- **Privileges** — Article 194 (same as Article 105 for Parliament).
- **Quorum** — 10 members or one-tenth, whichever is greater (Article 189).
- **Article 212** — courts cannot inquire into the proceedings of the legislature on the ground of irregularity of procedure.

> **Mains angle:** For "role of the Governor" answers, use: Sarkaria Commission (1988), Punchhi Commission (2010) — fixed tenure, removal only through a resolution of the state legislature (Punchhi), Governor not to be an active politician, use of Article 356 only as a last resort, timelines for assent; *S.R. Bommai (1994)* — floor test is the only proof of majority; *Rameshwar Prasad (2006)* — Bihar example of misuse; *Nabam Rebia (2016)* — Governor cannot summon the Assembly on his own; *B.P. Singhal (2010)* — removal not arbitrary; 2025 Tamil Nadu judgment and Presidential reference on assent timelines.

## Key terms

### Mains questions

**1. "The office of the Governor has often been at the centre of Centre–state friction." Examine with reference to Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Article 153–163; nominal head with some discretion; "agent of the Centre" criticism.
- **Body:**
  - Constitutional position and discretionary powers.
  - Areas of friction: appointment of CM in hung Assemblies, Article 356 reports, delay in assent to bills, role as university chancellor.
  - Bihar examples: 2005 dissolution (Buta Singh) and *Rameshwar Prasad (2006)*; President's Rule eight times; 1999 revocation; chancellor-government disputes over university appointments.
  - Judgments: *Bommai*, *B.P. Singhal*, *Nabam Rebia*, the 2025 assent-timeline cases.
  - Commission recommendations: Sarkaria, Punchhi, Venkatachaliah (NCRWC).
- **Conclusion:** The Governor should be a constitutional bridge, not a political actor; codified conventions and time limits would help.

**2. Discuss the structure and functioning of the Bihar Legislature. Is a Legislative Council useful for Bihar? (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bicameral since 1937; Assembly 243, Council 75.
- **Body:** Composition (seats, reservation, Council electorates); powers of the Assembly vs Council; history (1912, 1937, 2000); functioning issues (few sitting days, disruptions, committee work); arguments for the Council (expertise of graduates and teachers, revising role, continuity) and against (cost, a route for defeated politicians, delay, only 6 states have it).
- **Conclusion:** The Council can add value if used for expert scrutiny, not patronage.

**3. Compare the powers and position of the President and the Governor. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Both are constitutional heads; key differences in selection and discretion.
- **Body:** Use the comparison table: election vs appointment, removal, discretion, pardon, emergency, ordinance, veto, reservation of bills; the Governor's dual role (head of state and link with the Centre).
- **Conclusion:** The Governor has more discretion but less security, which explains the controversies around the office.
