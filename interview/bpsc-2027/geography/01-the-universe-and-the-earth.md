This chapter starts geography from the biggest scale: the universe, the solar system and the Earth as a planet. BPSC Prelims asks one or two direct questions from here almost every year — planets, the Earth's motions, solstices and equinoxes, latitude and longitude, and time calculation. You also need these ideas to understand winds, seasons and the monsoon later in the book.

> **In simple words:** The universe began with the Big Bang. Our Sun is one ordinary star in the Milky Way galaxy. Eight planets go around the Sun; the Earth is the third. The Earth spins on its axis (this gives day and night) and goes around the Sun (this gives the year). Because the axis is tilted, we get seasons. Lines of latitude and longitude help us locate any place and calculate time.

## The universe

### The Big Bang

The most accepted theory of the origin of the universe is the **Big Bang theory**. All matter was once packed into one extremely small, hot and dense point. About **13.8 billion years ago** it began to expand rapidly. The expansion still continues — galaxies are moving away from each other.

- **Edwin Hubble** (1920s) showed that galaxies are moving away; the further a galaxy, the faster it moves. This is evidence that the universe is expanding.
- **Cosmic microwave background radiation** — faint heat left over from the Big Bang, found in 1965 — is the other main evidence.
- The older rival idea, the **Steady State theory** (Hoyle and others), said the universe always looked the same. It is now rejected.

### Galaxies and stars

A **galaxy** is a huge group of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity. Our galaxy is the **Milky Way** (in Hindi, *Akashganga*), a **spiral galaxy**. The nearest large galaxy is **Andromeda**.

- **Chandrasekhar limit** — about **1.44 times the mass of the Sun**. A dying star core heavier than this cannot remain a white dwarf; it collapses further. Named after **S. Chandrasekhar** (Indian-born, Nobel Prize in Physics 1983).
- A **black hole** has gravity so strong that even light cannot escape.

### Units of distance in space

| Unit | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Astronomical Unit (AU) | Mean Earth–Sun distance | ≈ 149.6 million km (≈ 15 crore km) |
| Light year | Distance light travels in one year | ≈ 9.46 trillion km (9.46 × 10¹² km) |
| Parsec | Used by astronomers | ≈ 3.26 light years |

> **Common mistake:** A light year is a unit of **distance**, not of time. Questions often test this.

## The Sun

The Sun is a medium-sized, middle-aged star, about **4.6 billion years old**. It holds about **99.8% of the mass** of the solar system.

| Feature | Fact |
|---|---|
| Composition | Mostly hydrogen (~71–74%) and helium (~24–27%) |
| Energy source | Nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium in the core |
| Core temperature | About 15 million °C |
| Surface (photosphere) temperature | About 5,500–6,000 °C |
| Outer layer | **Corona** — seen clearly during a total solar eclipse |
| Light to reach Earth | About **8 minutes 20 seconds** |
| Dark patches | **Sunspots** — cooler areas; their number rises and falls in an ~11-year cycle |
| Solar wind and flares | Charged particles; cause **auroras** near the poles and can disturb satellites and radio |

> **Prelims angle:** India's first solar mission is **Aditya-L1**, launched by ISRO on **2 September 2023**. It reached a halo orbit around the **Lagrange point L1** (about 1.5 million km from Earth, towards the Sun) in **January 2024**. At L1 the satellite can watch the Sun without any eclipse.

## The solar system

The solar system is the Sun plus everything held by its gravity: **8 planets**, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and meteoroids.

In **2006** the **International Astronomical Union (IAU)** defined a planet and moved **Pluto** to a new class called **dwarf planet**. Other dwarf planets: **Ceres** (in the asteroid belt), **Eris**, **Haumea**, **Makemake**.

### Planet fact table

| Planet | Position | Facts BPSC likes |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 1st | Smallest planet; closest to Sun; fastest orbit (~88 days); no moon; very large day–night temperature range |
| Venus | 2nd | **Hottest planet** (thick CO₂ atmosphere → strong greenhouse effect); brightest planet in our sky; "**morning star / evening star**"; called Earth's "twin" (similar size); rotates **east to west** (retrograde), so the Sun rises in the west there; a day on Venus is longer than its year; no moon |
| Earth | 3rd | Only known planet with life; "blue planet"; densest planet; one moon |
| Mars | 4th | "**Red planet**" (iron oxide dust); **Olympus Mons** — highest volcano in the solar system; two small moons **Phobos** and **Deimos**; India's **Mangalyaan** (Mars Orbiter Mission, launched Nov 2013, reached orbit Sept 2014) |
| Jupiter | 5th | **Largest** planet; **Great Red Spot** (a giant storm); fastest rotation (~10 hours); moon **Ganymede** is the largest moon in the solar system |
| Saturn | 6th | Bright **rings**; **lowest density** (would float on water); largest moon **Titan** has a thick atmosphere; has the most known moons (count keeps changing — check latest) |
| Uranus | 7th | Axis tilted about 98° — it "**rolls**" on its side; also rotates east to west; greenish due to methane; discovered by William Herschel (1781) |

- **Inner (terrestrial) planets** — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars: small, rocky, dense.
- **Outer (Jovian) planets** — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune: huge, mostly gas/ice, all have rings, many moons.
- Planets with **retrograde rotation**: **Venus and Uranus**.
- Planets with **no moon**: **Mercury and Venus**.

### Small bodies

| Body | What it is |
|---|---|
| Asteroids | Rocky pieces, mostly in the belt between Mars and Jupiter; Ceres is the largest object there |
| Comets | Balls of ice and dust from the outer solar system; near the Sun they grow a tail that always points **away from the Sun**; **Halley's Comet** returns about every 76 years (last 1986, next about 2061) |
| Meteor | A "shooting star" — a small rock that burns up in the atmosphere (mostly in the **mesosphere**) |
| Meteorite | A piece that survives and hits the ground; **Lonar Lake** (Maharashtra) is a crater lake made by a meteorite impact |

## The Moon

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean distance from Earth | About **3,84,400 km** |
| Diameter | About 3,475 km (roughly one-fourth of Earth's) |
| Gravity | About **one-sixth** of Earth's |
| Time for one orbit (sidereal month) | About **27.3 days** |
| New moon to new moon (synodic month) | About **29.5 days** |
| Light from Moon to Earth | About 1.3 seconds |

- The Moon rotates once in the same time it takes to go around the Earth, so **we always see the same face** (this is called *tidal locking*).
- No atmosphere → no sound, no weather, sky looks black, huge temperature range.
- Phases: new moon → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → **full moon** → waning … → new moon.

> **Prelims angle:** **Chandrayaan-1** (2008) found evidence of water molecules on the Moon. **Chandrayaan-3** landed near the Moon's **south pole** on **23 August 2023** — India became the first country to land near the south pole and the fourth to soft-land on the Moon. The landing site is named **Shiv Shakti point**, and **23 August** is celebrated as **National Space Day**.

### Eclipses

- **Solar eclipse** — the Moon comes **between** the Sun and the Earth; happens only on a **new moon** day. Types: total, partial, annular (a bright "ring of fire" when the Moon is far and looks smaller).
- **Lunar eclipse** — the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon; happens only on a **full moon** night. The Moon often looks reddish ("blood moon") because the Earth's atmosphere bends red light onto it.
- Eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about **5°** to the Earth's orbit.

## The Earth: shape and size

The Earth is not a perfect sphere. It is slightly flattened at the poles and bulges at the equator. This shape is called an **oblate spheroid**; scientists call the Earth's true shape the **geoid** ("earth-shaped").

## Motions of the Earth

### Rotation

- The Earth spins on its axis from **west to east** (anticlockwise when seen from above the North Pole). That is why the Sun, Moon and stars seem to rise in the east.
- One rotation relative to the stars takes **23 hours 56 minutes 4 seconds** (*sidereal day*). Relative to the Sun it takes 24 hours (*solar day*).
- The line between the lit half and the dark half is the **circle of illumination**.

Effects of rotation:
1. Day and night.
2. The **Coriolis force** — moving air and water turn to the **right in the Northern Hemisphere** and to the **left in the Southern Hemisphere**. It is zero at the equator and strongest at the poles. This shapes winds, cyclones and ocean currents (see chapters 3 and 4).
3. Tides are partly linked to rotation (twice-daily high tide).
4. The bulge at the equator.

### Revolution

- The Earth goes around the Sun in an **elliptical** (slightly oval) orbit in about **365 days 6 hours** (365.25 days). The extra quarter-day adds up to one day in four years → **leap year** (366 days, 29 February).
- Average orbital speed: about 30 km per second.

| Position | Date (about) | Distance from the Sun |
|---|---|---|
| **Perihelion** (nearest) | **3 January** | about 147 million km |
| **Aphelion** (farthest) | **4 July** | about 152 million km |

> **Common mistake:** The Earth is **nearest** to the Sun in **January**, when the Northern Hemisphere has winter. So distance does **not** cause the seasons. The **tilt of the axis** causes the seasons.

## Seasons, solstices and equinoxes

Because the axis is tilted and keeps pointing in the same direction in space, the Northern Hemisphere leans towards the Sun for half the year and away from it for the other half. - At the poles there are about **six months of day and six months of night**.

## Latitude and longitude

### Latitude

**Latitude** is the angular distance of a place north or south of the equator. Lines of equal latitude are called **parallels**. They run east–west and are full circles that get smaller towards the poles.

| Parallel | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Equator | 0° | Largest circle (a "great circle"); divides Earth into N and S hemispheres |
| Tropic of Cancer | 23.5°N | Northern limit of the overhead Sun |
| Tropic of Capricorn | 23.5°S | Southern limit of the overhead Sun |
| Arctic Circle | 66.5°N | 24-hour day/night at least once a year north of it |
| Antarctic Circle | 66.5°S | — |
| North / South Pole | 90°N / 90°S | Points, not circles |

Heat zones: **Torrid** (between the tropics, hottest), **Temperate** (between tropics and polar circles), **Frigid** (inside the polar circles).

- 1° of latitude ≈ **111 km** everywhere.

### Longitude

**Longitude** is the angular distance of a place east or west of the **Prime Meridian** (0°), which passes through **Greenwich** near London. Lines of longitude are called **meridians**. They are half-circles from pole to pole, all of the same length, and they meet at the poles.

- There are 360 meridians (180 east + 180 west).
- The distance between meridians is greatest at the equator (~111 km per degree) and zero at the poles.

## Longitude and time

The Earth turns **360° in 24 hours**. So:

<div class="formula">

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| 360° | 24 hours |
| **15°** | **1 hour** |
| **1°** | **4 minutes** |
| 1′ (minute of arc) | 4 seconds |
| Going **east** | time is **ahead** (add) |
| Going **west** | time is **behind** (subtract) |

</div>

**Worked example 1.** Indian Standard Time is based on **82°30′E**. Time difference from Greenwich = 82.5 × 4 min = 330 min = **5 hours 30 minutes ahead**. If it is 12:00 noon GMT, it is **5:30 pm IST**.

**Worked example 2.** Place A is at 30°E and place B at 60°W. Difference = 30 + 60 = 90° → 90 × 4 = 360 min = 6 hours. If it is 10 am at A, at B (west) it is **4 am**.

**Worked example 3.** Gujarat's western tip (~68°7′E) and Arunachal's eastern tip (~97°25′E) differ by about 29°. 29 × 4 ≈ **116 minutes ≈ 2 hours**. This is why the Sun rises about two hours earlier in Arunachal than in Gujarat.

### Indian Standard Time (IST)

- The **standard meridian of India is 82°30′E**. It passes near **Mirzapur** (Uttar Pradesh), close to Prayagraj.
- It passes through **five states**: **Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh**.
- IST = GMT/UTC **+ 5:30**.
- India uses **one time zone**. There is a long demand from the North-East for a separate time zone because the sun rises very early there. Assam tea gardens historically followed "**Chai bagan time**" (about one hour ahead of IST).

> **Common mistake:** Many students think 82°30′E passes through Bihar. It does **not**; it passes west and south of Bihar through UP. Bihar lies roughly between 83°19′E and 88°17′E, so Bihar's local time is a little **ahead** of IST.

### International Date Line (IDL)

- Roughly along the **180° meridian** in the Pacific Ocean.
- It zig-zags to avoid land, so that one country does not have two dates.
- Crossing it **westward** (from America towards Asia) you **gain a day** (add a day to the calendar). Crossing **eastward** you **lose** (repeat) a day.

### Mains questions

**Q1. "India should adopt two time zones." Examine the arguments for and against this demand. (BPSC style, about 300 words)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India stretches about 29° of longitude (68°7′E to 97°25′E), a gap of nearly 2 hours of solar time; IST is based on 82°30′E (Mirzapur), GMT + 5:30.
- **For two zones:** sunrise in the North-East as early as about 4 am in summer; offices open after much daylight is lost; extra electricity use in evenings; studies (e.g. by CSIR-National Physical Laboratory) have suggested a second zone is technically possible; tea-garden "Chai bagan time" shows the practical need; better worker productivity and sleep patterns.
- **Against:** risk of confusion in railways, flights and banking; national integration — one time is a symbol of unity; the country is narrow at the eastern end, so few people are affected; possible errors at the zone border; cost of changing systems.
- **Middle path:** staggered office hours in the North-East; daylight-saving style adjustments; studies before any decision.
- **Conclusion:** time is both science and administration; a decision should balance efficiency with simplicity and unity.

**Q2. Explain how the inclination of the Earth's axis produces seasons. Why are the seasons of the Northern and Southern Hemispheres opposite?**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** the Earth's axis is tilted 23.5° from the perpendicular and keeps a fixed direction in space while the Earth revolves.
- **Body:** (1) the tilt changes the angle of the Sun's rays — direct rays heat more; (2) the tilt changes the length of day; (3) on 21 June the Northern Hemisphere leans towards the Sun — summer solstice, Sun overhead at Tropic of Cancer; (4) on 22 December the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the Sun — its summer; (5) equinoxes on 21 March and 23 September — equal day and night; (6) distance is not the cause — the Earth is nearest the Sun in January; (7) a simple labelled diagram of the four positions; (8) link to India — the northward shift of the overhead Sun shifts pressure belts and draws in the south-west monsoon.
- **Conclusion:** without the tilt there would be no seasons and agriculture calendars like kharif and rabi would not exist.
