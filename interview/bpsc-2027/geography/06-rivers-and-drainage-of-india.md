Rivers are the most asked topic in Indian geography. BPSC loves "origin–confluence–tributary" questions, dam-and-river matches, and anything about the **Ganga and its Bihar tributaries** — Gandak, Kosi, Son, Bagmati, Punpun, Phalgu. For Mains GS II, rivers lead into floods, irrigation, interlinking and water disputes. This chapter gives the all-India picture and the Ganga system in full; the **flood problem and river-by-river detail inside Bihar** is in [Rivers, Floods and Climate of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/rivers-floods-and-climate-of-bihar) — read both together.

> **In simple words:** A river system is a main river with all the smaller rivers (tributaries) that join it. India has two kinds. Himalayan rivers are fed by snow and rain, flow all year, and carry a lot of silt — the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra. Peninsular rivers are fed only by rain, are shorter and seasonal — the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, and the west-flowing Narmada and Tapi. Bihar is the meeting ground of rivers from both sides: Himalayan rivers join the Ganga from the north, and plateau rivers like the Son join it from the south.

## Basic terms and drainage patterns

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Drainage** | The flow of water through well-defined channels |
| **Drainage basin / catchment** | Area drained by one river and its tributaries |
| **Watershed / water divide** | High land separating two drainage basins (e.g. the Western Ghats; the Ambala–Delhi ridge between the Indus and Ganga systems) |
| **Tributary** | A river that **joins** a bigger river |
| **Distributary** | A channel that **branches off** from a river, usually in a delta |
| **Confluence** | Point where two rivers meet (*prayag* or *sangam*) |
| **Antecedent river** | A river older than the mountains it crosses; it kept cutting its valley as the land rose — **Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra** (deep gorges) |

| Pattern | Looks like | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dendritic** | Branches of a tree | Most rivers of the **Northern Plains** (Ganga system) |

| **Centripetal** | Rivers flowing inward into a lake or basin | **Loktak lake** (Manipur) |
| **Rectangular** | Right-angle bends along joints in rock | Parts of the Vindhyas |

## Himalayan versus peninsular rivers

| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Glaciers and high mountains | Western Ghats, central highlands, plateaus |
| Water | **Snow + rain → perennial** (flow all year) | **Rain only → seasonal**, low flow in summer |
| Valleys | Deep **gorges** and V-valleys (antecedent rivers) | Broad, shallow, mature valleys |
| Course | Long; **meander** in the plains; change course often (Kosi) | Shorter, fixed courses in hard rock |
| Silt | Very heavy load | Less |
| Mouth | Big deltas (Ganga–Brahmaputra) | East-flowing: deltas; west-flowing: **estuaries** |
| Use | Irrigation canals, inland navigation | Hydropower (falls, rapids), tank irrigation |

- About **77%** of India's drainage area drains into the **Bay of Bengal** and about **23%** into the **Arabian Sea**.
- The **Ganga basin** is the **largest** river basin in India (about a quarter of the country).

### Indus Waters Treaty, 1960

- Signed at Karachi on **19 September 1960** by **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Ayub Khan**, with the **World Bank** as broker.
- **Eastern rivers — Ravi, Beas, Sutlej — to India**; **Western rivers — Indus, Jhelum, Chenab — to Pakistan**, with India allowed limited use (run-of-the-river hydropower, some irrigation).
- In **April 2025**, after the Pahalgam terror attack, India announced it would hold the treaty "**in abeyance**" (as of 2026 — check the latest).

## The Ganga system

### Source and course

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Headstreams | **Bhagirathi** from the **Gangotri glacier (Gaumukh)**, Uttarkashi; **Alaknanda** from the Satopanth glacier near **Badrinath** |
| Named Ganga | At **Devprayag**, where the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet |
| Enters the plains | **Haridwar** |
| Length | About **2,525 km** — **longest river of India** |
| States on its main course | Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, **Bihar**, Jharkhand (briefly), West Bengal |
| Divides | At **Farakka** (West Bengal): **Bhagirathi–Hooghly** flows south into India to the Bay of Bengal; the main stream flows into Bangladesh as the **Padma** |
| In Bangladesh | Padma joins the **Jamuna** (Brahmaputra), then the **Meghna**, and enters the Bay of Bengal |
| Delta | **Ganga–Brahmaputra delta — largest in the world**; mangrove **Sundarbans** |
| Status | Declared **National River** in **2008**; its dolphin is the **National Aquatic Animal** (2009) |

</div>

**Panch Prayag** (five confluences on the Alaknanda), from upstream:

| Prayag | Alaknanda meets |
|---|---|
| **Vishnuprayag** | Dhauliganga |
| **Nandprayag** | Nandakini |
| **Karnaprayag** | Pindar |
| **Rudraprayag** | Mandakini (from Kedarnath) |
| **Devprayag** | **Bhagirathi** → the river becomes the Ganga |

> **Memory trick:** Upstream to downstream — "**V**ery **N**ice **K**ids **R**ead **D**aily": Vishnu, Nand, Karna, Rudra, Dev.

| River | Source | Joins the Ganga | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Gomti** | **Fulhar Jheel**, Pilibhit (UP) — plains-fed | Near Ghazipur (Kaithi) | Lucknow, Jaunpur |

| River | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|

> **Bihar connection:** Every major Ganga tributary that matters for Bihar's floods — Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla, Kosi, Mahananda — comes from the **north (Nepal Himalaya)**. The southern rivers — Son, Punpun, Phalgu, Kiul — are rain-fed and seasonal, so south Bihar faces **drought** while north Bihar floods. The Ganga flows about **445 km** in Bihar from **Chausa (Buxar)** to near **Kahalgaon–Pirpainti** (Bhagalpur), past **Buxar, Patna, Mokama, Begusarai, Munger, Sultanganj and Bhagalpur**. At **Sultanganj** it turns north (*uttarvahini*). The **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary** (1991) covers the Sultanganj–Kahalgaon stretch. Details in [Rivers, Floods and Climate of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/rivers-floods-and-climate-of-bihar).

> **Common mistake:** The **Narmada and Tapi** flow west not because the plateau slopes west (it slopes east) but because they flow along **rift valleys** (faults). This is why they form estuaries and not deltas.

## Interlinking of rivers

- The **National Perspective Plan (1980)** proposed transferring water from surplus to deficit basins. The **National Water Development Agency (NWDA)** has identified **30 links** — **14 Himalayan** and **16 peninsular**.
- **Ken–Betwa link** (MP–UP, Bundelkhand) is the **first** to be taken up; its foundation stone was laid in **December 2024**.
- Other notable links: Par–Tapi–Narmada, Damanganga–Pinjal, Godavari–Kaveri (being studied), Mahanadi–Godavari.
- **Bihar:** the **Kosi–Mechi intra-state link** aims to carry surplus Kosi water to the Mahananda basin to irrigate Araria, Purnia, Kishanganj and Katihar and reduce floods; the Union Budget 2025–26 also announced help for the **Western Kosi Canal** in Mithila (check the latest status).

> **Mains angle:** Arguments **for** interlinking: flood and drought balancing, irrigation for more area, hydropower, navigation. **Against:** huge cost, displacement, forest and wildlife loss (Panna for Ken–Betwa), changes in delta ecology, inter-state and international disputes, uncertain future flows under climate change. A balanced conclusion: proceed link by link with full environmental review, while also improving local water harvesting.

### Mains questions

**Q1. Compare the Himalayan and peninsular river systems of India. How do the Ganga's northern and southern tributaries shape the flood–drought pattern of Bihar? (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India's drainage falls into two systems with different origins, regimes and uses.
- **Comparison:** source (glaciers vs plateau), regime (perennial vs seasonal), valleys (gorges vs broad), course (meandering, shifting vs fixed), silt load, mouths (deltas vs estuaries for west-flowing), uses (canals and navigation vs hydropower and tanks). A two-column table is ideal.
- **Bihar — north:** Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla, Kosi, Mahananda from the Nepal Himalaya; heavy monsoon rain in the catchment, steep-to-flat gradient, silt raises riverbeds, embankment breaches → about three-quarters of north Bihar is flood-prone.
- **Bihar — south:** Son, Punpun, Phalgu, Kiul are rain-fed and seasonal; low groundwater recharge in the plateau fringe → recurring drought in Gaya, Nawada, Aurangabad, Jamui.
- **Measures:** Indo-Nepal cooperation (Kosi high dam, flood forecasting), embankment maintenance, flood-plain zoning, Kosi–Mechi link, ahar–pyne revival in the south, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali.
- **Conclusion:** the same river system gives Bihar both fertile soil and recurring disaster; basin-level planning is the answer.

**Q2. "Interlinking of rivers is the solution to India's flood and drought problems." Critically examine with reference to Bihar.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** the National Perspective Plan (1980), NWDA's 30 links; Ken–Betwa as the first.
- **For:** move surplus monsoon water to deficit areas; more irrigated area; hydropower; navigation; drought proofing.
- **Against:** cost and time; displacement; forest and wildlife loss; reduced silt for deltas; inter-state and international (Nepal, Bangladesh) issues; climate uncertainty; maintenance burden.
- **Bihar:** Kosi–Mechi link to divert Kosi floodwater to the Mahananda basin; Western Kosi Canal; possible benefits for Seemanchal; but main flood volume comes from Nepal, so links alone cannot stop floods.
- **Conclusion:** interlinking is one tool, not a cure; combine it with watershed management, groundwater recharge and transboundary cooperation.

**Q3. Discuss the importance of the Ganga for Bihar's economy and culture, and the challenges to its health.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** ~445 km course across Bihar; national river.
- **Economy:** fertile alluvium and diara farming; irrigation; fisheries; inland waterway NW-1 (Patna terminal); water supply for cities; sand and tourism.
- **Culture:** Chhath on the ghats; Sultanganj uttarvahini and Shravani mela; Sonepur fair at the Gandak confluence; Simaria; Vikramshila dolphin sanctuary.
- **Challenges:** untreated sewage from towns; industrial effluents; silting and shifting channels; erosion of banks; threat to dolphins; sand mining.
- **Responses:** Namami Gange sewage treatment plants in Patna and other towns; riverfront development; dolphin research centre; community awareness.
- **Conclusion:** a healthy Ganga is central to Bihar's livelihood and identity.
