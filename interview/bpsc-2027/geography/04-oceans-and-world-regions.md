This chapter does two jobs. First, it explains the oceans — their floor, salinity, currents, tides and coral reefs — which BPSC tests through questions such as "which current is cold?" and "where do fishing grounds form?". Second, it gives world-geography **fact tables**: continents, peaks, rivers, deserts, straits, lakes, grasslands, tribes and boundary lines. These are pure Prelims memory items. Read them with an atlas open, and revise the tables many times rather than reading them once.

> **In simple words:** About 71% of the earth is ocean. The ocean floor has shelves, slopes, deep plains, ridges and trenches. Sea water is salty, about 35 parts salt in 1,000 parts water. Huge "rivers" in the sea, called currents, carry warm or cold water and change the climate of coasts. The Moon and Sun pull the sea to make tides. The world tables at the end tell you which river, desert, strait or tribe is where.

## The five oceans

| Ocean | Rank by area | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Pacific** | 1st (about one-third of the earth's surface — larger than all land combined) | Deepest (**Mariana Trench**, Challenger Deep ~11 km); most islands; **Ring of Fire** around it; roughly triangular |
| **Atlantic** | 2nd | **S-shaped**; **Mid-Atlantic Ridge** runs down its centre; busiest ocean for trade; widest continental shelves; receives the largest rivers (Amazon, Congo, Mississippi via Gulf of Mexico) |
| **Indian** | 3rd | **Only ocean named after a country**; closed on the north by Asia; currents in its northern part **reverse with the monsoon** |
| **Southern (Antarctic)** | 4th | Surrounds Antarctica up to 60°S; recognised by the International Hydrographic Organization in 2000 and by National Geographic in 2021 |
| **Arctic** | 5th | **Smallest and shallowest**; mostly frozen; around the North Pole |

## Temperature and salinity

- Surface ocean temperature falls from the equator towards the poles, and with depth. Below about 1,000–2,000 m water is cold (2–4 °C) everywhere. The layer where temperature drops fast is the **thermocline**.
- **Salinity** = total dissolved salts in sea water, in **parts per thousand (‰)**. Average ocean salinity ≈ **35‰** (35 g of salt in 1 kg of water). Common salt (**sodium chloride**) is the main salt.

| Raises salinity | Lowers salinity |
|---|---|
| High evaporation (hot, dry, clear skies) | Heavy rain |
| Enclosed seas with little inflow | Large river inflow |
| Ice formation (salt left behind) | Melting ice |

| Water body | Salinity fact |
|---|---|
| **Red Sea** | Highest salinity among open seas (~40‰ or more) — high evaporation, no rivers |
| Persian Gulf, Mediterranean | High |
| **Baltic Sea** | Low — many rivers, cold, little evaporation |
| Dead Sea, Lake Van (Turkey), Great Salt Lake (USA) | Very high (inland lakes; Dead Sea about 10 times saltier than the ocean) |
| Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea | **Bay of Bengal is less saline** (Ganga, Brahmaputra and other rivers pour in fresh water); the **Arabian Sea is more saline** |

## Ocean currents

An **ocean current** is a large, steady flow of surface water in a fixed direction.

**Causes:** planetary winds (main cause), differences in temperature and salinity (density), the **Coriolis force**, and the shape of coastlines.

**General pattern:**
- Currents form big loops called **gyres** — **clockwise** in northern oceans and **anticlockwise** in southern oceans.
- **Warm currents** flow from the tropics towards the poles along the **eastern coasts** of continents in low and middle latitudes.
- **Cold currents** flow from the poles towards the equator along the **western coasts** of continents in low latitudes.

**Effects of currents:**
1. **Climate** — warm currents make coasts warmer and wetter; cold currents make them cooler and drier. **Cold currents + subtropical high → coastal deserts** (Atacama, Namib, west Australian, Sahara's Atlantic coast).
2. **Fishing** — where warm and cold currents meet, plankton grows → great **fishing grounds**: **Grand Banks** (Newfoundland), **Dogger Bank** (North Sea), off **Japan**, off **Peru** (upwelling).
3. **Fog** where warm and cold waters meet.
4. **Navigation** — ships use favourable currents; ports stay ice-free.

> **Prelims angle:** A favourite trap is "Gulf Stream is cold" or "Humboldt is warm". Remember: currents going **towards the poles** are usually **warm**; those going **towards the equator** are usually **cold**.

## Waves, tides and tsunamis

- **Waves** are mostly made by wind blowing over the water surface.
- **Tides** are the regular rise and fall of sea level, caused mainly by the **Moon's gravitational pull** (and less by the Sun). Most places get **two high tides and two low tides** in about **24 hours 50 minutes**.

| Tide | When | Height |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring tide** | **New moon and full moon** — Sun, Moon and Earth in a line | Highest high tides and lowest low tides |
| **Neap tide** | First and last quarter — Sun and Moon at right angles | Smallest tidal range |

- The world's highest tides are in the **Bay of Fundy** (Canada).
- In India the **Gulf of Khambhat** and **Gulf of Kutch** have very high tides, which gives **tidal energy** potential; the **Sundarbans** (Hooghly) also have strong tides. Ships enter **Kolkata port** with the help of high tides.
- **Tsunami** (Japanese, "harbour wave") — a series of very long sea waves caused by an undersea earthquake, volcano or landslide. India's tsunami warning centre is at **INCOIS, Hyderabad**. The **26 December 2004** Indian Ocean tsunami hit Tamil Nadu, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

## Coral reefs

**Corals** are tiny sea animals (*polyps*) that live in colonies and build hard skeletons of **calcium carbonate**. They live with algae (*zooxanthellae*) that give them food and colour.

Conditions: warm water (about **20–29 °C**), **clear, shallow, sunlit** water, normal salinity, little mud. So they grow in the tropics and avoid river mouths and cold currents.

| Type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fringing reef** | Close to the shore | Gulf of Mannar, Andaman coasts |
| **Barrier reef** | Parallel to the coast, separated by a wide lagoon | **Great Barrier Reef** (Australia) — the largest reef system in the world |
| **Atoll** | Ring around a lagoon, no land in the middle | **Lakshadweep**, Maldives |

India's coral areas: **Gulf of Mannar**, **Gulf of Kutch**, **Lakshadweep**, **Andaman & Nicobar Islands** (and patches at Malvan, Maharashtra).

**Coral bleaching** — when water becomes too warm, corals expel their algae and turn white; repeated bleaching kills them. It is a strong sign of climate change.

## Law of the sea and India's ocean interests

The **UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), 1982** sets ocean zones measured from the coastal baseline:

<div class="formula">

| Zone | Width | Rights of the coastal country |
|---|---|---|
| **Territorial sea** | **12 nautical miles** | Full sovereignty (like land); foreign ships have "innocent passage" |
| **Contiguous zone** | Up to **24 nautical miles** | Control over customs, immigration, pollution, sanitation |
| **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** | Up to **200 nautical miles** | Sole rights over fish, oil, gas, minerals and energy |
| High seas | Beyond the EEZ | Open to all |

</div>

- 1 nautical mile = **1.852 km**.
- India's EEZ is about **2.02 million km²**.
- The **International Seabed Authority** (Jamaica) gives India a site in the **Central Indian Ocean Basin** to explore **polymetallic nodules**.
- India's **Deep Ocean Mission** (approved 2021) includes **Samudrayaan**, a crewed submersible **Matsya-6000** designed to reach 6,000 m (as of 2026 — check the latest status).
- **Blue economy** = sustainable use of ocean resources for growth: fisheries, ports, shipping, tourism, offshore energy.

> **Mains angle:** For "importance of the Indian Ocean to India", use: about 90% of India's trade by volume moves by sea; energy imports pass through **Hormuz** and **Bab-el-Mandeb**; trade with East Asia passes through **Malacca**; a 2.02 million km² EEZ; island territories; fisheries livelihoods; naval security (SAGAR — Security and Growth for All in the Region).

## Quick revision

- Pacific: largest, deepest (Mariana ~11 km); Atlantic: S-shaped, busiest; Indian: only ocean named after a country; Arctic: smallest.
- Continental shelf: shallow (to ~200 m), fishing and oil (Mumbai High).
- Average salinity 35‰; Red Sea highest open sea; Baltic low; Bay of Bengal less saline than Arabian Sea.
- Gyres clockwise in the north, anticlockwise in the south.
- Warm: Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift, Kuroshio, Brazil, Agulhas, East Australian.
- Cold: Labrador, Canary, Benguela, Humboldt, California, Oyashio, Falkland, West Australian.
- Grand Banks = Gulf Stream + Labrador; cold currents + 20°–30° west coasts = deserts.
- Spring tide at new/full moon; Bay of Fundy highest tides; INCOIS Hyderabad tsunami warning.
- Great Barrier Reef = largest; Lakshadweep = atolls.
- Territorial sea 12 nm, contiguous 24 nm, EEZ 200 nm; India EEZ ~2.02 million km².
- Highest peaks: Everest, Aconcagua, Denali, Kilimanjaro, Elbrus, Vinson, Kosciuszko.
- Nile longest; Amazon largest volume; Congo crosses equator twice; Danube 10 countries; Volga longest in Europe → Caspian.
- Atacama driest; Sahara largest hot desert; Gobi cold desert.
- Hormuz (Iran–Oman), Malacca (Malaysia–Sumatra), Bab-el-Mandeb (Yemen–Djibouti), Palk (India–Sri Lanka), Ten Degree (Andaman–Nicobar), Eight Degree (Minicoy–Maldives).
- Suez 1869 (Mediterranean–Red Sea); Panama 1914 (Atlantic–Pacific).
- Caspian largest lake; Superior largest freshwater; Baikal deepest; Titicaca highest navigable.

### Mains questions

**Q1. What are ocean currents? Explain the factors that produce them and their influence on climate and economic activities, with examples.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** large, steady movements of surface ocean water; warm or cold; form gyres.
- **Factors:** planetary winds (trades and westerlies); temperature and salinity differences; Coriolis force (clockwise gyres in the north, anticlockwise in the south); coastline shape and sea-floor relief.
- **Climate effects:** North Atlantic Drift keeps north-west Europe mild and Norway's ports ice-free; cold currents cause coastal deserts (Atacama, Namib); fog where currents meet; El Niño changes in the Humboldt current affect the global climate, including India's monsoon.
- **Economic effects:** fishing grounds where warm and cold waters meet or upwelling occurs (Grand Banks, Japan, Peru); navigation and shipping routes; ice-free ports.
- **India link:** monsoon-reversing currents of the north Indian Ocean; fisheries along both coasts.
- **Conclusion:** currents are the "conveyor belts" of heat; climate change can weaken them, with global consequences.

**Q2. Discuss the strategic and economic importance of the Indian Ocean for India.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India sits at the centre of the Indian Ocean with a long coastline, two island groups and an EEZ of ~2.02 million km².
- **Economic:** about 90% of trade by volume by sea; crude oil and LNG imports through Hormuz; trade with East Asia through Malacca; fisheries for millions of coastal people; offshore oil and gas (Mumbai High, KG basin); polymetallic nodules (Central Indian Ocean Basin); blue economy and port-led development (Sagarmala).
- **Strategic:** sea-lane security and choke points; piracy (Gulf of Aden); presence of extra-regional navies; Andaman & Nicobar near Malacca; SAGAR vision; cooperation through IORA and maritime exercises.
- **Environmental:** cyclones, tsunamis, coral bleaching, sea-level rise.
- **Conclusion:** the Indian Ocean is India's economic lifeline and first line of defence; capacity building and regional cooperation are both needed.
