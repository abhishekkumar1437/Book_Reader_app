This is the economic geography chapter: where India's minerals lie, how it makes power, why industries grew where they did, and how goods and people move. BPSC Prelims asks "mineral → leading state / mine", "industry → city", "port → feature", "nuclear plant → state" and "first railway / first refinery" type questions. Mains GS II asks about factors of industrial location, energy security and renewable energy, and Bihar's industrial and infrastructure gap after **Jharkhand's separation in 2000**, when most of Bihar's mines and heavy industry went to the new state. Bihar's own minerals and resources are in [Agriculture, Minerals and Natural Resources of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/agriculture-minerals-and-natural-resources-of-bihar); its economy in [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

> **In simple words:** Most of India's metallic minerals and coal lie in the old rocks of the Peninsula — especially the Chota Nagpur–Odisha plateau. Heavy industries like steel grew near coal and iron ore. India gets most of its electricity from coal, but solar and wind are growing fast. Roads, railways, rivers, ports and pipelines link mines, factories, farms and markets. Bihar today has few minerals but sits on major transport routes between north India and the east.

## Minerals

A **mineral** is a natural substance with a definite chemical composition. An **ore** is a rock from which a metal can be extracted profitably.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Metallic — ferrous** (contain iron, used for iron and steel) | Iron ore, manganese, chromite, nickel, cobalt |
| **Metallic — non-ferrous** | Copper, bauxite (aluminium), lead, zinc, gold, silver |
| **Non-metallic** | Mica, limestone, dolomite, gypsum, salt, pyrite, phosphate |
| **Energy minerals** | Coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium, thorium |

### Mineral belts

| Belt | States | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| **North-eastern plateau** (richest) | **Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal**, parts of MP | Coal, iron ore, manganese, bauxite, mica, copper, uranium |
| South-western plateau | Karnataka, Goa, Tamil Nadu and Kerala uplands | Iron ore, manganese, gold, bauxite, monazite (Kerala sands) |
| North-western | **Rajasthan**, Gujarat | Copper, lead–zinc, silver, gypsum, marble, petroleum (Gujarat, Rajasthan) |
| Himalayan, Indian Ocean | — | Limited; offshore oil and gas; polymetallic nodules |

### Minerals and leading states

| Mineral | Leading states (main mines) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron ore** | **Odisha** (largest producer — Keonjhar, Sundargarh, Mayurbhanj), **Chhattisgarh** (**Bailadila**, Dantewada), **Karnataka** (Ballari–Hosapete; **Kudremukh**), **Jharkhand** (**Noamundi**, Gua — Singhbhum), Goa | **Magnetite** has the highest iron content; **hematite** is the most used ore. Exports via Paradip, Visakhapatnam, Mormugao, Mangaluru |
| **Manganese** | Madhya Pradesh (**Balaghat**), Maharashtra (Nagpur–Bhandara), Odisha, Karnataka | For steel and batteries |
| **Chromite** | **Odisha** (**Sukinda valley**, Jajpur) — nearly all | For stainless steel |
| **Bauxite** | **Odisha** (**Panchpatmali**, Koraput — NALCO), Gujarat, Jharkhand (Lohardaga), Chhattisgarh (Mainpat, Amarkantak), Maharashtra | Ore of **aluminium** |
| **Copper** | **Madhya Pradesh** (**Malanjkhand**, Balaghat), **Rajasthan** (**Khetri**, Jhunjhunu), **Jharkhand** (Singhbhum — Ghatsila, Mosabani) | India imports much copper |
| **Lead and zinc** | **Rajasthan** (**Zawar**, Udaipur; **Rampura Agucha**, Bhilwara — one of the world's largest zinc mines) | Hindustan Zinc |
| **Gold** | **Karnataka** (**Hutti**, Raichur); Kolar Gold Fields closed in 2001; small amounts in Andhra Pradesh and Jharkhand | **Bihar's Jamui** district holds the largest **gold ore resources** in the country by estimate (exploration — check the latest) |
| **Silver** | Rajasthan (Zawar) | By-product of lead–zinc |
| **Mica** | Andhra Pradesh (**Nellore**), Rajasthan, **Jharkhand** (Koderma–Giridih–Hazaribagh belt) | Electrical insulator; **Koderma** was the "mica capital"; some mica in Bihar (Nawada, Jamui, Gaya) |
| **Limestone** | Rajasthan, MP, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat; **Bihar (Rohtas–Kaimur)** | Cement and steel |
| **Pyrite** | **Bihar — Amjhor (Rohtas)** | Source of sulphur (sulphuric acid) |
| **Gypsum** | **Rajasthan** | Cement, plaster, alkaline soil treatment |
| **Diamond** | **Madhya Pradesh — Panna** (Majhgawan, NMDC) | India's only working diamond mine |
| **Salt** | **Gujarat** (largest), Rajasthan (Sambhar), Tamil Nadu | Sea and lake salt |
| **Uranium** | **Jharkhand — Jaduguda** (first mine, 1967), Bagjata, Turamdih; **Andhra Pradesh — Tummalapalle**; Meghalaya (Domiasiat); Rajasthan | Nuclear fuel; Uranium Corporation of India (UCIL) |
| **Thorium** | **Monazite sands of Kerala** (Chavara), Tamil Nadu, Odisha | India has large thorium reserves → **three-stage nuclear programme** of **Homi J. Bhabha** |
| **Lithium** | **J&K — Reasi** (about **5.9 million tonnes** of inferred resources, announced Feb 2023) | For batteries; a **critical mineral**; **National Critical Mineral Mission (2025)** |

> **Memory trick:** "**O**dd **I**ron **C**hroms **B**right" — **O**disha leads in **I**ron, **C**hromite and **B**auxite. "**R**ajasthan **Z**inc **L**ead **S**ilver **G**ypsum" — "**R**ich **Z**amindars **L**ike **S**ilver **G**ifts".

> **Bihar connection:** When **Jharkhand was created on 15 November 2000**, nearly all of united Bihar's coal, iron, copper, mica, uranium and bauxite mines went with it. Today's Bihar has mainly **limestone** (Rohtas, Kaimur), **pyrite** (Amjhor), some **mica** (Nawada, Jamui, Gaya), **quartzite**, **sand** (Son, Ganga), and newly assessed **gold ore** at **Jamui (Sono–Karmatiya)**. This is why Mains questions on Bihar's development stress agriculture, agro-processing and services rather than mining.

## Coal

India is the **world's second largest producer and consumer of coal** (after China). Coal provides most of India's electricity. Annual production crossed **1 billion tonnes** in 2024–25 (check the latest).

| Type | Carbon content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthracite** | Highest (80–95%) | Best quality; very small amounts in J&K |
| **Bituminous** | 60–80% | **Most common** and most used; **coking coal** for steel is a type of bituminous coal |
| **Lignite** (brown coal) | 40–55% | High moisture; **Neyveli** (Tamil Nadu), Gujarat, Rajasthan |
| Peat | Below 40% | First stage of coal formation |

| Coal age | Share | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Gondwana** (about 250 million years old) | About **98%** of reserves | River valleys of the peninsula — **Damodar** (Jharkhand–WB), **Son** (MP–Chhattisgarh), **Mahanadi** (Odisha), **Godavari** (Telangana), **Wardha** (Maharashtra) |
| **Tertiary** | About 2% | North-East — Assam (Makum), Meghalaya, Arunachal; lignite in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Rajasthan |

| Coalfield | State | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Jharia** | Jharkhand | **Largest coking coal** field; long-burning underground fires |
| **Raniganj** | West Bengal | **Oldest** coalfield (mining since 1774) |
| Bokaro, Giridih, Karanpura | Jharkhand | Damodar valley |
| **Korba** | Chhattisgarh | Also BALCO aluminium and NTPC plant |
| **Talcher** | Odisha | — |
| **Singrauli** | MP–UP border | "Energy capital" — many super thermal power stations |
| **Singareni** | Telangana | Godavari valley |
| **Neyveli** | Tamil Nadu | **Lignite** |

- **Jharkhand** has the **largest coal reserves**; **Odisha** and **Chhattisgarh** now lead in **production**, with Jharkhand close behind (check the latest).
- **Coal India Limited** — 1975; coal mines were nationalised in 1971–73; commercial coal mining opened to private firms in 2020.

| Region | Fields | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| **Gujarat** | **Ankleshwar**, Kalol, Mehsana, Lunej (Cambay basin) | Onshore |
| **Mumbai High** | Offshore, about 160 km from Mumbai | Discovered **1974**; **largest producing field** |

| **Krishna–Godavari (KG) basin** | Offshore, Andhra Pradesh | Major **natural gas** |
| Cauvery basin | Tamil Nadu | Oil and gas |

- India imports about **85%** of its crude oil (check the latest) and is the **third largest oil consumer** in the world.
- **Refineries:** about two dozen; **Jamnagar** (Reliance, Gujarat) is the **world's largest single-site refining complex**. Others: **Barauni (Bihar, IOC, 1964, built with Soviet help)**, Digboi, Guwahati, Bongaigaon, Numaligarh, Haldia, Paradip, Mathura, Panipat, Koyali (Vadodara), Mumbai, Kochi, Mangaluru, Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Bina, Bathinda.
- **Strategic Petroleum Reserves** (underground rock caverns): **Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, Padur**.

## Power

India's installed electricity capacity is roughly **480–500 GW** (2025). In mid-2025, **non-fossil sources** (renewables, large hydro, nuclear) crossed **50% of installed capacity** — five years ahead of the 2030 target — although coal still produces most of the actual electricity (check the latest).

### Thermal power

- Coal-based plants at pitheads and near ports: **Singrauli**, **Korba**, **Ramagundam**, **Talcher**, **Vindhyachal** (largest NTPC station), **Farakka**, **Kahalgaon**, **Barh**.
- **NTPC** (1975) is India's largest power company.

### Hydropower

Big projects: **Bhakra–Nangal**, **Tehri**, **Nathpa Jhakri** (Sutlej, HP), Koyna, Srisailam, Nagarjuna Sagar, Sardar Sarovar, Teesta, Subansiri Lower. The Himalaya and North-East hold most of the untapped potential. **Shivasamudram** (Kaveri, 1902) was among Asia's first hydroelectric stations.

### Nuclear power

| Plant | State | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Tarapur** | Maharashtra | **India's first nuclear power station (1969)**, US help |
| Rawatbhata (Rajasthan Atomic Power Station) | Rajasthan | Canadian help (CANDU-type) |
| **Kalpakkam** (Madras Atomic Power Station) | Tamil Nadu | Indigenous; **Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor** (second stage of Bhabha's plan) |
| Narora | Uttar Pradesh | — |
| Kakrapar | Gujarat | First indigenous 700 MW reactors |
| Kaiga | Karnataka | — |
| **Kudankulam** | Tamil Nadu | **Largest**; Russian help |
| Gorakhpur (upcoming) | Haryana | — |

- Run by **Nuclear Power Corporation of India (NPCIL)**; atomic energy is guided by the **Department of Atomic Energy** and **Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC), Trombay**.
- The 2025–26 Budget announced a **Nuclear Energy Mission** (small modular reactors) with a target of **100 GW nuclear by 2047** (check the latest).

### Renewable energy

| Source | Leading areas / projects |
|---|---|
| **Solar** | **Rajasthan** (largest capacity; **Bhadla Solar Park**, Jodhpur), Gujarat (**Khavda** renewable energy park, Kutch — planned as the world's largest), Karnataka (**Pavagada**), MP (**Rewa**) |
| **Wind** | **Gujarat**, **Tamil Nadu** (**Muppandal**, Kanyakumari), Karnataka, Maharashtra |
| Geothermal | **Puga valley** (Ladakh), Manikaran (HP) — pilot stage |
| Tidal | **Gulf of Khambhat**, Gulf of Kutch, Sundarbans — potential |
| Bio-energy | Ethanol from sugarcane and grains — **20% ethanol blending (E20)** in petrol reached in 2025 (check the latest) |
| Green hydrogen | **National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023)** — 5 million tonnes a year by 2030 |

- Target: **500 GW** of non-fossil capacity by **2030**; **net zero by 2070**.
- **International Solar Alliance** — launched by **India and France** in **2015** (COP21, Paris); HQ **Gurugram**.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's major power stations are **NTPC Kahalgaon** (Bhagalpur), **NTPC Barh** (Patna), **Nabinagar** (Aurangabad — two NTPC-group stations), and **Kanti** (Muzaffarpur) and **Barauni** (Begusarai), both now run by NTPC. Bihar has little hydropower and depends on central-sector allocation. It is now adding **solar** — rooftop, canal-top and floating solar, and a large solar-plus-battery project at **Kajra (Lakhisarai)** (check the latest).

## Industries

### Factors of industrial location

| Factor | Example |
|---|---|
| **Raw material** (heavy, weight-losing materials) | Iron and steel near coal and iron ore (Chota Nagpur); sugar mills near cane fields |
| Power | Aluminium smelters near cheap power (Renukoot near Rihand; Korba; Hirakud) |
| Labour | Textile and IT industries |
| **Market** | Petroleum refineries near consumers (Mathura, Panipat); bakery, consumer goods |
| Transport | Ports (Visakhapatnam steel); rail junctions |
| Water | Steel, textiles, chemicals |
| Capital and government policy | Public-sector plants in backward regions; SEZs; tax incentives |
| Agglomeration | Industrial clusters share services (Mumbai–Pune, Bengaluru) |

**Alfred Weber's least-cost theory (1909):** industries locate where the combined cost of transporting raw material and finished goods is lowest.

### Major industries and centres

| Industry | Key facts and centres |
|---|---|
| **Cotton textiles** | First mill near Kolkata (Fort Gloster, 1818, failed); first successful mill **Mumbai, 1854** (C.N. Davar). Mumbai — "**Cottonopolis**"; **Ahmedabad** — "**Manchester of India**"; **Coimbatore** — "Manchester of South India"; **Kanpur** — "Manchester of the North"; Surat — synthetics; Tiruppur — knitwear. Black-soil cotton belt + humid coast + port + capital |
| **Jute** | First mill at **Rishra** (near Kolkata), **1855**; concentrated in the **Hooghly belt** (raw jute nearby, water, coal from Raniganj, cheap labour from Bihar–Odisha–UP, port). Bihar had jute mills at **Katihar** and **Purnia** (most closed) |
| **Sugar** | **Uttar Pradesh** (largest), Maharashtra (cooperatives), Karnataka, Tamil Nadu; shift to the south (higher sucrose, longer season, cooperatives). Bihar's sugar mills are in **West Champaran, Gopalganj, East Champaran, Sitamarhi, Samastipur** — many old mills closed |
| **Iron and steel** | **TISCO, Jamshedpur (1907)** — first large integrated plant (Sakchi; Subarnarekha–Kharkai). Public-sector plants (SAIL): **Bhilai** (Chhattisgarh — USSR help), **Rourkela** (Odisha — West Germany), **Durgapur** (WB — UK) — all in the **Second Plan**; **Bokaro** (Jharkhand — USSR, 1960s–70s); **Burnpur (IISCO)**; **Visakhapatnam (RINL)** — first **shore-based** plant; Salem, Bhadravati. Private: JSW Vijayanagar, Tata Kalinganagar. India is the **2nd largest crude steel producer** |
| **Aluminium** | **HINDALCO, Renukoot** (UP, near Rihand power); **BALCO, Korba** (Chhattisgarh); **NALCO** (Damanjodi refinery and Angul smelter, Odisha); Vedanta (Jharsuguda); Hirakud; Muri (Jharkhand) |
| **Copper smelting** | Khetri (Rajasthan), **Ghatsila** (Jharkhand), Malanjkhand (MP) — Hindustan Copper |
| **Cement** | First factory at Chennai (1904); large in Rajasthan, MP, AP, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Chhattisgarh; India is the **2nd largest producer**. Bihar: historic **Dalmianagar (Rohtas)**; plants in Rohtas–Kaimur and grinding units |
| **Fertilisers** | **Sindri** (1951, then in Bihar, now Jharkhand) — first big public-sector unit; revived plants at **Barauni (Bihar)**, Gorakhpur and Sindri (Hindustan Urvarak & Rasayan); neem-coated urea (2015); **nano urea** (IFFCO, 2021) |
| **Petrochemicals** | Vadodara, Jamnagar, Hazira, Mumbai, Haldia |
| **Automobiles** | **Gurugram–Manesar** (Maruti, 1983), **Pune**, **Chennai** ("**Detroit of Asia**"), Jamshedpur, Sanand |
| **Software / IT** | **Bengaluru** ("**Silicon Valley of India**"), Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Noida, Gurugram |
| **Shipbuilding** | **Visakhapatnam** (Hindustan Shipyard), **Kochi** (Cochin Shipyard — built **INS Vikrant**, commissioned 2022), Mumbai (Mazagon Dock), Kolkata (GRSE) |
| **Rail coaches and locomotives** | **Chittaranjan** (WB, 1950), **Varanasi** (Banaras Locomotive Works), **ICF Chennai** (Perambur), RCF Kapurthala, MCF Rae Bareli, Rail Wheel Factory Yelahanka; **in Bihar — Electric Locomotive Factory, Madhepura** (with Alstom; 12,000 HP engines), **Diesel Locomotive Factory, Marhowrah** (Saran), **Rail Wheel Plant, Bela** (Saran) |

### Industrial regions and corridors

- **Major industrial regions:** Mumbai–Pune; Hooghly belt; Bengaluru–Chennai; Gujarat (Ahmedabad–Vadodara); **Chota Nagpur** (Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Dhanbad); Visakhapatnam–Guntur; Gurugram–Delhi–Meerut; Kollam–Thiruvananthapuram.
- **Industrial corridors:** **Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC)** along the Western DFC; **Amritsar–Kolkata Industrial Corridor (AKIC)** along the Eastern DFC — it passes through **Bihar**, and an integrated manufacturing cluster at **Gaya** was approved in 2024 (check the latest); Chennai–Bengaluru; Bengaluru–Mumbai; Vizag–Chennai.

> **Bihar connection:** Post-2000 Bihar's industry is mostly **agro-based and small** — food processing (makhana, litchi, maize), dairy (Sudha), sugar, leather, textiles and handlooms (Bhagalpur silk), ethanol (Bihar's 2021 ethanol policy, among the first state ethanol policies — check), plus the Barauni refinery and fertiliser plant and the railway factories at Madhepura, Marhowrah and Bela. Mains answers on "industrial backwardness of Bihar" should list causes: loss of minerals in 2000, low power supply in the past, floods, land scarcity, small market towns, capital flight, and law-and-order perceptions earlier; and remedies: industrial policies (Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy), AKIC, food parks, logistics hubs, skilling.

### Mains questions

**Q1. Explain the factors that influence the location of the iron and steel industry in India. Why is it concentrated in the Chota Nagpur region? (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** iron and steel is a basic, heavy industry using weight-losing raw materials; India is the 2nd largest crude steel producer.
- **Factors:** iron ore, coking coal, limestone, dolomite, manganese close together; water; power; cheap transport (rail, port); labour; market; capital and government policy.
- **Chota Nagpur concentration:** Singhbhum iron ore, Jharia–Raniganj–Bokaro coal, limestone and manganese nearby; Damodar and Subarnarekha water; Kolkata port and market; railway network; history (TISCO 1907, IISCO, Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela and Bhilai in the wider belt).
- **New trends:** coastal plants using imported coking coal (Visakhapatnam, Paradip region, Dolvi); mini steel plants using scrap and electric furnaces near markets; private plants in Karnataka, Odisha.
- **Bihar angle:** Bokaro and Jamshedpur went to Jharkhand in 2000; Bihar can benefit as a market and through ancillary units along the Eastern DFC and AKIC.
- **Conclusion:** raw-material orientation is giving way to port and market orientation as technology and trade change.

**Q2. Bihar lost most of its minerals and heavy industry with the creation of Jharkhand. Examine the present industrial structure of Bihar and suggest a strategy for industrialisation based on its geographical strengths.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** 2000 bifurcation — mines, steel, coal, mica and many industries went to Jharkhand; Bihar became largely agrarian.
- **Present structure:** agro-processing (makhana, litchi, maize, sugar, dairy — Sudha), ethanol units, Barauni refinery and fertiliser plant, rail factories (Madhepura, Marhowrah, Bela), handloom and silk (Bhagalpur), leather, construction materials (cement in Rohtas–Kaimur), MSMEs.
- **Geographical strengths:** fertile Ganga plain and water; large labour force; location between north India, the North-East, Nepal and Kolkata–Haldia ports; NW-1, Eastern DFC (ends at Sonnagar), AKIC (Gaya cluster), GQ and East–West corridors; Urja Ganga gas pipeline; tourism (Buddhist circuit).
- **Strategy:** food parks and cold chains near production clusters; logistics hubs on DFC and NW-1; textile and leather parks; ethanol and bio-energy; electronics assembly; skilling and return migrants; land banks; single-window clearance; better power (solar + storage); flood-safe industrial areas.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's path is "agro-industry + logistics + services", not mining — the geography supports it.

**Q3. Assess the role of the National Waterway-1 in the development of Bihar.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** NW-1, Haldia–Prayagraj (~1,620 km), longest NW; Jal Marg Vikas Project.
- **Benefits:** cheap, fuel-efficient bulk transport (coal, fertiliser, food grains, cement); terminals at Patna (Gaighat) and nearby; links to Kolkata–Haldia and Bangladesh routes; tourism (river cruises); less road congestion and emissions; employment.
- **Challenges:** low depth in the lean season; shifting channels and silting; need for dredging (ecological concerns for dolphins); poor last-mile links; few cargo users so far.
- **Way forward:** assured channel depth with eco-friendly dredging; multimodal links with rail and NH; incentives for shippers; ro-ro services; integrating with the Indo-Bangladesh Protocol route.
- **Conclusion:** NW-1 can make the Ganga Bihar's "economic highway" again, as it was in history.
