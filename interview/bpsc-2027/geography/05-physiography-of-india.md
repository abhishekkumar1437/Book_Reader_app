**Physiography** means the shape and structure of the land — mountains, plains, plateaus, coasts. This is the backbone of Indian geography: rivers, climate, soils, crops, minerals and even population follow the physiography. BPSC Prelims asks about ranges, peaks, passes, the doabs, plateaus, coastal plains and islands. Mains GS II asks you to explain the divisions and their significance, often with "and Bihar" added. This chapter covers India; Bihar's own physiographic regions are in [Physical Geography of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/physical-geography-of-bihar), which you should read alongside.

> **In simple words:** India has three big building blocks. In the north stand the young, high **Himalaya**. In the south lies the old, hard **Peninsular Plateau**. Between them is the flat, fertile **Northern Plain** made of river mud. Around these are the **Thar Desert** in the west, narrow **coastal plains** along the seas, and two island groups. Bihar sits mostly on the Northern Plain, with a strip of plateau edge in the south.

## How India's land was formed

| Unit | Age | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Peninsular block** | Very old — part of **Gondwanaland** | Hard, stable crystalline rocks (granite, gneiss); worn-down hills, broad valleys; rich in metallic minerals and coal |
| **Himalaya** | Young — formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates (main uplift over the last ~40–50 million years) | Fold mountains of sedimentary rocks; still rising; earthquake-prone; deep gorges |
| **Indo-Gangetic–Brahmaputra plain** | Youngest — Quaternary (recent) deposits | Filled by sediment brought by Himalayan and peninsular rivers into a depression (foredeep) in front of the Himalaya |

See chapter 2, [Interior, Rocks and Landforms](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/interior-rocks-and-landforms), for plate tectonics.

### Rock systems of India (asked as "match the following")

| Rock system | Main features | Economic importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Archaean** | Oldest rocks; gneiss and schist; no fossils | Base of the peninsula; Bundelkhand gneiss |
| **Dharwar** | Oldest metamorphosed sedimentary rocks | **Metallic minerals** — iron ore, manganese, gold (Kolar, Hutti), copper |
| **Cuddapah** | Sandstone, limestone, shale | Cement-grade limestone, some iron, asbestos |
| **Vindhyan** | Sandstone, limestone; spread from Bihar (Sasaram, Rohtas) to Rajasthan | **Building stone** (red sandstone of Red Fort and Sanchi Stupa), **diamonds of Panna**, limestone |
| **Gondwana** | River-laid sediments in old basins (Damodar, Son, Mahanadi, Godavari) | About **98% of India's coal** |
| **Deccan Traps** | Flood basalt lava (end of the Cretaceous, about 66 million years ago) | Weathers into **black (regur) cotton soil** |
| **Tertiary** | Himalaya, Assam, Gujarat, Rajasthan | **Petroleum** (Assam, Gujarat), Tertiary coal and lignite (Assam, Meghalaya, Neyveli lignite in Tamil Nadu) |
| **Quaternary** | Alluvium of the Northern Plains | Fertile soils, groundwater |

## 1. The Himalaya

The **Himalaya** ("abode of snow") stretch about **2,400 km** from the **Indus gorge** in the west to the **Brahmaputra (Dihang) gorge** in the east, in an arc. Width varies from about **400 km in Kashmir to about 150 km in Arunachal Pradesh**. Height is greater in the east, width greater in the west.

### Trans-Himalaya

North of the Great Himalaya, mostly in Ladakh and Tibet: the **Karakoram**, **Ladakh** and **Zaskar** ranges.
- **K2 (Godwin Austen), 8,611 m** — the **highest peak in India's territory as claimed** (it lies in Gilgit-Baltistan, under Pakistan's illegal occupation) and the second highest in the world.
- **Siachen Glacier** (Karakoram, about 76 km) — one of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions; the highest battlefield in the world.
- The Karakoram is also called *Krishnagiri*.

### Three parallel ranges

| Range | Other name | Average height | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greater Himalaya** | **Himadri**, Inner Himalaya | About **6,000 m** | Most continuous; highest peaks; core of granite; snow-bound; glaciers (**Gangotri**, Yamunotri, **Zemu** in Sikkim, Milam, Pindari) |
| **Lesser Himalaya** | **Himachal**, Middle Himalaya | 3,700–4,500 m | Ranges: **Pir Panjal** (longest), **Dhauladhar**, **Mahabharat** (Nepal); valleys of **Kashmir**, **Kangra**, **Kullu**; hill stations — Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, Ranikhet, Darjeeling |
| **Shiwalik** | Outer Himalaya | 900–1,100 m | **Youngest** and lowest; made of unconsolidated sediments (gravel, clay); prone to landslides; flat longitudinal valleys called **duns** — **Dehradun**, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun |

### Purvanchal (eastern hills)

Beyond the Dihang gorge the Himalaya turn south as the **Purvanchal**: **Patkai Bum** (Arunachal–Myanmar), **Naga Hills** (highest peak **Saramati**, about 3,826 m), **Manipur hills**, **Mizo (Lushai) Hills** (highest peak Phawngpui, "Blue Mountain"). Arunachal has the **Dafla, Miri, Abor and Mishmi** hills. Mostly sandstone; dense forests; shifting cultivation (*jhum*).

### Why the Himalaya matter (Mains points)

1. **Climatic barrier** — block cold winds from Central Asia; force monsoon winds to rise and shed rain over north India.
2. **Source of perennial rivers** — glaciers feed the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems, which flow all year.
3. **Fertile plains** — rivers carry silt that built the Northern Plains.
4. **Hydropower** — huge potential (Tehri, Bhakra, Nathpa Jhakri, Teesta projects).
5. **Forests and biodiversity** — part of the Himalaya and Indo-Burma biodiversity hotspots; medicinal plants.
6. **Tourism and pilgrimage** — Kedarnath, Badrinath, Amarnath, Vaishno Devi; hill stations.
7. **Defence** — natural frontier (though passes and the LAC need guarding).
8. **Minerals** — limited, but some (limestone, slate).
9. **Hazards** — earthquakes, landslides, glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs), cloudbursts; fragile ecology.

## 2. The Northern Plains

The plains are formed by the **Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra** and their tributaries.

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Area | About **7 lakh km²** |
| Length | About 2,400 km |
| Width | About 240–320 km (wider in the west) |
| Slope | Extremely gentle — average fall is only a few centimetres per km; rivers meander and flood |
| Population | One of the most densely populated regions in the world |

</div>

### Relief belts from north to south

| Belt | Description |
|---|---|
| **Bhabar** | Narrow belt (about 8–16 km) at the foot of the Shiwaliks; **pebbles and boulders**; streams **disappear underground**; not suitable for farming |
| **Terai** | South of the Bhabar; streams come up again; **wet, marshy, swampy**, thick forests and wildlife; much now cleared for farming. Bihar's north edge (West Champaran) has Terai land |
| **Bhangar** | **Older alluvium**, on higher land above flood level; contains **kankar** (calcareous nodules); less fertile than khadar |
| **Khadar** | **Newer alluvium** of the floodplains, renewed by floods almost every year; **very fertile** |
| Reh / Kallar | Saline or alkaline patches in drier areas due to poor drainage and over-irrigation |

### Divisions of the plain

**(a) Punjab plain** (west) — formed by the Indus and its five tributaries. The land between two rivers is a **doab** ("two waters"):

| Doab | Between |
|---|---|
| **Bist Doab** (Bist-Jalandhar) | **Beas and Sutlej** |
| **Bari Doab** | **Beas and Ravi** |
| **Rachna Doab** | **Ravi and Chenab** |
| **Chaj Doab** | **Chenab and Jhelum** |
| Sind Sagar Doab | Jhelum–Chenab and Indus |

Only the Bist Doab and part of the Bari Doab are in India; the rest are in Pakistan. The **Ganga–Yamuna Doab** in western UP is the most famous doab of India.

**(b) Ganga plain** — from the Yamuna to the Bangladesh border; the largest part:
- **Upper Ganga plain** — western and central Uttar Pradesh (Ganga–Yamuna doab, Rohilkhand, Awadh).
- **Middle Ganga plain** — **eastern UP and Bihar**. Rivers shift courses often; floods common.
- **Lower Ganga plain** — West Bengal (delta region).

**(c) Brahmaputra plain** — the Assam valley; narrow; **Majuli** in the Brahmaputra is the **largest river island** in India (a district of Assam).

> **Bihar connection:** Almost all of Bihar lies in the **Middle Ganga plain**. The Ganga divides it into the **north Bihar plain** (built by the Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla and Kosi; very flood-prone; Terai at the Nepal border) and the **south Bihar plain** (narrower, older alluvium, rising towards the plateau). The **diara** lands are sandy riverine islands and flood-plain strips along the Ganga. In the extreme south lies the edge of the Chota Nagpur plateau and the **Kaimur (Rohtas) plateau**; in the north-west, the **Someshwar range** of the Shiwaliks in West Champaran holds the state's highest point. Details: [Physical Geography of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/physical-geography-of-bihar).

## 3. The Peninsular Plateau

A broad, **triangular** tableland of old, hard rocks, south of the Northern Plains. It is bounded by the **Aravalli** (north-west), the **Kaimur and Rajmahal hills** (north-east), the **Western Ghats** (west) and the **Eastern Ghats** (east). It slopes generally **towards the east**, which is why most big peninsular rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) flow east into the Bay of Bengal. The **Narmada** divides it into two parts.

### (a) Central Highlands (north of the Narmada)

| Unit | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aravalli** | Gujarat to Delhi through Rajasthan | **Oldest fold mountains** of India, now residual; highest peak **Guru Shikhar (1,722 m)**, on **Mount Abu**; Dilwara temples |
| **Malwa plateau** | Western Madhya Pradesh | Black soil; rivers Chambal, Betwa flow north-east |
| **Bundelkhand** | UP–MP border | Granite and gneiss; drought-prone |
| **Baghelkhand** | East of Bundelkhand (MP) | Limestone, sandstone |
| **Vindhya range** | Across central India, continues as **Kaimur hills** into Bihar | Separates north and south India; sandstone |
| **Chota Nagpur plateau** | Jharkhand and nearby parts of Chhattisgarh, Odisha, West Bengal | **Mineral heartland** of India (coal, iron, mica, bauxite, copper, uranium); **Ranchi plateau**; highest peak **Parasnath (1,365 m)**; Damodar flows through a rift valley with Gondwana coal |
| **Meghalaya (Shillong) plateau** | Meghalaya; extends as **Karbi Anglong** in Assam | Separated from Chota Nagpur by the **Garo–Rajmahal gap** (Malda gap); **Garo, Khasi, Jaintia** hills; **Mawsynram and Cherrapunji (Sohra)** — among the wettest places on earth |

### (b) Deccan Plateau (south of the Narmada)

- Bounded by the **Satpura**, **Mahadeo** and **Maikal** ranges in the north, the Western Ghats in the west and the Eastern Ghats in the east.
- **Satpura** — a **block mountain** between the Narmada and Tapi rift valleys; highest point **Dhupgarh (~1,350 m)** near **Pachmarhi** (Mahadeo hills).
- **Maikal range** — **Amarkantak** plateau is the source of the **Narmada** and **Son** (and the Mahanadi rises nearby at Sihawa).
- The north-west part is covered by the **Deccan Traps** (basalt) → black soil.

### Western and Eastern Ghats

| Feature | Western Ghats (Sahyadri) | Eastern Ghats |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | **Continuous** (broken only by gaps like Thal, Bhor, Palghat) | **Discontinuous** — cut by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri |
| Height | Higher — average 900–1,600 m | Lower — average about 600 m |
| Length | About 1,600 km, Tapi valley to Kanyakumari | From Odisha to the Nilgiris |
| Highest peak | **Anamudi (2,695 m)** in the **Anaimalai hills, Kerala** — highest peak of peninsular India; also **Doddabetta (2,637 m)** in the **Nilgiris** | **Jindhagada (about 1,690 m)** in Andhra Pradesh (older books say Mahendragiri, ~1,501 m, Odisha) |
| Local names | Sahyadri (Maharashtra–Karnataka), Nilgiri (Tamil Nadu), Anaimalai, Cardamom hills (Kerala) | Javadi, Shevaroy, Nallamala, Palkonda, Mahendragiri hills |
| Rainfall | Heavy orographic rain on the windward (west) side | Moderate |
| Status | **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2012)**; one of the world's biodiversity hotspots | — |

The two Ghats meet at the **Nilgiri hills** (Ooty — Udhagamandalam).

> **Prelims angle:** "Highest peak of south India" = **Anamudi**; "highest peak of the Aravalli" = **Guru Shikhar**; "highest peak of the Satpura" = **Dhupgarh**; "highest peak of Chota Nagpur / Jharkhand" = **Parasnath**; "highest peak of the Nilgiri" = **Doddabetta**.

## 4. The Indian (Thar) Desert

- Lies **west of the Aravalli**, mostly in **western Rajasthan**, extending into Pakistan; also called **Marusthali**.
- Very low rainfall (under about 15 cm in the far west and under 50 cm overall), high temperature, sparse vegetation.
- Features: **barchans** (crescent dunes), longitudinal dunes, **playas** (temporary salt lakes) — **Sambhar** (largest inland saline lake of India), Didwana, Pachpadra; mushroom rocks.
- The **Luni** is the only notable river; it ends in the **Rann of Kutch** (a salt marsh) and is saline in its lower course.
- The Thar was once under the sea (marine fossils near Jaisalmer, the Akal Wood Fossil Park).
- The **Indira Gandhi Canal** has turned parts into farmland.

## 5. The Coastal Plains

| Feature | Western coastal plain | Eastern coastal plain |
|---|---|---|
| Width | **Narrow** (Western Ghats close to the sea) | **Wider** |
| Rivers | Short, fast, form **estuaries** (no deltas) | Long rivers form big **deltas** (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) |
| Ports | Many **natural harbours** due to a submerged coast — Mumbai, Mormugao, Kochi | Fewer natural harbours; ports often artificial (Chennai) except Visakhapatnam |
| Rain | Heavy rain from the south-west monsoon | Rain also from the retreating / north-east monsoon (Tamil Nadu) |
| Special features | **Kayals** (backwaters, lagoons) of Kerala — **Vembanad**, Ashtamudi | Lagoons — **Chilika** (Odisha), **Pulicat** (AP–TN), Kolleru (fresh water, AP) |

| Western coast sections (north to south) | Eastern coast sections (north to south) |
|---|---|
| **Kachchh and Kathiawar** (Gujarat) | **Utkal** plain (Odisha) — Chilika lake |
| **Konkan** (Maharashtra, Goa) — Daman to Goa | **Northern Circars** (Andhra Pradesh) — between the Mahanadi and Krishna |
| **Kanara / Karnataka coast** | **Coromandel** coast (Tamil Nadu) — Krishna to Kanyakumari |
| **Malabar** (Kerala) — backwaters | — |

- **Chilika** — the **largest brackish-water lagoon** in India and the largest wintering ground for migratory birds in India; Ramsar site.
- **Pulicat** — second largest brackish lagoon; **Sriharikota** (ISRO's launch centre) lies on the barrier island that separates it from the sea.

## 6. The Islands

| Feature | Andaman & Nicobar Islands | Lakshadweep |
|---|---|---|
| Sea | **Bay of Bengal** (Andaman Sea) | **Arabian Sea** |
| Origin | Peaks of a **submerged mountain range** (extension of the Arakan Yoma of Myanmar); some volcanic | **Coral** islands (atolls) on the Laccadive–Chagos ridge |
| Number | Several hundred islands and islets | **36 islands** (about 10 inhabited) |
| Capital | **Sri Vijaya Puram** (renamed from Port Blair in 2024) | **Kavaratti** |
| Highest point | **Saddle Peak (~732 m)**, North Andaman | Very low, flat |
| Special | **Barren Island** — India's only active volcano; **Narcondam** — dormant; **Indira Point** (Great Nicobar) — India's southernmost point; **Ten Degree Channel** between Andaman and Nicobar groups; tribes — Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, **Sentinelese**, Shompen, Nicobarese | Largest island **Andrott**; southernmost **Minicoy**; **Pitti** island bird sanctuary; Nine Degree Channel and Eight Degree Channel |

In 2018 three Andaman islands were renamed: **Ross Island → Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Dweep**, **Neil Island → Shaheed Dweep**, **Havelock Island → Swaraj Dweep**.

> **Prelims angle:** The **Great Nicobar Island Project** (international transhipment port at Galathea Bay, airport, township, power plant) is a current topic because of its strategic value near the Malacca Strait and its ecological and tribal concerns (Shompen, leatherback turtles, coral) — as of 2026, check the latest.

## Quick revision

- Three geological units: old Peninsula (Gondwana), young Himalaya, youngest plains.
- Dharwar = metallic minerals; Gondwana = 98% coal; Vindhyan = building stone, Panna diamonds; Tertiary = petroleum; Deccan Traps = black soil.
- Himalaya: ~2,400 km, Indus to Brahmaputra gorge; wider in the west.
- Himadri (avg 6,000 m) → Himachal (Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar) → Shiwalik (youngest; duns).
- Punjab (Indus–Sutlej), Kumaon (Sutlej–Kali), Nepal (Kali–Teesta, highest), Assam (Teesta–Dihang).
- K2 8,611 m; Kanchenjunga 8,586 m; Nanda Devi 7,816 m (highest wholly in India).
- Zoji La: Srinagar–Leh; Banihal: Jammu–Srinagar; Rohtang: Manali–Lahaul (Atal Tunnel); Shipki La: Sutlej enters; Nathu La: Sikkim–Tibet trade; Lipulekh: Kailash route.
- Bhabar (pebbles, streams vanish) → Terai (marshy) → Bhangar (old, kankar) → Khadar (new, fertile).
- Bist Doab: Beas–Sutlej; Bari: Beas–Ravi; Rachna: Ravi–Chenab; Chaj: Chenab–Jhelum.
- Bihar = Middle Ganga plain; Majuli = largest river island.
- Aravalli: oldest fold mountains, Guru Shikhar 1,722 m; Parasnath 1,365 m (Jharkhand); Dhupgarh (Satpura).
- Anamudi 2,695 m — highest peak of the peninsula; Doddabetta 2,637 m (Nilgiri).
- Garo–Rajmahal gap separates Meghalaya plateau from Chota Nagpur.
- Western Ghats: continuous, UNESCO 2012; Eastern Ghats: broken by rivers.
- Sambhar = largest inland salt lake; Luni ends in Rann of Kutch.
- Chilika = largest brackish lagoon; Pulicat — Sriharikota; Vembanad — Kerala's longest lake.

### Mains questions

**Q1. Describe the major physiographic divisions of India and explain their significance for the country's economy. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India's land has three geological units and six physiographic divisions; each shapes resources and livelihoods.
- **Body:** (1) **Himalaya** — barrier to cold winds, monsoon rain, perennial rivers, hydropower, forests, tourism, defence; (2) **Northern Plains** — fertile alluvium, food bowl (wheat, rice, sugarcane), dense population, inland waterways (NW-1), but floods; (3) **Peninsular Plateau** — minerals (Chota Nagpur, Dharwar), black soil for cotton, dam sites, forests; (4) **Thar** — solar and wind energy, Indira Gandhi Canal agriculture, tourism; (5) **Coastal plains** — ports, fisheries, salt, coconuts, tourism; (6) **Islands** — strategic, EEZ, coral ecosystems, tourism. A small schematic diagram of the divisions helps.
- **Bihar angle:** Bihar is almost wholly the Middle Ganga plain, with Terai in the north-west and plateau edge in the south — rich farmland but flood-prone and mineral-poor after 2000.
- **Conclusion:** the divisions complement each other — the mountains feed the plains, the plateau gives minerals, the coasts give trade; planning must respect each region's limits.

**Q2. "The Himalaya are a boon for India." Discuss, also pointing out the hazards associated with them.**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** young fold mountains from plate collision, ~2,400 km arc.
- **Boons:** climatic shield; monsoon trap; glacier-fed perennial rivers; silt that made the plains (including Bihar's); hydropower; forests, medicinal plants, biodiversity; tourism and pilgrimage; natural frontier.
- **Hazards:** earthquakes (1934 Bihar–Nepal, 2015 Nepal); landslides and cloudbursts; glacial lake outburst floods; heavy sediment that raises riverbeds and causes floods in Bihar (Kosi); deforestation and unplanned construction (Joshimath land subsidence).
- **Bihar angle:** most of Bihar's floods start in the Nepal Himalaya; cooperation with Nepal on dams and early warning is needed.
- **Conclusion:** sustainable mountain development — careful road and dam building, forest protection, early warning — keeps the boon from turning into a curse.
