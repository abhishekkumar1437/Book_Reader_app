Population geography is where many BPSC questions hide Bihar facts: "Which state has the highest density?", "Bihar's sex ratio in Census 2011?", "least urbanised state?". This chapter gives the national picture from **Census 2011** (the latest completed census) with Bihar beside it, explains how settlements and towns are classified, and covers urban schemes for Mains. **Every Census number below is marked Census 2011.** The next census — **Census 2027** — is under way; its first results may appear before or after your exam, so watch the news. For a one-page Bihar data sheet see [Bihar Facts and Figures](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-facts-and-figures).

> **In simple words:** India counts its people once every ten years in the Census. The last full count was in 2011, when India had about 121 crore people. Population grew fast after 1951 because deaths fell faster than births. People are crowded in the fertile plains (Bihar is the most crowded state) and thin in deserts and mountains. About a third of Indians lived in towns in 2011, and that share is rising. Bihar is one of the least urbanised and youngest states.

## The Census in India

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| First census | **1872** (under **Lord Mayo**) — not held in all areas at the same time |
| First **synchronous** census | **1881** (under **Lord Ripon**; Census Commissioner W.C. Plowden) — held every ten years since |
| Law | **Census Act, 1948** |
| Conducted by | **Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, India**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** |
| Constitutional entry | **Census is in the Union List** (entry 69) |
| Census 2011 | **15th** census overall; **7th** since independence; theme "**Our Census, Our Future**" |
| Census 2021 | **Postponed** due to COVID-19 |

### Census 2027 (as of 2026 — check the latest)

- The government notified the next census in **June 2025**.
- **Reference date: 00:00 hours on 1 March 2027**; for snow-bound areas (**Ladakh, Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand**) the reference date is **1 October 2026**.
- **Two phases:** **House-listing and housing census** (in 2026, with each state choosing a 30-day window) and **population enumeration** (February 2027 in most areas).
- It will be India's **first digital census** (data collected on mobile apps, with an option of **self-enumeration**), and it will include **caste enumeration** — the first full caste count since 1931.
- Its results will matter for **delimitation** of constituencies, **women's reservation** in legislatures (the 106th Amendment links it to the census after the Act and delimitation), and the next Finance Commission data.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar conducted its own **caste-based survey in 2023** (results released in October 2023). It is a **state survey**, not a census. Its findings and the reservation changes that followed are covered in [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar).

| Phase | Period | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Stagnant** | 1901–1921 | High birth and death rates (famines, plague, the 1918 influenza); **1911–21 was the only decade with negative growth**; hence **1921 is called the "Great Divide"** |
| **Steady growth** | 1921–1951 | Death rate fell slowly (better sanitation, control of famine) |
| **Population explosion** | 1951–1981 | Death rate fell sharply (vaccines, antibiotics, food security) while birth rate stayed high; decadal growth above 24% (peak about 24.8% in 1961–71) |
| **High but slowing** | 1981–2011 | Birth rate began to fall; **2001–11 growth (17.7%)** was the lowest since independence and the first decade in which the absolute addition was smaller than in the previous decade |

### Demographic transition

The **demographic transition model** describes how countries move from **high birth + high death rates** (stage 1) → **high birth + falling death** (stage 2, population explosion) → **falling birth + low death** (stage 3) → **low birth + low death** (stage 4, stable). India is in the **late third stage**: national **Total Fertility Rate (TFR)** has fallen to about **2.0** (NFHS-5, 2019–21), **below the replacement level of 2.1**.

| TFR (NFHS-5, 2019–21) | Value |
|---|---|
| India | **2.0** |
| **Bihar** | **3.0** — **highest** among states |
| Uttar Pradesh | 2.4 |
| Lowest | Sikkim (about 1.1) |

(NFHS-6 results may be out by exam time — check the latest.)

## Distribution and density

**Population density** = number of people ÷ area (persons per km²).

| Highest density | Lowest density |
|---|---|
| **Bihar 1,106** (highest among states), West Bengal 1,028, Kerala 860, Uttar Pradesh 829 (Census 2011) | **Arunachal Pradesh 17** (lowest), Mizoram, Sikkim |
| UTs: **NCT of Delhi 11,320** (highest overall), Chandigarh | UT: Andaman & Nicobar Islands |

**Factors affecting distribution:**
- **Physical:** relief (flat plains attract people — Ganga plain; mountains repel — Himalaya), climate (extreme heat or cold repels — Thar, Ladakh), soil fertility, water availability.
- **Economic:** industry and mining (Chota Nagpur), urban jobs, ports, transport.
- **Social and historical:** old settled regions, religious and cultural centres, migration history.

**Most populous states (Census 2011):** **Uttar Pradesh** (19.98 crore, about 16.5%), Maharashtra (11.24 crore), **Bihar** (10.41 crore), West Bengal, undivided Andhra Pradesh. **Least populous state: Sikkim**; least populous UT: Lakshadweep.

## Sex ratio

**Sex ratio** in India = number of **females per 1,000 males**.

| Census 2011 | Highest | Lowest |
|---|---|---|
| States | **Kerala (1,084)**, Tamil Nadu | **Haryana (879)**, Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab |
| UTs | Puducherry (1,037) | **Daman & Diu (618)**, Dadra & Nagar Haveli |
| Child sex ratio (0–6) | North-eastern states (Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Meghalaya — around 970) | **Haryana (834)**, Punjab |

- Causes of low sex ratio: son preference, sex-selective abortion, neglect of girl children, maternal mortality, and male migration effects in some areas.
- Responses: **PCPNDT Act 1994** (bans sex selection), **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015)**, Sukanya Samriddhi.
- **NFHS-5 (2019–21)** reported an overall sex ratio of **1,020** females per 1,000 males — the first time above 1,000 in a national survey — but this is a household survey, not a census.

## Literacy

A person aged **7 years and above** who can **read and write with understanding** in any language is counted as **literate**.

| Census 2011 | Value |
|---|---|
| India | **74.04%** (provisional): males 82.14%, females 65.46% (final tables: 72.98%) |
| Highest | **Kerala (about 94%)**, Lakshadweep, Mizoram |
| Lowest state | **Bihar (61.8%)**; Arunachal Pradesh, Rajasthan also low |
| Gender gap | Highest in Rajasthan |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's female literacy (Census 2011) was only about **51.5%**. State schemes like the **Mukhyamantri Balika Cycle Yojana (2006–07)** and **Poshak Yojana** are often cited as reasons for later improvement in girls' enrolment. Use this in Mains answers on women's empowerment and human development in Bihar — details in [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar).

### Language (Census 2011)

- **Hindi** is the mother tongue (including related speeches grouped under it) of about **43.6%** of people; then **Bengali** (about 8%), **Marathi**, **Telugu**, **Tamil**.
- **22 languages** are in the **Eighth Schedule**; **Maithili** was added in **2003** (92nd Amendment) — see [Art, Culture, Festivals and Languages](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/art-culture-festivals-and-languages).
- In Census 2011, **Bhojpuri, Magahi** and other Bihari languages were grouped under Hindi.

### Age structure and the demographic dividend

- Census 2011: about **31%** of people were under 15 and about **8.6%** were 60 or older.
- **Demographic dividend** — the growth opportunity when the **working-age population (15–64)** is large compared with dependants. India's window is expected to last until roughly the **2040s**; **Bihar**, being younger, will enjoy it **later and longer**.
- To use it, India needs **jobs, skills, health and education**; otherwise it becomes a "demographic burden".

### Workforce (Census 2011)

- **Work participation rate** about **39.8%** (males about 53%, females about 25%).
- Categories: **main workers** (worked 6 months or more), **marginal workers** (less than 6 months), **non-workers**.

## Migration

| Census 2011 fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Migrants (by place of last residence) | About **45 crore** — roughly **37%** of the population |
| Most common stream | **Rural → rural** (mostly women moving after marriage) |
| Main reason for women | **Marriage** |
| Main reason for men | **Work / employment** |
| Largest out-migration states | **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar** |
| Largest in-migration | **Maharashtra**, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana |

**Push factors** (in source areas): poverty, small holdings, floods, unemployment, low wages. **Pull factors** (in destination areas): jobs, higher wages, education, health facilities, city life.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar is one of India's biggest sources of **seasonal and long-term labour migration** — to Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, Maharashtra, Gujarat, West Bengal and the Gulf. Remittances support many rural households, but migration also shows lack of local jobs. The 2020 COVID lockdown return of lakhs of migrants highlighted the issue. Mains answers on Bihar's economy should link migration to floods, land fragmentation and low industrialisation.

## Rural settlements

A **settlement** is a place where people live together. **Rural settlements** depend mainly on primary activities (farming, fishing, forestry).

| Type | Description | Where in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Clustered (compact / nucleated)** | Houses built close together, fields around | **Northern plains** (including most of **Bihar**), Gujarat plains; also for defence (Bundelkhand) and water scarcity (Rajasthan) |
| **Semi-clustered** | A cluster plus scattered houses of other groups at its edge | Gujarat plains, parts of Rajasthan |
| **Hamleted** | Village broken into several units (*panna, para, palli, nagla, dhani*) by social or physical factors | **Middle and lower Ganga plain**, Chhattisgarh, lower Himalayan valleys |
| **Dispersed (isolated)** | Isolated huts or hamlets far apart | **Meghalaya**, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Kerala, tribal areas of central India |

Shapes (patterns) of villages: **linear** (along a road, river or canal), **rectangular**, **circular**, **star-like**, **T-shaped**, **double village** (on both banks of a river).

## Urban settlements

### What is a town? (Census definition)

1. **Statutory town** — any place with a municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee.
2. **Census town** — any other place that meets **all three** conditions: population **at least 5,000**; **at least 75% of male main workers** in non-agricultural work; and density **at least 400 persons per km²**.

| Census 2011 fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Towns | About **7,935** (4,041 statutory + 3,894 census towns) |
| Urban population | About **37.7 crore** (31.1%) |
| **Million-plus urban agglomerations / cities** | **53** |
| Largest urban agglomerations | **Greater Mumbai** (~1.84 crore), **Delhi** (~1.63 crore), **Kolkata** (~1.41 crore) |
| Most urbanised state | **Goa (about 62%)**; among UTs, **NCT of Delhi (about 97.5%)** |
| Least urbanised states | **Himachal Pradesh (about 10%)**, **Bihar (about 11.3%)**, Assam, Odisha |
| Bihar's million-plus city | **Patna** UA (about 20 lakh) |

**Size classes of towns:** Class I — 1 lakh and above (cities); Class II — 50,000–99,999; Class III — 20,000–49,999; Class IV — 10,000–19,999; Class V — 5,000–9,999; Class VI — below 5,000. **Million city** = 10 lakh+; **megacity** = 1 crore (10 million) +.

### Problems of urbanisation and missions

Problems: slums (Census 2011 counted about 6.5 crore slum dwellers), housing shortage, traffic, air and water pollution, waste, water scarcity, flooding of low areas (**Patna's 2019 waterlogging**), heat islands, informal jobs.

| Mission / scheme | Year | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| Swachh Bharat Mission | 2014 | Sanitation, open-defecation free, waste management |
| **Smart Cities Mission** | **2015** | **100 cities**; **Bihar's four: Patna, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Biharsharif** |
| AMRUT (AMRUT 2.0 from 2021) | 2015 | Water supply, sewerage, green spaces in 500 cities |
| PMAY-Urban | 2015 | Housing for all |

### Mains questions

**Q1. Analyse the main features of Bihar's population as revealed by Census 2011. What challenges and opportunities do they present? (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar — 10.41 crore people (Census 2011), 3rd most populous state, on about 2.9% of India's area.
- **Features:** highest density (1,106/km²); high decadal growth (25.4%); sex ratio 918; lowest literacy (61.8%) with a wide gender gap; very low urbanisation (11.3%); young age structure; high TFR (3.0 in NFHS-5); large out-migration; SC 15.9%, ST 1.3%.
- **Challenges:** pressure on land (tiny holdings), floods affecting dense populations, jobs, health and education services, urban infrastructure, malnutrition.
- **Opportunities:** demographic dividend lasting longer than in other states; large labour pool; consumer market; women's self-help movement (Jeevika) and girls' education gains.
- **Policy:** female education and health to lower fertility; skilling (Kushal Yuva); urban growth centres; agro-industry; better data from Census 2027.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's population can be a burden or an asset; human capital investment decides which.

**Q2. Discuss the trends and problems of urbanisation in India. Why is Bihar one of the least urbanised states?**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** urban share rose from about 17% (1951) to 31.1% (Census 2011); 53 million-plus cities.
- **Trends:** growth of census towns; metropolitan concentration; peri-urban sprawl; urban corridors; rising share in the south and west.
- **Problems:** slums, housing, transport, pollution, waste, water, urban floods, informal work, weak municipal finances.
- **Bihar's low urbanisation (11.3%):** agrarian economy; few industries after 2000; flood-prone north discouraging large towns; historical decline of old towns; small market towns not notified as urban; out-migration to cities of other states.
- **Measures:** Smart Cities (Patna, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Biharsharif), AMRUT, PMAY-U, HRIDAY (Gaya); new urban local bodies (Bihar notified many new nagar panchayats and councils — check the latest); metro (Patna); planned growth around transport corridors.
- **Conclusion:** urbanisation is an engine of growth; Bihar needs planned, flood-safe towns linked to agro-industry to create jobs near home.
