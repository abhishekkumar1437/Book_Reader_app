This chapter is for testing yourself, not for first reading. Use it on **Days 103–104** of the plan, again on each revision day of Phase 4 (Days 246–300), and once more in the last week before Prelims. Cover the answer column, say the answer aloud, then check. Mark every miss in your mistake log and re-read that chapter's **Quick revision** box. Themes marked ⭐ come up again and again in state PSC papers (stated qualitatively, not as counted statistics). There are no multiple-choice questions here — MCQ practice is in the separate Quiz tab.

> **In simple words:** Geography marks come from three things — remembering places on the map, understanding a few processes (monsoon, plate movement, river behaviour), and knowing India's and Bihar's facts and figures. This chapter checks all three. If you can answer about 110 of these 133 questions without looking, your geography is Prelims-ready.

## Mains questions with outlines

Write in about 300 words (38-mark style) or 150 words (shorter questions). Always add one **Bihar paragraph** and, where possible, a **small diagram** or table. More model answers are in [Economy and Geography Model Answers](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/economy-and-geography-model-answers).

**M1. ⭐ Describe the physiographic divisions of India and their economic significance.**
- Intro: three geological units, six divisions.
- Body: Himalaya (rivers, hydropower, barrier); Northern Plains (food bowl, population); Peninsula (minerals, black soil); Thar (solar, wind); coasts (ports, fisheries); islands (strategy, EEZ).
- Bihar: Middle Ganga plain + Terai + plateau fringe.
- Conclusion: complementary regions; region-specific planning.

**M2. ⭐ Explain the mechanism of the Indian monsoon and the causes of its variability.**
- Differential heating; ITCZ shift; Tibetan heating and TEJ; westerly jet shift; Mascarene High; Somali jet.
- Variability: ENSO, IOD, MJO, breaks.
- Effects: kharif, inflation, floods/droughts.
- Bihar: Bay of Bengal branch; north floods, south droughts.

**M3. ⭐ "Floods in north Bihar are a recurring disaster." Analyse the causes and suggest a long-term strategy.**
- Causes: Himalayan rivers from Nepal, heavy catchment rain, flat gradient, silt, embankment breaches (Kusaha 2008), drainage congestion, Ganga backwater.
- Strategy: Indo-Nepal cooperation (Kosi high dam, forecasting), embankment maintenance, flood-plain zoning, drainage improvement, raised habitations, crop calendars, insurance, Kosi–Mechi link.
- Conclusion: from "flood control" to "flood management".

**M4. ⭐ Discuss the causes of drought in south Bihar and measures for drought-proofing.**
- Causes: rain-shadow and lower rainfall in the south-west, seasonal plateau rivers, hard-rock aquifers, decline of ahar–pynes, deforestation.
- Measures: ahar–pyne revival, check dams, ponds (Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali), drip irrigation, drought-tolerant crops, watershed development, contingency plans.

**M5. Examine the arguments for and against the interlinking of rivers in India.**
- For: balance surplus/deficit basins, irrigation, power, navigation.
- Against: cost, displacement, ecology, disputes, climate uncertainty.
- Bihar: Kosi–Mechi link, Western Kosi canal.
- Conclusion: link-by-link, with local water harvesting.

**M6. ⭐ Classify Indian soils and discuss the problem of soil degradation.**
- Eight ICAR types with crops; degradation types; Bihar's riverbank erosion, sand-casting after floods, waterlogging.
- Measures: Soil Health Cards, organic farming, bunds, drainage, agroforestry.

**M7. Bihar has one of the lowest forest covers in India. Discuss the causes, consequences and remedies.**
- Causes: dense farming population, loss of forests to Jharkhand in 2000, fuelwood demand.
- Consequences: soil erosion in the south, heat, biodiversity loss, low carbon sink.
- Remedies: Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali plantation, agroforestry, roadside and canal-bank planting, protecting Valmiki and the Kaimur forests, community forestry.

**M8. ⭐ Why did the Green Revolution bypass eastern India? Suggest a strategy for a second Green Revolution in Bihar.**
- Causes: irrigation, power, floods, small holdings, institutions, markets.
- Strategy: BGREI, Agriculture Road Maps, rabi maize, zero tillage, seed and mechanisation, value chains (makhana, litchi), credit and storage.

**M9. Discuss the problem of groundwater depletion and contamination in India with reference to arsenic in Bihar.**
- Overuse causes; CGWB categories; arsenic in Ganga-belt districts; health effects.
- Measures: Atal Bhujal, micro-irrigation, recharge, crop diversification, safe surface-water supply (Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal).

**M10. Explain the factors of location of industries in India with suitable examples.**
- Raw material, power, labour, market, transport, water, capital, policy, agglomeration.
- Examples: steel (Chota Nagpur), aluminium (Renukoot, Korba), refineries (Mathura, Panipat), software (Bengaluru), textiles (Mumbai, Ahmedabad).
- Bihar: agro-based units near produce; rail factories; Barauni.

**M11. ⭐ Bihar lost its mineral base in 2000. Suggest an industrial strategy based on its geographical advantages.**
- Strengths: fertile plains, water, labour, location, NW-1, Eastern DFC, AKIC (Gaya), GQ and East–West corridors, gas pipeline.
- Strategy: agro-processing clusters, logistics parks, textile and leather, ethanol, electronics assembly, tourism, skilling.

**M12. Evaluate India's progress towards renewable energy and the challenges ahead.**
- Targets (500 GW non-fossil by 2030, net zero 2070); non-fossil share crossing half of capacity (2025); solar parks, wind; green hydrogen; ISA.
- Challenges: storage, grid, land, financing, coal dependence, imports of panels.
- Bihar: limited land and hydro; rooftop, canal-top, floating and solar-plus-battery projects.

**M13. ⭐ Assess the role of transport infrastructure in Bihar's development.**
- Roads (GQ via GT Road, East–West corridor, expressways), railways (ECR, Eastern DFC ending at Sonnagar, rail factories), NW-1, airports (Patna, Gaya, Darbhanga, Purnea), bridges over the Ganga.
- Impacts: markets for farmers, tourism, industry; challenges of floods and maintenance.

**M14. ⭐ Analyse the demographic features of Bihar (Census 2011) and their implications for development.**
- Density, growth, sex ratio, literacy, urbanisation, TFR, migration.
- Implications and policy; demographic dividend.

**M15. Discuss the causes and consequences of out-migration from Bihar.**
- Push and pull factors; remittances; skill gain; family stress, left-behind women and elderly; COVID return 2020.
- Responses: local jobs, skilling, MGNREGA, Jeevika, migrant welfare and data.

**M16. Discuss the main challenges of urbanisation in India. How can Bihar's towns be made flood-safe and liveable?**
- Slums, transport, pollution, waste, water, urban floods (Patna 2019).
- Missions: Smart Cities (four in Bihar), AMRUT, PMAY-U, HRIDAY (Gaya).
- Bihar: drainage master plans, pumping stations, planned growth on higher ground, solid-waste management, metro and buses.
