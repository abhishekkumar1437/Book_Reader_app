Industry turns raw materials into goods and creates jobs off the farm. BPSC Prelims asks about the industrial policies (1948, 1956, 1991), PSU categories, MSME definitions, the eight core industries and infrastructure schemes. Mains GS II repeatedly asks why Bihar did not industrialise and what policies can change that.

> **In simple words:** After independence the government built big industries itself (steel, heavy machines) and controlled private firms with licences. In 1991 it opened up: most licences were removed, foreign investment was allowed, and the private sector became the main driver. Today the focus is on manufacturing (Make in India), small firms (MSMEs) and roads, railways and power. Bihar lost most of its industries and minerals to Jharkhand in 2000 and is now trying to grow through food processing, ethanol, textiles and better infrastructure.

## Industrial policy before 1991

### Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR), 1948

- India's first industrial policy, presented by **Syama Prasad Mookerjee** (Industries Minister).
- Declared a **mixed economy**.
- Four categories of industries: (1) **state monopoly** — arms, atomic energy, railways; (2) new units by the state only — coal, iron and steel, aircraft, shipbuilding, telephone; (3) regulated private sector; (4) open to private sector.

### Industrial Policy Resolution, 1956 — the "Economic Constitution of India"

- Adopted the **socialistic pattern of society** (Avadi session of Congress, **1955**) and the **Mahalanobis** heavy-industry strategy.
- Three schedules:

| Schedule | Industries | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule A** | **17** industries (arms, atomic energy, iron & steel, heavy machinery, coal, railways, air transport, etc.) | **Exclusive state** responsibility |
| **Schedule B** | **12** industries (aluminium, machine tools, chemicals, fertilisers, road and sea transport) | State would increasingly set up units; private sector could supplement |
| **Schedule C** | All the rest | Private sector |

### Licence raj

- **Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951** — licence needed to set up or expand a factory.
- **MRTP Act, 1969** (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices) — controlled big business houses; replaced by the **Competition Act, 2002** (**Competition Commission of India**, 2003).
- **FERA, 1973** — strict foreign exchange control; replaced by **FEMA, 1999**.
- **Small-scale reservation** — many items reserved for small units (fully de-reserved by **2015**).
- **Industrial Policy 1977** (Janata govt, **George Fernandes**) — focus on small and cottage industries, **District Industries Centres (DICs, 1978)**.
- **Industrial Policy 1980** — revived role of large industry, productivity.

> **Prelims angle:** IPR **1956** had **17 + 12** industries in Schedules A and B. It is called the "**Economic Constitution**" of India. IPR 1948 was presented by **Syama Prasad Mookerjee**.

## New Industrial Policy, 1991

Announced on **24 July 1991** (same day as the budget, PM **P.V. Narasimha Rao**, FM **Manmohan Singh**).

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| **Licensing** | Abolished for all except a few industries; today compulsory licensing remains only for about **5**: alcoholic drinks, tobacco products (cigars and cigarettes), electronic aerospace and defence equipment, industrial explosives, specified hazardous chemicals |
| **Public sector reservation** | Cut from 17 to 8 (1991), now only **2**: **atomic energy** and **railway operations** |
| **MRTP** | Asset limit removed; later replaced by Competition Act 2002 |
| **Foreign investment** | Automatic approval up to **51%** in priority industries (now 100% in most sectors) |
| **Foreign technology** | Automatic approval for technology agreements |
| **Public sector** | Disinvestment, more autonomy, sick units referred to BIFR |
| **Location** | Fewer restrictions, except near big cities |

Details of the wider reforms (LPG — liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation) are in [Economic Reforms and Current Issues](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/economic-reforms-and-current-issues).

## Public sector enterprises and disinvestment

**Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs)** are companies in which the Central Government holds **51% or more**.

| Status | Introduced | Key criteria (simplified) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maharatna** | **2009** | Navratna status, listed, average annual turnover over ₹25,000 crore, net worth over ₹15,000 crore, net profit over ₹5,000 crore; can invest up to ₹5,000 crore in a project without government approval | ONGC, NTPC, SAIL, IOC, BHEL, Coal India, GAIL, BPCL, PGCIL, HPCL, Oil India, REC, PFC (list grows — check the latest) |
| **Navratna** | **1997** | Miniratna-I, good performance score; can invest up to ₹1,000 crore | HAL, BEL, Container Corporation, NMDC, etc. |
| **Miniratna** | 1997 | Category I and II, profit for 3 years, positive net worth | Many |

- **Disinvestment** = selling part or all of the government's shares in a PSU.
- Handled by **DIPAM (Department of Investment and Public Asset Management)**, Ministry of Finance (renamed in **2016** from the Department of Disinvestment, set up **1999**).
- **Strategic disinvestment** = sale of a large stake with transfer of management control. Example: **Air India** sold to the **Tata group** (completed **January 2022**).
- **New Public Sector Enterprise Policy (2021)** — in **4 strategic sectors** (atomic energy, space & defence; transport & telecom; power, petroleum, coal & minerals; banking, insurance & financial services) a **bare minimum** of CPSEs remain; in non-strategic sectors CPSEs to be privatised or closed.
- **LIC IPO** (May 2022) — the largest IPO in India at the time.

> **Bihar connection:** Major central PSU units in present-day Bihar: **IOCL Barauni Refinery** (Begusarai, Soviet help, 1964); **NTPC** power plants at **Barh, Kahalgaon, Nabinagar** and others; **HURL Barauni fertiliser plant** (revived and inaugurated **2022–23**); **Rail Wheel Plant, Bela** (Saran) and **Electric Locomotive Factory, Madhepura** (with Alstom; 12,000 HP engines); **Diesel Locomotive Factory, Marhowra** (Saran, with Wabtec). These are frequent Prelims facts.

## Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)

MSMEs are the backbone of non-farm employment — they employ **over 20 crore people** (Udyam data) and produce about **30%** of GDP and **45%** of exports (approximate; check the latest).

**MSMED Act, 2006**; definitions revised in **2020** (composite criteria of investment and turnover, no distinction between manufacturing and services) and **raised again from 1 April 2025**:

| Category | Investment in plant & machinery (up to) | Turnover (up to) |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro** | **₹2.5 crore** | **₹10 crore** |
| **Small** | **₹25 crore** | **₹100 crore** |
| **Medium** | **₹125 crore** | **₹500 crore** |

(As of 2025-26 — check the latest notification.)

Key MSME schemes: **Udyam registration** (2020), **CGTMSE** credit guarantee, **PM Vishwakarma** (2023, for 18 traditional trades), **PMEGP** (2008, KVIC), **MUDRA**, **TReDS** (invoice discounting), **RAMP**, **ZED certification**, **PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PMFME, 2020)** — "**One District One Product**" approach.

> **Bihar connection:** Under PMFME's **One District One Product (ODOP)**, Bihar's districts have products such as **makhana** (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia), **litchi** (Muzaffarpur), **mango** (Bhagalpur, Vaishali), **Magahi paan** (Nawada/Gaya), **honey**, **jaggery** (check the latest list). Bihar also has the largest number of **micro food-processing loans** under PMFME in some years. MSMEs are the realistic route to factory jobs in Bihar.

## Make in India, PLI and other missions

| Initiative | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Make in India** | **25 September 2014** | Raise manufacturing; symbol: a **lion** made of cogs; 25 sectors originally |
| **Startup India** | **16 January 2016** | Tax holiday, Fund of Funds (SIDBI), self-certification; **DPIIT** recognises startups; India is the **3rd largest** startup ecosystem |
| **Stand-Up India** | 2016 | Loans for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs |
| **Production Linked Incentive (PLI)** | **2020** | Cash incentive on **incremental sales** of goods made in India; **14 sectors** (mobile phones, electronics, pharma, auto, textiles, food processing, solar modules, ACs/LEDs, specialty steel, drones, etc.) |
| **India Semiconductor Mission** | 2021 | Chip fabs and assembly (Gujarat, Assam etc.) |
| **National Manufacturing Policy** | 2011 | Manufacturing to **25% of GDP**; National Investment and Manufacturing Zones (NIMZ) |
| **Industrial corridors** | — | Delhi–Mumbai (DMIC), **Amritsar–Kolkata (AKIC)** — passes through Bihar (Gaya region), Chennai–Bengaluru, etc. |
| **Ease of Doing Business** | — | World Bank stopped the report in **2021**; India had reached **63rd** rank (2020 report) |
| **National Logistics Policy** | **September 2022** | Bring logistics cost down; ULIP platform |
| **Jan Vishwas Act** | 2023 | Decriminalised minor offences in 42 laws (a second Jan Vishwas Bill followed — check latest) |

Manufacturing's share in India's GVA has stayed around **14–17%** for decades — the "missing middle" of India's structural change. China's share is about 27%.

## Measuring industrial growth: IIP and core industries

- **Index of Industrial Production (IIP)** — published monthly by the **NSO (MoSPI)**; base **2011-12** (new base **2022-23** being introduced in 2026 — check the latest). Sectors: **mining, manufacturing (largest weight ~78%), electricity**. Also classified by use: primary goods, capital goods, intermediate, infrastructure/construction, consumer durables, consumer non-durables.
- **Index of Eight Core Industries (ICI)** — published monthly by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT**. The eight make up about **40%** of IIP weight.

| Core industry | Weight (%) (base 2011-12) |
|---|---|
| **Refinery products** | **28.04** (largest) |
| Electricity | 19.85 |
| Steel | 17.92 |
| Coal | 10.33 |
| Crude oil | 8.98 |
| Natural gas | 6.88 |
| Cement | 5.37 |
| **Fertilisers** | **2.63** (smallest) |

> **Memory trick:** "**R**eal **E**lectric **S**teel **C**oal **C**rude **N**atural **C**ement **F**ertiliser" — **RESC-CNCF**, from largest to smallest weight. Learn "**Refinery largest, Fertiliser smallest**".

## Infrastructure

**Infrastructure** = basic facilities that support production: roads, railways, ports, airports, power, telecom, water, urban services. It has a **multiplier effect** on growth and jobs.

### Key programmes

| Programme | Year | Sector |
|---|---|---|
| **Golden Quadrilateral** | 1999–2001 (NHDP) | Highways linking Delhi–Mumbai–Chennai–Kolkata |
| **PM Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY)** | **25 December 2000** | All-weather roads to unconnected villages |
| **Bharatmala Pariyojana** | **2017** | Highways, economic corridors, border roads |
| **Sagarmala** | **2015** | Port-led development |
| **UDAN** (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) | **2016** | Regional air connectivity (first flight 2017, Shimla–Delhi) |
| **Dedicated Freight Corridors** | 2006 onwards | **Eastern DFC (Ludhiana–Sonnagar in Bihar)** and Western DFC (Dadri–JNPT) |
| **National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP)** | **2019** | ₹111 lakh crore projects for 2020–25 |
| **National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP)** | **2021** | ₹6 lakh crore by leasing existing assets |
| **PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan** | **13 October 2021** | GIS-based integrated planning across 16+ ministries; seven engines: roads, railways, airports, ports, mass transport, waterways, logistics |
| **National Waterways Act** | **2016** | **111** national waterways; **NW-1 = Ganga (Haldia–Prayagraj, 1,620 km)** via Patna, Bhagalpur |
| **Saubhagya** | 2017 | Household electrification |
| **Smart Cities Mission** | 2015 | 100 cities, incl. **Patna, Bhagalpur, Muzaffarpur, Biharsharif** |
| **AMRUT** | 2015 | Urban water and sewerage |

### Financing infrastructure

- **Public–Private Partnership (PPP)** models: **BOT** (Build–Operate–Transfer), **BOOT**, **EPC** (government pays for construction), **HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model, 2016)** — government pays 40% during construction, rest as annuity.
- **InvITs** (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) and **REITs**.
- **NaBFID** (2021) and **NIIF** (2015).
- Government **capex** has been raised sharply — Union capital expenditure is now over **3% of GDP** (check the latest budget).

### Power sector basics

- India is the **3rd largest** producer and consumer of electricity.
- **Coal** still gives about **70%** of generation, but **non-fossil capacity crossed 50%** of installed capacity in **2025** — reaching the Paris NDC target five years early (check the latest).
- Target: **500 GW** non-fossil capacity by **2030**.
- **UDAY** (2015) for DISCOM debt; **RDSS** (2021) for distribution reforms and smart meters.

> **Bihar connection — infrastructure:**
> - **Ganga bridges**: **Mahatma Gandhi Setu** (Patna–Hajipur, **1982**, about **5.75 km**, once India's longest river bridge), **Digha–Sonpur rail-road bridge (J.P. Setu, 2016)**, **Munger rail-road bridge (Shrikrishna Setu, 2016)**, **Vikramshila Setu** (Bhagalpur, 2001), new six-lane bridges under construction (check the latest).
> - **Kosi rail mahasetu** (2020) reconnected Mithila and Kosi regions after the 1934 earthquake broke the link.
> - **NW-1** and the multimodal terminal at **Kalughat** (Saran) and Patna's Gaighat terminal.
> - **Power**: Bihar now gets near **24-hour supply** in most areas; per-capita power consumption is still among the **lowest** in India (around one-third of the national average — check the latest). Bihar's electricity companies were restructured in **2012** (BSPHCL with NBPDCL and SBPDCL); Bihar was among the first states to roll out **prepaid smart meters** at scale.
> - **Patna Metro** — first stretch opened in **2025** (check the latest).

## The services sector

The **tertiary (services) sector** is India's largest — about **55%** of GVA and about **30%** of employment.

| Sub-sector | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Trade, hotels, transport, communication** | Largest service group |
| **Financial, real estate, professional services** | IT/ITeS here |
| **Public administration, defence and other services** | Education, health |

- **IT–BPM**: India is the world's largest IT outsourcing destination; IT services exports exceed **$200 billion** (check the latest).
- **Global Capability Centres (GCCs)** — 1,700+ in India.
- **Tourism**: contributes about 5% of GDP; Incredible India; **Swadesh Darshan 2.0**, **PRASHAD** (pilgrimage).
- **Services exports** now nearly equal merchandise exports in value and run a big **surplus**.

**Why services led India's growth** (Mains): skilled English-speaking workforce, IT revolution and telecom liberalisation, low capital need, policy neglect of labour-intensive manufacturing, rigid labour laws (now being reformed through **four Labour Codes**, notified in **2025** — check the latest).

> **Mains angle:** "**Premature deindustrialisation**" (Dani Rodrik) — India moved from agriculture to services without a big manufacturing phase. Services jobs need education; manufacturing could absorb low-skilled rural workers. For Bihar, this means migrants from villages go to factories and construction sites in other states.

- **Historical**: Permanent Settlement drained surplus to zamindars, not into industry; **freight equalisation (1952–93)** removed the mineral-cost advantage.
- **2000 bifurcation**: Jharkhand got nearly all minerals (coal, iron ore, mica, bauxite) and big plants (Bokaro, Jamshedpur, HEC Ranchi).
- **Closure of sugar mills and jute mills** in north Bihar (Bihar once had over 30 sugar mills; many closed by the 1990s).
- **Infrastructure deficit** (power, roads) until the mid-2000s; now improved greatly.
- **Land** — dense population, small plots, high land cost, land-acquisition disputes.
- **Low credit–deposit ratio**, low private investment, perceived law-and-order problems.
- **Landlocked** — no port; dependence on Kolkata/Haldia.

### Bihar's industrial policies and strengths

| Policy / body | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **BIADA** (Bihar Industrial Area Development Authority) | **1974** | Industrial areas and land bank |
| **Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Policy** | **2016** | Priority sectors: food processing, tourism, small machines, IT, textiles, leather, renewable energy; **single-window clearance** (Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Act, 2016) |
| **Bihar Ethanol Production Promotion Policy** | **2021** | **First state** in India with a dedicated ethanol policy; maize and broken rice as feedstock; plants at Purnia, Gopalganj, etc. |
| **Bihar Textile and Leather Policy** | **2022** | Garment units, incentives on wages and power |
| **Bihar Startup Policy** | 2017 (revised 2022) | Seed funding through a trust fund |
| **Bihar Business Connect** | 2023, 2024 | Investors' summits; MoUs worth lakhs of crores announced (check implementation) |
| **Bihar Industrial Investment Promotion Package** | 2025 | New incentives (check the latest) |

Strengths: **food processing** (makhana, maize, litchi, dairy — Sudha), **ethanol**, **textiles and garments** (Bhagalpur silk — "**Silk City**"), **leather** (Muzaffarpur), **handicrafts** (Madhubani painting, Sikki, Sujani), **tourism** (Bodh Gaya, Rajgir, Nalanda, Vaishali, Patna Sahib), **IT parks** (Patna).

> **Bihar connection:** For the latest share of manufacturing in Bihar's GSVA and number of factories (Annual Survey of Industries), see [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar). Bihar's share of India's factories is very small (around 1.5–2%, check latest ASI) though it has about 9% of India's population.

### Mains questions

**1. Analyse the causes of industrial backwardness of Bihar and suggest a strategy for its industrialisation. (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar's low manufacturing share and factory count; growth led by services and construction.
- **Body (causes):** colonial land system; freight equalisation; bifurcation 2000; closed sugar/jute mills; weak power and roads till mid-2000s; land scarcity; low C-D ratio; landlocked; governance image.
- **Body (strategy):** agro- and food-processing clusters (makhana, maize, litchi, dairy); ethanol policy; textiles and leather policy; MSMEs and ODOP; Amritsar–Kolkata industrial corridor and Eastern DFC; NW-1 logistics; skilling of youth and returning migrants; land banks through BIADA; single window and faster approvals.
- **Conclusion:** Labour-intensive, agro-based industry suits Bihar's comparative advantage; consistent policy can turn its demographic dividend into jobs.

**2. "India's growth has been services-led, bypassing manufacturing." Discuss its consequences and the role of Make in India and PLI. (38 marks)**

Outline: data on shares; reasons for services boom; consequences — jobless growth, informality, premature deindustrialisation; Make in India and PLI results (mobile phones, electronics exports); limits — low employment intensity, import dependence; labour codes and logistics reforms; conclusion.

**3. Explain the importance of infrastructure for Bihar's development with examples of recent projects. (15 marks)**

Outline: multiplier effect; roads (PMGSY, state highways), Ganga bridges, Kosi mahasetu, NW-1, power reforms, airports and metro; benefits — markets, investment, women's mobility; gaps; conclusion.
