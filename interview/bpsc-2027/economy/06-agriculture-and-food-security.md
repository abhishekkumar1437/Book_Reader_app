Agriculture employs nearly half of India's workers and about half of Bihar's. BPSC asks Prelims questions on MSP, CACP, NFSA, land reform laws and schemes (PM-KISAN, PMFBY), and Mains questions on land reforms in Bihar, farm distress, food security and agricultural diversification. This chapter covers the economics; the physical side (soils, crops, irrigation) is in [Agriculture and Irrigation](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/agriculture-and-irrigation) and Bihar's crops in [Agriculture, Minerals and Natural Resources of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/agriculture-minerals-and-natural-resources-of-bihar).

> **In simple words:** Most Indian farmers have tiny plots, depend on rain, and sell at low prices. The government helps by fixing minimum support prices, buying grain, giving cheap grain to the poor through ration shops, and giving cash, credit and insurance. Bihar was the first state to abolish zamindari, but land is still very unequally held and farms are the smallest in India.

## Features of Indian agriculture

- **Share in GVA:** about **16–18%** (agriculture and allied). **Share in employment:** about **45%** (PLFS). In Bihar the share in employment is higher (about half).
- **Small and marginal holdings:** **86%** of holdings are below 2 hectares (Agriculture Census 2015-16). Average holding in India **1.08 ha**; in **Bihar about 0.39 ha** — among the smallest in the country.
- **Monsoon dependence:** about half of the net sown area is still rain-fed.
- **Low productivity** compared with China or the USA.
- **Disguised unemployment** and underemployment.
- **Allied sectors** (livestock, fisheries, dairy) are growing faster than crops.

| Size class (Agriculture Census) | Area |
|---|---|
| Marginal | Below 1 ha |
| Small | 1–2 ha |
| Semi-medium | 2–4 ha |
| Medium | 4–10 ha |
| Large | 10 ha and above |

> **Prelims angle:** India is the **largest producer of milk** (Operation Flood, **Verghese Kurien**), **pulses**, and jute, and the **second largest** producer of rice, wheat, fruits and vegetables. India became the **largest rice exporter**. The **Agriculture Census** is conducted every **5 years** (11th census 2021-22 — check the latest results).

## The Green Revolution and other "revolutions"

**Green Revolution** (from **1966–67**): use of **High-Yielding Variety (HYV)** seeds of wheat (from Mexico — **Norman Borlaug**) and rice, chemical fertilisers, irrigation, pesticides, credit.

- In India: **M.S. Swaminathan** (scientist), **C. Subramaniam** (Agriculture Minister).
- Areas: **Punjab, Haryana, western UP** — mostly **wheat**.
- Gains: food self-sufficiency, end of dependence on US **PL-480** wheat imports.
- Problems: regional inequality (eastern India left out), only wheat and rice, soil degradation, falling water tables, rising inequality between big and small farmers.

**Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India (BGREI)** — launched **2010-11** for **Bihar**, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, eastern UP, West Bengal and Assam, mainly for rice.

| Blue | Fish | — |

| Golden | Horticulture, honey | — |

> **Common mistake:** "Pink revolution" has more than one meaning in books — onion (and pharmaceuticals) in older books, meat and poultry (or prawns) in newer ones. In an exam, decide from the options given; do not assume only one meaning.

## Land reforms

Land reforms aim to give land to the tiller and reduce inequality. Four main parts:

1. **Abolition of intermediaries** (zamindari).
2. **Tenancy reforms** — regulate rent, security of tenure, ownership to tenants.
3. **Ceiling on landholdings** — redistribute surplus land.
4. **Consolidation of holdings** (chakbandi) and updating **land records**.

### Abolition of zamindari: Bihar led the way

- The **Permanent Settlement (1793)** of **Lord Cornwallis** created zamindars in Bengal, Bihar and Odisha who paid fixed revenue to the British and squeezed peasants (see [British Policies, Reforms and Economic Impact](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/british-policies-reforms-and-economic-impact)).
- **Bihar** passed the **Bihar Abolition of Zamindaris Act, 1948** (re-enacted as the **Bihar Land Reforms Act, 1950**) — **the first state in India** to take this step.
- The Patna High Court struck it down in **Kameshwar Singh v. State of Bihar (1951)** (the Maharaja of Darbhanga) on equality grounds.
- In response, Parliament passed the **First Amendment Act, 1951**, which added **Articles 31A and 31B** and created the **Ninth Schedule** to protect land reform laws from court challenge. The Bihar Land Reforms Act was the **first entry** in the Ninth Schedule.
- By the mid-1950s, intermediaries were largely abolished across India.

### Tenancy, ceilings and Bhoodan in Bihar

- **Bihar Tenancy Act, 1885** (colonial) — first protection to occupancy raiyats.
- **Bihar Land Reforms (Fixation of Ceiling Area and Acquisition of Surplus Land) Act, 1961** — ceiling of about **15–18 acres** for the best irrigated land and higher for poorer land (amended in **1973**). Implementation was weak; much surplus land was never recovered due to *benami* (fake-name) transfers and court cases.
- **Bhoodan Movement** — started by **Vinoba Bhave** at **Pochampally** (Telangana) on **18 April 1951**. Bihar received the largest Bhoodan pledges (over 20 lakh acres pledged; much was uncultivable or never distributed). **Bihar Bhoodan Yagna Act, 1954**.
- **Sharecroppers (bataidars)** — still largely unrecorded in Bihar, so they get no crop loans, insurance or compensation.
- **Bihar Land Reforms Commission (2006–08)** headed by **D. Bandyopadhyay** (architect of **Operation Barga** in West Bengal) recommended registering sharecroppers, a ceiling of 15 acres, giving homestead land to the landless. Its sharecropper proposals were **not implemented** due to political opposition.
- **Bihar Special Survey and Settlement** — a new statewide land survey with digital records (under the Bihar Special Survey and Settlement Act, 2011), aimed at updating records last made a century ago (as of 2026 — check the latest progress).
- **Bihar Consolidation of Holdings and Prevention of Fragmentation Act, 1956** — chakbandi, poorly implemented.

> **Bihar connection:** BPSC's favourite land-reform facts: Bihar was the **first** state to abolish zamindari; **Kameshwar Singh case** led to the **First Amendment** and the **Ninth Schedule**; **Bandyopadhyay Commission** (2006) on sharecroppers; Bhoodan's large pledges in Bihar. Unequal land, unrecorded tenancy and tiny plots are root causes of **rural poverty and migration** from Bihar.

### Land records and leasing (national)

- **Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP)**, 2008 (merged NLRMP).
- **SVAMITVA** (2020) — property cards for village houses using drones.
- **Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act, 2016** (NITI Aayog, **T. Haque Committee**) — to legalise and record tenancy.
- **ULPIN** — 14-digit Unique Land Parcel Identification Number ("Aadhaar for land").

## Agricultural prices: MSP and procurement

### Minimum Support Price (MSP)

**MSP** is the price at which the Government promises to buy a crop from farmers, so that prices do not crash below it.

- Recommended by the **Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)** — set up in **1965** as the Agricultural Prices Commission; renamed **CACP in 1985**. It is an **attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture** (not statutory).
- **Approved by** the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)**, chaired by the PM.
- MSP is announced for **22 mandated crops** (14 kharif, 6 rabi, 2 commercial — jute and copra), plus **toria** and **de-husked coconut** derived from them; for **sugarcane**, the Centre fixes the **Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP)**, and some states set a higher **State Advised Price (SAP)**.
- MSP has **no legal backing** (there is no law guaranteeing it) — a key demand of the 2020–21 farmers' protest.

### Cost concepts used by CACP

| Cost | Includes |
|---|---|
| **A2** | All paid-out costs: seeds, fertiliser, pesticides, hired labour, machinery, irrigation, fuel, leased land rent |
| **A2 + FL** | A2 + imputed value of **family labour** |
| **C2** | A2 + FL + rent of **owned land** + interest on owned capital (comprehensive cost) |

Since **2018-19**, MSP is fixed at **at least 1.5 times A2 + FL** cost. The **Swaminathan Commission** (National Commission on Farmers, **2004–06**) had recommended MSP at **C2 + 50%**.

#### Worked example: MSP formula

For paddy, suppose A2 + FL = ₹1,600 per quintal and C2 = ₹2,100.

- Government formula: 1.5 × 1,600 = **₹2,400** per quintal.
- Swaminathan formula: 1.5 × 2,100 = **₹3,150** per quintal.

The gap of ₹750 explains why farmer groups say the current MSP is not "Swaminathan MSP".

### Procurement and FCI

- **Food Corporation of India (FCI)** — set up in **1965** under the Food Corporations Act, 1964; first office in Chennai; HQ New Delhi.
- FCI procures wheat and rice, stores them, keeps **buffer stocks**, and moves them to states for PDS.
- **Decentralised Procurement (DCP)** (from **1997-98**) — states procure and distribute grain themselves. **Bihar** follows DCP for paddy, using **PACS (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies)** and **Vyapar Mandals** as procurement centres.
- **Buffer norms** — minimum stock FCI must hold on 1 April, 1 July, 1 October, 1 January.
- **Price Support Scheme (PSS)** for pulses and oilseeds through **NAFED**; **PM-AASHA** (2018) umbrella for price support.
- **Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS)** — FCI sells extra grain to control prices.

> **Prelims angle:** Effective procurement at MSP happens mostly for **wheat and rice** and mostly in a few states (Punjab, Haryana, MP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh). Most farmers in **Bihar** sell below MSP to private traders, especially for maize and pulses.

### Agricultural marketing

- **APMC Acts** (state laws) — regulated markets (mandis) with licensed commission agents.
- **Bihar abolished its APMC Act in 2006** — the **first** state to do so. Expected private markets did not grow much; farmers often get low prices. This is a key Mains point both for and against market reform.
- **e-NAM** (National Agriculture Market) — online trading platform launched **14 April 2016**; run by **SFAC**.
- **Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)** — **10,000 FPOs** scheme (2020).
- Three **farm laws** (September **2020**) — repealed in **November–December 2021** after farmer protests.
- **Gramin Agricultural Markets (GrAMs)** — upgrading rural haats.

## Farm credit, insurance and income support

| Scheme | Year | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** | **1998** (R.V. Gupta Committee) | Short-term crop loans; interest subvention — effective **4%** with prompt repayment; extended to animal husbandry and fisheries (2018-19); limit under modified interest subvention raised to ₹5 lakh (Budget 2025-26 — check latest) |
| **PM Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)** | **2016** | Farmer's premium: **2%** kharif, **1.5%** rabi, **5%** commercial/horticulture crops; rest paid by Centre and states |
| **PM-KISAN** | **February 2019** | **₹6,000 a year** in three instalments of ₹2,000 by DBT to landholding farmer families (central sector scheme) |
| **Soil Health Card** | **2015** | Nutrient status of soil and fertiliser advice |
| **PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)** | **2015** | "**Har Khet Ko Pani**" and "**More Crop Per Drop**" |
| **Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana** | 2015 | Organic farming clusters |
| **PM-KUSUM** | 2019 | Solar pumps for farmers |
| **Agriculture Infrastructure Fund** | 2020 | ₹1 lakh crore financing for post-harvest infrastructure |
| **PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana** | 2025 | Focus on 100 low-productivity agri districts (check latest) |
| **National Mission on Natural Farming** | 2024 | Chemical-free farming |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar **did not adopt PMFBY**; from **2018** it runs its own **Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata Yojana**, which pays farmers directly for crop loss (no premium). Bihar's large number of **sharecroppers** without land papers cannot get PM-KISAN or KCC easily — a classic Mains point.

## Food security

**Food security** (FAO) = all people at all times have **physical and economic access** to **sufficient, safe and nutritious food**. Three pillars: **availability, access, absorption (utilisation)**; FAO adds **stability**.

### Public Distribution System (PDS) — evolution

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1940s | Rationing during **Bengal famine (1943)** and WWII |
| 1965 | **FCI** and **APC** set up |
| 1992 | **Revamped PDS** for backward/remote blocks |
| **June 1997** | **Targeted PDS (TPDS)** — APL and BPL categories |
| **December 2000** | **Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY)** — poorest of the poor, **35 kg** grain per family per month |
| **2013** | **National Food Security Act (NFSA)** |
| 2019–2023 | **One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC)** — portability across states |
| 2020 | **PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY)** — free extra grain during COVID |
| **1 January 2024** | Free grain under NFSA (PMGKAY) extended for **5 years** |

### National Food Security Act, 2013

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Up to **75% of rural** and **50% of urban** population (about **two-thirds** of India, ~81 crore people) |
| Priority households | **5 kg** foodgrain **per person per month** |
| AAY households | **35 kg per family per month** |
| Prices (original) | Rice ₹3, wheat ₹2, coarse grains ₹1 per kg — **free since January 2023** under PMGKAY |
| Women's empowerment | **Eldest woman (18+)** of the household is the **head** for ration card |
| Maternity benefit | **₹6,000** to pregnant and lactating women (implemented through **PM Matru Vandana Yojana**) |
| Children | Nutritious meals through **ICDS (anganwadi)** and **Mid-Day Meal (PM POSHAN)** |
| Food security allowance | If grain is not supplied |
| Grievance | District Grievance Redressal Officer, **State Food Commission** |
| Coverage of states | State-wise coverage based on NSSO 2011-12 consumption data |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's NFSA coverage ratio is among the **highest**: about **85.1% rural** and **74.5% urban** population (the Centre's state-wise coverage) — reflecting its high poverty. Bihar gains a lot from **ONORC**, because lakhs of migrant workers from Bihar can collect ration in Delhi, Punjab or Maharashtra. Bihar has digitised ration cards and installed **ePoS** machines in fair price shops; leakages have fallen compared with the 2000s (check the latest data).

### Other food and nutrition programmes

- **PM POSHAN** (earlier Mid-Day Meal, started nationally **1995**) — hot cooked meal in government schools; renamed **2021**.
- **ICDS** (**1975**) — anganwadi services; **POSHAN Abhiyaan** (**March 2018**, launched from **Jhunjhunu**, Rajasthan).
- **Fortified rice** distribution through PDS (completed 2024).
- **International Year of Millets** — **2023** (India's proposal at the UN); millets called "**Shree Anna**".

> **Mains angle:** "Food security has moved from **availability** to **nutrition**." India produces surplus grain, yet NFHS-5 shows high stunting (35.5%) and anaemia. **Bihar** has among the highest child stunting (around **43%**, NFHS-5). Points: diversify PDS (pulses, millets, oils), strengthen anganwadis, women's education, sanitation, fortification.

## Problems of Indian agriculture and the way forward

| Problem | Way forward |
|---|---|
| Small and fragmented holdings | Land leasing law, consolidation, cooperatives, FPOs |
| Low productivity | Better seeds, micro-irrigation, extension services, mechanisation through custom hiring centres |
| Dependence on monsoon | PMKSY, watershed, groundwater recharge |
| Poor marketing and price crashes | e-NAM, FPOs, warehouses, cold chains, food processing |
| Low credit and insurance reach (tenants) | Record tenants, KCC to sharecroppers, SHG credit |
| Climate change, floods and droughts | Climate-resilient varieties, crop diversification, early warning |
| Low investment in agriculture | Public capex in irrigation, rural roads, research (ICAR) |
| Over-use of fertiliser (urea) | Nano urea, soil health cards, nutrient-based subsidy |

## Bihar's agriculture: road maps and strengths

### Agriculture Road Maps of Bihar

| Road map | Period | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | **2008–12** | Seeds, "Rainbow revolution" idea, SRI (System of Rice Intensification) |
| **Second** | **2012–17** | Productivity, extension; Bihar won **Krishi Karman Awards** (for example, for record rice production in 2011-12) |
| **Third** | **2017–22** (extended to 2023) | Organic corridor along the Ganga, diversification |
| **Fourth** | **2023–28** | Climate-resilient farming, millets, horticulture, food processing, dairy and fisheries (check latest) |

### Bihar's strengths

- **Makhana (fox nut)** — Bihar produces about **85–90%** of India's makhana, mainly in **Mithilanchal and Kosi–Seemanchal** (Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia, Katihar, Saharsa). **Mithila Makhana** got a **GI tag** (2022). A **National Makhana Board** was announced in the Union Budget **2025-26** for Bihar (as of 2026 — check the latest on its working).
- **Litchi** — Bihar is the **largest producer** of litchi in India; **Shahi Litchi of Muzaffarpur** has a GI tag (2018); **National Research Centre on Litchi**, Muzaffarpur.
- **Maize** — Bihar is a major maize producer (Kosi–Seemanchal belt), with high yields in rabi maize; supplies ethanol and poultry-feed industries.
- **Vegetables** — among the top states in vegetables (potato, onion, cauliflower, okra).
- Other GI products: **Katarni rice** (Bhagalpur), **Magahi paan** (Magadh), **Jardalu mango** (Bhagalpur), **Shahi litchi**, **Mithila makhana**.
- **Dairy** — **COMFED** (Bihar State Milk Co-operative Federation, brand **Sudha**, 1983) — one of the most successful cooperatives in eastern India.
- **Fisheries** — Bihar has become nearly self-sufficient in fish production through ponds and *chaurs* (wetlands).

### Bihar's problems

- **Floods in north Bihar** (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati) and **drought in south Bihar** (Gaya, Aurangabad, Nawada).
- **Tiny holdings** (~0.39 ha) and unrecorded tenancy.
- **Low seed replacement, low mechanisation**, weak extension.
- **No APMC since 2006** and weak private markets → farmers sell below MSP.
- **Low irrigation efficiency**; many tube wells run on diesel.
- **Migration of male workers** — "**feminisation of agriculture**": more women do farm work but lack land titles.

> **Bihar connection:** For numbers on Bihar's crop production, productivity and the share of agriculture in GSVA, use the latest **Bihar Economic Survey** summarised in [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

### Mains questions

**1. Evaluate the success and failures of land reforms in Bihar. What more needs to be done? (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** Permanent Settlement legacy; Bihar first to abolish zamindari.
- **Body:**
  1. Zamindari abolition (1950) — success in removing intermediaries; Kameshwar Singh case and First Amendment.
  2. Ceiling Act 1961 — weak implementation, benami transfers, court cases.
  3. Bhoodan — large pledges, poor distribution.
  4. Tenancy — sharecroppers unrecorded; Bandyopadhyay Commission not implemented.
  5. Consolidation — failed in most districts.
  6. Consequences — tiny holdings, poverty, caste-class conflict in the 1970s–90s, migration.
  7. Present steps — Special Survey and Settlement, digitised records, homestead land to landless (Mahadalit), Abhiyan Basera.
  8. Way forward — record tenants, land leasing law, land banks, FPOs, women's land rights.
- **Conclusion:** Land reform is "unfinished business" in Bihar; secure titles are the base of agricultural growth.

**2. Discuss the role of MSP and procurement in ensuring food security. Why do farmers in Bihar often sell below MSP? (38 marks)**

Outline: purpose of MSP; CACP and cost concepts; procurement by FCI and DCP states; benefits — food stocks for NFSA, price floor; distortions — wheat–rice bias, groundwater, regional concentration; Bihar — APMC abolished, few procurement centres, PACS capacity, small surplus per farmer, delayed payments, maize and pulses not procured; solutions — more PACS, e-NAM, FPOs, PM-AASHA, warehouses; conclusion.

**3. "India has achieved food security but not nutrition security." Discuss with reference to Bihar. (15 marks)**

Outline: production surplus vs NFHS-5 stunting/anaemia; NFSA and PMGKAY; Bihar's stunting; reasons — diet diversity, sanitation, women's education; solutions — millets and pulses in PDS, POSHAN 2.0, fortification; conclusion.

**4. Assess the potential of makhana and litchi for agricultural diversification in Bihar. (15 marks)**

Outline: Bihar's share; GI tags; processing, export, Makhana Board; issues — traditional harvesting, middlemen, cold chain for litchi, AES myth/health scare; solutions — clusters, FPOs, branding; conclusion.
