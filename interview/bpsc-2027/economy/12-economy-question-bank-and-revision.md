## Mains GS II economy questions with outlines

Each question is 38-mark / about 300-word style unless marked. Structure: **introduction (2–3 lines with a fact) → 5–8 body points with Bihar angle and data → way forward → conclusion (1–2 lines)**. For full model answers see [Economy and Geography Model Answers](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/economy-and-geography-model-answers).

### ⭐ 1. Bihar's high growth and low per-capita income — explain and suggest remedies.

- Intro: latest GSDP growth vs per-capita income (Bihar Economic Survey).
- Body: low base; population density and TFR; agriculture-heavy jobs; weak manufacturing; bifurcation; low urbanisation; low C-D ratio; migration.
- Way forward: agro-industry, skills, girls' education, urbanisation, infrastructure, credit.
- Conclusion: convert growth into jobs and productivity.

### ⭐ 2. Causes of industrial backwardness of Bihar and strategy for industrialisation.

- Intro: manufacturing share and factory count.
- Body: colonial land system; freight equalisation; 2000 bifurcation; closed sugar/jute mills; land, power, credit; landlocked.
- Strategy: food processing, ethanol, textiles policy, MSMEs/ODOP, AKIC corridor, DFC, NW-1, BIADA land bank, single window.
- Conclusion: labour-intensive industry suits Bihar.

### ⭐ 3. Land reforms in Bihar — achievements and failures.

- Intro: Permanent Settlement; first to abolish zamindari.
- Body: 1950 Act and Kameshwar Singh case; ceiling act weak; Bhoodan; sharecroppers and Bandyopadhyay Commission; consolidation; land survey now.
- Way forward: tenancy records, leasing law, women's titles.
- Conclusion: unfinished agenda.

### ⭐ 4. Role of the Finance Commission in fiscal federalism with reference to Bihar.

- Intro: Article 280; vertical and horizontal gaps.
- Body: 41% share; criteria and weights; income distance; new GDP criterion; Bihar 10.06% → 9.95%; local body grants; cesses outside pool; special category demand.
- Conclusion: balance equity and efficiency.

### ⭐ 5. Food inflation and its impact on the poor; role of monetary and supply-side policy.

- Intro: latest CPI and food inflation.
- Body: food share of poor households; rural vs urban; causes; RBI tools and limits; OMSS, PSF, export curbs; storage and cold chain; Bihar vegetable price swings.
- Conclusion: supply-side reforms + inflation targeting.

### 6. Evaluate the monetary policy framework and MPC.

- Intro: 2016 amendment.
- Body: target and band; MPC structure; transmission (EBLR); successes; breaches (2022); supply shocks; 2026–31 renewal; October 2026 hike context.
- Conclusion: credible framework needing fiscal and supply support.

### ⭐ 7. Is MSP-based procurement sustainable? Why do Bihar's farmers sell below MSP?

- Intro: MSP and CACP.
- Body: cost concepts; wheat–rice bias; regional concentration; WTO amber box; Bihar — APMC abolished, PACS capacity, small surplus.
- Way forward: FPOs, e-NAM, PM-AASHA, warehouses, crop diversification.
- Conclusion.

### ⭐ 8. Multidimensional poverty in Bihar — progress and gaps.

- Intro: NITI MPI numbers.
- Body: drivers (electricity, LPG, toilets, accounts, schooling); remaining deprivations (nutrition, maternal health); income poverty (HCES, caste survey).
- Conclusion: from deprivations to incomes.

### ⭐ 9. Migration from Bihar — causes, consequences and policy.

- Intro: scale and destinations.
- Body: push–pull; remittances; feminisation of agriculture; social change; COVID reverse migration; e-Shram, ONORC.
- Way forward: local jobs, skills, safe migration.
- Conclusion.

### 10. Impact of GST on federalism and Bihar's finances. (15 marks)

- Destination principle; GST Council; compensation end; GST 2.0; Bihar's dependence; compliance of small traders; conclusion.

### 11. Financial inclusion and the role of SHGs (JEEViKA) in Bihar.

- PMJDY, JAM, DBT; SHG–bank linkage; Lakhpati Didi; C-D ratio; challenges; conclusion.

### 12. Critically evaluate the 1991 reforms; how far did Bihar benefit?

- Crisis; LPG; achievements; concerns; regional pattern; Bihar's late convergence; what Bihar needs.

### 13. Infrastructure as a driver of Bihar's growth. (15 marks)

- Roads, Ganga bridges, Kosi mahasetu, NW-1, power reforms, airports and metro; multiplier effect; gaps; conclusion.

### 14. Role of agriculture diversification (makhana, litchi, maize, dairy, fisheries) in Bihar. (15 marks)

- Bihar's share; GI tags; Makhana Board; COMFED; ethanol demand for maize; challenges — cold chain, processing, floods; conclusion.

### 15. "Employment guarantee has moved from MGNREGA to VB-G RAM G." Examine the implications for Bihar.

- MGNREGA achievements; new Act — 125 days, 60:40; state fiscal burden; demand vs allocation; asset creation; social audit; Bihar's capacity; conclusion.

### 16. Explain the significance of the external sector for India; how can Bihar benefit from trade? (15 marks)

- BoP; CAD financing; remittances; FTAs; Nepal trade via Raxaul/Jogbani; agri exports (makhana, litchi); logistics; conclusion.

> **Mains angle:** In every economy answer, add **one data point** (latest Economic Survey / Bihar Economic Survey), **one Bihar example**, **one scheme**, and **one diagram** where possible (a flowchart of transmission, a simple bar of sector shares). That combination lifts an average answer.
