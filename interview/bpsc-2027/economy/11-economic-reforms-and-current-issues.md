This chapter connects economic history with today's news. BPSC Prelims asks about the 1991 reforms (who, when, what), demonetisation, UPI and NPCI, Aadhaar, GST and labour codes. Mains GS II asks you to evaluate three decades of reforms, the digital economy, and what reforms Bihar needs. Current issues change every month — treat the second half as a framework to update from the newspaper.

> **In simple words:** In 1991 India nearly ran out of foreign currency. The government responded by opening the economy: it removed most licences, allowed foreign investment, cut import duties and let the market set more prices. This is called LPG — Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation. Later reforms built a common tax (GST), a bankruptcy law, digital payments (UPI) and new labour laws. Bihar's challenge is to use these reforms to bring industry and jobs into the state.

## The 1991 crisis and the new economic policy

### Why the crisis came

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Fiscal deficit** | About **8%** of GDP (Centre) by 1990-91; high revenue deficit |
| **Current account deficit** | About **3%** of GDP |
| **Gulf War (1990–91)** | Oil prices jumped; remittances from Kuwait and Iraq stopped; workers returned |
| **Forex reserves** | Fell to about **$1 billion** — barely **2–3 weeks** of imports |
| **Political instability** | Short-lived governments 1989–91 |
| **Credit rating downgrade** | NRIs withdrew deposits; foreign lenders stopped lending |
| **Inflation** | Above **13%** |

India **pledged gold** (about **47 tonnes** to the Bank of England and Bank of Japan; earlier, seized smuggled gold was sold to UBS), devalued the rupee in **July 1991**, and took an **IMF loan** under the **Stand-By Arrangement** and Compensatory and Contingency Financing Facility.

### Who and when

- **PM:** **P.V. Narasimha Rao**. **Finance Minister:** **Dr Manmohan Singh**.
- **Budget and New Industrial Policy:** **24 July 1991**.
- Famous quote from the budget speech (quoting **Victor Hugo**): "No power on earth can stop an idea whose time has come."

### Two kinds of measures

| Stabilisation measures (short term, demand side) | Structural reforms (long term, supply side) |
|---|---|
| Cut fiscal deficit | Industrial delicensing |
| Control inflation | Trade liberalisation — tariff cuts, removal of quotas |
| Rupee devaluation | Financial sector reform (Narasimham Committee) |
| Build forex reserves | Tax reform (Raja Chelliah Committee) |
| — | Opening to FDI and FPI; disinvestment |

The **IMF and World Bank** supported these as "**structural adjustment**" programmes.

### LPG explained

| Component | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Liberalisation** | Removing government controls on economic activity | End of licence raj; interest rate deregulation; lower CRR/SLR; rupee convertibility on current account (1994) |
| **Privatisation** | Increasing the private sector's role | Disinvestment; opening telecom, airlines, insurance (**IRDA Act 1999**), banking to private players; public-sector reservation cut to 2 sectors |
| **Globalisation** | Integrating with the world economy | Peak customs duty cut from over **150%** (1991) to about **10%** by the late 2000s; FDI; WTO membership (1995) |

> **Prelims angle:** Committees of the reform era: **Narasimham I (1991)** — banking; **Raja Chelliah (1991–93)** — tax reform; **Malhotra Committee (1993)** — insurance (led to IRDA); **Rangarajan Committee (1993)** — disinvestment; **Tarapore (1997)** — capital account convertibility; **Kelkar (2002–04)** — tax and GST idea.

## Results of the reforms (balanced view for Mains)

| Achievements | Concerns |
|---|---|
| GDP growth rose to **6–8%** on average; India became a top-5 economy | **Jobless growth**; slow growth of manufacturing jobs |
| Forex reserves from $1 bn to over **$700 bn** | **Agriculture neglected**; farm distress |
| Telecom, IT, aviation boom; consumer choice | **Rising inequality** — income and wealth |
| Poverty fell sharply | **Regional imbalance** — coastal states gained more than Bihar, UP |
| Inflation lower; financial sector stronger | Informal sector remained large (~90% workers) |
| Services exports and global firms | Environmental stress; crony capitalism concerns |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar gained less from the reforms in the 1990s: private investment went to states with ports, power and skilled workers (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka). Bihar's growth picked up only after **2005**, driven by public spending on roads, power, schools and law-and-order, not by private industry. This "**late convergence**" is a useful Mains point.

## Second-generation reforms (2000s onwards)

| Reform | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **FRBM Act** | 2003 | Fiscal discipline |
| **Competition Act** (CCI) | 2002 | Replaced MRTP |
| **SEZ Act** | 2005 | Export zones |
| **Aadhaar** | 2009 (UIDAI); Aadhaar Act **2016** | Unique ID; base of DBT |
| **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)** | **1 January 2013** | Subsidy directly into bank accounts; LPG subsidy (**PAHAL**, 2014–15) |
| **Monetary Policy Framework** | 2016 | Inflation targeting, MPC |
| **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)** | **2016** | Time-bound resolution (originally 180 + 90 days, now 330-day outer limit) through **NCLT**; **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI)** |
| **GST** | **1 July 2017** | One national market; **GST 2.0** rate rationalisation **22 September 2025** |
| **RERA** | 2016 | Real estate regulation |
| **Corporate tax cut** | 2019 | 22% (15% for new manufacturing firms) |
| **Four Labour Codes** | Passed **2019–2020**; brought into force **21 November 2025** | 29 central laws merged into 4: **Code on Wages (2019)**, **Industrial Relations Code (2020)**, **Code on Social Security (2020)**, **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code (2020)** (check the latest rules) |
| **Production Linked Incentives** | 2020 | Manufacturing |
| **New Income-tax Act** | 2025 (from 1 April 2026) | Simpler language; replaces the 1961 Act |

### Privatisation and disinvestment

- **Disinvestment Commission** (1996, **G.V. Ramakrishna**). **Ministry of Disinvestment** (1999–2004); now **DIPAM** under the Ministry of Finance.
- Notable strategic sales: **BALCO** (2001, to Sterlite), **VSNL**, **Hindustan Zinc**, **Maruti** (stake sale), **Air India** (to **Tata**, January 2022), **Neelachal Ispat Nigam** (2022).
- **New PSE Policy 2021** — minimum presence in strategic sectors; exit from non-strategic sectors.
- **Bank privatisation** of two PSBs and one general insurer was announced in **Budget 2021-22** (progress slow — check the latest; IDBI Bank stake sale was under way).

## Demonetisation (2016)

- On **8 November 2016**, the Government announced that **₹500 and ₹1,000** notes would cease to be legal tender from midnight.
- These notes formed about **86%** of currency in circulation by value (about ₹15.4 lakh crore).
- Aims: curb **black money**, **counterfeit currency**, **terror financing**; push **digital payments** and formalisation.
- Result (RBI Annual Report 2018): about **99.3%** of the demonetised notes came back to banks — so little black cash was destroyed.
- Effects: short-term fall in GDP growth and informal-sector jobs; rise in digital payments and tax filers.
- New notes of **₹2,000** and ₹500 were issued; **₹2,000 notes were withdrawn from circulation in May 2023** (still legal tender).
- **Supreme Court** (**Vivek Narayan Sharma case, January 2023**) upheld the decision by a **4:1** majority.

Earlier demonetisations: **1946** (₹1,000 and ₹10,000) and **1978** (₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000 — Janata govt).

| Element | Year | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Aadhaar** | First number issued **29 September 2010**, Tembhli village (Maharashtra) | 12-digit biometric ID by **UIDAI**; over 130 crore numbers |
| **UPI** (Unified Payments Interface) | Launched **11 April 2016** by **NPCI** (pilot); public from August 2016 | Instant account-to-account payments by mobile; **BHIM** app (December 2016); now **well over 15–20 billion transactions a month** (check latest NPCI data); India accounts for nearly half of the world's real-time digital payments |
| **NPCI** | **2008** | Not-for-profit set up by RBI and Indian Banks' Association; runs UPI, RuPay, IMPS, FASTag, AePS, NACH |
| **RuPay** | 2012 | India's domestic card network |
| **AePS** | — | Aadhaar-enabled payments (bank correspondents in villages — useful in rural Bihar) |
| **DigiLocker** | 2015 | Digital documents |
| **GeM** (Government e-Marketplace) | **2016** | Online public procurement |
| **Account Aggregator** | 2021 | Consent-based sharing of financial data |
| **ONDC** (Open Network for Digital Commerce) | **2022** | Open e-commerce network so small sellers can reach buyers on any app |
| **e-Rupee (CBDC)** | Wholesale Nov 2022; retail **December 2022** | RBI digital currency |
| **UPI international** | 2023 onwards | Linked with Singapore (PayNow, 2023), UAE, France, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Nepal, Bhutan and others (check latest) |
| **DPDP Act** (Digital Personal Data Protection) | **2023** (rules notified 2025) | Data protection; Data Protection Board |
| **Crypto** | 2022 budget | **30% tax** on gains from virtual digital assets + **1% TDS**; crypto is **not** legal tender |

> **Prelims angle:** UPI and RuPay are run by **NPCI**, not by the RBI directly. **Aadhaar** is issued by **UIDAI** (under MeitY; statutory under the **Aadhaar Act, 2016**). The Supreme Court in **K.S. Puttaswamy (2018)** upheld Aadhaar for welfare and PAN, but not for bank accounts or mobile SIMs as mandatory. **Right to privacy** became a fundamental right in **Puttaswamy (2017)**.

> **Bihar connection:** Digital payments and DBT have changed welfare delivery in Bihar — scholarships, cycle and uniform money, pensions, JEEViKA loans and crop-loss compensation (Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata) are paid by **DBT**. Challenges: low smartphone ownership among women, weak connectivity in flood areas, **cyber fraud** (some districts became hubs of cyber crime), and digital literacy gaps.

### Digital divide and risks

- Gaps by **gender**, **rural/urban**, **state** (Bihar has among the lowest internet penetration — check the latest TRAI data).
- **Cyber fraud**, data privacy, exclusion when biometrics fail.
- **Platform monopoly** concerns — two apps handle most UPI volume (NPCI's market-share cap deadline has been extended — check the latest).

## Current issues (update from the newspaper)

These themes are likely to appear in a 2027 exam. Learn the background here and update the numbers.

### 1. Viksit Bharat @2047

- Goal: make India a **developed nation by 2047** (100 years of independence).
- Needs about **7–8% real growth** for two decades, more manufacturing jobs, higher female workforce participation, better human capital.
- India's GDP crossed about **$4 trillion** in nominal terms; the target of a **$5 trillion** economy keeps getting re-dated (check the latest IMF numbers).

### 2. Green growth and climate

- **Net zero by 2070** (announced at **COP26 Glasgow, 2021**); **updated NDC (2022)**: reduce emissions intensity of GDP by **45%** from 2005 levels by 2030; **50%** of installed power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030 (achieved in **2025**).
- **National Green Hydrogen Mission** (**January 2023**) — 5 million tonnes a year by 2030.
- **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (2023)** under the Energy Conservation Act.
- **Mission LiFE** (Lifestyle for Environment), **Green Credit Programme** (2023).
- **Ethanol blending** — **20% blending (E20)** reached in **2025**, ahead of the original 2030 target. This connects directly with **Bihar's ethanol policy** and maize farmers.

### 3. Jobs, gig economy and AI

- **Gig and platform workers** (delivery, ride-hailing) — about **77 lakh** in 2020-21, projected to reach **2.35 crore by 2029-30** (NITI Aayog report, 2022). The **Code on Social Security** covers them; some states (Rajasthan 2023, then Karnataka, Jharkhand and others) have passed or proposed gig-worker welfare laws (check the latest list, including whether Bihar has one).
- **AI and automation** — threat to routine jobs; opportunity in IT services. **IndiaAI Mission (2024)**.
- **Demographic dividend** — window until about **2040s**; Bihar's younger population means its dividend lasts longer.

### 4. Trade shocks and supply chains

- **US tariffs (2025)** on many Indian exports raised costs for textiles, gems, shrimp and other sectors; India pursued trade deals with the UK, EFTA, EU and others (check the latest status).
- **China+1** strategy — firms moving some production out of China (electronics assembly in India).
- **Oil price shocks** and geopolitical conflicts affect inflation and the rupee — the RBI raised the repo rate in **October 2026**, citing oil and inflation risks (check the latest).

### 5. Fiscal federalism debates

- **16th Finance Commission (2026–31)** retained **41%** devolution; new GDP-contribution criterion (see [Public Finance, Budget and Taxation](/interview/bpsc-2027/economy/public-finance-budget-and-taxation)).
- States' share of cesses and surcharges; GST compensation ending; state debt levels; **freebies vs welfare** debate (Supreme Court and RBI reports).

### 6. Census and caste data

- India's next **Census** is due with **caste enumeration** (house-listing phase from **2026**, population count with reference date **1 March 2027** — check the latest notification). It will affect Finance Commission formulas, delimitation and women's reservation.

> **Bihar connection — Bihar's reform agenda for Mains:**
> - **Bihar Caste-based Survey (2023)** — first state to publish caste data (population about **13.07 crore**; EBC about 36%, OBC about 27%, SC about 19.65%, ST about 1.68%, general about 15.5%). Based on it, Bihar raised reservation to **65%** (plus 10% EWS) in **November 2023**; the **Patna High Court struck down** the increase in **June 2024**; the matter went to the Supreme Court (as of 2026 — check the latest).
> - **Prohibition (2016)** — social gains vs revenue loss (excise was a major source of state revenue) and enforcement costs.
> - **Special Category Status / special package** demand.
> - **Land records** (Special Survey), **agriculture road map**, **ethanol and food processing**, **industrial policy**, **migration and skills**, **flood management** (Kosi–Mechi link project).
> - Data source for all numbers: the **Bihar Economic Survey** (presented before the state budget each year) — see [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Liberalisation | Removing government controls |
| Privatisation | Larger role for private ownership |
| Globalisation | Linking the economy with the world |
| Stabilisation | Short-term steps to control deficits and inflation |
| Structural reform | Long-term changes in rules and institutions |
| Strategic disinvestment | Sale of PSU with control |
| Demonetisation | Withdrawing legal tender status of currency notes |
| DPI | Digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI etc.) |
| Account Aggregator | Consent-based data-sharing network |
| Gig worker | Worker paid per task through an app/platform |
| Net zero | Emissions balanced by removals |
| China+1 | Firms adding a second production base outside China |
| Demographic dividend | Growth benefit from a large working-age population |

### Mains questions

**1. Critically evaluate three decades of economic reforms in India. How far have their benefits reached Bihar? (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** 1991 crisis and LPG.
- **Body:**
  1. Achievements — growth, reserves, poverty fall, IT and telecom, consumer choice.
  2. Second-generation reforms — GST, IBC, DBT, labour codes.
  3. Weaknesses — jobless growth, agrarian distress, inequality, informality.
  4. Regional pattern — investment concentrated in coastal states.
  5. Bihar — late convergence after 2005; public-investment-led growth; little private industry; migration; DBT gains.
  6. What Bihar needs — land, power, logistics, skills, agro-industry, investor confidence.
- **Conclusion:** Reforms must be "**inclusive and place-based**" so that lagging states benefit.

**2. "UPI has transformed India's payment landscape." Discuss its benefits and challenges, especially for rural areas. (15 marks)**

Outline: UPI design (NPCI, interoperability); scale; benefits — low cost, inclusion, formalisation, small merchants, DBT link; global reach; challenges — cyber fraud, digital literacy, women's access, network gaps, market concentration; Bihar example — AePS, cyber-fraud hubs; conclusion.

**3. Discuss the significance of the four Labour Codes for India's workers and employers. (15 marks)**

Outline: consolidation of 29 laws; universal minimum wage and floor wage; social security for gig workers; easier compliance; fixed-term employment; concerns — higher threshold for lay-offs (300 workers), union rights, state rules; relevance for Bihar's migrant workers; conclusion.

**4. "Prohibition in Bihar is a social reform with economic costs." Examine. (15 marks)**

Outline: 2016 law and women's demand; social benefits — domestic violence, savings, health; economic costs — excise revenue loss, enforcement, illicit liquor deaths, tourism; evaluation studies; balanced conclusion.
