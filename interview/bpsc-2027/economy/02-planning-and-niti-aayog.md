India ran its economy through **five-year plans** from 1951 to 2017. BPSC Prelims loves plan-wise facts: the model behind a plan, its focus, which steel plant came in which plan, the "plan holiday". Mains asks you to compare the **Planning Commission** with **NITI Aayog** and to judge whether planning helped backward states like Bihar.

> **In simple words:** Planning means the government decides in advance the goals for the next five years (how much growth, which sectors) and how much money each sector will get. India's Planning Commission did this from 1950. In 2015 it was replaced by NITI Aayog, a "think tank" that advises the Centre and states but does not hand out plan money.

## What is economic planning?

**Economic planning** is the process of setting national goals for a fixed period and allocating resources to reach them. India's planning was:

- **Democratic** — done by an elected government, with private property allowed.
- **Indicative** (after 1991) — the plan *indicates* targets, the market does much of the work. Before 1991 it was closer to **imperative** planning for the public sector.
- **Centralised** at first, with the Planning Commission in Delhi making plans and states following.

Types of planning you should know:

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Imperative / directive** | State gives orders; targets binding | USSR |
| **Indicative** | State sets broad goals; market follows signals | France; India from 8th Plan |
| **Rolling plan** | Plan revised every year; targets roll forward | India 1978–80 (Janata govt; idea by **Gunnar Myrdal**) |
| **Perspective plan** | Long-term vision (15–20 years) | NITI Aayog's 15-year vision idea |

## Planning before independence

| Year | Plan / body | By whom | Main idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1934** | *Planned Economy for India* | **M. Visvesvaraya** | First book-length plan; double national income in 10 years |
| **1938** | **National Planning Committee** | INC; chaired by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (Congress president **Subhas Chandra Bose** set it up) | First official attempt by the national movement |
| **1944** | **Bombay Plan** | Eight industrialists incl. **J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla**, Purushottamdas Thakurdas | State support for industry; triple income in 15 years |
| **1944** | **Gandhian Plan** | **Shriman Narayan Agarwal** | Village self-sufficiency, cottage industries |
| **1945** | **People's Plan** | **M.N. Roy** | Socialist, agriculture-first, based on Marxism |
| **1950** | **Sarvodaya Plan** | **Jayaprakash Narayan** | Gandhian + Sarvodaya; agriculture, small industry, land reform |

> **Bihar connection:** **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)**, author of the **Sarvodaya Plan (1950)**, was born in **Sitab Diara** (Saran/Ballia border). BPSC links him to the Sarvodaya Plan, the Bhoodan movement and the **1974 JP Movement**.

> **Memory trick:** "**V**ery **N**ice **B**ombay **G**andhi **P**eople **S**ay" → Visvesvaraya 1934, National Planning Committee 1938, Bombay 1944, Gandhian 1944, People's 1945, Sarvodaya 1950.

## The Planning Commission and NDC

- **Planning Commission** set up on **15 March 1950** by a **Cabinet resolution** (an executive order). It was **neither constitutional nor statutory** (an extra-constitutional, non-statutory body).
- **Chairman:** Prime Minister (first: **Jawaharlal Nehru**). **Deputy Chairman** was the working head (first: **Gulzarilal Nanda**; last: **Montek Singh Ahluwalia**).
- **National Development Council (NDC)** set up on **6 August 1952** to bring states into planning. Members: PM, Union Cabinet ministers, all Chief Ministers, members of the Planning Commission. It **approved** the five-year plans. NDC is also extra-constitutional.
- Planning appears in the Constitution only indirectly: "**Economic and social planning**" is entry **20 of the Concurrent List**.
- The Planning Commission was abolished and replaced by **NITI Aayog** on **1 January 2015**.

> **Prelims angle:** Trap statements — "The Planning Commission was a constitutional body" (false). "NDC approved the plans" (true). "Economic and social planning is in the State List" (false — Concurrent List).

## The twelve five-year plans

The table below is the single most important table of this chapter. "Achieved" growth figures differ slightly between sources (some use GDP at factor cost, some GDP at market prices); learn the **approximate** numbers and the **focus**.

| Plan | Period | Model / leader | Main focus and key events | Target / achieved growth (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1951–56 | **Harrod–Domar** model; drafted by **K.N. Raj** | **Agriculture**, irrigation, power (food shortage, refugees after Partition). Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud, **Damodar Valley Corporation** (1948) projects. Community Development Programme (**1952**) | 2.1 / ~3.6 (success) |
| **2nd** | 1956–61 | **P.C. Mahalanobis** model | **Heavy industry**, rapid industrialisation, public sector. Steel plants: **Bhilai** (USSR), **Rourkela** (West Germany), **Durgapur** (UK). Industrial Policy 1956 | 4.5 / ~4.2 |
| **3rd** | 1961–66 | Called "Gadgil Yojana" in some books; goal "self-reliant, self-generating economy" | Agriculture + industry; **failed** due to **1962 China war, 1965 Pakistan war, 1965–66 drought**. Bokaro Steel planned (USSR help) | 5.6 / ~2.4 (failure) |
| **Plan holiday** | 1966–69 | Three annual plans | **Green Revolution** begins (HYV seeds); devaluation of rupee (1966) | — |
| **4th** | 1969–74 | **D.R. Gadgil**; "growth with stability" and "progressive achievement of self-reliance" | **Nationalisation of 14 banks (1969)**; **Gadgil formula** for central assistance to states; 1971 war; drought; oil shock 1973 | 5.7 / ~3.3 |
| **5th** | 1974–78 (ended a year early) | Drafted by **D.P. Dhar** | **Garibi Hatao** (poverty removal) and self-reliance; **Minimum Needs Programme**; **20-Point Programme (1975)**; ended by Janata government | 4.4 / ~4.8 |
| **Rolling plan** | 1978–80 | Janata govt (Gunnar Myrdal's idea) | Annual revision of targets | — |
| **6th** | 1980–85 | — | **Poverty eradication**, employment; **IRDP**, **NREP**, **RLEGP**; start of liberalisation in a small way | 5.2 / ~5.7 (success) |
| **7th** | 1985–90 | — | "**Food, work and productivity**"; **Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (1989)** | 5.0 / ~6.0 (success) |
| **Annual plans** | 1990–92 | — | Political instability, BoP crisis 1991 | — |
| **8th** | 1992–97 | **Rao–Manmohan** model | **Human resource development**; first plan after **1991 reforms**; indicative planning; Panchayati Raj (73rd/74th amendments) | 5.6 / ~6.8 (success) |
| **9th** | 1997–2002 | — | "**Growth with social justice and equality**"; East Asian crisis; growth fell short | 6.5 (revised 7) / ~5.4 |
| **10th** | 2002–07 | — | **8% growth**; **monitorable targets** (poverty down 5 points by 2007, literacy, etc.); states given growth targets | 8.0 / ~7.6 |
| **11th** | 2007–12 | Deputy Chairman **Montek Singh Ahluwalia** | "**Faster and more inclusive growth**"; RTE, NRHM, NREGA scaled up; 2008 global crisis | 9.0 / ~8.0 |
| **12th** | 2012–17 | — | "**Faster, more inclusive and sustainable growth**"; last five-year plan | 8.0 / ~6.7 |

Say: "**A**ll **I**ndians **S**hould **S**ee **P**lans **P**roperly; **F**ood **H**elps **S**ocial **M**obility, **I**nclusion, **S**ustainability."

### Plans that succeeded and failed (exam favourites)

- **Most successful in meeting targets:** 1st, 5th (despite early end), 6th, 7th, 8th.
- **Clear failure:** **3rd** Plan (wars, drought) — led to the plan holiday.
- **Highest achieved growth:** **11th** Plan (~8%).
- **Plan that ended early:** **5th** (1974–78).

### Models behind the plans

- **Harrod–Domar model** (1st Plan): Growth = Savings rate ÷ Capital–output ratio. More savings and investment → more growth. Example: savings 7% ÷ COR 3.5 ≈ 2% growth target.
- **Mahalanobis model** (2nd Plan): invest in **capital goods / heavy industry** first; this builds the base for long-run growth. Criticised for neglecting agriculture and consumer goods.
- **Gadgil formula** (from 4th Plan): a formula for dividing **central plan assistance among states** — by population (60%), per-capita income (10%; later more weight to backward states), tax effort, ongoing projects and special problems. Revised as **Gadgil–Mukherjee formula (1990)**.

> **Mains angle:** The **Mahalanobis strategy** built India's heavy industry base (steel, BHEL, HAL), but it ignored agriculture and labour-intensive industry, which hurt populous states like Bihar where most people depended on farming.

## Why planning was replaced

Problems pointed out by experts and the government in 2014:

1. **One-size-fits-all** plans from Delhi; states had little voice.
2. Planning Commission became a **fund-allocating body** that controlled states through "plan grants" and centrally sponsored schemes.
3. After 1991 the private sector and markets became the main drivers; five-year targets became less meaningful.
4. Overlap with the **Finance Commission** (which also transfers money to states).
5. A need for **cooperative and competitive federalism**.

## NITI Aayog

**NITI** = **National Institution for Transforming India**. Set up on **1 January 2015** by a **Cabinet resolution** (like the Planning Commission, it is **non-constitutional and non-statutory**).

### Structure

| Part | Who |
|---|---|
| **Chairperson** | Prime Minister |
| **Vice-Chairperson** | Appointed by PM (first: **Arvind Panagariya**; then Rajiv Kumar; **Suman Bery** from 2022 — as of 2026, check the latest) |
| **Full-time members** | Experts (e.g. V.K. Saraswat, Ramesh Chand, V.K. Paul — check latest) |
| **Part-time members** | Up to two, from universities/research bodies, rotating |
| **Ex-officio members** | Up to **four Union ministers** nominated by PM |
| **Special invitees** | Experts nominated by PM |
| **CEO** | Secretary-rank officer appointed by PM for a fixed tenure (**B.V.R. Subrahmanyam** from 2023 — check the latest) |
| **Governing Council** | PM, **all Chief Ministers** of states and UTs with legislatures, **Lt Governors** of other UTs; replaced the role of NDC |
| **Regional Councils** | Formed for specific issues affecting more than one state; chaired by PM or nominee |

### Planning Commission vs NITI Aayog

| Point | Planning Commission | NITI Aayog |
|---|---|---|
| Set up | 15 March 1950 | 1 January 2015 |
| Nature | Extra-constitutional, non-statutory | Same — extra-constitutional, non-statutory |
| Role | Made plans, **allocated funds** to states and ministries | **Think tank**, advisory; **no power to allocate funds** |
| Approach | Top-down | Bottom-up (village → state → nation) |
| States' role | Limited; NDC approved plans | Governing Council of all CMs; **cooperative federalism** |
| Planning horizon | Five-year plans | 15-year vision, 7-year strategy, 3-year action agenda (originally) |
| Federalism | Centralised | **Cooperative + competitive federalism** (state rankings) |
| Funds to states | Plan grants decided by it | Decided by Finance Ministry / Finance Commission |

> **Prelims angle:** NITI Aayog **does not** allocate funds; the Ministry of Finance does. Its Governing Council includes all CMs. Its first Vice-Chairperson was **Arvind Panagariya** — who later chaired the **16th Finance Commission**.

### Main functions and work of NITI Aayog

- **Cooperative federalism** — Governing Council meetings, sub-groups of CMs (e.g. on centrally sponsored schemes, Swachh Bharat, skill development).
- **Competitive federalism** — ranks states on indices so they compete to improve.
- **Monitoring and evaluation** — **DMEO (Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office)** evaluates schemes.
- **Innovation** — **Atal Innovation Mission (AIM)**: Atal Tinkering Labs in schools, Atal Incubation Centres.
- **Long-term vision** — work on **Viksit Bharat @2047** (developed India by the 100th year of independence).
- **Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP)** — launched **January 2018**, **112** most backward districts, ranked monthly on **49 indicators** in five themes: health & nutrition (30% weight), education (30%), agriculture & water resources (20%), financial inclusion & skill development (10%), basic infrastructure (10%). Method: **Convergence, Collaboration (Centre–state–district), Competition**.
- **Aspirational Blocks Programme (ABP)** — launched **January 2023** for **500** blocks.

### Indices published or released by NITI Aayog (Prelims)

| Index | Subject |
|---|---|
| **SDG India Index** | State progress on the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (first 2018) |
| **National Multidimensional Poverty Index** | Poverty in health, education, living standards (with OPHI, UNDP) |
| **Composite Water Management Index** | Water management by states |
| **School Education Quality Index** | School education |
| **State Health Index** | Health outcomes (with MoHFW, World Bank) |
| **India Innovation Index** | Innovation capacity of states |
| **Export Preparedness Index** | States' export readiness |
| **Fiscal Health Index** | State finances (first released 2025) |

Note: some indices are published only once or irregularly. Always mention the year (check the latest).

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar usually ranks **at or near the bottom** of the SDG India Index and the national MPI, though it showed the **largest absolute fall in multidimensional poverty** (NITI Aayog's 2023 and 2024 reports). **13 of Bihar's districts** are in the Aspirational Districts list: **Araria, Aurangabad, Banka, Begusarai, Gaya, Jamui, Katihar, Khagaria, Muzaffarpur, Nawada, Purnia, Sheikhpura, Sitamarhi**. This is one of the highest numbers for any state (Jharkhand also has many). Use this in any Mains answer on regional disparity.

- **Damodar Valley Corporation (1948)** — India's first multipurpose river-valley project, modelled on the **Tennessee Valley Authority (USA)**; the Damodar area is now mostly in Jharkhand and West Bengal.
- **Kosi Project** — agreement with Nepal (**1954**); Kosi barrage at **Bhimnagar** (Nepal side) for flood control and irrigation.
- **Gandak Project** — agreement with Nepal (**1959**); barrage at **Valmikinagar**.
- **Barauni Oil Refinery** (Begusarai) — built with **Soviet** help, commissioned in **1964**; Barauni also got a fertiliser plant and a thermal power station.
- **Bokaro Steel Plant** — Soviet help, began production in the 1970s; now in **Jharkhand**.
- **Freight Equalisation Policy (1952–1993)** — the Centre made the transport cost of minerals like coal and iron ore the **same everywhere in India**. Factories therefore had no reason to locate near the mines of Bihar (then including Jharkhand). Bihar lost its natural advantage. This is a classic Mains point.
- **Bihar's bifurcation (15 November 2000)** — Jharkhand took most mines, heavy industry and forests; Bihar was left with the flood-prone Gangetic plain.
- **Special Category Status** — Bihar has long demanded it; the Centre has not granted it (the 14th Finance Commission ended the distinction for devolution purposes). Mention it as a political-economic debate.
- **Backward Regions Grant Fund (BRGF, 2007)** and a **Bihar special plan** (~₹1,000 crore per year in the 11th Plan, under BRGF) — supported roads, power and irrigation.

> **Mains angle:** For "Has planning reduced regional disparities?" — Yes partly (backward area programmes, Gadgil formula favouring poor states, BRGF, Aspirational Districts), but the pattern of investment (Mahalanobis heavy-industry focus, freight equalisation, private investment going to coastal states) kept states like Bihar behind. Recommend: targeted investment, better devolution formulas, state capacity building.

## Bihar's own planning set-up

- Bihar has a **State Planning Board** (advisory) and earlier prepared **annual plans** in line with national plans.
- After 2015 Bihar, like other states, moved to a vision-and-scheme approach. Its flagship programmes — **Saat Nischay** (Seven Resolves) I and II, **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali** — act as the state's development agenda. See [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar).
- District-level planning under the **73rd and 74th Amendments**: **District Planning Committees** (Article **243ZD**) consolidate panchayat and municipal plans.

### Mains questions

**1. Compare the Planning Commission with NITI Aayog. Has the shift strengthened cooperative federalism? (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** 1950 → 2015 shift; both extra-constitutional.
- **Body:**
  1. Role: allocator vs think tank.
  2. Top-down vs bottom-up planning.
  3. States' voice: NDC vs Governing Council; regional councils.
  4. Competitive federalism: indices (SDG, health, water), Aspirational Districts.
  5. Funds now via Finance Ministry and Finance Commission — critics say states lost a forum to bargain for funds.
  6. Limitations: advisory only, Governing Council meets rarely, centrally sponsored schemes still decided by the Centre.
  7. Bihar view: ranks low on indices; needs funds, not only rankings; demand for special category status.
- **Conclusion:** NITI has improved dialogue and data-based governance; to deepen federalism, give it a stronger role in scheme design and regular CM consultations.

**2. "Planning in India could not remove regional imbalances." Examine with reference to Bihar. (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** Balanced regional development was a goal from the 2nd Plan.
- **Body:** Mahalanobis focus on heavy industry in mineral belts (now Jharkhand); freight equalisation; private investment in coastal states; Green Revolution limited to Punjab–Haryana–west UP; Bihar's floods and weak irrigation; Gadgil formula and BRGF as corrective steps; bifurcation 2000; Aspirational Districts; current growth catch-up.
- **Conclusion:** Disparities narrowed only partly; need a place-based strategy — agro-industry, infrastructure, human capital, fiscal transfers.

**3. Explain the Aspirational Districts Programme. How can it help Bihar? (15 marks / 200 words)**

Outline: launch 2018; 3 Cs; 49 indicators, 5 themes with weights; real-time ranking (Champions of Change dashboard); 13 Bihar districts; gains in health and nutrition; suggestions — focus on learning outcomes, female workforce, irrigation.
