Schemes are one of the most frequent sources of BPSC Prelims questions ("Which ministry runs…", "When was … launched", "What is the benefit under …"). Mains asks you to evaluate schemes and to show how Bihar has used them. This chapter gives a stable core of facts for each flagship scheme. **Scheme names, targets and amounts change often — every scheme here is "as of 2026, check the latest"**, and you should keep a tracker using [National Schemes and Missions Tracker](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/national-schemes-and-missions-tracker).

> **In simple words:** The social sector means health, education, nutrition, water, sanitation, housing and social security — the things that build "human capital". The Centre runs flagship schemes in each area, often sharing costs with states. Bihar has used these schemes and added its own (like bicycles for schoolgirls and the Saat Nischay programme) to improve schooling, electricity, water and roads.

## Why the social sector matters

- **Human capital** — healthy and educated workers are more productive (**Amartya Sen's "capability approach"**).
- **Demographic dividend** — India's working-age population is large; the dividend comes only if young people are healthy and skilled.
- **Constitutional basis** — DPSPs: **Article 41** (right to work, education, public assistance), **Article 42** (maternity relief), **Article 45** (early childhood care), **Article 47** (nutrition and public health); **Article 21A** (free and compulsory education 6–14 years, added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**).
- **Spending**: India's combined (Centre + states) social-services spending is about **7–8% of GDP**; public health spending about **1.5–2% of GDP** (target **2.5%** by **2025** in the **National Health Policy 2017**); education spending about **4–4.5%** (target **6%** in **NEP 2020**) (check the latest Economic Survey).

> **Mains angle:** Use the phrase "**From welfare to empowerment**" — schemes now use **DBT, Aadhaar and mobile (JAM)**, focus on **outcomes** (learning levels, stunting), and target **women** as beneficiaries (Ujjwala in women's names, PMAY houses co-owned by women).

## Health

| Scheme | Launch | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **National Health Mission (NHM)** | NRHM **2005**, NUHM **2013**, merged as NHM **2013** | **ASHA** worker in every village (1 per 1,000 people), Janani Suraksha Yojana, Rogi Kalyan Samitis |
| **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)** | **2005** | Cash for **institutional delivery** (higher in low-performing states like **Bihar**) |
| **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK)** | 2011 | Free delivery, C-section, drugs, transport |
| **Mission Indradhanush** | **December 2014** | Full immunisation of children and pregnant women; **U-WIN** digital vaccine register (2024) |
| **Ayushman Bharat** | **2018** | Two parts: (1) **Ayushman Arogya Mandir** (earlier Health and Wellness Centres) — 1.5 lakh+ primary care centres; (2) **PM-JAY** |
| **PM-JAY** (Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana) | **23 September 2018**, from **Ranchi** | Health cover **₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary and tertiary hospital care; cashless; beneficiaries from **SECC 2011** (about 12 crore families); extended to **all citizens aged 70+** regardless of income (**Ayushman Vay Vandana**, October 2024) |
| **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)** | **September 2021** | **ABHA** (14-digit health ID), digital health records |
| **PM-ABHIM** | 2021 | Health infrastructure — critical care blocks, labs |
| **National TB Elimination Programme** | — | "TB Mukt Bharat" target 2025 (ahead of SDG 2030); Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana — nutrition support to TB patients |
| **Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana** | 2008 (renamed 2015/16) | Cheap generic medicines through Jan Aushadhi Kendras |
| **PM Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan** | 2016 | Free check-up on the **9th of every month** for pregnant women |

Key health indicators (NFHS-5, 2019-21 — the NFHS-6 results should be checked when released):

| Indicator | India | Bihar |
|---|---|---|
| Total Fertility Rate (TFR) | **2.0** (below replacement 2.1) | **about 3.0** (highest among states) |
| Stunting (children under 5) | 35.5% | about 43% |
| Institutional births | 88.6% | about 76% |
| Women aged 15–49 anaemic | 57% | about 63% |

Other indicators from SRS (Sample Registration System): India's **IMR** about **26–28 per 1,000** live births and **MMR** about **93–97 per lakh** live births in recent SRS bulletins; Bihar's IMR is close to the national average and MMR somewhat higher (check the latest SRS).

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's health system has a big shortage of doctors and nurses and depends heavily on **ASHA** workers and **JEEViKA** SHGs for outreach. New AIIMS: **AIIMS Patna** (2012) and **AIIMS Darbhanga** (approved 2020, under construction — check the latest). Bihar runs the **Mukhyamantri Chikitsa Sahayata Kosh** for treatment of serious diseases. Bihar has a high burden of **kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis) in north Bihar districts and of **Acute Encephalitis Syndrome (AES)** in the Muzaffarpur region, though both have declined sharply.

## Education

| Scheme / law | Year | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **RTE Act** | **2009** (in force **1 April 2010**) | Free and compulsory education for **6–14** years; **25%** reservation in private unaided schools for weaker sections; pupil–teacher ratio norms; no detention policy (changed in **2019** — states may hold back students in classes 5 and 8) |
| **Samagra Shiksha** | **2018** | Merged **SSA** (2001), **RMSA** (2009) and Teacher Education; pre-school to class 12 |
| **National Education Policy (NEP)** | **29 July 2020** | Drafted by the **K. Kasturirangan** committee; **5+3+3+4** structure; mother tongue as medium up to class 5 where possible; **GER 50%** in higher education by **2035**; **6% of GDP** public spending; four-year undergraduate programme with multiple exits; **NCF** frameworks |
| **NIPUN Bharat** | **2021** | Foundational literacy and numeracy by class 3 by 2026-27 |
| **PM SHRI schools** | **2022** | 14,500+ model schools implementing NEP |
| **PM-USHA** | 2023 | Higher education (earlier RUSA, 2013) |
| **PM Vidyalaxmi** | 2024 | Collateral-free education loans for students in top institutions |
| **Data sources** | — | **UDISE+** (school data), **AISHE** (higher education), **ASER** (Pratham's learning survey), **PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan** (earlier NAS) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's school schemes are famous examples of **demand-side** incentives:
> - **Mukhyamantri Balika Cycle Yojana (2006)** — bicycles for girls entering class 9 (extended to boys later); studies credited it with a big rise in girls' secondary enrolment.
> - **Mukhyamantri Balika Poshak Yojana** — school uniform money for girls.
> - **Mukhyamantri Kanya Utthan Yojana (2018)** — cash at different stages from birth to graduation (₹50,000 on graduation as revised — check the latest).
> - **Bihar Student Credit Card Scheme (2016)** — education loans up to ₹4 lakh at low interest (part of Saat Nischay).
> - Large-scale teacher recruitment through the **BPSC Teacher Recruitment Exams (TRE)** from **2023**.
> Bihar's literacy rate was **61.8%** in Census 2011 — the **lowest** among states (female literacy about 51.5%).

## Sanitation, water, energy and housing

| Scheme | Launch | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Swachh Bharat Mission (Gramin & Urban)** | **2 October 2014** | Toilets; rural India declared **ODF** on **2 October 2019**; **SBM-G Phase II** (2020-25): **ODF Plus** — solid and liquid waste management; SBM-U 2.0 (2021): garbage-free cities |
| **Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM) — Har Ghar Jal** | **15 August 2019** | Functional household **tap connection** (55 litres per person per day) to every rural home; extended to **2028** (Budget 2025-26); **Pani Samitis** of the village |
| **AMRUT** / **AMRUT 2.0** | 2015 / 2021 | Urban water supply and sewerage |
| **PM Ujjwala Yojana** | **1 May 2016**, **Ballia** (UP) | Free **LPG connection** in the name of the **woman** of a poor household; Ujjwala 2.0 (2021) for migrants |
| **Saubhagya** | **2017** | Electricity connection to all households |
| **PM Awas Yojana — Gramin** | **2016** (from Indira Awaas Yojana, 1985) | Pucca house; ₹1.2 lakh in plains (₹1.3 lakh in hilly areas); house in the name of woman or jointly; PMAY-G extended with 2 crore more houses (2024–29) |
| **PM Awas Yojana — Urban** | **25 June 2015** | Housing for urban poor; **PMAY-U 2.0** (2024) for 1 crore houses |
| **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** | **2024** | Rooftop solar for 1 crore homes; up to 300 units free per month |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's **Saat Nischay** (Seven Resolves, **2015**) ran in parallel with central schemes:
> 1. **Arthik Hal, Yuvaon ko Bal** (Student Credit Card, Self-Help Allowance, Kushal Yuva Programme)
> 2. **Aarakshit Rozgar, Mahilaon ka Adhikar** (**35%** reservation for women in all state government jobs, 2016)
> 3. **Har Ghar Bijli** (electricity to every household)
> 4. **Har Ghar Nal ka Jal** (piped water — Bihar's own version before JJM)
> 5. **Ghar tak Pakki Gali-Naliyan** (paved lanes and drains)
> 6. **Shauchalay Nirman, Ghar ka Samman** (toilets)
> 7. **Avsar Badhe, Aage Padhe** (new colleges, ITIs, polytechnics, nursing and medical colleges)
>
> **Saat Nischay Part 2 (2020)** focused on youth skills and jobs, women's entrepreneurship, irrigation to every field, clean villages, street lights (solar), better health and animal care. **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan** (**2 October 2019**) covers ponds, wells, check dams, rainwater harvesting and tree plantation. For full details and the latest phase, see [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar).

## How to remember schemes: a method

1. **Launch year and place** — PM-JAY (2018, Ranchi), Ujjwala (2016, Ballia), BBBP (2015, Panipat), POSHAN (2018, Jhunjhunu).
2. **Ministry** — e.g. Ujjwala → Petroleum; JJM → Jal Shakti; PMAY-G → Rural Development; PM-KISAN → Agriculture; Saubhagya → Power.
3. **Target / benefit amount** — PM-JAY ₹5 lakh; PM-KISAN ₹6,000; PMMVY ₹5,000; APY ₹1,000–5,000.
4. **Type** — central sector (100% Centre) or centrally sponsored (shared, usually 60:40).
5. **Bihar's version** — Har Ghar Nal ka Jal (before JJM), Bihar Rajya Fasal Sahayata (instead of PMFBY), JEEViKA (before NRLM).

> **Memory trick:** "**PURB**" for launch places: **P**anipat (BBBP), **U**P-Ballia (Ujjwala), **R**anchi (PM-JAY), **B**andlapalli-Anantapur (NREGA). "PURB" = east, like Bihar.

> **Common mistake:** "Ayushman Bharat" is the umbrella; **PM-JAY** is its insurance part. "Swachh Bharat" started on **2 October 2014**, but rural India was declared **ODF on 2 October 2019** — two different dates.

## Evaluating schemes (Mains framework)

When asked to "evaluate" a scheme, use this checklist:

| Dimension | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | How many reached? Who is left out (migrants, sharecroppers, urban poor)? |
| **Outcomes** | Did health/learning/income improve, not just spending? |
| **Quality** | Toilets used? Taps working? Teachers present? |
| **Leakages** | DBT and Aadhaar reduced fake beneficiaries? |
| **Federal issues** | State share burden (60:40) on poor states like Bihar |
| **Gender** | Women as owners/beneficiaries |
| **Sustainability** | Maintenance (O&M) after construction |
| **Data** | NFHS, PLFS, UDISE+, ASER, CAG audits |

### Mains questions

**1. Evaluate the impact of Bihar's girl-centric schemes on education and women's empowerment. (38 marks)**

Outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar's low female literacy (2011) and the cycle scheme (2006).
- **Body:** cycle and uniform schemes — enrolment and mobility; Kanya Utthan — delayed marriage, graduation; 50% panchayat reservation — political voice; 35% job reservation — women police and teachers; JEEViKA — income and confidence; prohibition — women's demand; outcomes — NFHS data on early marriage, fertility; challenges — learning levels (ASER), dropouts after class 10, female LFPR still low.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's model shows that incentives plus representation can shift norms; next step is quality education and jobs.

**2. "Health insurance alone cannot ensure universal health coverage." Discuss with reference to PM-JAY and Bihar. (38 marks)**

Outline: PM-JAY features and reach; Bihar's low card enrolment and hospital availability; out-of-pocket spending; need for primary care (Arogya Mandirs), doctors, district hospitals, AIIMS Darbhanga; NHM and ASHA; Kala-azar and AES success; conclusion — insurance + public provision.

**3. Assess the role of the Jal Jeevan Mission and Bihar's Har Ghar Nal ka Jal in improving rural life. (15 marks)**

Outline: targets; women's time saved; health (water-borne disease, arsenic and fluoride in Bihar's groundwater); O&M challenges; water quality testing; Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali for sources; conclusion.
