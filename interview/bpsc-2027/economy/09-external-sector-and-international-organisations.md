The external sector is India's economic relationship with the rest of the world: trade, investment, remittances and the rupee's value. Prelims asks about the parts of the balance of payments, current and capital account convertibility, FDI routes, WTO boxes, and the headquarters and reports of the IMF and World Bank. Mains GS II asks about the current account deficit, rupee depreciation, FTAs and how global trade affects Bihar.

> **In simple words:** The balance of payments is a country's yearly account of all money coming in from and going out to other countries. If India buys more goods than it sells, it has a trade deficit. That gap is covered partly by software exports and money sent home by Indians abroad, and partly by foreign investment. The exchange rate is the price of the rupee in dollars. The WTO makes rules for world trade; the IMF helps countries in currency crises; the World Bank lends for development.

## Balance of Payments (BoP)

The **Balance of Payments** is a systematic record of all **economic transactions** between the **residents** of a country and the **rest of the world** in a year. In India the RBI compiles it (quarterly).

### Structure

| Account | Main items |
|---|---|
| **Current account** | (a) **Merchandise trade** — exports and imports of goods → **trade balance**; (b) **Invisibles** — **services** (IT, travel, transport, banking), **primary income** (interest, dividends, profits, wages), **secondary income / transfers** (**remittances**, gifts, grants) |
| **Capital account** (and financial account) | **FDI**, **FPI** (portfolio investment in shares and bonds), **external commercial borrowings (ECBs)**, external assistance, **NRI deposits**, short-term trade credit, banking capital |
| **Errors and omissions** | Statistical difference |
| **Change in forex reserves** | Balancing item (RBI adds to or uses reserves) |

**Overall BoP = Current account balance + Capital account balance + Errors & omissions**. A BoP surplus adds to forex reserves; a deficit is met by using reserves (or IMF help).

> **Prelims angle:** **Remittances** come under the **current account** (secondary income), not the capital account. **FDI, FPI, external loans and NRI deposits** are **capital/financial account** items. "Invisibles" = services + income + transfers.

### Worked example: BoP of a year (US$ billion)

| Item | Inflow | Outflow | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merchandise exports / imports | 440 | 720 | **−280 (trade deficit)** |
| Services | 390 | 200 | +190 |
| Primary income | 30 | 80 | −50 |
| Transfers (mostly remittances) | 135 | 15 | +120 |
| **Current account balance** | | | **−280 + 190 − 50 + 120 = −20** |
| FDI (net) | | | +10 |
| FPI (net) | | | +15 |
| Loans, NRI deposits, others (net) | | | +20 |
| **Capital account balance** | | | **+45** |
| Errors and omissions | | | −2 |
| **Overall balance → addition to reserves** | | | **+23** |

If GDP = $4,000 billion, CAD = 20 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **0.5% of GDP**.

Lesson: India runs a **large trade deficit** (oil, gold, electronics imports), which is largely covered by **services exports** and **remittances**. The remaining **CAD** is financed by capital inflows. A CAD of **up to about 2–2.5% of GDP** is usually considered safe for India.

> **Remember:** India is the **world's largest recipient of remittances** — over **$130 billion** a year in recent years (check the latest RBI/World Bank data). Gulf countries, the USA and the UK are the main sources.

### The 1991 BoP crisis (Mains background)

- Causes: high fiscal deficits, Gulf war oil-price jump (1990), fall in remittances from Kuwait, political instability, NRIs withdrawing deposits.
- Forex reserves fell to barely **2–3 weeks of imports** (about $1 billion).
- India **pledged gold** (about **47 tonnes** with the Bank of England and Bank of Japan) and took an **IMF loan**.
- Response: **devaluation** of the rupee (July 1991, about 18–19% in two steps) and the **LPG reforms**.

## Exchange rate

The **exchange rate** is the price of one currency in terms of another (e.g. ₹88 = $1 means one dollar costs ₹88).

| System | Meaning | India |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed / pegged** | Government fixes the rate | 1947–1971 (pegged to pound sterling), to a basket from 1975 |
| **Floating** | Market demand and supply decide | — |
| **Managed float** | Market decides, but the central bank intervenes to reduce volatility | **India since March 1993** |

### Devaluation vs depreciation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Devaluation** | Government/central bank **officially lowers** the value in a fixed system — India: **1949, 1966, 1991** |
| **Depreciation** | Value **falls due to market forces** in a floating system |
| **Revaluation / Appreciation** | Opposite of the above |

### Worked example: depreciation and trade

The rupee moves from ₹80/$ to ₹88/$ (depreciation of 10%).

- An Indian rice exporter selling at $500/tonne earned ₹40,000; now earns **₹44,000** → **exports become more attractive**.
- A refinery importing oil at $80/barrel paid ₹6,400; now pays **₹7,040** → **imports costlier**, petrol prices and **inflation** may rise.
- A student paying $20,000 fees abroad now needs ₹17.6 lakh instead of ₹16 lakh.

So depreciation helps exporters and remittance-receiving families (Bihar's Gulf migrants' families get more rupees), but hurts importers and raises inflation.

### Steps in India's exchange-rate reform

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| **1992** | **LERMS** (Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System) — dual rate |
| **March 1993** | Unified market-determined exchange rate |
| **August 1994** | **Full current account convertibility** (India accepted **Article VIII** of the IMF) |
| 1997, 2006 | **Tarapore Committees I and II** on **capital account convertibility** — India has only **partial** capital account convertibility |
| 1999 | **FEMA** replaced **FERA** |
| 2010 | Rupee symbol **₹** adopted (designed by **D. Udaya Kumar**) |
| 2022 onwards | RBI framework for **international trade settlement in rupees** (rupee Vostro accounts) |

- **Current account convertibility** = freely convert rupees into foreign currency for trade, travel, education, remittances.
- **Capital account convertibility** = freely convert for buying foreign assets (property, shares, loans). India allows it **partially**; individuals can send up to **$2,50,000** a year under the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**.

### Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) and NEER

- **NEER** = weighted average of the rupee against a basket of trading partners' currencies.
- **REER** = NEER adjusted for **inflation differences**. A REER above 100 means the rupee is **overvalued** (hurts exports).

## Foreign trade

- India's merchandise exports are about **$430–450 billion** a year and imports about **$700 billion+**; services exports are about **$380–400 billion** (recent years — check the latest).
- **Top export items**: petroleum products, engineering goods, gems and jewellery, **electronics (smartphones)**, pharmaceuticals, rice, textiles.
- **Top imports**: **crude oil**, electronics, **gold**, coal, edible oil, machinery.
- **Largest trading partner** (total trade): **USA** in recent years (China close behind); **largest export destination: USA**; **largest import source: China** (check the latest).
- In **2025** the **USA imposed high tariffs** on many Indian goods; trade talks for a bilateral trade agreement were under way (as of 2026 — check the latest status).

### Trade policy

- **Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023** — effective **1 April 2023**, with **no end date** (dynamic, updated as needed); aims for **$2 trillion exports by 2030**; promotes rupee trade, e-commerce exports, "**Districts as Export Hubs**".
- **Special Economic Zones (SEZs)** — **SEZ Act, 2005**. Asia's **first Export Processing Zone** was **Kandla (1965)**, Gujarat.
- **Export promotion**: **RoDTEP** (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Export Products, 2021), EPCG, Advance Authorisation.
- **Anti-dumping duty** (DGTR), **countervailing duty** and **safeguard duty** to protect domestic industry.

### Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) — recent ones

| Agreement | Year |
|---|---|
| **SAFTA** (South Asia) | 2004 (in force 2006) |
| India–ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement | 2009 (review under way) |
| India–Japan CEPA, India–Korea CEPA | 2011, 2009 |
| **India–UAE CEPA** | **2022** |
| **India–Australia ECTA** | **2022** |
| **India–EFTA TEPA** (Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) | Signed **March 2024**, in force **October 2025**; $100 billion investment commitment |
| **India–UK CETA** | Signed **July 2025** (check entry into force) |
| India–EU FTA | Negotiations concluded/under way (check the latest) |

India **did not join RCEP** (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership) in **2019** — fear of Chinese imports flooding Indian markets.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar is **landlocked** and its exports are small (a few thousand crore rupees — check the latest). Export items include **makhana, litchi, mango, rice, maize, Bhagalpur silk, leather goods and Madhubani art products**. The **Raxaul–Birgunj Integrated Check Post (ICP, 2016)** and **Jogbani–Biratnagar ICP (2020)** on the **Nepal border** make Bihar the main gateway for India–Nepal trade. **Remittances** from Gulf migrants (notably from **Siwan, Gopalganj, Saran** districts) and from inter-state migrants support rural Bihar's consumption.

## Foreign investment

| Feature | FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) | FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | **Long-term**, management interest | **Short-term**, financial returns |
| Stake | **10% or more** of a listed company's equity | **Less than 10%** |
| Stability | Stable — "patient capital" | Volatile — "**hot money**" |
| Brings | Technology, jobs, management | Liquidity in stock markets |
| Regulator | DPIIT policy; RBI under FEMA | **SEBI** (FPIs register with SEBI) |

The **10% line** between FDI and FPI was set on the **Arvind Mayaram Committee** recommendation (2014).

### FDI routes

- **Automatic route** — no prior government approval needed (most sectors, e.g. **100%** in manufacturing, single-brand retail, e-commerce marketplace, insurance — raised to **100%** in Budget 2025-26, check the latest).
- **Government route** — prior approval needed (e.g. multi-brand retail **51%**, print media 26%, defence beyond 74%).
- **Prohibited**: lottery, gambling, chit funds, Nidhi companies, real estate business (trading), tobacco cigars/cigarettes manufacture, atomic energy, railway operations (except permitted activities).
- **FIPB** (Foreign Investment Promotion Board) was **abolished in 2017**; approvals now by the ministries concerned.
- **Press Note 3 (2020)** — FDI from **countries sharing land borders** with India (e.g. China) needs **government approval**.
- **Top FDI sources**: **Mauritius, Singapore, USA**, Netherlands, Japan (Mauritius and Singapore due to tax treaties).
- **Top states receiving FDI**: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat, Delhi, Tamil Nadu. Bihar receives very little FDI.

### Other external flows

- **ECB (External Commercial Borrowing)** — loans by Indian companies from abroad.
- **Masala bonds** — rupee-denominated bonds issued abroad (first by **IFC** in 2014; first Indian issuer **HDFC**, 2016).
- **ADR / GDR** — depository receipts to list Indian shares abroad.
- **Participatory Notes (P-Notes)** — offshore derivative instruments.

## Foreign exchange reserves

- India's forex reserves are **among the top five in the world**; they reached a record of over **$700 billion** and were about **$726 billion** in **October 2026** (RBI weekly data — check the latest).
- Components: **Foreign Currency Assets (largest)**, **gold**, **SDRs**, **Reserve Tranche Position with the IMF**.
- Reserves give confidence, cover about **10–11 months** of imports, and let the RBI reduce rupee volatility.

## The WTO and world trade

### From GATT to WTO

- **GATT** (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade) — **1947**, India a founder member.
- **Uruguay Round (1986–94)** → **Marrakesh Agreement (April 1994)** → **WTO** began on **1 January 1995**.
- **HQ: Geneva, Switzerland.** Members: **166** (after Comoros and Timor-Leste joined in 2024).
- Highest body: **Ministerial Conference** (every two years). **MC12** Geneva (2022), **MC13** Abu Dhabi (February 2024), **MC14** Yaoundé, Cameroon (March 2026) (check outcomes).
- **Director-General**: **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala** (Nigeria), first woman and first African DG; second term from 2025 (check the latest).
- **Dispute Settlement Body**; the **Appellate Body** has been **non-functional since December 2019** because the USA blocked appointments.
- **Principles**: **Most Favoured Nation (MFN)** — no discrimination among members; **National Treatment** — treat imported goods like domestic goods once inside.

### Main WTO agreements

| Agreement | Subject |
|---|---|
| **AoA** (Agreement on Agriculture) | Market access, domestic support, export subsidies |
| **TRIPS** | Intellectual property — patents, copyright, **GI** |
| **TRIMS** | Investment measures that affect trade (e.g. local content rules) |
| **GATS** | Trade in services (four modes; Mode 4 = movement of persons) |
| **SPS / TBT** | Food safety and technical standards |
| **Trade Facilitation Agreement** | 2017; faster customs |

### Agriculture "boxes" (domestic support)

| Box | Meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Amber box** | Trade-distorting support — **MSP**, input subsidies | Limited by **de minimis**: **10%** of production value for developing countries (**5%** for developed) |
| **Blue box** | Amber support with production limits | No limit |
| **Green box** | Minimal distortion — research, extension, food stocks for development, direct income support not linked to production | No limit |
| **Development box** (Article 6.2) | Input subsidies to low-income farmers in developing countries | Exempt |

- **Peace Clause (Bali, MC9, 2013)** — members will not challenge India's **public stockholding for food security** even if it breaches the amber box limit, until a permanent solution is found. India has invoked it for rice.

> **Mains angle:** India's MSP and NFSA procurement face WTO challenges (amber box). India argues for a **permanent solution** for public stockholding, and against cuts in fisheries subsidies for small fishermen. Link to food security of poor states like Bihar.

## IMF, World Bank and other bodies

### Bretton Woods institutions

Both were created at the **Bretton Woods Conference (July 1944, New Hampshire, USA)**; both have their **HQ in Washington, D.C.** India is a **founder member**.

| Point | IMF | World Bank |
|---|---|---|
| Main role | **Short-term** help for **balance-of-payments** problems; global monetary stability; surveillance | **Long-term** loans for **development** and poverty reduction |
| Head (by convention) | European (MD: **Kristalina Georgieva** — check the latest) | American (President: **Ajay Banga** — check the latest) |
| Special tool | **SDR (Special Drawing Rights, 1969)** — basket of **5 currencies**: US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi, Japanese yen, pound sterling | — |
| Main reports | **World Economic Outlook**, Global Financial Stability Report, Fiscal Monitor | **World Development Report**, Global Economic Prospects, Poverty & Equity briefs, (Doing Business — discontinued 2021) |
| Quotas | Decide voting and borrowing — India about **2.7%** | — |

### World Bank Group (five institutions)

| Institution | Year | Role | India member? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IBRD** | 1944 | Loans to middle-income countries | Yes |
| **IDA** | 1960 | Soft loans/grants to poorest countries | Yes |
| **IFC** | 1956 | Private-sector investment | Yes |
| **MIGA** | 1988 | Political risk insurance for investors | Yes |
| **ICSID** | 1966 | Settlement of investment disputes | **No** |

> **Memory trick:** "India is in **4 of 5** — not in **ICSID**." The IBRD + IDA together are called the "World Bank".

### Other important organisations

| Body | HQ | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asian Development Bank (ADB)** | **Manila** | 1966 | India a founder; Japan largest shareholder |
| **AIIB** (Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank) | **Beijing** | 2016 | China largest, **India second-largest** shareholder |
| **New Development Bank (NDB)** | **Shanghai** | 2015 | BRICS bank; first president **K.V. Kamath** (India) |
| **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** | **Basel** | 1930 | "Central bank of central banks"; Basel norms |
| **OECD** | Paris | 1961 | India not a member |
| **UNCTAD** | Geneva | 1964 | World Investment Report |
| **ILO** | Geneva | 1919 | World Employment and Social Outlook |
| **World Economic Forum** | Cologny/Geneva | 1971 | Global Gender Gap Report, Global Competitiveness |
| **UNDP** | New York | 1965 | Human Development Report |

For more groupings (G20, BRICS, SCO, BIMSTEC) and their India links, see [International Organisations and Groupings](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/international-organisations-and-groupings).

> **Bihar connection:** The **World Bank** has funded major Bihar projects: **JEEViKA** (Bihar Rural Livelihoods Project), **Bihar Kosi Flood Recovery Project** (after the **2008 Kusaha breach** of the Kosi embankment in Nepal), **Bihar Kosi Basin Development Project**, health and education projects. **ADB** has funded Bihar's state highways and the new **Ganga bridge** projects (e.g. Kacchi Dargah–Bidupur) (check the latest project list on the World Bank and ADB websites).

## Key terms

|---|---|
| Balance of payments | Record of all transactions with the rest of the world |
| Invisibles | Services, income and transfers (not goods) |
