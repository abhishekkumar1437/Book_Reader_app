This is your daily source of answer-writing questions from Day 29 to the exam. Questions are arranged by **syllabus line** of GS Paper I and II. Each has an **outline** — intro, body points, conclusion — so you can check your own answer. Questions with a full model answer elsewhere in this book link to it.

> **In simple words:** Pick one question a day, write it in the time limit, then compare with the outline. If your answer misses more than two outline points, read the content chapter again and rewrite.

**How to read this bank**

- All questions are **practice questions in BPSC style**, not claimed previous-year questions.
- ⭐ marks a **high-priority theme** — a topic central to the syllabus line or to Bihar, worth preparing first. It is not a claim about how often it has been asked.
- Word lengths: (S) = short, about 250–300 words; (L) = long, about 500–600 words.
- Facts that change (schemes, data, court cases) are marked "check latest".

## GS Paper I — Modern history of India

### 1857 and the 19th century

**H1. ⭐ Discuss the causes of the Revolt of 1857. Why did it fail?** (L)
*Outline:* Intro — 10 May 1857, Meerut; "first war of independence" debate. Body — political (Doctrine of Lapse, annexation of Awadh 1856); economic (land revenue, ruin of artisans); social-religious (missionaries, Widow Remarriage Act fears); military (greased cartridges, General Service Enlistment Act 1856); causes of failure — limited area, no unified leadership, lack of modern arms, loyal princes and South India, British control of communication. Bihar — Kunwar Singh. Conclusion — end of Company rule; Government of India Act 1858.

**H2. ⭐ Assess the role of Kunwar Singh in the Revolt of 1857.** (L)
*Outline:* see [Model answer 1](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/modern-history-and-culture-model-answers). Danapur 25 July 1857; Arrah; campaign to Azamgarh; 23 April 1858 victory; death 26 April 1858; strengths and limits.

**H3. Describe the Wahabi movement and its centre at Patna.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — inspired by Syed Ahmad of Rae Bareli; Patna as the main centre. Body — leaders Wilayat Ali and Inayat Ali; religious reform turned anti-British; network up to the North-West Frontier; British trials in the 1860s (Ambala and Patna); suppression by the 1870s. Conclusion — an early organised anti-British movement with Bihar as its base.

**H4. Examine the impact of the Permanent Settlement (1793) on Bihar's agrarian society.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — Cornwallis, 1793, revenue fixed with zamindars. Body — zamindars became landowners; rack-renting and illegal cesses on tenants; sub-infeudation; no investment in land; peasant indebtedness; famines and poverty; base of later Kisan Sabha struggles. Conclusion — long-term agrarian backwardness; abolished after independence (Bihar Land Reforms Act 1950).

**H5. Explain the "drain of wealth" theory and its significance for nationalism.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — Dadabhai Naoroji, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*. Body — forms of drain (home charges, salaries and pensions, profits, interest); R.C. Dutt's *Economic History of India*; effect on poverty and famines; demolished the myth of British benevolence. Conclusion — economic critique became the base of the national movement.

**H6. Examine the contribution of 19th-century socio-religious reform movements.** (L)
*Outline:* see [Model answer 2](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/modern-history-and-culture-model-answers).

Body — aim of self-government within the Empire; propaganda through papers and libraries; branches across India including Bihar towns; new generation of leaders; prepared ground for Gandhi; faded by 1918–19. **H16. Discuss the major tribal uprisings in the Bihar region in the 19th century.** (L)
*Outline:* Intro — tribal areas of the then Bihar region (now mostly Jharkhand). **H18. Discuss the role of Jayaprakash Narayan in the national movement.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — born 1902, Sitab Diara. Body — Congress Socialist Party (1934); Quit India — Hazaribagh escape, Azad Dasta; ideas of democratic socialism; post-1947 Bhoodan and the 1974 movement (one line). Conclusion — "Lok Nayak".

**H19. Describe the contribution of revolutionaries from Bihar to the freedom struggle.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — revolutionary stream alongside Gandhian methods. Body — **Muzaffarpur bomb case (30 April 1908)** by Khudiram Bose and Prafulla Chaki, aimed at Kingsford; **Yogendra Shukla** and **Baikunth Shukla** (hanged in 1934) linked with the HSRA; revolutionary networks in north Bihar; JP's Azad Dasta in 1942. Conclusion — courage and sacrifice inspired youth.

**H20. Discuss the role of women in the freedom struggle with examples from Bihar.** (S)

**H21. Examine the role of the press in the national movement, with reference to Bihar.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — press as the voice of nationalism. Body — national papers (Amrita Bazar Patrika, Kesari, The Hindu); Vernacular Press Act 1878; Bihar — *Bihar Bandhu* (1872 onward), *Bihar Times* (1894), *The Searchlight* (1918), *The Motherland* (1921); role in separate Bihar campaign and Gandhian movements; repression. Conclusion — press built public opinion and political awareness.

**H22. Explain Gandhi's constructive programme and its significance.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — work during non-agitation phases. Body — khadi and village industries, Hindu–Muslim unity, removal of untouchability (Harijan Sevak Sangh 1932), prohibition, basic education (Wardha 1937), women's uplift; Bihar — Sadaqat Ashram, Bihar Vidyapith, Champaran schools. Conclusion — kept the movement alive at the grassroots.

**H23. Assess the contribution of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — Congress president 1938, 1939; Forward Bloc. Body — escape 1941; Azad Hind government (1943); INA campaigns in the North-East; Rani of Jhansi Regiment; INA trials 1945–46 and public anger; naval mutiny 1946. Conclusion — hastened the end of British rule.

**H24. Discuss the events leading to the partition of India and Gandhi's work in Bihar during 1946–47.** (L)
*Outline:* Intro — Cabinet Mission 1946 and its failure. Body — Direct Action Day (August 1946); riots in Noakhali and then Bihar (late 1946); Gandhi's visit to Bihar in 1947 to restore peace; Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947); Indian Independence Act 1947. Conclusion — freedom came with partition and violence; Gandhi's peace mission showed moral leadership.

**H25. Examine the struggle for zamindari abolition in Bihar after independence.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — Kisan Sabha demand. Body — **Bihar Land Reforms Act 1950**; **Kameshwar Singh case** in Patna High Court; First Amendment and Ninth Schedule (1951); Bhoodan in Bihar (Vinoba Bhave); weak implementation of ceiling laws; D. Bandyopadhyay Land Reforms Commission (2006). Conclusion — zamindari ended, but land inequality remained.

**H26. Discuss the role of backward-caste and Dalit movements in Bihar's social history.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — caste and land in Bihar. Body — **Triveni Sangh** (1930s) of backward castes in Shahabad; Ambedkar's national work and its influence; Jagjivan Ram (born Chandwa, Bhojpur) — Depressed Classes League, later Union minister; Karpoori Thakur's reservation policy (1978, check details); social justice politics. Conclusion — democratisation of Bihar society.

**C7. ⭐ Discuss the Buddhist art and architecture of Bihar.** (L)
*Outline:* Intro — Bihar as the land of the Buddha's enlightenment. Body — **Mahabodhi Temple** (Bodh Gaya, UNESCO 2002); **Nalanda Mahavihara** (UNESCO 2016); Vikramshila; stupas at Vaishali and **Kesaria**; Mauryan rock-cut **Barabar caves** (Lomas Rishi); Mauryan polish — **Didarganj Yakshi** (Patna Museum / Bihar Museum); Pala bronzes and sculpture; Ashokan pillars (Lauriya Nandangarh, Vaishali). **C8. Assess Sher Shah Suri's contribution to administration and architecture.** (S)

**C9. Discuss Bihar's contribution to Indian classical music.** (S)
*Outline:* Intro — Bihar's courts and temples nurtured music. Body — **Dhrupad** traditions of **Bettiah** and **Darbhanga** gharanas; **Ustad Bismillah Khan** (born in Dumraon, Buxar; Bharat Ratna 2001); folk-classical forms (Chaiti, Kajari, Thumri links); Vidyapati's songs. **C10. "Heritage conservation is also economic development." Discuss with reference to Bihar.** (S)

## GS Paper I — Statistical analysis, graphs and diagrams

For each problem type, the "outline" is the method. Full worked examples are in [Chapter 06](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/statistics-graphs-and-diagrams) and [Chapter 07](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/statistics-practice-problems).

**S1. ⭐ Find the mean, median and mode of a grouped frequency distribution and comment on its shape.**
*Method:* class marks → Σfx/N; cf → median class → L + ((N/2 − cf)/f)h; modal class → L + ((f₁ − f₀)/(2f₁ − f₀ − f₂))h; compare mean, median, mode for skewness.

**S2. ⭐ Find the standard deviation of grouped data and the coefficient of variation.**
*Method:* step deviation u = (x − A)/h; σ = h√(Σfu²/N − (Σfu/N)²); CV = σ/x̄ × 100.

**S3. Compare the consistency of two series (players, crop yields, prices).**
*Method:* compute mean and SD of both → CV → lower CV is more consistent.

**S4. ⭐ Draw a pie chart from the given data.**
*Method:* angle = value/total × 360°; table with check row = 360°; draw clockwise from 12 o'clock; label percentages; title.

**S5. ⭐ Draw a bar diagram (simple/multiple/sub-divided) and interpret it.**
*Method:* scale from zero; equal widths and gaps; legend for multiple series; percentages for sub-divided bars; 2–3 line interpretation.

**S6. Draw a line graph of a time series and find growth rates.**
*Method:* time on x-axis; % change year-on-year; overall % change; CAGR if asked; comment on trend.

**S7. Draw a histogram, frequency polygon and ogive; find the median graphically.**
*Method:* touching bars; adjust unequal widths; polygon through mid-points; "less than" ogive on upper limits; read x at N/2.

**S8. Construct price index numbers (simple, Laspeyres, Paasche, Fisher).**
*Method:* tables of p₁q₀, p₀q₀, p₁q₁, p₀q₁; formulas; interpret as % change in cost of basket.

*Outline:* role of civil servants; neutrality (All India Services Conduct Rules); commitment to constitutional values; district administration (DM, SDO, BDO) in Bihar; reforms — Mission Karmayogi, lateral entry debate, 2nd ARC. Conclusion — neutral in politics, committed to people.

**P21. Write a note on the Gram Kachahari system of Bihar.** (S)
*Outline:* Bihar Panchayat Raj Act 2006; elected Sarpanch and Panch; Nyaya Mitra and Nyaya Sachiv; jurisdiction over small civil and criminal matters; benefits — cheap, quick, local justice; problems — training, enforcement, local power. Conclusion — a model of community justice.

**Ec9. ⭐ Evaluate the "Saat Nischay" programme of Bihar.** (L)

Body — the seven: support for youth (Student Credit Card, Kushal Yuva, self-help allowance), 35% reservation for women in government jobs, Har Ghar Bijli, Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal, paved lanes and drains, toilets, more colleges and institutions; achievements — near-universal electricity and tap connections (check latest); concerns — water quality, maintenance, job creation. Conclusion — strong on basic services; next step is livelihoods.

**Ec10. Discuss the role of infrastructure in Bihar's development.** (S)

**Ec11. Examine the state of financial inclusion and credit in Bihar.** (S)
*Outline:* Jan Dhan; SHG bank linkage (Jeevika); low credit–deposit ratio in Bihar (savings deposited in Bihar are lent elsewhere — check latest); causes; measures — priority sector lending, Kisan Credit Card, banking correspondents. Conclusion — more credit for local investment.

**Ec12. Discuss land reforms in Bihar and their limitations.** (S)
*Outline:* zamindari abolition 1950; ceiling laws; tenancy reform weak (no Operation Barga-type registration); Bhoodan; D. Bandyopadhyay Commission (2006) recommendations; land survey and digitisation (check latest). Conclusion — land records and tenancy security still needed.

**Ec13. Analyse the fiscal health of the Bihar government.** (S)

**Ec14. Discuss the potential of dairy and fisheries in Bihar.** (S)

*Outline:* Intro — area 94,163 sq km, landlocked. Body — (1) **Shivalik/Someshwar hills** in the north-west (West Champaran); (2) **North Ganga plain** — Himalayan rivers, floods, tal and chaur lands; (3) **South Ganga plain** — Son, Punpun, Phalgu; (4) **Southern plateau fringe** — hills of Rajgir, Kharagpur, Kaimur. **G6. Describe the soils of Bihar and their agricultural significance.** (S)
*Outline:* alluvial (khadar — new, bangar — old); **tal** lands (Mokama, Barahiya — waterlogged, rabi pulses); **diara** lands (river islands); **chaur** (low-lying wetlands); red and yellow soils in the south; balsundari soils. Conclusion — soil-specific crop planning.

**G7. Discuss the importance of wetlands in Bihar.** (S)

Conclusion — wetlands are both ecology and economy.

**G8. Examine Bihar's vulnerability to earthquakes.** (S)
*Outline:* Himalayan proximity; seismic zones IV and V; **1934 Bihar–Nepal earthquake** (15 January 1934); 1988 earthquake; soft alluvium increases shaking; measures — building codes, retrofitting schools and hospitals, drills. Conclusion — preparedness saves lives.

**G9. Discuss forests and wildlife conservation in Bihar.** (S)
*Outline:* low forest cover (check the latest India State of Forest Report); **Valmiki Tiger Reserve** (West Champaran); sanctuaries — Kaimur, Bhimbandh, Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary; Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali tree planting; human–wildlife conflict; agroforestry. Conclusion — increase green cover outside forests.

**G10. Describe the mineral resources of Bihar after the separation of Jharkhand.** (S)
*Outline:* most minerals went to Jharkhand (2000); remaining — limestone (Rohtas, Kaimur), pyrite (Amjhore, Rohtas), mica belt (Nawada–Gaya region), quartzite, sand; recent exploration of gold and other minerals in Jamui (check latest). Conclusion — limited minerals; focus on agro-industry.
