The BPSC syllabus keeps saying "with special reference to Bihar". This chapter shows how to add a Bihar angle naturally (not forced), which facts and numbers are safe to quote, and which diagrams lift an answer. It ends with a ready Bihar data bank you should revise every week.

> **In simple words:** Every answer should have three value-adds where they fit: **one Bihar link**, **one correct number or name**, and **one simple diagram or table**. Each costs two to three lines but shows depth.

## Why the Bihar angle matters

1. **The syllabus asks for it.** GS II Polity is "with Bihar focus"; Economy and Geography include Bihar's economy and geography; S&T asks about the development of India **and Bihar**; GS I History includes Bihar's role in the freedom struggle.
2. **It separates you from other candidates.** Most candidates write general India-level answers. A specific Bihar point stands out.
3. **The interview continues it.** Board members often ask about your district and Bihar's problems. The facts you collect for Mains help there too.

> **Common mistake:** Forcing Bihar into every line ("Bihar is great…"). One clear paragraph or two to three precise Bihar points is enough. If the question is purely international (for example, about a global treaty), a single line on how it affects Bihar is enough — or none if it does not fit.

## Five ways to add Bihar

| Way | Where to put it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. A Bihar sub-heading** | When the question asks "India and Bihar" | "Bihar's role in the Quit India Movement" as a separate block |
| **2. A Bihar example inside a point** | Any India-level answer | "Decentralisation works best with women's participation — Bihar's 50% reservation in panchayats (2006) is an example." |
| **3. A Bihar data line** | Economy, geography, social questions | "Bihar's sex ratio was 918 in Census 2011." |
| **4. A Bihar scheme or law** | Governance, welfare, S&T | Bihar Right to Public Services Act 2011; Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali; Jeevika |
| **5. A Bihar way-forward point** | Conclusion of problem questions | "For Bihar, flood-resilient agriculture and Kosi basin cooperation with Nepal are priorities." |

### Turning an India answer into a Bihar-rich answer

**Practice question:** *Discuss the role of self-help groups (SHGs) in women's empowerment.*

| India-level point | Bihar addition |
|---|---|
| SHGs give women access to savings and credit | **Jeevika** (Bihar Rural Livelihoods Promotion Society), started in 2006–07 with World Bank support, organises rural women into SHGs |
| SHGs build leadership | Jeevika "didis" run community kitchens, canteens in hospitals and other enterprises (check latest examples) |
| SHGs help social change | Jeevika groups supported the campaign for prohibition in Bihar (2016) |
| National mission | DAY-NRLM — Jeevika is Bihar's implementing agency |

## The data rule: quote only stable, sure numbers

Numbers impress examiners, but a wrong number hurts more than no number. Follow this rule:

> **Remember:** Use (1) **Census 2011** figures with the words "Census 2011"; (2) **constitutional and legal numbers** (articles, seats, years of Acts) which do not change; (3) **recent survey figures** only with their source and year, and update them from the latest Bihar Economic Survey and current affairs before the exam ("check latest"). If unsure, write it qualitatively.

### Data you must update before the exam ("check latest")

| Item | Where to update it |
|---|---|
| GSDP, growth rate, per-capita income, sector shares | Latest **Bihar Economic Survey** (finance.bihar.gov.in), released with the state budget |
| State budget size and priorities | Bihar Budget speech of the year |
| Multidimensional poverty share | NITI Aayog National MPI reports |
| Caste-based survey 2023 shares and reservation status | Official survey report; follow court cases on the 2023 reservation increase |
| Ramsar sites, GI tags, tiger reserves count | Current affairs (counts keep rising) |
| Scheme names and targets | State government portals; newspaper |

> **Bihar connection:** A safe sentence pattern for changing data: "According to the Bihar Economic Survey (latest), Bihar's growth rate has been above the national average in many recent years, but its per-capita income remains the lowest among major states." Verify this pattern against the latest survey before using it.

| Value-add | Examples you can use often |

| **Supreme Court judgments** | Kesavananda Bharati (1973, basic structure), S.R. Bommai (1994, Art 356), Maneka Gandhi (1978, Art 21), Indra Sawhney (1992, 50% ceiling), Vishaka (1997) |

| **Reports / indices** | NITI Aayog SDG India Index, National MPI, NFHS (check latest values) |
| **Personalities** | Rajendra Prasad, Jayaprakash Narayan, Karpoori Thakur, Swami Sahajanand Saraswati, Kunwar Singh, Bhikhari Thakur, Ramdhari Singh Dinkar |
| **Global links** | SDGs, Paris Agreement, UN reports — one line only |

### 3. Timeline (history, evolution of a law)

Draw a horizontal line with 4–6 dated ticks. Example for Bihar's map: **1912** Bihar and Orissa separated from Bengal → **1936** Orissa separate → **2000** Jharkhand separate.

### 4. Table (comparison)

Best for "compare/distinguish": Moderates vs Extremists; FR vs DPSP; Lok Sabha vs Rajya Sabha. Three columns, five to seven rows.

### 5. Simple schematic map

You may draw a rough outline box for Bihar with the Ganga as a line across the middle, rivers from the north (Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla, Kosi, Mahananda) and from the south (Karmanasa, Son, Punpun, Phalgu, Kiul). Label only what the answer needs. Do not attempt a detailed map — a neat schematic is better than a wrong map.

### 6. Cycle diagram

Best for: vicious circle of poverty, flood–poverty–migration cycle. Four boxes in a circle with arrows.

> **Common mistake:** Spending eight minutes on a diagram. Practise each type until you can draw it in under two minutes with a ruler-free neat hand.

## Putting it together: one paragraph, three value-adds

**Practice question (part of an answer):** *Explain the importance of decentralised governance for rural development.*

> "Decentralisation brings decisions close to people. The **73rd Amendment (1992)** gave panchayats constitutional status and listed **29 subjects** in the **Eleventh Schedule**. **Bihar** went further: the **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006** reserved **50% seats for women** and set up **Gram Kachahari** (village courts) to settle small disputes locally."

In four lines: one amendment, one number, one schedule, one Bihar Act, one Bihar institution.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Bihar angle | A link to Bihar's facts, places, schemes or people inside an answer |
| Value addition | Something extra (data, diagram, case, judgment) that raises answer quality |
| Census | Official count of the population, done (normally) every 10 years |
| Economic Survey | Yearly government report on the economy, released with the budget |
| Gram Kachahari | Village court in Bihar's panchayat system for small civil and criminal matters |
| Schematic map | A simplified, not-to-scale sketch showing only key features |
| Hub-and-spoke | Diagram with a central idea and connected aspects around it |
| NFHS | National Family Health Survey — health and population data by state |

### Mains questions

**1. Practice question: "Highlight the demographic features of Bihar as per Census 2011 and their implications for development."** (about 300 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* Bihar is India's most densely populated state (1,106 per sq km, Census 2011).
- *Body:* population ~10.41 crore; high decadal growth (~25.4%); low sex ratio (918); lowest literacy (61.8%) with a wide gender gap; low urbanisation (~11.3%); young population and high fertility (NFHS-5 TFR about 3.0). Implications: pressure on land, demographic dividend if skilled, need for girls' education, health and urban planning.
- *Diagram:* small table of Bihar vs India figures.
- *Conclusion:* Investment in education, health and skills can turn numbers into strength.

**2. Practice question: "Data and diagrams improve a descriptive answer only when used correctly." Comment.** (about 250 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* Value-adds show depth but errors reduce trust.
- *Body:* stable vs changing data; source and year; relevance; neatness and title; time limit; one per answer.
- *Conclusion:* Accuracy first, decoration never.
