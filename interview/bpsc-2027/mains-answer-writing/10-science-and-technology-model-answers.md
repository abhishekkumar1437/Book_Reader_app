GS Paper II section 3 asks about the **role and impact of science and technology in the development of India and Bihar**. The examiner is not testing physics formulas here; the question is always "**how does this technology help people and development, especially in Bihar?**" This chapter gives a toolkit and nine full model answers. Content comes from the [General Science & Technology](/interview/bpsc-2027/science) book, especially its chapter on [S&T for Bihar's development](/interview/bpsc-2027/science/science-and-technology-for-bihars-development).

> **In simple words:** For every S&T question: say **what the technology is** in two lines, list **applications** by sector, give a **Bihar use**, mention **challenges**, and end with a **way forward**. Avoid deep technical detail; focus on impact.

## S&T answer toolkit

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Intro | Define the technology simply + one recent Indian milestone |
| Applications | Sector-wise: agriculture, health, education, governance, disaster, industry |
| India's initiatives | Mission, policy, institution, year |
| Bihar | A specific use, institution or problem it solves |
| Challenges | Cost, skills, infrastructure, ethics, privacy |
| Way forward | Research funding, skilling, regulation, partnerships |

> **Memory trick:** **D-A-B-C-W** — **D**efine, **A**pplications, **B**ihar, **C**hallenges, **W**ay forward.

Missions and launch timelines change quickly. Facts that may change are marked "check latest". All questions are **practice questions in BPSC style**.

**Practice question:** *Discuss the role of science and technology in improving agriculture in Bihar.* (about 600 words)

**Introduction**

Agriculture supports most of Bihar's people, but it faces floods, droughts, small holdings and low productivity. Science and technology can raise yields, cut costs and reduce risk. Bihar has a strong research base, including **Dr Rajendra Prasad Central Agricultural University (Pusa, Samastipur)** and **Bihar Agricultural University (Sabour, Bhagalpur)**.

1. **Improved and climate-resilient seeds:** flood-tolerant paddy such as **Swarna-Sub1** survives about two weeks under water — valuable in north Bihar; heat-tolerant wheat for late sowing.
2. **Resource-conserving methods:** **zero tillage** wheat after paddy, **direct seeded rice (DSR)** saving water and labour, laser land levelling.
3. **Soil health:** **Soil Health Cards** (from 2015) for balanced fertiliser use; neem-coated urea.
4. **Water management:** drip and sprinkler irrigation, **solar pumps (PM-KUSUM)**; revival of traditional **ahar-pyne** in south Bihar under Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali.
5. **Weather and crop advisories:** IMD agro-met advisories, Kisan Call Centres, mobile apps; crop estimation using satellite data.
6. **Mechanisation:** custom hiring centres for small farmers; **drones** for spraying and crop monitoring (Kisan drones, Namo Drone Didi for SHG women — check latest).
7. **Post-harvest technology:** cold storage, solar dryers, **makhana processing machines** (traditional popping is hard manual work), litchi pulping.
8. **Research centres in Bihar:** ICAR Research Complex for Eastern Region (Patna); **National Research Centre on Litchi (Muzaffarpur)**; makhana research at **Darbhanga**; Krishi Vigyan Kendras in every district.
9. **Digital markets:** **e-NAM** and FPO platforms for better prices.

- Higher productivity of rice, wheat and maize over the last two decades (check latest Economic Survey).
- New commercial crops — rabi maize in Kosi region, vegetables, makhana.
- Reduced risk through flood-tolerant varieties and advisories.

**Challenges**

- **Small and scattered holdings** limit machine use.
- **Low awareness** and weak extension services.
- Cost of technology; limited credit.
- Poor electricity and internet in remote areas.
- Research not always reaching women farmers.

**Way forward**

- Strengthen **KVKs** and farmer field schools.
- **FPOs** to share machines and access markets.
- Public–private partnerships in seeds and processing.
- Climate-smart village models.
- Digital agriculture mission and land-record digitisation for targeted services.

**Conclusion**

Science and technology can turn Bihar's fertile land into a source of prosperity. The key is to make technology small-farmer friendly, affordable and supported by strong extension services.

> **Why this scores:** opens with Bihar institutions; a hub diagram; nine application points with specific technologies (Swarna-Sub1, DSR, ahar-pyne revival, litchi research centre); impact, challenges and way forward all present.

## Model answer 2 — India's space programme and development

**Practice question:** *"India's space programme is a tool of development, not just prestige." Discuss.* (about 600 words)

**Introduction**

The **Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)**, set up in **1969**, was founded on **Vikram Sarabhai's** vision that space technology should solve the real problems of people. India's achievements — like **Chandrayaan-3's landing near the Moon's south pole on 23 August 2023** (now celebrated as National Space Day) — bring prestige, but most of the programme serves development.

**Development applications**

| Area | Application | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | TV, telephony, internet to remote areas | GSAT series; telemedicine and tele-education |
| Weather and disasters | Cyclone, flood and rainfall monitoring | INSAT series; early warnings saved many lives in cyclones |
| Agriculture | Crop estimation, drought assessment | FASAL-type crop forecasting |
| Land and water | Mapping land use, groundwater, wetlands | **Bhuvan** geoportal |
| Navigation | Indian regional navigation system | **NavIC** — used by fishermen, transport |
| Governance | Monitoring of schemes (e.g. geo-tagging of assets) | Geo-tagged MGNREGA assets, PMAY houses |
| Science | Moon, Sun, Mars missions | Chandrayaan-3, **Aditya-L1** (launched 2 September 2023) |

**Bihar applications**

1. **Flood mapping:** satellite images from **NRSC (Hyderabad)** help map flood-hit areas of north Bihar each monsoon and assess crop losses.
2. **River course monitoring** of the Kosi and Gandak.
3. **Land records and planning:** satellite maps support land surveys and urban planning.
4. **Lightning and weather alerts** using satellite and radar data.

**New directions (check latest)**

- **Gaganyaan** human spaceflight programme — test flights and crewed mission timelines.
- **SpaDeX** docking experiment (successful docking in January 2025).
- **Indian Space Policy 2023** and **IN-SPACe** (2020) open the sector to private companies and start-ups.
- Plans for an Indian space station (Bharatiya Antariksh Station) — check latest.

**Prestige also has value**

- Inspires students, including in Bihar, towards science careers.
- Low-cost launches earn foreign exchange and diplomatic goodwill (e.g. the South Asia Satellite, 2017).

**Challenges**

Funding compared with leading space powers; space debris; need for more private investment; skilled workforce.

**Conclusion**

From weather alerts to flood maps for Bihar, India's space programme directly supports development. Prestige missions and practical applications reinforce each other, staying true to Sarabhai's vision.

> **Why this scores:** frames the answer with Sarabhai's vision; an applications table with named satellites and platforms; a Bihar section with concrete uses; recent missions with dates; "prestige also has value" shows balance.

## Model answer 3 — E-governance in Bihar

**Practice question:** *Discuss the role of information and communication technology (ICT) in improving governance in Bihar.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

**E-governance** means using ICT to deliver government services, share information and improve accountability. India's **Digital India** programme (2015) and Bihar's own initiatives have taken many services online.

**ICT initiatives in Bihar (check latest names and coverage)**

1. **RTPS online services** under the **Bihar Right to Public Services Act, 2011** — certificates such as caste, income and residence.
2. **Land records digitisation** — online records, mutation and maps under the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme; the state's land survey programme.
3. **Scholarship and welfare portals** with **Direct Benefit Transfer**.
4. **Dial 112** emergency response system and police computerisation (CCTNS).
5. **Online grievance redressal** under the 2015 grievance law.
6. **Health:** eSanjeevani teleconsultation; digital health IDs under ABDM.
7. **Education:** online attendance, digital content and smart classes in government schools.

**Benefits**

- Speed and transparency; fewer middlemen.
- Reduced corruption through DBT.
- Data for better planning.
- Services closer to rural citizens through Common Service Centres.

**Challenges**

- **Digital divide** — low internet use among rural women and the elderly.
- Weak **connectivity** in flood-prone and remote areas.
- **Cyber security** and data privacy.
- Staff capacity; resistance to change.

**Way forward**

BharatNet broadband to all panchayats; digital literacy campaigns (PMGDISHA); local-language interfaces (Hindi, Maithili, Bhojpuri); assisted services at panchayat level; strong data protection.

**Conclusion**

ICT has made governance in Bihar faster and more transparent. Bridging the digital divide will ensure that its benefits reach the poorest citizen.

> **Why this scores:** seven specific Bihar initiatives; benefits and challenges balanced; way forward includes local-language access — a Bihar-specific idea.

## Model answer 4 — Artificial Intelligence

**Practice question:** *What are the opportunities and risks of Artificial Intelligence for India? Discuss with reference to Bihar.* (about 600 words)

**Introduction**

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** means computer systems that can perform tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognising images and making predictions. India approved the **IndiaAI Mission** in **March 2024** with an outlay of over **₹10,000 crore** to build computing capacity, datasets, skills and applications.

**Opportunities**

| Sector | AI use | Bihar relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture | Pest and disease detection from phone photos; yield forecasts; advisory chatbots | Litchi, makhana, maize farmers |
| Health | Reading X-rays and scans; TB and diabetic eye screening; health chatbots | Shortage of specialist doctors in rural Bihar |
| Education | Personalised learning; local-language tutoring | Large student population; learning gaps |
| Language | Translation and speech tools — **Bhashini** platform supports Indian languages, including Maithili | Services in Hindi, Maithili, Bhojpuri |
| Governance | Detecting fraud in welfare; grievance analysis | DBT-heavy schemes |
| Disaster | Flood forecasting; damage assessment from satellite images | North Bihar floods |
| Jobs | New IT and data jobs | Youth employment |

**India's initiatives (check latest)**

- **IndiaAI Mission (2024)** — GPU compute access, AIKosh datasets platform, Centres of Excellence, start-up support.
- **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** — rules for personal data.
- India hosted a global **AI Impact Summit** in New Delhi in **February 2026**.
- AI in Digital Public Infrastructure — voice-based access to services.

**Risks**

1. **Job displacement** in routine work (data entry, call centres).
2. **Bias** — AI trained on unrepresentative data can treat the poor or women unfairly.
3. **Deepfakes and misinformation**, especially during elections.
4. **Privacy** and surveillance concerns.
5. **Digital divide** — benefits may go to cities and English speakers first.
6. **Dependence on foreign technology** and chips.

**For Bihar**

- Low internet use and digital literacy mean AI tools must be **voice-based and in local languages**.
- AI-based advisories can help small farmers if linked with KVKs and Jeevika groups.
- Engineering colleges and IIT Patna can train AI talent (check latest programmes).

**Way forward**

- **Responsible AI** rules — transparency, accountability, human oversight.
- **Skilling** in schools and ITIs; AI literacy for teachers.
- Public datasets in Indian languages.
- Use AI to **support**, not replace, frontline workers (ASHA, teachers, agriculture extension).

**Conclusion**

AI can help a state like Bihar leapfrog gaps in doctors, teachers and extension workers. With strong safeguards and local-language access, it can become a tool of inclusion rather than inequality.

> **Why this scores:** clear definition and mission facts; a three-column table puts India and Bihar side by side; risks are specific; Bihar section gives realistic conditions (voice, local language); recent events marked "check latest".

## Model answer 5 — Science and public health in Bihar

Bihar has faced several public health challenges, including **Acute Encephalitis Syndrome (AES)** in Muzaffarpur and **kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis) in north and east Bihar. Scientific research, diagnostics and better health systems have reduced their burden.

- Studies (including one published in **The Lancet Global Health, 2017**) linked many cases to **hypoglycaemia** (low blood sugar) in **undernourished children** who ate litchi and skipped the evening meal; litchi contains natural toxins that block glucose production.
- **Response:** "feed the child before sleeping" campaigns; quick **glucose correction**; **paediatric ICUs** at SKMCH Muzaffarpur; ambulances and protocols; vaccination against **Japanese Encephalitis**, another cause of AES.

**Kala-azar**

- Spread by the **sandfly**; affects the poorest, mud-house communities.
- Science-based tools: **rapid diagnostic test (rK39)**, **single-dose liposomal amphotericin B** treatment, **indoor residual spraying** with safer insecticides, and pucca housing.
- India reported reaching the **elimination target** (fewer than 1 case per 10,000 people at block level) around 2023; sustaining it and WHO validation are next steps (check latest).

**Wider health technology in Bihar**

eSanjeevani telemedicine; digital health records under **ABDM (2021)**; new **AIIMS at Darbhanga** (approved) besides **AIIMS Patna**.

**Challenges**

Malnutrition; doctor shortage; weak primary health centres; awareness.

**Conclusion**

Bihar's experience shows that science — when combined with community awareness and health systems — can control even long-standing diseases. Nutrition and primary care are the foundation.

> **Why this scores:** two Bihar diseases explained scientifically in simple words; a causal chain diagram; named tools (rK39, liposomal amphotericin B); balanced with challenges; elimination status marked "check latest".

## Model answer 6 — Renewable energy for Bihar

**Practice question:** *Discuss the scope of renewable energy in Bihar. What are the constraints?* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

Bihar's power demand has grown rapidly with near-universal household electrification, but the state has limited own generation and depends heavily on central power stations, mostly coal-based. **Renewable energy** — solar, biomass and bio-fuels — can provide clean, local power and support India's climate goals.

**Scope**

1. **Solar energy:** good sunshine for much of the year. **Rooftop solar** (PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, 2024), **solar pumps** (PM-KUSUM), solar on government buildings, and **floating solar** on ponds and reservoirs (useful because land is scarce).
2. **Biomass:** crop residue (rice husk, maize stalks) for power and briquettes — also reduces stubble burning.
3. **Ethanol:** under the **Bihar Ethanol Production Promotion Policy (2021)**, grain-based ethanol plants (for example in Purnia) use maize and broken rice — linking farmers to energy.
4. **Biogas:** from cattle dung, with Bihar's large livestock population (GOBARdhan).
5. **Small hydro** on canals in the Gandak and Kosi systems.

**Constraints**

| Constraint | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Land scarcity | High density (1,106 per sq km) — large solar parks are difficult |
| Floods | Risk to ground-mounted installations in the north |
| Finance | High upfront costs for households |
| Grid | Distribution network needs upgrading for rooftop solar |
| Awareness | Low knowledge of schemes in villages |

**Way forward**

Floating and canal-top solar; agri-voltaics (solar panels above crops); cooperative or SHG-run solar pumps; easy financing; battery storage; skill training of solar technicians.

**Conclusion**

Bihar's renewable future lies in **decentralised** energy — rooftops, ponds, pumps and bio-fuels — rather than large land-hungry plants. It can bring clean power, farm income and local jobs together.

> **Why this scores:** starts with Bihar's real energy situation; five sources with schemes and the state policy; constraints table explains why Bihar needs a different model; creative but realistic way forward.

## Model answer 7 — Technology for disaster management in Bihar

**Introduction**

Bihar is one of India's most disaster-prone states — **floods** in the north, **lightning** deaths every monsoon, heat waves and earthquakes (parts of Bihar lie in seismic zones IV and V). Technology can shift disaster management from **relief** to **early warning and preparedness**.

**Floods**

1. **Forecasting:** Central Water Commission flood forecasts; Bihar's **Flood Management Improvement Support Centre (FMISC)** uses river data, models and satellite images.
2. **Satellites and drones:** mapping of flooded areas and damaged embankments; NRSC flood maps.
3. **Data sharing with Nepal** on rainfall and river levels in the catchment.
4. **Mobile alerts:** location-based SMS through the national **Common Alerting Protocol (Sachet)** system.
5. **Relief by DBT:** cash assistance directly to affected families' bank accounts.

Earthquake-resistant building codes; heat action plans with temperature alerts; GIS-based vulnerability maps; community radio.

## Model answer 8 — Biotechnology in agriculture

**Practice question:** *Examine the potential and concerns of biotechnology in Indian agriculture.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

**Agricultural biotechnology** uses living organisms and molecular tools to improve crops and animals — through tissue culture, marker-assisted breeding, genetic modification (GM) and **genome editing**. India launched the **BioE3 policy (2024)** to promote biomanufacturing and bio-economy.

**Potential**

1. **Higher yields and pest resistance:** **Bt cotton** (approved in **2002**, India's only commercially cultivated GM crop) reduced bollworm damage.
2. **Climate resilience:** marker-assisted breeding produced flood-tolerant **Swarna-Sub1** rice.
3. **Genome-edited rice:** India released its first two genome-edited rice varieties in **2025**, claimed to give higher yield with less water (check latest).
4. **Biofortification:** zinc-rich wheat and rice, iron-rich pearl millet — fight "hidden hunger".
5. **Tissue culture:** disease-free planting material for banana (important in Vaishali/Hajipur), potato and sugarcane.
6. **Bio-fertilisers and bio-pesticides** reduce chemical use.

**Bihar angle**

Banana tissue culture, improved makhana varieties developed at Bihar Agricultural University, Sabour, and flood-tolerant paddy are direct uses in Bihar.

**Concerns**

- **Biosafety:** effects on health and environment; gene flow to wild relatives.
- **GM mustard (DMH-11)** received environmental release approval from the **GEAC** in **2022**, but the matter has been before the Supreme Court (check latest).
- **Farmer dependence** on seed companies; seed prices.
- **Pest resistance** — pink bollworm developed resistance to Bt cotton.
- Public trust and labelling.

**Regulation**

**Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)** under the Environment Ministry; certain genome-edited plants without foreign DNA were exempted from strict GM rules (2022).

**Conclusion**

Biotechnology can help India grow more food on less land and water, with less chemical use. A science-based, transparent regulatory system is needed to balance innovation with safety.

> **Why this scores:** definition with the latest policy; six potential points with real Indian examples and years; a Bihar link; concerns include a pending case marked "check latest"; regulator named.

## Model answer 9 — Cyber security

**Practice question:** *Discuss the challenges of cyber security in India and the measures taken to address them.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

With UPI, DBT and online services, crores of Indians now depend on digital systems. **Cyber security** means protecting computers, networks and data from attacks, theft and damage. Cyber fraud has become one of the fastest-growing crimes in India, including in Bihar.

**Challenges**

1. **Financial fraud:** fake calls, OTP and UPI scams, fake loan apps, "digital arrest" scams.
2. **Attacks on critical infrastructure:** power grids, hospitals, banks.
3. **Data breaches** of personal information.
4. **Misinformation and deepfakes**.
5. **Low awareness** among new internet users, especially in rural areas.
6. **Shortage of skilled cyber police** and forensic labs.
7. **Cross-border** nature of crime — hard to investigate.

**Measures**

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **IT Act, 2000** | Main law on cyber crime (amended 2008) |
| **CERT-In** | National agency for responding to cyber incidents |
| **NCIIPC** | Protects critical information infrastructure |
| **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** and helpline **1930** | Citizens report fraud quickly to freeze money |
| **I4C** (Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre) | Coordinates police action across states |
| **DPDP Act, 2023** | Data protection duties for organisations |
| **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023** | New criminal law including organised cyber crime |

**Bihar angle**

Bihar Police has set up cyber police stations and an Economic Offences Unit to handle cyber crime (check latest); awareness campaigns are needed for first-time users and DBT beneficiaries.

**Way forward**

A national cyber security strategy (updated); more cyber police and labs; school-level cyber hygiene; banks' fraud monitoring; international cooperation.

**Conclusion**

As India becomes more digital, cyber security is part of national security and of everyday trust. Awareness, skilled policing and strong laws together can protect citizens.

> **Why this scores:** clear definition; seven specific challenges; a measures table with laws and institutions; Bihar policing angle; practical way forward.

### Mains questions

**1. Practice question: "Discuss the role of technology in improving school education in Bihar."** (about 300 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* large student population; learning gaps.
- *Body:* digital classrooms; teacher training online (DIKSHA); attendance monitoring; e-content in Hindi; mobile learning during COVID-19; challenges — devices, electricity, teacher skills; way forward — blended learning, local-language content.
- *Conclusion:* technology supports, not replaces, the teacher.

**2. Practice question: "Nuclear energy is essential for India's energy security." Comment.** (about 300 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* clean, base-load power; three-stage programme (Homi Bhabha).
- *Body:* thorium reserves; current share small; safety, cost, waste; Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act 2010; small modular reactors (check latest policy); balance with renewables.
- *Conclusion:* a complement to renewables for a low-carbon grid.

**3. Practice question: "Explain how drones can be used for development in Bihar."** (about 250 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* Drone Rules 2021 liberalised use.
- *Body:* crop spraying, land survey (SVAMITVA), flood assessment, medicine delivery, SHG women as drone pilots; challenges — cost, training, regulations.
- *Conclusion:* a small technology with big rural impact.
