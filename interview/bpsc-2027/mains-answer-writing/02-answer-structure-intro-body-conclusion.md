Every good Mains answer has the same skeleton: a short introduction, a body organised under sub-headings, and a conclusion. This chapter teaches each part with ready-to-use templates, and then shows three weak answers rewritten into strong ones so you can see the difference.

> **In simple words:** Intro = tell the examiner you understood the question. Body = give the content in clear blocks. Conclusion = close with balance or a way forward. Plan for one minute before you write.

## Step 0: decode the question (one minute)

Before writing anything, break the question into parts. Use this four-box method in your rough space.

**Example.** *"Discuss the causes and consequences of floods in North Bihar."*

| Box | What you write in rough |
|---|---|
| Directive | Discuss → explain from several sides |
| Topic | Floods in North Bihar |
| Parts | (a) causes — natural, human; (b) consequences — economic, social; (c) implied: management |
| Bihar link | Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati; Kosi 2008 breach at Kusaha (Nepal) |

Your body sub-headings are now ready: *Natural causes · Human causes · Consequences · Way forward*.

> **Remember:** If a question has two parts ("causes **and** consequences"), give each part roughly equal space. Examiners often split marks across parts.

## The introduction (2–4 lines)

The introduction has one job: show that you know what the topic is and where the answer is going. Never copy the question. Never write a paragraph of history unless the question is historical.

### Seven ways to open

| Type | When to use | Example opening line |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Concept questions | "Cooperative federalism means the Centre and states work as partners, sharing power and resources to achieve common goals." |
| **Fact / data** | Economy, geography | "About three-fourths of Bihar's geographical area is considered flood-prone, most of it in the north." |
| **Historical background** | History questions | "The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 was Gandhi's first mass satyagraha in India." |
| **Constitutional provision** | Polity | "Article 243G empowers states to give panchayats powers to plan and implement schemes listed in the Eleventh Schedule." |
| **Recent event** | Current events | "India's G20 presidency in 2023 made the African Union a permanent member of the group." |
| **Contrast** | Debatable topics | "Bihar is rich in fertile soil and water, yet its farm incomes remain among the lowest in India." |
| **Quote (only if exact)** | Culture, ethics | Use only a quote you know word for word. |

> **Common mistake:** Starting with "India is a vast country with diversity…" for every answer. This wastes two lines and tells the examiner nothing. Go straight to the topic.

## The body: where the marks are

The body carries most of the marks. Build it with these rules.

### Rule 1: use sub-headings that mirror the question

If the question asks for "role and impact", your sub-headings are **Role** and **Impact**. If it says "challenges and measures", use **Challenges** and **Measures**. This makes relevance visible.

### Rule 2: points, not long paragraphs

Write 2–3 line points. Begin each point with a **bold-sounding key phrase**, then explain. In your handwritten booklet, underline the key phrase.

> Example: "**Tinkathia system:** peasants were forced to grow indigo on 3/20th of their land (3 kathas out of every 20)."

### Rule 3: specific beats general

| General (weak) | Specific (strong) |
|---|---|
| "Many people supported Gandhi." | "Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha and J.B. Kripalani worked with Gandhi in Champaran." |
| "The government made a law." | "The Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918 abolished the tinkathia system." |
| "Women got reservation." | "Bihar reserved 50% of panchayat seats for women under the Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006." |

### Rule 4: show analysis words

Examiners give extra marks for reasoning. Use link words: *because, as a result, therefore, however, on the other hand, this shows that*.

### Rule 5: one value-addition in every long answer

Add at least one of: a **flowchart**, a **table**, a **timeline**, a **small map-like sketch**, a **data point**, a **committee/judgment name**. See [Chapter 03](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/adding-the-bihar-angle-data-and-diagrams).

### Body frameworks you can reuse

| Question type | Body framework |
|---|---|
| Historical event | Background → Causes → Course/events → Bihar's role → Significance/impact |
| Movement / organisation | Origin → Aims → Methods → Leaders → Achievements → Limitations |
| Policy / scheme | Aim → Features → Achievements → Gaps → Way forward |
| Problem (floods, poverty) | Causes (natural/human) → Impacts (economic/social/environmental) → Measures taken → Way forward |
| Constitutional body | Provision → Composition → Powers → Issues → Reforms suggested |
| S&T topic | What it is → Applications (agriculture, health, governance…) → Bihar use → Challenges → Way forward |
| "Compare" | Table with 5–7 rows of criteria |

> **Memory trick:** For any problem question, think **PESTLE** to find body points fast: **P**olitical, **E**conomic, **S**ocial, **T**echnological, **L**egal, **E**nvironmental.

## The conclusion (2–4 lines)

The conclusion should not repeat the body. It should close the answer with one of these:

| Type | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Balanced judgement** | Evaluate/critically examine | "Thus, despite limits, the movement turned Gandhi into a national leader and made peasants a force in politics." |
| **Way forward** | Problems, schemes | "An integrated basin approach with Nepal, early warning and flood-resilient farming can reduce losses." |
| **Link to a goal** | Development topics | "Strong panchayats are essential for Bihar to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals at the village level." |
| **Constitutional value** | Polity | "This keeps the spirit of cooperative federalism envisaged by the Constitution." |
| **Historical significance** | History | "Champaran became a model for later movements like Kheda and Non-Cooperation." |

> **Remember:** Keep the conclusion positive and forward-looking. Avoid ending on a complaint.

## Before and after: three weak answers made strong

### Example 1 — History (about 300 words)

**Practice question:** *Examine the significance of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917) in India's freedom struggle.*

**Weak answer (what many candidates write):**

> Champaran is in Bihar. Gandhi went there in 1917. The farmers were very poor and the British planters troubled them a lot. Gandhi helped them. Many leaders joined him. The government had to listen. It was a very important event and it made Gandhi famous. After this India became free in 1947. Thus Champaran was very significant.

**What is wrong:** no structure; no specific facts (tinkathia, committee, Act, leaders' names); "examine significance" is not answered point by point; no analysis of *why* it mattered; conclusion jumps to 1947.

**Strong answer:**

> **Introduction:** The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) in north-west Bihar was Gandhi's first satyagraha on Indian soil. It was a peasant struggle against the forced cultivation of indigo by European planters.
>
> **Background:** Under the **tinkathia system**, peasants had to grow indigo on 3 of every 20 kathas of land. When synthetic dye reduced indigo's value, planters demanded higher rents (*sharahbeshi*) and lump-sum payments (*tawan*). **Rajkumar Shukla**, a local peasant, persuaded Gandhi to visit after the Lucknow Congress (1916).
>
> **Significance:**
> 1. **First test of satyagraha in India:** Gandhi defied the order to leave the district and was ready to be punished; the case was withdrawn.
> 2. **Mass contact:** statements of thousands of peasants were recorded — a new method of collecting evidence.
> 3. **Rise of new leaders:** Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Mazharul Haque and J.B. Kripalani joined; Bihar's leadership was formed here.
> 4. **Concrete victory:** Gandhi was made a member of the **Champaran Agrarian Committee**; the **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** ended tinkathia; planters refunded part of the money.
> 5. **Constructive work:** schools, sanitation and health work were started in villages.
> 6. **Model for later movements:** Kheda (1918) and Ahmedabad mill strike (1918) followed; it prepared the ground for Non-Cooperation.
>
> **Conclusion:** Champaran turned Gandhi from an Indian known for his South Africa work into a mass leader, and made Bihar the starting point of Gandhian mass politics.

**Why the strong one scores:** clear intro; background in two lines; six numbered significance points with specific names, an Act and a committee; Bihar leaders named; conclusion states the larger meaning.

### Example 2 — Polity (about 300 words)

**Practice question:** *Evaluate the role of women's reservation in Panchayati Raj institutions of Bihar.*

**Weak opening:** "Women are the half of society. Without women no country can progress. In ancient India women were respected…" (three lines spent; topic not reached).

**Strong opening:** "Bihar became the first state to reserve **50% of seats** for women in Panchayati Raj institutions, through the **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006** — above the one-third minimum required by **Article 243D** of the Constitution."

Then the strong body uses the "evaluate" frame:

| Sub-heading | Points |
|---|---|
| Achievements | Large number of women representatives; confidence and visibility; focus on water, schools, health; women in SHGs (Jeevika) linking to panchayats |
| Limitations | "Mukhiya-pati" / proxy rule by male relatives; low literacy and training; limited funds and functions devolved; social barriers |
| Way forward | Capacity building; strict action on proxy representation; more financial devolution; women-friendly gram sabhas |

**Conclusion:** "Reservation has created a political space for rural women in Bihar; the next step is to turn numbers into real decision-making power."

### Example 3 — Economy (about 250 words)

**Practice question:** *Why is Bihar's urbanisation low? Suggest measures.*

**Weak body:** one long paragraph mixing reasons and solutions, no data.

**Strong body:**

- **Data first:** Bihar's urban population was about **11.3%** in Census 2011 against India's **31.1%** (Census 2011 figures; the next census will update them).
- **Reasons (numbered):** agrarian economy; few large industries after 2000 (mineral areas went to Jharkhand); floods limiting urban growth in the north; weak municipal finances; out-migration of workers to other states' cities.
- **Measures (numbered):** develop industrial corridors and food-processing hubs; empower urban local bodies; satellite towns around Patna, Gaya, Bhagalpur, Muzaffarpur; skill development; better transport links.

The data line at the start immediately shows the examiner you know the subject.

## A one-minute planning template

Use this in the rough space before every answer:

```text
Q no. __   Directive: ______   Words: ___
Parts: (a) ______ (b) ______ (c) ______
Intro type: definition / fact / background / article
Body heads: 1 ______ 2 ______ 3 ______ 4 ______
Bihar point: ______
Diagram/table: ______
Conclusion type: judgement / way forward / significance
```

## Presentation in the answer booklet

- Leave **one line** between points and **two lines** between answers.
- Underline **key words**, not whole sentences (2–4 underlines per page).
- Draw diagrams with a pencil or the same pen, with a short **title**.
- Use a **margin** on the left; do not write to the edge.
- Number points 1, 2, 3 in the body; use sub-headings in capital letters or underlined.
- Write the question number clearly at the top of each answer.

> **Common mistake:** Using many colours of ink or highlighting every line. Keep it simple: one pen (blue or black, as instructed), underlines, and boxes for diagrams.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Introduction | The opening 2–4 lines that set the answer's direction |
| Body | The main part with sub-headings and points |
| Conclusion | The closing 2–4 lines with judgement or way forward |
| Sub-heading | A short title over a block of points |
| Value addition | Extra item that lifts quality — diagram, data, case, judgment |
| PESTLE | Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental — a checklist for points |
| Framework | A reusable order of sub-headings for a type of question |
| Proxy representation | When a male relative exercises power in place of an elected woman |

## Practice

### Recall questions

1. What are the four boxes for decoding a question?
2. How long should an introduction be?
3. Name four types of introduction.
4. What framework suits a "problem" question?
5. What framework suits a scheme or policy question?
6. What is PESTLE?
7. If a question has two parts, how should space be divided?
8. Name three types of conclusion.
9. What was the tinkathia system?
10. Which article sets the one-third minimum reservation for women in panchayats?
11. What share of panchayat seats does Bihar reserve for women, and under which Act?
12. Why should the conclusion not repeat the body?

### Answers

1. Directive, Topic, Parts, Bihar link.
2. 2–4 lines (about 10% of the answer).
3. Any four: definition, fact/data, historical background, constitutional provision, recent event, contrast, exact quote.
4. Causes → Impacts → Measures taken → Way forward.
5. Aim → Features → Achievements → Gaps → Way forward.
6. Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental — a checklist to find points.
7. Roughly equally.
8. Any three: balanced judgement, way forward, link to a goal, constitutional value, historical significance.
9. Forced indigo cultivation on 3 out of every 20 kathas of a peasant's land in Champaran.
10. Article 243D.
11. 50%, under the Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006.
12. It wastes words; a conclusion should add closure — a judgement or way forward.

### Mains questions

**1. Rewrite practice:** Take any answer you wrote last week. Rewrite it using the one-minute template. Compare word count, number of specific facts and sub-headings. (Self-check exercise.)

**2. Practice question: "Discuss the importance of a good introduction and conclusion in a descriptive answer."** (about 250 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* The examiner's first and last impression comes from these two parts.
- *Body:* intro shows understanding and direction; saves the examiner's time; types of intro; conclusion shows completeness, balance; way forward adds maturity; both together frame the body.
- *Conclusion:* A strong frame makes average content look organised and good content look excellent.
