GS Paper I section 2 asks about current events of national and international importance. The questions are usually not about a single news headline but about an **issue behind the news**: climate change, digital economy, women's representation, migration, India's foreign policy. This chapter gives a method for turning news into answer material and five model answers on stable themes you can reuse for many questions. For the background content, use the [Current Affairs Toolkit](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs).

> **In simple words:** Do not memorise news. Collect **issues**. For each issue, keep one page: background, facts, government action, problems, way forward, Bihar link. Then any question on that issue can be answered.

## The issue-page method

When a news item appears, ask: "Which bigger issue does this belong to?" Then add it to that issue page.

### Issue page template

```text
ISSUE: ______________________   Last updated: ____
1. What it is (2 lines, definition)
2. Why in news (latest event, date)
3. Key facts / data (with source + year)
4. Constitutional / legal / policy base
5. Government measures (India) — scheme, law, mission
6. Bihar angle — data, scheme, place, problem
7. Challenges (4–6)
8. Way forward (4–6), committee or report suggestions
9. International link (treaty, UN body, other country example)
10. One good opening line + one good closing line
```

### The current-events answer framework

| Part | What to write |
|---|---|
| Intro | Define the issue + why it is in news (one recent fact) |
| Body 1 | Significance / why it matters |
| Body 2 | Government measures |
| Body 3 | Challenges |
| Body 4 | Bihar angle |
| Conclusion | Way forward, link to a national goal or SDG |

> **Remember:** Current events change fast. In your answer, write facts with a **date or year** ("in 2023", "as of 2026"). If a figure might have changed, use a qualitative phrase. Update your issue pages from the newspaper and monthly compilations every month.

## Model answer 1 — Climate change and India's commitments

**Practice question:** *Discuss India's commitments to fight climate change. How is Bihar vulnerable to climate change?* (about 600 words)

**Introduction**

Climate change means long-term change in temperature and weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse gas emissions from burning fossil fuels. India is among the countries most exposed to its effects, yet its per-person emissions are far below those of developed countries. India therefore follows the principle of **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR)** while taking ambitious action.

**India's commitments**

| Commitment | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Net zero** | Target year **2070** (announced at COP26, Glasgow, 2021) |
| **Updated NDC (2022)** | Reduce emissions intensity of GDP by **45% by 2030** from the 2005 level; about **50%** of installed electric power capacity from non-fossil sources by **2030**; additional carbon sink of **2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂ equivalent** through forest and tree cover |
| **Non-fossil capacity** | The 50% installed-capacity share was reported as achieved in 2025, ahead of 2030 (check latest figures) |
| **LiFE** | Mission "Lifestyle for Environment" — sustainable consumption by individuals |
| **NAPCC (2008)** | National Action Plan on Climate Change with **8 national missions** (solar, energy efficiency, water, Himalayan ecosystem, sustainable habitat, green India, sustainable agriculture, strategic knowledge) |
| **International Solar Alliance** | Launched by India and France (2015), headquarters at Gurugram |

**Domestic measures**

1. **Renewable energy:** solar parks, rooftop solar, PM-KUSUM for farm solar pumps.
2. **Energy efficiency:** LED lighting (UJALA), star labelling, Perform-Achieve-Trade scheme.
3. **Clean transport:** ethanol blending in petrol, electric vehicle incentives.
4. **Green hydrogen:** National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023).
5. **Forests:** Green India Mission; compensatory afforestation.

**Bihar's vulnerability**

1. **Floods:** north Bihar's Himalayan rivers (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla) carry heavy silt; heavier rainfall bursts raise flood risk.
2. **Drought:** south Bihar (Gaya, Nawada, Aurangabad, Jamui) often faces rainfall deficit.
3. **Heat waves:** high summer temperatures affect health and labour productivity.
4. **Lightning:** Bihar records many lightning deaths each year in the monsoon.
5. **Agriculture dependence:** most workers depend on farming, which is sensitive to rainfall.
6. **High population density** (1,106 per sq km, Census 2011) means many people are exposed.

**Bihar's response**

- **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan (2019):** water conservation, renovation of ponds and wells, tree planting, solar energy.
- **Bihar State Action Plan on Climate Change** and lightning-alert apps (such as "Indravajra", check latest).
- Crop diversification and climate-resilient agriculture programmes (check latest scheme names).

**Challenges**

Finance for adaptation; coal dependence for power; weak early warning in remote areas; lack of awareness.

**Conclusion**

India is balancing development with climate responsibility through targets, missions and global leadership like the International Solar Alliance. For Bihar, the priority is **adaptation** — flood and drought resilience, early warning and climate-smart farming — so that its poor and farm-dependent people are protected.

> **Why this scores:** both parts answered — commitments (table with exact targets) and Bihar vulnerability (diagram + six points); principle of CBDR shows conceptual clarity; a fast-changing figure is marked "check latest"; conclusion separates mitigation (India) and adaptation (Bihar).

## Model answer 2 — Digital public infrastructure

**Practice question:** *What is Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)? Discuss its role in India's development with examples.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

**Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)** means shared digital systems — like digital identity, payments and data exchange — built for public use, on which government and private services can be built. India's model, often called the **India Stack**, was highlighted during India's **G20 presidency (2023)**.

**Main layers of India's DPI**

| Layer | Example | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **Aadhaar** (UIDAI) | 2009 (Aadhaar Act 2016) |
| Payments | **UPI** (National Payments Corporation of India) | 2016 |
| Data and documents | **DigiLocker**, Account Aggregator | 2015 onward |
| Bank access | **Jan Dhan accounts** — JAM (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) | 2014 |
| Commerce | ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) | 2022 |

**Role in development**

1. **Financial inclusion:** crores of Jan Dhan accounts brought the poor into banking.
2. **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT):** subsidies and pensions go straight to bank accounts — fewer leakages.
3. **Digital payments:** UPI made small payments cheap and instant for vendors and farmers.
4. **Ease of living:** DigiLocker documents, online certificates.
5. **Health:** CoWIN during COVID-19; Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission.
6. **Global role:** India offers DPI to other developing countries.

**Bihar angle**

DBT is used for state schemes such as scholarships, pensions and the cycle and uniform schemes for students; services under the **Bihar Right to Public Services Act (2011)** are delivered through online and counter systems (check latest).

**Challenges**

Digital divide (internet access, smartphone ownership, digital literacy, especially among rural women); cyber fraud; data privacy — now addressed by the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023**; exclusion when biometrics fail.

**Conclusion**

DPI has made India a global model of low-cost digital inclusion. For states like Bihar, closing the digital literacy gap is the key to turning DPI into real empowerment.

> **Why this scores:** definition first; a table packs layers, examples and years; Bihar angle; challenges include the 2023 data protection law; balanced conclusion.

## Model answer 3 — Women's reservation in legislatures

**Practice question:** *Examine the significance of the Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023. What lessons does Bihar's experience of women's reservation offer?* (about 600 words)

**Introduction**

The **Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023**, called the **Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam**, reserves **one-third of seats** for women in the **Lok Sabha, state legislative assemblies and the Delhi assembly**. Women's share in Parliament has long been low, and this law aims to correct that imbalance.

**Main provisions**

| Provision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seats reserved | One-third of directly elected seats in Lok Sabha, state assemblies and the Delhi assembly |
| SC/ST seats | One-third of seats reserved for SCs and STs will also be for women from these groups |
| New articles | Article **330A** (Lok Sabha) and **332A** (state assemblies); amendment to **239AA** (Delhi) |
| Start | After **delimitation** based on the first census after the Act begins |
| Duration | **15 years**, can be extended by Parliament |
| Rotation | Reserved seats to rotate after each delimitation |
| Not covered | Rajya Sabha and state legislative councils |

**Significance**

1. **Representation:** brings a fairer share of women into law-making.
2. **Policy focus:** research on panchayats shows women leaders often give more attention to water, health, nutrition and schools.
3. **Role models:** encourages girls' education and political participation.
4. **Completes local reservation:** the 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) already reserved at least one-third of local body seats for women; now the higher levels follow.
5. **Global commitments:** supports **SDG 5** (gender equality).

**Concerns**

- **Delay:** implementation depends on the next census and delimitation.
- **No OBC sub-quota** — a demand of some parties.
- **Proxy representation** risk.
- Rotation may reduce the incentive to work for one constituency.

**Lessons from Bihar**

Bihar is an important case study because it went beyond the national minimum at the local level.

1. **50% reservation in panchayats** under the **Bihar Panchayat Raj Act, 2006** — Bihar was the first state to do this; urban local bodies followed.
2. **35% reservation for women** in state government jobs (2016), which raised the visibility of women in police and other services.
3. **Jeevika SHGs** built women's leadership at the grassroots.
4. **Girls' education schemes** — Mukhyamantri Balika Cycle Yojana (2006), Poshak Yojana, Kanya Utthan Yojana — improved enrolment and confidence.

**What Bihar's experience teaches:**

| Lesson | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Numbers come first, power follows | Initially many women depended on male relatives ("mukhiya-pati"); over terms, independent leaders emerged |
| Training matters | Capacity building and literacy are essential |
| Supporting schemes help | Education and livelihood programmes make reservation effective |
| Social change is slow | Patriarchal norms need awareness campaigns |

**Way forward**

Complete the census and delimitation quickly; train women legislators; inner-party democracy and more tickets to women even before reservation begins; strict action against proxy practices.

**Conclusion**

The 106th Amendment is a historic step for gender justice in Indian democracy. Bihar's experience shows that reservation works best when it is combined with education, livelihood and leadership training — then women become decision-makers, not only seat-holders.

> **Why this scores:** exact constitutional details (articles, 15 years, delimitation condition); significance and concerns both covered; the Bihar part is not decoration but a "lessons" table — real analysis; way forward is practical.

## Model answer 4 — Migration from Bihar

**Practice question:** *Analyse the causes and consequences of out-migration from Bihar. Suggest measures.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

Bihar is one of India's largest sources of **out-migrant workers**, who go to cities like Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Surat and to Punjab and Haryana for farm work. The scale of this movement became visible when lakhs of workers returned home during the **COVID-19 lockdown (2020)**.

**Causes (push and pull)**

| Push factors (Bihar) | Pull factors (destination) |
|---|---|
| Small and fragmented landholdings | Jobs in construction, manufacturing, services |
| Floods in the north, drought in the south | Higher wages |
| Limited industry and urban jobs | Social networks of earlier migrants |
| High population density and young workforce | Better education and health facilities |

**Consequences**

*Positive:*
1. **Remittances** support household consumption, education and housing.
2. **Skills and exposure** — returning migrants bring new ideas.
3. Reduced pressure on land.

*Negative:*
1. **Loss of working-age men** — women manage farms and households alone (the "feminisation of agriculture").
2. **Poor conditions** at destination — informal jobs, no social security, poor housing.
3. **Children's education** suffers with seasonal migration.
4. **Social pain** — a theme even in Bhikhari Thakur's play *Bidesiya*.

**Measures**

- **Local jobs:** food processing (makhana, litchi, maize), textiles, logistics; MSME and start-up policies.
- **Skill development** linked to local industry.
- **Portability of benefits:** **One Nation One Ration Card**; registration on the **e-Shram** portal (2021) for social security.
- **Migrant support centres** and data systems at the state level (check latest).
- **Flood management** to stabilise farm incomes.

**Conclusion**

Migration is both a survival strategy and a sign of limited local opportunity. Bihar needs to make migration a **choice, not compulsion**, through local industry, skills and portable social protection.

> **Why this scores:** "analyse" is met with a push-pull table and consequences split into positive and negative; a cultural link adds depth; measures include national tools (ONORC, e-Shram); memorable closing line.

## Model answer 5 — India and the Global South

**Practice question:** *Discuss India's role as a voice of the Global South with reference to its G20 presidency.* (about 300 words)

**Introduction**

The **Global South** refers broadly to developing countries of Asia, Africa, Latin America and the Pacific. India held the **G20 presidency** from December 2022 to November 2023 under the theme **"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — One Earth, One Family, One Future"** and used it to raise the concerns of developing countries.

**India's actions as a voice of the Global South**

1. **Voice of Global South Summit (January 2023):** India consulted developing countries before setting G20 priorities.
2. **African Union as permanent member:** the **African Union** joined the G20 as a permanent member at the **New Delhi Summit (September 2023)**.
3. **New Delhi Leaders' Declaration:** adopted by consensus despite global divisions; stressed debt relief, climate finance and SDGs.
4. **Digital Public Infrastructure:** India offered its DPI model to other developing countries.
5. **Global Biofuels Alliance** launched; **India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC)** announced.
6. **Vaccine Maitri** (COVID-19 vaccines to many countries) and humanitarian aid built trust.

**Why India can play this role**

- Large developing democracy with experience of poverty reduction.
- Member of both the G20 and Global South forums (BRICS, G77 history, Non-Aligned Movement tradition).
- Strategic autonomy — relations with all major powers.

**Challenges**

Competition with other powers for influence in the Global South; limited aid budget compared with major donors; balancing its own interests with group interests.

**Bihar link**

The image of the **ancient Nalanda Mahavihara** formed the backdrop at the G20 leaders' dinner in New Delhi, presenting India's knowledge tradition. The new campus of **Nalanda University** at Rajgir, revived through an East Asia Summit initiative, was inaugurated in **2024**.

**Conclusion**

India's G20 presidency showed it can bridge developed and developing nations. Continued leadership on climate finance, digital inclusion and debt relief will strengthen its position as a trusted voice of the Global South.

> **Why this scores:** precise G20 facts (theme, African Union, Voice of Global South Summit, consensus declaration); "why India" adds analysis; an elegant Bihar link through Nalanda; balanced challenges.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| NDC | Nationally Determined Contribution — a country's climate targets under the Paris Agreement |
| Net zero | Emissions released are balanced by emissions removed |
| CBDR | Common But Differentiated Responsibilities — rich and poor countries share responsibility differently |
| DPI | Digital Public Infrastructure — shared digital systems for public use |
| DBT | Direct Benefit Transfer — money sent directly to beneficiaries' bank accounts |
| Delimitation | Redrawing constituency boundaries based on population |
| Push and pull factors | Reasons that force people to leave and reasons that attract them elsewhere |
| Remittance | Money sent home by a migrant |
| Global South | Developing countries of Asia, Africa, Latin America and the Pacific |

### Mains questions

**1. Practice question: "Heat waves are an emerging disaster in India." Discuss with reference to Bihar.** (about 300 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* define heat wave (IMD criteria based on temperature above normal); rising frequency.
- *Body:* causes — climate change, urban heat island, land use; impacts — health, labour, crops, power demand; Bihar — summer heat in south and central Bihar, school timing changes, lightning and storms in pre-monsoon; measures — heat action plans, early warning, cooling shelters, work-hour changes.
- *Conclusion:* treat heat as a disaster in planning and budgets.

**2. Practice question: "Discuss the importance of the National Education Policy 2020 for a state like Bihar."** (about 300 words)

Outline:
- *Intro:* NEP 2020 replaced the 1986 policy; 5+3+3+4 structure.
- *Body:* foundational literacy and numeracy; mother tongue in early classes (Maithili, Bhojpuri, Magahi); vocational education; higher education reform; teacher training; Bihar — large student population, teacher recruitment drives (check latest), Nalanda heritage.
- *Conclusion:* implementation and funding decide success.
