Answer writing is a skill like riding a bicycle: you learn it by doing it every day, not by reading about it. This chapter tells you exactly **what to write on which day**, from Day 29 of the roadmap to Day 390, and gives you a rubric to mark your own answers honestly. It follows the shared plan in the [BPSC 2027 Roadmap](/interview/bpsc-2027/roadmap).

> **In simple words:** Write 1–2 answers every day from Day 29. Mark them with the rubric. Keep a mistake log. In the Mains sprint, write one full GS paper every week. By the exam you will have written several hundred answers — that is what makes the 3-hour paper feel normal.

## Phase 4: Prelims mode (Days 246–300)

Prelims comes first; Mains marks count only if you clear it. In this phase:

- Write **three answers a week** (for example on Monday, Wednesday, Friday) — 15 minutes each.
- Choose questions linked to what you revise for Prelims (a Bihar history topic, a polity article).
- Do not start new Mains material. Keep your issue pages updated from the newspaper.

## Phase 5: the Mains sprint (Days 301–390)

After Prelims, Mains preparation becomes full-time. The roadmap divides it into seven sprints (Days 301–314, 315–328, 329–342, 343–356, 357–370, 371–384, 385–390).

|---|---|---|---|

> **Mains angle:** In the sprint, the target is not more reading but **better conversion**: every test should show fewer unattempted questions, more Bihar points, and more diagrams than the last one.

## The self-evaluation rubric (score out of 10)

Mark each answer with this rubric. Be honest — an inflated score only hurts you.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** — answered every part and the directive | Off-topic | Partly | Fully |
| **Structure** — intro, sub-headings, conclusion | No structure | Some | Clear all three |
| **Content** — specific, correct facts (names, dates, articles, data) | Vague or wrong | A few | Many, correct |
| **Analysis and Bihar angle** — causes/effects, both sides, Bihar point | None | One of these | Both |
| **Presentation and time** — neat, diagram/table, within time and word limit | Poor | Average | Good |

**Score guide:** 8–10 = exam-ready · 6–7 = good, polish content · 4–5 = rework structure and facts · below 4 = re-read the model answer and rewrite.

### Mistake log format

| Date | Q no. | Score | Main mistake | Fix for next time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day 45 | Hist-07 | 5/10 | No Bihar point, conclusion missing | Add Bihar sub-heading; reserve last 2 min for conclusion |
| Day 46 | Pol-03 | 7/10 | Wrong article number | Revise Art 243 series |

Read your mistake log every Sunday (revision day). If the same mistake appears three times, write it on a card and keep it on your desk.

## Ways to get feedback

1. **Self-evaluation** with the rubric (always).
2. **Peer review:** exchange answers with one or two serious aspirants; mark each other with the same rubric.
3. **Compare with model answers** in this book — list the points you missed.
4. **Test series:** if you join one, choose one with detailed feedback; one good test series is enough.
5. **Read toppers' copies** only for structure and presentation, not to copy content.

> **Common mistake:** Writing many answers but never checking them. Ten checked answers teach more than fifty unchecked ones.

## Improving speed and handwriting

| Problem | Practice |
|---|---|
| Slow writing | Daily 10-minute timed copying of a model answer; aim for about 20 words a minute |
| Untidy handwriting | Write slightly larger, use clear spacing; slow down a little — legibility beats speed |
| Hand pain in long tests | Use a comfortable pen; practise 3-hour tests at least 8–10 times before Mains |
| Too much time on intro | Pre-prepare openings for common topics in your issue pages |
| Diagrams too slow | Practise each of the six diagram types until each takes under 2 minutes |

## Tracking progress

Keep a simple tracker:

| Week | Answers written | Average score | Full tests | Unattempted questions in test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 5 | 7 | 5.1 | 0 | — |
| Week 30 | 21 | 6.8 | 1 (half) | 2 |
| Sprint 4 | 25 | 7.6 | 2 | 0 |

The goal by the exam: an average rubric score of **7 or more**, **zero unattempted questions** in full tests, and at least **one Bihar point** in every relevant answer.
