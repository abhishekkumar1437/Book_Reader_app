The roadmap gives you **1 hour every day** for the newspaper and current-affairs notes. Used well, this hour covers most of the current-events questions in Prelims, gives you examples and data for Mains, and prepares you for the interview. Used badly, it becomes two hours of reading crime news and forgetting everything. This chapter teaches a fixed method: what to read, what to skip, how to write notes, and how to turn daily notes into a monthly compilation you can revise.

> **In simple words:** Read with the syllabus in mind. Note only what can become a question. Write it short, under a syllabus heading, with the static background. Once a month, put it together and revise.

## Why the newspaper matters for BPSC

- **Prelims:** current events of national and international importance is a separate syllabus head, and many "static" questions (schemes, places, organisations) start from news.
- **Mains GS I:** current events is a full part of the paper.
- **Mains GS II and Essay:** you need recent examples, data and government initiatives for every answer.
- **Interview:** the board often asks about the day's news, especially Bihar news.

## What to read

### Read (in this order)

| Section | What to look for | Syllabus link |
|---|---|---|
| Front page and national news | Big government decisions, laws, court judgments, schemes | Polity, economy, current events |
| **Bihar page** | State schemes, budget, projects, appointments, floods, culture, Bihar in national rankings | Bihar in every paper |
| Editorial and opinion (1–2 articles) | Analysis of one big issue: arguments, data, way forward | Mains GS, essay |
| Economy / business page | RBI decisions, GDP, inflation, budget, trade, new economic terms | Economy |
| International page | India's relations, summits, groupings, conflicts that affect India | Current events, IR basics |
| Science and environment | Space missions, health, new technology, climate, wildlife | Science, S&T Mains |
| Sports (headlines only) | Major tournaments, records, Indian winners, Bihar athletes | Prelims one-liners |

### Skip

- Crime stories, accidents and court cases about individuals (unless a landmark judgment).
- Day-to-day political statements, party fights, election speculation.
- Celebrity, film and entertainment news.
- Stock market tips, company results (unless a big policy event).
- Local news of cities outside Bihar.
- Long opinion columns that repeat the same argument.

> **Memory trick:** Before you read an article, ask: "**Which paper, which part of the syllabus?**" If you cannot answer in five seconds, skip it.

## The one-hour routine

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Scan all headlines. Mark 6–10 relevant articles. |
| 10–40 min | Read the marked articles. Underline facts and arguments. |
| 40–55 min | Write notes (see format below). |
| 55–60 min | Look up the **static background** of one item (e.g., what is the scheme's ministry, the organisation's headquarters). |

Keep it to one hour. If you finish early, use the time to revise last week's notes.

## Note format

### The four-line rule

For each news item, write at most **four lines**:

1. **What** happened (one line, with date/month).
2. **Key facts** (numbers, names, places).
3. **Background** (static: what the scheme/body/law is).
4. **Exam use** (Prelims fact? Mains point? Bihar angle?).

**Example of a note (a stable, illustrative item):**

```text
Heading: Economy → Banking
What: RBI kept the repo rate unchanged at its policy meeting (month, year).
Facts: Repo rate __ %; inflation target 4% (±2%); MPC has 6 members.
Background: Monetary Policy Committee set up under the amended RBI Act (2016).
Use: Prelims (MPC facts); Mains GS II (inflation control).
```

(Fill the actual numbers from the news when you write your notes — they change.)

### Arrange notes by syllabus, not by date

Do **not** keep a diary ("12 March: ..., 13 March: ..."). Instead, keep sections under the syllabus heads:

| Section in your notebook | Examples of entries |
|---|---|
| Polity & governance | New laws, court judgments, commissions, elections |
| Economy | RBI, budget, GDP, inflation, trade, schemes |
| International | Summits, groupings, India's bilateral visits |
| Science & technology | ISRO, defence, health, AI, biotech |
| Environment | Reports, conventions, protected areas, disasters |
| Awards, sports, persons | Awards, appointments, sports events |
| **Bihar** | All Bihar news under sub-heads: schemes, economy, culture, infrastructure, administration |
| Reports & indices | Who publishes, India's rank (check latest), Bihar's rank |

This is how questions are asked, and it makes revision fast: before Prelims you revise "all Environment" in one sitting.

> **Bihar connection:** Give Bihar its **own** section, with sub-heads. Bihar current affairs is often the most valuable part of your notes for BPSC — both for Prelims and for the interview.

### Mains notes: the "issue page"

For big issues that run for weeks (a major law, floods, migration, a new state policy), keep **one page per issue**:

| Box | Content |
|---|---|
| Issue | One line |
| Background | 2–3 lines |
| Data | 3–5 figures with source and year |
| Arguments for | 3–5 points |
| Arguments against / challenges | 3–5 points |
| Government action | Schemes, laws, committees |
| Bihar angle | How it affects Bihar / what Bihar has done |
| Way forward | 3–4 suggestions |

One issue page = one ready Mains answer and part of an essay.

## Monthly compilation

At the end of every month (use one of the roadmap's revision days):

1. Read all of the month's notes once.
2. Cross out items that turned out to be unimportant.
3. Fill gaps from **one** monthly compilation (coaching or magazine) — only what you missed.
4. Write a **one-page summary** of the month: the 20–30 most testable facts.
5. Recall test: close the notes and write 10 facts from memory. Check.

## Which period to cover

For a Prelims around mid-to-late 2027, cover roughly the **12–18 months before the exam** (so from about mid-2026). For Mains, the most recent year matters most, and big long-running issues from earlier also count. Details are in [How to Cover Current Affairs](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/how-to-cover-current-affairs).

## Notes for static subjects

The same principles apply to subject notes:

- **Do not copy the book.** This shelf's chapters already work as notes. Write only what is **not** in them or what you keep forgetting.
- **Margin notes:** Add new facts (from PYQs, newspapers) in the margin or in a short "additions" page per chapter.
- **Short notes for revision:** For each chapter, make a one-page "last week" sheet: the 15–20 facts you find hardest. The "Quick revision" box in each chapter is a good starting point.
- **Mains points:** Keep a separate small notebook of **Bihar data and examples** by theme (agriculture, floods, health, education, women, migration, industry, culture). You will use it in every Mains paper and essay.

## Digital vs paper notes

| Factor | Paper notebook | Digital (phone/laptop app) |
|---|---|---|
| Memory | Writing by hand helps many people remember | Typing is faster but may be remembered less |
| Rearranging | Hard to move items | Easy to sort by heading, search, tag |
| Distraction | None | High (notifications, social media) |
| Carrying | Heavy over a year | Always with you |
| Revision before exam | Easy to flip through | Easy to search; harder to "scan" visually |
| Risk | Lost or damaged notebook | Lost data if not backed up |

**A sensible mix:**

- **Paper** for daily study notes, Mains issue pages and the Bihar data notebook.
- **Digital** for collecting links (PIB releases, Economic Survey PDFs) and for a searchable list of facts — **only if** you can keep notifications off.
- Whatever you choose, use **one system** and keep it for the whole preparation. Switching systems in the middle wastes weeks.

> **Common mistake:** Saving 50 screenshots a day from Telegram channels. Screenshots are not notes. If you did not write it in your own words under a syllabus heading, you have not learned it.

## Key points

- Spend exactly one hour a day: scan, read 6–10 relevant articles, write notes, check one background.
- Read national news, the Bihar page, 1–2 editorials, economy, international and science; skip crime, politics gossip and entertainment.
- Use four-line notes (what, facts, background, use), arranged by syllabus head, with a separate Bihar section.
- Keep one-page "issue pages" for big Mains issues.
- Make a one-page monthly summary and test yourself on it.
- Choose paper or digital (or a fixed mix) and stick to it.

## Practice

1. Read today's newspaper using the one-hour routine and write notes for 6 items in the four-line format. Note how long each step took.
2. Set up your current-affairs notebook (or file) with the eight section headings above, including a Bihar section with sub-heads.
3. Make one Mains "issue page" for a current Bihar issue (for example floods, migration, or a new state scheme).
4. Decide your notes system (paper, digital or mix) and write the rule down: what goes where.
5. Put the monthly compilation day for the next three months into your calendar (use a roadmap revision day).
