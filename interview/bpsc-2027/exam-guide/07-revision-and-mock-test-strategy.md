Reading a chapter once gives you a feeling of knowing. A month later, most of it is gone. The candidates who clear BPSC are not those who read the most, but those who **revise the most** and **test themselves** often. This chapter gives you a simple revision system (the 1-7-30 rule), shows how to use the weekly revision days in the roadmap, and explains how to take mocks, analyse them, and keep a mistake log so the same error never costs you twice.

> **In simple words:** Revise each topic the next day, after a week, and after a month. Test yourself instead of re-reading. In mocks, the marks matter less than what you learn from each mistake.

## Why we forget, and how to stop it

Memory fades fastest in the first few days after learning. Each time you **recall** something just as you are starting to forget it, the memory becomes stronger and fades more slowly. This is called **spaced repetition** (revising at growing gaps). The second idea is **active recall**: trying to bring the answer out of your memory (by answering questions, writing from memory) instead of just reading again. Both together are the strongest study methods known.

| When | What you do | Where it is in the roadmap |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 0** (learning day) | Read the chapter, make short notes, do the recall questions | 3-hour main subject block |
| **+1 day** | 15 minutes: recall yesterday's key facts **without looking**, then check | The daily 15-min revision block |
| **+7 days** | Revise the week; redo recall questions you got wrong; write 2 longer answers | Every 7th day (Day 7, 14, 21 …) |
| **+30 days** | Revise the month's chapters from your short notes and "Quick revision" boxes | One revision day each month (combine with the monthly current-affairs summary) |
| **Phase 4** (Days 246–300) | Two full revision rounds of all subjects | Prelims mode |
| **Last 15 days** | Only short notes, Quick revision boxes, mistake log | Last-15-days plan in the roadmap |

> **Remember:** The +1 review is the most skipped and the most valuable. Fifteen minutes the next morning saves hours later.

## How to revise (active, not passive)

| Passive (weak) | Active (strong) |
|---|---|
| Reading the chapter again | Closing the book and writing the 10 main facts |
| Highlighting | Answering the chapter's recall questions |
| Watching a video summary | Explaining the topic aloud as if teaching a friend |
| Reading someone else's notes | Making a one-page sheet from memory, then checking |
| "I know this" feeling | Drawing a timeline or a river map from memory |

**Practical tools:**

- **Recall questions** at the end of each concept chapter on this shelf — cover the answers and test yourself.
- **Quick revision boxes** — read them, then cover them and recall.
- **The app's Quiz tab** — MCQ practice for Prelims-style recall.
- **Flash cards** (paper or app) — for dates, articles, Bihar facts. Front: question; back: answer.
- **Blank-page method:** on a revision day, take a blank page, write the chapter title, and write everything you remember. Then check what you missed — that is your weak list.

## The weekly revision day

Every 7th day in the roadmap is a revision day. A sample plan for 5.5 hours:

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 1 h 30 min | Revise the week's main-subject chapters (blank-page method + recall questions) |
| 1 h | Revise the week's current-affairs notes; write a short weekly summary |
| 1 h | Write **2 longer Mains answers** on the week's topics |
| 45 min | Redo GMA questions you got wrong this week |
| 45 min | Update the mistake log; plan next week |

## Mock tests

### When to start

| Phase | Mock type | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Phases 1–3 (Days 8–245) | **Sectional tests** after each subject (e.g., after Polity, a 50–100 question polity test) | After each subject block |
| Phase 4 start (from Day 246) | Full-length Prelims mocks (150 Q, 2 h, −1/3 and option E) | **2 per week** |
| Phase 4 middle | Full-length mocks | **Alternate days** |
| Last 2 weeks | Full-length mocks + official PYQ papers | **Daily** |
| Mains sprint (Days 301–390) | GS I and GS II full-length tests, essay | **Weekly** |

### How to take a mock

- Same time as the real exam (the 72nd paper was announced for **12:00 noon to 2:00 pm**).
- Use an OMR sheet with options A–E. Apply the real rule (see [Negative Marking and Option E Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/negative-marking-and-option-e-strategy)).
- No phone, no breaks, no checking answers in between.
- Analyse the **same day** while the paper is fresh.

> **Common mistake:** Taking mocks and only noting the score. A mock without analysis is wasted. The analysis (60–90 minutes) is more important than the test itself.

### Worked example — reading the analysis

Suppose after mock 5 your breakdown shows: Polity 14/16 right, Geography 9/15, GMA 18/22, Current affairs 8/18. Wrong-type count: "Did not know" 12, "Misread" 6, "Bad guess" 9.

Decisions:

1. **Current affairs** is the weakest: spend extra time on your monthly summaries this week.
2. **Geography**: redo the Bihar rivers and India physiography recall questions.
3. **6 misreads** cost you 6 × (1 + 1/3) ≈ 8 marks (each could have been +1 instead of −1/3). Start underlining "not", "incorrect", "only".
4. **9 bad guesses** cost about 3 marks net. Tighten your guessing rule.

## The mistake log

A mistake log is a notebook (or file) where you write **every** mistake from mocks, PYQs and practice — and the correct fact. It becomes your most valuable revision material in the last 15 days.

**Format:**

| No. | Source (mock / PYQ / chapter) | Topic | My wrong idea | Correct fact | Why I erred | Revised on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mock 3 | Bihar rivers | Son joins from north | Son joins Ganga from the **south** (near Patna region) | Confused with Gandak | |
| 2 | Mock 4 | Polity | 73rd Amendment = municipalities | 73rd = **panchayats**; 74th = municipalities | Similar numbers | |

**Rules:**

- Write the **correct** fact in a full, clear sentence — that is what you will revise.
- Revise the log every **revision day** and fully in the **last 15 days**.
- When you get the same fact right three times in a row, tick it off.

> **Memory trick:** "**A mistake made once is learning; a mistake made twice is laziness.**" The log is how you make sure it happens only once.

## Mains revision

- **Answer bank:** Keep every answer you write (Day 29 onwards). Re-read 5 old answers each revision day and improve one.
- **Theme sheets:** For each GS syllabus part, keep a one-page sheet of key arguments, data and Bihar examples.
- **Essay:** Keep a list of quotes, examples and data by theme (see the [Essay](/interview/bpsc-2027/essay) book).
- **Hindi:** Revise grammar rules and 10 idioms per revision day.

## What if you fall behind?

- **Never skip revision days to catch up on new chapters.** Unrevised chapters are mostly lost.
- Instead, shorten new reading: read the chapter's main sections and Quick revision box, skip extra reading, and keep the revision schedule.
- The roadmap's "what to do if you fall behind" section ([How to Use This Roadmap](/interview/bpsc-2027/roadmap/how-to-use-this-roadmap)) gives a catch-up plan.

## Key points

- Use spaced repetition (1-7-30) and active recall (test yourself, do not just re-read).
- Daily 15-minute review of yesterday; weekly revision day; monthly revision; two full rounds in Phase 4.
- Sectional tests in Phases 1–3; full-length mocks from Day 246: 2/week → alternate days → daily in the last 2 weeks.
- Analyse every mock: score, accuracy, subject breakdown, wrong-answer types.
- Keep a mistake log with the correct fact in a full sentence; revise it every revision day and in the last 15 days.

## Practice

1. Set up your mistake log (notebook or file) with the seven columns above. Add the first entry today from any practice question you got wrong.
2. Plan your next weekly revision day hour by hour using the sample plan.
3. Try the blank-page method on the last chapter you studied. Count how many key facts you recalled vs. missed.
4. Prepare a printed mock analysis sheet (the three tables) — make 30 copies for Phase 4.
5. Choose one sectional-test source for each subject in Phase 1 and note when you will take each test.
