Knowing **how** a question is asked is half the battle. The same fact — say, "Champaran Satyagraha was in 1917" — can come as a direct question, as one statement out of three, as a pair in a matching list, or as a Mains question asking you to "evaluate its significance". This chapter describes the main styles BPSC uses in Prelims and Mains, shows how to handle each, and tells you how to turn this into a study habit.

> **In simple words:** BPSC mostly asks direct, fact-based questions, with a growing share of statement-type questions, plenty of Bihar, and some current affairs. Learn facts with their "neighbours" (dates, places, people, reasons), because the question may come from any side.

> **Important:** Every sample question in this chapter is **invented** and labelled "**example in BPSC style**". These are **not** real previous-year questions (PYQs). For real PYQs, download the official question booklets from the **Question Booklets** page of bpsc.bihar.gov.in and solve them in Phase 4.

## The big picture (qualitative)

Based on recent papers as discussed widely by teachers and candidates (no exact percentages are claimed here):

- **Direct one-line questions** are still common in BPSC Prelims — more than in UPSC.
- **Statement-based** questions ("Which of the above statements is/are correct?") have become more frequent in recent cycles.
- **Bihar** appears in many subjects: history, geography, economy, polity, culture, current affairs.
- **Current affairs** carries a solid share, and often includes Bihar news.
- **GMA** (reasoning and arithmetic) gives a steady set of questions that you can answer with certainty if you practise.
- **Language:** Questions are printed in both Hindi and English. If one version seems unclear, read the other.

> **Common mistake:** Studying only from "one-liner" GK books. One-liners help with direct questions, but statement questions need **understanding**. You must know why something is true, not only the fact.

## Prelims styles

### Style 1 — Direct (one-line) question

The simplest form: one fact, four options.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Who founded the Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha in 1929?
(A) Jayaprakash Narayan
(B) Swami Sahajanand Saraswati
(C) Rajendra Prasad
(D) Karyanand Sharma
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (B). **How to handle:** Answer quickly in round 1. Spend no more than 20–30 seconds.

**How to study for it:** Learn facts in **tables** — person ↔ organisation ↔ year ↔ place. All concept chapters on this shelf use such tables.

### Style 2 — Statement-based ("which is/are correct")

Two to four statements; you decide which are true.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Consider the following statements:
1. The Kosi river enters Bihar from Nepal.
2. The Son river joins the Ganga from the north.
3. The Gandak is also called the Narayani in Nepal.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 1 and 3 only
(C) 2 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (B). The Son is a **southern** tributary.

**How to handle:**

1. Test each statement alone: True / False / Not sure.
2. Use the options to eliminate. If statement 2 is surely false, remove A, C and D.
3. Watch for small "traps": one wrong word (north/south, first/second, 1919/1920).

**How to study for it:** For every important fact, also learn its **opposite or neighbour** (which river joins from the south, which from the north). Use the "Prelims angle" callouts in each chapter — they list such traps.

### Style 3 — Match the following

Two lists; you pair them.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Match List-I (art form) with List-II (region/place of origin):
List-I                 List-II
a. Madhubani painting  1. Patna
b. Manjusha art        2. Mithila
c. Patna Qalam         3. Bhagalpur
d. Sikki craft         4. North Bihar (grass craft)
Codes:
     a b c d
(A)  2 3 1 4
(B)  3 2 1 4
(C)  2 1 3 4
(D)  1 3 2 4
(E)  Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (A).

**How to handle:** Start with the pair you are surest of (Madhubani → Mithila). Remove options that do not have that pair. Usually one more sure pair leaves one option.

**How to study for it:** Make **two-column lists** in your notes: dynasty–capital, river–city, scheme–ministry, dance–state, festival–month, sanctuary–district.

### Style 4 — Assertion and Reason

Two sentences; you judge whether both are true and whether the reason explains the assertion. BPSC uses this less often than UPSC-style papers, but it appears in practice sets and may appear in the paper.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Assertion (A): North Bihar faces floods almost every year.
Reason (R): Rivers like the Kosi carry heavy silt from the Himalaya and change their course.
(A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
(B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
(C) A is true, but R is false
(D) A is false, but R is true
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (A).

**How to handle:** First decide A true/false, then R true/false, then the link ("because" test: "A because R" — does it make sense?).

### Style 5 — Chronology (correct order)

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Arrange in chronological order:
1. Champaran Satyagraha
2. Separation of Bihar from Bengal
3. Quit India Movement
4. Formation of the Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha
(A) 2, 1, 4, 3
(B) 1, 2, 4, 3
(C) 2, 4, 1, 3
(D) 1, 2, 3, 4
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (A) — 1912, 1917, 1929, 1942.

**How to study for it:** Keep **timelines** for history and the national movement. The history and bihar-special books have them.

### Style 6 — Bihar-specific questions

BPSC loves Bihar. Questions come on Bihar's history, places, rivers, crops, minerals, wildlife sanctuaries, festivals, art, languages, personalities, schemes, administration and economy.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Valmiki Tiger Reserve is located in which district of Bihar?
(A) Kaimur
(B) West Champaran
(C) Gaya
(D) Jamui
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (B).

**How to study for it:** Read the [Bihar Special](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special) book alongside each subject (Phase 1), then fully again in Phase 2 (Days 162–182). Keep a **district-wise** list of important places — see [Bihar Facts and Figures](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-facts-and-figures).

> **Bihar connection:** For many topics, BPSC asks the Bihar version of a national question: not "which state has the most X" but "which district of Bihar has the most X". Always ask yourself, "What is the Bihar angle here?"

### Style 7 — Current affairs

Current affairs questions cover national and international events, appointments, awards, sports, reports, schemes, summits, and Bihar news, usually from the 12–18 months before the exam.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Which of the following organisations publishes the World Economic Outlook?
(A) World Bank
(B) International Monetary Fund
(C) World Economic Forum
(D) UNCTAD
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (B).

**How to handle:** Many current-affairs questions are really **static** questions ("who publishes it?", "where is its headquarters?"). If you know the background, you can answer even if you missed the news.

**How to study for it:** Follow [How to Cover Current Affairs](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/how-to-cover-current-affairs) and the monthly compilation template.

### Style 8 — Science of everyday life

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness?
(A) Vitamin A
(B) Vitamin B12
(C) Vitamin C
(D) Vitamin D
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (A).

**How to study for it:** Learn science as **"thing → why"** pairs. The science book's Quick revision boxes list the most-tested facts.

### Style 9 — General Mental Ability

Number series, coding, blood relations, directions, ranking, simple arithmetic and data reading.

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
A shopkeeper marks an article 25% above cost and gives a 10% discount.
What is his profit percentage?
(A) 10%
(B) 12.5%
(C) 15%
(D) 17.5%
(E) Not attempted
```

**Solution:** Cost = 100 → marked price = 125 → selling price = 125 × 0.9 = 112.5 → profit = **12.5%**. **Answer:** (B).

**How to study for it:** The 45-minute daily GMA block in the roadmap (Days 1–245), then GMA inside mocks. See [How GMA Is Asked in BPSC](/interview/bpsc-2027/mental-ability/how-gma-is-asked-in-bpsc).

### Style 10 — "More than one" / "None of the above" inside the options

Some questions include options like "More than one of the above" or "None of the above" among A–D. This is different from option **E** under the 72nd rule (which means "not attempted").

**Example in BPSC style (invented, not a PYQ):**

```text
Which of the following is/are UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Bihar?
(A) Mahabodhi Temple Complex, Bodh Gaya
(B) Excavated remains of Nalanda Mahavihara
(C) Both (A) and (B)
(D) None of the above
(E) Not attempted
```

**Answer:** (C).

**How to handle:** Test each option separately before choosing "both" or "none". Do not confuse "None of the above" (an answer) with E (not attempting).

For every important fact, write five neighbours in your notes: **who, when, where, why, result**. Statement and matching questions are built from these neighbours. (For Champaran: Gandhi, 1917, West Champaran district, the *tinkathia* system of forced indigo cultivation, Raj Kumar Shukla who invited Gandhi, and the Champaran Agrarian Act of 1918.)

## Mains styles

Mains questions are descriptive. The key is the **directive word** — the verb that tells you what to do.

| Directive word | What it wants | How to structure |
|---|---|---|
| Describe / Explain | Clear account of what and how | Intro → points in logical order → short conclusion |
| Discuss | Several sides, with your balanced view | Intro → arguments for → arguments against / limits → way forward |
| Analyse / Examine | Break into parts; causes, effects, links | Intro → factor-wise sub-headings → conclusion |
| Evaluate / Assess / Critically examine | Judge value or success, with evidence | Intro → achievements → shortcomings → verdict |
| Comment | Your informed opinion, backed by facts | Intro → 2–3 supporting points → 1–2 limits → opinion |
| "Write a short note on" | Compact factual answer | Definition → key facts → significance |

**Examples in BPSC style (invented, not PYQs):**

- "Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement of 1942." (GS I, history)
- "Evaluate the impact of the Bihar Panchayati Raj Act, 2006 on women's political participation." (GS II, polity)
- "Examine the causes of recurring floods in north Bihar and suggest technological solutions." (GS II, S&T + geography)
- "The table below shows the population of five districts. Calculate the mean and median and comment on the distribution." (GS I, statistics)

> **Mains angle:** Almost every Mains question allows a **Bihar angle** even if it does not ask for one. A question on India's agriculture can include a line on Bihar's makhana and litchi; a question on federalism can mention Bihar's demand for special category status. This shows the examiner that you know the state you want to serve.

Mains structure and marking are covered in [Mains Answer Writing Basics](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/mains-answer-writing-basics) and in depth in the [Mains GS Answer Writing](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing) book.

## How to use real PYQs (the right way)

1. **Download** the official Prelims question booklets (and the final answer keys) of recent CCEs from bpsc.bihar.gov.in.
2. **Phase 1–2:** After finishing a subject, read that subject's questions from the last few papers — **not to score**, but to see what kind of facts are asked.
3. **Phase 4:** Solve full past papers as timed mocks with the −1/3 rule.
4. **Classify** each PYQ by syllabus line and by style (direct / statement / matching / Bihar / current). This tells you your weak style.
5. **Never memorise PYQ answers blindly.** BPSC rarely repeats the exact question; it repeats the **topic**. Read around each PYQ topic.

## Key points

- BPSC Prelims mixes direct questions with a growing share of statement-based and match-the-following questions.
- Bihar appears in almost every subject; current affairs often includes Bihar news.
- Handle statement and matching questions by testing each part and eliminating options.
- Learn each fact with its five neighbours: who, when, where, why, result.
- In Mains, the directive word (discuss, evaluate, examine) decides your structure; add a Bihar angle wherever it fits.
- All examples here are invented; use the official question booklets on bpsc.bihar.gov.in for real PYQs.

## Practice

1. Download the most recent CCE Prelims question booklet from bpsc.bihar.gov.in. Classify 30 questions by style (direct, statement, matching, chronology, Bihar, current affairs, GMA). Which style is most common?
2. Take one fact from your recent study and write it in four styles: direct, statement-based, matching and chronology. This trains you to see facts from all sides.
3. Make a two-column list of 15 Bihar "pairs" (place–district, river–direction, festival–month, art–region) for matching questions.
4. Write down the six directive words from the Mains table and one sentence on how your structure changes for each.
5. Pick one invented Mains example above and write a 10-line outline with a Bihar angle.
