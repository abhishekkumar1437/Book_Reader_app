This last chapter collects the questions aspirants ask most often and the mistakes that most often cost a year. Each answer is short and points you to the chapter that has the detail. Facts about rules come from the 72nd CCE advertisement (May 2026); check them again in the 73rd notification.

> **In simple words:** Most failures come from a few repeat mistakes: too many sources, no revision, no answer writing until after Prelims, ignoring Hindi and Bihar, careless OMR filling, and missing document rules. Avoid these and you are ahead of most candidates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Eligibility and application

**Q: I am in the final year of graduation. Can I apply?**
A: The 72nd advertisement required the graduation (or provisional) certificate to be issued **by the last date of the online application**, and it says certificates issued later are not valid. Read the 73rd notification on this point carefully; if your result comes after the last date, you may not be eligible for that cycle.

**Q: How is age calculated?**
A: As on the cut-off date in the notification (**1 August 2026** for the 72nd). Date of birth is taken only from your **Matriculation** certificate. See [The BPSC CCE Exam Explained](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/the-bpsc-cce-exam-explained).

**Q: How many attempts do I get?**
A: There is **no limit on attempts** for ordinary candidates (the limit was removed in 2003). Bihar government employees who use the age relaxation have a limit of **5 attempts** during service. You must still be within the age limit.

**Q: I am from another state. Can I apply?**
A: Yes, but you apply as **unreserved**. Reservation, including the 35% women's reservation (in the 72nd), is only for **permanent residents of Bihar**.

**Q: What is the fee?**
A: ₹100 per exam in the 72nd, plus ₹200 biometric fee if you do not give your Aadhaar number. Check the 73rd notification.

**Q: What is OTR?**
A: **One Time Registration** on bpsconline.bihar.gov.in. You must register once and then apply for each exam. Name, parents' names, gender and date of birth in OTR cannot be changed later, so fill them exactly as in your certificates.

### Pattern and scoring

**Q: Is there negative marking?**
A: Yes. **1/3 mark** for each wrong answer. A row left completely blank also loses 1/3. Mark **E** if you do not want to answer. See [Negative Marking and Option E Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/negative-marking-and-option-e-strategy).

**Q: Do Prelims marks count in the final merit?**
A: No. Prelims is only a screening test. Final merit = GS I + GS II + Essay (900) + Interview (120) = **1,020**.

**Q: Does the optional subject count?**
A: For the regular (Table I) posts, **no**: it is a 100-mark MCQ paper you must pass, but it is not added to merit. For CDPO, FAO and Sub-Divisional Protection Officer posts, a 300-mark descriptive optional counts.

**Q: What if I fail General Hindi?**
A: You need **30%** (30 out of 100). If you fail, your other papers are not considered. Prepare it with the [General Hindi](/interview/bpsc-2027/general-hindi) book.

**Q: Should I write Mains in Hindi or English?**
A: Choose the language in which you write fastest and most correctly. You may use Hindi, English or Urdu (one script). The medium itself does not change marks.

**Q: What is the Prelims cut-off?**
A: There is a **minimum qualifying mark** (General 40%, BC 36.5%, EBC 34%, SC/ST/women/PwD 32%) and an **actual cut-off** that depends on the paper's difficulty and the number of posts (about 10 × vacancies go to Mains). Past cut-offs are in BPSC result notices; they change every year. Aim for a safe cushion in mocks rather than one number.

### Preparation

**Q: Is 5–6 hours a day enough?**
A: Yes, if they are **regular and focused** for about 300 days, with revision. This shelf's roadmap is built for 5.5 hours a day. See [How to Use This Roadmap](/interview/bpsc-2027/roadmap/how-to-use-this-roadmap).

**Q: Do I need coaching?**
A: Not necessarily. Many candidates clear with self-study, good sources and a test series. Coaching can help with discipline, answer-writing feedback and mocks. If you join, still follow your own revision plan. What matters is regular study, revision and answer practice.

**Q: Can I prepare while working?**
A: Yes, but you will have fewer hours. Use weekdays for 2–3 hours (newspaper + one subject topic), and weekends for longer study and answer writing. Stretch the 300-day plan accordingly and protect revision days.

**Q: Should I prepare for UPSC and BPSC together?**
A: The overlap is large (history, polity, geography, economy, science). But BPSC needs much more **Bihar**, a qualifying **Hindi** paper, **statistics** in GS I and a different Prelims style. If BPSC is your main target, follow this shelf and add Bihar daily.

**Q: When should I start answer writing?**
A: From **Day 29** — 1 answer a day on the topic you studied. Do not wait for the Prelims result; the gap between Prelims and Mains is too short to learn writing from zero.

**Q: How do I choose the optional?**
A: Choose your graduation subject or a subject you can revise quickly, because it is only qualifying. See [Official Syllabus Decoded](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/official-syllabus-decoded).

**Q: Where do I get previous-year papers?**
A: From the **Question Booklets** page of bpsc.bihar.gov.in, with answer keys on the result/notice pages.

**Q: The exam date keeps changing. What do I do?**
A: Keep studying on the roadmap. When the date is announced, shift Phase 4 so that Day 300 is the day before Prelims. The 72nd Prelims was postponed twice (from 26 July 2026 and then from 25 October 2026), so delays are normal — use the extra time for revision and answer writing.

## Common mistakes (and fixes)

### Strategy mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too many books and PDFs | Nothing is revised; facts mix up | One standard source per subject + this shelf; see [Booklist and Sources](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/booklist-and-sources) |
| Reading without revising | Most of it is forgotten in a month | 1-7-30 rule; weekly revision days; see [Revision and Mock Test Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/revision-and-mock-test-strategy) |
| Prelims only, Mains "later" | 2–3 months after Prelims is not enough to learn writing | Start answer writing on Day 29 |
| Ignoring Bihar | BPSC asks Bihar in almost every subject | Read Bihar chapters alongside each subject |
| Ignoring General Hindi | Failing Hindi wastes your entire Mains | Hindi practice in Phase 3 and the Mains sprint |
| Skipping GMA | Loses certain marks in Prelims | 45 minutes daily (Days 1–245) |
| Skipping statistics in GS I | It is the most scoring Mains part | Learn the formulas and solve 40+ problems |
| Following old-pattern advice | "No negative marking" was true only before the 68th CCE | Use the current notification's rules |
| Collecting screenshots instead of notes | No memory, no revision | Four-line notes under syllabus headings |
| Studying 12 hours one day, 0 the next | Inconsistent memory and burnout | Fixed 5.5-hour routine every day |

### Exam-hall mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Leaving rows blank on the OMR | Darken **E** for every unattempted question; check all 150 rows |
| Filling the OMR only at the end | Transfer answers every page / 25 questions |
| One-row shift on the OMR | Check that the question number matches the row number |
| Misreading "NOT correct" / "incorrect" | Underline such words in the booklet |
| Blind guessing many questions | Guess only after eliminating at least one option |
| Spending 5 minutes on one hard question | Skip and return in round 2 |
| Writing very long Mains answers early and leaving the last ones | Fixed time per question; attempt every question |
| No Bihar example in Mains | Add at least one Bihar example or data point per answer where it fits |

### Document and form mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Certificate issued after the application deadline | Get all certificates (caste, non-creamy layer, EWS, domicile, disability, degree) before the last date |
| Married woman's caste certificate in husband's name | It must be in the **father's** name and address (as per the 72nd notice) |
| Wrong category or details in the form | Read every field; OTR core details cannot be corrected later |
| Not choosing a special post (CDPO/FAO) in the form | You cannot claim it later; select every post you want while applying |
| Not filling post preferences carefully at Mains | Your post is decided by rank + preference; think before you fill |
| Missing the interview | You are excluded from the final merit list |

> **Common mistake:** Not checking bpsc.bihar.gov.in regularly. Admit cards, corrigenda, date changes and instructions are announced there. Fix one day each week to check the "What's New" page.

### Mindset mistakes

- **Comparing with others on social media.** Their schedule is not yours. Follow your plan.
- **Waiting for the "perfect" time to start.** Start Day 1 on the date you choose, even if the notification is not out.
- **Giving up after one bad mock.** Mocks are for learning. Look at the trend over 5–6 mocks.
- **Neglecting health.** Sleep 7 hours, walk or exercise daily, and take one short break every 60–90 minutes. A tired mind does not remember.

## Key points

- No attempt limit for ordinary candidates; age as on the notification cut-off date; date of birth from the Matriculation certificate.
- Prelims marks do not count; Mains (900) + Interview (120) make your rank; Hindi needs 30%; the optional is qualifying.
- The biggest strategy mistakes: too many sources, no revision, late answer writing, ignoring Bihar, Hindi and statistics.
- The biggest exam mistakes: blank OMR rows, late OMR filling, misreading, blind guessing, unattempted Mains questions.
- The biggest form mistakes: late or wrong-name certificates and careless post choices.
