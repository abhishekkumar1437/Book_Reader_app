The Personality Test (interview) carries **120 marks** and is the last step before the final merit list. It is not a test of facts alone. The board wants to see whether you can become a good officer for Bihar: honest, balanced, calm, clear in thinking, aware of the state and of the world, and respectful to people. This chapter explains how the interview works, where the questions come from, how to prepare, and how to behave on the day. Prepare it seriously in the last part of the Mains sprint (roadmap Days 385–390) and again after the Mains result.

> **In simple words:** The interview is a conversation about you, your background, Bihar and current issues. Know your own form well, know your district and Bihar, follow the news, be honest, and stay calm.

## How the interview works

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Marks | **120** (counted in final merit; total 1,020) |
| Who is called | About **2.5 times** the vacancies, category-wise, from Mains results |
| Attendance | **Compulsory** — absent candidates are left out of the final merit list |
| Where | BPSC office, 15 Nehru Path (Bailey Road), Patna (check your interview letter) |
| Documents | Original certificates (degree, caste, domicile, EWS, non-creamy layer, disability, etc.) as uploaded in the online form |
| After the interview | Medical examination by the Commission's medical board (and physical standards for DSP) |
| Duration | Commonly reported as about **15–25 minutes** per candidate (varies) |

</div>

> **Remember:** At the interview stage you must show **original** certificates matching what you uploaded. Many candidates lose their claim (for example, reservation) because a certificate was issued after the application deadline or in the wrong name. Check this months before.

### The board

A board is usually chaired by the **Chairman or a Member of BPSC**, with **expert members** (for example, a senior retired officer, a subject expert, an academic). Members take turns asking questions. One member may lead on your educational subject, another on your district or hobbies, another on administration and current affairs.

## What the board judges

| Quality | What it looks like in the room |
|---|---|
| Clarity of thought | Short, direct, logical answers |
| Balanced judgement | Seeing both sides; no extreme views |
| Honesty | "I do not know, sir/madam" when you do not know |
| Awareness | Knowledge of Bihar, your district, current issues |
| Administrative aptitude | Practical, legal and humane solutions to situations |
| Communication | Simple language, polite tone, good listening |
| Integrity and empathy | Concern for the poor, women, marginalised groups; respect for rules |
| Composure | Calm under pressure or disagreement |

## Where questions come from

### 1. Your DAF (Detailed Application Form)

The **DAF** is the detailed information you give in the online form: name, date of birth, hometown and district, education (school, college, subjects, marks), work experience, hobbies, achievements, extra-curricular activities, post preferences. Many questions start here.

| DAF item | Typical questions |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Meaning of your name; a famous person with the same name |
| **Hometown / district** | History, famous places, rivers, crops, problems, development schemes, famous personalities of your district |
| **Education** | Basic questions from your graduation subject; why you chose it; how it helps administration |
| **Work experience** | What you did; why you are leaving; what you learnt |
| **Hobbies** | Deep questions on your hobby (if "reading", which books, which author, latest book) |
| **Post preferences** | Why SDM first? Why DSP? What does a Revenue Officer do? |
| **Gap years** | What you did in those years |

> **Common mistake:** Writing a hobby you do not really have ("chess", "reading philosophy") to look impressive. Board members often know the hobby well and ask detailed questions. Write only true hobbies and prepare them deeply.

### 2. Bihar

Expect questions on:

- Your district and division; neighbouring districts.
- Bihar's history (Magadha, Nalanda, Champaran, Quit India), culture (Chhath, Madhubani), geography (Kosi, floods), economy (per-capita income, migration, agriculture), polity (legislature, Panchayati Raj, reservation), and the main state schemes (check latest).
- Problems of Bihar and your **solutions** as an officer: floods, migration, low industrialisation, education quality, health, land disputes, law and order.
- Recent Bihar news.

### 3. Current affairs

- Big national and international events of the last few months.
- Your **opinion** on an issue — give a balanced view with facts.
- The day's newspaper — read it on the morning of the interview.

### 4. The post and administration

- Duties of the posts you chose: SDM (law and order, revenue, magistrate powers, disaster management at the sub-division), DSP (police supervision of a sub-division), BDO-type and Revenue Officer roles (land records, mutation, schemes).
- Basic laws an officer uses — at concept level: e.g. what is the prohibitory-order power that was Section 144 of the old CrPC (now Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, in force since 1 July 2024), what is mutation of land, what is the Right to Public Services Act.

### 5. Situation (administrative) questions

You are given a problem and asked what you would do. There is rarely one "right" answer; the board checks your values and practicality.

**How to answer a situation question:**

1. **Understand** — restate the problem in one line.
2. **Immediate action** — safety of people, law and order, the most urgent step.
3. **Within the law** — follow rules and procedure; inform seniors.
4. **People-centred** — think of the weakest people affected.
5. **Long-term fix** — prevent it happening again.

## Sample questions and answer approaches

All questions below are **examples in BPSC style** — invented for practice, not reported questions from a real board.

**Q1. "Tell us about yourself."**
*Approach:* 60–90 seconds. Name, district, education, one or two true strengths or experiences, why public service. Do not repeat your whole form. End with what you want to do as an officer.

**Q2. "Your district is often affected by floods. As SDM, what would you do before the monsoon?"**
*Approach:* Preparedness: check embankments with the water resources department; identify flood shelters; stock food, medicine and boats; update contact lists of volunteers and NGOs; mock drills; early-warning communication through panchayats; special care for pregnant women, the elderly and disabled people. Mention coordination with SDRF/NDRF and district disaster plans.

**Q3. "Why do so many people migrate from Bihar for work?"**
*Approach:* Balanced: low industrial base, small landholdings, floods, population density — and the positive side (remittances, skills). Then what helps: skill training, agro-processing (makhana, litchi), MSMEs, better roads and power, social security for migrants. Mention a Bihar initiative you know well (check latest).

**Q4. "You chose DSP as your first preference. Why not SDM?"**
*Approach:* Be honest and positive. Give a genuine reason (interest in law and order, a personal experience). Do not criticise the other post.

**Q5. "What is your view on prohibition in Bihar?"**
*Approach:* Facts first (Bihar's prohibition law of 2016). Then a balanced view: social benefits reported (domestic violence, household savings) and challenges (illicit liquor, enforcement burden, court cases). As an officer, you would implement the law fairly and support awareness and de-addiction. Avoid political statements.

**Q6. "A powerful local person asks you to change a land record in his favour. What will you do?"**
*Approach:* Refuse politely and firmly; follow the procedure; record the request if needed; inform seniors if pressure continues; make sure the rightful owner is protected. Integrity is the point of this question.

**Q7. A question you cannot answer — "What is the latest rank of Bihar in index X?"**
*Approach:* "I am sorry, sir/madam, I do not remember the exact figure." Optionally add what you do know ("I know it is published by ..."). Never guess a number.

**Q8. "What is the meaning of Nalanda, and why is it important today?"**
*Approach:* Ancient university founded in the Gupta period (Kumaragupta I, 5th century CE), famous in Harsha's time and under the Palas, visited by Xuanzang; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016); revival as **Nalanda University** at Rajgir (re-established by an Act of Parliament in 2010). Link to Bihar's knowledge heritage and education today.

## How to prepare

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Throughout | Newspaper daily; Bihar notes; opinions on major issues in a balanced format |
| Mains sprint end (Days 385–390) | Interview outline: DAF analysis, district profile, Bihar profile |
| After Mains result | Mock interviews (2–4 is enough), DAF question bank, current affairs of the last 3–4 months |
| Week before | Read your own DAF many times; revise district and Bihar profiles; plan clothes and documents |
| Day before | Read the news; sleep well; keep documents ready in order |

**Make three documents:**

1. **DAF question bank** — for every line of your form, 5–10 possible questions with short answers.
2. **District profile** — history, geography, rivers, crops, industries, famous persons, problems, schemes, recent news.
3. **Bihar profile** — one page each on history, culture, geography, economy (latest figures, check), polity and schemes.

## Dress and behaviour

### Dress

- **Simple, clean, formal.** Men: light-coloured formal shirt, dark trousers, formal shoes; a tie or a blazer is optional. Women: a simple saree or salwar-kameez in a sober colour, or formal western wear; simple footwear.
- Avoid bright colours, heavy jewellery, strong perfume.
- Wear something comfortable that you have worn before.

### Behaviour

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Knock, ask permission to enter, greet the board | Sit before being asked |
| Sit straight, hands relaxed, look at the person asking | Look at the floor or ceiling |
| Listen to the full question; pause 2–3 seconds before answering | Interrupt a member |
| Answer briefly and to the point; expand only if asked | Give long speeches |
| Say "I do not know" honestly | Bluff or guess numbers |
| Hold your view politely if you are sure; accept a correction gracefully | Argue or become defensive |
| Thank the board at the end | Show tension if a member is strict — it may be a stress test |

> **Bihar connection:** It is fine to speak in Hindi or English — speak in the language you are comfortable in, unless asked otherwise. Simple, correct language is better than difficult words. Knowing a few lines about your own district's folk culture or dialect (Bhojpuri, Maithili, Magahi, Angika, Bajjika) often makes a good impression.

## Key points

- 120 marks; about 2.5 × vacancies called; attendance compulsory; original documents checked.
- The board judges clarity, balance, honesty, awareness, administrative aptitude and composure.
- Questions come from your DAF, your district, Bihar, current affairs, your chosen posts and situations.
- Prepare a DAF question bank, a district profile and a Bihar profile; take a few mock interviews.
- In situation questions: urgent action → within the law → people-centred → long-term fix.
- Dress simply and formally; be polite, brief and honest.

## Practice

1. Write a 90-second "tell us about yourself" answer and practise it aloud until it sounds natural.
2. Make a one-page profile of your home district (history, rivers, crops, famous people, problems, recent news).
3. List five true hobbies or interests and write five likely questions for each.
4. Write your answer approach for this situation (example in BPSC style): "A school in your block has no teacher coming for weeks. As the officer, what will you do?"
5. Ask a friend or family member to ask you 10 questions from this chapter. Note where you hesitated, and prepare those areas.
