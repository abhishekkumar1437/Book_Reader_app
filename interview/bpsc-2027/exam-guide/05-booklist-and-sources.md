The most common reason for failure in state PSC exams is not "too few books" but **too many**. Candidates buy ten books for one subject, read none of them twice, and forget everything. This chapter gives you a short, complete list: NCERTs for the base, one standard book per subject, official Bihar sources for the state part, one newspaper, and this shelf to tie it together. It also lists what to avoid.

> **In simple words:** NCERT first, then one standard book per subject, then Bihar official documents, then current affairs — and revise the same sources many times. One book read five times beats five books read once.

How this shelf fits in: the subject books here (history, polity, geography, economy, science, bihar-special and others) are written at **state-PSC depth** and are meant to be your **main reading and revision notes**. Use NCERTs to build the base and clear doubts; use one standard book where you want more depth; use official Bihar documents for current Bihar data.

## NCERT books (class 6–12) by subject

NCERT books are free to read and download at **ncert.nic.in** (e-textbooks) and through the ePathshala app. Both Hindi and English versions exist.

> **Remember:** NCERT changed (rationalised) several textbooks in recent years and removed some chapters. If a topic is missing in the new edition, read it in the older edition or in this shelf's chapter. For history in particular, many teachers still recommend the **old NCERT** books as well.

### History and culture

| Class | Book | What to take from it |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | *Our Pasts – I* | Prehistory, Harappa, Vedic, Mahajanapadas, Mauryas |
| 7 | *Our Pasts – II* | Medieval India basics, Sultanate, Mughals, Bhakti–Sufi |
| 8 | *Our Pasts – III* | British rule, 1857, national movement (simple) |
| 11 | *Themes in World History* | Optional; skim only |
| 12 | *Themes in Indian History* Parts I–III | Harappa, Mauryas, Buddhism, Vijayanagar, Mughals, 1857, Gandhi, Constitution |
| 11 | *An Introduction to Indian Art* (Fine Arts) | Very useful for art and culture (Mauryan, Pala art, temples) |
| Old NCERT | Ancient India (R.S. Sharma), Medieval India (Satish Chandra), Modern India (Bipan Chandra) | Classic base books; widely used |

## Standard books (one per subject)

You do not need all of these. Pick **one per subject** and read it alongside this shelf's chapters where you want more depth or more practice. The names below are standard, widely-used titles; use the latest edition you can find.

| Subject | Commonly used standard book | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Polity | *Indian Polity* — M. Laxmikanth | Articles, bodies, amendments; reference while reading the polity book |
| Modern history | *A Brief History of Modern India* (Spectrum) — Rajiv Ahir, or *India's Struggle for Independence* — Bipan Chandra and others | National movement depth |
| Ancient & medieval history | Old NCERTs (R.S. Sharma, Satish Chandra) | Base; this shelf's history book for exam focus |
| Art & culture | *Indian Art and Culture* — Nitin Singhania | Optional; NCERT Fine Arts + this shelf may be enough |
| Geography | *Certificate Physical and Human Geography* — G.C. Leong + an atlas | World physical geography |
| Atlas | Oxford School Atlas or an equivalent recent atlas | Maps for India and world; trace Bihar's rivers and districts |
| Economy | *Indian Economy* — Ramesh Singh (or a similar standard text) | Concepts, terms |
| General knowledge | *Lucent's General Knowledge* | Quick revision of facts; good for Prelims science and history one-liners |
| Reasoning & arithmetic | One standard reasoning book + the [Quantitative Aptitude](/interview/quant-aptitude) book on this app | GMA practice |
| General Hindi | One standard *सामान्य हिंदी* grammar book (class 10 level) | Grammar rules and practice |

> **Common mistake:** Reading a 1,000-page standard book cover to cover in Phase 1. Use standard books **topic-wise** with this shelf: read the shelf chapter first, then open the standard book only for the parts you want deeper.

## Bihar-specific sources

BPSC values Bihar knowledge, and the best Bihar data comes from the **state government itself**.

| Source | Where | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| **Bihar Economic Survey** (आर्थिक सर्वेक्षण) | Finance Department, Government of Bihar — finance.bihar.gov.in; released before the state Budget each year | Growth rate, per-capita income, sector shares, agriculture, industry, health, education data. Read the summary chapter and the chapter overviews. |
| **Bihar Budget** speech and highlights | finance.bihar.gov.in; news coverage | Budget size, priorities, new schemes |
| State government portal | state.bihar.gov.in; department websites | Scheme details, official names, launch years |
| Bihar Legislative Assembly / Council | official websites | Seats, structure |
| Census of India 2011 (Bihar) | censusindia.gov.in | Population, density, sex ratio, literacy (until the next census results are out) |
| Bihar caste-based survey 2023 reports | state government | Population shares by category, socio-economic data — verify figures from official sources |
| Patna High Court, BPSC, Bihar Police websites | official sites | Institutional facts |
| **This shelf:** [Bihar Special](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special) | — | History, culture, geography, economy, polity, schemes and facts of Bihar in one place |

**Bihar-focused books:** Many publishers sell "Bihar GK" or "Bihar Samanya Gyan" books, and some write Bihar history, culture and economy books for BPSC. Pick **one** recent edition from a reliable publisher if you want extra practice. Check figures against the official Economic Survey, because private books often carry old data.

> **Bihar connection:** For Mains, two or three **official Bihar figures** (per-capita income, growth rate, literacy, flood-prone area) in an answer make it look like an officer's answer. Always write the source and year ("as per the Bihar Economic Survey 2025-26"). Use the latest survey available when you write — figures change every year.

## Newspapers

Read **one** national newspaper daily, plus Bihar news from one source.

| Type | Options | Note |
|---|---|---|
| National English | *The Hindu* or *The Indian Express* | Editorials and national/international news |
| National Hindi | *Dainik Jagran*, *Hindustan*, *Dainik Bhaskar* (Bihar editions) | Good for Bihar news; also fine as the main paper if you prefer Hindi |
| Government sources | PIB (pib.gov.in), All India Radio / DD News bulletins | Official versions of schemes and events |

> **Remember:** You do not need two full newspapers. One national paper + the Bihar page of one Hindi paper (or a Bihar news section online) is enough. Spend **1 hour a day** in total, as per the roadmap. The method is in [Reading the Newspaper and Making Notes](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/reading-the-newspaper-and-making-notes).

## Magazines and monthly compilations

- **One** monthly current-affairs compilation (from any reliable coaching institute or a monthly magazine). Use it to fill gaps in your own notes, not as a replacement.
- Government magazines: *Yojana* and *Kurukshetra* (Publications Division) — good for Mains themes (rural development, schemes). Read selectively: one or two articles per issue.
- A yearly current-affairs book in the last 2–3 months before Prelims (optional).

## Previous-year papers

- Official Prelims question booklets and answer keys: **bpsc.bihar.gov.in → Question Booklets**.
- Mains papers: also available from BPSC and many coaching websites. Use them from Phase 3.
- PYQ "solved" books are helpful, but check the answers against BPSC's final answer key.

## What NOT to buy

| Do not buy | Why |
|---|---|
| Several standard books for one subject | You will not finish or revise them |
| Thick "complete GS in one book" guides | Shallow, error-prone, hard to revise |
| Multiple current-affairs magazines | Repetition; wastes the 1-hour CA block |
| Old editions with outdated pattern advice ("no negative marking", "optional 300 marks") | Wrong strategy since the 68th CCE |
| Every mock-test series in the market | Choose **one** good series for Phase 4, plus official PYQs |
| Bihar GK books with no edition year | Old data costs marks |
| Notes bought from toppers before you have read the basics | Their notes help them, not you; make your own |

> **Common mistake:** "Collecting" PDFs on the phone. Downloading 5 GB of material feels like progress, but it is not. If a source is not in your revision plan, delete it.

## Key points

- Base = NCERT class 6–12 (free at ncert.nic.in); use old NCERTs for history if needed.
- One standard book per subject at most, used topic-wise with this shelf.
- Bihar data from official sources: Bihar Economic Survey, Bihar Budget, state portals, Census 2011.
- One newspaper + Bihar news; one monthly compilation; PIB for official facts.
- PYQs and answer keys from bpsc.bihar.gov.in.
- Do not buy many books, old-pattern guides or multiple magazines. Revise few sources many times.
