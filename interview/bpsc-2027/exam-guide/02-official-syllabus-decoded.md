The official BPSC syllabus is short — a few lines for Prelims and a few paragraphs for each Mains paper. Short does not mean small. Each line hides a large area of study. This chapter takes every syllabus line, explains what it means, says how deep to go, and links you to the chapter on this shelf that covers it. Use it as your master checklist: tick a line only when you have read the chapter, made notes and done its practice.

> **In simple words:** The syllabus is a map. This chapter writes the street names on it. When you are confused about "do I need to study this?", come back here.

## How to read the syllabus

- **Prelims** tests **breadth** — many facts across all subjects, at "well-educated person" level.
- **Mains** tests **depth and expression** — can you explain, analyse and add Bihar examples in 300 words?
- **Bihar** appears in almost every line. BPSC writes "India **and Bihar**" again and again. Treat the Bihar part of every topic as compulsory.
- Where a line is vague (for example "current events"), past papers show the real scope. Chapter 4 ([How BPSC Asks Questions](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/how-bpsc-asks-questions)) describes the patterns.

> **Remember:** The official syllabus is published on bpsc.bihar.gov.in (Syllabus page → Combined Competitive Exam). The wording below follows the 72nd CCE advertisement and the standard published CCE syllabus. Check it again with the 73rd notification.

## Prelims — General Studies (150 marks)

The 72nd advertisement says the paper will have questions on: General Science; current events of national and international importance; History of India and salient features of Bihar's history; general geography, Bihar's main geographical divisions and its important rivers; Indian polity and economy, and major changes in Bihar's economy after independence; the Indian National Movement and Bihar's contribution to it; and questions testing general mental ability.

### Line 1 — General Science

**What it means:** Science of everyday observation and experience, at the level expected of a well-educated person who has **not** specialised in science. Physics, chemistry and biology basics; human body; diseases and nutrition; plus science and technology in the news.

**How deep:** NCERT class 6–10 science fully, selected class 11–12 biology topics (human physiology, genetics basics). Facts, units, inventors, "why" of everyday things (why the sky is blue, why ice floats).

## Mains — General Studies Paper I (300 marks)

GS Paper I has three parts.

### GS I, Part 1 — Modern history of India and Indian culture

**What it means:** History of India from about the **middle of the 19th century** — the British policies, 1857, social reform, the national movement up to independence — with **Bihar's role** in it (1857 in Bihar, the Santhal and Birsa movements, Champaran, Quit India, the Kisan Sabha). It also includes **Indian culture**: art, architecture, painting (including Bihar's Patna Qalam and Madhubani), music, dance, literature, and the main ideas of great thinkers (Gandhi, Tagore, Nehru and others).

**How deep:** Prelims facts + "why and how" + Bihar examples. You must be able to write 300 words with structure, not just lists.

## Mains — Optional subject (100 marks, MCQ, qualifying)

**What it means:** 100 objective questions in **one** subject of your choice (2 hours). You must reach the minimum mark fixed by the state government, but the marks **do not count** in merit (for Table I posts).

**What to choose:** The subject of your graduation, or a subject you find easy and quick to revise (for example History, Geography, Political Science, Public Administration, Sociology, Hindi Literature). Since it is only qualifying, aim for **safe passing**, not a high score. Spend about **2–3 weeks** in the Mains sprint, using a standard graduation-level book and that subject's MCQ practice.

This shelf does **not** have an optional book. Your GS reading already covers much of History, Geography, Polity and Economics optionals.

> **Common mistake:** Choosing an optional "because toppers chose it". For a qualifying paper, choose comfort, not fashion.

## Interview (120 marks)

There is no syllabus. The board asks about your **DAF** (Detailed Application Form: education, hometown, hobbies, job), Bihar, current affairs, and situation questions about administration. See [The Interview (Personality Test)](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/the-interview-personality-test).

## Key points

- The Prelims syllabus has seven heads; **Bihar** is attached to history, geography, economy and the national movement.
- GS I = modern history & culture (from mid-19th century, with Bihar) + current events + **statistics, graphs and diagrams**.
- GS II = polity (India and Bihar) + economy and geography (India and Bihar) + **role of S&T in India's and Bihar's development**.
- Essay = 3 sections, 1 essay each; General Hindi needs 30%; the optional is 100 MCQs, qualifying only.
- Each syllabus line above has a book and chapter on this shelf. Use this chapter as your master checklist.
