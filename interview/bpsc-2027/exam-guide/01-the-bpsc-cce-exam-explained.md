This chapter explains the exam you are preparing for: what the BPSC Combined Competitive Examination (CCE) is, which jobs it gives, how its three stages work, who can apply, and how the pattern has changed in recent cycles. All numbers come from the official **Integrated 72nd CCE advertisement (5 May 2026)** and later official notices on **bpsc.bihar.gov.in**. Your exam will most likely be the **73rd CCE** ("BPSC 2027"). Its notification is not out yet (as of October 2026). Assume it follows the 72nd pattern, and check every rule again when the 73rd advertisement is published.

> **In simple words:** BPSC runs one big exam every year to fill many Bihar government officer posts — SDM, DSP, Revenue Officer, Block officers and more. You clear Prelims (a filter), then Mains (where your rank is really made), then an Interview. Your final rank comes from 900 Mains marks plus 120 interview marks.

## What BPSC and the CCE are

**BPSC** means the **Bihar Public Service Commission**. It is a constitutional body. Article 315 of the Constitution says each state shall have a Public Service Commission. BPSC's office is at **15, Nehru Path (Bailey Road), Patna**. (You will study state PSCs in [Constitutional and Statutory Bodies](/interview/bpsc-2027/polity/constitutional-and-statutory-bodies).)

The **Combined Competitive Examination (CCE)** is BPSC's main civil-services exam. "Combined" means one exam fills many different services at once: administration, police, finance, revenue, rural development, cooperation, labour, supply and others. People often call it "BPSC 70th", "BPSC 71st" and so on. The number is the exam's serial number. The official name is now "**Integrated** 72nd Combined Competitive Examination", because it is combined with some special posts (see below).

Why this matters for you: one application gives you a chance at more than 20 posts. You choose your post preference order in the Mains application. Your rank and your preference together decide your post.

## The posts (idea level)

The 72nd advertisement listed posts in two tables.

- **Table I (Sāraṇī I)** — the regular CCE posts. All candidates write the same Mains papers.
- **Table II (Sāraṇī II)** — "other" special posts with an extra subject condition and a different Mains optional paper.

The notices themselves give these totals. The lesson: **the number of posts can change after the notification.** The advertisement itself says the number may go up or down. Always use the latest official corrigendum (शुद्धि-पत्र).

> **Prelims angle:** Questions about BPSC itself are rare in Prelims. But Article 315–323 (Public Service Commissions) is standard polity content. Know that state PSC members are appointed by the Governor and removed only by the President.

### Stage 1 — Preliminary Examination (Prelims)

<div class="formula">

| Fact | Detail (72nd CCE, official) |
|---|---|
| Paper | One paper: General Studies |
| Questions / marks | 150 MCQs, 150 marks (1 mark each) |
| Time | 2 hours (72nd: 12:00 noon to 2:00 pm was announced) |
| Options | Five: A, B, C, D, **E** |
| Option E | Must be marked if you do **not** want to answer a question |
| Row left fully blank | Treated as un-answered: **−1/3** mark |
| Wrong answer | **−1/3** mark |
| Language | Hindi and English |
| Minimum qualifying marks | General 40% · BC 36.5% · EBC 34% · SC/ST, women, PwD 32% |
| Who goes to Mains | About **10 times** the vacancies, category-wise; everyone at the cut-off mark is included |
| Counts in final merit? | **No** — screening only |

</div>

Chapter 3 ([Negative Marking and Option E Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/negative-marking-and-option-e-strategy)) explains this scoring in detail.

**Prelims syllabus (official heads):** General Science; current events of national and international importance; History of India and salient features of Bihar's history; general geography, Bihar's main geographical divisions and important rivers; Indian polity and economy, and major changes in Bihar's economy after independence; the Indian National Movement and Bihar's contribution; General Mental Ability. Chapter 2 decodes each line.

### Stage 2 — Main (written) Examination

| Paper | Marks | Time | Counts in merit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| General Hindi | 100 | 3 h | No — but you **must score 30%** (30 marks) |
| General Studies Paper I | 300 | 3 h | Yes |
| General Studies Paper II | 300 | 3 h | Yes |
| Essay | 300 | 3 h | Yes |
| Optional subject (one of 34) | 100 (100 MCQs) | 2 h | No — must reach the minimum set by the state government |

- Papers are printed in **Hindi and English**.
- You may answer non-language papers in **Hindi (Devanagari), English or Urdu**, but in **one** script only. Technical terms may be given in English in brackets.
- The notification also sets minimum qualifying marks in the Mains written exam: **40%** General, **36.5%** BC, **34%** EBC, **32%** SC/ST, women and PwD.
- About **2.5 times** the vacancies (category-wise) are called for interview.
- For Table II posts (CDPO, FAO, Protection Officer) the Mains is General Hindi + GS I + GS II + a **300-mark descriptive optional** (3 h) that **counts**; merit = GS I + GS II + optional (900) + interview (120).

**The 34 optional subjects (Table I posts):** Agriculture; Animal Husbandry & Veterinary Science; Anthropology; Botany; Chemistry; Civil Engineering; Commerce & Accountancy; Economics; Electrical Engineering; Geography; Geology; History; Labour & Social Welfare; Law; Management; Mathematics; Mechanical Engineering; Philosophy; Physics; Political Science & International Relations; Psychology; Public Administration; Sociology; Statistics; Zoology; and the language-and-literature papers of Hindi, English, Urdu, Bengali, Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic, Pali and Maithili.

### Stage 3 — Personality Test (Interview)

- **120 marks.** Attendance is compulsory; if you skip it, you are left out of the final merit list.
- A medical examination by the Commission's medical board follows. DSP candidates also have physical standards (published in the advertisement's appendix).
- Final merit: **GS I 300 + GS II 300 + Essay 300 + Interview 120 = 1,020**.
- Tie rule: higher Mains written marks rank higher; if still equal, the older candidate ranks higher; if still equal, the name order in the Devanagari alphabet decides.

> **Mains angle:** Because Prelims marks do not count, many toppers are people who wrote strong Mains answers, not people with the highest Prelims score. That is why this shelf starts answer writing on Day 29, not after Prelims.

## Eligibility

### Education

- A **bachelor's degree** (or equivalent) from a recognised university. Any subject is fine for Table I posts.
- Table II posts need the specific subjects shown above.
- The degree certificate (or provisional certificate) must be issued **by the last date of the online application**. A certificate issued after that date is not accepted. (Final-year students: read the 73rd notification carefully on this point; the 72nd rule was strict.)

### Age (as on 1 August 2026, for the 72nd CCE)

<div class="formula">

| Category | Maximum age |
|---|---|
| Unreserved (UR) — male | 37 years |
| Unreserved — female | 40 years |
| BC and EBC — male and female | 40 years |
| SC and ST — male and female | 42 years |

</div>

**Minimum age depends on the post:**

| Minimum age | Posts (72nd CCE) |
|---|---|
| 20 years | DSP (as printed in the May 2026 advertisement) |
| 21 years | Table I posts no. 12–21 (Probation Officer, Asst Planning Officer, Executive Officer, Rural Development Officer, Revenue Officer, BPRO, Cooperative Extension Officer, Labour Enforcement Officer, Supply Inspector, Block SC/ST Welfare Officer); CDPO; Sub-Divisional Protection Officer |
| 22 years | SDM and Table I posts no. 3–11 and 22; FAO |

> **Common mistake:** Do not assume the same minimum age for all posts. There is even a small conflict in the official papers: the May advertisement prints **20 years** for DSP, while one of the two 3 September 2026 corrigendum versions prints **22 years** in the DSP row. When your notification comes, check the age column for every post you want.

**Date of birth** is taken only from your **Matriculation** (class 10) certificate.

### Relaxations and attempts (72nd rules, summary)

- **No limit on the number of attempts** for ordinary candidates (Bihar removed the attempt limit in 2003).
- **Bihar government employees:** up to **5 extra years** in the upper age limit, but only **5 attempts in total** during their service (counted from the December 2022 resolution).
- **Persons with benchmark disability (PwBD):** **10 years** extra.
- **Ex-servicemen:** relaxation equal to military service plus 3 years (actual age not above 57 at application); details in the notification.
- Former NCC cadet instructors and others: see the notification.

### Reservation — the Bihar domicile rule

- Reservation benefits (SC, ST, EBC, BC, BCL — backward-class women, EWS) are given **only to permanent residents (domicile) of Bihar**. Candidates from other states can apply but only as unreserved.
- **35% horizontal reservation for women** — in the 72nd notification this is given **only to women who are permanent residents of Bihar** (following a July 2025 government decision).
- **4%** horizontal reservation for PwBD (Bihar domicile).
- **2%** for grandchildren of freedom fighters.
- **10% EWS** reservation (income-and-asset certificate valid one year).
- BC/EBC candidates need a **non-creamy-layer** certificate. A married woman's caste certificate must be in her **father's** name.
- All certificates must be issued **on or before the last date of application**.

> **Bihar connection:** The 2025 change that limited women's 35% reservation to Bihar domiciles was big news in Bihar. Know it for the interview too — it is a domicile and gender-justice question.

### Fee (72nd)

**₹100** per exam (for example, 72nd CCE, CDPO, FAO and Protection Officer are counted as separate exams). Candidates who do not give an Aadhaar number pay an extra **₹200** biometric fee. Apply only on **bpsconline.bihar.gov.in** after **One Time Registration (OTR)**; the 72nd window was 7–31 May 2026.

## What has changed in recent years

| Change | Roughly when | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| **Essay paper (300) added** to Mains, optional cut from 300 marks descriptive to **100 marks MCQ, qualifying only** | Announced for the 68th CCE (late 2022) | Essay is now one-third of your written merit. Optional needs only basic preparation. |
| **Negative marking** introduced in Prelims | 68th CCE (Prelims Feb 2023), first reported as 1/4 mark | Random guessing is no longer free. |
| Negative marking at **1/3 mark** | Later cycles; confirmed in the 72nd advertisement | Each wrong answer costs one-third of a correct one. |
| **Five options (A–E)** on the OMR | Recent cycles | E has changed meaning over time (see below). |
| **Option E = "I do not want to answer"**; a fully blank row is penalised like a wrong answer | 72nd advertisement (May 2026) | You must fill **every** row. |
| "Integrated" CCE: CDPO, FAO, Protection Officer posts through the same Prelims | Recent cycles | One Prelims, several chances. |
| Women's 35% reservation limited to Bihar domicile | 72nd (based on July 2025 decision) | Affects women candidates from other states. |

> **Common mistake:** Many old books and videos still say "no negative marking in BPSC" or "optional is 300 marks". These were true before the 68th CCE. Do not follow outdated strategy.

### The changing meaning of option E

In some earlier CCE Prelims papers, coaching summaries describe option E as an answer choice such as "More than one of the above / None of the above". In the 72nd advertisement, the official rule is different: **E is the option you must mark when you do not wish to answer**, and if you mark none of the five, you lose 1/3 mark. Some questions may still list "More than one of the above" or "None of the above" **inside** options A–D. So:

- Read the instructions on **your** notification, admit card and OMR sheet.
- Never leave a row blank.

## How much time do you have?

If your 73rd CCE Prelims comes around August–September 2027, a start on 1 November 2026 gives you about 300 days. That is exactly why this shelf has a 300-day plan to Prelims and a 90-day Mains sprint. With 5–6 hours a day, it is enough — if you are regular. Being regular matters more than studying many hours on a few days.

## Key points

- BPSC CCE fills 20+ Bihar officer posts through **Prelims → Mains → Interview**.
- Prelims: 150 MCQs, 150 marks, 2 h, **−1/3** per wrong answer, **option E** for "not answering", blank row also **−1/3**; qualifying only; minimum 40/36.5/34/32%.
- Mains: Hindi 100 (30% to pass), GS I 300, GS II 300, Essay 300, optional 100 MCQ (qualifying). Final merit **1,020** = 900 + 120 interview.
- Eligibility: graduate; age as on 1 August of the notification year; max 37 (UR male), 40 (UR female, BC, EBC), 42 (SC/ST); min 20–22 by post.
- Reservation only for Bihar domiciles; women's 35% only for Bihar-domicile women (72nd).
- Post numbers and dates change through corrigenda and postponements — follow bpsc.bihar.gov.in.

## Practice

1. Download the latest CCE advertisement from bpsc.bihar.gov.in (72nd now; 73rd when it comes). Write in your notebook: your category, your age on the cut-off date, and the posts you are eligible for.
2. Rank your top 8 posts from the Table I list in order of preference, with one line on why for each. You will need this for the Mains form and the interview.
3. Make a one-page "exam facts" sheet (pattern, marks, negative marking, option E, Hindi 30%) and stick it near your desk.
4. Check whether your caste, domicile, non-creamy-layer and EWS certificates are valid, in the right name, and issued before a likely application deadline. List anything you must get made.
5. Bookmark the BPSC "What's New" page and decide one fixed day each week to check it.
