Your rank is made in Mains: GS Paper I, GS Paper II and the Essay give **900 of the 1,020** merit marks. Mains is descriptive — you write answers in your own words — so knowledge alone is not enough. You must write clearly, in structure, within the word limit, with examples and a Bihar angle, and finish the paper in time. This chapter gives you the basics so that you can start the daily **30-minute answer-writing block from Day 29**. For full depth and model answers, use the [Mains GS Answer Writing](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing) book.

> **In simple words:** Read the question carefully, plan for one minute, write an introduction, 5–8 clear points under small headings, a Bihar example, and a short forward-looking conclusion — and finish every question.

## How descriptive papers are marked

BPSC does not publish a detailed marking scheme for each answer. But examiners of all civil-service exams look for broadly the same things. Think of every answer as being judged on:

| What the examiner looks for | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Relevance** | You answer the exact question asked (the directive word and all its parts) |
| **Content** | Correct facts, concepts, examples, data |
| **Structure** | Introduction, body with clear points, conclusion |
| **Bihar and India link** | Where relevant, examples from Bihar and India |
| **Balance** | For "discuss/evaluate", both sides and a fair judgement |
| **Presentation** | Readable handwriting, headings, short paragraphs, underlining (a little) |
| **Completeness** | All questions attempted; word limits respected |

> **Remember:** Examiners check many copies quickly. An answer that is easy to read — with headings, points and a clear flow — is easier to reward. A long block of text hides your good points.

### The qualifying papers

- **General Hindi (100):** you need **30%**. It is marked like a school exam: correct grammar, a structured essay, an accurate précis. See [Paper Pattern and Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/general-hindi/paper-pattern-and-strategy).
- **Optional (100 MCQs):** objective; no answer writing. Only the minimum mark is needed.

### Language

You may write in **Hindi, English or Urdu** (one script for the whole of non-language papers). Choose the language in which you can write **fastest and most correctly**. Marks do not depend on which language you choose; they depend on clarity.

## Word limits and length

Read the instructions on the question paper: they state the number of questions to attempt and the word limit or marks for each. As a working guide (check the latest paper):

| Question type | Typical length | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Long GS question (around 38 marks, as in recent papers) | **About 300–400 words**, or as stated on the paper | **About 18–20 minutes** |
| Statistics question (GS I) | Calculations + table/graph + short interpretation | About 18–20 minutes |
| Essay (one from each of three sections) | About **700–1,000 words** each, as commonly advised (check the paper) | About 55–60 minutes each |

> **Common mistake:** Writing 800 words for one question and then leaving the last question unattempted. An unattempted question gets zero; a reasonable 300-word answer gets a fair share of marks. **Attempt every question.**

### Introduction (about 10–15% of the words)

Choose **one** way to open:

- A **definition** ("Panchayati Raj is the system of rural local self-government ...").
- The **context** ("In August 1942, after the arrest of national leaders, ...").
- A **fact or data** with source ("As per Census 2011, Bihar's literacy rate was ...").
- A **constitutional or legal reference** ("Article 243 ...").

### Body (about 70–75%)

- Break the question into its **parts** and use sub-headings that match them. If the question asks "causes and consequences", use exactly those two headings.
- Write **points**, each 2–3 lines: a clear claim, why/how, and an example.
- Add **at least one Bihar example** wherever it fits.
- Use **data** with source and year where you are sure. Never invent numbers.
- Use a simple **diagram** (flowchart, small map sketch, table) when it saves words.

### Conclusion (about 10–15%)

- A balanced judgement, or a **way forward** (2–3 practical suggestions), or a link to a larger goal (inclusive growth, cooperative federalism, sustainable development).
- Keep it positive and practical; avoid repeating the body.

> **Mains angle:** Directive words decide the body. "Discuss" needs both sides; "evaluate" needs achievements, shortcomings and a verdict; "examine" needs causes/factors in depth; "describe" needs a clear account. See the table in [How BPSC Asks Questions](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/how-bpsc-asks-questions).

## A short worked example (outline)

**Question (example in BPSC style, invented, not a PYQ):** "Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement."

**Plan (1 minute):** Intro — 8 August 1942 resolution; Body — (1) Patna Secretariat, 11 August 1942; (2) spread to districts; (3) leaders; (4) underground activity — JP's escape from Hazaribagh jail; (5) peasants and students; (6) British repression; Bihar angle is the whole answer; Conclusion — significance.

**Outline:**

- **Intro:** The Quit India resolution was passed at the Bombay session of the AICC on 8 August 1942. Bihar became one of the strongest centres of the movement.
- **Body:**
  - **Patna Secretariat, 11 August 1942:** students tried to hoist the national flag; **seven students were killed** in police firing — the "Saat Shaheed" memorial in Patna marks them.
  - **Spread:** railway lines, telegraph lines and police stations were attacked across north and central Bihar; British control broke down in several areas for some time.
  - **Leaders:** Rajendra Prasad and other leaders were arrested; local leaders and students led the movement.
  - **Underground phase:** **Jayaprakash Narayan escaped from Hazaribagh jail** (November 1942) and organised underground resistance, including the Azad Dasta.
  - **Mass participation:** peasants, students, women; Kisan Sabha workers.
  - **Repression:** firing, collective fines, arrests.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's role showed the depth of mass nationalism and produced leaders who shaped post-independence politics.

Full model answers with word counts are in [Modern History and Culture Model Answers](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/modern-history-and-culture-model-answers). The facts are in [Quit India to Independence](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/quit-india-to-independence) and [Bihar in the Freedom Struggle](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-in-the-freedom-struggle).

## The Bihar angle

BPSC is selecting officers for Bihar. Show that you know the state.

| GS area | Typical Bihar angle |
|---|---|
| Modern history | 1857 (Kunwar Singh), Santhal and Birsa movements, Champaran, Quit India, Kisan Sabha, Bihar leaders |
| Culture | Madhubani, Manjusha, Patna Qalam, Chhath, Nalanda and Vikramshila, Vidyapati, Dinkar, Renu |
| Polity | Bihar Legislature (bicameral), Panchayati Raj Act 2006 (50% for women), Right to Public Services Act 2011, prohibition 2016 |
| Economy | Per-capita income gap, agriculture, floods, migration, makhana and litchi, industrial policy, Economic Survey data |
| Geography | Kosi floods, north-south divide, Ganga plains, drought in south Bihar |
| Science & tech | Flood forecasting, agri-tech, health tech, e-governance in Bihar |
| Current events | State schemes and their outcomes (check latest) |

How to add it without forcing:

- **One line, one example:** "In Bihar, the Kosi floods of 2008 showed ...".
- **One data point:** "As per the Bihar Economic Survey (latest year), ...".
- **One scheme:** "Bihar's Jeevika self-help group programme ...".

See [Adding the Bihar Angle, Data and Diagrams](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/adding-the-bihar-angle-data-and-diagrams).

## Time management in a 3-hour paper

### Plan before you write

- **First 5–8 minutes:** read the whole paper. Choose your questions (where there is a choice). Mark the order: start with the question you know best.
- **During:** keep a **time budget per question** (see below). When time is up, conclude in two lines and move on.
- **Last 5–10 minutes:** finish any incomplete answer with a few points; check question numbers.

### A sample time budget (for example, if a paper has 8 long questions)

| Activity | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Read paper and choose | 8 |
| 8 questions × 20 minutes | 160 |
| Buffer and final check | 12 |
| **Total** | **180** |

Within 20 minutes per question: **1–2 minutes** to plan (write 4–6 key words in the margin), **16–17 minutes** to write, **1 minute** to check. If your paper has a different number of questions or marks, divide the time in proportion to marks.

### The statistics section (GS I)

- These questions have a **definite answer**. Do them carefully; they are among the most scoring.
- Show every step: formula → substitution → result → **one-line interpretation**.
- Draw graphs with a ruler, label axes and give a title.
- Practise with [Statistics, Graphs and Diagrams](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/statistics-graphs-and-diagrams) and [Statistics Practice Problems](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/statistics-practice-problems).

## Presentation tips

- Write on alternate lines only if your handwriting is large; otherwise normal spacing with a gap between paragraphs.
- **Short paragraphs** (2–4 lines) and **points**.
- **Sub-headings** — underline them lightly.
- A small **diagram** or **table** in one or two answers per paper.
- Leave a small margin.
- Do not write anything that identifies you (name, roll number) inside the answers; follow the answer-book instructions.

## How to start practising (from Day 29)

| Stage | Days | Daily practice |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 29–60 | 1 answer a day on the topic you studied, no time limit; focus on structure |
| Build | 61–196 | 1–2 answers a day, 20 minutes each; add Bihar examples |
| Integrate | 197–245 | Full sections; one 3-hour paper a fortnight |
| Sprint | 301–390 | Full-length GS I and GS II tests weekly; essay weekly |

Self-check each answer: Did I answer every part? Is there an intro and a conclusion? 5–8 points? One Bihar example? Within the limit? Readable?

## Key points

- GS I + GS II + Essay = 900 of 1,020 merit marks — Mains decides your rank.
- Examiners reward relevance, correct content, structure, Bihar/India examples, balance, presentation and completeness.
- Structure: intro (10–15%) → body with 5–8 points under sub-headings (70–75%) → conclusion (10–15%).
- Follow the word limit on the paper; a long GS answer is about 300–400 words in roughly 18–20 minutes.
- Add one Bihar example or data point wherever it fits; never invent data.
- Read the paper first, keep a time budget, and attempt every question.

2. Take any one topic you studied this week and write an intro in three different ways (definition, context, data).
