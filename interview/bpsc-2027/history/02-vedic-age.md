The Vedic Age (about 1500–600 BCE) is the period of the four Vedas and the people who composed them. BPSC asks factual questions here: which Veda deals with music, how many hymns the Rigveda has, what *Sabha* and *Samiti* were, where the "Battle of Ten Kings" was fought. For Mains, this period gives the roots of the caste system, Indian philosophy and the Bihar story of **Videha** and **King Janaka**.

> **In simple words:** After the Harappan cities faded, a cattle-keeping people who spoke an early form of Sanskrit and called themselves *Arya* spread in north-west India. They composed hymns called the Rigveda. Over centuries they moved east into the Ganga plain, used iron, became farmers, and formed kingdoms. Society became more unequal, rituals became bigger, and in reaction thinkers wrote the Upanishads. North Bihar (Videha/Mithila) became an important centre of this later Vedic culture.

## Sources and dates

The main source is **Vedic literature** itself. Archaeology adds the **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)** culture of the Ganga–Yamuna doab (c. 1000–600 BCE), which is linked with the Later Vedic people, and the use of iron.

| Phase | Commonly accepted dates | Core region | Main text |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Early Vedic / Rigvedic** | c. **1500–1000 BCE** | **Sapta Sindhu** (Punjab and around) | Rigveda |
| **Later Vedic** | c. **1000–600 BCE** | Ganga–Yamuna doab, then east to Kosala, Kashi, **Videha** | Sama, Yajur, Atharva Vedas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads |

> **Remember:** The date **1500 BCE** for the Rigveda was proposed by **Max Müller** and is the standard exam answer. Some scholars push it earlier (Tilak, Jacobi); some Indian writers reject the idea of migration altogether. In the exam, use **c. 1500–1000 BCE** for Early Vedic and **1000–600 BCE** for Later Vedic.

### Where did the Aryans come from?

"Aryan" here means a **language group** (Indo-European speakers), not a race. Theories of their original home:

| Theory | Proposed by |
|---|---|
| **Central Asia** (most accepted) | **Max Müller** |
| Arctic region | **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (*The Arctic Home in the Vedas*) |
| Tibet | **Swami Dayananda Saraswati** |
| Sapta Sindhu (native) | A.C. Das, some others |
| Europe / Hungary–Austria | P. Giles and others |

> **Prelims angle:** The **Boghazkoi inscription** (Turkey, c. **1400 BCE**) — a treaty between the **Hittites** and **Mitanni** kings — names the Vedic gods **Indra, Mitra, Varuna and the Nasatyas**. It is evidence of the westward links of Vedic-speaking people.

### The four Vedas

| Veda | Content | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Rigveda** | Hymns of praise to gods | Oldest; **10 mandalas (books)**, **1,028 hymns** (1,017 + 11 *Valakhilya*), about 10,600 verses. Mandalas **II–VII** are the oldest ("family books"); I and X are latest. Priest: **Hotri** |
| **Samaveda** | Rigvedic verses set to **melody** | Book of **chants**; origin of **Indian music**; priest: **Udgatri** |
| **Yajurveda** | Formulas for **sacrifices** (prose + verse) | Two parts: **Shukla** (white) and **Krishna** (black); priest: **Adhvaryu** |
| **Atharvaveda** | Charms, spells, cures, daily life | Folk beliefs, medicine; calls **Sabha and Samiti "two daughters of Prajapati"**; mentions Magadha |

Important Rigveda facts:

- **Mandala III** has the **Gayatri Mantra**, addressed to **Savitri** (Sun), composed by **Vishvamitra**.
- **Mandala VII** describes the **Battle of Ten Kings** (*Dasarajna*), and is attributed to **Vasishtha**.
- **Mandala IX** is entirely devoted to **Soma**.
- **Mandala X** has the **Purusha Sukta** (first mention of the four varnas, born from the body of the cosmic man), the **Nasadiya Sukta** (hymn of creation) and the **Nadistuti** (praise of rivers).
- Women composers of hymns: **Lopamudra, Ghosha, Apala, Vishvavara**.

### Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads

| Veda | Brahmana | Upanishads (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| Rigveda | **Aitareya**, Kaushitaki | Aitareya, Kaushitaki |
| Yajurveda | **Shatapatha** (largest; Shukla Yajurveda), Taittiriya | **Brihadaranyaka** (oldest, largest), Isha, Katha, Taittiriya |
| Samaveda | Panchavimsha (Tandya), Jaiminiya | **Chandogya**, Kena |
| Atharvaveda | **Gopatha** | **Mundaka**, Mandukya, Prashna |

- There are **108 Upanishads** in the traditional list; about **13** are principal.
- **"Satyameva Jayate"** (India's national motto) is from the **Mundaka Upanishad**.
- The **Katha Upanishad** has the dialogue of **Nachiketa and Yama** (god of death) on the soul.
- The **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** has the debates of **Yajnavalkya** with **Gargi** and his teaching to **Maitreyi** at **King Janaka's** court — the Bihar link.
- Upanishads teach **Brahman** (universal soul), **Atman** (individual soul), **karma**, **rebirth** and **moksha** (liberation).

### Smriti and later texts

- **Six Vedangas** (limbs of the Veda): **Shiksha** (phonetics), **Kalpa** (ritual), **Vyakarana** (grammar — Panini's *Ashtadhyayi*), **Nirukta** (etymology — Yaska), **Chhanda** (metre — Pingala), **Jyotisha** (astronomy).
- **Upavedas**: **Ayurveda** (medicine, with Rigveda), **Dhanurveda** (archery/war, Yajurveda), **Gandharvaveda** (music, Samaveda), **Shilpaveda** (crafts/architecture, Atharvaveda). Some lists vary.
- **Sutras**: Shrauta (big sacrifices), Grihya (household rites), Dharma (law), **Shulba Sutras** (geometry of altars — early Indian mathematics).
- **Smritis**: **Manusmriti** (oldest law book), Yajnavalkya Smriti (later; *Mitakshara* commentary by Vijnaneshwara), Narada, Parashara.
- **Six schools of Indian philosophy (Shad-darshana):**

| School | Founder | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Sankhya** | **Kapila** | Dualism of Purusha (spirit) and Prakriti (matter); oldest school |
| **Yoga** | **Patanjali** | Discipline of body and mind (*Yoga Sutra*) |
| **Nyaya** | **Gautama (Akshapada)** | Logic |
| **Vaisheshika** | **Kanada** | Atoms (*parmanu*) |
| **Purva Mimamsa** | **Jaimini** | Vedic ritual |
| **Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta)** | **Badarayana** (*Brahma Sutra*) | Brahman; later Shankaracharya's Advaita |

> **Memory trick:** **Kanada → atoms** (*kana* means "particle"). **Kapila → Sankhya** ("K–S", the oldest school). **Gautama → Nyaya** (logic — do not confuse with Gautama Buddha). **Jaimini → ritual** (Purva = earlier part of the Veda); **Badarayana → Vedanta** (Uttara = later part).

## Early Vedic (Rigvedic) period, c. 1500–1000 BCE

### Geography: the land of seven rivers

| Rigvedic name | Modern river |
|---|---|
| **Sindhu** | Indus (most mentioned river) |
| **Vitasta** | Jhelum |
| **Asikni** | Chenab |
| **Parushni** | Ravi |
| **Vipasha** | Beas |
| **Shutudri** | Sutlej |
| **Saraswati** | Ghaggar–Hakra (most sacred; called **Naditama**, "best of rivers") |
| Gomati, Krumu, Kubha | Gomal, Kurram, Kabul (Afghanistan side) |
| Drishadvati | Chautang (Haryana) |

The **Ganga** is mentioned only **once** and the **Yamuna** about three times in the Rigveda — evidence that the early Vedic people lived mainly in the north-west.

### Polity

- Society was **tribal** (*jana*). The tribal chief was the **Rajan** — he protected the tribe and cattle; his position was not fully hereditary.
- Popular assemblies: **Sabha** (council of elders), **Samiti** (general assembly of the people, which could elect the chief), **Vidatha** (oldest assembly; also religious and military), **Gana**. Women attended the Sabha and Vidatha in this period.
- Officials: **Purohita** (chief priest, most important), **Senani** (army chief), **Gramani** (head of the village).
- **Bali** was a **voluntary** gift to the chief, not a regular tax.
- No regular army; war was often **Gavishti** — literally "search for cows".
- **Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna):** fought on the banks of the **Parushni (Ravi)**. **Sudas** of the **Bharata** tribe (advised by **Vasishtha**) defeated a confederacy of ten tribes (supported by **Vishvamitra**). The Bharatas later merged with the Purus to form the **Kurus**. India's name "Bharat" is linked with the Bharata tribe.

Social units, smallest to largest: **Kula** (family) → **Grama** (group of families) → **Vis** (clan) → **Jana** (tribe).

### Society

- **Patriarchal** family (head = *kulapa*).
- Varna (colour/class) divisions existed but were **not rigid or hereditary**; the four-varna idea appears only in the late **Purusha Sukta** (Mandala X).
- Women were respected: could attend assemblies, choose husbands, remarry; **no child marriage, no sati**. *Niyoga* (levirate) was practised.
- Food: milk, ghee, barley (*yava*), meat; drink: **Soma** (ritual) and **Sura** (alcohol, disapproved).

### Economy

- **Pastoral**: cattle were the main wealth (*gomat* = rich man; *gavishti* = war; *godhuli* = time of day; *duhitri* = daughter, "one who milks").
- Limited farming — **barley** (*yava*). Plough mentioned (*langala*/*sira*) in later parts.
- Metals: copper/bronze called **ayas**; iron not yet known.
- Barter; **nishka** (gold ornament) used as a unit of value.
- Artisans: carpenter, chariot-maker, potter, weaver, metal-worker.

### Religion

- Worship of **nature gods** through prayers and simple sacrifices; **no temples, no idols**.
- **Indra** — most important god (about **250 hymns**), god of rain and war, called **Purandara** ("breaker of forts").
- **Agni** — second (about **200 hymns**), fire god, messenger between gods and humans.
- **Varuna** — upholder of **Rita** (cosmic and moral order).
- **Soma** — god of the plant drink. **Surya, Savitri, Pushan, Ushas** (dawn goddess), **Aditi**, **Prithvi**, Maruts (storm gods), Ashvins.

## Later Vedic period, c. 1000–600 BCE

### Expansion east and the Bihar story

The Later Vedic people moved into the **Ganga–Yamuna doab** and beyond. Important regions were **Kuru** (around Delhi–Meerut) and **Panchala** (western UP), then **Kosala**, **Kashi** and **Videha**.

The **Shatapatha Brahmana** tells the story of **Videgha Mathava**, a king who, guided by **Agni** (fire), moved east from the Saraswati and crossed the river **Sadanira** (the **Gandak**) to settle **Videha** (north Bihar). Historians read this as a memory of forest clearing and settlement — probably with the help of fire and, later, iron tools.

> **Bihar connection:** 
> - **Videha** (capital **Mithila**, roughly today's Mithila region of north Bihar and the Janakpur area of Nepal) was a leading Later Vedic kingdom. **King Janaka** was famous as a philosopher-king; his court saw the debates of **Yajnavalkya**, **Gargi Vachaknavi** and **Maitreyi** (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad).
> - The Rigveda mentions **Kikata**, a people ruled by **Pramaganda**; Kikata is usually identified with **Magadha** (south Bihar).
> - The **Atharvaveda** refers to Magadha and to the **Vratyas** (people outside Vedic orthodoxy) living there — Magadha was seen as outside proper Vedic culture, which helps explain why heterodox movements (Buddhism, Jainism) later grew strong in Bihar.

### Polity: from tribe to territory

- Tribes joined into larger units; the word **Rashtra** (territory) appears. Kingship became **hereditary** and more powerful.
- Big royal sacrifices to show power:

| Sacrifice | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Rajasuya** | Royal consecration; gave supreme power |
| **Ashvamedha** | Horse sacrifice; the horse wandered freely and the area it crossed came under the king |
| **Vajapeya** | Chariot race in which the king won; restored energy |

- **Ratnins** ("jewels") — about 12 high officials, including **Purohita, Senani, Gramani, Sangrahitri (treasurer), Bhagadugha (tax collector), Suta (charioteer/bard), Akshavapa (accounts/dice), Mahishi (chief queen)**.
- **Bali, Shulka, Bhaga** became **regular** taxes.
- **Sabha and Samiti** lost importance; **Vidatha disappeared**; women could no longer attend the Sabha.
- Titles: *Samrat*, *Ekarat*, *Virat*, *Sarvabhauma* show growing power.

### Society

- **Varna system became rigid** and largely hereditary: **Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra**. Shudras were assigned service.
- **Gotra** (lineage) system started; marriage within the same gotra forbidden.
- **Ashrama system**: *Brahmacharya* (student), *Grihastha* (householder), *Vanaprastha* (retreat), *Sannyasa* (renunciation) — all four clearly listed first in the **Jabala Upanishad**.
- **Status of women declined**: barred from assemblies; some child marriage; though learned women like **Gargi** and **Maitreyi** existed.

### Economy

- **Agriculture** became the main occupation. **Iron** (called **shyama ayas** or **krishna ayas**, "black metal") was in use from about **1000 BCE** (Atranjikhera, UP).
- Crops: **rice (vrihi)**, **wheat (godhuma)**, barley, lentils, sesame. The **Shatapatha Brahmana** describes ploughing rituals.
- Pottery: **Painted Grey Ware (PGW)**.
- Trade grew; guild-like groups (*shreni*) and money-like units (*nishka, shatamana, krishnala*) are mentioned.

### Religion and philosophy

- **Prajapati** (creator) became the top god; **Vishnu** (protector) and **Rudra** (later Shiva) rose in importance; Indra and Agni declined.
- **Sacrifices became complex and costly**; priests (Brahmins) gained great power.
- **Reaction**: the **Upanishads** rejected empty ritual and stressed knowledge (*jnana*), Atman–Brahman unity, karma and moksha. This intellectual churning — much of it in **Videha** and the eastern Gangetic plain — prepared the ground for **Buddhism and Jainism** in the 6th century BCE.

## Comparing the two phases

| Feature | Early Vedic | Later Vedic |
|---|---|---|
| Region | Sapta Sindhu | Ganga–Yamuna doab → Videha |
| Economy | Pastoral; barley | Agrarian; rice, wheat, iron plough |
| Metal | Copper/bronze (*ayas*) | Iron (*shyama ayas*) |
| Polity | Tribal chief, assemblies strong | Hereditary king, territorial state, assemblies weak |
| Tax | Bali voluntary | Bali, bhaga, shulka compulsory |
| Varna | Flexible | Rigid, birth-based |
| Women | Higher status, attended assemblies | Lower status, excluded |
| Gods | Indra, Agni, Varuna | Prajapati, Vishnu, Rudra |
| Worship | Simple prayers | Elaborate sacrifices; Upanishadic reaction |
| Pottery | Ochre Coloured / black-and-red | Painted Grey Ware |

> **Mains angle:** Questions like "Trace the evolution of the varna system in ancient India" or "Status of women in the Vedic age" need this comparison table turned into points. Always mention **change over time** — the Early Vedic period was relatively egalitarian; the Later Vedic saw hierarchy, rigid varna and decline of women's position.

> **Prelims angle:** Watch for traps: "Gayatri Mantra is in the Samaveda" (wrong — **Rigveda, Mandala III**). "Satyameva Jayate is from the Rigveda" (wrong — **Mundaka Upanishad**). "Ganga is the most mentioned river in the Rigveda" (wrong — **Indus**). "Most sacred river" → **Saraswati**.

### Mains questions

**1. Compare the political and social life of the Early Vedic and Later Vedic periods.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Two phases; shift from Sapta Sindhu to Ganga plain over ~900 years.
- Polity: tribal Rajan and strong assemblies → hereditary kings, territorial *rashtra*, ratnins, assemblies weaken.
- Taxation: voluntary bali → compulsory bali, bhaga, shulka.
- Economy: pastoral → agrarian with iron.
- Varna: flexible → rigid, birth-based; gotra and ashrama.
- Women: assemblies, remarriage → exclusion, decline (yet Gargi, Maitreyi).
- Religion: nature gods → Prajapati, costly rituals → Upanishadic reaction.
- *Conclusion:* Later Vedic changes laid the base for states (mahajanapadas) and heterodox reform.

**2. Discuss the contribution of Mithila (Videha) to the intellectual life of the Later Vedic period.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Videha in north Bihar; settled after crossing the Sadanira (Shatapatha Brahmana).
- King Janaka — patron of philosophy; hosted debates.
- Yajnavalkya — Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, Atman–Brahman teaching; Yajnavalkya Smriti tradition.
- Gargi and Maitreyi — learned women; evidence of women's learning.
- Shift from ritual to knowledge; prepared ground for Buddhism and Jainism in the same region.
- *Conclusion:* Mithila's tradition of learning continued into medieval times (Nyaya school, Vidyapati).

**3. "The Upanishads were a reaction against ritualism." Explain.** (150 words)

- *Intro:* Later Vedic sacrifices became costly and priest-dominated.
- Upanishads stressed knowledge (*jnana*) over ritual (*karma-kanda*).
- Core ideas: Brahman, Atman, their unity; karma, rebirth, moksha.
- Many teachers were Kshatriyas (Janaka, Ajatashatru of Kashi), showing a challenge to priestly monopoly.
- *Conclusion:* Influenced Buddhism, Jainism and later Vedanta.
