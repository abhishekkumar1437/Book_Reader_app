This chapter covers the religious and regional history of medieval India. BPSC loves "match the saint with the teaching/region/text" questions, Sufi orders and their founders, Vijayanagar rulers and foreign visitors, and Shivaji's administration. For Mains GS I (Indian culture), "the impact of the Bhakti and Sufi movements on Indian society" is a classic, and Bihar's own contributors — **Vidyapati**, **Sharfuddin Yahya Maneri** and **Guru Gobind Singh's birth at Patna** — give you the Bihar angle.

> **In simple words:** Bhakti means loving devotion to one god. It began with Tamil poet-saints in the south and spread to every region. Saints sang in local languages, rejected caste pride and empty ritual, and said that anyone could reach god through love. At the same time, Sufis — Muslim mystics — taught love of god and service of people through their *khanqahs*. Both movements brought Hindus and Muslims closer and made regional languages rich. Politically, the Delhi Sultanate's decline allowed regional powers to rise: Vijayanagar and the Bahmanis in the Deccan, then the Marathas under Shivaji.

## The Bhakti movement

### Key ideas

- **Bhakti** = personal, loving **devotion** to god; salvation through devotion, not through ritual or birth.
- **Equality** before god — many saints came from "low" castes (weavers, cobblers, tailors, barbers).
- Use of **regional languages** — Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Hindi (Awadhi, Braj), Bengali, Assamese, Maithili, Punjabi.
- A **guru** is needed to guide the devotee.
- Two streams:

| Stream | God is worshipped as | Saints |
|---|---|---|
| **Saguna** | **With form and qualities** — Rama, Krishna, Shiva, Vithoba | Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya, Vallabhacharya, Vidyapati |
| **Nirguna** | **Without form** — formless, beyond qualities | **Kabir**, **Guru Nanak**, Raidas, Dadu Dayal |

### Beginnings in the south: Alvars and Nayanars (6th–9th century)

| Group | Devotees of | Number | Key names and texts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alvars** | **Vishnu** | **12** | **Andal** (the only woman Alvar), Nammalvar, Periyalvar; hymns collected as ***Nalayira Divya Prabandham*** ("4,000 sacred compositions") |
| **Nayanars** | **Shiva** | **63** | **Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar** (their hymns form the ***Tevaram***), **Manikkavasagar** (*Tiruvasagam*), **Karaikkal Ammaiyar** (woman saint) |

They travelled from temple to temple, sang in **Tamil**, and opposed the influence of Buddhism and Jainism. **Chola** kings patronised them.

### The Acharyas (philosopher-saints)

| Acharya | Period | Philosophy | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shankaracharya** | c. 8th century (often 788–820 CE) | **Advaita** (non-dualism — Brahman alone is real; the world is *maya*) | Born at **Kalady (Kerala)**; founded **four mathas** — **Sringeri** (south, Karnataka), **Dwarka** (west), **Puri** (east), **Jyotirmath/Joshimath** (north, near Badrinath); commentaries on Upanishads, Gita, Brahma Sutra. A **jnana** (knowledge) path more than bhakti |
| **Ramanuja** | 1017–1137 (traditional) | **Vishishtadvaita** (qualified non-dualism) | Srirangam (Tamil Nadu); **Sri Vaishnavism**; bhakti to Vishnu as the way to moksha |
| **Nimbarka** | c. 12th century | **Dvaitadvaita** (dualism-cum-non-dualism, *Bhedabheda*) | Radha–Krishna worship; **Vrindavan** |
| **Madhvacharya** | 1238–1317 | **Dvaita** (dualism — god and soul are separate) | **Udupi** (Karnataka) |
| **Vallabhacharya** | 1479–1531 | **Shuddhadvaita** (pure non-dualism) | **Pushti Marga** (path of grace); Krishna as **Srinathji**; **Surdas** was in his tradition |
| **Basavanna** (Basava) | 12th century | **Virashaiva / Lingayat** movement | Minister of **Kalachuri king Bijjala** at **Kalyani** (Karnataka); rejected caste, idol worship and rituals; **vachanas** (Kannada sayings); **Anubhava Mantapa** (hall of spiritual experience) |

> **Memory trick:** "**S**hankara **A**dvaita, **R**amanuja **V**ishisht, **M**adhva **D**vaita, **N**imbarka **D**vaitadvaita, **V**allabha **S**huddha" — the **non-dualism scale**: Advaita (fully one) → Vishishtadvaita (one with qualities) → Dvaitadvaita (both) → Dvaita (two).

### Maharashtra: the Varkari saints

The **Varkari** tradition worships **Vithoba (Vitthala)** of **Pandharpur**; devotees walk to Pandharpur on the annual *wari* pilgrimage.

| Saint | Period | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Jnaneshwar (Dnyaneshwar)** | 13th century | ***Jnaneshwari*** (*Bhavarthadipika*) — Marathi commentary on the **Bhagavad Gita** |
| **Namdev** | 13th–14th century | A **tailor**; songs in Marathi and Hindi; some verses in the **Guru Granth Sahib** |
| **Eknath** | 16th century | Marathi *Bhagavata*; opposed caste |
| **Tukaram** | 17th century (Shivaji's time) | **Abhangas** (devotional songs) |
| **Ramdas (Samarth)** | 17th century | ***Dasbodh***; spiritual guide associated with Shivaji (not Varkari) |

### North India

| Saint | Period | Stream / god | Facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ramananda** | 14th–15th century | Rama (Saguna) | Took the bhakti of Ramanuja's tradition to the north (Varanasi); preached in **Hindi**; disciples from all castes and both sexes — **Kabir, Raidas, Dhanna (Jat), Sena (barber), Pipa (Rajput), Padmavati** |
| **Kabir** | 15th century (traditionally 1440–1518) | **Nirguna** | A **weaver** of **Varanasi** brought up by a Muslim family; **dohas** (couplets), collected in the ***Bijak***; criticised idol worship, caste, and rituals of both Hindus and Muslims; called god Rama, Hari, Allah; died at **Maghar** (UP); followers are **Kabirpanthis** |
| **Raidas (Ravidas)** | 15th century | Nirguna | A **cobbler**; verses in the Guru Granth Sahib; Mirabai considered him her guru (tradition) |
| **Guru Nanak** | 1469–1539 | Nirguna | Founder of **Sikhism** (see below) |
| **Dadu Dayal** | 1544–1603 | Nirguna | Rajasthan (Gujarat-born); **Dadu Panth**; said to have met Akbar |
| **Surdas** | 16th century | Krishna (Saguna) | Blind poet of **Agra**; ***Sursagar*** in **Braj bhasha**; disciple of Vallabhacharya |
| **Tulsidas** | 1532–1623 | Rama (Saguna) | ***Ramcharitmanas*** (in **Awadhi**), *Vinay Patrika*, *Kavitavali*, *Hanuman Chalisa* (attributed); Akbar–Jahangir era |
| **Mirabai** | c. 1498–1546 | Krishna (Saguna) | Rajput princess of **Merta**; married into the **Mewar** royal family; devotional songs (*bhajans*) in Rajasthani and Braj |

### East India

| Saint | Region | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Jayadeva** | Bengal (12th century) | ***Gita Govinda*** (Sanskrit) — love of Radha and Krishna; Lakshmanasena's court |
| **Vidyapati** | **Mithila (Bihar)** | See the Bihar box below |
| **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (1486–1533) | Bengal (Nadia/Nabadwip), later **Puri** | **Gaudiya Vaishnavism**; **kirtan** (group singing) and dance; *achintya bhedabheda*; worshipped Krishna; considered by followers an avatar of Krishna; his life written in *Chaitanya Charitamrita* (Krishnadas Kaviraj) |
| **Shankaradeva** (1449–1568) | **Assam** | **Ekasarana dharma** (devotion to one god, Krishna/Vishnu); **satras** (monasteries) and **namghars** (prayer halls); **Borgeet** songs and **Ankiya Naat** plays (origin of **Sattriya** dance) |

> **Bihar connection:** **Vidyapati** (c. 1352–1448) is Bihar's greatest medieval poet.
> - Court poet of the **Oinwara** kings of Mithila, especially **Raja Shivasimha** (and queen Lakhima Devi).
> - His **Padavali** — love songs of **Radha and Krishna** in **Maithili** — made Maithili a great literary language and influenced **Chaitanya** and Bengali Vaishnava poets (who called him a Bengali for a long time).
> - Also wrote in Sanskrit (*Purusha Pariksha*, *Likhanavali*, *Shaiva Sarvasva Sara*) and in Avahatta (*Kirtilata*, *Kirtipataka* — history of the Oinwara rulers).
> - Titles: **Maithil Kavi Kokil** ("cuckoo of Mithila"), **Abhinava Jayadeva**. His Shiva songs (*Nachari*) and his legend of **Ugna** (Shiva serving him as a servant) are part of Mithila folk culture.
> - Other Bihar saints: **Dariya Sahib** (18th century, Dariya Panth), and the Kabirpanthi and Sikh traditions spread widely in Bihar.
> - For Maithili literature and Bihar's languages, see [Art, Culture, Festivals and Languages of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/art-culture-festivals-and-languages).

### Sikhism (an offshoot of the Nirguna tradition)

| Guru | Period | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Guru Nanak** | 1469–1539 | Born at **Talwandi (Nankana Sahib, Pakistan)**; **one formless god** (*Ik Onkar*), honest work, sharing; *langar*; settled at **Kartarpur** |
| 2. **Angad** | 1539–1552 | Standardised the **Gurmukhi** script |
| 3. **Amar Das** | 1552–1574 | Organised **manjis** (preaching centres), made **langar** compulsory; opposed sati and purdah |
| 4. **Ram Das** | 1574–1581 | Founded **Amritsar** (land granted by **Akbar**) |
| 5. **Arjan Dev** | 1581–1606 | Compiled the ***Adi Granth* (1604)**; built the **Harmandir Sahib**; **executed by Jahangir (1606)** |
| 6. **Hargobind** | 1606–1644 | Militarised the Sikhs; built the **Akal Takht**; *miri* and *piri* (two swords) |
| 7. Har Rai · 8. Har Krishan | 1644–1664 | — |
| 9. **Tegh Bahadur** | 1664–1675 | Visited **Patna** and Assam; **executed by Aurangzeb (1675)** in Delhi (Gurudwara Sis Ganj) |
| 10. **Gobind Singh** | 1675–1708 | **Born at Patna (1666)**; founded the **Khalsa (1699)** at **Anandpur Sahib**; the **five Ks**; declared the **Guru Granth Sahib** the eternal Guru; died at **Nanded (1708)** |

After him, **Banda Bahadur** led the Sikhs (executed 1716); later **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** (1780–1839) built the Sikh kingdom of Punjab (Treaty of Amritsar with the British, 1809).

> **Bihar connection:** **Takht Sri Harmandir Ji, Patna Sahib** — the birthplace of **Guru Gobind Singh** (1666) — is one of the **five Takhts** (seats of authority) of Sikhism. The 350th birth anniversary (Prakash Parv) was celebrated in Patna in 2017.

## The Sufi movement

### What is Sufism?

- **Sufism** is the mystical side of Islam. The word may come from *suf* (wool — the woollen robes of early mystics) or *safa* (purity).
- Sufis stressed **love of god**, **service of humanity**, **simple living** and **tolerance**.
- **Key terms:** the teacher is the **pir / shaikh**; the disciple is the **murid**; the successor is the **khalifa**; the hospice is the **khanqah**; the shrine (tomb) of a saint is the **dargah**; the death anniversary is the **urs**; spiritual music is **sama** (leading to **qawwali**); remembrance of god is **zikr**.
- Orders (**silsilas**) were of two kinds: **ba-shara** (followed Islamic law, *sharia*) and **be-shara** (did not strictly follow it — e.g. *qalandars*).
- **Al-Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh)** of Lahore (11th century) wrote ***Kashf-ul-Mahjub***, the earliest Persian treatise on Sufism in the subcontinent.
- Philosophy: **Wahdat-ul-Wujud** ("unity of being" — **Ibn Arabi**; all existence is one with god) versus **Wahdat-ush-Shuhud** ("unity of witness/appearance" — **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi**, more orthodox).

### Main Sufi orders in India

| Silsila | Founder in India / main saints | Centre | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chishti** | **Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti** (came c. 1192; d. 1236; "**Gharib Nawaz**") | **Ajmer** | Most popular in India; **sama** (music); kept away from the state and refused royal grants; liberal |
| | **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** | **Delhi (Mehrauli)** | Qutb Minar named after him |
| | **Baba Farid** (Fariduddin Ganj-i-Shakar) | **Pakpattan** (Punjab, Pakistan) | His verses are in the **Guru Granth Sahib** |
| | **Nizamuddin Auliya** (d. 1325) | **Delhi** | "**Mehboob-e-Ilahi**"; saw seven Sultans; disciple **Amir Khusrau**; "Hunuz Dilli dur ast" |
| | Nasiruddin **Chirag-i-Dilli** | Delhi | "Lamp of Delhi" |
| | **Bandanawaz Gesudaraz** | **Gulbarga** | Took the Chishti order to the Deccan |
| | **Shaikh Salim Chishti** | **Fatehpur Sikri** | Blessed Akbar with a son (Salim/Jahangir) |
| **Suhrawardi** | **Shaikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi** (founder, Baghdad); in India **Bahauddin Zakariya** | **Multan**, Sindh | Accepted state service and grants; more orthodox |
| **Firdausi** | Branch of the Suhrawardi order; brought to India by **Badruddin Samarqandi** | **Bihar** | Flourished in **Bihar** under **Sharfuddin Yahya Maneri** |
| **Qadiri** | **Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani** (founder, Baghdad); in India 15th century (Shah Niamatullah, Makhdum Muhammad Jilani) | Uch, Punjab | **Dara Shikoh** was a follower (Mian Mir, Mulla Shah) |
| **Naqshbandi** | **Khwaja Baqi Billah** (came in Akbar's reign) | Delhi, Sirhind | **Orthodox**; **Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi** ("**Mujaddid Alf-i-Sani**" — renewer of the second millennium) opposed Akbar's policies; later **Shah Waliullah** (18th c.) |
| **Shattari** | Shah Abdullah Shattari | Gwalior, Bengal | **Muhammad Ghaus Gwaliori** (linked with Tansen) |
| **Rishi** | **Nuruddin Wali (Nund Rishi)** | **Kashmir** | Local order; influenced by Shaiva mystic **Lal Ded (Lalleshwari)** |

> **Bihar connection:**
> - **Makhdum Sharfuddin Ahmad Yahya Maneri** (1263–1381) of the **Firdausi** order lived at **Bihar Sharif** (the town's "Sharif" honours him). His letters to disciples, ***Maktubat-i-Sadi*** ("Hundred Letters"), are classics of Sufi teaching. Born at **Maner** (Patna district).
> - **Maner Sharif** — the **Bari Dargah** (tomb of Hazrat Yahya Maneri, the saint's father) and the **Chhoti Dargah** (tomb of **Shah Daulat**, built 1616 in Jahangir's time).
> - **Phulwari Sharif** (Patna) — the **Khanqah Mujibia**, a famous Sufi centre (17th century onwards).
> - Many Bihar towns have dargahs where Hindus and Muslims both visit — an example of composite culture.

> **Prelims angle:** Pairs that are asked: **Moinuddin Chishti–Ajmer**, **Nizamuddin Auliya–Delhi**, **Baba Farid–Pakpattan**, **Bahauddin Zakariya–Multan (Suhrawardi)**, **Gesudaraz–Gulbarga**, **Salim Chishti–Fatehpur Sikri**, **Sharfuddin Maneri–Bihar Sharif (Firdausi)**, **Ahmad Sirhindi–Naqshbandi**. "Which order kept away from the state?" → **Chishti**. "Which order accepted state patronage?" → **Suhrawardi**.

### Impact of the Bhakti and Sufi movements

| Area | Impact |
|---|---|
| Religion | Personal devotion over ritual; criticism of priestly and orthodox authority |
| Society | Challenge to caste (Kabir, Raidas, Basava, Nanak); some status for women (Mirabai, Andal, Lal Ded, Lakhima) — but caste did not disappear |
| Hindu–Muslim relations | Common shrines, shared ideas of love and equality; Kabir and Nanak spoke to both; Akbar's Sulh-i-kul was influenced by this climate |
| Languages and literature | Growth of **Hindi** (Awadhi, Braj), **Marathi**, **Bengali**, **Assamese**, **Punjabi**, **Maithili**, Kannada; **Urdu/Hindavi** through Sufis (Amir Khusrau) |
| Music and art | Qawwali, kirtan, bhajan, abhanga; Sattriya and Ankiya Naat |
| New religions | **Sikhism**, Kabirpanth, Dadu Panth, Lingayatism |

> **Mains angle:** "Discuss the contribution of the Bhakti and Sufi movements to the composite culture of India." Write: common ideas (love, equality, guru/pir), language growth, attack on caste and ritual, shrines visited by all, music (sama/kirtan), effect on rulers (Akbar), limits (caste survived, orthodox reaction — Sirhindi), and the Bihar examples (Vidyapati, Maneri, Patna Sahib). Conclude with relevance to social harmony today.

## Regional kingdoms

### Vijayanagar Empire (1336–1646)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Founded | **1336** by **Harihara I and Bukka I** (the **Sangama** brothers), with the blessing of the sage **Vidyaranya** (Madhavacharya) of Sringeri |
| Capital | **Vijayanagara (Hampi)** on the **Tungabhadra** (Karnataka) — **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)** |
| Rival | **Bahmani kingdom** — wars over the fertile **Raichur doab** (between the **Krishna and Tungabhadra**) |

| Dynasty | Years | Key rulers |
|---|---|---|
| **Sangama** | 1336–1485 | Harihara I, Bukka I, **Devaraya I** (dam on the Tungabhadra; **Nicolo de Conti** (Italian) visited c. **1420**), **Devaraya II** (recruited Muslim cavalry and archers; **Abdur Razzaq**, envoy of the Timurid ruler Shah Rukh of Persia, visited **1443**) |
| **Saluva** | 1485–1505 | Saluva Narasimha |
| **Tuluva** | 1505–1570 | **Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529)**, Achyuta Raya, Sadashiva Raya (regent **Aliya Rama Raya**) |
| **Aravidu** | 1570–1646 (approx.) | Tirumala (capital moved to **Penukonda**, later **Chandragiri**), Venkata II |

#### Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529)

- Greatest ruler; defeated **Bijapur** (took **Raichur, 1520**), the **Gajapatis of Odisha**, and kept the Bahmani successors in check.
- Friendly with the **Portuguese** (Albuquerque) — got horses through Goa.
- Wrote ***Amuktamalyada*** (**Telugu**, on statecraft and the story of Andal) and ***Jambavati Kalyanam*** (Sanskrit).
- Titles: **Andhra Bhoja**, **Abhinava Bhoja**, *Andhra Pitamaha* (some sources).
- Court of **Ashtadiggajas** (eight great Telugu poets), including **Allasani Peddana** (***Manucharitam***; "*Andhra Kavita Pitamaha*") and **Tenali Ramakrishna** (Tenali Raman).
- Built the **Vitthala temple** (with its **stone chariot**), **Hazara Rama temple**, the town of **Nagalapura**; added *raya gopurams*.
- Visitors: **Domingo Paes** (Portuguese, c. 1520–22) described Hampi as "as large as Rome"; **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese). Later **Fernão Nuniz** (Portuguese horse trader) under Achyuta Raya.

#### Battle of Talikota (1565)

- Date: **23 January 1565**; also called **Rakshasa-Tangadi** or **Bannihatti**.
- The four Deccan Sultanates — **Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda and Bidar** (Berar stayed out) — joined against **Aliya Rama Raya**, who was killed.
- **Hampi was destroyed** and never recovered; the empire continued in a smaller form from Penukonda and Chandragiri.

#### Administration and society

- **Nayankara system**: military chiefs (**nayakas / amaranayakas**) got land (**amaram**) and had to maintain troops and pay tribute — similar to the Mughal mansabdari/iqta. Later nayakas became independent (Madurai, Thanjavur, **Mysore** under the Wodeyars, Keladi).
- **Ayagar system**: **12** hereditary village functionaries (*ayagars*) paid by tax-free land.
- Units: **rajya/mandalam** (province) → **nadu** → **sthala** → **grama**.
- Land revenue about **one-sixth** (higher in practice).
- Temples were economic centres; *devadasis*; sati is mentioned by foreign travellers.
- **Vijayanagara architecture**: tall **raya gopurams** (gateway towers), **kalyana mandapas** (marriage halls), pillars carved with **yalis** (mythical lions) and horses; **Lotus Mahal**, Virupaksha temple at Hampi.

### The Bahmani kingdom (1347–1527) and Deccan Sultanates

- Founded in **1347** by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (Hasan Gangu), a rebel noble of Muhammad bin Tughlaq; capital **Gulbarga (Ahsanabad)**, later **Bidar** (shifted by **Ahmad Shah Wali**, 1425).
- **Firoz Shah Bahmani** — built an observatory at Daulatabad; promoted learning.
- **Mahmud Gawan** — a Persian, prime minister (*vakil-us-sultanat*); great **madrasa at Bidar**; land revenue reforms; executed in **1481** due to court rivalry (Deccani vs foreign nobles — *Afaqis*) → decline.
- Russian merchant **Afanasy Nikitin** visited (1469–72).
- The kingdom split into **five Deccan Sultanates**:

| Sultanate | Dynasty | Founder | Year | Remember |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bijapur** | **Adil Shahi** | Yusuf Adil Shah | 1489–90 | **Gol Gumbaz** (tomb of **Muhammad Adil Shah**, one of the largest domes in the world; whispering gallery); **Ibrahim Adil Shah II** wrote ***Kitab-i-Nauras*** (songs) and was called *Jagadguru*; annexed by Aurangzeb 1686 |
| **Ahmadnagar** | **Nizam Shahi** | Malik Ahmad | 1490 | **Chand Bibi** resisted Akbar; annexed by Shah Jahan 1633 |
| **Berar** | **Imad Shahi** | Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk | 1490 | Absorbed by Ahmadnagar (1574) |
| **Golconda** | **Qutb Shahi** | Quli Qutb Shah | 1518 | **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** founded **Hyderabad (1591)** and built the **Charminar**; diamond mines (Kohinoor traditionally from Kollur); annexed 1687 |
| **Bidar** | **Barid Shahi** | Amir Ali Barid | 1528 | Absorbed by Bijapur (1619) |

### The Marathas

#### Shivaji (1630–1680)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | **19 February 1630** (widely accepted date) at **Shivneri fort** near Junnar (Pune) |
| Parents | **Shahji Bhonsle** (a Bijapur noble) and **Jijabai** |
| Guardian / teacher | **Dadaji Konddev**; spiritual influences **Ramdas** and **Tukaram** |
| Early forts | **Torna** (1646), Rajgad, Kondana, Purandar |
| **Afzal Khan** | Bijapur general, killed by Shivaji at **Pratapgarh (1659)** — the "tiger claws" (*wagh nakh*) episode |
| **Shaista Khan** | Mughal governor attacked at **Lal Mahal, Pune (1663)** — lost his fingers |
| **Surat** | Sacked in **1664** and again **1670** |
| **Treaty of Purandar** | **1665** with **Raja Jai Singh** (Aurangzeb's general) — Shivaji gave up **23 of 35 forts** and agreed to visit Agra |
| **Agra** | Visited Aurangzeb's court (**1666**), was kept under house arrest, and **escaped** (in baskets of sweets, as the story goes) |
| **Coronation** | **1674** at **Raigad**, by **Gaga Bhatta** of Varanasi; titles **Chhatrapati**, **Haindava Dharmoddharak** |
| South campaign | **Karnataka expedition (1677–78)** — took **Jinji** and **Vellore** |
| Death | **1680** at Raigad |

**Shivaji's administration — the Ashtapradhan (council of eight ministers):**

| Minister | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Peshwa** (Mukhya Pradhan) | Prime minister, general administration |
| **Amatya** (Majumdar) | Finance / accounts |
| **Sachiv** (Surunavis) | Royal correspondence |
| **Mantri** (Waqia-navis) | Daily records, intelligence, household |
| **Senapati** (Sar-i-Naubat) | Commander-in-chief |
| **Sumant** (Dabir) | Foreign affairs |
| **Nyayadhish** | Justice |
| **Panditrao** | Religious affairs, charity |

All except the Nyayadhish and Panditrao also held military commands.

**Revenue:**
- Land revenue reformed on the lines of Malik Ambar (Ahmadnagar's minister) — assessment at about **two-fifths (40%)** of produce; **jagirs discouraged**, officials paid in cash.
- **Chauth** — **one-fourth (25%)** of the revenue of neighbouring (Mughal/other) areas, collected as protection money.
- **Sardeshmukhi** — an additional **one-tenth (10%)** claimed as the hereditary *sardeshmukh* (chief head of the region).
- Strong **forts** (each with a havaldar, sabnis and sarnobat of equal rank) and a **navy** (bases at **Kolaba**, **Sindhudurg**, Vijaydurg).
- Army: light cavalry and **guerrilla tactics** (*ganimi kava*); *bargirs* (soldiers given horses by the state) and *shiledars* (with their own horses).

| Ruler | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Sambhaji** (1680–89) | Gave shelter to Aurangzeb's rebel son Akbar II; captured and **executed by Aurangzeb (1689)** |
| **Rajaram** (1689–1700) | Fled to **Jinji** (Tamil Nadu) — the fort held out for years |
| **Tarabai** | Rajaram's widow; led resistance for her son Shivaji II |
| **Shahu** (1707–1749) | Released by the Mughals in 1707; civil war with Tarabai; **Battle of Khed (1707)**; appointed **Balaji Vishwanath** as Peshwa — power shifted to the Peshwas |

#### The Peshwas

| Peshwa | Years | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Balaji Vishwanath** | 1713–1720 | Got a **Mughal farman (1719)** from **Farrukhsiyar** (through the **Sayyid brothers**) granting **chauth and sardeshmukhi of the six Deccan subas** — called the "**Magna Carta of the Maratha empire**" |
| **Baji Rao I** | 1720–1740 | Greatest general; defeated the **Nizam at Palkhed (1728)** and Bhopal (1738); **raided Delhi (1737)**; idea of "**Hindu-pad-padshahi**"; took Malwa and Bundelkhand (helped Chhatrasal); captured Salsette and Bassein from the Portuguese (1739, by his brother Chimaji Appa) |
| **Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb)** | 1740–1761 | Maratha power reached Punjab; after Shahu's death (1749) the Peshwa became the real ruler (Sangola agreement, 1750); **Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761)** — **Ahmad Shah Abdali** defeated the Marathas under **Sadashivrao Bhau**; Vishwasrao killed; Nana Saheb died soon after |
| **Madhav Rao I** | 1761–1772 | Restored Maratha power in the north; brought Shah Alam II back to Delhi (1771) |
| Narayan Rao, Raghunath Rao, Sawai Madhav Rao, **Baji Rao II** | 1772–1818 | Internal quarrels; **Nana Phadnavis** (minister); the three **Anglo-Maratha wars** ended Maratha power in **1818** (see the next part) |

**Maratha Confederacy** (major houses): **Peshwa** (Pune), **Gaekwad** (Baroda), **Holkar** (Indore — **Ahilyabai Holkar**), **Scindia/Shinde** (Gwalior — Mahadji Scindia), **Bhonsle** (Nagpur).

> **Bihar connection:** The **Bhonsles of Nagpur** sent cavalry raiders — the **Bargis** — into **Bengal, Bihar and Odisha** from **1742 to 1751** during the rule of Nawab **Alivardi Khan**. The raids caused great suffering (remembered in Bengali lullabies about "bargis"). In **1751** Alivardi agreed to pay **chauth** (₹12 lakh a year) and gave up Odisha's revenue to the Marathas. Bihar was part of the Nawab of Bengal's territory at this time — the background to Plassey (1757).

| Kingdom | Capital | Famous ruler / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Bengal** | Gaur, Pandua | **Ilyas Shahi** dynasty; **Alauddin Husain Shah** (1494–1519) — patron of Bengali literature (Mahabharata translated), Chaitanya's contemporary; his rule extended into **north Bihar** |
| **Jaunpur (Sharqi)** | Jaunpur (UP) | Founded by **Malik Sarwar (1394)**, title *Malik-us-Sharq*; Jaunpur called the "**Shiraz of India**"; **Atala Masjid**; controlled much of Bihar; ended by the Lodis |

| **Odisha (Eastern Gangas, Gajapatis)** | Cuttack, Puri | **Anantavarman Chodaganga** began the **Jagannath temple, Puri**; **Narasimhadeva I** built the **Konark Sun Temple** (13th century; **UNESCO 1984**); Gajapati **Kapilendra**, Prataparudra |

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the impact of the Bhakti and Sufi movements on medieval Indian society and culture.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Parallel devotional movements, 8th–17th century.
- Common ideas: love of god, equality, guru/pir, criticism of ritual and orthodoxy.
- Social: attack on caste (Kabir, Raidas, Basava), new space for women (Mirabai, Lal Ded).
- Composite culture: shared shrines; Kabir and Nanak spoke to both communities; influence on Akbar.
- Literature: growth of Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Assamese, Punjabi, Maithili; Hindavi/Urdu.
- Music: qawwali, kirtan, abhanga.
- New sects: Sikhism, Kabirpanth, Lingayats.
- Limits: caste survived; orthodox reaction (Sirhindi).
- Bihar: Vidyapati, Maneri, Patna Sahib.
- *Conclusion:* Lasting base for India's pluralism.

**2. Assess the contribution of Vidyapati to the literature and culture of Mithila.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* c. 1352–1448; Oinwara court (Shivasimha).
- Padavali — Radha–Krishna songs in Maithili; Nachari (Shiva songs).
- Sanskrit and Avahatta works (Purusha Pariksha, Kirtilata, Kirtipataka).
- Influence on Chaitanya and Bengali, Odia, Assamese Vaishnava poetry.
- Folk culture: songs at weddings and festivals; Ugna legend.
- *Conclusion:* Made Maithili a literary language — today in the Eighth Schedule (2003).

**3. Describe the administration of Shivaji. How was it different from the Mughal system?** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Shivaji (1630–80), founder of the Maratha state; coronation 1674.
- Ashtapradhan and their duties.
- Revenue: 40% assessment; cash salaries; jagirs discouraged; chauth and sardeshmukhi.
- Forts and navy; guerrilla army.
- Provinces (prants), villages with patil and kulkarni.
- Differences: no mansabdari/jagirs, smaller and more personal state, ministers not hereditary, stress on forts and navy.
- *Conclusion:* A people-based regional state that later grew into a confederacy.

**4. "Vijayanagar was a centre of cultural synthesis." Discuss.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* 1336–1565 heyday; Hampi.
- Krishnadevaraya — Telugu and Sanskrit literature, Ashtadiggajas.
- Architecture: raya gopurams, kalyana mandapas, Vitthala temple; Indo-Islamic arches in Lotus Mahal.
- Muslim cavalry under Devaraya II; Portuguese trade.
- Foreign accounts: Nicolo de Conti, Abdur Razzaq, Paes, Nuniz.
- *Conclusion:* A wealthy, plural society; Talikota ended its glory.
