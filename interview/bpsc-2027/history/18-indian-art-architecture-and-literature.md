Mains GS Paper I includes "**Indian culture — art, culture and literature**", and Prelims regularly asks about temples, sculptures, paintings, dances and books. This chapter gives you a structured map of Indian culture from the Mauryas to modern times. Bihar is central to this story — Mauryan polish, the Barabar caves, the Didarganj Yakshi, Pala bronzes, Nalanda, Sher Shah's tomb, Madhubani painting, Vidyapati and Dinkar — so every section has a Bihar box. For festivals, folk arts and languages of Bihar in detail, read [Art, Culture, Festivals and Languages of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/art-culture-festivals-and-languages).

> **In simple words:** Indian art grew in layers. First came Buddhist and Mauryan art (pillars, stupas, caves). Then Hindu temples in three styles — **Nagara** (north), **Dravida** (south), **Vesara** (mixed). Then Islamic rulers brought arches, domes and gardens, and the Mughals mixed Persian and Indian styles (Taj Mahal). Painting moved from cave walls (Ajanta) to palm leaves (Pala), to miniatures (Mughal, Rajput), to folk art (Madhubani). Music, dance and literature grew alongside, often around religion and royal courts.

## Mauryan art (4th–2nd century BCE)

Mauryan art has two parts: **court art** (made for the king) and **popular art** (made by local people).

### Court art

- **Ashokan pillars**: single blocks of **Chunar sandstone**, with a famous **mirror-like polish** ("Mauryan polish"). Each has a shaft, a bell-shaped (inverted lotus) base of the capital, an abacus and an animal on top.
- **Sarnath lion capital** — four lions back to back; abacus with **wheel (chakra)**, elephant, bull, horse, lion. It is India's **National Emblem** (adopted 26 January 1950); the motto **"Satyameva Jayate"** comes from the **Mundaka Upanishad**.
- **Palace of Pataliputra** — the pillared hall at **Kumhrar** (Patna); Megasthenes praised the wooden palace.

> **Bihar connection:** Ashokan pillars in Bihar:
> - **Lauriya Nandangarh** (West Champaran) — pillar with a **single lion capital**, still in place.
> - **Rampurva** (West Champaran) — two pillars: the **bull capital** (now at **Rashtrapati Bhavan**) and a lion capital (Indian Museum, Kolkata).
> - **Lauriya Araraj** (East Champaran) — inscribed pillar without capital.
> - **Kolhua, Vaishali** — a thick pillar with a **single lion** facing north, near a stupa.
> - **Kumhrar (Patna)** — remains of the **80-pillared hall** of the Mauryan palace.

### Popular art

- Large free-standing figures of **yakshas and yakshis** (nature spirits).
- **Didarganj Yakshi** — a life-size **chauri (fly-whisk) bearer** of polished sandstone, found in **1917 at Didarganj on the Ganga bank, Patna**. It is a masterpiece of Indian sculpture and is now in the **Bihar Museum**, Patna. (Its date is debated — Mauryan polish, but some scholars place it later; for the exam, link it to the Mauryan tradition.)
- **Parkham Yaksha** (Mathura), **Besnagar Yakshi**.

### Rock-cut caves

- **Barabar Hills** (Jehanabad/Gaya, Bihar) — the **oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India**, cut under **Ashoka** for the **Ajivika** sect. The **Lomas Rishi cave** has a carved **chaitya-arch entrance** with elephants — the model for later Buddhist caves. The **Sudama cave** has an Ashokan inscription. Nearby **Nagarjuni caves** were cut under **Dasharatha** (Ashoka's grandson).
- The interiors have the famous Mauryan polish. E.M. Forster's "**Marabar Caves**" in *A Passage to India* are based on them.

## Buddhist architecture: stupa, chaitya and vihara

| Structure | Meaning | Famous examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Stupa** | A dome-shaped mound over relics of the Buddha or a monk | **Sanchi** (MP — Ashoka; gateways/toranas under Satavahanas), **Bharhut** (MP), **Amaravati** (AP), **Vaishali** relic stupa, **Kesaria** (East Champaran — one of the tallest Buddhist stupas), **Dhamek** (Sarnath) |
| **Chaitya** | Prayer hall with a stupa at one end | **Karle** (largest), Bhaja, Ajanta caves 9, 10, 19, 26 |
| **Vihara** | Monastery — living quarters for monks | Ajanta, Ellora, **Nalanda**, **Vikramshila** |

Parts of a stupa: **anda** (dome), **harmika** (railing on top), **chhatra** (umbrella), **vedika** (railing around), **torana** (gateway), **pradakshina path** (path for walking around).

> **Bihar connection:** **Mahabodhi Temple, Bodh Gaya** — a brick temple of the **Gupta** period (rebuilt many times), with the **Vajrasana** and the Bodhi tree; **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2002)**. **Nalanda Mahavihara** (excavated site) — **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016)**. **Vikramshila** (Antichak, Bhagalpur) — founded by Pala king **Dharmapala**. **Kesaria stupa** (East Champaran) and the **Vaishali relic stupa** are other key sites.

## Sculpture schools (1st century BCE–3rd century CE)

| Feature | **Gandhara** | **Mathura** | **Amaravati** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region | NW (Pakistan–Afghanistan) | Mathura (UP) | Krishna valley (AP) |
| Patrons | **Kushanas** (also Indo-Greeks) | **Kushanas** | **Satavahanas** (Ikshvakus) |
| Material | **Grey-blue schist**, stucco | **Spotted red sandstone** | **White marble** |
| Influence | **Greco-Roman** — wavy hair, muscular body, heavy folded robes | Fully **Indian** — fleshy, smiling, light robe | Indian; **narrative** panels, dynamic movement |
| Religion | Mainly Buddhist | Buddhist, Jain, Hindu | Mainly Buddhist |

> **Prelims angle:** The **first images of the Buddha** in human form were made in **Gandhara and Mathura** around the same time (Kushana period). Earlier he was shown only by symbols (footprints, empty seat, Bodhi tree, wheel).

**Gupta sculpture** (4th–6th centuries) — the "classical age": **Sarnath Buddha** (preaching pose), the **Sultanganj Buddha** (a large copper image found at **Sultanganj, Bhagalpur, Bihar**, now in Birmingham Museum), Varaha at Udayagiri.

## Hindu temple architecture

### Parts of a Hindu temple

- **Garbhagriha** — sanctum with the main deity.
- **Mandapa** — hall for devotees.
- **Shikhara / Vimana** — tower over the sanctum (**shikhara** in the north, **vimana** in the south).
- **Amalaka** — ribbed stone disc on top of a Nagara shikhara; **kalasha** — pot-shaped finial.
- **Antarala** — passage between sanctum and hall.
- **Gopuram** — gateway tower (south).
- **Pradakshina patha** — path around the sanctum.

### Early Gupta temples

- Flat-roofed, simple: **Sanchi Temple 17**; later with a shikhara: **Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh** (UP), **Bhitargaon brick temple** (Kanpur), Mahabodhi (Bodh Gaya).

| Feature | **Nagara** (North) | **Dravida** (South) | **Vesara** (Deccan) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region | North India, Odisha, Gujarat, central India | Tamil Nadu, Kerala, south Karnataka | Karnataka, Deccan |
| Tower | **Curved shikhara** with amalaka | **Pyramidal, stepped vimana** | Mix — lower, often **star-shaped (stellate) plan** |
| Boundary wall | Usually none | **High compound wall with gopurams** | Varies |
| Water tank | Not common | Often inside the complex | — |
| Main dynasties | Chandelas, Solankis, Eastern Gangas, Pratiharas | **Pallavas, Cholas**, Pandyas, Vijayanagara, Nayakas | **Chalukyas of Badami/Kalyani, Hoysalas** |

**Nagara sub-schools and examples:**
- **Odisha** — **Lingaraja** (Bhubaneswar), **Jagannath** (Puri, Anantavarman Chodaganga), **Sun Temple, Konark** (13th century, **Narasimhadeva I**, Eastern Ganga; shaped as the Sun's chariot with 24 wheels; "Black Pagoda"; UNESCO). Odisha terms: deul (sanctum), jagamohana (hall).
- **Khajuraho** (MP) — **Chandela** kings (c. 950–1050); **Kandariya Mahadeva**, Lakshmana temple; erotic sculpture; UNESCO.
- **Solanki (Gujarat)** — **Sun Temple, Modhera (1026)**, with a stepped tank (Surya Kund); **Rani ki Vav** (stepwell, Patan; UNESCO).
- **Mount Abu** — **Dilwara Jain temples** (marble; Vimal Vasahi, Luna Vasahi).

**Dravida examples:**
- **Pallavas**: **Mahabalipuram** — **Pancha Rathas** (monolithic, under **Narasimhavarman I "Mamalla"**), **Shore Temple** (structural, **Narasimhavarman II "Rajasimha"**); **Kailasanatha temple, Kanchipuram** (Rajasimha).
- **Cholas**: **Brihadeeswarar (Rajarajeshwara) temple, Thanjavur** (**Rajaraja I**, completed c. 1010) — tallest vimana of its time; **Gangaikondacholapuram** (**Rajendra I**); **Airavatesvara, Darasuram** — the three are the UNESCO "**Great Living Chola Temples**". Chola **bronze** sculpture — the **Nataraja** (lost-wax method).
- **Vijayanagara and Nayakas**: tall **gopurams**, pillared halls (**kalyana mandapa**), **Hampi** (Virupaksha, Vitthala with musical pillars and stone chariot), **Meenakshi temple, Madurai**.

**Vesara examples:**
- **Chalukyas of Badami**: **Aihole** ("**cradle of Indian temple architecture**"; **Durga temple**, **Ladkhan temple**), **Badami** caves, **Pattadakal** (UNESCO; both Nagara and Dravida temples side by side — **Virupaksha temple**).
- **Hoysalas**: **Belur** (Chennakesava), **Halebidu** (Hoysaleswara), **Somanathapura**; star-shaped plans, soapstone, very fine carving. (The "Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas" became a UNESCO site in 2023.)
- **Rashtrakutas**: **Kailasa temple, Ellora**.

> **Memory trick:** "**N**orth **N**agara curves; **D**ravida **D**ances up in steps; **V**esara **V**aries (mix)."

> **Common mistake:** Konark is a **Nagara** (Kalinga sub-style) temple, not Dravida. Pattadakal has **both** Nagara and Dravida temples. The Kailasa temple at Ellora is **Dravida** in style but cut from rock by the **Rashtrakutas**.

## Pala art of Bihar and Bengal (8th–12th centuries) ⭐

> **Bihar connection:** The **Pala dynasty** (Gopala, **Dharmapala**, Devapala) ruled Bihar and Bengal and was the last great patron of Buddhism in India. Pala art is a favourite Mains topic for BPSC. Read with [Harsha and Early Medieval India](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/harsha-and-early-medieval-india) and [Ancient Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/ancient-bihar).

- **Centres:** **Nalanda**, **Vikramshila**, **Odantapuri** (Bihar Sharif), Somapura (Bangladesh), Kurkihar.
- **Sculpture:** in **black basalt** stone and **bronze (eight-metal alloy, ashtadhatu)**; images of the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, **Tara**, and Hindu gods (Vishnu, Surya). Smooth, elegant figures with rich jewellery and an ornamented back-slab.
- **Kurkihar hoard** (near **Gaya**) — over 200 bronze images found in 1930, many now in the **Patna Museum / Bihar Museum**. **Nalanda bronzes** are equally famous.
- **Painting:** **palm-leaf manuscript illustrations** — e.g. of the ***Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita*** (Perfection of Wisdom) text — miniature paintings in red, blue, yellow and black, with smooth outlines. These are among the **earliest Indian miniature paintings**.
- **Influence:** Pala art influenced **Nepal, Tibet, Myanmar, Thailand and Indonesia** (Java) through monks and scholars like **Atisha Dipankara** of Vikramshila, who went to Tibet.
- **Architecture:** large monastic universities; the **Somapura Mahavihara** (Bangladesh, UNESCO) and **Vikramshila** have a cross-shaped central shrine.

## Indo-Islamic architecture

### Features

- **True arch, dome**, minarets, **use of lime mortar**, **calligraphy** (Quranic verses), **arabesque** (geometric and floral patterns), **jaali** (stone lattice), **pietra dura** (inlaid stones, from Jahangir's time), **charbagh** (garden divided into four parts), **red sandstone and white marble**.
- No human or animal figures in religious buildings.

### Delhi Sultanate

| Building | Builder / features |
|---|---|
| **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque**, Delhi | Qutbuddin Aibak — first mosque in Delhi; used temple material; corbelled arches |
| **Qutub Minar** | Started by **Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish**; repaired by Firoz Tughlaq (and Sikandar Lodi); UNESCO |
| **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra**, Ajmer | Aibak |
| **Alai Darwaza** (1311) | **Alauddin Khalji** — first **true arch and true dome** in India |
| Tughlaqabad; Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq's tomb | **Sloping (battered) walls**, plain style |
| Lodi Gardens tombs | **Double dome**; octagonal tombs |

### Sher Shah and the Sur architecture

> **Bihar connection:** **Sher Shah Suri's tomb at Sasaram (Rohtas district)** — built around **1545**, an **octagonal tomb in the middle of an artificial lake**, on a high platform; about **37 m** high (commonly given); designed by architect **Aliwal Khan**. It is the finest example of **Indo-Afghan (Sur) architecture** and a bridge between Lodi and Mughal styles. Also at Sasaram: the tomb of his father **Hasan Khan Sur**. Sher Shah also built the **Qila-i-Kuhna mosque** in the **Purana Qila, Delhi**, the **Rohtasgarh** fort area works, and the **Sher Shah Suri Masjid in Patna**. Read [Mughal Empire and Sher Shah](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/mughal-empire-and-sher-shah).

### Mughal architecture

| Ruler | Buildings | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | Mosques at Panipat, Ayodhya, Sambhal; gardens (Ram Bagh, Agra) | Charbagh introduced |
| Humayun | Din Panah (Delhi) | — |
| **Akbar** | **Humayun's Tomb, Delhi** (built by his widow **Hamida Banu Begum** / Bega Begum, architect **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas**; first **garden tomb**, double dome; UNESCO); **Agra Fort**; **Fatehpur Sikri** — **Buland Darwaza** (victory over Gujarat), Jama Masjid, Panch Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas, **tomb of Salim Chishti**; tomb at **Sikandra** (completed by Jahangir) | **Red sandstone**; mix of Hindu and Persian elements (chhatris, brackets) |
| **Jahangir** | **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah**, Agra (built by **Nur Jahan**; first full use of **pietra dura**, white marble); Shalimar Bagh (Srinagar) | Gardens, paintings more than buildings |
| **Shah Jahan** — "golden age of Mughal architecture" | **Taj Mahal** (for Mumtaz Mahal; chief architect **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**; UNESCO); **Red Fort, Delhi**; **Jama Masjid, Delhi**; **Moti Masjid** (Agra Fort); Peacock Throne | **White marble**, pietra dura, symmetry, bulbous domes |
| Aurangzeb | **Badshahi Mosque, Lahore**; **Moti Masjid** (Red Fort, Delhi); **Bibi ka Maqbara**, Aurangabad (built by his son **Azam Shah** — "Taj of the Deccan") | Decline |

> **Mains angle:** "Mughal architecture was a synthesis of Persian and Indian traditions." Points: Persian — charbagh, double dome, iwan (arched portal), calligraphy; Indian — chhatris, brackets, lotus motifs, jharokhas, use of local sandstone and Rajput craftsmen; evolution from Humayun's tomb to the Taj; regional influence (Rajput palaces, Sikh gurdwaras). Bihar example: Sher Shah's tomb as a forerunner; Mughal-period buildings in Patna and **Maner Sharif** (the shrines of the Sufi saints of Maner, including a fine Mughal tomb known as the **Chhoti Dargah**).

### Colonial architecture

- **Indo-Saracenic** style — mix of Indian, Islamic and Gothic: **Gateway of India** (Bombay), **Victoria Memorial** (Calcutta), Madras High Court, Mysore Palace.
- **Victorian Gothic**: **Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus** (F.W. Stevens; UNESCO).
- **New Delhi** (capital from 1911–12, inaugurated 1931): **Edwin Lutyens** and **Herbert Baker** — Rashtrapati Bhavan, North and South Blocks, India Gate; Parliament House (circular, Lutyens and Baker).

## Music

- Roots: **Sama Veda** (chanting); Bharata's ***Natyashastra*** (c. 2nd century BCE–2nd century CE); Matanga's *Brihaddeshi* (term **raga**); Sharangadeva's ***Sangita Ratnakara*** (13th century).
- **Hindustani** (north) and **Carnatic** (south) separated around the 13th–14th centuries.

| Hindustani forms | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Dhrupad** | Oldest form; developed under **Raja Man Singh Tomar** of Gwalior; **Dagar** family; **Darbhanga** (Mallick family) and **Bettiah gharanas** of Bihar are famous dhrupad schools |
| **Khayal** | Linked with **Amir Khusrau**; popular from Muhammad Shah's time (Sadarang) |
| Thumri, Dadra, Tappa, Ghazal, Qawwali | Lighter forms (qawwali — Khusrau, Sufi shrines) |

- **Amir Khusrau** (court of Alauddin Khalji) — "Tuti-i-Hind"; credited with qawwali and some instruments (traditionally sitar and tabla, though historians doubt this).
- **Tansen** (Akbar's court; one of the Navaratnas) — ragas Miyan ki Malhar, Darbari Kanada.
- **Carnatic**: **Purandara Dasa** ("father of Carnatic music"); the **Trinity** — **Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri** (18th–19th c.); forms — kriti, varnam.

> **Bihar connection:** **Ustad Bismillah Khan** (shehnai) was born at **Dumraon (Buxar district), Bihar** in **1916**; **Bharat Ratna (2001)**. **Sharda Sinha**, "Bihar Kokila", the voice of Chhath and Maithili–Bhojpuri folk songs, was awarded the **Padma Vibhushan (2025, posthumously)**. **Bhikhari Thakur** (1887–1971), the "**Shakespeare of Bhojpuri**", created the **Bidesiya** folk theatre about migrant workers and the women left behind. The **Darbhanga and Bettiah dhrupad gharanas** are Bihar's classical heritage. Folk songs of Bihar: **Sohar** (birth), **Kajari** (rainy season), **Chaita**, **Jhumar**, **Birha**, **Bidesia**, Chhath songs.

## Dance

**Classical dances** — eight recognised by the **Sangeet Natak Akademi** (the Ministry of Culture also lists **Chhau**):

| Dance | State | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Bharatanatyam** | Tamil Nadu | Oldest; from **Sadir/Dasiattam** of temple dancers; revived by **Rukmini Devi Arundale** (Kalakshetra) and E. Krishna Iyer |
| **Kathak** | North India (UP, Rajasthan) | Storytellers (kathakars); **chakkars** (spins); Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras gharanas; **Birju Maharaj** |
| **Kathakali** | Kerala | Dance-drama; heavy make-up (green = noble); Ramayana–Mahabharata; **Vallathol Narayana Menon** founded **Kerala Kalamandalam** (1930) |

**Folk dances of Bihar:** **Jat–Jatin** (Mithila, about a married couple separated), **Jhijhiya** (women, praying for rain), **Bidesiya** (dance-drama), **Domkach** (women, at weddings), **Jhumar**, **Paika** (martial), **Kajari**, **Sama–Chakeva** (festival songs and play), **Launda Naach** (men dressing as women). Neighbouring Jharkhand's **Seraikela Chhau** and **Paika** were part of old Bihar.

**Folk theatre of India:** **Bidesiya** (Bihar), **Nautanki** (UP), **Jatra** (Bengal), **Tamasha** (Maharashtra), **Yakshagana** (Karnataka), **Bhavai** (Gujarat), **Swang** (Haryana), **Ramlila**, **Koodiyattam** (Kerala — Sanskrit theatre; UNESCO Intangible Heritage list, 2008).

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the main features of Pala art and its contribution to Asian culture. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Palas (8th–12th c.) of Bihar–Bengal — last great royal patrons of Buddhism; Nalanda, Vikramshila, Odantapuri.
- **Sculpture:** black basalt and ashtadhatu bronze; Buddha, Tara, Avalokiteshvara, and Hindu deities; elegant, ornamented, with back-slab (prabhavali).
- **Centres and finds:** Kurkihar hoard (Gaya), Nalanda bronzes — now in Bihar Museum.
- **Painting:** palm-leaf manuscripts (*Prajnaparamita*) — earliest miniatures; line and colour.
- **Architecture:** monastic universities, cross-shaped shrines (Vikramshila, Somapura).
- **Spread:** Atisha to Tibet; influence on Nepal, Tibet, Myanmar, Java — through monks, students and portable bronzes.
- **Decline:** Turkish invasions (Bakhtiyar Khalji, c. 1200), loss of patronage.
- **Conclusion:** Pala art made Bihar the artistic centre of Buddhist Asia; its legacy lives in Himalayan Buddhist art.

**2. "Mughal architecture represents a synthesis of Indian and Persian traditions." Elucidate. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** From Babur's gardens to Shah Jahan's marble.
- **Persian elements:** charbagh, double dome, iwan, calligraphy, symmetry.
- **Indian elements:** chhatris, brackets, jharokhas, lotus motifs, jaali, Rajput craftsmen, red sandstone.
- **Evolution:** Humayun's tomb → Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza, Panch Mahal) → Itimad-ud-Daulah (pietra dura) → Taj Mahal, Red Fort → Bibi ka Maqbara.
- **Forerunners:** Sher Shah's tomb at Sasaram — Indo-Afghan bridge.
- **Influence:** Rajput palaces, Sikh gurdwaras, colonial Indo-Saracenic style.
- **Conclusion:** a symbol of composite Indian culture.

**3. Describe Bihar's contribution to Indian art, architecture and literature from ancient to modern times. (38 marks)** ⭐

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar — land of Magadha, Nalanda, Mithila — a cradle of Indian culture.
- **Ancient:** Mauryan pillars (Lauriya, Rampurva, Vaishali), Kumhrar palace, Barabar caves, Didarganj Yakshi; Mahabodhi (UNESCO); Nalanda (UNESCO); Aryabhata, Kautilya.
- **Early medieval:** Pala sculpture and painting; Vikramshila; spread to Tibet.
- **Medieval:** Sher Shah's tomb (Sasaram); Maner Sharif; Patna Sahib; Vidyapati's Maithili poetry.
- **Colonial:** Golghar; Patna Kalam; Khuda Bakhsh Library.
- **Folk arts:** Madhubani, Manjusha, Tikuli, Sikki; Bidesiya; Chhath songs.
- **Modern literature and music:** Dinkar, Renu, Nagarjuna, Benipuri, Bhikhari Thakur; Bismillah Khan, Sharda Sinha, dhrupad gharanas.
- **Conclusion:** continuity from Mauryan polish to Madhubani — Bihar's culture is both classical and folk; GI tags and UNESCO status show its living value.

**4. Bring out the differences between the Nagara and Dravida styles of temple architecture with examples. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Hindu temple basics — garbhagriha, mandapa, tower.
- **Nagara:** curved shikhara, amalaka, no big walls; Khajuraho, Konark, Lingaraja, Modhera.
- **Dravida:** stepped vimana, gopurams, compound walls, tanks; Shore temple, Brihadeeswarar, Madurai.
- **Vesara:** mixed; Aihole, Pattadakal, Belur, Halebidu.
- **Bihar note:** Mahabodhi as an early brick-shikhara temple.
- **Conclusion:** regional styles with a common spiritual plan.
