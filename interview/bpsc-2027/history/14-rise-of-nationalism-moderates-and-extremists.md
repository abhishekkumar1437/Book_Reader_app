This chapter starts the National Movement proper. It covers 1858 to 1917: how a national feeling grew, how the Indian National Congress was formed, the two early streams (Moderates and Extremists), the Swadeshi movement, and the creation of Bihar as a separate province in 1912. Two big reference tables are here — **Viceroys 1858–1947** and **Congress sessions** — because Prelims asks "who presided over the Congress session at X" and "under which Viceroy did Y happen" every year.

> **In simple words:** British rule united India under one administration, one law, railways and English education. Educated Indians saw that British rule was making India poor. They formed associations and then, in 1885, the Congress. The early leaders (Moderates) asked politely for reforms. When that gave little result, a new group (Extremists) demanded **Swaraj** and used boycott and mass action. The Partition of Bengal (1905) made the anger national.

## Why nationalism grew

1. **Political and administrative unity** — one government, one law, a common civil service.
2. **Modern communication** — railways (1853), telegraph, post (1854) helped people and ideas travel.
3. **Western education and English** — a common language for leaders from all regions; ideas of liberty, democracy and nationalism (Mill, Burke, Mazzini, the French Revolution).
4. **The press and literature** — newspapers in English and Indian languages; patriotic writing (Bankim's *Anandamath* and "Vande Mataram", Bharatendu Harishchandra in Hindi).
5. **Rediscovery of India's past** — Orientalist scholars (William Jones, Max Müller, James Prinsep who read Ashoka's Brahmi in 1837) and reformers gave pride.
6. **Economic exploitation** — the **drain of wealth** analysis (Naoroji, R.C. Dutt).
7. **Racial discrimination** — the **Ilbert Bill controversy (1883)**: Ripon's bill to let Indian judges try Europeans was diluted after European protest. Indians learnt the power of organised agitation.
8. **Reactionary policies of Lytton** (1876–80): Delhi Durbar during a famine (1877), **Vernacular Press Act (1878)**, **Arms Act (1878)**, ICS age cut to **19 (1876)**, cotton duties removed to help Lancashire.
9. **Socio-religious reform movements** (see [British Policies and Reforms](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/british-policies-reforms-and-economic-impact)).

## The Indian National Congress, 1885

- Founded on **28 December 1885** at **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay**. **72 delegates** attended.
- **Allan Octavian Hume**, a retired ICS officer, took the initiative. The first name proposed was "Indian National Union".
- **First president: Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee).**
- Viceroy at the time: **Lord Dufferin** (he later called the Congress a "microscopic minority").
- The venue was shifted from **Pune** to Bombay because of a cholera outbreak in Pune.

**The "safety valve" debate:**
- Theory: Hume founded the Congress as a **safety valve** to release Indian discontent and save British rule (argued by **Lala Lajpat Rai** in *Young India*, 1916, based on W. Wedderburn's biography of Hume).
- **Bipan Chandra's view**: Indian leaders used Hume as a **"lightning conductor"** — a respected Englishman would protect the young organisation from official suspicion. The Congress was the result of Indian political growth, not a British plan.

> **Prelims angle:** Year 1885, Bombay, 72 delegates, W.C. Bonnerjee president, Hume founder, Dufferin Viceroy. Second session Calcutta 1886 (Naoroji), third Madras 1887 (**Badruddin Tyabji — first Muslim president**), fourth Allahabad 1888 (**George Yule — first English president**).

## The Moderates (1885–1905)

**Leaders:** Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** (Gandhi's political guru; founded the **Servants of India Society, 1905**), Surendranath Banerjee ("**Rashtraguru**"; "Surrender-not"), W.C. Bonnerjee, Badruddin Tyabji, M.G. Ranade, R.C. Dutt, Dinshaw Wacha, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Anand Mohan Bose.

**Beliefs and methods:** faith in British justice; **constitutional methods** — petitions, prayers, protests (**"3 Ps"**), resolutions, memoranda, delegations to England; British Committee of the INC in London (1889) and its paper *India*.

**Demands:**
- Expansion and reform of legislative councils; more Indians in them.
- **Simultaneous ICS examinations** in India and England; higher age limit.
- Separation of the judiciary from the executive.
- Reduction of military expenditure and land revenue; relief for peasants.
- Abolition of the salt tax; protection for Indian industry.
- Freedom of speech and press.

**Achievements:** the **economic critique of colonialism** (drain theory) — their greatest contribution; built a common political platform; trained public opinion; won the **Indian Councils Act 1892**; **simultaneous ICS exams** resolution in the House of Commons (1893).

**Limits:** narrow social base (urban educated middle class); did not involve the masses; too much faith in British goodwill.

## The Extremists (Assertive nationalists)

**Leaders:** **Lal–Bal–Pal** — **Lala Lajpat Rai** ("Punjab Kesari"), **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** ("Lokmanya"; "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it**"), **Bipin Chandra Pal**; and **Aurobindo Ghosh**.

**Why they arose:** Moderates' limited success; Curzon's policies (Calcutta Corporation Act 1899, Official Secrets Act 1904, **Indian Universities Act 1904**, **Partition of Bengal 1905**); famines and plague (1896–97) and harsh plague measures (Rand murdered by the **Chapekar brothers, 1897**); international events — **Japan defeated Russia (1905)**, Ethiopia (Abyssinia) defeated Italy at Adowa (1896); pride from Vivekananda, Dayananda.

**Methods and ideas:** **Swaraj** as the goal; **boycott** of foreign goods, schools, courts; **Swadeshi**; **national education**; **passive resistance** (Aurobindo's essays); mass mobilisation through religious festivals — Tilak's **Ganapati festival (1893)** and **Shivaji festival (1895)**.

| Point | Moderates | Extremists |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Reforms within British empire; self-government later | **Swaraj** |
| Faith in British | Yes | No |
| Methods | Petitions, speeches, resolutions | Boycott, Swadeshi, passive resistance, mass action |
| Social base | Educated urban middle class | Wider — students, lower middle class, some peasants |
| Inspiration | Western liberalism | Indian history and culture, plus Western nationalism |

## The Partition of Bengal and the Swadeshi Movement (1905–11)

- **Curzon** announced the partition on **19–20 July 1905**; it came into effect on **16 October 1905**.
- **Official reason:** Bengal (with Bihar and Orissa) was too big to administer.
- **Real aim:** to weaken Bengal, the centre of nationalism — by dividing **Bengali speakers** (making them a minority in the western province, which included **Bihar and Orissa**) and by **religion** (a Muslim-majority **Eastern Bengal and Assam** with capital **Dacca**).
- **7 August 1905**: the **boycott resolution** at the **Calcutta Town Hall** — formal start of the **Swadeshi movement**.
- **16 October 1905** was observed as a **day of mourning**: people fasted, bathed in the Ganga, and tied **rakhis** to one another as a sign of unity (on **Rabindranath Tagore's** call). Tagore wrote **"Amar Sonar Bangla"** (later Bangladesh's national anthem).
- **Leaders:** Surendranath Banerjee, Aurobindo Ghosh, Bipin Chandra Pal, Tilak (in Maharashtra), Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh (Punjab), **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** (Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company at Tuticorin), Syed Haidar Raza (Delhi).

**Forms of struggle:**
- Boycott of foreign cloth, sugar, salt; bonfires of foreign cloth.
- Swadeshi enterprises — **Bengal Chemicals** (Acharya **P.C. Ray**, 1901), textile mills, soap and match factories, **Banga Lakshmi Cotton Mills**.
- **National education** — **Bengal National College** (Aurobindo as principal), **National Council of Education (1906)**.
- **Samitis** (volunteer bodies) — **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** (Ashwini Kumar Dutt, Barisal).
- Songs and culture — Tagore, Rajanikanta Sen, D.L. Roy, Mukunda Das; **Abanindranath Tagore's *Bharat Mata*** painting.
- Women and students joined in large numbers.

**Decline (after 1908):** heavy repression; **Surat split** (1907); arrest of leaders (Tilak deported to **Mandalay for 6 years, 1908**); lack of mass base among peasants; Muslims were drawn away by communal politics; shift of some youth to **revolutionary terrorism**.

**Partition annulled**: announced at the **Delhi Durbar, 12 December 1911**, by **King George V** (Viceroy **Hardinge II**). Also announced: **capital shifts from Calcutta to Delhi**; **Bihar and Orissa** to form a separate province; Assam a separate Chief Commissionership.

> **Mains angle:** The Swadeshi movement was a **turning point**: (1) from elite politics to mass politics; (2) new methods (boycott, national education, Swadeshi industry) that Gandhi later used; (3) cultural nationalism in songs and art; (4) its weaknesses (narrow peasant base, communal gap) also taught lessons.

## Muslim League and the Morley–Minto Reforms

- **Simla Deputation (1 October 1906)**: Muslim leaders led by the **Aga Khan** met Viceroy Minto and asked for **separate electorates**.
- **All India Muslim League**: founded **30 December 1906** at **Dacca**, at the All India Muhammadan Educational Conference; **Nawab Salimullah** of Dacca, the **Aga Khan** (first president), Nawab Mohsin-ul-Mulk, Viqar-ul-Mulk.
- **Morley–Minto Reforms (1909)** gave **separate electorates** to Muslims.
- **Ghadar Party** (1913, San Francisco), **Komagata Maru** (1914) — see [Revolutionaries, Socialists and Other Streams](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/revolutionaries-socialists-and-other-streams).

## The Surat Split (1907)

- **1906 Calcutta session** (president **Dadabhai Naoroji**): to avoid a split, **Swaraj** was declared the goal and resolutions on **Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education** were passed. Naoroji used the word **"Swaraj"** for the first time from the Congress platform.
- **1907 Surat session**: Extremists wanted **Lajpat Rai** (or Tilak) as president and the four resolutions confirmed; Moderates chose **Rash Behari Ghosh**. The session broke up in chaos — the **Surat split**.
- Moderates controlled the Congress until **1916**. The British followed "**rally the Moderates, suppress the Extremists**".
- **Reunion**: **Lucknow session, 1916** (president **Ambica Charan Mazumdar**).

## Home Rule Movement (1916–18)

| | Tilak's League | Annie Besant's League |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | **April 1916, Belgaum** | **September 1916, Madras (Adyar)** |
| Area | Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar | Rest of India |
| Papers | *Kesari*, *Mahratta* | *New India*, *Commonweal* |
| Helpers | Joseph Baptista, N.C. Kelkar | George Arundale, C.P. Ramaswamy Aiyar, B.P. Wadia |

- **Aim:** self-government (Home Rule) within the British Empire, like Ireland's Home Rule movement.
- Annie Besant was **arrested in June 1917**; protests forced her release (September 1917). She became **Congress president at Calcutta, 1917 — the first woman president**.
- **Lucknow Pact (1916)**: Congress and Muslim League agreed on joint demands; Congress **accepted separate electorates**. Tilak and Jinnah were key figures.
- **Montagu's Declaration, 20 August 1917**: the goal of British policy would be "the progressive realisation of **responsible government**" — the Home Rule agitation helped force this.
- The movement declined after the declaration and after Besant accepted the reforms; Gandhi's rise followed.

> **Bihar connection:** A **Bihar branch of the Home Rule League** was formed at **Patna in 1916**, with **Mazharul Haque** as president. Mazharul Haque had also presided over the **Muslim League session at Bombay in 1915**, where he supported Hindu–Muslim unity — a background to the Lucknow Pact.

## The making of Bihar: separation from Bengal, 1912 ⭐

Before 1912, Bihar was part of the **Bengal Presidency**. Bengali officials, lawyers and teachers held most jobs in Bihar, and Biharis felt neglected. The demand for a separate Bihar grew from the 1870s.

### The movement

- **Early voices:** papers like ***Bihar Bandhu*** (Hindi, 1872) raised the issue; the slogan "**Bihar for Biharis**" spread. The move to make **Hindi in Kaithi/Devanagari** the court language (1880s) strengthened Bihari identity.
- **Mahesh Narayan** and **Sachchidananda Sinha** started the ***Behar Times* (1894)** and wrote pamphlets (Sinha and Mahesh Narayan's *The Partition of Bengal or the Separation of Bihar*, 1906).
- Other leaders: **Ali Imam, Hasan Imam, Mazharul Haque, Nand Kishore Lal, Deep Narayan Singh, Parmeshwar Lal**.
- **First Bihar Provincial Conference, Patna, 1908**, presided over by **Ali Imam**, demanded a separate province.
- **Bihari Students' Conference** (from 1906) — young **Rajendra Prasad** was one of its organisers.
- **Sir Ali Imam**, as Law Member in the Viceroy's Executive Council (1910), is believed to have pressed the case inside the government.

### The decision

- **12 December 1911, Delhi Durbar**: King George V announced the creation of a new province of **Bihar and Orissa**.
- The province came into being by proclamation on **22 March 1912** — celebrated every year as **Bihar Diwas** (Bihar Day). It started functioning from **1 April 1912**.
- Capital: **Patna**. First Lieutenant-Governor: **Sir Charles Stuart Bayley**.
- New institutions followed: **Patna High Court (1916)**, **Patna University (1917)**.
- **27th Congress session at Bankipore (Patna), December 1912**, presided over by **R.N. Mudholkar** — the first Congress session in Bihar.
- Later: Bihar and Orissa became a **Governor's province (1920)** under the 1919 Act with **Lord S.P. Sinha** as the first Indian Governor; **Orissa** was separated on **1 April 1936**; **Jharkhand** was separated on **15 November 2000**.

> **Remember:** **22 March 1912 = Bihar Diwas.** **12 Dec 1911 = Delhi Durbar** announcement. **1 April 1912** = province begins functioning. Read further in [Bihar After Independence](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-after-independence) and [Bihar in the Freedom Struggle](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-in-the-freedom-struggle).

### Mains questions

**1. Trace the movement for the separation of Bihar from Bengal. What was its significance? (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar was part of the Bengal Presidency until 1912; Biharis felt neglected in jobs, education and administration.
- **Causes:** dominance of Bengali officials; little investment in Bihar's education; separate language and identity (Hindi, Kaithi script).
- **Press and leaders:** *Bihar Bandhu* (1872), *Behar Times* (1894) — Sachchidananda Sinha and Mahesh Narayan; Ali Imam, Hasan Imam, Mazharul Haque, Nand Kishore Lal, Deep Narayan Singh.
- **Organisation:** Bihar Provincial Conference (1908, Ali Imam); Bihari Students' Conference; petitions; support of some British officials.
- **Partition of Bengal (1905)** strengthened the case; Ali Imam in the Viceroy's council.
- **Decision:** Delhi Durbar (12 Dec 1911); new province 22 March 1912; capital Patna.
- **Significance:** Patna HC (1916), Patna University (1917); political awakening — Bankipore session 1912; Bihar's own leadership for Champaran and later movements; identity marked by Bihar Diwas.
- **Conclusion:** A peaceful, constitutional movement that gave Bihar its political identity.

**2. Compare the ideology and methods of the Moderates and the Extremists. How did each contribute to the national movement? (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Two phases/streams of the early national movement.
- **Moderates — ideology:** faith in British justice; liberalism; gradual reform.
- **Methods:** 3 Ps; resolutions; London lobbying.
- **Contribution:** drain theory; common platform; Councils Act 1892; trained public opinion.
- **Extremists — ideology:** Swaraj; cultural nationalism; self-reliance.
- **Methods:** Swadeshi, boycott, national education, mass festivals, passive resistance.
- **Contribution:** mass politics; students and women; new methods later used by Gandhi.
- **Limits of both:** Moderates' elitism; Extremists' religious symbols that distanced Muslims; neither reached peasants fully.
- **Conclusion:** Complementary — Moderates laid the intellectual base, Extremists the emotional and mass base; Gandhi combined both.

**3. "The Swadeshi Movement was the first great mass movement of modern India." Comment. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Response to Curzon's partition of Bengal (1905).
- Boycott and Swadeshi; bonfires; Swadeshi industries (Bengal Chemicals, Tuticorin shipping).
- National education; samitis; women and students.
- Cultural side — Tagore, Abanindranath.
- Spread outside Bengal — Tilak, Lajpat Rai, V.O. Chidambaram Pillai.
- Limits — peasants and Muslims not fully drawn in; repression; Surat split.
- **Conclusion:** It prepared the methods of the Gandhian era.
