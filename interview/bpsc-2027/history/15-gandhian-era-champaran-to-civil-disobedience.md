This chapter covers 1915 to 1939, when Gandhi turned the Congress into a mass movement. It is the most-asked period in BPSC: Prelims wants dates, places and leaders; Mains wants analysis, especially of **Champaran (1917)** — Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, fought in Bihar. Learn Champaran so well that you can write 300 words on it without notes.

> **In simple words:** Gandhi came back from South Africa with a new weapon — **satyagraha** (non-violent resistance based on truth). He first used it for peasants in Champaran, Bihar (1917), then for mill workers and peasants in Gujarat (1918). Then he led three great national movements: **Non-Cooperation (1920–22)**, **Civil Disobedience (1930–34)** and later **Quit India (1942)**. Each brought more ordinary people — peasants, women, students, workers — into the freedom struggle.

## Gandhi before India

- **Born:** 2 October 1869, Porbandar (Gujarat). Studied law in London.
- Went to **South Africa in 1893** (case of Dada Abdulla). Thrown off a train at **Pietermaritzburg**.
- Founded the **Natal Indian Congress (1894)**, *Indian Opinion* (1903), **Phoenix Farm (1904)** and **Tolstoy Farm (1910)**.
- First satyagraha against the registration law ("Black Act") in **1906**.
- Book ***Hind Swaraj*** (1909) — criticism of modern civilisation.
- **Returned to India on 9 January 1915** (now **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**). On **Gokhale's** advice he toured India for a year with "ears open and mouth shut".
- Founded the **Sabarmati (Satyagraha) Ashram** near Ahmedabad (1915–17).

### What is satyagraha?

- **Satya** (truth) + **agraha** (firmness) = "holding on to truth".
- A satyagrahi does not hate the opponent, does not use violence, accepts suffering and punishment, and tries to change the opponent's heart.
- Forms: non-cooperation, civil disobedience (breaking an unjust law openly), fasting, hartal, boycott, constructive work (khadi, village industries, Hindu–Muslim unity, removal of untouchability).

## The Champaran Satyagraha, 1917 ⭐⭐

> **Bihar connection:** This is the single most important Bihar topic in the National Movement. Also read [Bihar in the Freedom Struggle](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-in-the-freedom-struggle).

### Background

- **Champaran** (north-west Bihar, now East and West Champaran districts) had many European **indigo planters**. Much land was leased from the **Bettiah Raj** estate.
- **Tinkathia system**: tenants were forced to grow indigo on **3 kathas out of every 20 kathas (one bigha)** of their land — i.e. **3/20** — and sell it to the planter at a fixed low price.
- When **German synthetic dye** made natural indigo unprofitable (early 1900s), planters released tenants from indigo but demanded:
  - **Sharahbeshi** — a big increase in rent; and
  - **Tawan** — a lump-sum payment as compensation.
- They also took illegal cesses (**abwabs**) on marriages, festivals and so on, and used beatings and false cases.
- Earlier protests (notably **1907–08** around Bettiah and Motihari) had been crushed (see [Tribal and Peasant Movements](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/tribal-and-peasant-movements)).

### How Gandhi came

- **Raj Kumar Shukla**, a peasant of Champaran (village Murli Bharahwa), met Gandhi at the **Lucknow Congress session (December 1916)** and kept following him, begging him to come.
- Gandhi reached **Patna on 10 April 1917** with Shukla. He went to **Rajendra Prasad's** house (Prasad was away; the servants did not know who he was). **Mazharul Haque**, who knew Gandhi from London, helped him.
- He went to **Muzaffarpur** the same night, where **Acharya J.B. Kripalani** (then a teacher at the local college) received him. He met lawyers and the planters' association, and the Commissioner of Tirhut, who asked him to leave.
- He reached **Motihari on 15 April 1917**.

### The defiance

- On **16 April 1917** he was served an **order to leave Champaran** (under **Section 144 of the Criminal Procedure Code**).
- Gandhi **refused to obey**. In court (**18 April 1917**) he said he would obey a "higher law — the voice of conscience" and was ready to accept the punishment.
- The government, fearing public reaction, **withdrew the case**. The Lieutenant-Governor (Sir Edward Gait) allowed him to carry on his inquiry.
- This was the **first act of civil disobedience by Gandhi in India**.

### The inquiry

- Gandhi and a team of Bihari lawyers recorded the statements of **thousands of peasants** (the commonly quoted figure is about **8,000**), carefully checking each one.
- **Team:** **Rajendra Prasad**, **Brajkishore Prasad**, **Anugrah Narayan Sinha**, **Ramnavami Prasad**, **Dharnidhar Prasad**, **Shambhusharan Varma**, **Gorakh Prasad**, **J.B. Kripalani**, **Mahadev Desai**, **Narhari Parikh**, and many others. Mazharul Haque supported throughout.
- Peasants lost their fear; huge crowds gathered wherever Gandhi went.

### The result

- In **June 1917** the government set up the **Champaran Agrarian Committee** with **F.G. Sly** as chairman and **Gandhi as a member**.
- The committee's report (October 1917) accepted most of the peasants' complaints.
- The **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** **abolished the tinkathia system**; planters agreed to **refund about 25%** of the money taken illegally (Gandhi accepted a compromise to settle quickly). Within about a decade most planters had left Champaran.

### Constructive work in Champaran

- Gandhi opened **basic schools** — the first at **Barharwa Lakhansen** (13 November 1917), then at **Bhitiharwa** and **Madhuban**. Kasturba Gandhi, Avantikabai Gokhale and other volunteers taught there.
- Work on **sanitation, health and hygiene**, and against purdah and untouchability.
- The **Bhitiharwa Ashram** (West Champaran) is now a memorial.

### Significance

1. **First satyagraha in India** and Gandhi's **first experiment with civil disobedience** here.
2. Made Gandhi a **national leader**; the title "**Mahatma**" spread widely after this (it had been used earlier, including by Tagore).
3. Brought **Bihar's young leaders** — Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Brajkishore Prasad — into Gandhian politics for life.
4. Showed that **peasants** could be organised peacefully against a powerful system.
5. Combined **struggle with constructive work** (schools, health).
6. Created the model for Kheda (1918) and later mass movements.
7. Rajendra Prasad recorded it in his book ***Satyagraha in Champaran*** (1922). The centenary was observed in 2017.

> **Prelims angle:** Raj Kumar Shukla (invited); Lucknow 1916 (meeting); 15 April 1917 (Motihari); Section 144 (order); F.G. Sly (committee); 1918 Act (tinkathia abolished); 3/20 (tinkathia share). Champaran = **first civil disobedience**; Ahmedabad = **first hunger strike**; Kheda = **first non-cooperation** (non-payment of revenue).

## Ahmedabad and Kheda (1918)

- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (February–March 1918):** dispute between mill owners and workers over the "plague bonus". Gandhi advised a strike and a **35%** wage rise; he went on his **first hunger strike in India**. **Anasuya Sarabhai** (sister of the mill-owner Ambalal Sarabhai) supported the workers. The owners agreed to arbitration; workers got the 35% rise.
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918):** crops failed, but the government demanded full revenue. Gandhi, **Vallabhbhai Patel** and Indulal Yagnik led a **no-revenue campaign**. The government quietly suspended collection from poor peasants.

## Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)

- The **Rowlatt Act** (Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act), **March 1919**, based on the **Sedition Committee** of **Justice Sidney Rowlatt**: allowed **arrest without warrant** and **detention without trial** for up to two years; special courts with no appeal. Popular slogan: "**No dalil, no vakil, no appeal**".
- All Indian members of the Imperial Legislative Council opposed it; **Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya and Mazharul Haque resigned** from the Council in protest.
- Gandhi formed the **Satyagraha Sabha** (February 1919) and called a **nationwide hartal on 6 April 1919** — the first all-India mass protest.
- In Punjab, leaders **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr Satyapal** were arrested (10 April).
- **13 April 1919 (Baisakhi)**: **General Reginald Dyer** fired on a peaceful crowd at **Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar**, without warning; the only exit was blocked. Official figure: 379 dead; actual numbers were much higher.
- Martial law in Punjab; humiliating punishments ("crawling order").
- **Protests:** **Rabindranath Tagore returned his knighthood**; Gandhi gave up the **Kaiser-i-Hind** medal (in 1920).
- **Hunter Committee** (government inquiry, 1919) — mild; the **Congress set up its own inquiry** (Motilal Nehru, Gandhi, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji, M.R. Jayakar).
- **Udham Singh** later shot **Michael O'Dwyer** (the Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab in 1919) in London in **1940**.

## The Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) ⭐

### The Khilafat issue

- After World War I, Britain and its allies broke up the **Ottoman (Turkish) empire**. The Sultan of Turkey was the **Khalifa** (spiritual head of Sunni Muslims). Indian Muslims wanted his position protected.
- **Khilafat Committee** (1919): **Ali brothers (Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali)**, **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**, **Hakim Ajmal Khan**, **Hasrat Mohani**. **Khilafat Day: 17 October 1919**. Gandhi presided over the All-India Khilafat Conference (Delhi, November 1919).
- Gandhi saw a chance for **Hindu–Muslim unity** and joined the Khilafat issue with the Punjab wrongs and Swaraj.

### The movement

- **Launched on 1 August 1920** (the day **Tilak died**).
- **Special session, Calcutta (September 1920)** — **Lala Lajpat Rai** presiding — approved Non-Cooperation; **Nagpur session (December 1920)** confirmed it. Goal: **Swaraj within one year**, by peaceful and legitimate means.
- **Programme:**
  - **Boycott**: of government titles and honours, **schools and colleges**, **law courts**, **legislative councils** (elections of November 1920), foreign cloth, and later non-payment of taxes.
  - **Constructive**: **national schools and colleges**, **panchayats** for disputes, **khadi and charkha**, **Tilak Swaraj Fund** (target Rs 1 crore), Hindu–Muslim unity, removal of untouchability.
- **National institutions** founded: **Jamia Millia Islamia** (Aligarh, 1920; later Delhi), **Gujarat Vidyapith**, **Kashi Vidyapith**, **Bihar Vidyapith**.
- **Prince of Wales visit (17 November 1921)** was boycotted with hartals across India.
- The movement merged with local struggles: Awadh peasants, Malabar, Punjab's Akali movement, tribal movements in Andhra, and the Tana Bhagats in Chotanagpur.

### Chauri Chaura and the withdrawal

- On **5 February 1922**, at **Chauri Chaura** (Gorakhpur, UP), an angry crowd set fire to a police station; **22 policemen** were killed.
- Gandhi **withdrew the movement** (Bardoli resolution, **12 February 1922**), saying people were not yet ready for non-violence.
- Many leaders (Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Subhas Bose) criticised the decision.
- Gandhi was arrested (March 1922) and sentenced to **six years** (released in 1924 on health grounds).

### Bihar in the Non-Cooperation Movement

- **Rajendra Prasad** gave up his flourishing legal practice; so did **Mazharul Haque**, Brajkishore Prasad and others.
- **Mazharul Haque** gave land for **Sadaqat Ashram** at Patna (on the Ganga, at Digha), which became the headquarters of the **Bihar Congress**.
- **Bihar Vidyapith** was inaugurated by **Gandhi on 6 February 1921** at Patna — Mazharul Haque its chancellor, Brajkishore Prasad vice-chancellor, Rajendra Prasad principal (of its college). The **Bihar National College** was opened alongside.
- Mazharul Haque started ***The Motherland*** (1921) and Rajendra Prasad the Hindi weekly ***Desh*** (1920).
- Students left government colleges; lawyers left courts; liquor shops were picketed; **khadi** spread.
- Gandhi toured Bihar many times during 1920–21.
- The **Tana Bhagats** of Chotanagpur joined the movement.
- The Prince of Wales's visit to Patna (December 1921) was met with a complete hartal.

> **Mains angle:** Significance of Non-Cooperation: first truly **all-India mass movement**; Congress became a **mass party** with a new constitution (Nagpur 1920 — provincial committees on **linguistic basis**, membership fee 4 annas); **Hindu–Muslim unity** at its peak; national education; khadi; fear of British power broken. Weakness: Khilafat was a religious issue that later fed communal politics; sudden withdrawal. Bihar angle: Sadaqat Ashram, Bihar Vidyapith, leaders' sacrifices.

## Swarajists and No-changers (1922–27)

- **Gaya session (December 1922)**, president **C.R. Das**: Das and **Motilal Nehru** proposed **council entry** — to enter the legislatures and obstruct them from inside. The **No-changers** — **Rajendra Prasad**, **C. Rajagopalachari**, **Vallabhbhai Patel**, **M.A. Ansari** — wanted to continue constructive work.
- The resolution failed; **C.R. Das resigned** and with Motilal formed the **Congress-Khilafat Swaraj Party** (**1 January 1923**).
- The **Swarajists** did well in the **1923 elections**; **Vithalbhai Patel** became the **first Indian President (Speaker) of the Central Legislative Assembly (1925)**.
- Gandhi allowed both groups to work. After **C.R. Das died (1925)**, the Swarajists weakened.
- Meanwhile: communal riots (1920s), the rise of the **RSS (1925)**, the **Communist Party (1925)**, the **HRA/HSRA** revolutionaries, and the **Kakori** case (1925).

> **Bihar connection:** The **Gaya session (1922)** was held in Bihar and is remembered for the **Swarajist–No-changer split**. Bihar's Rajendra Prasad was a leading No-changer.

## Simon Commission and the Nehru Report (1927–29)

- **Simon Commission** (Indian Statutory Commission): announced **November 1927** under Sir **John Simon**; **all 7 members were British** — no Indian. It reached Bombay on **3 February 1928**.
- Slogan: "**Simon Go Back**". Black flags and hartals everywhere, including **Patna**.
- At **Lahore (October 1928)**, **Lala Lajpat Rai** was beaten in a lathi-charge; he died on **17 November 1928**. (Bhagat Singh and comrades shot the police officer **J.P. Saunders** in December 1928 in revenge.)
- **Lord Birkenhead**, Secretary of State, challenged Indians to frame an agreed constitution.
- **Nehru Report (August 1928)** — committee headed by **Motilal Nehru**: **Dominion Status**, **joint electorates** with reservation for minorities, a list of **fundamental rights**, linguistic provinces. Muslim League (Jinnah) and Hindu Mahasabha objected; **Jinnah's Fourteen Points (March 1929)**.
- Young leaders (**Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Bose**) wanted **complete independence** and formed the **Independence for India League (1928)**.
- **Irwin's Deepavali Declaration (31 October 1929)**: Dominion Status was the "natural issue" of India's progress; a Round Table Conference would be held.

## Purna Swaraj — Lahore, 1929

- **Lahore session, December 1929**, president **Jawaharlal Nehru**.
- **Purna Swaraj (complete independence)** declared the goal.
- The **tricolour** was hoisted on the bank of the **Ravi** at midnight on **31 December 1929**.
- **26 January 1930** observed as **Independence Day** across India (that is why the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950).
- The Congress authorised a **Civil Disobedience Movement** and **boycott of the Round Table Conference**.

## The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34) ⭐

### Dandi March

- Gandhi sent Irwin **11 demands** (31 January 1930) — including abolition of the **salt tax**, 50% cut in land revenue, release of political prisoners, reduction of military spending. They were rejected.
- **Salt** was chosen because it touched every Indian, rich and poor, and the tax was clearly unjust.
- **Dandi March: 12 March–6 April 1930**, from **Sabarmati Ashram** to **Dandi** (Gujarat coast), about **385 km (240 miles)**, with **78 followers**. On **6 April 1930** Gandhi broke the salt law.

### Spread across India

| Place | Leader / event |
|---|---|
| **Dharasana** salt works (Gujarat) | **Sarojini Naidu**, Imam Sahib, Manilal Gandhi; brutal beating reported by American journalist **Webb Miller** |
| **Vedaranyam** (Tamil Nadu) | **C. Rajagopalachari** — march from Trichy |
| Malabar (Kerala) | **K. Kelappan** |
| **Peshawar (NWFP)** | **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** ("**Frontier Gandhi**") and his **Khudai Khidmatgars** ("Red Shirts"); soldiers of the **Garhwal Rifles** under **Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused to fire on the crowd |
| Sholapur (Maharashtra) | Textile workers set up a parallel government (May 1930) |
| Manipur–Nagaland | **Rani Gaidinliu** (aged 13 when she joined) |
| Central Provinces, Maharashtra, Karnataka | **Forest satyagraha** |
| Bihar, Bengal, Gujarat | **No-chowkidari-tax** and no-revenue campaigns |

**Features:** breaking salt laws, boycott of foreign cloth and liquor, non-payment of taxes, **large participation of women** (much more than in 1920–22), and of peasants and merchants. Less Muslim participation than in 1920–22.

### Bihar in Civil Disobedience

- Salt laws were broken from **April 1930** in many districts — **Champaran and Saran** were among the first; in **Patna** a symbolic salt-making was planned at the **Nakhas pond**; at **Garhpura** (Begusarai, then in Munger district) the salt law was broken under **Sri Krishna Sinha**; the long satyagraha at **Bihpur (Bhagalpur)** saw police violence on volunteers.
- Bihar had little sea salt, so the movement quickly took the form of a **no-chowkidari-tax campaign** (refusal to pay the tax for village watchmen) — very strong in **Saran, Monghyr (Munger), Bhagalpur, Champaran and Muzaffarpur**. The government confiscated property in response.
- Picketing of **liquor and foreign-cloth shops**; women came out in large numbers.
- **Tarapur firing (Munger), 15 February 1932**: volunteers who tried to hoist the tricolour on the Tarapur police station were fired upon; **34 bodies** were found at the spot (unofficial claims were higher). Since **2022** the Bihar government observes **15 February** as **Shaheed Diwas** for Tarapur. It is often called the biggest police firing after Jallianwala Bagh.
- Leaders: **Rajendra Prasad**, **Anugrah Narayan Sinha**, **Sri Krishna Sinha**, **Jagat Narayan Lal**, **Prof. Abdul Bari** and many others were jailed.

### Round Table Conferences, Gandhi–Irwin Pact and Poona Pact

| Event | Date | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **First RTC** (London) | Nov 1930–Jan 1931 | **Congress boycotted**; princes, Muslim League, Hindu Mahasabha, Ambedkar, Liberals attended; little result |
| **Gandhi–Irwin Pact** (Delhi Pact) | **5 March 1931** | Congress to stop CDM and attend the 2nd RTC; government to release non-violent prisoners, return confiscated property, allow **salt-making for personal use** on the coast, allow peaceful picketing. Not accepted: inquiry into police excesses, commutation of Bhagat Singh's death sentence |
| **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged** | **23 March 1931** | Lahore |
| **Karachi session** | March 1931 | **Vallabhbhai Patel**; endorsed the pact; resolutions on **Fundamental Rights** and **National Economic Programme** |
| **Second RTC** | Sept–Dec 1931 | **Gandhi was the sole Congress representative**; failed over the communal question; Gandhi returned empty-handed |
| CDM resumed | January 1932 | Viceroy **Willingdon**; harsh repression; Congress banned |
| **Communal Award** | **16 August 1932** | British PM **Ramsay MacDonald**: separate electorates for Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, and **depressed classes** |
| Gandhi's fast | From 20 September 1932, in **Yerwada Jail** (Pune) | Against separate electorates for depressed classes |
| **Poona Pact** | **24 September 1932** | **Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar**: separate electorates for depressed classes dropped; instead **reserved seats** in a **joint electorate** — **148** seats in provincial legislatures (instead of 71 under the Award), and 18% in the Central Legislature |
| **Third RTC** | Nov–Dec 1932 | Congress did not attend |
| CDM suspended / withdrawn | May 1933 / April 1934 | — |

- Gandhi then focused on **Harijan work**: **Harijan Sevak Sangh (1932)**, weekly *Harijan* (1933), an all-India **Harijan tour (1933–34)**.

> **Common mistake:** Gandhi attended only the **Second** RTC. **Ambedkar attended all three.** The Poona Pact did **not** abolish reservation — it **increased** reserved seats but within joint electorates.

### The Bihar earthquake, 1934

- On **15 January 1934**, a huge earthquake struck **north Bihar** (and Nepal). Munger, Muzaffarpur, Darbhanga, Motihari and Sitamarhi were badly hit; thousands died.
- **Rajendra Prasad** (just out of jail) organised the **Bihar Central Relief Committee**; Gandhi toured the area.
- Gandhi said the earthquake was a "**divine chastisement**" for the sin of **untouchability**. **Tagore publicly disagreed**, saying natural events have physical causes — a famous debate between the two.
- The **Agricultural Research Institute at Pusa** was damaged and later moved to Delhi.

## Government of India Act 1935 and the 1937 ministries

- Elections under the 1935 Act were held in **early 1937**. Congress won a majority in **7 of 11 provinces** (later 8), including **Bihar**.
- Congress at first refused office until it got an assurance that Governors would not interfere. In the meantime, **Mohammad Yunus** (Muslim Independent Party) formed a short-lived ministry in Bihar (**April–July 1937**) — he is called the **first Premier (Prime Minister) of Bihar**.
- On **20 July 1937**, **Sri Krishna Sinha** became the **Premier of Bihar** with **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** as Finance Minister.
- Work of the Congress ministry in Bihar: release of political prisoners, tenancy relief laws (reducing rent, restoring some bakasht land — though the Kisan Sabha wanted more), prohibition in some areas, and expansion of education.
- In **February 1938**, the Bihar and UP ministries **resigned** over the Governor's refusal to release political prisoners; the Viceroy gave in and they returned.
- All Congress ministries **resigned in October–November 1939** because Britain declared India a party to World War II without consulting Indians. The Muslim League celebrated **"Deliverance Day" (22 December 1939)**.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Satyagraha | Non-violent resistance based on truth and willingness to suffer |
| Civil disobedience | Openly and peacefully breaking an unjust law and accepting punishment |
| Non-cooperation | Refusing to work with or use the institutions of an unjust government |
| Hartal | Closing shops and stopping work as a protest |
| Tinkathia | Forced indigo cultivation on 3/20 of a tenant's land |
| Khilafat | The office of the Khalifa (Caliph), held by the Turkish Sultan |
| Swarajists | Congress members who entered legislatures to obstruct from within (1923) |
| No-changers | Congress members who opposed council entry and stayed with constructive work |
| Purna Swaraj | Complete independence |
| Chowkidari tax | Village tax for paying the village watchman (chowkidar) |
| Communal Award | 1932 British decision on separate electorates |
| Constructive programme | Gandhi's social work — khadi, Hindu–Muslim unity, removal of untouchability, basic education |

### Mains questions

**1. "Champaran was the laboratory of Gandhian satyagraha." Discuss the causes, course and significance of the Champaran Satyagraha. (38 marks, ~300 words)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** 1917, north Bihar; Gandhi's first satyagraha in India; turned a local peasant grievance into a national symbol.
- **Causes:** tinkathia (3/20), sharahbeshi and tawan after synthetic dye; abwabs; planters' violence; Bettiah Raj leases; earlier failed protests (1907–08).
- **Course:** Raj Kumar Shukla's invitation; Patna–Muzaffarpur–Motihari (April 1917); Section 144 order defied; case withdrawn; inquiry with Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Kripalani, Mahadev Desai; thousands of statements.
- **Outcome:** Sly Committee (June 1917) with Gandhi; Champaran Agrarian Act 1918 — tinkathia abolished; 25% refund; planters left.
- **Constructive work:** schools (Barharwa Lakhansen, Bhitiharwa, Madhuban), sanitation, health.
- **Significance:** first civil disobedience; method of truth-based inquiry; fearlessness among peasants; rise of Bihar's leadership; model for Kheda and later mass movements; Gandhi emerges as a national leader.
- **Limits:** compromise on refund; indigo was already declining.
- **Conclusion:** Champaran showed that non-violence could defeat a powerful system — the first lesson of the Gandhian era was written in Bihar.

**2. Assess the role of Bihar in the Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience Movements. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** After Champaran, Bihar became one of the strongest bases of Gandhian politics.
- **NCM:** leaders gave up practice (Rajendra Prasad, Mazharul Haque); Sadaqat Ashram; Bihar Vidyapith (6 Feb 1921) and national schools; *The Motherland*, *Desh*; boycott of the Prince of Wales; Tana Bhagats.
- **Gaya session 1922:** Swarajist debate; Bihar leaders as No-changers.
- **Simon boycott** in Patna.
- **CDM:** salt satyagraha (Champaran, Saran, Patna, Bihpur); no-chowkidari-tax campaign (Saran, Munger, Bhagalpur); liquor and cloth picketing; women's participation; Tarapur firing (1932).
- **Constructive side:** khadi, Harijan work; 1934 earthquake relief led by Rajendra Prasad.
- **Political result:** Congress victory in 1937; Sri Krishna Sinha ministry.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's peasants, students and women turned Gandhian programmes into mass action.

**3. Examine the significance of the Non-Cooperation Movement in the history of the freedom struggle. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** 1920–22, first all-India mass movement.
- Hindu–Muslim unity through Khilafat.
- Congress reorganised (Nagpur 1920 constitution, linguistic PCCs).
- National education, khadi, Tilak Swaraj Fund.
- Spread to peasants, workers, tribals.
- Withdrawal after Chauri Chaura — debate.
- **Conclusion:** It made nationalism a people's movement and Gandhi its undisputed leader.

**4. Discuss the circumstances leading to the Poona Pact and its importance. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Communal Award (16 Aug 1932) by Ramsay MacDonald.
- Separate electorates for depressed classes — Gandhi saw it as dividing Hindu society.
- Gandhi's fast in Yerwada (from 20 Sept 1932).
- Ambedkar's position — political protection for Dalits.
- Pact (24 Sept 1932): reserved seats (148) in joint electorates.
- Importance — basis of SC reservation in the Constitution; Harijan Sevak Sangh; temple entry campaigns.
- **Conclusion:** A compromise between national unity and social justice.
