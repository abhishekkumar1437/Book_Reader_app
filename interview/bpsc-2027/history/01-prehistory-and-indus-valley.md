This chapter covers the long period before written history in India: the Stone Ages, the first farmers, the copper-using villages, and the Harappan (Indus Valley) civilisation. BPSC Prelims asks short factual questions here: "Which site has a dockyard?", "Who discovered Harappa?", "Chirand is in which district?". Mains rarely asks this period directly, but a good culture answer often starts with Harappan town planning or art.

> **In simple words:** People in India first lived by hunting and gathering with stone tools. Slowly they started farming, keeping animals and living in villages. Around 2600 BCE some villages in the north-west grew into planned cities with drains, baths and seals — the Harappan civilisation. It faded after about 1900 BCE. Bihar's own story in this period is mainly about early farming villages like Chirand.

## How historians divide prehistory

**Prehistory** means the time before writing. We know it only from **archaeology** (the study of things people left behind: tools, bones, pots, houses). **Protohistory** means a culture that had writing, but we cannot read it — the Harappan civilisation is protohistoric because its script is still not deciphered. **History** proper starts when we can read written records.

Stone Age cultures are named after the kind of stone tools people made:

| Age | Greek meaning | Rough time in India | Main tools | Way of life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Palaeolithic** (Old Stone Age) | palaeo = old, lithos = stone | c. 2 million / 1.5 million years ago – c. 10,000 BCE | Hand-axes, cleavers, choppers (quartzite) | Hunting, gathering, no farming |
| **Mesolithic** (Middle Stone Age) | meso = middle | c. 10,000 – 6000 BCE (varies by region) | **Microliths** — tiny blades fixed on wood/bone | Hunting, fishing, first domestication of animals |
| **Neolithic** (New Stone Age) | neo = new | c. 7000 BCE (Mehrgarh) – c. 1000 BCE (south & east) | Polished stone axes (celts), bone tools | Farming, cattle, pottery, settled villages |
| **Chalcolithic** (Copper–Stone Age) | chalco = copper | c. 3000 – 700 BCE (varies) | Copper plus stone tools | Village farming, painted pottery |

> **Remember:** These dates overlap a lot because different regions changed at different times. The Neolithic began in Balochistan around 7000 BCE but in Bihar and the south only around 2500–2000 BCE. In exams, the *order* of ages and the *key sites* matter more than exact dates.

### Palaeolithic sites

The Palaeolithic is split into **Lower** (hand-axes, cleavers — the *Acheulian* tradition), **Middle** (flakes — tools made from flakes struck off a stone) and **Upper** (blades, burins; also the first art and the arrival of modern humans).

| Site | State | Why it is famous |
|---|---|---|
| **Pallavaram** (near Chennai) | Tamil Nadu | First Palaeolithic tool in India found here by **Robert Bruce Foote** in **1863** |
| **Attirampakkam** | Tamil Nadu | Acheulian tools dated to about 1.5 million years ago (one of the oldest in India) |
| **Bhimbetka** | Madhya Pradesh (Raisen district) | Rock shelters with paintings from Upper Palaeolithic to later periods; found by **V.S. Wakankar** (1957); **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2003)** |
| **Hunsgi** | Karnataka | Lower Palaeolithic factory site |
| **Soan valley** | Punjab (Pakistan) | "Soanian" chopper-chopping tools |
| **Belan valley** | Uttar Pradesh (Prayagraj area) | Sequence from Palaeolithic to Neolithic |
| **Didwana** | Rajasthan | Lower to Middle Palaeolithic |
| **Paisra** | **Bihar (Munger district)** | Lower Palaeolithic (Acheulian) site in the Kharagpur hills |

### Mesolithic sites

The Mesolithic is the bridge between hunting and farming. Microliths were fitted into handles to make arrows, sickles and spears.

| Site | State | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Bagor** | Rajasthan | Largest Mesolithic site; animal domestication |
| **Adamgarh** | Madhya Pradesh | Early evidence of animal domestication |
| **Sarai Nahar Rai, Mahadaha, Damdama** | Uttar Pradesh (Pratapgarh) | Human burials, hearths |
| **Langhnaj** | Gujarat | Microliths, burials |
| **Bhimbetka** | Madhya Pradesh | Many paintings of animals and hunting scenes belong to this phase |

> **Prelims angle:** "Earliest evidence of domestication of animals" → **Adamgarh (MP)** and **Bagor (Rajasthan)**. "Rock paintings" → **Bhimbetka**. "First Palaeolithic tool" → **Pallavaram, Robert Bruce Foote, 1863**.

## The Neolithic revolution: farming begins

The **Neolithic revolution** is the change from food *gathering* to food *producing* (farming and herding). It made settled villages possible. Archaeologist **V. Gordon Childe** coined the term.

| Site | State / Region | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Mehrgarh** | Balochistan (Pakistan) | Oldest farming settlement in the subcontinent (c. **7000 BCE**); wheat, barley, mud-brick houses |
| **Burzahom** | Kashmir | **Pit dwellings**; **dogs buried with their masters** |
| **Gufkral** | Kashmir | Pit dwellings ("cave of the potter") |
| **Koldihwa / Lahuradewa** | Uttar Pradesh | Early evidence of **rice** cultivation (Lahuradewa is now considered among the earliest) |
| **Chirand** | **Bihar (Saran district)** | Neolithic **bone and antler tools** in large numbers; later Chalcolithic layers |
| **Chechar, Senuar, Taradih, Maner** | **Bihar** (Vaishali, Rohtas, Bodh Gaya, Patna) | Neolithic–Chalcolithic farming villages |
| **Maski, Brahmagiri, Piklihal, Utnur** | Karnataka | **Ash mounds** (burnt cattle pens) |
| **Daojali Hading** | Assam | Neolithic of the north-east; cord-marked pottery |
| **Paiyampalli** | Tamil Nadu | Southern Neolithic |

> **Bihar connection:** **Chirand** (Saran district, on the Ganga near its meeting with the Ghaghara) is Bihar's most famous prehistoric site. Excavations (led by B.S. Verma of the Bihar State Archaeology Department, 1960s–70s) found Neolithic bone tools, polished stone axes, rice, wheat and pottery, followed by Chalcolithic and later layers. **Senuar** (Rohtas) and **Taradih** (near Bodh Gaya) are other Neolithic–Chalcolithic sites. BPSC has asked "Chirand is located in which district?" — answer **Saran**.

> **Memory trick:** **"Burzahom Buries dogs; Chirand Carves bones."**

## The Chalcolithic cultures

Chalcolithic people used copper along with stone. Most were rural; they made painted pottery (often black-on-red) and lived in mud houses. They existed both before and after the Harappan cities.

| Culture | Region | Sites | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ahar–Banas** | South-east Rajasthan | Ahar, Gilund, Balathal | Copper smelting; black-and-red ware |
| **Kayatha** | Madhya Pradesh | Kayatha | Early (c. 2000 BCE) |
| **Malwa** | Madhya Pradesh | Navdatoli, Eran | Large villages |
| **Jorwe** | Maharashtra | Inamgaon, Daimabad, Nevasa | Inamgaon had a planned layout and pot burials; Daimabad bronze chariot |
| **Eastern (Chalcolithic of Bihar & Bengal)** | Bihar, West Bengal | Chirand, Chechar, Senuar, Pandu Rajar Dhibi | Black-and-red ware |
| **Ochre Coloured Pottery (OCP) & Copper Hoards** | Ganga–Yamuna doab | Atranjikhera, Saipai | Copper weapons found in hoards |

## The Harappan (Indus Valley) civilisation

### Discovery

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| First site excavated | **Harappa** (on the **Ravi**, Punjab, Pakistan) — **Daya Ram Sahni, 1921** |
| Second | **Mohenjo-daro** (on the **Indus**, Larkana, Sindh) — **R.D. (Rakhal Das) Banerji, 1922** |
| Director-General of ASI who announced it | **Sir John Marshall** (1924) |
| Name used by archaeologists | **Harappan civilisation** (after the first site found) |
| Type | **Bronze Age** urban civilisation; protohistoric (script not deciphered) |

### Dates — where historians differ

- **NCERT (Themes in Indian History I):** Mature Harappan phase **c. 2600–1900 BCE**; Early Harappan before it, Late Harappan after it.
- **Radiocarbon dating (D.P. Agrawal):** about **2500–1750 BCE** — this is the date many guidebooks (e.g. Lucent) give.
- **John Marshall** had suggested 3250–2750 BCE; that is outdated.

> **Remember:** If the question asks the *mature phase* as per NCERT, write **2600–1900 BCE**. If the options give 2500–1750 BCE, that is the radiocarbon answer. Both point to "third millennium BCE, ending early second millennium".

### Extent

The civilisation covered about **1.3 million sq km** — larger than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined. Most sites lie along the Indus and the (now dry) **Ghaggar–Hakra** river.

> **Prelims angle:** Classic pairs: **Lothal–dockyard**, **Kalibangan–ploughed field & fire altars**, **Chanhudaro–bead factory/no citadel**, **Mohenjo-daro–Great Bath & Dancing Girl**, **Dholavira–water reservoirs & signboard**, **Banawali–toy plough**, **Rakhigarhi–largest in India**, **Ropar–first after Independence**. Also: the **largest Harappan site overall** is usually given as **Mohenjo-daro** (some recent surveys say Rakhigarhi is bigger) — read the option wording ("in India" → Rakhigarhi).

> **Common mistake:** Lothal and Dholavira are both in **Gujarat** — Lothal is near the Gulf of Khambhat (Ahmedabad district); Dholavira is in the **Rann of Kutch**. Kalibangan is in **Rajasthan**, not Haryana.

### Town planning

- Cities were laid out in a **grid pattern**: streets crossed at **right angles**.
- Usually two parts: a raised **citadel** (western, smaller — public buildings) and a larger **lower town** (eastern — houses). Dholavira had three parts.
- **Standardised burnt bricks** in the ratio **1 : 2 : 4** (height : width : length).
- **Covered drains** along streets, with inspection holes — the best drainage of the ancient world.
- Houses faced side lanes, had courtyards, wells and bathrooms; some had two storeys.
- **Great Bath** (Mohenjo-daro): about 12 m × 7 m × 2.4 m deep, made watertight with bitumen; probably for ritual bathing.
- **Granaries** at Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (the Mohenjo-daro "granary" is the largest building, though some scholars now doubt it was a granary).

### Economy

| Area | Facts |
|---|---|
| Crops | Wheat, barley, peas, sesame, mustard, rice (Lothal, Rangpur), **cotton** — the Harappans were the **first to grow cotton**; Greeks later called it **sindon** |
| Animals | Cattle (humped bull), sheep, goats, buffalo, pigs, dogs; elephant known; **horse not clearly attested** (Surkotada bones disputed) |
| Metals | Copper, bronze (copper + tin), gold, silver; **no iron** |
| Crafts | Bead-making (carnelian, steatite), pottery (red with black designs), terracotta toys, seals, shell work |
| Weights | Cubical chert weights; **binary system** (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 …), 16 as a basic unit |
| Trade | With **Mesopotamia**, which called the Indus region **Meluhha**; intermediate stations **Dilmun** (Bahrain) and **Magan** (Oman). Harappan seals found at Ur, Kish. Barter; no coins |

### Society, religion and art

- **No temples** or palaces have been found; rulers are unknown (possibly merchants or priests).
- **Mother Goddess** terracotta figurines — fertility worship.
- **Pashupati seal** (Mohenjo-daro): a seated, horned figure surrounded by an **elephant, tiger, rhinoceros and buffalo**, with **two deer** below — John Marshall called it **proto-Shiva**.
- Worship of trees (**peepal**), animals (humped bull), **fire altars** (Kalibangan, Lothal), and possibly **linga–yoni** symbols.
- The most common animal on seals is the **"unicorn"** (one-horned bull).
- **Seals**: mostly of **steatite** (soapstone), square, with an animal and a short inscription; used for trade and identity.
- **Script**: pictographic, about **400 signs**, written mainly **right to left** (some lines alternate direction — *boustrophedon*). **Not deciphered.**
- Burials: extended burial with pots; at Lothal, joint (couple) burials; at Ropar and Burzahom (Neolithic) dogs with humans.
- Art: **bronze Dancing Girl** (lost-wax technique), **steatite Priest-King** with trefoil-patterned shawl, red sandstone torso (Harappa), terracotta toys with movable heads, carts.

> **Mains angle:** For a GS I culture answer on Indian architecture or urban planning, open with the Harappans: grid streets, 1:2:4 bricks, covered drains, Great Bath, Dholavira's water harvesting. These link nicely to today's "smart city" and water-conservation themes.

### Decline — main theories

The cities declined from about **1900 BCE**. People moved east and south into smaller villages ("Late Harappan" or "post-urban" phase). No single cause is accepted.

| Theory | Scholar(s) |
|---|---|
| Aryan invasion | **Mortimer Wheeler** — now **rejected** (no evidence of a massacre) |
| Floods of the Indus | **John Marshall**, Robert Raikes, M.R. Sahni |
| Tectonic shifts / change of river course, drying of the Ghaggar–Hakra | Raikes, H.T. Lambrick, others |
| Climate change (less rainfall) | Aurel Stein, A.N. Ghosh |
| Ecological imbalance (over-use of forests and land) | **Fairservis** |
| Decline of trade with Mesopotamia | Various |

> **Remember:** Most historians today think of a **combination** of environmental change (drying rivers, weaker monsoon) and the collapse of long-distance trade.

> **Bihar connection:** No mature Harappan city has been found in Bihar. Bihar's prehistory is about **Paisra** (Palaeolithic, Munger) and **Chirand, Chechar, Senuar, Taradih** (Neolithic–Chalcolithic). These sites show that the middle Ganga plain was being farmed long before the rise of Magadha. Read [Ancient Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/ancient-bihar) alongside this chapter.

### Mains questions

**1. "The Harappan civilisation was the first urban civilisation of the Indian subcontinent." Discuss its town planning and economic life.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Bronze Age civilisation, c. 2600–1900 BCE, discovered 1921 at Harappa; area larger than Egypt + Mesopotamia.
- Grid plan; citadel and lower town; Dholavira's three divisions.
- Standard burnt bricks (1:2:4), covered drains, wells, bathrooms — public health awareness.
- Public buildings: Great Bath, granaries, assembly hall.
- Agriculture: wheat, barley, rice, first cotton; ploughed field at Kalibangan.
- Crafts: beads (Chanhudaro, Lothal), seals, bronze casting.
- Trade: standard weights, Lothal dockyard, Meluhha links with Mesopotamia via Dilmun and Magan.
- *Conclusion:* Planning and water management (Dholavira) remain lessons for modern cities.

**2. Discuss the various theories about the decline of the Harappan civilisation.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Decline from c. 1900 BCE; shift to rural Late Harappan phase.
- Aryan invasion (Wheeler) — rejected; no evidence of war.
- Floods (Marshall, Raikes); tectonic change and river shift.
- Drying of the Ghaggar–Hakra; weaker monsoon (climate change).
- Ecological imbalance — deforestation, overgrazing (Fairservis).
- Collapse of trade with Mesopotamia.
- *Conclusion:* Gradual, many-cause decline; cultural continuity into later Indian life (bull, peepal, Mother Goddess, weights).

**3. Trace the development of prehistoric cultures in Bihar.** (150 words)

- *Intro:* Bihar's prehistory known from archaeology in the Ganga plain and southern hills.
- Palaeolithic: Paisra (Munger), Kaimur–Rohtas hills with rock art.
- Neolithic: Chirand (Saran) — bone/antler tools, rice; Senuar (Rohtas), Chechar (Vaishali), Taradih (Bodh Gaya).
- Chalcolithic: black-and-red ware at the same sites; copper tools.
- *Conclusion:* Early farming base explains later rise of Magadha.
