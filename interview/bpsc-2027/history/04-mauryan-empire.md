The Mauryan Empire (c. 321–185 BCE) was India's first empire covering almost the whole subcontinent, and its capital **Pataliputra** is today's **Patna**. BPSC asks about the sources (Arthashastra, Indica), Ashoka's edicts (which edict says what, where they are found), the administration, and Bihar's Mauryan monuments (Kumrahar, Didarganj Yakshi, Lauriya-Nandangarh, Rampurva, Barabar caves). For Mains, Ashoka's Dhamma and Mauryan administration are frequent themes.

> **In simple words:** With the help of his teacher Chanakya, Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the last Nanda king and built an empire from Afghanistan to Karnataka. His son Bindusara kept it, and his grandson Ashoka conquered Kalinga. Shocked by the bloodshed, Ashoka gave up war and spread a moral code called *Dhamma* through edicts carved on rocks and pillars. The empire was run by a large, organised bureaucracy from Pataliputra. After Ashoka, weak kings and money problems led to its end in 185 BCE.

> **Remember:** Ashoka **came to the throne c. 273 BCE** but was **crowned c. 269/268 BCE** after a four-year succession struggle. Ashoka's reign is commonly given as **c. 268–232 BCE** (or 273–232 BCE). Kalinga war: **c. 261 BCE** (eighth year after coronation).

## The rulers

### Chandragupta Maurya (c. 321–297 BCE)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origin | Disputed — Buddhist texts: **Moriya** clan of Kshatriyas (Pipphalivana); Brahmanical texts / *Mudrarakshasa*: low birth (*Vrishala*). Greeks called him **Sandrocottus** (Justin) / Androcottus |
| Rise | With **Chanakya (Kautilya)** overthrew **Dhana Nanda** (c. 321 BCE); also cleared Greek garrisons in the Punjab |
| War with **Seleucus Nicator** | c. 305 BCE; treaty c. 303 BCE — Seleucus gave **Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Balochistan), Paropanisadae (Kabul)** and probably **Aria (Herat)**; received **500 elephants**; a marriage alliance; sent **Megasthenes** as ambassador to Pataliputra |
| Extent | Afghanistan to Bengal, Kashmir to the Deccan; western India (Saurashtra — governor Pushyagupta built Sudarshana lake) |
| Religion | Became a **Jain**; went with **Bhadrabahu** to **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** during the 12-year famine and died there by **sallekhana** (Jain ritual fasting) on **Chandragiri hill** (Jain tradition) |

### Bindusara (c. 297–273 BCE)

- Greek name **Amitrochates** (from Sanskrit **Amitraghata**, "slayer of enemies").
- Asked **Antiochus I** of Syria for **sweet wine, dried figs and a sophist** (philosopher) — he got the wine and figs, but was told Greeks do not sell philosophers.
- Greek envoy **Deimachus** (from Antiochus I); **Dionysius** was sent by **Ptolemy II Philadelphus** of Egypt (Pliny).
- Followed the **Ajivika** sect.
- Tibetan historian **Taranatha** credits him with conquering the "land between the two seas" (the Deccan). Ashoka, as prince, put down a **revolt at Taxila**.

### Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Before becoming king | Viceroy at **Ujjain** (and at **Taxila**) |
| Titles | **Devanampiya Piyadassi** ("beloved of the gods, of pleasing appearance") |
| Name "Ashoka" appears | In the **Maski** minor rock edict (Karnataka, found **1915** by C. Beadon), and also at Gujarra (MP), Nittur and Udegolam (Karnataka) |
| **Kalinga war** | c. **261 BCE** — described in **Major Rock Edict XIII**: **1,50,000 deported, 1,00,000 killed**, many more died; Ashoka's remorse |
| Conversion | To Buddhism under monk **Upagupta** (or **Nigrodha** in Sri Lankan texts) |
| Buddhist acts | **Third Buddhist Council at Pataliputra** (c. 250 BCE); sent son **Mahendra** and daughter **Sanghamitra** to **Sri Lanka** with a branch of the Bodhi tree; missionaries to Kashmir, Gandhara, Himalayas, Suvarnabhumi (Myanmar/Thailand), etc. |
| Dhamma officers | **Dhamma Mahamattas** appointed in the **13th year** after coronation (Major Rock Edict V) |
| Dhamma yatras | Pilgrimage tours instead of hunting trips (*vihara yatra*); visited **Bodh Gaya** in the 10th year (MRE VIII), **Lumbini** in the 20th (Rummindei pillar) |
| Extent | From Afghanistan (Kandahar) to Karnataka (Brahmagiri, Maski); the far south (Cholas, Pandyas, Satyaputras, Keralaputras) and Sri Lanka were outside but friendly |
| Queens | **Karuvaki** (mother of prince **Tivara**) is named in the **Queen's Edict** (Allahabad pillar) |

| Ruler | Note |
|---|---|

| **Samprati** | Patron of Jainism (Jain tradition) |

## Ashoka's inscriptions and Dhamma

### Types of inscriptions

| Type | Number / Key sites | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **14 Major Rock Edicts** | **Girnar** (Gujarat), **Kalsi** (Uttarakhand), **Shahbazgarhi** and **Mansehra** (Pakistan — in **Kharosthi**), **Dhauli** and **Jaugada** (Odisha), **Sopara** (Maharashtra), **Yerragudi** (Andhra Pradesh), **Kandahar** (Afghanistan — **Greek & Aramaic**), Sannati (Karnataka) | At **Dhauli and Jaugada** (Kalinga), edicts **XI–XIII are left out** and replaced by **two Separate Kalinga Edicts** ("all men are my children") |
| **Minor Rock Edicts** | **Maski**, Brahmagiri, Gujarra, Rupnath, Bairat, **Sasaram (Bihar)**, Ahraura, Yerragudi, Nittur, Udegolam | Personal statements of Ashoka's Buddhist faith; **Bhabru (Bairat)** edict shows his faith in Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha |
| **7 Pillar Edicts** | **Delhi–Topra** (only one with **all seven**; brought to Delhi by **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**), Delhi–Meerut, Allahabad–Kosam, **Lauriya-Araraj**, **Lauriya-Nandangarh**, **Rampurva** (all three in **Bihar**) | |
| **Minor Pillar Edicts** | **Rummindei (Lumbini)**, Nigali Sagar (Nepal), Sanchi, Sarnath, Allahabad | Rummindei: Ashoka visited Lumbini, **exempted the village from bali** and reduced bhaga to **one-eighth**. Sanchi/Sarnath/Allahabad: **Schism Edict** (warning monks against splitting the Sangha) |
| **Cave inscriptions** | **Barabar hills (Jehanabad, Bihar)** | Gift of caves to **Ajivikas** |

Scripts used: **Brahmi** (most), **Kharosthi** (north-west), **Greek and Aramaic** (Afghanistan). Language: **Prakrit** mostly.

### What the 14 Major Rock Edicts say

| Edict | Subject |
|---|---|
| I | Ban on **animal sacrifice** and festive gatherings (*samaja*) |
| II | **Medical treatment for humans and animals**; names **Cholas, Pandyas, Satyaputras, Keralaputras** and **Tamraparni (Sri Lanka)**, and the Greek king **Antiochus** |
| III | Generosity to Brahmins and Shramanas; **Yuktas, Rajukas and Pradeshikas** to tour every **five years** |
| IV | **Dhammaghosha** (sound of Dhamma) replaces **bherighosha** (sound of war drums) |
| V | Appointment of **Dhamma Mahamattas**; kind treatment of slaves |
| VI | King ready to hear the people's matters **at any time**; welfare of subjects |
| VII | **Tolerance** among all sects |
| VIII | **Dhamma yatras**; visit to **Bodh Gaya** (Sambodhi) |
| IX | Criticism of useless ceremonies; stress on moral conduct |
| X | Fame and glory only through Dhamma |
| XI | Explanation of Dhamma |
| XII | **Respect for all sects**; dialogue between faiths; mentions *Ithijhaka Mahamattas* (officers for women) |
| XIII | **Kalinga war** and Ashoka's remorse; **conquest by Dhamma (dhammavijaya)**; names five Greek kings — **Antiochus II** (Syria), **Ptolemy II** (Egypt), **Antigonus Gonatas** (Macedonia), **Magas** (Cyrene), **Alexander** (Epirus/Corinth) |
| XIV | Purpose of writing the edicts |

> **Memory trick:** **"XIII = Kalinga"** (an unlucky number for the war). **"II = Hospitals"** (two kinds of patients — humans and animals). **"XII = tolerance dialogue"**.

### Ashoka's Dhamma — what it was

Dhamma (Sanskrit *dharma*) was **not Buddhism as a religion**. It was a **moral code** for everyone:

- Obedience to parents and elders; respect for teachers.
- Generosity to Brahmins, Shramanas (ascetics), friends and relatives.
- Kind treatment of slaves and servants.
- **Non-violence** to living beings; ban on animal sacrifice; royal kitchen cut meat.
- **Tolerance** — "honour another's sect" (MRE XII).
- Few wants and few possessions.
- Welfare works: wells, shade trees, rest-houses, hospitals for humans and animals, medicinal herbs.
- Officers: **Dhamma Mahamattas**; tours: Dhamma yatras.

Historians' views: **Romila Thapar** sees Dhamma as a **social policy** to hold together a diverse empire; others see it as Ashoka's personal Buddhist faith applied to government.

> **Mains angle:** "Ashoka's Dhamma was a code of social ethics and not a religion — comment." Points: its contents (above), its lack of Buddhist doctrines (no Four Noble Truths/nirvana mentioned), its purpose (unity of a multi-ethnic empire, reduce conflict), methods (edicts in local scripts, Dhamma Mahamattas, yatras), results (welfare, tolerance; criticism — weakened army, angered Brahmins), relevance today (tolerance, welfare state, animal welfare; the Ashoka Chakra and lion capital as national symbols).

## Megasthenes and Pataliputra

Megasthenes lived in **Pataliputra** (he called it **Palibothra**) and described it:

- On the meeting of the **Ganga and the Erannoboas (Son)**.
- A parallelogram about **80 stadia long and 15 stadia wide** (roughly 14.5 km × 2.8 km).
- Surrounded by a **wooden wall** with **570 towers and 64 gates**, and a deep **moat**. (Wooden palisade remains were found at **Bulandi Bagh**, Patna.)
- The **royal palace** was grander than the palaces of Persia (Susa, Ecbatana).

**City administration** — **6 committees of 5 members each (30 members)**:

| Committee | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Industrial arts and crafts |
| 2 | Foreigners (lodging, care) |
| 3 | **Registration of births and deaths** |
| 4 | Trade and commerce, weights and measures |
| 5 | Supervision of manufactured goods |
| 6 | **Collection of tax on sales (one-tenth)** |

The **army** was also run by **6 committees of 5** — infantry, cavalry, elephants, chariots, **navy**, and transport/supplies.

**Indian society according to Megasthenes — seven "castes":** philosophers, farmers, soldiers, herdsmen, artisans, overseers/magistrates, councillors. He also wrote that there was **no slavery** in India and **no famine** — historians say both statements are inaccurate (the Arthashastra mentions slaves, *dasas*).

> **Bihar connection:** Mauryan remains in Patna:
> - **Kumrahar** — remains of an **80-pillared hall** (polished sandstone pillars), excavated by **D.B. Spooner (1912–13)** and later by the K.P. Jayaswal Research Institute (1951–55). Often linked to Ashoka's palace / Third Council.
> - **Bulandi Bagh** — wooden palisade (city wall) described by Megasthenes.
> - **Didarganj Yakshi** — polished sandstone female **chauri (fly-whisk) bearer**, found in **1917** at Didarganj on the Ganga bank; now in the **Bihar Museum**, Patna. Usually dated to the Mauryan period (a few scholars place it later).

### Central government

- The **King** had supreme power (Arthashastra calls the king the centre of the **saptanga** — seven limbs of the state: *swami* (king), *amatya* (ministers), *janapada* (territory and people), *durga* (fort), *kosha* (treasury), *danda* (army), *mitra* (ally)).
- **Mantrins** (small inner council) and a larger **Mantriparishad** (council of ministers).
- **18 Tirthas** (highest officials) — important ones:

| Official | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Samaharta** | Chief collector of revenue |
| **Sannidhata** | Treasurer (chief of the treasury and stores) |
| **Senapati** | Commander-in-chief |
| **Purohita** | Chief priest |
| **Yuvaraja** | Crown prince |
| **Dauvarika** | Chief of the palace guards / chamberlain |
| **Pradeshta** | Head of criminal justice / commissioner |
| **Vyavaharika** | Head of civil justice |
| **Nayaka** | City/army chief |
| **Antapala** | Guard of the frontier |
| **Durgapala** | Guard of forts |
| **Paura** | City governor |

- **Adhyakshas** (superintendents) — the Arthashastra lists about 27:

| Adhyaksha | Department |
|---|---|
| **Sitadhyaksha** | Crown (state) agricultural land |
| **Panyadhyaksha** | Commerce / state trading |
| **Akaradhyaksha** | Mines |
| **Lohadhyaksha** | Iron |
| **Lakshanadhyaksha** | Mint |
| **Pautavadhyaksha** | Weights and measures |
| **Shulkadhyaksha** | Tolls and customs |
| **Suradhyaksha** | Excise (liquor) |
| **Sutradhyaksha** | Textiles (spinning and weaving) |
| **Navadhyaksha** | Ships / waterways |
| **Ganikadhyaksha** | Courtesans |
| **Mudradhyaksha** | Passports |
| **Sunadhyaksha** | Slaughterhouses |

### Provinces

| Province | Capital |
|---|---|
| **Uttarapatha** (north) | **Taxila** |
| **Avantiratha** (west) | **Ujjayini** |
| **Dakshinapatha** (south) | **Suvarnagiri** |
| **Kalinga** (east) | **Tosali** |
| **Prachya / Prasii** (centre, ruled directly) | **Pataliputra** |

Provinces were governed by **Kumaras/Aryaputras** (princes). Below them: districts (*ahara/vishaya*) with **Pradeshika** (district head), **Rajuka** (land measurement and later judicial powers), **Yukta** (accounts and secretarial work); villages under the **Gramika** (village headman).

### Spies, justice, army, revenue

- **Spies (gudhapurusha)**: **Samsthana** (stationary) and **Sanchara** (wandering); women spies too.
- **Courts**: **Dharmasthiya** (civil) and **Kantakashodhana** ("removal of thorns" — criminal). Punishments were harsh.
- **Army**: huge — Pliny gives 6,00,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry and 9,000 elephants (Greek estimate); the **six-wing** army including a navy.
- **Revenue**: **Bhaga** — land tax, usually **one-sixth** of produce (could rise to one-fourth); **Bali** (extra levy); **Pindakara** (lump sum from villages); **Hiranya** (tax in cash); **Udakabhaga** (water tax — one-fifth to one-third for irrigation); **Sita** (income from crown land); tolls and customs. **Vishti** = forced labour.
- **Coins**: **Pana** (silver), **Mashaka** (copper), **Kakani** (small copper).

### Economy

- **Agriculture**: state farms (*sita* land) worked by slaves and labourers; Shudras settled on new land (*janapada nivesha*).
- **Irrigation**: Sudarshana lake (Girnar).
- **Crafts and guilds (shreni)**: textiles (Kashi, Vanga, Madurai), metal work, mining were state-controlled.
- **Trade routes**: the **Uttarapatha** (royal road) from **Taxila to Pataliputra** and on to Tamralipti (port in Bengal) — a forerunner of the **Grand Trunk Road**.

## Mauryan art and architecture

Mauryan art has two streams: **court art** (pillars, palaces, caves — sponsored by the king) and **popular art** (yaksha-yakshi figures, terracotta).

| Monument | Place | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillars** | Many sites | **Monolithic** (single stone), **Chunar sandstone** (near Varanasi), with the famous **Mauryan polish**; capital with an **inverted lotus (bell)**, abacus, and animal |
| **Sarnath Lion Capital** | Sarnath (UP) | **Four lions** back to back; abacus with **wheel (24 spokes), elephant, bull, horse, lion**; adopted as **India's National Emblem on 26 January 1950** with the motto *Satyameva Jayate* |
| **Rampurva** | West Champaran, **Bihar** | **Two pillars** — **bull capital** (now at **Rashtrapati Bhavan**, New Delhi) and **lion capital** (Indian Museum, Kolkata) |
| **Lauriya-Nandangarh** | West Champaran, **Bihar** | Pillar with **lion capital still in place**; inscribed with pillar edicts |
| **Lauriya-Araraj** | East Champaran, **Bihar** | Inscribed pillar **without capital** |
| **Vaishali (Kolhua)** | Vaishali, **Bihar** | Thick pillar with a **single lion** facing north; **no edict** |
| **Sanchi stupa** | Madhya Pradesh | Originally a brick stupa built by **Ashoka**; enlarged later by Shungas and Satavahanas |
| **Barabar caves** (Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Karna Chaupar, Vishwa Zopri) | **Jehanabad, Bihar** | **Oldest rock-cut caves** in India; mirror-polished inner walls; **Lomas Rishi** has a carved gateway imitating wooden architecture |
| **Nagarjuni caves** | Jehanabad, Bihar | Given by **Dasharatha** to Ajivikas |
| **Dhauli elephant** | Odisha | Rock-cut elephant near the edicts |
| **Didarganj Yakshi** | Patna, **Bihar** | Polished sandstone chauri-bearer |
| **Parkham Yaksha** | Mathura (UP) | Large popular-art figure |

> **Prelims angle:** "Bull capital at Rashtrapati Bhavan" → **Rampurva (Bihar)**. "Oldest rock-cut caves" → **Barabar (Bihar)**. "Pillar with all seven pillar edicts" → **Delhi–Topra**. "Minor rock edict in Bihar" → **Sasaram (Rohtas)**.

## Decline of the Mauryan Empire

| Theory | Historian | Argument |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahmanical reaction** | **H.P. Shastri** | Ashoka's ban on sacrifices and support of Buddhism angered Brahmins; Pushyamitra (a Brahmin) overthrew the dynasty |
| **Ashoka's pacifism** | **H.C. Raychaudhuri** | Policy of non-violence weakened the army |
| **Financial crisis** | **D.D. Kosambi** | Huge army and bureaucracy drained the treasury; debasement of coins |
| **Over-centralisation** | **Romila Thapar** | Loyalty to the king, not the state; weak successors could not hold the system |
| Others | — | Weak later kings, partition of the empire, provincial revolts, neglect of the north-west frontier → **Bactrian Greek** invasions |

> **Mains angle:** Present the decline as **multi-causal** and mention that Raychaudhuri's view is disputed because Ashoka never abolished the army or capital punishment. The Arthashastra-style centralised state could not survive weak rulers.

### Mains questions

**1. Describe the main features of Mauryan administration. How far was it a centralised system?** (300 words)

- *Intro:* First pan-Indian empire; sources Arthashastra, Indica, edicts.
- King at the centre; saptanga theory; mantriparishad, 18 tirthas.
- Departments under adhyakshas: mines, mint, trade, agriculture, excise.
- Provinces under princes (Taxila, Ujjain, Suvarnagiri, Tosali); districts with pradeshika, rajuka, yukta.
- Pataliputra's 6 committees of 5; army of 6 wings.
- Spy network, two kinds of courts, revenue system (bhaga 1/6, sita land).
- Limits to centralisation: provinces had autonomy; local customs; Ashoka's rajukas got judicial powers; distance and communication.
- *Conclusion:* Highly centralised by ancient standards; model for later empires; its weakness appeared under weak kings.

**2. "Ashoka's Dhamma was a code of conduct rather than a religion." Discuss.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Kalinga war (MRE XIII) as turning point.
- Contents of Dhamma: tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders, kindness to slaves, welfare.
- Not Buddhist doctrine — no Four Noble Truths or nirvana in the edicts.
- Purpose: unity of a diverse empire, reduce sectarian conflict (Thapar).
- Means: edicts in local scripts, Dhamma Mahamattas, yatras, welfare works.
- Personal Buddhism separate: Bhabru edict, Third Council, missions.
- Impact and criticism.
- *Conclusion:* A secular, ethical state policy — echoed in India's emblem and constitutional values.

**3. Discuss the contribution of Pataliputra and Bihar to Mauryan history and art.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Capital of the empire for ~140 years.
- Megasthenes' Palibothra: walls, 570 towers, 64 gates, municipal committees.
- Remains: Kumrahar hall, Bulandi Bagh palisade.
- Art: Didarganj Yakshi, Rampurva bull and lion, Lauriya-Nandangarh lion, Kolhua lion pillar.
- Barabar and Nagarjuni caves — oldest rock-cut caves; Sasaram edict.
- Third Buddhist Council at Pataliputra; missions abroad.
- *Conclusion:* Bihar was the political and cultural centre of India's first empire.
