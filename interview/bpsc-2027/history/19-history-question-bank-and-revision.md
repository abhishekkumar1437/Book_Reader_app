Use this chapter after you finish the history book, and again in the Prelims revision rounds (Phase 4) and the Mains sprint (Phase 5). Cover the answer, say it aloud, then check. Mark the ones you miss and redo them on your next revision day. Themes marked ⭐ are the ones BPSC returns to most often (in general terms, from the pattern of past papers — not a statistic). All questions are recall or descriptive. There are no MCQs here; for MCQ practice use the Quiz tab.

> **In simple words:** Questions 1–100 test facts. Mains questions 1–15 test whether you can build an answer. The last-week box at the end is what you read on the final days before Prelims.

## How to use this bank

- **First round (end of the history block in Phase 1):** attempt all 100. Aim for 70+ right.
- **Revision days:** redo only the questions you got wrong.
- **Phase 4:** use the ⭐ questions as a daily 10-minute warm-up.
- **Phase 3 and 5 (Mains):** write two Mains outlines a week from the list below in about 12 minutes each; write one in full (about 300 words) in 20 minutes.
- For each wrong answer, go back to the chapter: links are given at the top of each section.

9. Kundagrama near Vaishali; Pawapuri (Nalanda district).
10. Chandragupta Maurya; Chanakya (Kautilya).
11. Megasthenes, ambassador of Seleucus at the court of Chandragupta Maurya.

17. Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang).
18. Dharmapala; Antichak, Bhagalpur district.

## Mains questions with model outlines

Write in this pattern: **Intro (2–3 lines) → 5–8 body points with facts, dates, Bihar examples → Conclusion (way forward or significance).** For more full answers see [Modern History and Culture Model Answers](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/modern-history-and-culture-model-answers).

**1. ⭐ Discuss the role of Bihar in the Indian freedom struggle from 1857 to 1947. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar was a front-line province at every stage — from Jagdishpur to the Patna Secretariat.
- **1857:** Kunwar Singh; Danapur; Arrah; Peer Ali; Chotanagpur leaders.
- **Tribal and peasant resistance:** Santhal Hul; Birsa; Tana Bhagats; indigo struggles.
- **Early nationalism:** separation movement (1912); Bankipore session; Home Rule (Mazharul Haque).
- **Gandhian phase:** Champaran (1917); NCM — Sadaqat Ashram, Bihar Vidyapith; CDM — salt, no-chowkidari tax, Tarapur (1932).
- **Kisan and socialist streams:** Sahajanand's Kisan Sabha; CSP born in Patna (1934); JP.
- **Quit India:** Patna Secretariat martyrs; mass uprising; JP's escape and Azad Dasta.
- **Constitution-making:** Sachchidananda Sinha and Rajendra Prasad.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar gave the movement its first satyagraha, its peasant voice and its socialist conscience.

**2. ⭐ "The Champaran Satyagraha marked the beginning of a new phase in the national movement." Examine. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** 1917 — first satyagraha in India.
- Background: tinkathia, sharahbeshi, tawan.
- Course: Shukla; Motihari; Section 144 defied; inquiry.
- Result: Sly Committee; Agrarian Act 1918.
- New features: mass participation of peasants; non-violence; truth-based inquiry; constructive work.
- Leaders made: Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Kripalani.
- **Conclusion:** the template for Kheda, Non-Cooperation and later movements.

**3. ⭐ Assess the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** "Do or Die" and the leaderless rising.
- Patna Secretariat (11 Aug 1942); seven martyrs; Shaheed Smarak.
- District risings; railways and telegraph cut; British authority collapsed in parts of north Bihar.
- JP's escape (9 Nov 1942); Azad Dasta.
- Women and students (Tara Rani Srivastava).
- Repression — firing, aerial attacks, collective fines.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar made 1942 a people's revolution.

**4. Evaluate the contribution of Veer Kunwar Singh to the Revolt of 1857. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** An 80-year-old zamindar of Jagdishpur.
- Danapur sepoys; Arrah siege; defeat of Dunbar.
- Guerrilla campaign — Rewa, Kalpi, Lucknow, Azamgarh.
- Ganga crossing; sacrifice of his arm.
- Victory at Jagdishpur (23 April 1858); death (26 April).
- Hindu–Muslim unity; legacy — Vijayotsav.
- **Conclusion:** a symbol of courage and of Bihar's early anti-colonial spirit.

**5. ⭐ Discuss the causes and consequences of the tribal revolts of the Chotanagpur and Santhal Parganas regions. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** forests and dikus.
- Causes: land laws, mahajans, forest laws, begar, missionaries.
- Revolts: Tilka Manjhi, Kol, Bhumij, Santhal Hul, Sardari Larai, Ulgulan, Tana Bhagat.
- Nature: messianic leadership, local, violent then Gandhian.
- Consequences: SWFA (1834), Santhal Parganas (1855), CNT Act (1908); identity politics; Jharkhand movement.
- **Conclusion:** first fights for land and dignity.

**6. Examine the role of Swami Sahajanand Saraswati in the peasant movement. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** from sanyasi to "Kisan Pran".
- BPKS (1929), AIKS (1936).
- Kisan Manifesto; zamindari abolition demand.
- Bakasht struggle.
- Relations with Congress; Ramgarh 1940.
- **Conclusion:** led to zamindari abolition (1950).

**7. ⭐ Trace the movement for the separation of Bihar from Bengal. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar under Bengal Presidency until 1912.
- Grievances — jobs, education, identity.
- Press — *Bihar Bandhu*, *Behar Times*.
- Leaders — Sachchidananda Sinha, Mahesh Narayan, Ali Imam, Hasan Imam, Mazharul Haque.
- Bihar Provincial Conference (1908).
- Delhi Durbar (1911); province 22 March 1912.
- **Conclusion:** birth of modern Bihar's identity and institutions (Patna HC, Patna University).

**8. "The economic critique of colonialism was the greatest contribution of the Moderates." Discuss. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** Naoroji, R.C. Dutt, Ranade, Gokhale.
- Drain theory — Home Charges, profits, interest.
- De-industrialisation; land revenue; famines.
- Impact — exposed the "benevolent" image of the Raj.
- Other contributions — councils reform, public opinion.
- **Conclusion:** the intellectual foundation of later mass nationalism.

**9. Critically examine the Permanent Settlement and its long-term effects on Bihar. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Cornwallis, 1793.
- Features — fixed revenue, zamindar ownership, Sunset Law.
- Effects on zamindars, peasants, agriculture.
- Bihar estates; indigo; peasant movements.
- Zamindari abolition 1950; continuing land inequality.
- **Conclusion:** colonial safety at the cost of agrarian justice.

**10. ⭐ Evaluate the contribution of Dr Rajendra Prasad to the national movement and nation-building. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** Ziradei to Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- Champaran; NCM; No-changer.
- Earthquake relief 1934; Congress President three times.
- Constituent Assembly President; first President of India.
- Writings — *India Divided*.
- **Conclusion:** moral leadership and consensus-building.

**11. Discuss the significance of the Non-Cooperation Movement with special reference to Bihar. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** first all-India mass movement.
- National programme — boycott and construction.
- Bihar — Sadaqat Ashram, Bihar Vidyapith, leaders' sacrifices, Tana Bhagats.
- Chauri Chaura; withdrawal.
- **Conclusion:** made Congress a mass party.

**12. ⭐ "Ashoka's Dhamma was a code of social ethics, not a religion." Discuss. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** after the Kalinga war (MRE XIII).
- Contents — tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders, care for animals, welfare works.
- Means — edicts, Dhamma Mahamattas, tours (dhamma yatras).
- Not sectarian — respect for Brahmins, Shramanas, Ajivikas (Barabar caves gift).
- Political purpose — unity of a diverse empire.
- **Conclusion:** an early model of welfare state ethics; legacy in national symbols.

**13. Describe the importance of Nalanda and Vikramshila as centres of learning. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** Bihar's ancient universities.
- Nalanda — Kumaragupta I; Hiuen Tsang; subjects; library; UNESCO 2016; new Nalanda University (2010 Act).
- Vikramshila — Dharmapala; Tantric Buddhism; Atisha to Tibet.
- Decline — Turkish invasions (c. 1200).
- **Conclusion:** Bihar as Asia's knowledge centre; relevance for today's education.

**14. ⭐ Examine the administrative and economic reforms of Sher Shah Suri. (20 marks)**

- **Intro:** a Bihar-born ruler (Sasaram), ruled 1540–45.
- Revenue — land measurement, patta–qabuliyat, crop rates.
- Currency — silver rupiya.
- Roads and sarais — Grand Trunk Road; dak chowki.
- Military — dagh and chehra; justice and police.
- **Conclusion:** forerunner of Akbar's administration.

**15. ⭐ Describe Bihar's contribution to Indian art and literature. (38 marks)**

- **Intro:** classical and folk traditions.
- Mauryan art — pillars, Didarganj Yakshi, Barabar caves.
- Pala sculpture and painting.
- Sher Shah's tomb; Patna Sahib; Golghar; Patna Kalam.
- Folk — Madhubani, Manjusha, Tikuli, Sikki; Bidesiya.
- Literature — Vidyapati, Dinkar, Renu, Nagarjuna, Bhikhari Thakur.
- Music — Bismillah Khan, dhrupad gharanas, Sharda Sinha.
- **Conclusion:** a living heritage — UNESCO sites and GI tags.
