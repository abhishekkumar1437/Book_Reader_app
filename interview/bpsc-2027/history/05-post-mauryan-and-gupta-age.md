Between the fall of the Mauryas (185 BCE) and the end of the Guptas (c. 550 CE), India saw foreign rulers in the north-west, strong regional kingdoms in the Deccan and the Tamil south, and then a second great empire from Magadha — the **Guptas**. BPSC Prelims asks about eras (Vikram, Shaka, Gupta), inscriptions (Allahabad Prashasti, Junagadh, Nasik, Hathigumpha), schools of art (Gandhara, Mathura, Amaravati), Gupta writers and scientists, and Fa-Hien. Bihar links: **Pataliputra** (Gupta capital), **Aryabhata of Kusumapura**, the **Sultanganj Buddha** and **Nalanda**.

> **In simple words:** After the Mauryas, the north had the Shungas (Indian) and a series of foreign rulers — Greeks, Shakas, Parthians and Kushanas — who mixed into Indian society. The Deccan was ruled by the Satavahanas and the Tamil south by the Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas, whose poems form the Sangam literature. Around 320 CE the Guptas rose from Magadha again and created an age famous for Sanskrit literature, science, temples and the gold coin — often called the "Golden Age", though historians debate that label.

## Indian dynasties after the Mauryas

### Shungas (c. 185–73 BCE)

| Ruler | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Pushyamitra Shunga** | Brahmin commander of the last Maurya, **Brihadratha**; killed him c. 185 BCE. Capital **Pataliputra** (also Vidisha). Performed **two Ashvamedha** sacrifices (Ayodhya inscription of Dhanadeva). Grammarian **Patanjali** (*Mahabhashya*) is said to have been his priest. Fought the **Indo-Greeks (Yavanas)**. Buddhist texts call him a persecutor of Buddhism (disputed — the **Bharhut** and **Sanchi** stupas were expanded in his era) |
| **Agnimitra** | Son of Pushyamitra; hero of **Kalidasa's** play ***Malavikagnimitram*** |
| **Bhagabhadra** | **Heliodorus**, Greek ambassador of Indo-Greek king **Antialcidas** (of Taxila), set up the **Garuda pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha, MP)** and called himself a **Bhagavata** (devotee of Vasudeva/Vishnu) — early evidence of a foreigner adopting Indian religion |
| **Devabhuti** | Last Shunga; killed by his minister **Vasudeva Kanva** |

### Kanvas (c. 73–28 BCE)

Founded by **Vasudeva Kanva**; four rulers; ended by the Satavahanas.

### Kharavela of Kalinga (1st century BCE)

- King of the **Chedi (Mahameghavahana)** dynasty of Kalinga (Odisha).
- Known from the **Hathigumpha inscription** at **Udayagiri hills** near Bhubaneswar (in Prakrit, Brahmi).
- A **Jain**; invaded **Magadha** and brought back a **Jina image** that "a Nanda king" had taken from Kalinga; the inscription also mentions a canal dug by a Nanda king.
- Built the **Udayagiri–Khandagiri caves** for Jain monks.

### Satavahanas (Andhras) (c. 1st century BCE – 3rd century CE)

| Ruler | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Simuka** | Founder |
| **Satakarni I** | Performed **two Ashvamedhas**; **Nanaghat inscription** of his queen **Naganika** |
| **Hala** | Poet-king; compiled ***Gatha Saptashati*** (Prakrit love poems) |
| **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (c. 106–130 CE) | Greatest ruler; destroyed the **Shaka Kshaharata** ruler **Nahapana**; called "destroyer of **Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas**"; his mother **Gautami Balashri's Nasik inscription** records his deeds |
| **Vashishthiputra Pulumayi** | Successor; inscriptions at Amaravati |
| **Yajna Sri Satakarni** | Coins with **ship** motif — sea trade |

Key facts: capital **Pratishthana (Paithan, Maharashtra)**, later **Dhanyakataka/Amaravati** (Andhra). Kings used their **mothers' names** (matronymics — Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra) though the family was patrilineal. They were the **first to give tax-free land grants** to Brahmins and Buddhist monks. Coins of **lead and potin**. Language **Prakrit**. Monuments: **Karle chaitya**, **Nasik caves**, **Amaravati stupa**, **Nagarjunakonda**.

## Foreign rulers in the north-west

| Group | Main rulers | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks)** | Demetrius, **Menander (Milinda)** | Menander's capital **Sakala (Sialkot)**; converted to Buddhism by **Nagasena** (*Milindapanho*). First to issue **coins with the ruler's portrait and name** in India; brought Greek astronomy (*Romaka Siddhanta*, the word *hora*), the theatre curtain (**yavanika**), and started the **Gandhara** art style |
| **Shakas (Scythians)** | **Nahapana** (Kshaharata, western Deccan), **Chashtana** and **Rudradaman I** (Kardamaka, Ujjain) | Five branches (Taxila, Mathura, Nasik, Ujjain, and north). **Rudradaman I's Junagadh inscription (150 CE)** — the **first long inscription in classical Sanskrit**; he **repaired the Sudarshana lake** |
| **Parthians (Pahlavas)** | **Gondophernes** | **Takht-i-Bahi** inscription; Christian tradition says **St Thomas** came to his court |
| **Kushanas** (a branch of the **Yuezhi** of Central Asia) | Kujula Kadphises, **Vima Kadphises**, **Kanishka** | See below |

### The Kushanas and Kanishka

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Kujula Kadphises | Founder; copper coins |
| **Vima Kadphises** | Issued **gold coins on a large scale**; devotee of **Shiva** |
| **Kanishka** | Accession in **78 CE** — beginning of the **Shaka era** (most accepted view). Capitals **Purushapura (Peshawar)** and **Mathura**. Empire from Central Asia to the Ganga (Varanasi). Titles **Devaputra** (son of god), **Kaisara** (Caesar). Patron of **Mahayana Buddhism**; held the **Fourth Buddhist Council in Kashmir**. Greek engineer **Agesilaus** built a great stupa at Peshawar. **Rabatak inscription** (Afghanistan, found 1993) gives the family tree |
| Kanishka's court | **Ashvaghosha** (*Buddhacharita*, *Saundarananda*), **Nagarjuna** (Madhyamika philosophy, *Prajnaparamita*), **Vasumitra**, **Parshva**, **Charaka** (physician — ***Charaka Samhita***) |
| Last important Kushana | Vasudeva I |
| Trade | Controlled part of the **Silk Route**; trade with Rome and China |

### Schools of sculpture

| Feature | **Gandhara** | **Mathura** | **Amaravati** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region | North-west (Peshawar, Taxila, Afghanistan) | Mathura (UP) | Krishna–Godavari delta (Andhra) |
| Patrons | **Kushanas** (also Indo-Greeks) | **Kushanas** | **Satavahanas** (later Ikshvakus) |
| Material | **Blue-grey schist**, stucco | **Spotted red sandstone** | **White marble** |
| Influence | **Greek–Roman (Hellenistic)** | **Indigenous** | Indigenous; narrative |
| Subjects | Mainly **Buddhist** | **Buddhist, Jain and Hindu** | Mainly Buddhist (life stories, Jatakas) |
| Buddha's look | Wavy hair, moustache sometimes, heavy folded robes, calm "spiritual" face | Shaven or spiral-curled head, smiling face, muscular, light robe | Shown in narrative scenes, slender figures in motion |

(For a full comparison of art styles across periods, see [Indian Art, Architecture and Literature](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/indian-art-architecture-and-literature).)

> **Remember:** The **first images of the Buddha** in human form were made in the **Kushana period**, in **both** Gandhara and Mathura. Earlier (Mauryan–Shunga) art showed the Buddha only through **symbols** (footprints, wheel, tree, stupa).

## The Sangam Age (brief)

"Sangam" means an **assembly of Tamil poets**, said to be held at **Madurai** under **Pandya** patronage. The surviving literature is dated roughly **3rd century BCE – 3rd century CE**.

| Sangam | Place | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First | Ten Madurai (submerged) | Presided by **Agastya**; no works survive |
| Second | **Kapatapuram** | **Tolkappiyam** (Tamil grammar by **Tolkappiyar**) survives |
| Third | **Madurai** | Presided by **Nakkirar**; most surviving poems |

| Kingdom | Capital / port | Emblem | Famous ruler |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cheras** | Vanji (Karur); port **Muziris** | **Bow** | **Senguttuvan** — started the **Pattini (Kannagi) cult** |
| **Cholas** | **Uraiyur**; port **Puhar (Kaveripattinam)** | **Tiger** | **Karikala** — built the **Kallanai (Grand Anicut)** on the **Kaveri** |
| **Pandyas** | **Madurai**; port Korkai | **Fish** | **Nedunjeliyan** |

**Texts:** *Tolkappiyam*; **Ettuthogai** (8 anthologies) and **Pattuppattu** (10 idylls); **Tirukkural** by **Tiruvalluvar** ("Tamil Veda", on ethics); the twin epics **Silappadikaram** (by **Ilango Adigal** — story of **Kannagi and Kovalan**) and **Manimekalai** (by **Sittalai Sattanar** — Buddhist). Roman trade: **Arikamedu** (near Puducherry), Muziris — Roman gold coins and pottery.

## The Gupta Empire (c. 319–550 CE)

### Origin

The Guptas probably began in **Magadha / eastern UP**. Founder **Sri Gupta** (c. 240–280 CE), then **Ghatotkacha**. The real founder of the empire was **Chandragupta I**.

### The rulers

| Ruler | Dates (approx.) | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Chandragupta I** | 319/320–335 CE | Title **Maharajadhiraja**; married **Licchavi** princess **Kumaradevi** — the **"Chandragupta–Kumaradevi" gold coins** show the marriage; started the **Gupta era (319–20 CE)** |
| **Samudragupta** | 335–375 CE | Known from the **Allahabad (Prayag) Prashasti**, written in Sanskrit by his court poet **Harishena** on an **Ashokan pillar**. Called the **"Napoleon of India" by V.A. Smith**. Coins show him **playing the veena**; title **Kaviraja** (king of poets). Performed **Ashvamedha** (Ashvamedha coins). The king of **Sri Lanka, Meghavarna**, sent an embassy for permission to build a **monastery at Bodh Gaya** |
| (Ramagupta) | — | Elder brother, disputed; known from Vishakhadatta's play *Devichandraguptam* |
| **Chandragupta II "Vikramaditya"** | c. 375/380–413/415 CE | **Defeated the Shakas** of western India (Rudrasimha III) — title **Shakari**; issued **silver coins** in the west; made **Ujjain** a second capital. **Marriage alliances**: married **Kuberanaga** (Naga princess); daughter **Prabhavatigupta** married the **Vakataka** king **Rudrasena II**. The **Mehrauli iron pillar** (Delhi) of "king Chandra" is linked to him. **Fa-Hien** visited during his reign. Tradition of **Navaratnas** (nine gems) |
| **Kumaragupta I** | 415–455 CE | Title **Mahendraditya**; **founded Nalanda Mahavihara** (Hiuen Tsang names its founder **Shakraditya**, identified with Kumaragupta I); **Kartikeya (peacock) coins**; first **Hun** pressure and **Pushyamitra** tribe revolt at the end |
| **Skandagupta** | 455–467 CE | **Repulsed the Huns** — **Bhitari pillar inscription** (Ghazipur, UP); his **Junagadh inscription** records **repair of the Sudarshana lake** by governor **Parnadatta** (and his son Chakrapalita); title **Vikramaditya** |
| Later Guptas | 467–c. 550 CE | **Budhagupta**; **Narasimhagupta Baladitya** (built at Nalanda; Hiuen Tsang says he defeated the Hun **Mihirakula**); **Bhanugupta** (Eran inscription, **510 CE** — **first epigraphic evidence of sati**); **Vishnugupta** — last |

#### Samudragupta's conquests (Allahabad Prashasti)

| Region | Policy |
|---|---|
| **Aryavarta** (north India) — 9 kings | **Prasabhoddharana** — uprooted and **annexed** |
| **Dakshinapatha** (south) — 12 kings incl. **Vishnugopa of Kanchi** | **Grahana–Moksha–Anugraha** — captured, then **freed and reinstated** as tributaries |
| **Atavika** (forest kingdoms of central India) | Made servants |
| **Pratyanta** (frontier kingdoms) — Samatata, Davaka, Kamarupa, Nepal, Kartripura | Paid tribute |
| **Republics** — Malavas, Yaudheyas, Arjunayanas, Abhiras, Madrakas | Paid tribute, accepted overlordship |
| **Foreign** — Shakas, "Daivaputra Shahi Shahanushahi" (Kushanas), Simhala (Sri Lanka) | Sent gifts, sought friendship |

> **Prelims angle:** "Who wrote the Allahabad Prashasti?" → **Harishena** (he was Samudragupta's *sandhivigrahika*, minister of peace and war). "Napoleon of India?" → **Samudragupta** (V.A. Smith). "Licchavi queen on Gupta coins?" → **Kumaradevi**. "First inscriptional evidence of sati?" → **Eran, 510 CE**.

> **Common mistake:** The Allahabad pillar has **three** layers: **Ashoka's** edicts, **Samudragupta's** Prashasti, and **Jahangir's** inscription. It was originally at **Kaushambi**.

### The Navaratnas (traditional)

Tradition places nine scholars at "Vikramaditya's" court: **Kalidasa** (poet), **Amarasimha** (*Amarakosha*, a Sanskrit dictionary), **Varahamihira** (astronomer), **Dhanvantari** (physician), **Vararuchi** (grammarian), **Vetala Bhatta**, **Ghatakarpara**, **Kshapanaka**, **Shanku**.

> **Remember:** Historians doubt this list — **Varahamihira lived in the 6th century**, after Chandragupta II. For Prelims, accept the traditional list; for Mains, mention it is a later tradition.

### Fa-Hien (Faxian)

- Chinese Buddhist monk; travelled **399–414 CE** (in India c. 401–411) during **Chandragupta II's** reign.
- Came by land, returned by sea via Sri Lanka; wrote ***Fo-Kuo-Ki*** ("Record of the Buddhist Kingdoms").
- Stayed about three years in **Pataliputra** learning Sanskrit; admired **Ashoka's palace** ("built by spirits") and noted **free hospitals** run by rich families.
- Noted: mild government, **no capital punishment** (fines instead), people did not eat meat, onions or garlic, did not drink wine; **Chandalas** (untouchables) lived outside towns and struck a piece of wood to warn others of their approach.
- He **did not name the king**.

### Gupta administration

Compared with the Mauryas, the Gupta system was **less centralised** — more power to local officials, feudatories and land-grant holders.

| Level | Unit | Officer |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | Kingdom | King — titles **Maharajadhiraja, Paramabhattaraka, Parameshvara**; divine status ("equal to gods") |
| Province | **Bhukti** | **Uparika** |
| District | **Vishaya** | **Vishayapati** |
| Sub-district | Vithi / Peth | — |
| Village | **Grama** | **Gramika / Gramadhyaksha** with village elders (*mahattaras*) |

| Central official | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Kumaramatya** | Senior officer cadre, often provincial heads |
| **Sandhivigrahika** | Minister of **peace and war** (Harishena) |
| **Mahabaladhikrita** | Commander-in-chief |
| **Mahadandanayaka** | Chief judicial / military officer |
| **Dandapashika** | Police chief |
| **Bhandagaradhikrita** | Treasury |
| **Mahapratihara** | Chief of the palace |

- **Town councils** (adhishthana-adhikarana) included the chief banker (*nagarashreshthi*), chief merchant (*sarthavaha*), chief artisan (*prathama kulika*) and chief scribe (*prathama kayastha*) — known from **Damodarpur** copper plates (Bengal).
- **Land revenue**: one-sixth to one-fourth (*bhaga*); other taxes *bhoga*, *kara*, *uparikara*, *udranga*; **vishti** (forced labour) common.
- **Land grants (agrahara)** to Brahmins and temples, often tax-free with administrative rights — historians like **R.S. Sharma** see this as the start of **Indian feudalism**.
- Land types: **kshetra** (cultivated), **khila** (waste), **aprahata** (jungle), **vasti** (habitable), **gopata sarah** (pasture).
- **Coins**: the Guptas issued the **largest number of gold coins** (**dinara**); silver **rupaka** (in the west after the Shaka conquest); copper coins rare.

### Science and mathematics

| Scholar | Work | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Aryabhata** (b. **476 CE**) | ***Aryabhatiya*** (499 CE) | Lived and worked at **Kusumapura (Pataliputra)**; said the **Earth rotates on its axis**; explained **eclipses** by shadows (not Rahu); value of **π ≈ 3.1416**; place-value system; trigonometry (sine tables). India's **first satellite (1975)** was named after him |
| **Varahamihira** | ***Pancha Siddhantika***, ***Brihat Samhita*** (encyclopaedia), *Brihat Jataka* | Astronomy and astrology at **Ujjain** (6th century) |
| **Brahmagupta** | *Brahmasphuta Siddhanta* (628 CE) | Rules of operating with **zero**; gravity idea (post-Gupta) |
| **Sushruta** | *Sushruta Samhita* | **Surgery** — "father of surgery"; plastic surgery (rhinoplasty) — date debated |
| **Charaka** | *Charaka Samhita* | Medicine (Kushana period) |
| **Vagbhata** | *Ashtanga Hridaya* | Ayurveda (c. 7th century) |
| **Palakapya** | *Hastyayurveda* | Veterinary medicine for elephants |
| Metallurgy | **Mehrauli iron pillar** | Has not rusted in about 1,600 years |

> **Bihar connection:** **Aryabhata** worked at **Kusumapura (Pataliputra)**; the **Aryabhatiya** says so. Bihar honours him through **Aryabhatta Knowledge University** (Patna) and the tradition of an observatory at **Khagaul** near Patna. The **Sultanganj Buddha** — a **copper statue about 2.3 m tall** from the Gupta–post-Gupta period, found in **1861–62** at **Sultanganj (Bhagalpur district)** during railway work — is now in the **Birmingham Museum, UK**. The Gupta capital was **Pataliputra**, and **Nalanda** was founded in this age.

### Art and architecture

**Temples** — the Gupta age began the **structural Hindu temple**: a square **garbhagriha** (sanctum) with a porch, later a **shikhara** (tower).

| Temple | Place | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dashavatara temple, Deogarh** | Lalitpur, UP | **Earliest temple with a shikhara**; *panchayatana* plan; Vishnu reliefs |
| **Bhitargaon temple** | Kanpur, UP | **Brick** temple with shikhara |
| **Temple No. 17, Sanchi** | MP | Flat-roofed, early stage |
| Tigawa (Kankali Devi), Bhumara (Shiva), Nachna Kuthara (Parvati) | MP | Early stone temples |

**Caves and painting:** **Ajanta** (Maharashtra) — most paintings belong to the Gupta–**Vakataka** period (caves 16, 17, 1, 2; Vakataka king **Harishena**'s minister Varahadeva patronised cave 16); **Bagh caves** (MP); **Udayagiri caves** (near Vidisha, MP) — the giant **Varaha** (boar avatar) relief.

**Sculpture:** the **Sarnath school** (Buddha images in Chunar sandstone, transparent drapery, calm face), Mathura school; the **Sultanganj Buddha**.

### Nalanda Mahavihara

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Founded | **5th century CE** by **Kumaragupta I** (Shakraditya) |
| Location | **Nalanda district, Bihar** (near Rajgir; Bargaon village) |
| Patrons | Guptas, **Harshavardhana**, **Palas** (Dharmapala, Devapala), Sailendra king **Balaputradeva** of Java/Sumatra (Nalanda copper plate of Devapala) |
| Famous scholars | **Shilabhadra** (head, teacher of Hiuen Tsang), **Dharmapala**, **Nagarjuna** (traditionally linked), **Aryadeva**, **Shantarakshita** |
| Famous students / visitors | **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)** — studied here (7th century); **I-Tsing (Yijing)** — stayed about 10 years (late 7th century) |
| Library | **Dharmaganja**, with three buildings: **Ratnasagara, Ratnodadhi, Ratnaranjaka** |
| Admission | Through a tough oral test by gatekeeper scholars (*dvarapandita*) |
| Destroyed | By **Bakhtiyar Khalji**, c. **1193** (some date it c. 1200–1203) |
| UNESCO | "Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara" — **World Heritage Site in 2016** |
| Modern revival | **Nalanda University** (Act of Parliament 2010; classes from 2014; new campus at Rajgir inaugurated in **June 2024**) |

> **Mains angle:** "Was the Gupta age a Golden Age?" — **For:** political unity, gold coins, Sanskrit classics (Kalidasa), science (Aryabhata), temples, Ajanta, Nalanda, Puranic Hinduism. **Against (R.S. Sharma, D.N. Jha):** decline of long-distance trade and towns, land grants and **feudalism**, rigid caste, **sati** (Eran 510 CE), lower status of women, rise of untouchability (Fa-Hien's Chandalas), forced labour. **Conclusion:** a "classical age" for elite culture rather than a golden age for all.

### Decline of the Guptas

- **Hun invasions** — **Toramana** and **Mihirakula** (a Shaivite who persecuted Buddhists); Mihirakula was defeated by **Yashodharman of Malwa** (Mandasor inscription) and by Narasimhagupta.
- Rise of **feudatories** — Maukharis (Kannauj), **Later Guptas of Magadha**, Maitrakas (Vallabhi), Pushyabhutis (Thanesar), Vakatakas.
- Loss of western trade; fall in gold content of coins; weak successors; land grants weakening the treasury.

### The Vakatakas (Deccan allies)

Founded by **Vindhyashakti**; **Pravarasena I** performed many sacrifices; **Rudrasena II** married **Prabhavatigupta** (daughter of Chandragupta II), who ruled as regent; **Pravarasena II** wrote the Prakrit poem ***Setubandha***; **Harishena** patronised **Ajanta**.

### Mains questions

**1. "The Gupta period is called the Golden Age of ancient India." Critically examine.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* c. 319–550 CE; empire from Pataliputra.
- Political: unity under Samudragupta and Chandragupta II; relative peace.
- Economy: plentiful gold dinaras; but declining trade later.
- Literature: Kalidasa, Shudraka, Vishakhadatta, Panchatantra, Amarakosha.
- Science: Aryabhata, Varahamihira, metallurgy (iron pillar).
- Art: Deogarh temple, Ajanta, Sarnath Buddha, Sultanganj Buddha; Nalanda.
- Critique: land grants and feudalism, caste rigidity, untouchability (Fa-Hien), sati (Eran), forced labour, women's status.
- *Conclusion:* Golden for elite culture; a "classical age" rather than golden for all.

**2. Assess the contribution of foreign rulers (Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Kushanas) to Indian culture.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Post-Mauryan north-west under foreign dynasties; they were absorbed into Indian society.
- Coinage: portrait coins (Indo-Greeks), gold coins (Kushanas).
- Art: Gandhara school, first Buddha images; Mathura under Kushanas.
- Religion: Mahayana, Fourth Council; Heliodorus as Bhagavata; Menander and Nagasena.
- Science: Greek astronomy (Romaka Siddhanta), Charaka.
- Literature/language: Rudradaman's Sanskrit inscription; Ashvaghosha.
- Trade: Silk Route, Roman trade.
- Calendar: Shaka era as national calendar.
- *Conclusion:* A phase of synthesis that enriched Indian culture.

**3. Discuss the importance of Nalanda Mahavihara as a centre of learning.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Founded by Kumaragupta I, 5th century, in Bihar.
- Royal and foreign patronage (Harsha, Palas, Balaputradeva).
- Subjects: Buddhism (Mahayana), Vedas, logic, grammar, medicine, mathematics.
- Scholars and foreign students: Shilabhadra, Hiuen Tsang, I-Tsing; Tibetan links.
- Library Dharmaganja; entrance test.
- Destruction c. 1193; UNESCO 2016; revival 2010–2024.
- *Conclusion:* Symbol of Bihar's intellectual heritage and India's soft power.
