The freedom struggle was not one stream. Besides the Gandhian Congress there were armed revolutionaries, socialists and communists, peasant and worker movements, women's organisations, and the movements of Dalits and backward castes. This chapter covers these streams and ends with profiles of Bihar's leaders — a favourite area for BPSC in both Prelims ("Who is known as Bihar Kesari?") and Mains ("Evaluate the contribution of Dr Rajendra Prasad to the national movement").

> **In simple words:** Some young Indians felt petitions and even satyagraha were too slow. They used bombs and guns to frighten the British and inspire people — these were the **revolutionaries**. Others wanted freedom plus **economic equality** for workers and peasants — the **socialists and communists**. Women and Dalits fought both British rule and the injustice inside Indian society. Bihar gave leaders to every one of these streams.

## Revolutionary nationalism: first phase (1897–1918)

### Why it arose

- Failure of the Moderates; repression of the Swadeshi movement; the Surat split (1907) left youth without leadership.
- Inspiration from **Irish nationalists, Russian nihilists, Italian revolutionaries (Mazzini, Garibaldi)**, and India's own heroes (Shivaji).
- Method: **individual heroic action** — killing oppressive officials, robbing government treasuries for funds ("swadeshi dacoities"), secret societies.

### The Muzaffarpur episode (1908) — Bihar link ⭐

- Target: **Douglas Kingsford**, a former Chief Presidency Magistrate of Calcutta known for harsh sentences on Swadeshi activists. He had been transferred to **Muzaffarpur** (Bihar) as District Judge.
- On **30 April 1908**, **Khudiram Bose** (about 18) and **Prafulla Chaki** threw a bomb at a carriage they thought was Kingsford's. It carried **Mrs Kennedy and her daughter**, who were killed.
- **Prafulla Chaki** shot himself at **Mokama** (Patna district) when police surrounded him.
- **Khudiram Bose** was caught at **Waini (Pusa Road) station**, tried, and **hanged at Muzaffarpur jail on 11 August 1908** — one of the youngest martyrs of the freedom struggle. The station is now named **Khudiram Bose Pusa**.
- Tilak's articles defending the revolutionaries (in *Kesari*) led to his trial and **six years in Mandalay**.

> **Bihar connection:** Muzaffarpur (bombing and Khudiram's execution), Mokama (Prafulla Chaki's death) and Pusa Road (Khudiram's arrest) are all in Bihar. The **Khudiram Bose memorial** stands at Muzaffarpur.

## Revolutionary nationalism: second phase (1922–34)

After Non-Cooperation was withdrawn (1922), many young people turned again to revolution, now with **socialist ideas**.

### HRA → HSRA

- **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)**: **October 1924, Kanpur** — **Sachindranath Sanyal**, **Ramprasad Bismil**, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee. Aim: a federal republic of the United States of India.
- **Kakori Train Robbery (Kakori Conspiracy), 9 August 1925** — near Lucknow, a train carrying government money was looted. **Ramprasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri and Roshan Singh** were **hanged** (1927). Chandrashekhar Azad escaped.
- **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**: **September 1928**, at **Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi** — **Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Bhagwati Charan Vohra**; "Socialist" was added; aim: a socialist republic.
- **Saunders killing (17 December 1928, Lahore)**: to avenge Lala Lajpat Rai's death, Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Azad killed **J.P. Saunders** (they had aimed at Scott).
- **Central Legislative Assembly bomb (8 April 1929)**: **Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt** threw harmless bombs in the Assembly to protest against the **Public Safety Bill** and the **Trade Disputes Bill** — "to make the deaf hear". They shouted "**Inquilab Zindabad**" and courted arrest.
- **Jatin Das** died after a **63-day hunger strike** in Lahore jail (13 September 1929) demanding better treatment for political prisoners.
- **Lahore Conspiracy Case**: **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were hanged on 23 March 1931** (observed as **Shaheed Diwas**).
- **Chandrashekhar Azad** died in a gunfight at **Alfred Park, Allahabad**, on **27 February 1931**.
- Bhagat Singh's essay "**Why I am an Atheist**" and the slogan "Inquilab Zindabad" (popularised by them; coined by Maulana Hasrat Mohani) are often asked.

### Bengal revolutionaries

- **Chittagong Armoury Raid (18 April 1930)**: **Surya Sen ("Masterda")** and the Indian Republican Army; women revolutionaries **Pritilata Waddedar** (died attacking the Pahartali European Club, 1932) and **Kalpana Dutt**. Surya Sen was hanged in **1934**.
- **Bina Das** fired at the Bengal Governor (1932); **Shanti Ghosh and Suniti Choudhury** shot a magistrate in Comilla (1931).
- **Writers' Building attack, Calcutta (December 1930)**: Benoy, Badal and Dinesh.

### Bihar and the revolutionaries

- **Batukeshwar Dutt** (Bhagat Singh's companion in the Assembly bomb case) spent his later life in **Patna**, where he died in 1965.
- **Yogendra Shukla** (from Muzaffarpur district) was an HSRA member, served a long term in the **Cellular Jail (Andamans)**, and later escaped from Hazaribagh jail with JP in 1942.
- **Baikunth Shukla** killed **Phanindra Nath Ghosh** — an HSRA member who had turned **approver** (government witness) in the Lahore Conspiracy Case and whose evidence helped send Bhagat Singh to the gallows — at Bettiah in 1932. Baikunth Shukla was **hanged in Gaya jail in 1934**.
- Bihar groups linked with the HSRA worked in Champaran, Muzaffarpur and Patna.

> **Mains angle:** Assess the revolutionaries: **positive** — courage and sacrifice inspired youth; kept the spirit alive when mass movements were down; Bhagat Singh's group gave a **socialist and secular** vision; the HSRA used courts as a platform. **Limits** — small numbers, no mass base, repression broke them; violence was opposed by Gandhi and the Congress. Conclusion: a complementary, not competing, stream.

## The Left: communists, workers and socialists

### Communists and the workers' movement

- **Communist Party of India**: founded by **M.N. Roy** and others at **Tashkent (October 1920)**; the Indian conference at **Kanpur (December 1925)** is taken as the founding of the CPI inside India.
- British conspiracy cases against communists: **Peshawar (1922–23)**, **Kanpur Bolshevik Conspiracy Case (1924)** — S.A. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmed, Shaukat Usmani, Nalini Gupta; **Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929)** — 31 labour leaders including three Englishmen (Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Lester Hutchinson).
- **Workers' and Peasants' Parties** (1926–27).
- **All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC)**: **31 October 1920, Bombay** — first president **Lala Lajpat Rai**, general secretary **N.M. Joshi**. Laws: **Trade Unions Act (1926)**, **Trade Disputes Act (1929)**.
- In 1942 the CPI **opposed Quit India** ("People's War" line after the USSR joined the Allies).

### The Congress Socialist Party (CSP) — born in Patna ⭐

- Young Congress leaders in jail during Civil Disobedience discussed **socialism** and decided to form a socialist group **inside** the Congress.
- **17 May 1934**: a meeting at **Patna** (at the **Anjuman Islamia Hall**), presided over by **Acharya Narendra Dev**, decided to form the **Congress Socialist Party**. The **formal founding conference** was held at **Bombay in October 1934**.
- **Narendra Dev** — president (of the founding conference); **Jayaprakash Narayan** — **general secretary**.
- Other leaders: **Ram Manohar Lohia, Achyut Patwardhan, Minoo Masani, Asoka Mehta, Yusuf Meherally, Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay, Purushottam Trikamdas**.
- **Aims:** complete independence; socialist economy; abolition of zamindari and princely states; rights for peasants and workers; work inside the Congress to push it left.
- The CSP played a big role in the **Quit India underground (1942)** — JP, Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Achyut Patwardhan.
- In **1948** the socialists left the Congress and formed the **Socialist Party**.

> **Bihar connection:** The **CSP was born in Patna (May 1934)** and its first general secretary, **JP**, was from Bihar. Bihar socialists like **Ganga Sharan Sinha, Ramvriksh Benipuri, Basawon Singh** and **Suraj Narayan Singh** were active in it. Bihar later became the main base of Indian socialist politics (Lohia, JP, **Karpoori Thakur**).

### Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Bose as "left" leaders

- Both were influenced by socialism: Nehru's **Lucknow (1936)** presidential speech; Bose's **Haripura (1938)** and the **National Planning Committee** (Nehru chairman, 1938).
- **Karachi Resolution (1931)** on Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy — drafted by Nehru.

## Dalit and backward-class movements

### Dr B.R. Ambedkar

- **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)**; papers ***Mooknayak*** (1920), ***Bahishkrit Bharat*** (1927).
- **Mahad Satyagraha (1927)** — right to draw water from the Chavdar tank; burnt the *Manusmriti* (25 December 1927).
- **Kalaram Temple entry satyagraha**, Nasik (1930).
- Attended **all three Round Table Conferences**; **Poona Pact (1932)**.
- **Independent Labour Party (1936)**; **Scheduled Castes Federation (1942)**.
- Chairman of the **Drafting Committee** of the Constitution; book *Annihilation of Caste* (1936).

> **Bihar connection:** **Jagjivan Ram** organised the depressed classes in Bihar and nationally — he helped form the **All India Depressed Classes League** (1935) and worked with the Congress; he was jailed in the Individual Satyagraha and Quit India. Among backward castes in Bihar, the **Triveni Sangh** (1930s; Yadav, Kurmi and Koeri peasants of Shahabad and central Bihar) organised for political and social rights — an early sign of the social-justice politics Bihar is known for. In **1953**, CM **Sri Krishna Sinha** personally led Dalits into the **Baidyanath Dham temple** at Deoghar.

## Profiles of Bihar's leaders ⭐

> **Bihar connection:** Learn each with: birth (year, place), title, three key roles, one book or institution. These are frequent one-liners in Prelims and add a strong "Bihar angle" to Mains answers.

### Dr Rajendra Prasad (1884–1963) — "Desh Ratna", "Ajatashatru"

- Born **3 December 1884**, **Ziradei** (Siwan district). Topped the Calcutta University entrance exam; lawyer at Calcutta and Patna High Courts.
- **Bihari Students' Conference** (1906 onwards) organiser; **Champaran (1917)** — Gandhi's chief helper; wrote ***Satyagraha in Champaran***.
- Left legal practice in **Non-Cooperation**; **Bihar Vidyapith**; Hindi weekly *Desh*; leading **No-changer** (1922).
- Led relief in the **1934 Bihar earthquake**.
- **Congress President: 1934 (Bombay), 1939 (after Bose resigned), 1947 (after Kripalani resigned)**.
- **Interim Government (1946)**: Food and Agriculture.
- **President of the Constituent Assembly** (from 11 December 1946).
- **First President of India**, **26 January 1950–13 May 1962** — the only President elected twice (1952, 1957).
- **Bharat Ratna (1962)**. Died **28 February 1963** at **Sadaqat Ashram, Patna**.
- Books: ***India Divided*** (1946, against the two-nation theory), *Atmakatha* (autobiography), *At the Feet of Mahatma Gandhi*, *Satyagraha in Champaran*.
- His birthday (3 December) is observed as **Advocates' Day** (Lawyers' Day).

### Dr Anugrah Narayan Sinha (1887–1957) — "Bihar Vibhuti"

- Born **18 June 1887**, **Poiwan** (Aurangabad district).
- Joined Gandhi in **Champaran (1917)**; Non-Cooperation; jailed many times (CDM, Individual Satyagraha, Quit India).
- **Finance Minister** in the 1937 ministry and **first Deputy Chief Minister and Finance Minister of Bihar** after 1946 (until his death).
- Member of the Constituent Assembly. Died **5 July 1957**.
- The **A.N. Sinha Institute of Social Studies**, Patna, is named after him.

### Dr Sri Krishna Sinha (1887–1961) — "Bihar Kesari", "Shri Babu"

- Born **21 October 1887** (Nawada district; ancestral village Maur, Begusarai).
- Broke the salt law at **Garhpura** (1930); jailed many times.
- **Premier of Bihar (1937–39)**; **first Chief Minister of Bihar (1946–1961)** — the longest-serving early CM; died in office on **31 January 1961**.
- Under him: **Bihar was the first state to abolish zamindari** (**Bihar Land Reforms Act, 1950**); industrial projects (Barauni refinery, Sindri fertiliser, Bokaro planning, bridges); Dalit temple entry at **Deoghar (1953)**.

### Mazharul Haque (1866–1930)

- Born in **Patna district** (Bahpura); barrister.
- Muslim League president (**Bombay, 1915**); **Home Rule League** president in Bihar (1916).
- Resigned from the Imperial Legislative Council against the **Rowlatt Act (1919)**.
- Supported Gandhi in **Champaran**; gave up practice in **NCM**; founded **Sadaqat Ashram** (Patna); first chancellor of **Bihar Vidyapith**; started ***The Motherland*** (1921).
- A lifelong symbol of **Hindu–Muslim unity**. The **Maulana Mazharul Haque Arabic and Persian University**, Patna, is named after him.

### Dr Sachchidananda Sinha (1871–1950)

- Born **10 November 1871**, **Murar** (Buxar district); barrister; editor of *Hindustan Review*; co-founder of *Behar Times* (1894).
- **Architect of the separate Bihar movement** (1912).
- First Indian **Finance Member** (Executive Councillor) of a province — **Bihar and Orissa (1921)**.
- Founded the **Sinha Library**, Patna (in memory of his wife Radhika).
- **Interim president of the Constituent Assembly (9 December 1946)**.

### Jagjivan Ram (1908–1986) — "Babuji"

- Born **5 April 1908**, **Chandwa** (Bhojpur district). Studied at BHU and Calcutta.
- Organised the depressed classes; Congress leader; jailed in 1940 and 1942.
- **Labour Minister in the Interim Government (1946)**; later held many ministries (Defence during the **1971 war**); **Deputy Prime Minister (1977–79)**.
- His birthday (5 April) is observed in Bihar as **Samta Diwas** (Equality Day).

### Brajkishore Prasad (1877–1946)

- Lawyer of Darbhanga; with Gandhi in **Champaran (1917)**; raised the indigo question in the Bihar Legislative Council; Vice-Chancellor of Bihar Vidyapith; father of **Prabhavati Devi** and father-in-law of JP.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Revolutionary nationalism | Use of armed action by secret groups to end British rule |
| Approver | An accused person who becomes a government witness against his companions |
| Swadeshi dacoity | Robbery of government money by revolutionaries for their cause |
| Socialism | Idea that the means of production should serve society, with equality for workers and peasants |
| Congress Socialist Party | Socialist group working inside the Congress (1934) |
| Sampoorna Kranti | "Total Revolution" — JP's call in 1974 |
| Sarvodaya | "Welfare of all" — Gandhian ideal |
| Bhoodan | Land-gift movement of Vinoba Bhave (from 1951) |
| Depressed classes | Old official term for the communities now called Scheduled Castes |
| Temple entry | Movement for the right of Dalits to enter Hindu temples |

### Mains questions

**1. Evaluate the contribution of Dr Rajendra Prasad to the Indian national movement and the making of the Republic. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** From Ziradei to Rashtrapati Bhavan — a Gandhian of simplicity, called Ajatashatru.
- **Champaran (1917):** organised the inquiry; wrote its history.
- **NCM:** gave up practice; Bihar Vidyapith; *Desh*; No-changer at Gaya (1922).
- **CDM and Quit India:** led in Bihar; long imprisonments.
- **Social service:** 1934 earthquake relief; Harijan work; Hindi promotion.
- **Organisation:** Congress President 1934, 1939, 1947 — steadied the party in crises.
- **Constitution:** President of the Constituent Assembly — impartial chair, consensus builder.
- **Republic:** first President (1950–62); set conventions of the office.
- **Writings:** *India Divided*, *Atmakatha*.
- **Conclusion:** He united moral leadership with organisational skill; Bihar's greatest gift to the Republic.

**2. Discuss the role of the Congress Socialist Party in the national movement, with special reference to Bihar. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Formed 1934 (Patna meeting, Bombay conference) by young Congressmen.
- Aims — independence plus socialism; zamindari abolition; workers' and peasants' rights.
- Leaders — Narendra Dev, JP, Lohia, Patwardhan, Masani.
- Pushed Congress left (Faizpur agrarian programme; Haripura; Tripuri).
- Links with the Kisan Sabha in Bihar.
- Leadership of the 1942 underground (JP's escape, Azad Dasta).
- **Conclusion:** gave the national movement a social-economic programme; Bihar became the heartland of socialist politics.

**3. "The revolutionary movement was a complementary rather than a contradictory stream of the freedom struggle." Discuss. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** two phases — 1897–1918, 1922–34.
- **First phase:** Bengal and Maharashtra groups; Muzaffarpur (Bihar); abroad — India House, Ghadar.
- **Second phase:** HRA–HSRA, Kakori, Assembly bomb, Chittagong; socialist and secular ideas.
- **Complementary:** inspired youth when mass movements paused; Bhagat Singh's popularity matched Gandhi's in 1931; Congress defended INA and revolutionaries in court.
- **Differences:** violence vs non-violence; small groups vs mass movement.
- **Bihar angle:** Khudiram at Muzaffarpur, Baikunth Shukla, Yogendra Shukla, B.K. Dutt in Patna.
- **Conclusion:** different methods, same goal — both kept the spirit of freedom alive.

**4. Assess the role of women in India's freedom struggle with examples from Bihar. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** from social reform to mass politics.
- Organisations — WIA, AIWC.
- Gandhian movements — salt and picketing (Sarojini Naidu, Kamaladevi).
- Revolutionaries — Pritilata, Kalpana Dutt, Durga Bhabhi.
- Quit India — Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta, Matangini Hazra; INA's Rani Jhansi Regiment.
- Bihar — Kasturba in Champaran schools; Prabhavati Devi; Tara Rani Srivastava at Siwan; women picketers 1930–32.
- **Conclusion:** women's participation widened the movement and prepared the ground for equal rights in the Constitution.
