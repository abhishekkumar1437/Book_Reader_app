The Mughal period (1526–1707 for the "Great Mughals") is the most-asked part of medieval history. For a Bihar candidate it has an extra star: **Sher Shah Suri**, the Afghan ruler from **Sasaram (Rohtas district)**, who defeated Humayun at **Chausa (Buxar)** and created a revenue, road and currency system that Akbar and even the British built on. BPSC asks about battles and dates, mansabdari, Todar Mal's revenue system, Din-i-Ilahi, Mughal buildings, travellers, and Sher Shah's reforms — often in Mains too.

> **In simple words:** Babur, a prince from Central Asia, defeated Ibrahim Lodi in 1526 and founded the Mughal empire. His son Humayun lost it to Sher Shah, an Afghan from Bihar, who ruled only five years (1540–45) but reformed land revenue, coins, roads and justice. Humayun came back in 1555. His son Akbar built a large, well-run empire with Rajput support, a ranking system for officers (mansabdari) and a policy of religious tolerance. Jahangir and Shah Jahan presided over great painting and buildings like the Taj Mahal. Aurangzeb expanded the empire to its largest size but faced many revolts; after his death in 1707 the empire declined.

## Babur (1526–1530)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Origin | Born **1483** in **Fergana** (Uzbekistan); descended from **Timur** (father's side) and **Chengiz Khan** (mother's side); a **Chagatai Turk** |
| Invitation | By **Daulat Khan Lodi** (Punjab governor) and **Rana Sanga** (as Babur claims) |
| **First Battle of Panipat** | **21 April 1526** — defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**. Tactics: **tulughma** (flanking — dividing the army to attack from sides and rear), **araba** (carts tied together to shield gunners), **artillery** under **Ustad Ali** and **Mustafa** |
| **Battle of Khanwa** | **1527** (near Fatehpur Sikri) — defeated **Rana Sanga** of Mewar; declared it a **jihad**, took the title **Ghazi**, gave up wine, and abolished **tamgha** (trade tax) for Muslims |
| **Battle of Chanderi** | **1528** — defeated **Medini Rai** |
| **Battle of Ghaghra** | **1529** — on the **Ghaghra in Bihar** (near its meeting with the Ganga) — defeated the **Afghans** under **Mahmud Lodi** and **Nusrat Shah** of Bengal; first battle fought on both land and water |
| Title | **Padshah** (first ruler in India to use it) |
| Memoir | ***Tuzuk-i-Baburi* (Baburnama)** in **Turkish (Chagatai)**; translated into Persian by **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** under Akbar |
| Death | **1530** at Agra; buried at **Kabul** (Bagh-e-Babur) |
| Buildings | Mosques at **Panipat** (Kabuli Bagh) and **Sambhal**; the Ayodhya mosque built by his general **Mir Baqi** (1528) |

> **Memory trick:** Babur's four battles in four years — **"Panipat–Khanwa–Chanderi–Ghaghra" = 1526–27–28–29**.

## Humayun (1530–1540 and 1555–1556)

- Divided the empire with his brothers (**Kamran** got Kabul and Punjab) — a mistake.
- Built **Din-panah** in Delhi (later Purana Qila).
- Fought **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat** (1535) and the Afghan **Sher Khan** of Bihar.
- **Battle of Chausa (26 June 1539)** — at **Chausa near Buxar, Bihar**; Sher Khan's surprise night attack; Humayun escaped by crossing the Ganga with the help of a water-carrier (*bhishti*) **Nizam**, who was later made "king for a day".
- **Battle of Kannauj / Bilgram (17 May 1540)** — final defeat; Humayun fled.
- **Exile (1540–1555)** in Sindh and **Persia** (helped by **Shah Tahmasp**). **Akbar was born at Amarkot (Sindh) on 15 October 1542**, in the house of Rana Virsal (Prasad).
- Returned and won at **Machhiwara** and **Sirhind (1555)** against the Surs.
- **Died in January 1556** after falling down the stairs of his library (**Sher Mandal**) in Purana Qila.
- His sister **Gulbadan Begum** wrote ***Humayun-nama***.
- **Humayun's Tomb (Delhi)** — built by his widow **Haji Begum (Bega Begum)**; architect **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas**; completed c. 1570; the **first garden-tomb** of India on the *charbagh* plan, with a double dome; **UNESCO 1993**.

> **Prelims angle:** Lane-Poole's famous line: Humayun "**tumbled through life and tumbled out of it**".

## Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545) — the Afghan from Sasaram

This is the most important medieval topic for a Bihar candidate. Study it fully.

### Early life and rise in Bihar

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Original name | **Farid Khan** |
| Birth | Date and place are **disputed**: c. **1472** or **1486**; at **Sasaram** (Bihar) or at **Hisar/Narnaul** (Haryana). The exam-safe statement is: "**Sher Shah of Sasaram**" — his father's jagir and his tomb are at Sasaram |
| Father | **Hasan Khan Sur**, holder of the jagir of **Sasaram and Khawaspur Tanda** (Rohtas region, Bihar) under Jamal Khan |
| Training | Studied at **Jaunpur**; managed his father's jagir and learned revenue administration at the village level — the root of his later reforms |
| Title "Sher Khan" | Given by **Bahar Khan Lohani** (who ruled Bihar as **Sultan Muhammad**) after Farid **killed a tiger** |
| Rise | Became deputy (*vakil*) and guardian of the young **Jalal Khan Lohani**; virtual ruler of **Bihar**; married **Lad Malika** and gained the strong **Chunar fort** (UP) |
| **Battle of Surajgarh (1534)** | At **Surajgarh (Munger/Lakhisarai area, Bihar)** — defeated the army of the **Bengal Sultan** and **Jalal Khan**; became master of Bihar |
| Bengal | Attacked **Gaur** (1537–38), the Bengal capital |
| **Battle of Chausa (1539)** | Defeated **Humayun**; took the title **Sher Shah** ("Shah" = king) and **Sultan-ul-Adil** |
| **Battle of Kannauj (1540)** | Final defeat of Humayun; Sher Shah became ruler of **Delhi and Agra** — founded the **Sur dynasty** |

### Conquests as emperor (1540–1545)

| Year | Campaign |
|---|---|
| 1540–42 | Punjab; against the **Gakkhars**; built **Rohtas fort (Jhelum, Pakistan)** — UNESCO 1997 |
| 1542 | **Malwa** |
| 1543 | **Raisen** — Rajput chief **Puranmal** surrendered on promise of safety but was attacked (a blot on Sher Shah's record) |
| **1544** | **Marwar** — **Battle of Sammel (Giri-Sumel)** against **Rao Maldeo**; a narrow win. Sher Shah said: "**I had almost given away the empire of Hindustan for a handful of millet (bajra)**" (Marwar is a poor, dry land) |
| 1544 | Chittor surrendered |
| **1545** | Siege of **Kalinjar** (Bundelkhand) — **died on 22 May 1545** when a **gunpowder (rocket) explosion** occurred; the fort was captured the same day |

### Land revenue reforms

- **Measurement of all cultivable land** using the **Sikandari gaz** (32 digits) and a **rope (jarib)** of hemp.
- Land divided into **three classes — good, middle, bad** — and average produce worked out; the state's share was **one-third** of the average produce.
- A **crop-rate schedule called *rai*** fixed the state's demand for each crop.
- **Patta** (title-deed given to the peasant, stating his land and tax) and **qabuliyat** (deed of acceptance signed by the peasant) — a written contract between state and peasant.
- Payment in **cash or kind** (cash preferred).
- Extra fees: **jaribana** (measurement fee, 2.5%) and **muhasilana** (collection fee, 5%).
- A cess for famine relief; **loans** in bad seasons; soldiers punished for damaging crops.

> **Remember:** Sher Shah's revenue system was the base on which **Raja Todar Mal** (who had worked under Sher Shah) built **Akbar's zabti system**. Historians call Sher Shah the "**forerunner of Akbar**".

### Currency

- Issued the silver **rupiya** (about **178 grains**) — the ancestor of today's **rupee**.
- Copper **dam** (40 dams = 1 rupiya under Akbar) and gold **ashrafi/mohur**.
- Standard weight and purity; old debased coins withdrawn; names in **Devanagari and Persian** on some coins.

### Roads, sarais and posts

- Rebuilt and extended the old **Uttarapatha** as **Sadak-e-Azam** ("great road"), later called the **Grand Trunk Road** — from **Sonargaon (Bangladesh) to Attock/Peshawar** (about 2,500 km in his time). Other roads: **Agra–Burhanpur**, **Agra–Jodhpur–Chittor**, **Lahore–Multan**.
- About **1,700 sarais** (rest houses), one every 2 kos (about 6–8 km), with **separate rooms for Hindus and Muslims**, food, water, and stables; shade trees along roads.
- Sarais doubled as **dak chowkis** — relay posts with horses for news and royal mail; markets grew around them ("**the arteries of the empire**" — K.R. Qanungo).

### Army, justice and trade

- Standing army of about **1,50,000 cavalry and 25,000 infantry** (sources vary), personally recruited; **dagh** (horse branding) and **chehra** (descriptive roll) — revived Alauddin's methods.
- Justice was strict and equal — "**justice is the most excellent of religious rites**"; Qazis and Mir-i-Adls in towns.
- Trade: customs duty collected only twice — at the point of entry and at the point of sale; internal tolls abolished.

### Buildings

| Monument | Place | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Sher Shah's Tomb** | **Sasaram (Rohtas, Bihar)** | **Octagonal tomb in the middle of an artificial lake**, on a stepped platform; red sandstone; large dome with small kiosks (*chhatris*); architect **Aliwal Khan**; finished in **1545** (after his death, under **Islam Shah**); a masterpiece of **Indo-Afghan** architecture, often called a link between Lodi and Mughal styles |
| Tomb of **Hasan Khan Sur** | Sasaram | His father's tomb |
| Tomb of **Islam Shah** | Sasaram | Unfinished |
| **Qila-i-Kuhna mosque** and **Purana Qila** | Delhi | Rebuilt Humayun's Din-panah as his citadel |
| **Rohtas fort** | Jhelum, **Pakistan** | Massive fort against the Gakkhars; UNESCO 1997 |
| **Rohtasgarh fort** | Rohtas district, **Bihar** | Captured by Sher Shah (1538) from a Hindu chief; later Man Singh built a palace here |
| **Patna** | Bihar | Sher Shah built a **fort at Patna (1541)** and revived it as a town and administrative centre; **Sher Shah Suri Masjid** in Patna |

### Assessment and sources

- Source: ***Tarikh-i-Sher Shahi*** (*Tuhfa-i-Akbar Shahi*) by **Abbas Khan Sarwani**, written in Akbar's reign.
- Hindus held high posts (e.g. **Brahmajit Gaur** as a general); he did not reimpose jaziya beyond custom, but he was not a reformer of religion like Akbar.
- His **Rajput** campaigns (Raisen) show harshness.
- His **successors**: **Islam Shah** (1545–53) — strengthened the system; **Muhammad Adil Shah** — his Hindu minister **Hemu** (Hemachandra Vikramaditya) won 22 battles, took Delhi in 1556, and was defeated by Akbar at the **Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556)**; **Sikandar Sur** lost at **Sirhind (1555)**.

> **Bihar connection:** Sher Shah is Bihar's greatest medieval ruler. His jagir and tomb are at **Sasaram**; he became master of Bihar at **Surajgarh (1534)**, defeated Humayun at **Chausa (1539)** in **Buxar district**, rebuilt **Patna (1541)**, and held **Rohtasgarh**. His **rupiya**, **GT Road** and **patta–qabuliyat** revenue method shaped Indian administration for centuries.

> **Mains angle:** "**Sher Shah was the forerunner of Akbar.**" or "**Evaluate Sher Shah's administrative reforms.**" Structure: intro (5-year reign but lasting impact) → land revenue (measurement, three classes, 1/3, patta–qabuliyat, rai) → currency (rupiya) → roads & sarais & dak → central/local administration (sarkar, pargana, village responsibility) → army (dagh, chehra) → justice and trade → continuity under Akbar (Todar Mal) and British (rupee, GT Road) → limits (short reign, Afghan tribal politics, harshness at Raisen) → conclusion.

## Akbar (1556–1605)

### Early years

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | **15 October 1542**, **Amarkot** (Sindh) |
| Crowned | **February 1556** at **Kalanaur** (Punjab), aged about 13 |
| Regent | **Bairam Khan** (1556–1560) |
| **Second Battle of Panipat** | **5 November 1556** — defeated **Hemu** (Hemu was hit in the eye by an arrow, captured and killed) |
| **"Petticoat government"** | 1560–1562 — influence of his foster mother **Maham Anga** and her son **Adham Khan** (Akbar threw Adham Khan from a terrace in 1562) |

### Expansion

| Year | Conquest |
|---|---|
| 1561 | Malwa (Baz Bahadur — Rani Rupmati story) |
| 1562 | Alliance with **Raja Bharmal of Amber** — married his daughter (Harkha Bai, later **Mariam-uz-Zamani**, mother of Jahangir) |
| 1564 | Gondwana (**Rani Durgavati** died fighting) |
| **1568** | **Chittor** (Rana **Udai Singh** fled; Jaimal and Patta defended) |
| 1569 | Ranthambore, Kalinjar |
| **1572–73** | **Gujarat** — the **Buland Darwaza** at Fatehpur Sikri commemorates this victory; Akbar saw the sea for the first time |
| **1574–76** | **Bihar and Bengal** — defeated the Afghan **Daud Khan Karrani** (killed after the **Battle of Rajmahal, 1576**) |
| **1576** | **Battle of Haldighati (18 June 1576)** — Mughal army under **Raja Man Singh** against **Maharana Pratap** of Mewar; Pratap escaped and continued resistance (his horse **Chetak**) |
| 1581 | Kabul (against his half-brother Mirza Hakim) |
| 1586 | Kashmir |
| 1591–95 | Sindh, Odisha (1592), Baluchistan, **Kandahar (1595)** |
| 1600 | **Ahmadnagar** (**Chand Bibi** resisted and was killed) |
| **1601** | **Asirgarh** — **Akbar's last conquest** |

### Religious policy — the road to Sulh-i-kul

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1562 | Stopped enslaving prisoners of war |
| **1563** | Abolished the **pilgrimage tax** |
| **1564** | Abolished **jaziya** |
| **1575** | Built the **Ibadat Khana** (House of Worship) at **Fatehpur Sikri** — at first for Muslim debates, from 1578 open to Hindus, Jains, Zoroastrians, Christians (Jesuits **Rudolf Acquaviva** and **Antonio Monserrate**, 1580) and others |
| **1579** | **Mahzar** ("infallibility decree") — drafted by **Shaikh Mubarak**; Akbar as **Imam-i-Adil** could decide among conflicting religious opinions |
| **1582** | **Din-i-Ilahi** (**Tauhid-i-Ilahi**) — a spiritual order, not a religion; few followers; **Birbal** the only prominent Hindu member |
| Throughout | **Sulh-i-kul** — "**universal peace**" / peace with all; jharokha darshan, tuladan; Persian translations of the Mahabharata (**Razmnama**), Ramayana, Atharvaveda in the **Maktab Khana** (translation bureau) |

### Akbar's Navaratnas (nine gems)

**Abul Fazl** (historian, *Akbarnama*, *Ain-i-Akbari*), **Faizi** (his brother, poet laureate; translated *Lilavati*), **Birbal** (Mahesh Das, wit; died fighting the Yusufzais, 1586), **Raja Todar Mal** (finance), **Raja Man Singh** (general), **Tansen** (musician, from Gwalior; disciple of Swami Haridas), **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** (son of Bairam Khan; poet in Hindi — Rahim's dohe), **Mulla Do-Piyaza**, **Fakir Aziao-Din**. (The list is a later tradition.)

### Mansabdari system

- Introduced around **1570s** (commonly **1573–75**). **Mansab** = rank. Every officer (civil or military) held a mansab.
- Two numbers: **zat** (personal rank and salary) and **sawar** (number of horsemen to maintain) — **sawar** was added later (c. 1595).
- Ranks ranged from **10 to 10,000** (and higher for princes; ordinary nobles up to 5,000, later raised to 7,000).
- Paid either in **cash (naqdi)** or by a **jagir** (assignment of revenue from land) — the holder was a **jagirdar**; jagirs were **transferable**, not hereditary.
- Under Jahangir: **du-aspa sih-aspa** (extra horses). Under Shah Jahan: "month-scale" (*mashrut*).
- **Mir Bakhshi** managed the mansabdars.

### Land revenue — Todar Mal's bandobast

- **Zabti system** / **Dahsala** ("ten-year") system, introduced **1580** by **Raja Todar Mal**: land measured (with a **bamboo jarib linked by iron rings**, more accurate than rope); **average produce and average prices of the past 10 years** worked out; the state's share was **one-third**, payable **in cash**.
- Land classified by cultivation:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Polaj** | Cultivated every year |
| **Parauti** | Left fallow for 1–2 years |
| **Chachar** | Fallow for 3–4 years |
| **Banjar** | Fallow for 5 years or more |

- Other systems in some areas: **batai / ghalla-bakhshi** (crop-sharing), **kankut** (estimate of crop), **nasaq** (rough assessment on past record).
- **Ilahi gaz** introduced (replacing the Sikandari gaz).

### Mughal administration (Akbar's model)

| Level | Officials |
|---|---|
| Centre | **Vakil** (prime minister — role reduced after Bairam Khan), **Wazir/Diwan** (finance), **Mir Bakhshi** (army, mansabdars, intelligence), **Mir Saman** (royal household and **karkhanas** — workshops), **Sadr-us-Sudur** (religious grants, charity), **Qazi-ul-Quzat** (justice) |
| Province — **suba** (12 in 1580, 15 by 1605) | **Subadar** (governor), **Diwan** (revenue — reported to the central diwan, a check on the subadar), **Bakhshi**, **Sadr**, **Qazi**, **Waqia-navis** (news writer) |
| District — **sarkar** | **Faujdar** (law and order), **Amalguzar** (revenue) |
| **Pargana** | **Shiqdar**, **Amil**, **Qanungo** (records), Fotedar |
| Town | **Kotwal** (police, markets) |
| Village | **Muqaddam**, **Patwari** |

> **Bihar connection:** **Bihar** was one of Akbar's original **12 subas** (capital **Patna**). After Daud Khan Karrani's defeat (1576), Bihar was firmly Mughal. **Raja Man Singh** was governor of Bihar (c. 1587–94); he built a palace at **Rohtasgarh** and campaigned in Odisha from Bihar.

Akbar died in **1605**; his **tomb is at Sikandra** (near Agra).

## Jahangir (1605–1627)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | **Salim** (named after Sufi **Salim Chishti** of Fatehpur Sikri) |
| **Nur Jahan** | **Mehr-un-Nisa**, married **1611**; the "**Nur Jahan junta**" — her father **Itimad-ud-Daula**, brother **Asaf Khan** and Prince Khurram; coins in her name |
| Chain of justice | **Zanjir-i-Adl** with 60 bells at Agra fort |
| Sikhs | Executed **Guru Arjan Dev (1606)** for blessing rebel prince **Khusrau** |
| Mewar | Treaty with **Rana Amar Singh (1615)** — ended the long war |
| English | **Captain William Hawkins** (1608–11) and **Sir Thomas Roe** (1615–19, ambassador of **James I**) — got permission for the English factory at **Surat** |
| Losses | **Kandahar lost to Persia (1622)**; revolt of Khurram (1622–25) and of **Mahabat Khan (1626)** |
| Memoir | ***Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri*** (in Persian) |
| Painting | Peak of Mughal painting — **Ustad Mansur** (birds and animals; title *Nadir-ul-Asr*), **Abul Hasan** (*Nadir-uz-Zaman*), **Bishandas** (portraits) |
| Buildings | **Itimad-ud-Daula's tomb** (Agra, built by **Nur Jahan**) — first Mughal building fully in **white marble** with **pietra dura** (stone inlay); **Shalimar Bagh** (Kashmir); Akbar's tomb at Sikandra completed |
| Death | 1627; tomb at **Shahdara (Lahore)** |

## Shah Jahan (1628–1658)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | **Khurram**; wife **Mumtaz Mahal** (Arjumand Bano Begum) died at **Burhanpur (1631)** |
| Deccan | **Annexed Ahmadnagar (1633)**; treaties with **Bijapur and Golconda (1636)**; Aurangzeb as Deccan viceroy |
| North-west | Failed **Balkh** campaign (1646–47); **Kandahar lost finally (1649)** |
| Buildings | **Taj Mahal** (Agra, c. **1632–1653**; chief architect **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**; **UNESCO 1983**); **Red Fort, Delhi** (**Shahjahanabad**, 1639–48 — Diwan-i-Am, Diwan-i-Khas, Rang Mahal); **Jama Masjid, Delhi** (largest mosque in India); **Moti Masjid, Agra**; **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** — taken away by **Nadir Shah (1739)** with the Kohinoor |
| Court history | ***Padshahnama*** by **Abdul Hamid Lahori** |
| Prince **Dara Shikoh** | Translated **52 Upanishads** into Persian (***Sirr-i-Akbar***); wrote ***Majma-ul-Bahrain*** ("meeting of two oceans" — Islam and Hinduism) |
| **War of succession (1657–58)** | Among **Dara, Shuja, Aurangzeb, Murad**: **Bahadurpur** (1658, Shuja lost to Dara's son), **Dharmat** (April 1658, Aurangzeb won), **Samugarh** (May 1658, Aurangzeb defeated Dara), **Khajwa** (1659, Shuja lost), **Deorai** (1659, Dara lost, later executed) |
| End | Imprisoned in **Agra fort** by Aurangzeb; died **1666** |

Travellers of this time: **François Bernier** (French physician, 1656–68 — *Travels in the Mogul Empire*; said there was no private property in land), **Jean-Baptiste Tavernier** (French jeweller), **Niccolao Manucci** (Italian — *Storia do Mogor*), **Peter Mundy** (English).

> **Prelims angle:** Shah Jahan's reign is called the **"Golden Age of Mughal architecture"**; Jahangir's the **peak of painting**; Akbar's the peak of **administration**; Aurangzeb's the **largest territory**.

## Aurangzeb (1658–1707)

| Area | Facts |
|---|---|
| Title | **Alamgir** ("world-conqueror"); called **Zinda Pir** (living saint) for his simple life — earned money by copying the Quran and sewing caps |
| Religious policy | **Reimposed jaziya (1679)**; banned music at court (but music flourished outside), stopped **jharokha darshan**, **tuladan**, Nauroz; compiled ***Fatawa-i-Alamgiri*** (Islamic law digest); some temples destroyed (Vishvanath at Varanasi, Keshava Rai at Mathura); but also gave grants to some temples |
| Sikhs | Executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675)** |
| Revolts | **Jats** (Gokula, 1669; Rajaram), **Satnamis** (1672, Narnaul), **Bundelas** (**Chhatrasal**), **Rajputs** (Marwar after Jaswant Singh's death, 1679 — **Durgadas Rathore**), **Afghans** (Yusufzais), **Marathas** (Shivaji; **Sambhaji executed 1689**) |
| Deccan | Annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)**; spent his last ~25 years in the Deccan fighting the Marathas — the "**Deccan ulcer**" |
| Buildings | **Bibi ka Maqbara** (Aurangabad — built by his son **Azam Shah** for his mother; "**Taj of the Deccan**"), **Badshahi Mosque** (Lahore), **Moti Masjid** in the Red Fort (Delhi) |
| Death | **1707** at **Ahmednagar**; buried in a simple grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad) |
| Extent | Largest Mughal empire — Kabul to Jinji (Tamil Nadu) |

## Mughal culture

### Architecture

| Feature | Example |
|---|---|
| Red sandstone, Persian + Indian elements (Akbar) | **Agra Fort**, **Fatehpur Sikri** (Buland Darwaza, Jama Masjid, Panch Mahal, Diwan-i-Khas, tomb of **Salim Chishti**, Jodha Bai's palace) — **UNESCO 1986** |
| Garden-tomb (charbagh), double dome | **Humayun's Tomb** |
| White marble, pietra dura | **Itimad-ud-Daula** (Jahangir period) |
| Perfection of symmetry, marble | **Taj Mahal** |
| Gardens | **Shalimar** (Kashmir, Lahore), **Nishat Bagh**, Pinjore |

### Painting

- Began under **Humayun** with Persian masters **Mir Sayyid Ali** and **Abdus Samad**, who led Akbar's studio (*tasvir khana*).
- Akbar: illustrated **Hamzanama** (*Dastan-i-Amir Hamza*), *Razmnama*; Indian painters **Daswanth** and **Basawan**.
- **Jahangir**: portraits, nature studies — **Ustad Mansur**, **Abul Hasan**, **Bishandas**; Jahangir claimed he could tell each painter's hand.
- Declined under Aurangzeb; painters moved to regional courts — **Rajput, Pahari** schools, and later the **Patna Kalam** (Company school) in Bihar.

| Work | Author |
|---|---|
| *Akbarnama*, *Ain-i-Akbari* | **Abul Fazl** |
| *Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh* (critical of Akbar) | **Abdul Qadir Badauni** |

Music: **Tansen** (Akbar's court); Hindustani ragas such as *Miyan ki Malhar*; Aurangzeb's reign actually produced many Persian works on music.

## Bihar under the Mughals

> **Bihar connection:**
> - **Battles in Bihar**: Ghaghra (1529, Babur), **Surajgarh (1534)** and **Chausa (1539)** — Sher Shah.
> - **Sasaram** — Sher Shah's tomb and the tombs of his father and son.
> - **Patna** — Sher Shah's fort (1541); capital of **Suba Bihar** under Akbar; renamed **Azimabad** by Prince **Azim-us-Shan** (Aurangzeb's grandson, governor from 1703).
> - **Sikh link**: **Guru Tegh Bahadur** visited Patna; **Guru Gobind Singh was born at Patna in 1666** — **Takht Sri Harmandir Sahib (Patna Sahib)** is one of the five Takhts of Sikhism.
> - **Trade**: Patna was a centre of **saltpetre** (for gunpowder), cotton, sugar and opium trade; the **English** opened a factory at Patna (1620) and the **Dutch** too.
> - **Maner** — the **Chhoti Dargah** (tomb of Shah Daulat, built 1616 under Jahangir by Ibrahim Khan) — a fine Mughal monument.
> - **Rohtasgarh** — Man Singh's palace (1597).
>
> More detail: [Medieval Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/medieval-bihar). Mughal buildings and painting are compared with other periods in [Indian Art, Architecture and Literature](/interview/bpsc-2027/history/indian-art-architecture-and-literature).

## Decline of the Mughal Empire (after 1707)

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Aurangzeb's policies | Long Deccan wars drained money; religious policy alienated Rajputs, Sikhs, Jats, Marathas |
| Wars of succession | No fixed rule of succession (Bahadur Shah I, Jahandar Shah, Farrukhsiyar…) |
| Weak later emperors | Controlled by nobles — **Sayyid brothers** ("king-makers", 1713–20) |
| **Jagirdari crisis** | Too many mansabdars, too few good jagirs (*be-jagiri*) |
| Rise of regional powers | Marathas, Sikhs, Jats; **Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad** became independent in practice |
| Foreign invasions | **Nadir Shah (1739)** — sacked Delhi, took the Peacock Throne and Kohinoor; **Ahmad Shah Abdali** (several raids; Third Panipat 1761) |
| Europeans | The East India Company's rise (see the next part) |

### Mains questions

**1. "Sher Shah Suri was the forerunner of Akbar." Examine with reference to his administrative reforms.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Afghan ruler from Sasaram; ruled 1540–45; source Abbas Khan Sarwani.
- Land revenue: measurement, three classes, rai, 1/3 share, patta–qabuliyat; Todar Mal later refined it into zabti.
- Currency: rupiya, dam — continued by Mughals and British.
- Roads, sarais, dak chowkis — GT Road.
- Administration: sarkar–pargana officers; village responsibility for crime.
- Army: dagh and chehra; standing army.
- Justice and trade (two-point customs).
- Limits: short reign, no lasting dynasty.
- *Conclusion:* Akbar's empire stood on Sher Shah's foundations; Bihar's pride.

**2. Discuss the main features of Akbar's mansabdari and land revenue systems.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Akbar's aim — a centralised, loyal nobility and stable revenue.
- Mansabdari: ranks, zat and sawar, cash or jagir, transfer, Mir Bakhshi.
- Advantages: merit, loyalty, mixed nobility (Rajputs, Iranis, Turanis, Indian Muslims).
- Zabti/dahsala: measurement, 10-year averages, cash, 1/3.
- Land classes: polaj, parauti, chachar, banjar; other methods (batai, kankut, nasaq).
- Officials: diwan, amalguzar, qanungo, patwari.
- Later problems: jagirdari crisis.
- *Conclusion:* Strong base that kept the empire stable for a century.

**3. Explain Akbar's religious policy and the idea of Sulh-i-kul.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Background — Bhakti, Sufi ideas, Rajput alliances.
- Steps: pilgrimage tax (1563), jaziya (1564) abolished; Ibadat Khana (1575); Mahzar (1579); Din-i-Ilahi (1582).
- Translations (Razmnama), festivals, marriages.
- Criticism: Badauni.
- *Conclusion:* A policy of state neutrality and tolerance; contrast with Aurangzeb.

**4. Assess the causes of the decline of the Mughal Empire.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Rapid decline after 1707.
- Aurangzeb's Deccan wars and religious policy.
- Wars of succession, weak rulers, Sayyid brothers.
- Jagirdari crisis and financial strain.
- Regional states and revolts.
- Invasions: Nadir Shah, Abdali.
- Rise of the English.
- *Conclusion:* Structural and political causes together.
