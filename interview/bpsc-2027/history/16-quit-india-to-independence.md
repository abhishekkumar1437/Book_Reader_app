This chapter covers 1939–1947, the final and most dramatic phase of the freedom struggle. For BPSC, **Quit India in Bihar** is a top topic: the **Patna Secretariat firing of 11 August 1942**, Jayaprakash Narayan's escape from Hazaribagh jail, and the near-collapse of British control in large parts of north Bihar. Prelims asks dates and plans (Cripps, Wavell, Cabinet Mission, Mountbatten). Mains asks "Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement" — which you must be ready to write.

> **In simple words:** When World War II began, Britain dragged India into it without asking. Congress ministries resigned. Britain made offers (August Offer, Cripps) but would not give real power. In August 1942 Gandhi gave the call "**Quit India**" and "**Do or Die**". Leaders were jailed at once, so ordinary people — students, peasants, workers — led a huge, partly violent uprising. Bihar was one of its strongest centres. After the war, the INA trials, the naval mutiny and Britain's weakness made independence certain. But communal politics led to **Partition**, and India became free on **15 August 1947**.

## World War II and Indian politics (1939–41)

- **3 September 1939**: Viceroy **Linlithgow** declared India at war with Germany **without consulting** Indian leaders.
- Congress asked Britain to state its war aims and promise independence. Britain refused.
- **Congress ministries resigned (October–November 1939)**. The Muslim League observed **"Deliverance Day" (22 December 1939)**.
- **Ramgarh session (March 1940)**, president **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** — Ramgarh was then in Bihar (now Jharkhand). Subhas Bose and Swami Sahajanand held an **Anti-Compromise Conference** at Ramgarh at the same time.
- **Lahore Resolution of the Muslim League (23 March 1940)** — demanded separate "independent states" for Muslim-majority areas (later called the **Pakistan Resolution**). The name "Pakistan" had been coined earlier by **Choudhry Rahmat Ali** (1933).

### August Offer (8 August 1940)

- By **Linlithgow**: **Dominion Status** as the goal (no date); expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council with more Indians; a **War Advisory Council**; after the war, a body of Indians to frame the constitution; **no constitution without the consent of minorities** (a veto to the League).
- **Rejected** by Congress. The League welcomed the veto part.

### Individual Satyagraha (October 1940–1941)

- Gandhi chose **individual satyagrahis** to make anti-war speeches — to assert **freedom of speech** without disturbing the war effort by mass action.
- **First satyagrahi: Acharya Vinoba Bhave (17 October 1940)**; **second: Jawaharlal Nehru**; third: Brahma Dutt.
- Also called the "**Delhi Chalo**" movement because satyagrahis were to march towards Delhi.
- About 25,000 were jailed. It was suspended in December 1941.

## Cripps Mission (March–April 1942)

- Japan was advancing in South-East Asia (Singapore fell in February 1942; Rangoon in March). Britain needed Indian support; the USA (Roosevelt) and China pressed Britain.
- **Sir Stafford Cripps**, a Labour member of Churchill's War Cabinet, came in **March 1942**.
- **Proposals:**
  - **Dominion Status** after the war, with the right to leave the Commonwealth.
  - A **Constituent Assembly** after the war — members elected by provincial assemblies and nominated by princes.
  - **Any province could stay out** of the new union and make its own constitution (opened the door to Partition).
  - During the war, defence would stay under British control.
- **Rejected** by Congress (no real power now; provinces could leave; princes' nominees) and by the League (no clear Pakistan).
- Gandhi called it "**a post-dated cheque**" ("on a crashing bank" is often added).

## The Quit India Movement (1942) ⭐⭐

### Why

- Failure of the Cripps Mission.
- Rising prices and war shortages; fear of a Japanese invasion and of British "scorched earth" policy (as in Burma and Malaya).
- Racist treatment of Indian refugees from Burma and Malaya.
- Belief that British power was weakening; a mood of "now or never".

### The resolution

- The Congress Working Committee met at **Wardha (14 July 1942)** and approved the idea.
- The **All-India Congress Committee** passed the **Quit India Resolution** at **Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay, on 8 August 1942** (now **August Kranti Maidan**).
- Gandhi's speech: "**Do or Die**" — "We shall either free India or die in the attempt."
- Instructions to groups: government servants should declare allegiance to Congress; soldiers should not fire on their own people; students could leave studies; peasants should refuse land revenue if zamindars were anti-government.

### The British response

- In the early hours of **9 August 1942** ("**Operation Zero Hour**"), **Gandhi and all top leaders were arrested**. Gandhi was kept at the **Aga Khan Palace, Pune** (where **Kasturba Gandhi** and **Mahadev Desai** died during the detention); members of the Working Committee at **Ahmednagar Fort**.
- **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the tricolour at Gowalia Tank on 9 August — called the "**Grand Old Lady**" (or heroine) of 1942.
- **Usha Mehta** ran the underground **Congress Radio** from Bombay.
- Underground leaders: **Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Achyut Patwardhan, Sucheta Kripalani, Biju Patnaik, R.P. Goenka**.

### Course of the movement

1. **Phase 1 (first week)**: strikes, hartals, demonstrations in cities; **students** led.
2. **Phase 2 (from mid-August)**: movement spread to villages, especially **Bihar, eastern UP, Bengal (Midnapore), Maharashtra, Orissa, Karnataka**. Attacks on **railway lines, stations, telegraph wires, police stations, post offices** — symbols of British power.
3. **Phase 3**: underground activity and **parallel governments**.

### Parallel governments

| Place | Period | Leader / name |
|---|---|---|
| **Ballia** (eastern UP) | August 1942 (a week) | **Chittu Pandey** |
| **Tamluk** (Midnapore, Bengal) | Dec 1942–Sept 1944 | **Jatiya Sarkar**; Vidyut Vahini (army) |
| **Satara** (Maharashtra) | 1943–1945 (longest) | **Prati Sarkar** — Y.B. Chavan, **Nana Patil** |
| Talcher (Orissa) | 1942 | — |

### Repression and end

- **Over 60,000** arrests (commonly given), police and military firing, **collective fines** on villages, whipping; in some places in Bihar and Bengal **aircraft were used to fire on crowds**.
- Gandhi fasted for **21 days (February 1943)** in detention against the government blaming Congress for the violence.
- By late 1943 the mass phase was crushed; underground work went on. Gandhi was released in **May 1944** on health grounds.

> **Prelims angle:** 8 August 1942 = resolution (Gowalia Tank). 9 August = arrests (Operation Zero Hour) — now observed as **August Kranti Divas**. Aga Khan Palace = Gandhi's detention. Communists (CPI) **opposed** the movement (they backed the "People's War" after Germany attacked the USSR); the Muslim League and Hindu Mahasabha also stayed away.

## Bihar in the Quit India Movement ⭐⭐

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar, with eastern UP, saw the most intense uprising of 1942. Read with [Bihar in the Freedom Struggle](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/bihar-in-the-freedom-struggle).

### The Patna Secretariat martyrs, 11 August 1942

- On **11 August 1942**, students of Patna marched to the **Secretariat** (seat of government) to **hoist the tricolour** on it.
- The **District Magistrate of Patna, W.G. Archer**, ordered the police to fire. **Seven students were killed**; several others were wounded. One of them is said to have managed to plant the flag.
- **The seven martyrs** (as commonly listed):
  1. **Umakant Prasad Sinha** (Raman ji)
  2. **Ramanand Singh**
  3. **Satish Prasad Jha**
  4. **Jagatpati Kumar**
  5. **Devipada Choudhary**
  6. **Rajendra Singh**
  7. **Ramgovind Singh**
- Most were **school students** (six of them high school students, one a college student); the youngest was about 15.
- **Shaheed Smarak (Martyrs' Memorial)**: a bronze sculpture of the seven youths carrying the flag, made by sculptor **Devi Prasad Roy Chowdhury**, stands in front of the old Secretariat. Its **foundation was laid on 15 August 1947** and it was unveiled by **Dr Rajendra Prasad in 1956**.

> **Remember:** **W.G. Archer**, the official who ordered the firing, later became famous as a writer on Indian art — he wrote on **Mithila (Madhubani) painting**, which he had seen after the 1934 earthquake. BPSC can link these two facts.

### Spread across Bihar

- After 11 August the movement spread to almost every district. Students, peasants and workers attacked **railway stations, lines, telegraph wires, post offices and police stations**. For several days the **railway and telegraph links of north Bihar were cut** and British authority collapsed in many areas.
- Strong centres: **Patna, Saran (Chhapra), Siwan, Muzaffarpur, Sitamarhi, Champaran, Darbhanga, Monghyr (Munger), Bhagalpur, Purnea, Shahabad, Gaya**, and the Chotanagpur region.
- **Siwan**: the story of **Phulendu Babu**, shot while leading a march to the police station, and his wife **Tara Rani Srivastava**, who bandaged him and **carried the flag forward** to the police station — a symbol of women's courage in 1942.
- Students of **Bhagalpur, Patna and Muzaffarpur** colleges led local actions; women came out in large numbers.
- The government used troops, collective fines and air attacks (machine-gunning from aircraft) in some places; many villages were burnt.

### Jayaprakash Narayan's escape and the Azad Dasta

- **JP** was in **Hazaribagh Central Jail** (arrested earlier for anti-war activity).
- On **Diwali night, 9 November 1942**, he escaped by climbing the jail wall with five companions: **Yogendra Shukla, Suraj Narayan Singh, Gulab Chand Gupta, Ramnandan Mishra and Shaligram Singh**.
- He organised the **Azad Dasta** (freedom squads) for **guerrilla action**, with a training base in the **Nepal terai**. When JP and Ram Manohar Lohia were arrested in Nepal (1943), Azad Dasta members **freed them from the Hanuman Nagar jail** (Nepal).
- JP was re-arrested in **1943** (in a train near Amritsar) and tortured in **Lahore Fort**.
- This made JP a **national hero** of 1942.

> **Mains angle:** For a Bihar–Quit India answer, use five headings: (1) Patna Secretariat firing (11 Aug); (2) mass uprising in districts — railways/telegraph cut, parallel authority; (3) underground and guerrilla action — JP's escape (9 Nov), Azad Dasta; (4) women and students (Tara Rani Srivastava; students as martyrs); (5) British repression — firing, aircraft, collective fines. Conclude: Bihar turned "Do or Die" into a people's revolt.

## Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA ⭐

- **Bose**: Congress president at **Haripura (1938)** and **Tripuri (1939)**; resigned after differences with Gandhi; formed the **Forward Bloc (May 1939)**.
- Under house arrest in Calcutta, he **escaped in January 1941** (disguised as a Pathan, "Ziauddin"), went via Kabul to **Moscow** and then **Berlin**. He formed the **Free India Centre** and the **Indian Legion** (from Indian prisoners of war) in Germany; started **Azad Hind Radio**. Germans called him "Netaji".
- **Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj)**:
  - Idea and first INA: **Captain Mohan Singh** (with Japanese help, from Indian POWs, **1942**) and **Rash Behari Bose** (**Indian Independence League**, Tokyo/Bangkok, 1942).
  - Subhas reached **Singapore** by submarine and took over in **July 1943**.
  - **21 October 1943**: formed the **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind)** in Singapore; it declared war on Britain and the USA.
  - Japan handed over the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**, renamed **Shaheed** and **Swaraj** (Bose hoisted the flag at Port Blair, **30 December 1943**).
  - Brigades named **Gandhi, Nehru, Azad** and **Subhas**; women's regiment — **Rani Jhansi Regiment**, led by **Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan (Sahgal)**.
  - Slogans: "**Delhi Chalo**", "**Jai Hind**", "**Give me blood, and I will give you freedom**" ("Tum mujhe khoon do, main tumhe azadi dunga").
  - Bose called Gandhi the "**Father of the Nation**" in a radio broadcast from Singapore (**6 July 1944**).
  - INA fought with the Japanese in the **Imphal–Kohima campaign (1944)**; it reached Moirang (Manipur) where the tricolour was hoisted (April 1944). Failed because of Japanese defeat, monsoon, supply problems.
  - Bose reportedly died in an **air crash at Taipei (Taihoku), 18 August 1945** (this is officially accepted but still debated).

### INA trials (Red Fort, November 1945)

- Three officers — **Prem Kumar Sahgal** (Hindu), **Shah Nawaz Khan** (Muslim) and **Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** (Sikh) — were tried together, which made the trial a symbol of national unity.
- Defence lawyers: **Bhulabhai Desai** (lead), **Tej Bahadur Sapru, Jawaharlal Nehru (wore his barrister's gown again), Asaf Ali, K.N. Katju**.
- Huge public protests; INA Day; the sentences were remitted by the Commander-in-Chief.

### RIN Mutiny (February 1946)

- **18 February 1946**: ratings (sailors) of the **Royal Indian Navy** on **HMIS Talwar** in Bombay went on strike against racial discrimination, bad food and the INA trials. It spread to Karachi, Calcutta, Madras and other ports; flags of Congress, League and Communists were flown together.
- **Sardar Patel and Jinnah** persuaded them to surrender (23 February). The mutiny showed Britain that **Indian armed forces could no longer be relied on**.

## From the war's end to Independence

| Event | Date | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **C. Rajagopalachari (C.R.) Formula** | 1944 | Congress–League cooperation; after the war, plebiscite in Muslim-majority areas on separation — Jinnah rejected |
| **Gandhi–Jinnah talks** | September 1944 | Failed |
| Desai–Liaqat Pact | Jan 1945 | Plan for an interim government (not accepted) |
| **Wavell Plan / Simla Conference** | **June–July 1945** | Executive Council with all members Indian except Viceroy and Commander-in-Chief; **equal Hindu and Muslim (caste Hindu) members**. **Failed** because Jinnah insisted the League alone would nominate all Muslim members |
| Labour Party wins in Britain | July 1945 | **Clement Attlee** PM — more willing to leave India |
| **Elections** (central and provincial) | Dec 1945–early 1946 | Congress swept general seats; **League won most Muslim seats** |
| **Cabinet Mission** | Arrived **24 March 1946** | **Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State), **Stafford Cripps**, **A.V. Alexander** |
| Cabinet Mission Plan | **16 May 1946** | **Rejected Pakistan**; a **Union of India** with only defence, foreign affairs, communications; provinces grouped into **Groups A** (Hindu-majority), **B** (NW Muslim-majority), **C** (Bengal & Assam); a **Constituent Assembly** elected by provincial assemblies (by community); an **interim government** |
| Constituent Assembly elections | July 1946 | Congress 208 seats, League 73 (commonly given) |
| **Direct Action Day** | **16 August 1946** | League withdrew acceptance of the plan; **"Great Calcutta Killings"** |
| **Interim Government** | **2 September 1946** | **Nehru** as Vice-President of the Executive Council; League joined **26 October 1946** (Liaquat Ali Khan — Finance) |
| Noakhali and **Bihar riots** | Oct–Nov 1946 | Gandhi walked through Noakhali; then came to **Bihar (March 1947)** |
| **Constituent Assembly first meets** | **9 December 1946** | **Sachchidananda Sinha** interim president; **Rajendra Prasad** elected permanent president on **11 December 1946**; League boycotted |
| **Attlee's statement** | **20 February 1947** | British to leave India **by June 1948** |
| **Mountbatten** arrives | 24 March 1947 | Last Viceroy |
| **Mountbatten Plan (3 June Plan)** | **3 June 1947** | **Partition** into India and Pakistan; Punjab and Bengal assemblies to vote on division; referendum in **NWFP** and **Sylhet**; boundary commission (**Cyril Radcliffe**); princely states free to join either; date advanced to **15 August 1947** |
| **Indian Independence Act** | **18 July 1947** (royal assent) | Two dominions from 15 August 1947 |
| **Independence** | **15 August 1947** | Nehru's "**Tryst with Destiny**" speech (midnight, 14–15 Aug); Mountbatten first Governor-General of free India; Jinnah GG of Pakistan |

> **Bihar connection:** In the **Interim Government (Sept 1946)**, two members were from Bihar: **Dr Rajendra Prasad** (**Food and Agriculture**) and **Jagjivan Ram** (**Labour**). The **Constituent Assembly** was opened by **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** as interim president and then led by **Dr Rajendra Prasad** as permanent president — both from Bihar. See [Making of the Constitution](/interview/bpsc-2027/polity/making-of-the-constitution).

### The 1946 riots in Bihar and Gandhi's visit

- After the Calcutta killings (August) and the **Noakhali** riots (East Bengal, October 1946), there was **severe communal violence in Bihar (late October–November 1946)**, especially in **Patna, Monghyr, Bhagalpur, Gaya and Saran** districts, against Muslims.
- Nehru and other leaders rushed to Bihar; Gandhi threatened to fast. Gandhi came to Bihar in **March 1947** and toured the affected villages for peace.
- This is a painful chapter, but BPSC Mains may ask about the communal background of Partition — mention it honestly and briefly.

### Why did Partition happen? (Mains points)

1. **British policy** of divide and rule — separate electorates (1909 onwards), Communal Award (1932).
2. Growth of **Muslim League** after 1937 and **two-nation theory**; Lahore Resolution 1940.
3. **Congress mistakes** (as argued by some historians): refusal to share power with the League in UP (1937); resignation of ministries in 1939 left the field to the League.
4. **Communal violence** of 1946–47 made living together look impossible.
5. **Britain's haste** to leave after the war; Mountbatten advanced the date.
6. Congress leaders accepted Partition to avoid civil war and to get a strong centre.

## Integration of princely states (brief)

- About **560+ princely states**. **Sardar Patel** (States Ministry) and **V.P. Menon** secured accession through the **Instrument of Accession** (defence, external affairs, communications) before 15 August 1947 for almost all.
- Problem states: **Junagadh** (plebiscite, February 1948), **Hyderabad** (police action **"Operation Polo", September 1948**), **Kashmir** (Maharaja Hari Singh signed accession on **26 October 1947** after a tribal invasion from Pakistan).

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement. (38 marks, ~300 words)** ⭐

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** 8 August 1942 "Do or Die"; with leaders jailed, the people of Bihar took the lead; Bihar and eastern UP saw the most intense rising.
- **Patna Secretariat (11 Aug):** students marched to hoist the flag; DM W.G. Archer's firing; seven martyrs; Shaheed Smarak.
- **Mass uprising:** railway lines, stations, telegraph, post offices, thanas attacked in Saran, Siwan, Muzaffarpur, Champaran, Monghyr, Bhagalpur, Purnea, Shahabad; British authority collapsed in parts of north Bihar for days.
- **Underground and guerrilla phase:** JP's escape from Hazaribagh (9 Nov 1942); Azad Dasta from the Nepal terai; rescue at Hanuman Nagar; Yogendra Shukla, Suraj Narayan Singh.
- **Women and youth:** Tara Rani Srivastava at Siwan; students as martyrs.
- **Repression:** firing, collective fines, burning of villages, aerial firing.
- **Significance:** showed mass anti-British feeling; made JP a national hero; proved that British rule could not continue without force.
- **Conclusion:** Bihar's 1942 made it a frontline province of the final struggle; the Shaheed Smarak remains its symbol.

**2. "The Quit India Movement was the final mass movement that made independence inevitable." Examine. (38 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** August 1942; context of war and Cripps failure.
- **Features:** leaderless spontaneity; students, peasants, workers, women; parallel governments (Ballia, Tamluk, Satara); underground (JP, Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta).
- **Repression:** 60,000+ arrests; it was crushed by 1943.
- **Why it still mattered:** independence placed on the immediate agenda; loyalty of Indian services and army shaken; British realised they could not rule by force after the war.
- **Followed by:** INA trials, RIN mutiny, Labour government — a chain to 1947.
- **Limits:** CPI, League and Mahasabha stayed away; violence vs Gandhian ideals.
- **Conclusion:** Quit India did not win freedom immediately, but it made freedom a matter of "when", not "if".

**3. Evaluate the contribution of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to India's freedom. (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bose's break with Gandhi; Forward Bloc; escape (1941).
- INA — Mohan Singh, Rash Behari Bose, Bose's leadership from 1943.
- Azad Hind government; Andamans; Rani Jhansi Regiment; slogans.
- Military failure in Imphal–Kohima.
- INA trials united India; impact on the armed forces (RIN mutiny).
- **Conclusion:** militarily unsuccessful, politically decisive.

**4. Why did the Cabinet Mission Plan fail, and how did this lead to Partition? (20 marks)**

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** May 1946 plan — union with weak centre; grouping; Constituent Assembly; interim govt.
- Disputes on grouping (compulsory or optional); Nehru's statement on the Assembly being sovereign.
- League withdrew; Direct Action Day; riots in Calcutta, Noakhali, Bihar.
- Interim government deadlock (League's Finance portfolio).
- Attlee's deadline; Mountbatten's 3 June Plan.
- **Conclusion:** Failure of the last plan for unity made Partition the "lesser evil" in the eyes of Congress leaders.
