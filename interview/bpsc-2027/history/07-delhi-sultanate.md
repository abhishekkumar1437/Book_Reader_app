The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) was the first long period of Turkish and Afghan rule over north India. BPSC asks direct factual questions: who started the iqta system, who introduced token currency, who built the Alai Darwaza, which Sultan brought Ashoka's pillars to Delhi, who conquered Bihar. Mains GS I occasionally uses this period for administration and Indo-Islamic architecture. For Bihar, the key figure is **Bakhtiyar Khalji** — and the destruction of **Nalanda, Vikramshila and Odantapuri**.

> **In simple words:** In the late 12th century, Muhammad Ghori of Afghanistan defeated the Rajput kings. His slave-general Qutbuddin Aibak became the first Sultan of Delhi in 1206. Five dynasties ruled from Delhi over 320 years. Some Sultans, like Alauddin Khalji, built huge armies and controlled market prices; Muhammad bin Tughlaq tried bold but failed experiments; Firoz Shah Tughlaq built canals and towns. The Sultanate brought new administration (iqta), new architecture (true arch and dome), Persian learning, and a meeting of cultures. It ended when Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat in 1526.

## The Turkish conquest

### Muhammad Ghori (Muizzuddin Muhammad bin Sam)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1175 | Took **Multan** |
| 1178 | Defeated by **Bhima II** (Solanki) of Gujarat near Mount Abu (his first defeat in India) |
| **1191** | **First Battle of Tarain** — **Prithviraj Chauhan won** |
| **1192** | **Second Battle of Tarain** — **Ghori won**; Prithviraj captured and killed. This battle **opened north India to Turkish rule** |
| **1194** | **Battle of Chandawar** (near Etawah/Firozabad, UP) — defeated **Jaichand Gahadavala** of Kannauj |
| 1206 | Ghori killed by the **Khokhars** (Punjab tribe) |

His general **Qutbuddin Aibak** captured Delhi (1193) and much of north India.

> **Bihar connection:** **Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji**, a Turkish commander, conquered **Bihar** and **Bengal** without direct help from Ghori:
> - Around **1193** (dates in sources vary from 1193 to 1203) he attacked **Odantapuri** (Bihar Sharif), which he mistook for a fort, and destroyed **Nalanda** and **Vikramshila**. According to **Minhaj-us-Siraj** (*Tabaqat-i-Nasiri*), the monks were killed and the libraries burnt.
> - The region came to be called **"Bihar"** after the many *viharas* (monasteries); **Bihar Sharif** became the early Muslim capital.
> - About **1204–05** he took **Nadia** (Bengal) from **Lakshmanasena** with only 18 horsemen in the first rush (Minhaj's account) and made **Lakhnauti** his capital.
> - He died after a failed campaign to **Tibet/Assam** (c. 1206).
>
> In exams, the commonly given answer is that Bakhtiyar Khalji destroyed **Nalanda around 1193**.

> **Memory trick:** **"My Kind Teacher Seems Lazy"** — **M**amluk, **K**halji, **T**ughlaq, **S**ayyid, **L**odi.

### 1. Mamluk / Slave / Ilbari dynasty (1206–1290)

"Mamluk" means a slave (military slave). The rulers were Turkish slaves or their descendants. The name **Ilbari** comes from the Turkish tribe of Iltutmish and Balban.

| Ruler | Years | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Qutbuddin Aibak** | 1206–1210 | Slave of Ghori; capital **Lahore**; called **Lakh Bakhsh** ("giver of lakhs") for his generosity; began the **Qutb Minar** (named after Sufi **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**); built **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque** (Delhi) and **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer); died after falling from a horse while playing **chaugan** (polo) at Lahore |
| Aram Shah | 1210 | Weak; removed |
| **Iltutmish** | 1211–1236 | **Real founder** of the Sultanate; made **Delhi the capital**; organised the **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** ("the Forty" — a group of Turkish nobles); introduced the **iqta system**; issued the **silver tanka** and **copper jital** (Arabic coinage); received **investiture from the Abbasid Caliph (1229)** — legal recognition; avoided **Chengiz Khan** (1221) by refusing shelter to **Jalaluddin Mangbarni**; **completed the Qutb Minar**; brought Bihar and Bengal under Delhi |
| **Razia Sultan** | 1236–1240 | **First and only woman ruler** of Delhi; nominated by Iltutmish; discarded the veil, rode elephants; favoured the Abyssinian **Jamaluddin Yaqut**, which angered the Forty; married **Altunia** of Bhatinda; killed near **Kaithal** in 1240 |
| Bahram Shah, Masud Shah | 1240–1246 | Weak, controlled by the Forty |
| **Nasiruddin Mahmud** | 1246–1266 | Pious, simple (said to copy the Quran for a living); real power with **Balban**; **Minhaj-us-Siraj** dedicated ***Tabaqat-i-Nasiri*** to him |
| **Ghiyasuddin Balban** | 1266–1287 | **"Blood and iron" policy**; **destroyed the Chahalgani**; theory of kingship — **Zil-i-Ilahi** ("shadow of God on earth"), *niyabat-i-khudai*; court rituals **sijda** (prostration) and **paibos** (kissing the feet); started the Persian festival **Nauroz**; created **Diwan-i-Arz** (army department); strong spy system (**barid**); crushed **Tughril Khan's** revolt in Bengal; defended against the Mongols (his son Muhammad died fighting them) |
| Kaiqubad, Kayumars | 1287–1290 | Weak; ended by the Khaljis |

### 2. Khalji dynasty (1290–1320)

The Khalji revolution (1290) ended the monopoly of the Turkish nobility; power went to a wider group including Indian Muslims.

| Ruler | Years | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Jalaluddin Firoz Khalji** | 1290–1296 | Founder; elderly and mild; killed by his nephew **Alauddin** at **Kara** (1296) |
| **Alauddin Khalji** | 1296–1316 | See below |
| Qutbuddin Mubarak Shah | 1316–1320 | Killed by **Khusrau Khan** (a convert), who ruled briefly; Ghazi Malik (Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq) overthrew him |

#### Alauddin Khalji — the most-asked Sultan

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Title** | **Sikandar-i-Sani** (second Alexander) on his coins; dreamed of a new religion (stopped by Ala-ul-Mulk) |
| **Army** | First Sultan to keep a **large standing army** paid in **cash**; **dagh** (branding of horses) and **huliya / chehra** (descriptive roll of soldiers) to stop fraud |
| **Market reforms** | **Price control** of grain, cloth, horses, slaves and cattle in Delhi; four separate markets; officials — **Shahna-i-Mandi** (market superintendent), **Diwan-i-Riyasat** (minister of trade), **barid** and **munhiyan** (spies); government granaries; strict punishment for under-weighing. Aim: pay soldiers low salaries and still keep them content (Barani) |
| **Land revenue** | **First Sultan to measure land** (unit **biswa**) — revenue at **50% of produce** (*kharaj*); also **charai** (grazing tax) and **ghari** (house tax); cut the privileges of the **khuts, muqaddams and chaudhris** (rural chiefs) |
| **Mongols** | Repelled several invasions (Kili 1299, etc.) |
| **North conquests** | **Gujarat (1299)** — got **Malik Kafur**; **Ranthambore (1301)** — **Hammir Deva**; **Chittor (1303)** — **Rana Ratan Singh** (the Padmini legend comes from ***Padmavat*** by **Malik Muhammad Jayasi**, written in **1540** — not historical evidence); **Malwa (1305)**; Siwana, Jalor |
| **South campaigns (Malik Kafur)** | **Devagiri** (Yadava **Ramachandradeva**, 1307), **Warangal** (Kakatiya **Prataparudra II**, 1309–10), **Dwarasamudra** (Hoysala **Ballala III**, 1311), **Madurai** (Pandyas, 1311) — kingdoms made tributaries, huge wealth brought |
| **Buildings** | **Siri fort** (new capital), **Alai Darwaza** (1311 — gateway to Qutb complex; **first true dome and true arch built on scientific lines**), **Hauz-i-Alai (Hauz Khas)**, Alai Minar (unfinished) |
| **Court** | **Amir Khusrau** (*Khazain-ul-Futuh*), **Amir Hasan Dehlavi** |

### 3. Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1414)

| Ruler | Years | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** (Ghazi Malik) | 1320–1325 | Founder; built **Tughlaqabad** fort; mild revenue policy (no more than 1/10–1/11 increase a year); **canals** for irrigation; conquered **Warangal** (1323) and **Tirhut (Mithila, 1324)** — ended the Karnata dynasty; conflict with Sufi **Nizamuddin Auliya** ("**Hunuz Dilli dur ast**" — "Delhi is still far"); died when a wooden pavilion collapsed at **Afghanpur** (1325) |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (Jauna Khan) | 1325–1351 | See below |
| **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** | 1351–1388 | See below |
| Later Tughlaqs | 1388–1414 | Weak; **Timur's invasion (1398)** during **Nasiruddin Mahmud Tughlaq's** reign — Delhi sacked |

#### Muhammad bin Tughlaq — five experiments

| Experiment | Year | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Taxation in the Doab** | c. 1326–27 | Raised taxes in the Ganga–Yamuna doab during a famine → peasant revolt; later set up **Diwan-i-Kohi** (department of agriculture) to extend cultivation with loans (*taqavi*) |
| **Transfer of capital** | **1327** | From **Delhi to Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad)** in Maharashtra; people forced to move; later allowed to return |
| **Token currency** | **1329–30** | **Copper/bronze coins** at the value of silver tanka; massive forging ("every house became a mint") → withdrawn; government bought back fake coins with gold/silver |
| **Khurasan expedition** | Planned | Army raised and then disbanded without fighting |
| **Qarachil expedition** | c. 1333 | Campaign into the Kumaon–Garhwal hills; army destroyed |

Other facts: the **most learned Sultan** (philosophy, mathematics, medicine, calligraphy); **Ibn Battuta** (Moroccan traveller) arrived **1333**, was made **qazi of Delhi**, and was sent as ambassador to **China (1342)**; wrote ***Rihla***. Many revolts — **Vijayanagar (1336)** and the **Bahmani kingdom (1347)** were founded in his reign. He died at **Thatta** (Sindh) in 1351 while chasing a rebel. Barani's comment: "The Sultan was freed from his people and they from their Sultan."

#### Firoz Shah Tughlaq — welfare and orthodoxy

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Taxes | Collected only **four taxes allowed by Sharia** — **kharaj** (land tax), **khams** (1/5 of war booty), **jaziya** (on non-Muslims; he **extended it to Brahmins**), **zakat** (on Muslims); added an irrigation tax (*haq-i-shurb*, 1/10) |
| Irrigation | Built **canals** from the **Yamuna** and **Sutlej** (Western Yamuna Canal is linked to his) |
| New departments | **Diwan-i-Khairat** (charity — marriage of poor girls), **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (slaves — he had about 1,80,000), **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** (pensions), **Dar-ul-Shifa** (free hospital), an **employment bureau** |
| Towns | **Firozabad** (Firoz Shah Kotla, Delhi), **Fatehabad**, **Hisar**, **Jaunpur** (named after Jauna Khan, i.e. Muhammad bin Tughlaq) |
| Ashokan pillars | Brought the **Topra** and **Meerut** pillars to Delhi |
| Books | Autobiography ***Fatuhat-i-Firozshahi***; ordered translation of Sanskrit texts (e.g. *Dalail-i-Firozshahi*); repaired the Qutb Minar (added a storey after lightning) |
| Weakness | Made **iqtas and army posts hereditary** → weakened the state |

### 4. Sayyid dynasty (1414–1451)

**Khizr Khan** (Timur's governor of Multan) founded it; he did not take the title of Sultan (called himself *Rayat-i-Ala*). **Mubarak Shah** (Yahya bin Ahmad Sirhindi wrote *Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi*), **Muhammad Shah**, **Alam Shah** (retired to Badaun, gave Delhi to Bahlul Lodi).

### 5. Lodi dynasty (1451–1526) — first Afghan rulers

| Ruler | Years | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahlul Lodi** | 1451–1489 | Founder; treated Afghan nobles as equals; **conquered Jaunpur** (Sharqi kingdom); issued the **Bahloli** copper coin |
| **Sikandar Lodi** | 1489–1517 | Greatest Lodi; **founded Agra (1504)** and made it the capital; introduced the **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (measuring yard of 32 digits, used till Akbar); wrote Persian poetry under the pen-name **Gulrukhi**; **conquered Bihar** (defeated **Husain Shah Sharqi**); religious orthodoxy (destroyed temples at Mathura) |
| **Ibrahim Lodi** | 1517–1526 | Quarrelled with nobles; **Daulat Khan Lodi** (Punjab) and **Alam Khan** invited **Babur**; killed at the **First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526)** — the only Sultan to die on the battlefield |

## Administration of the Sultanate

### Theory of kingship

- The Sultan claimed to rule on behalf of the **Abbasid Caliph** (Iltutmish got investiture in 1229); **Alauddin** ignored the ulema in state matters ("I do what is good for the state"); **Balban** stressed divine kingship.
- The **ulema** (Islamic scholars) advised on law (*Sharia*); the Sultan also made his own laws (*zawabit*).

### Central departments

| Department | Head | Work |
|---|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Wizarat** | **Wazir** | Finance and revenue (chief minister) |
| **Diwan-i-Arz** | **Ariz-i-Mumalik** | **Army** — recruitment, inspection (started by **Balban**) |
| **Diwan-i-Insha** | Dabir-i-Mumalik | Royal correspondence |
| **Diwan-i-Risalat** | — | Religious matters / appeals (some say foreign affairs) |
| **Diwan-i-Qaza** | **Qazi-ul-Quzat** | Justice |
| Sadr-us-Sudur | — | Religious endowments and Islamic law |
| **Barid-i-Mumalik** | — | Intelligence and posts |
| Diwan-i-Mustakhraj | — | Arrears of revenue (Alauddin) |
| **Diwan-i-Kohi** | — | Agriculture (Muhammad bin Tughlaq) |

### Iqta system

- **Iqta** = a territory whose revenue was assigned to a noble (**muqti** or **iqtadar**) in place of salary. He kept troops and maintained order.
- Introduced in India by **Iltutmish**; strictly controlled (and transferred) by Balban and Alauddin; made **hereditary by Firoz Shah** — a cause of decline.

### Provinces and local units

**Iqta / province** (muqti, wali) → **shiq** (district, under **shiqdar**) → **pargana** (under **amil**; with **qanungo** keeping records) → **village** (headmen **muqaddam/khut/chaudhri** and the accountant **patwari**).

### Taxes

| Tax | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kharaj** | Land tax (1/3 to 1/2 of produce) |
| **Ushr** | Land tax on Muslims' land (5–10%) |
| **Jaziya** | Tax on non-Muslims (exemption from military service) |
| **Zakat** | 2.5% religious tax on Muslims' wealth |
| **Khams** | Share of war booty — 1/5 to the state by Sharia (many Sultans took 4/5; Firoz restored 1/5) |

## Economy and society

- New crafts: **paper**, **spinning wheel (charkha)**, carding bow, **Persian wheel (rahat)** for irrigation — spread in this period.
- Towns grew: Delhi, Lahore, Multan, Jaunpur, Lakhnauti, Cambay.
- Coins: silver **tanka** and copper **jital** (Iltutmish); Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token coins; the Bahloli (Lodis).
- Society: Turkish and Afghan nobles, Indian Muslims, Hindu chiefs (*rais, ranas*); slaves (*bandagan*); women in purdah among elites; the growth of **Bhakti and Sufi** movements (next chapters).

## Architecture — Indo-Islamic style

New features: the **true arch** (built with voussoirs and keystone), the **dome**, **lime mortar**, **minarets**, decoration by **calligraphy**, **arabesque** (geometric and floral patterns), and **jali** work. Indian craftsmen added lotus, bell and kalash motifs.

| Building | Ruler | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque** (Delhi) | Aibak | First mosque in Delhi; built with temple material |
| **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer) | Aibak | Mosque (legend: built in two and a half days) |
| **Qutb Minar** (Delhi) | Begun by **Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish**; repaired by **Firoz** (and later by Sikandar Lodi) | About 72.5 m; red and buff sandstone; **UNESCO 1993** (Qutb complex) |
| **Sultan Ghari** (Delhi) | Iltutmish | **First Islamic tomb** in India (for his son Nasiruddin Mahmud) |
| **Balban's tomb** | Balban | **First true arch** in India |
| **Alai Darwaza** | **Alauddin** (1311) | **First true dome** on scientific lines; red sandstone with white marble |
| **Tughlaqabad** & Ghiyasuddin's tomb | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | **Sloping (battered) walls**, simple, massive |
| Firoz Shah Kotla, Hauz Khas madrasa | Firoz Shah | Plain style |
| **Lodi tombs** (Lodi Garden) | Sayyids & Lodis | **Octagonal tombs**; **double dome** (Sikandar Lodi's tomb) |

> **Prelims angle:** "First true arch" → **Balban's tomb**; "first true dome" → **Alai Darwaza**; "first Islamic tomb" → **Sultan Ghari**; "double dome" → **Sikandar Lodi's tomb**; "sloping walls" → **Tughlaq** buildings.

## Bihar under the Sultanate

> **Bihar connection:**
> - **Bakhtiyar Khalji** (c. 1193–1205) — conquest of south Bihar; destruction of Odantapuri, Nalanda, Vikramshila; capital **Bihar Sharif**.
> - **Iltutmish** brought Bihar under Delhi (c. 1225); Bihar was later administered from Lakhnauti (Bengal) or Delhi depending on power.
> - **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** conquered **Tirhut (Mithila) in 1324**, ending the **Karnata dynasty** (Harisimhadeva fled to Nepal). Mithila was then ruled by the Brahmin **Oinwara (Kameshwar) dynasty** as feudatories — patron of the poet **Vidyapati** (see Chapter 9).
> - **Firoz Shah Tughlaq's** governor **Malik Ibrahim Baya** — his **tomb at Bihar Sharif (1353)** stands on a hill (Pir Pahari).
> - Sufi saint **Makhdum Sharfuddin Ahmad Yahya Maneri** (1263–1381) of the **Firdausi** order lived at **Bihar Sharif**; his letters ***Maktubat-i-Sadi*** are famous. **Maner** (Patna district) has the tombs of his family (later, the **Maner Sharif** dargah of Shah Daulat, built in Jahangir's time).
> - In the 15th century, parts of Bihar were under the **Sharqis of Jaunpur** and the **Bengal Sultans**; **Sikandar Lodi** took Bihar from the Sharqis (c. 1495). The Afghan **Lohani** chiefs then ruled at Bihar — the stage for **Sher Khan (Sher Shah)**, covered in the next chapter. Read [Medieval Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/medieval-bihar) alongside this part.

## Decline of the Sultanate

- Weak successors after Firoz; **hereditary iqtas** and slaves' interference.
- **Timur's invasion (1398)** shattered Delhi.
- Rise of regional kingdoms: **Bengal, Jaunpur (Sharqi), Malwa, Gujarat, Bahmani, Vijayanagar**, Mewar.
- Afghan nobles' independence under the Lodis; Ibrahim Lodi's conflict with them.
- Babur's superior tactics (artillery, *tulughma*) at **Panipat, 1526**.

> **Mains angle:** "Evaluate the administrative and economic reforms of Alauddin Khalji" or "Muhammad bin Tughlaq was a man of ideas but failed in execution — discuss" are typical. For Muhammad bin Tughlaq: the ideas were sound (central capital, token money is used today, agricultural department) but timing, preparation and public trust were missing. Use Barani and Ibn Battuta as sources.

### Mains questions

**1. Critically examine the market control policy and other reforms of Alauddin Khalji.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* 1296–1316; Mongol threat and need for a large army.
- Standing cash-paid army; dagh and huliya.
- Market regulations: four markets, Shahna-i-Mandi, Diwan-i-Riyasat, fixed prices, granaries, spies, punishments.
- Revenue: land measurement, 50% kharaj, charai, ghari; curbing khuts and muqaddams.
- Results: prices stable, Mongols repelled, southern conquests.
- Limits: confined to Delhi; hard on peasants and traders; collapsed after his death.
- *Conclusion:* Bold state control, unusual for medieval times.

**2. "Muhammad bin Tughlaq was a mixture of opposites." Discuss with reference to his experiments.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* 1325–51; most learned Sultan; sources Barani, Ibn Battuta.
- Doab taxation; Diwan-i-Kohi and taqavi.
- Transfer of capital to Daulatabad.
- Token currency.
- Khurasan and Qarachil expeditions.
- Positives: vision, learning, religious tolerance (honoured Jain monks), efforts for agriculture.
- Negatives: poor execution, harshness, revolts, rise of Vijayanagar and Bahmani.
- *Conclusion:* Ideas ahead of time; failures of planning and trust.

**3. Discuss the impact of the Turkish conquest on Bihar.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Bakhtiyar Khalji's campaigns c. 1193–1205.
- Destruction of Nalanda, Vikramshila, Odantapuri; decline of Buddhism.
- New capital at Bihar Sharif; name "Bihar".
- Administration under Delhi/Lakhnauti; Tughlaq conquest of Tirhut (1324).
- Sufi centres — Maneri at Bihar Sharif, Maner; Indo-Islamic buildings (Ibrahim Baya's tomb).
- Mithila's culture continued under Oinwaras (Vidyapati).
- *Conclusion:* Loss of Buddhist learning but birth of a composite Indo-Islamic culture.
