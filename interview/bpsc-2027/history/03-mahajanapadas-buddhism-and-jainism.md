This is the most important ancient-history chapter for a Bihar candidate. The sixth century BCE is when **Magadha** (south Bihar) began its rise to become India's first empire, when **Vaishali** ran one of the world's earliest republics, and when the **Buddha** (enlightened at Bodh Gaya) and **Mahavira** (born near Vaishali, died at Pawapuri) taught. BPSC asks about these every year: rulers and their deeds, Buddhist councils, birth and death places, Jain sects, and why Magadha rose.

> **In simple words:** Around 600 BCE, north India had sixteen big states called Mahajanapadas. Some were ruled by kings, some by councils of clan leaders (republics). Magadha, in today's south Bihar, defeated the others because it had iron, fertile land, rivers, elephants, safe capitals and strong rulers. In the same period, two great teachers from the Bihar region — the Buddha and Mahavira — rejected costly Vedic rituals and the caste hierarchy and taught simple, moral paths to liberation.

## The sixth century BCE: second urbanisation

The sixth century BCE is called the period of **second urbanisation** (the first was Harappan). Key changes:

- Wide use of **iron** tools — forests of the middle Ganga plain could be cleared and farmed.
- Surplus rice production → growth of towns, trade and taxation.
- First Indian coins — **punch-marked coins** (mostly silver, called *karshapana*).
- A typical luxury pottery — **Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)**, c. 700–200 BCE.
- The **janapada** (territory where a tribe settled) grew into the **mahajanapada** ("great janapada").
- New thinkers: about **62 sects** are mentioned in Buddhist texts — among them the Buddha, Mahavira, the Ajivikas and the materialists.

## The sixteen Mahajanapadas

The list comes from the Buddhist text **Anguttara Nikaya**. (The Jain text *Bhagavati Sutra* gives a slightly different list.)

| Mahajanapada | Capital | Present region |
|---|---|---|
| **Anga** | **Champa** | Bhagalpur–Munger, **Bihar** |
| **Magadha** | **Girivraja / Rajagriha** (Rajgir), later **Pataliputra** | Patna–Gaya–Nalanda, **Bihar** |
| **Vajji (Vrijji)** | **Vaishali** | North **Bihar** (Vaishali–Muzaffarpur) |
| **Malla** | **Kushinara** (Kushinagar) and **Pava** | Deoria–Gorakhpur, UP |
| **Kashi** | Varanasi | UP |
| **Kosala** | **Shravasti** (also Ayodhya/Saketa) | Eastern UP (Awadh) |
| **Vatsa** | **Kaushambi** | Near Prayagraj, UP |
| **Chedi** | Shuktimati (Sotthivati) | Bundelkhand (MP–UP) |
| **Kuru** | Indraprastha | Delhi–Meerut–Haryana |
| **Panchala** | Ahichhatra (north), Kampilya (south) | Rohilkhand, western UP |
| **Matsya** | Viratnagar | Jaipur area, Rajasthan |
| **Shurasena** | **Mathura** | UP |
| **Avanti** | **Ujjayini** (north), Mahishmati (south) | Malwa, MP |
| **Ashmaka (Assaka)** | Potana / Potali | On the **Godavari** — the **only Mahajanapada in south India** |
| **Gandhara** | **Taxila** (Takshashila) | North-west Pakistan |
| **Kamboja** | Rajapura | North-west (Afghan border) |

> **Memory trick:** The three Bihar Mahajanapadas run west-to-east and north-to-south as **"Vajji above, Magadha below, Anga to the east"**. The four strongest rivals were **Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti** — remember **"MaKoVA"**.

> **Prelims angle:** "Which Mahajanapada lay in the south?" → **Ashmaka (Godavari)**. "Capital of Anga?" → **Champa**. "Capital of Avanti?" → **Ujjayini**. "Capital of Vatsa?" → **Kaushambi**.

### Monarchies and republics (gana-sanghas)

Most Mahajanapadas were **monarchies**. But some were **gana-sanghas** — states ruled by an assembly of clan heads (*rajas*) who met in a hall called the **santhagara**. Decisions were taken by discussion and vote (with tokens called *shalaka*).

| Republic | Capital / area |
|---|---|
| **Vajji confederacy** (8 clans, incl. **Licchavis**, Videhas, **Jnatrikas** — Mahavira's clan) | **Vaishali** |
| **Mallas** | Kushinara, Pava |
| **Shakyas** (Buddha's clan) | **Kapilavastu** |
| **Koliyas** | Ramagrama |
| **Moriyas** | Pipphalivana |
| Bhaggas, Bulis, Kalamas | Smaller clans of the Terai |

> **Bihar connection:** The **Licchavi republic of Vaishali** is often called **one of the world's earliest republics** (some say the first). Buddhist texts say it had **7,707 rajas**. The Buddha praised the Vajjis' "seven conditions of welfare" (meeting often, deciding together, respecting elders and women, honouring shrines) — told to Ajatashatru's minister Vassakara in the *Mahaparinibbana Sutta*. Vaishali was also the city of the courtesan **Amrapali**, who became a Buddhist nun. This is a favourite Mains point on "democratic traditions of ancient Bihar". For more Bihar detail, read [Ancient Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/ancient-bihar) alongside.

## The rise of Magadha

### Why Magadha won — the causes

| Factor | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Iron** | Rich iron ore near Rajgir/Gaya and in the Chotanagpur region (now Jharkhand) → better weapons and tools |
| **Fertile land** | Alluvial soil, heavy rainfall → large rice surplus → more taxes |
| **Rivers** | **Ganga, Son, Gandak, Punpun** — transport, trade, defence |
| **Safe capitals** | **Rajagriha** was ringed by **five hills**; **Pataliputra** at the meeting of the **Ganga and Son** (with Gandak and Punpun nearby) was a natural water-fort (*jaladurga*) |
| **Elephants** | From eastern forests — used in war (Nandas reportedly had thousands) |
| **Society** | Less bound by Vedic orthodoxy; open to new ideas and mixed peoples |
| **Rulers** | A series of ambitious, able kings: Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Mahapadma Nanda, then the Mauryas |
| **Trade** | Control over the Ganga trade route and the eastern sea route |

> **Mains angle:** "Discuss the factors responsible for the rise of Magadha" is a classic BPSC Mains question. Write geography (iron, soil, rivers, forts) + economy (surplus, trade) + society (heterodox, open) + leadership (dynastic policies: marriages, wars, administration). Conclude that these advantages made Magadha the base of the Mauryan and Gupta empires.

> **Remember:** These dates are **traditional** (based on the Buddhist chronology with the Buddha's death in 483 BCE). Some historians place them about 60–80 years later. Use the traditional dates in the exam.

#### Haryanka dynasty

| Ruler | Dates (traditional) | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Bimbisara** (Shrenika in Jain texts) | c. 544–492 BCE | Founder of Magadhan power; contemporary and follower of the **Buddha** (also claimed by Jains). Capital **Girivraja/Rajagriha**. **Marriage alliances**: **Kosaladevi** (sister of Prasenajit of Kosala — brought **Kashi** village as dowry), **Chellana** (Licchavi princess, daughter of **Chetaka** of Vaishali), **Khema** (Madra princess, Punjab). **Annexed Anga** (king Brahmadatta). Sent royal physician **Jivaka** to cure **Pradyota** of Avanti. Gifted **Venuvana** (bamboo grove) to the Buddha. Was imprisoned and killed by his son |
| **Ajatashatru** (Kunika) | c. 492–460 BCE | Most aggressive ruler. Fought **Kosala** (Prasenajit) and the **Vajji/Licchavis**; his minister **Vassakara** broke Licchavi unity. Used new war machines: **mahashilakantaka** (catapult for stones) and **rathamusala** (chariot with a mace/blades). Fortified **Pataligrama** on the Ganga. Hosted the **First Buddhist Council** at **Saptaparni cave, Rajagriha (483 BCE)** |
| **Udayin** (Udayabhadra) | c. 460–444 BCE | **Founded Pataliputra** (Kusumapura) at the confluence of the **Ganga and Son** and shifted the capital there |

Later Haryanka kings were weak; the people (according to Sri Lankan chronicles) replaced them with **Shishunaga**, a minister.

#### Shishunaga dynasty

| Ruler | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Shishunaga** | **Destroyed the Pradyota dynasty of Avanti** — ended a 100-year rivalry; briefly moved the capital to **Vaishali** |
| **Kalashoka** (Kakavarni) | Shifted capital back to **Pataliputra**; hosted the **Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali (383 BCE)** |

#### Nanda dynasty

| Ruler | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Mahapadma Nanda** (Ugrasena) | Founder; called **"Sarvakshatrantaka"** (destroyer of all Kshatriyas) and **"Ekarat"** (sole ruler) in the Puranas; considered the **first empire-builder** of India from a **non-Kshatriya** background; conquered **Kalinga** (the **Hathigumpha inscription** of Kharavela mentions a canal dug by a Nanda king) |
| **Dhana Nanda** | Last Nanda; very rich but unpopular for heavy taxes; contemporary of **Alexander** (326 BCE) — Greek writers call him *Agrammes/Xandrames* and describe his huge army, which discouraged Alexander's soldiers. Overthrown by **Chandragupta Maurya** and **Chanakya** (c. 321 BCE) |

> **Prelims angle:** Trap pairs — **Udayin** (not Ajatashatru) **founded Pataliputra**; Ajatashatru only built a fort at Pataligrama. **Shishunaga** (not Bimbisara) defeated **Avanti**. **Bimbisara** annexed **Anga**.

## Foreign invasions of the north-west

### Persian (Achaemenid) invasion

- **Cyrus** (558–530 BCE) reached the borders of India.
- **Darius I** (522–486 BCE) conquered **Gandhara** and the Indus region; it became the **20th satrapy** (province) of Persia — the richest, paying 360 talents of gold dust (Herodotus). His **Behistun** and **Naqsh-i-Rustam** inscriptions mention Hindush/Gandhara.
- Effects: the **Kharosthi** script (written right to left, from Aramaic) came to the north-west; Ashoka later used it in his NW edicts. Persian ideas may have influenced Mauryan pillars and edict-writing.

### Alexander's invasion (326 BCE)

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Entered India | 326 BCE |
| Submitted | **Ambhi**, king of **Taxila** |
| Battle | **Battle of the Hydaspes (Jhelum)** — Alexander defeated **Porus (Puru)**, but restored his kingdom |
| Stopped at | River **Beas (Hyphasis)** — his army refused to go further (fear of the Nandas, homesickness) |
| Left India | 325 BCE |
| Died | 323 BCE at **Babylon** |
| Effects | Opened land and sea routes between India and the West; Greek satrapies created a power vacuum that helped Chandragupta Maurya; Greek accounts give dates for Indian history |

## The Buddha and Buddhism

### Life of Gautama Buddha

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| Birth name | **Siddhartha** (clan name Gautama); also called **Shakyamuni** |
| Born | **563 BCE** (traditional; many scholars now prefer c. **480 BCE**) at **Lumbini** near Kapilavastu (Nepal) |
| Father / Mother | **Shuddhodana** (Shakya chief) / **Mahamaya** (Koliya princess, died 7 days after his birth) |
| Brought up by | Stepmother **Mahaprajapati Gautami** |
| Wife / Son | **Yashodhara** / **Rahula** |
| Horse / Charioteer | **Kanthaka** / **Channa** |
| Four sights | An old man, a sick man, a dead body, an ascetic |
| **Mahabhinishkramana** (Great Renunciation) | Left home at **29** |
| Teachers | **Alara Kalama**, **Udraka Ramaputra** |
| **Enlightenment** (Sambodhi/Nirvana) | At **35**, at **Uruvela (Bodh Gaya, Bihar)**, under a **pipal (Bodhi) tree** on the bank of the **Niranjana** (Lilajan, a source of the Phalgu) after 49 days of meditation |
| **Dharmachakra Pravartana** (first sermon) | **Sarnath** (Rishipatana / Deer Park, near Varanasi) to **five ascetics** |
| Most rainy seasons spent at | **Shravasti** (Kosala) — **Jetavana** monastery, gifted by merchant **Anathapindika** |
| **Mahaparinirvana** (death) | At **80**, in **483 BCE** (traditional), at **Kushinagar** (Malla territory, UP), after a meal from **Chunda** the smith at **Pava** |

| Event | Symbol |
|---|---|
| Birth | **Lotus** and **bull** |
| Great Renunciation | **Horse** |
| Enlightenment | **Bodhi tree** |
| First sermon | **Wheel** (Dharmachakra) |
| Death | **Stupa** |

> **Memory trick:** The **four great places** — **"Lumbini–Bodh Gaya–Sarnath–Kushinagar"** = **"Born, Bodhi, Sermon, Kushi(death)"**. Only **Bodh Gaya** is in Bihar.

### Teachings

- **Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya):** (1) life is suffering (*dukkha*); (2) suffering has a cause — desire/craving (*tanha*); (3) suffering can end (*nirodha*); (4) the path to end it (*marga*) — the Eightfold Path.
- **Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga):** right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.
- **Middle Path** — avoid both luxury and extreme self-torture.
- **Three Jewels (Triratna):** **Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha**.
- **Pratityasamutpada** — dependent origination: everything arises from causes.
- **Anatta** (no permanent soul) and **Anicca** (impermanence). The Buddha was silent on God.
- Rejected Vedic rituals, animal sacrifice and the authority of the Vedas; opposed caste hierarchy; **Sangha open to all**, including women.
- Preached in the people's language, **Pali** (a Prakrit), not Sanskrit.
- Goal: **Nirvana** — the "blowing out" of desire.

### Buddhist texts

| Text | Content |
|---|---|
| **Tripitaka** ("three baskets", in **Pali**) | **Sutta Pitaka** — sermons; **Vinaya Pitaka** — rules for monks; **Abhidhamma Pitaka** — philosophy |
| **Jatakas** | Stories of the Buddha's previous births |
| **Milindapanho** | Dialogue between Indo-Greek king **Menander (Milinda)** and monk **Nagasena** |
| **Buddhacharita** | Life of the Buddha in Sanskrit by **Ashvaghosha** |
| **Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa** | Sri Lankan chronicles (useful for Mauryan history) |
| **Divyavadana, Lalitavistara** | Sanskrit Buddhist texts (Mahayana) |

### Buddhist councils — the most-asked table

| Council | Year | Place | Patron | President | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | **483 BCE** | **Saptaparni cave, Rajagriha (Rajgir, Bihar)** | **Ajatashatru** | **Mahakassapa** | **Sutta Pitaka** (recited by **Ananda**) and **Vinaya Pitaka** (by **Upali**) compiled |
| **Second** | **383 BCE** | **Vaishali (Bihar)** | **Kalashoka** | **Sabakami** | Split into **Sthaviravadins** (elders) and **Mahasanghikas** over monastic rules |
| **Third** | **c. 250 BCE** | **Pataliputra (Bihar)** | **Ashoka** | **Moggaliputta Tissa** | **Abhidhamma Pitaka** added; missionaries sent abroad |
| **Fourth** | **1st century CE** (c. 72 CE) | **Kundalvana, Kashmir** | **Kanishka** | **Vasumitra** (deputy **Ashvaghosha**) | Split into **Hinayana** and **Mahayana**; *Mahavibhasha* compiled in Sanskrit |

> **Bihar connection:** **Three of the four Buddhist councils were held in Bihar** — Rajgir, Vaishali and Pataliputra. A favourite one-liner.

### Sects of Buddhism

| Sect | Features |
|---|---|
| **Hinayana** ("lesser vehicle"), also **Theravada** | Buddha as a teacher, not god; individual salvation; Pali texts; Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand |
| **Mahayana** ("greater vehicle") | Buddha as god, **idol worship**; **Bodhisattvas** (beings who delay their own nirvana to help others — Avalokiteshvara, Manjushri, Maitreya); Sanskrit texts; China, Japan, Korea; philosopher **Nagarjuna** (Madhyamika) |
| **Vajrayana** ("thunderbolt vehicle") | Tantric rituals, female deities (Taras); grew in **Bihar and Bengal under the Palas** (Vikramshila); spread to Tibet |

### Buddhist sites of Bihar

| Site | District | Why important |
|---|---|---|
| **Bodh Gaya** | Gaya | Enlightenment; **Mahabodhi Temple — UNESCO World Heritage Site (2002)**; Vajrasana (diamond throne) |
| **Rajgir (Rajagriha)** | Nalanda | **Gridhrakuta (Vulture Peak)** — many sermons; **Venuvana** gifted by Bimbisara; **Saptaparni cave** — First Council; Vishwa Shanti Stupa (modern) |
| **Vaishali** | Vaishali | Buddha's **last sermon** here and announced his coming death; **order of nuns** started (Mahaprajapati Gautami admitted on Ananda's request); Amrapali's mango grove; **Second Council**; **Ashokan lion pillar at Kolhua** |
| **Nalanda** | Nalanda | Later great monastery-university (see Gupta and Pala chapters) |
| **Kesaria** | East Champaran | **Kesaria stupa**, among the **largest/tallest excavated stupas** in the world (excavated by ASI from 1998); linked to the Buddha giving his begging bowl to the Licchavis |
| **Pataliputra** | Patna | **Third Council** under Ashoka |

## Mahavira and Jainism

### The tirthankaras

Jains believe in **24 tirthankaras** ("ford-makers" — those who help people cross the ocean of rebirth).

| No. | Tirthankara | Symbol | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Rishabhadeva (Adinath)** | Bull | Founder of Jainism as per Jain tradition; mentioned in the Rigveda / Puranas |
| 12th | Vasupujya | Buffalo | Born and attained nirvana at **Champapuri (Bhagalpur, Bihar)** |
| 22nd | **Neminath (Arishtanemi)** | Conch | Linked with Krishna; Girnar |
| 23rd | **Parshvanath** | Snake | Prince of **Kashi**; gave **four vows**; died at **Sammed Shikhar (Parasnath hill, now Jharkhand)** |
| 24th | **Vardhamana Mahavira** | **Lion** | Added the fifth vow; the historical systematiser of Jainism |

### Life of Mahavira

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| Birth name | **Vardhamana** |
| Born | **540 BCE** (common exam date; Jain tradition gives 599 BCE) at **Kundagrama near Vaishali, Bihar** |
| Clan | **Jnatrika** (a Vajji clan) |
| Father / Mother | **Siddhartha** / **Trishala** (Licchavi princess, sister of **Chetaka**) |
| Wife / Daughter | **Yashoda** / **Anojja (Priyadarshana)** |
| Son-in-law | **Jamali** — first disciple and first to split from him |
| Renunciation | At **30** |
| Companion for six years | **Makkhali Gosala** (later founded the Ajivika sect) |
| **Kaivalya** (perfect knowledge) | At **42**, after 12 years of penance, at **Jrimbhikagrama** on the bank of the **Rijupalika** river under a **sal** tree |
| Titles | **Jina** (conqueror), **Mahavira** (great hero), **Kevalin**, **Nirgrantha** (free from bonds) |
| Death (**Nirvana**) | At **72**, in **468 BCE** (traditional Jain date 527 BCE), at **Pavapuri (Pawapuri), Nalanda district, Bihar** — the **Jal Mandir** stands here |

> **Common mistake:** The Buddha died at **Kushinagar (UP)**, but Mahavira died at **Pawapuri (Bihar)**. Do not mix up "Pava" of the Mallas (where the Buddha ate his last meal, in UP) with **Pawapuri** in Nalanda district — Jain tradition places Mahavira's nirvana at Pawapuri, Bihar.

### Teachings

- **Triratna (three jewels):** **Right faith** (*samyak darshana*), **right knowledge** (*samyak jnana*), **right conduct** (*samyak charitra*).
- **Five vows (Pancha Mahavrata):** **Ahimsa** (non-violence), **Satya** (truth), **Asteya** (non-stealing), **Aparigraha** (non-possession) — these four from **Parshvanath** — plus **Brahmacharya** (celibacy) added by **Mahavira**. Monks follow them strictly (*mahavrata*); lay people follow a milder form (*anuvrata*).
- **Anekantavada** — reality has many sides; **Syadvada** — "may be" (seven-fold way of stating truth, *saptabhangi*).
- Everything — even stones and water — has a **soul (jiva)**; hence extreme non-violence.
- No creator god; liberation (**kaivalya/nirvana**) through penance and right conduct; the soul is freed from karma.
- Language of preaching: **Ardhamagadhi Prakrit**.

### Jain sects and councils

Around **300 BCE**, a **12-year famine** struck Magadha (in Chandragupta Maurya's time).

- **Bhadrabahu** led a group of monks south to **Shravanabelagola (Karnataka)** — Chandragupta Maurya went with him, as per Jain tradition. They kept strict rules, going naked → **Digambaras** ("sky-clad").
- **Sthulabhadra** stayed in Magadha; his followers wore white clothes → **Shvetambaras** ("white-clad").

| Council | Year | Place | President | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | c. **300 BCE** | **Pataliputra (Bihar)** | **Sthulabhadra** | **12 Angas** compiled |
| **Second** | **5th–6th century CE** (**512 CE** in most guidebooks; some give 453 CE) | **Vallabhi (Gujarat)** | **Devardhi Kshamashramana** | Final compilation of the canon (12 Angas, 12 Upangas) |

- Jain texts are in **Prakrit**; the **Kalpasutra** (life of tirthankaras) is attributed to **Bhadrabahu**.
- Royal patrons: **Bimbisara, Ajatashatru** (claimed by Jains too), **Chandragupta Maurya**, **Kharavela** of Kalinga (Hathigumpha inscription), Rashtrakuta **Amoghavarsha**, Chalukya **Kumarapala** (with scholar **Hemachandra**).

### Jain sites of Bihar

| Site | District | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| **Kundagrama (Basokund)** | Vaishali | Birthplace of Mahavira |
| **Pawapuri** | Nalanda | Nirvana of Mahavira; **Jal Mandir** in a lotus tank |
| **Rajgir** | Nalanda | Mahavira spent many rainy seasons here; Jain temples on its five hills (Vipulachal etc.) |
| **Champapuri** | Bhagalpur | Birth and nirvana of the 12th tirthankara Vasupujya |
| **Parasnath / Sammed Shikhar** | Giridih (**now Jharkhand**, earlier Bihar) | Nirvana of 20 tirthankaras incl. Parshvanath |

## Other sects: Ajivikas and materialists

| Teacher / School | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Makkhali Gosala — Ajivikas** | **Niyati** (fate / determinism) — everything is pre-decided. **Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha** gifted the **Barabar and Nagarjuni caves (Jehanabad district, Bihar)** to Ajivikas — the **oldest rock-cut caves** in India (incl. **Lomas Rishi** cave) |
| **Ajita Kesakambalin** | Materialism — the body is made of four elements; nothing survives death |
| **Charvaka / Lokayata** | Materialist school (traditionally founded by **Brihaspati**) — only perception is valid knowledge |
| **Purana Kassapa** | *Akriyavada* — actions have no moral result |
| **Pakudha Kachchayana** | Seven eternal elements |
| **Sanjaya Belatthiputta** | Scepticism |

## Buddhism and Jainism compared

| Point | Buddhism | Jainism |
|---|---|---|
| Founder / systematiser | Gautama Buddha | Mahavira (24th tirthankara) |
| Bihar link | Enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; councils at Rajgir, Vaishali, Pataliputra | Born near Vaishali; died at Pawapuri; first council at Pataliputra |
| Path | Middle Path, Eightfold Path | Strict penance, five vows |
| Soul | No permanent soul (*anatta*) | Soul in all things (*jiva*) |
| Non-violence | Moderate | Extreme |
| Language | Pali | Ardhamagadhi Prakrit |
| Goal | Nirvana | Kaivalya / nirvana |

> **Mains angle:** For "Why did Buddhism and Jainism arise in the 6th century BCE?", use: (1) reaction to costly Vedic sacrifices and Brahmin dominance; (2) Kshatriya resentment of Brahmin supremacy; (3) new agrarian economy needed cattle, which sacrifices killed; (4) rising traders (Vaishyas) wanted higher status and disliked usury restrictions; (5) the eastern Gangetic plain was less orthodox; (6) simple teaching in people's language. For "Why did Buddhism decline in India?": loss of royal patronage, Sangha corruption and wealth, Mahayana idol worship blurring the difference with Hinduism, revival of Brahmanism (Shankaracharya, Bhakti), use of Sanskrit, Hun and Turkish attacks (Bakhtiyar Khalji's destruction of Nalanda and Vikramshila, c. 1193–1203).

### Mains questions

**1. Discuss the factors responsible for the rise of Magadha as the most powerful Mahajanapada.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Sixteen Mahajanapadas in the 6th c. BCE; Magadha emerged supreme by the 4th c. BCE and became the base of the Mauryan empire.
- Geography: iron ores, fertile alluvium, rice surplus.
- Rivers: Ganga, Son, Gandak — trade and defence; Pataliputra as a water-fort.
- Strategic capitals: Rajgir ringed by five hills.
- Elephants in the army.
- Open, less orthodox society; support of new sects.
- Able rulers: Bimbisara (marriages, Anga), Ajatashatru (Kosala, Vajji, new weapons), Udayin (Pataliputra), Shishunaga (Avanti), Mahapadma Nanda (Kalinga, large army).
- *Conclusion:* A mix of natural advantage and political skill; Bihar became the political heart of India for nearly a thousand years.

**2. "Bihar was the cradle of Buddhism and Jainism." Examine.** (300 words)

- *Intro:* Both religions rose in the eastern Gangetic plain in the 6th c. BCE.
- Buddha: enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; Rajgir (Venuvana, Gridhrakuta); Vaishali (nuns' order, last sermon); patrons Bimbisara and Ajatashatru.
- Councils: three of four in Bihar (Rajgir, Vaishali, Pataliputra); Ashoka's missions from Pataliputra.
- Mahavira: born near Vaishali, Jnatrika clan; nirvana at Pawapuri; first Jain council at Pataliputra.
- Later institutions: Nalanda, Vikramshila, Odantapuri spread Buddhism to Tibet and South-East Asia.
- Monuments: Mahabodhi (UNESCO), Kesaria stupa, Jal Mandir, Barabar caves.
- *Conclusion:* Bihar's spiritual heritage today drives Buddhist-circuit tourism and India's soft power.

**3. Compare the teachings of the Buddha and Mahavira.** (200 words)

- *Intro:* Contemporaries, Kshatriya princes from republican clans, both from the Bihar–Nepal region.
- Common: rejected Vedas, rituals, caste; karma and rebirth; non-violence; people's languages.
- Differences: Middle Path vs extreme penance; *anatta* vs soul in all things; moderate vs extreme ahimsa; Sangha with laity vs strict monkhood.
- Spread: Buddhism across Asia; Jainism within India among traders.
- *Conclusion:* Both shaped Indian ethics — ahimsa later inspired Gandhi.

**4. Describe the working of the gana-sanghas with special reference to the Vajji confederacy.** (150 words)

- *Intro:* Republics existed beside monarchies in the 6th c. BCE.
- Assembly of rajas in the santhagara; voting by shalaka.
- Vajji: 8 clans, Licchavis leading; capital Vaishali; 7,707 rajas.
- Buddha's seven conditions of welfare.
- Decline: internal division exploited by Ajatashatru's minister Vassakara.
- *Conclusion:* Evidence of early democratic traditions in Bihar.
