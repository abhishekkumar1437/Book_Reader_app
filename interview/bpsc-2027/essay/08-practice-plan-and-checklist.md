Reading about essays does not make you an essay writer. Writing does. This chapter turns the whole book into a schedule that fits the 300 + 90 day plan: a light content-building habit from Day 1, a focused essay block on Days 225–235, a pause during Prelims mode, and a weekly essay rhythm through the 90-day Mains sprint. It also gives you a self-evaluation rubric so that you can mark your own essays honestly.

> **In simple words:** Collect examples from Day 1. Learn the method on Days 225–235. Keep essay work light during Prelims revision. After Prelims, write at least one full essay every week, mark it with the rubric, fix your top two mistakes, and repeat.

## The 100-point self-evaluation rubric

There is no published official marking scheme for the BPSC essay. This rubric is a **self-check tool** built on what essay examiners commonly reward. Use it after every essay. Mark honestly — or better, ask a friend to mark you.

<div class="formula">

| Area | Marks | What full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Understanding of topic | 10 | Keywords decoded; thesis matches exact wording; no drift |
| 2. Introduction | 10 | Engaging opening; clear thesis in the last line |
| 3. Structure and flow | 15 | One idea per paragraph; logical order; transitions; signposts |
| 4. Depth of analysis | 15 | Causes, effects, "why" and "so what"; not just description |
| 5. Range of dimensions | 10 | 6+ dimensions (social, economic, political, environmental, ethical, cultural, etc.) |
| 6. Evidence and examples | 15 | 3–5 Bihar examples spread out; India and world examples; accurate data with source |
| 7. Balance and counter-view | 10 | Problems admitted; a fair counter-argument answered |
| 8. Conclusion | 10 | Summary + way forward + memorable line; no new issue |
| 9. Language and presentation | 5 | Simple, correct English; neat; paragraphs clearly separated |
| **Total** | **100** | |

</div>

| Your score | What it means | What to do next |
|---|---|---|
| 75–100 | Exam-ready | Keep writing weekly; experiment with harder philosophical topics |
| 60–74 | Good, with gaps | Fix your two lowest areas in the next essay |
| 45–59 | Average | Re-read Chapter 2; write outlines for 5 topics before the next full essay |
| Below 45 | Needs basics | Practise paragraphs (PEEL) and introductions daily for a week, then try again |

> **Common mistake:** Giving yourself high marks for "language" because you used difficult words. Simple and correct beats complex and wrong. Mark language on clarity and correctness only.

## Language fixes for English-medium essays

English is your second language, and that is fine. Avoid these common errors, and your language will look clean.

| Avoid | Write instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "In this essay we will discuss…" | Go straight into your opening | Filler; wastes the introduction |
| "Nowadays, in today's modern world…" | "Today…" | Repetition |
| "Govt.", "w.r.t.", "&", "etc. etc." | "government", "with respect to", "and" | Abbreviations look careless |
| "Very very important" | "Crucial" or "vital" | Repetition |
| "Women are not getting education" | "Many women still lack education" | Avoid sweeping claims |
| "Bihar is the most backward state" | "Bihar lags behind on several indicators, such as per-capita income" | Precise, respectful |
| "Due to the reason because" | "Because" | Double phrase |
| "Each and every citizen" | "Every citizen" | Wordy |
| Very long sentences with many "and"s | Two or three short sentences | Clarity |
| Writing "I think" in every paragraph | State the argument directly; use "I" sparingly, if at all | Confident tone |

> **Memory trick:** **"Short, sure, simple."** Short sentences, sure facts, simple words.

### Speed and handwriting drill

- 10 minutes a day during the sprint: copy a paragraph from a good newspaper editorial at exam speed.
- Target: about 15–20 words a minute with readable handwriting. At that speed, 43 minutes of writing gives you 650–850 words.
- Use a pen you are comfortable with; practise with the same kind of pen you will use in the exam.

## Getting feedback

- **Peer review:** Exchange essays with one serious friend each week. Mark each other with the rubric. Discuss only the two lowest areas.
- **Read toppers' essays carefully** (if available legitimately from coaching or published sources) — but study their structure, not their sentences. Never memorise someone else's essay.
- **Self-review after 24 hours:** Re-read your essay the next day. Mistakes you missed while writing become obvious.

## Exam-day checklist

**Before you begin writing**

- [ ] Read the instructions on the booklet: number of sections, number of topics per section, word limits, marks.
- [ ] Spend the first 10 minutes reading all topics and choosing one per section.
- [ ] For each choice, jot 8–10 points in the margin or rough area.

**For each essay**

- [ ] Write the topic exactly as given at the top.
- [ ] Underline keywords; write a one-line demand in rough.
- [ ] Make an outline (8–10 minutes): intro idea, 8–12 body points, conclusion idea.
- [ ] Introduction ends with a clear thesis using the keywords.
- [ ] One idea per paragraph; transitions; at least one counter-view paragraph.
- [ ] 3–5 Bihar examples spread through the essay.
- [ ] Only facts and quotes I am sure of; otherwise trends and paraphrases.
- [ ] At minute 50 of each essay, start the conclusion — no matter what.
- [ ] Conclusion: summary, way forward, memorable final line.
- [ ] Two to three minutes to read through and fix errors.

**At the end**

- [ ] All three essays complete, from three different sections, correctly numbered.
- [ ] Rough work neatly crossed out (if required by the instructions).

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Rubric | A scoring guide that lists what is checked and how many marks each part gets |
| Mock paper | A full practice paper written under exam conditions |
| Mistake log | A record of your errors and how you will fix them |
| Blind outline | An outline made without looking at notes |
| Peer review | Checking a friend's work while they check yours |
| Rules card | A one-page list of your personal essay rules, made from your mistakes |

## Practice

### Writing tasks

**Task 1 — Build your rules card (20 minutes).** After your first three essays, write a one-page "essay rules" card: your top five mistakes and the fix for each, your favourite opening types, your five most useful Bihar examples, and your 10 surest quotes.

**Task 2 — Full mock paper (3 hours).** Pick three topics from three different themes in Chapter 7 (for example, one Bihar, one governance, one philosophical). Write all three essays in exactly 3 hours. Mark each with the 100-point rubric.

**Task 3 — Rewrite drill (40 minutes).** Take your lowest-scoring essay. Rewrite only its introduction, counter-view paragraph and conclusion. Mark it again and note the change in score.

**Task 4 — Peer marking (30 minutes).** Mark a friend's essay with the rubric. Write two strengths and two improvements in full sentences.
