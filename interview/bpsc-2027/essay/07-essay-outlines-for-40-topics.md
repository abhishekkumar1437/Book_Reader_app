You cannot predict the exact topics in your essay paper, but you can predict the **themes**. Almost every topic BPSC can set will fall close to one of the 40 below. If you have thought through these outlines, you will never face a blank page: you will adapt an outline you know to the exact wording in front of you. These are practice topics chosen by theme — they are not claimed to be previous-year questions.

> **In simple words:** Do not memorise these outlines word for word. Learn the **ideas, examples and flow** of each. In the exam, re-read the exact topic, then pick and reorder points from the nearest outline.

## How to use this chapter

1. **First reading (Days 225–235):** Read all 40 once. Tick the ones you feel confident about.
2. **Active practice:** Each day, take two topics. Close the book, make your own outline in 8 minutes, then compare it with the one here and add what you missed.
3. **Full essays:** In the Mains sprint, write full essays on at least 12 of these — at least one from each theme.
4. **Adapt, don't copy:** If the exam topic is "Migration: a mirror of regional inequality", use outline 2 but put "inequality" at the centre of your thesis.

> **Remember:** Every figure marked "check latest" must be updated from the latest Bihar Economic Survey, NFHS, PLFS or current affairs before the exam. Every quote below is either well established or given as a saying without a name. Do not add names to the unattributed ones.

> **Common mistake:** Writing an outline-based essay that ignores the exact topic wording. If the topic is "Floods are a man-made disaster", you must argue about *man-made*, not just describe floods.

## Theme A — Bihar and development

### 1. Bihar@2047: a vision for a developed Bihar

*Type:* visionary/descriptive. *Keywords:* 2047 (100 years of independence), developed.

- **Intro idea:** Imagine a young person in Bihar in 2047 — what does her day look like? School, job, hospital, clean river, safe street. Contrast with today. Thesis: the vision is achievable if growth becomes broad-based.
- **Body points:**
  1. What "developed" means — income, but also health, education, dignity, environment (Sen's idea).
  2. Starting point — low per-capita income, low urbanisation, high density, young population (check latest).
  3. Agriculture to agri-business — food processing, makhana, litchi, dairy, fisheries.
  4. Industry and services — MSMEs, logistics, IT-enabled services, tourism.
  5. Human capital — learning quality, skills, nutrition, health.
  6. Infrastructure — roads, bridges, power, internet, urban planning.
  7. Women and social justice — work participation, safety, inclusive policies.
  8. Environment — flood-resilient planning, clean energy, water security.
  9. Governance — digital services, accountability, strong local bodies.
- **Bihar examples:** Saat Nischay; JEEViKA; Nalanda University revival; Agriculture Road Maps.
- **Quote:** Rabindranath Tagore — "Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high…"
- **Conclusion idea:** A vision is a promise to the next generation; Bihar's 2047 begins with today's classroom.

### 2. Migration from Bihar: compulsion or choice?

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* migration, compulsion (push factors), choice (pull factors and aspiration).

- **Intro idea:** A railway platform in Darbhanga after Chhath — thousands of young men returning to cities. Thesis: for most, migration is a compulsion today; policy should make it a choice.
- **Body points:**
  1. Scale and pattern — seasonal and long-term; to Delhi, Punjab, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Kerala; also to the Gulf (check latest data).
  2. Push factors — small landholdings, floods, low wages, few industries, weak urban jobs.
  3. Pull factors — higher wages, skills, education, aspiration.
  4. Benefits — remittances, new skills, women's autonomy at home, reduced pressure on land.
  5. Costs — family separation, children's schooling, exploitation, unsafe work, discrimination.
  6. COVID-19 lockdown of 2020 — the long walk home showed migrants' vulnerability.
  7. Policy — One Nation One Ration Card, e-Shram portal for unorganised workers, state migrant support (check latest).
  8. Counter-view — "migration is natural and good for growth" → yes, but distress migration is not.
  9. Way forward — local jobs, skills, social security portability, data on migrants, safe housing in cities.
- **Bihar examples:** Remittance-supported villages; returning migrants starting businesses; flood-prone Kosi belt.
- **Quote:** Warsan Shire (poet), in her poem "Home": "no one leaves home unless home is the mouth of a shark."
- **Conclusion idea:** The goal is not to stop migration but to make staying possible and leaving a free choice.

### 3. Floods in north Bihar: natural calamity or man-made disaster?

*Type:* argumentative ("or"). *Keywords:* natural, man-made.

- **Intro idea:** The Kusaha breach of the Kosi embankment on 18 August 2008 — a river returned to an old course and flooded lakhs of homes. Thesis: floods are natural; disasters are largely man-made.
- **Body points:**
  1. Natural causes — Himalayan rivers, heavy monsoon rain in Nepal, high silt load, flat land, shifting courses (Kosi, "Sorrow of Bihar").
  2. Man-made causes — embankments that trap silt and fail, deforestation upstream, encroachment on floodplains and wetlands, poor drainage, roads blocking water flow.
  3. Scale — a large share of north Bihar is flood-prone (check latest figure).
  4. Impacts — lives, crops, livestock, schools closed, disease, migration, debt.
  5. Positive role of floods — fertile silt, groundwater recharge, fish.
  6. Inter-state and international dimension — rivers from Nepal; need for cooperation.
  7. Responses — SDRF/NDRF, early warning, flood shelters, embankment repair, Kosi–Mechi link (check latest).
  8. Counter-view — "embankments are the only solution" → evidence of breaches and waterlogging.
  9. Way forward — "living with floods": raised homes, flood-resilient crops, wetland revival, community preparedness, Netherlands-style "room for the river".
- **Bihar examples:** Kosi 2008; Bagmati and Kamla basins; flood shelters; community boats.
- **Quote:** Saying without attribution — "We cannot fight a river forever; we must learn to live with it."
- **Conclusion idea:** Nature brings the water; human choices decide whether it becomes a disaster.

### 4. Why Bihar lagged in industrialisation — and how it can catch up

*Type:* descriptive-analytical. *Keywords:* lagged (causes), catch up (solutions).

- **Intro idea:** Bihar's farmers grow excellent produce, yet the factories that process it are often in other states. Thesis: Bihar's industrial gap has clear causes and realistic remedies.
- **Body points:**
  1. History — Permanent Settlement (1793) and absentee landlords; little industrial investment.
  2. Post-1947 — freight equalisation (as many economists argue), slow land reform, power shortages.
  3. 2000 bifurcation — mines and heavy industry went to Jharkhand.
  4. Present constraints — land scarcity (high density), power and logistics costs, skills gap, perceptions about law and order.
  5. Strengths — large market, cheap labour, rivers, agriculture base, location near Nepal, Bengal and the North-East.
  6. Possible sectors — food processing, textiles and leather, ethanol, logistics, tourism, IT-enabled services.
  7. Policy — industrial investment promotion policies, single-window clearance, industrial areas (check latest).
  8. Counter-view — "Bihar should focus only on agriculture" → agriculture alone cannot absorb its youth.
  9. Way forward — MSME clusters, skill parks, better urban centres, credit, ease of doing business.
- **Bihar examples:** Sudha (COMFED) dairy; makhana and litchi processing; Bhagalpuri silk.
- **Quote:** M. Visvesvaraya's slogan — "Industrialise or perish."
- **Conclusion idea:** Bihar does not need to copy others' factories; it must build industries on its own strengths.

### 5. Urbanisation in Bihar: the missing link of development

*Type:* descriptive-argumentative. *Keywords:* urbanisation, missing link.

- **Intro idea:** In Census 2011, only about 11% of Bihar's people lived in towns, among the lowest in India. Thesis: well-planned towns could be the missing link between farms and jobs.
- **Body points:**
  1. Why towns matter — jobs, markets, services, ideas; cities as engines of growth.
  2. Bihar's pattern — few big cities; Patna dominant; many small towns with weak services.
  3. Causes of slow urbanisation — agrarian economy, little industry, migration to other states' cities.
  4. Problems in existing towns — waterlogging, waste, traffic, slums, air pollution.
  5. Governance — weak urban local bodies (74th Amendment), low own revenue, staff shortages.
  6. Opportunities — Smart City projects (Patna, Muzaffarpur, Bhagalpur, Biharsharif — check latest), metro in Patna, satellite towns.
  7. Rural-urban continuum — market towns linking villages to processing and services.
  8. Counter-view — "India lives in villages" → strong villages need strong towns nearby.
  9. Way forward — master plans, drainage, affordable housing, transport, empowered municipalities.
- **Bihar examples:** Patna waterlogging; Patna Metro; small towns like Begusarai, Purnia growing as hubs.
- **Quote:** Mahatma Gandhi often said that India lives in its villages — use it as the starting point to argue that villages and towns now rise together.
- **Conclusion idea:** Bihar's towns should be bridges, not barriers, between its villages and the future.

### 6. Roads, bridges and connectivity: arteries of Bihar's growth

*Type:* descriptive. *Keywords:* connectivity (physical and digital), arteries (life-giving flow).

- **Intro idea:** Sher Shah Suri of Sasaram built roads and sarais that tied an empire together; five centuries later, roads and bridges are again transforming Bihar. Thesis: connectivity is the first condition of development.
- **Body points:**
  1. History — Grand Trunk Road; railways; river transport on the Ganga.
  2. Rural roads — access to schools, hospitals, markets; PMGSY and state schemes.
  3. Bridges over the Ganga and Kosi — Mahatma Gandhi Setu (1982) and newer bridges linking north and south Bihar.
  4. Economic effects — lower transport costs, faster sale of perishables, investment.
  5. Social effects — girls' education, faster ambulances, women's mobility.
  6. Airports and railways — Darbhanga airport (UDAN), rail freight corridors (check latest).
  7. Digital connectivity — mobile internet, digital payments, e-services.
  8. Problems — maintenance, poor quality, floods damaging roads, land acquisition, environmental impact.
  9. Way forward — climate-proof design, maintenance funds, inland waterways, last-mile links.
- **Bihar examples:** Gandhi Setu; Kosi bridge; rural roads under Saat Nischay ("Ghar tak Pakki Gali-Naliyan").
- **Quote:** Saying without attribution — "Roads are the arteries through which the economy flows."
- **Conclusion idea:** A road reaching a village carries not only trucks but also opportunity.

## Theme B — Society

### 7. Women empowerment: from reservation to real power

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* reservation (formal seats), real power (actual decision-making).

- **Intro idea:** A woman mukhiya who first came to office as a "proxy" for her husband now chairs meetings herself. Thesis: reservation opens the door; education, money and confidence give real power.
- **Body points:**
  1. Constitutional basis — Article 15(3), 243D (one-third); Bihar's 50% panchayat reservation (2006).
  2. 106th Amendment (2023) — one-third seats in Lok Sabha and assemblies after the next census and delimitation.
  3. Impact — women's voice, focus on water, schools, sanitation (research trend).
  4. Problems — proxy leadership ("mukhiya-pati"), low education, lack of training, family pressure.
  5. Economic power — JEEViKA SHGs; 35% reservation in Bihar government jobs (check latest).
  6. Social power — later marriage, mobility, safety.
  7. Legal power — property rights (Hindu Succession Amendment 2005), protection from domestic violence (2005 Act).
  8. Counter-view — "reservation produces token leaders" → token today, leader tomorrow, with support.
  9. Way forward — training, control over funds, women's collectives, men as allies.
- **Bihar examples:** 50% panchayat seats; women's turnout above men's in recent Bihar Assembly elections; JEEViKA; women in Bihar police.
- **Quote:** Kofi Annan — "There is no tool for development more effective than the empowerment of women."
- **Conclusion idea:** A seat is a beginning; power is what a woman does with it.

### 8. Prohibition in Bihar: social reform through law

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* prohibition, social reform, law (can law change behaviour?).

- **Intro idea:** In 2016, women's groups in Bihar's villages, many linked to JEEViKA, welcomed total prohibition as protection from domestic violence and wasted wages. Thesis: prohibition has social gains, but law alone cannot finish the reform.
- **Body points:**
  1. Background — total prohibition from April 2016; Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016; Article 47 (DPSP).
  2. Ethical and Gandhian roots — temperance in the freedom movement.
  3. Social gains reported — reduced public drunkenness, women's safety, household savings (cite as "reports and surveys suggest").
  4. Economic dimension — loss of excise revenue vs gains in household spending and productivity.
  5. Problems — illicit liquor and hooch tragedies, smuggling, burden on courts and jails, enforcement costs.
  6. Legal changes — amendments to ease penalties for first-time offenders (check latest).
  7. Comparison — other states that tried and withdrew prohibition; Gujarat's long-standing prohibition.
  8. Counter-view — "individual liberty" → balance between liberty and harm to family and society.
  9. Way forward — de-addiction centres, awareness, livelihood for those dependent on liquor trade, focused enforcement on suppliers.
- **Bihar examples:** 2016 Act; women's SHG campaigns; human chain campaigns for social reform.
- **Quote:** Article 47 — the State shall endeavour to bring about prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health.
- **Conclusion idea:** Law can close the shop; only society can close the demand.

### 9. Education: the key to Bihar's future

*Type:* descriptive-argumentative. *Keywords:* education (access and quality), key, future.

- **Intro idea:** Nalanda once drew scholars from across Asia; today, lakhs of Bihar's students leave the state for coaching and colleges. Thesis: Bihar's future depends on making learning — not just enrolment — strong at home.
- **Body points:**
  1. Heritage — Nalanda, Vikramshila, Odantapuri.
  2. Progress — near-universal enrolment, girls' enrolment (cycle and uniform schemes), new teachers.
  3. Quality gap — low learning levels in many schools (ASER-type surveys — check latest), teacher absence, large classes.
  4. Higher education — limited quality seats, delayed sessions, migration of students.
  5. Constitutional and policy base — Article 21A, RTE Act 2009, NEP 2020 (mother tongue, foundational literacy, vocational education).
  6. Economic returns — skills, productivity, income.
  7. Social returns — less child marriage, better health, citizenship.
  8. Digital learning — opportunities and the digital divide.
  9. Way forward — foundational literacy, teacher training and accountability, school infrastructure, skills, revived universities, Student Credit Card.
- **Bihar examples:** Balika Cycle Yojana; Super 30; Nalanda University (new campus 2024); Student Credit Card.
- **Quote:** Nelson Mandela — "Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the world."
- **Conclusion idea:** Bihar taught the world once; it can do so again if it teaches its own children well.

### 10. Caste and social justice in 21st-century India

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* caste, social justice, 21st century (change and continuity).

- **Intro idea:** A Dalit girl topping her district's board exam and the same girl facing discrimination at a village function — two realities of today. Thesis: caste has weakened in public life but remains strong in social relations; social justice must move from representation to real equality.
- **Body points:**
  1. History — varna and jati; reform movements (Phule, Ambedkar, Periyar); Bihar's Kisan and backward-class movements.
  2. Constitution — Articles 14, 15, 16, 17; reservation; SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989.
  3. Political change — rise of backward and Dalit leadership; Karpoori Thakur's reservation policy in Bihar.
  4. Data and policy — Bihar's caste-based survey (results October 2023); its use for policy (check latest legal status of reservation changes).
  5. Economic dimension — land, education and jobs still unequal.
  6. Social dimension — inter-caste marriage, honour crimes, residential segregation.
  7. Urbanisation and education as dissolvers of caste.
  8. Counter-view — "caste-based policy keeps caste alive" → explain need for targeted justice until equality is real.
  9. Way forward — quality education, land and skill access, strict anti-discrimination enforcement, social mixing, merit and justice together.
- **Bihar examples:** Karpoori Thakur (Bharat Ratna 2024); caste survey 2023; Mahadalit programmes.
- **Quote:** B. R. Ambedkar — "Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social democracy."
- **Conclusion idea:** Social justice is complete only when birth no longer predicts destiny.

### 11. Health for all: Bihar's public health challenge

*Type:* descriptive. *Keywords:* health for all (universal access), public health.

- **Intro idea:** A pregnant woman in a flood-hit village waits for a boat to reach the nearest health centre. Thesis: universal health needs strong primary care close to people.
- **Body points:**
  1. Meaning — WHO definition of health; prevention and cure.
  2. Bihar's indicators — high fertility, stunting, anaemia (NFHS-5, check latest); improvements in institutional delivery and immunisation.
  3. Infrastructure — shortage of doctors and specialists; PHC and sub-centre gaps; Health and Wellness Centres (Ayushman Arogya Mandirs).
  4. Financial protection — Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY (check latest coverage).
  5. Communicable diseases — kala-azar (Bihar's long fight), TB, encephalitis (AES in Muzaffarpur).
  6. Non-communicable diseases — diabetes, hypertension, mental health.
  7. Social determinants — nutrition, clean water, sanitation, women's education.
  8. Human resources — ASHAs, ANMs, medical colleges; new AIIMS in Darbhanga (check latest).
  9. Way forward — primary care first, telemedicine, health workforce, nutrition, preventive campaigns.
- **Bihar examples:** Kala-azar elimination efforts; AES in Muzaffarpur; ASHA network; Didi ki Rasoi in hospitals.
- **Quote:** WHO Constitution (1948) — health is "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."
- **Conclusion idea:** A healthy Bihar is the foundation for an educated and prosperous Bihar.

### 12. The changing Indian family and care of the elderly

*Type:* descriptive-argumentative. *Keywords:* changing family (joint to nuclear, migration), elderly.

- **Intro idea:** An old couple in a Bihar village whose three sons work in three different cities, and who talk to grandchildren only on video calls. Thesis: the family is changing, and society must create new forms of care.
- **Body points:**
  1. Traditional joint family — support, shared work, values.
  2. Drivers of change — migration, urbanisation, education, women's work, smaller families.
  3. Positive changes — more individual choice, women's autonomy, equal partnerships.
  4. Challenges — loneliness of elders, health costs, elder abuse, children without parental time.
  5. Law and policy — Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007; old-age pensions (state and central, check latest).
  6. Health — geriatric care, Ayushman Bharat for elders (check latest).
  7. Cultural values — respect for elders in Indian tradition.
  8. Counter-view — "nuclear families destroy values" → values can survive in new forms.
  9. Way forward — community elder care, day-care centres, digital literacy for elders, intergenerational programmes.
- **Bihar examples:** Migrant households with left-behind elders; old-age pension schemes in Bihar (check latest).
- **Quote:** Taittiriya Upanishad — "Matru devo bhava, pitru devo bhava" (Treat mother and father as divine).
- **Conclusion idea:** The shape of the family may change; its duty of care must not.

### 13. An inclusive society: dignity for persons with disabilities and the marginalised

*Type:* descriptive. *Keywords:* inclusive, dignity.

- **Intro idea:** A wheelchair user who cannot enter a polling booth or a school building — the barrier is in the building, not in the person. Thesis: inclusion is a measure of a society's civilisation.
- **Body points:**
  1. Meaning — from charity to rights; social model of disability.
  2. Law — Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 (21 disabilities; 4% reservation in government jobs).
  3. Constitution — equality and dignity (Articles 14, 21).
  4. Other marginalised groups — transgender persons (2019 Act), the destitute, Mahadalits.
  5. Accessibility — Accessible India Campaign; ramps, sign language, Braille, accessible websites.
  6. Education and jobs — inclusive schools, skill training.
  7. Social attitudes — stigma, pity, exclusion.
  8. Counter-view — "inclusion is costly" → cost of exclusion is higher (lost talent).
  9. Way forward — universal design, data, awareness, enforcement.
- **Bihar examples:** Disability pension and assistive devices schemes (check latest); inclusive polling booths in elections.
- **Quote:** Gandhi's Talisman — "Recall the face of the poorest and the weakest man whom you may have seen…"
- **Conclusion idea:** A society is as strong as the way it treats its weakest members.

## Theme C — Economy and agriculture

### 14. Agriculture is still the backbone of Bihar's economy

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* backbone (central support), still (despite change).

- **Intro idea:** A farmer in Samastipur growing maize, vegetables and makhana on less than one acre. Thesis: agriculture remains Bihar's backbone, but the backbone needs strengthening, not replacement.
- **Body points:**
  1. Importance — most rural workers depend on it; fertile Gangetic plains.
  2. Strengths — rice, wheat, maize, vegetables, fruits, makhana, litchi, fish, dairy.
  3. Problems — tiny holdings, floods and drought, low mechanisation, weak markets (APMC Act repealed in 2006 — markets remain thin), low credit.
  4. Policies — four Agriculture Road Maps (4th: 2023–28); PM-KISAN; crop insurance (state scheme — check latest).
  5. Technology — seeds, soil testing, weather advisories, zero tillage, drones.
  6. Diversification — horticulture, dairy, fisheries, beekeeping.
  7. Value addition — food processing, FPOs.
  8. Counter-view — "jobs must move out of farms" → true, but farm productivity funds that shift.
  9. Way forward — irrigation, market reform, FPOs, processing, climate-resilient practices.
- **Bihar examples:** Makhana (GI); Muzaffarpur litchi (GI); Sudha dairy; Agriculture Road Maps.
- **Quote:** Lal Bahadur Shastri — "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan."
- **Conclusion idea:** Strengthen the backbone, and the whole body of Bihar's economy stands taller.

### 15. Unemployment: the biggest challenge before India's youth

*Type:* descriptive-argumentative. *Keywords:* unemployment (open, disguised, under-employment), youth.

- **Intro idea:** Thousands of graduates applying for a few hundred posts — the long queues outside recruitment exams. Thesis: India's youth need not just more jobs but better jobs and better skills.
- **Body points:**
  1. Types — open, disguised (many on small farms), seasonal, educated unemployment.
  2. Data — PLFS trends (check latest); high informality.
  3. Causes — jobless growth in some sectors, skills mismatch, slow manufacturing, preference for government jobs.
  4. Bihar's situation — migration, farm dependence, few factories.
  5. Social effects — frustration, delayed marriage, crime, mental health.
  6. Policies — Skill India, Start-up India, MUDRA loans, MGNREGA, PM Vishwakarma (check latest); Bihar's youth schemes.
  7. New sectors — gig economy, digital services, renewable energy, tourism, care work.
  8. Counter-view — "AI will take jobs" → it will also create new ones; skills decide who benefits.
  9. Way forward — labour-intensive manufacturing, MSMEs, vocational education, apprenticeships, women's work participation.
- **Bihar examples:** Migrant workers; Student Credit Card; JEEViKA enterprises; MSME clusters.
- **Quote:** Saying without attribution — "A job gives not only income but dignity."
- **Conclusion idea:** India's demographic advantage becomes a dividend only when every young hand finds productive work.

### 16. Cooperatives and self-help groups: the collective strength of rural Bihar

*Type:* descriptive. *Keywords:* cooperatives, SHGs, collective strength.

- **Intro idea:** A single farmer cannot bargain with a trader; a thousand farmers together can — this is how Amul began. Thesis: collective action is rural Bihar's most powerful tool against poverty.
- **Body points:**
  1. Meaning — voluntary groups owned by members; "each for all".
  2. History — Indian cooperative movement; Operation Flood (from 1970, Verghese Kurien).
  3. Bihar's dairy — COMFED (Sudha) and milk unions.
  4. JEEViKA — women's SHGs, village organisations, cluster federations; savings, credit, enterprises (numbers — check latest).
  5. Economic benefits — better prices, credit, bulk buying, processing.
  6. Social benefits — women's confidence, collective action on social issues.
  7. Policy — Ministry of Cooperation (2021); FPO scheme; PACS computerisation (check latest).
  8. Problems — political interference, poor management, elite capture, weak marketing.
  9. Way forward — professional management, digital accounts, market links, training.
- **Bihar examples:** Sudha; JEEViKA; Didi ki Rasoi; PACS in Bihar.
- **Quote:** "Sahakar se Samriddhi" (Prosperity through Cooperation) — motto of India's Ministry of Cooperation.
- **Conclusion idea:** Alone, the rural poor are weak; together, they are an economy.

### 17. Growth with equity: why inequality matters

*Type:* argumentative. *Keywords:* growth, equity (fairness), inequality.

- **Intro idea:** A shining mall in Patna and a flood-hit hut in Supaul in the same state. Thesis: growth that leaves many behind is neither fair nor lasting.
- **Body points:**
  1. Meaning — income, wealth and opportunity inequality; regional inequality.
  2. India's poverty decline — multidimensional poverty falling (NITI Aayog MPI, check latest).
  3. Regional gaps — Bihar's per-capita income far below national average (check latest).
  4. Causes — unequal education, land, jobs, health; urban-rural gaps.
  5. Why it matters — weak demand, social tension, wasted talent, democracy at risk.
  6. Tools — progressive taxation, Finance Commission transfers, welfare schemes (NFSA, MGNREGA), direct benefit transfer.
  7. Bihar-specific — need for special support for backward regions, debates on special category status (balanced).
  8. Counter-view — "some inequality rewards effort" → fair inequality vs unfair inequality of opportunity.
  9. Way forward — quality public education and health, skills, credit, rural infrastructure.
- **Bihar examples:** Regional gaps between north and south Bihar; JEEViKA; DBT in Bihar's schemes.
- **Quote:** Amartya Sen's idea of *Development as Freedom* — development means expanding real freedoms.
- **Conclusion idea:** A rising tide must lift all boats — or it is only a flood for some.

### 18. Food security and nutrition: from full stomachs to healthy bodies

*Type:* descriptive. *Keywords:* food security (enough food), nutrition (right food).

- **Intro idea:** India grows enough grain to fill its warehouses, yet many children are stunted. Thesis: the next goal is nutrition security, not just food security.
- **Body points:**
  1. Journey — famines → Green Revolution → surplus.
  2. Law — National Food Security Act 2013; PDS; free grain under PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (check latest).
  3. Nutrition gap — stunting, wasting, anaemia (NFHS-5; Bihar high — check latest).
  4. Causes — poor diets, women's health, sanitation, early marriage.
  5. Programmes — PM POSHAN (mid-day meal), ICDS anganwadis, Poshan Abhiyan, fortified rice.
  6. Bihar's role — One Nation One Ration Card benefits migrants; anganwadi reach.
  7. Agriculture link — millets, pulses, vegetables, crop diversity.
  8. Counter-view — "free food creates dependence" → food security is a basic right and investment.
  9. Way forward — diverse diets, women's education, clean water, monitoring through technology.
- **Bihar examples:** PDS reforms; anganwadis; NFHS-5 stunting data; makhana as a nutritious crop.
- **Quote:** Article 47 — duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and the standard of living and to improve public health.
- **Conclusion idea:** A nation is fed when its children are not only full but strong.

## Theme D — Science and technology

### 19. Digital India: bridging or widening the divide?

*Type:* argumentative ("or"). *Keywords:* digital India, bridging, widening, divide.

- **Intro idea:** A farmer receiving money directly in his bank account through his phone, and his neighbour who cannot read the SMS. Thesis: digital India bridges many gaps but creates new ones unless access and skills spread.
- **Body points:**
  1. Digital public infrastructure — Aadhaar, UPI (2016), DigiLocker, DBT.
  2. Gains — less leakage, faster payments, online services, e-learning, telemedicine.
  3. Bihar — RTPS online services, digital land records, online scholarships (check latest).
  4. Divides — rural-urban, gender (women own fewer phones), language, age, skill.
  5. Risks — cyber fraud, data privacy (Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023), exclusion when biometrics fail.
  6. Education — online learning during COVID showed the divide.
  7. Economy — digital commerce, start-ups, gig work.
  8. Counter-view — "digital is only for the elite" → UPI's spread among street vendors.
  9. Way forward — affordable devices, digital literacy, local-language content, assisted access points (CSCs), cyber-safety awareness.
- **Bihar examples:** RTPS counters; Common Service Centres; cyber-fraud hotspots and police response.
- **Quote:** Saying without attribution — "Technology is a good servant but a bad master."

## Practice

### Writing tasks

**Task 1 — Blind outlining (8 minutes per topic).** Pick any five topic titles above. Without looking at the outline, make your own in 8 minutes each. Then compare and note what you missed.

**Task 2 — Adapt an outline (15 minutes).** Adapt outlines to these new wordings: (a) "Migration: a mirror of regional inequality" (use 2 and 17); (b) "Rivers: Bihar's blessing and curse" (use 3 and 24); (c) "Technology cannot replace the teacher" (use 9 and 19).

**Task 3 — Full essays (60 minutes each).** In the Mains sprint, write full essays on at least 12 topics — at least one from each theme A to H.

**Task 4 — Philosophical intro drill (20 minutes).** Write introductions for topics 36–40 using a different opening type each time (story, quote, question, contrast, striking fact).

**Self-check rubric for each outline**

- [ ] The thesis uses the exact keywords of the topic.
- [ ] Body points cover at least six dimensions.
- [ ] At least three Bihar examples, placed in different body points.
- [ ] One clear counter-view with a reasoned answer.
- [ ] A way-forward point that is specific, not "government should do more".
- [ ] The quote is either surely attributed or given without a name.
- [ ] The conclusion idea links back to the introduction idea.
- [ ] All "check latest" facts have been updated from current sources.
