Structure gives an essay its shape; content gives it weight. In the exam hall you have no time to search your memory for a good example. You need a **ready bank** of quotes, examples and a few reliable figures that fit many topics. This chapter gives you that bank and shows you how to build your own notebook during the 300-day plan.

> **In simple words:** Collect three kinds of material: (1) a few quotes you are sure of, (2) many real examples — especially from Bihar — and (3) a small set of reliable facts and figures. Use examples freely, quotes sparingly, and numbers only when you are certain.

## The golden rules of content

1. **Accuracy first.** One wrong quote or wrong figure can make the examiner doubt the whole essay. If unsure, use a paraphrase without a name ("As a popular saying goes…") or write the trend instead of a number.
2. **Examples beat quotes.** A real story from a Bihar village is more convincing than a famous line.
3. **One or two quotes per essay is enough.** Typically one in the introduction or conclusion and perhaps one in the body.
4. **Spread Bihar examples across the essay**, not all in one paragraph.
5. **Data carries a date.** Write "according to Census 2011" or "NFHS-5 (2019–21)", so the examiner knows the source. Fast-changing figures must be updated from current affairs before the exam — every figure marked "check latest" below.
6. **Be fair.** Present government schemes with both achievements and gaps. Never write party-political praise or attacks.

### Quotes to avoid (often misattributed)

These lines are popular but either the speaker never said them in this form or the source is disputed. If you want the idea, write it **without a name**.

| Popular line | Problem | Safe way to use the idea |
|---|---|---|
| "Be the change you wish to see in the world" — Gandhi | Not his exact words; a later summary of his idea | "As the Gandhian idea goes, reform begins with oneself." |
| "It is not the strongest of the species that survives…" — Darwin | Not found in Darwin's writings | "Those who adapt, survive." |
| "The only thing necessary for the triumph of evil is for good men to do nothing" — Burke | Source not established | "Silence of good people allows wrong to grow." |
| "I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it" — Voltaire | Written by a later biographer summing up his views | "Freedom of speech means defending even views we dislike." |
| "Insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results" — Einstein | No reliable source | "Repeating the same method and expecting a new result is unwise." |
| "An eye for an eye makes the whole world blind" — Gandhi | Attribution not established | "Revenge only multiplies suffering." |
| "We do not inherit the earth from our ancestors, we borrow it from our children" | Origin unclear | Use without any name |

> **Common mistake:** Inventing a quote or giving a famous quote to a convenient name ("As Gandhiji said…") when you are not sure. Examiners know these lines. A paraphrase without a name is always safe.

### Turning a fact into an argument

- **Fact only:** "Bihar gave 50% reservation to women in panchayats in 2006."
- **Fact + meaning:** "Bihar gave 50% reservation to women in panchayats in 2006, the first state to do so. Within a few years, lakhs of women were sitting in panchayat offices for the first time, changing how villages see women's leadership."
- **Fact + meaning + link to topic:** "…This shows that when the state opens a door, women walk through it — but education decides how far they go."

## Building your own essay notebook

Begin on Day 1 of the plan, alongside GS study. Ten minutes a day is enough.

1. Keep **one notebook** (or one digital file) with **one page per theme**: Bihar development, women, education, health, agriculture, environment, technology, democracy, governance, culture, ethics, youth.
2. Each page has four columns: **Examples · Quotes · Data (with source and date) · Counter-views**.
3. While reading the newspaper, add **one example per day** to a theme page.
4. On every revision day (every 7th day), read two theme pages.
5. In the Mains sprint, mark the best two items on each page with a star for last-week revision.

## Practice

2. What is Gandhi's Talisman?
3. Which article directs the State to prohibit intoxicating drinks harmful to health?
4. Which Bihar man carved a path through a hill over about 22 years?
5. When and where did the Kosi embankment breach of 2008 happen?
6. What is Bihar's population density in Census 2011?
7. Why should you avoid "Be the change you wish to see in the world — Gandhi"?
8. Name Bihar's only tiger reserve.
9. In which year did Bihar give 50% panchayat reservation to women?
10. What are the four columns of an essay-notebook page?

### Answers

1. B. R. Ambedkar, in the Constituent Assembly on 25 November 1949.
2. Gandhi's advice (1948): before any step, recall the poorest person you have seen and ask whether the step will help him.
3. Article 47.
4. Dashrath Manjhi of Gehlaur, Gaya.
5. 18 August 2008, at Kusaha in Nepal.
6. 1,106 persons per sq km (highest among states).
7. It is not Gandhi's exact words; it is a later summary. Use the idea without the name.
8. Valmiki Tiger Reserve, West Champaran.
9. 2006.
10. Examples, Quotes, Data (with source and date), Counter-views.

### Writing tasks

**Task 1 — Build three theme pages (30 minutes).** Make notebook pages for "Women", "Environment" and "Democracy". Fill each with at least 4 examples (2 from Bihar), 1 quote and 1 counter-view.

**Task 2 — Fact to argument (15 minutes).** Take three facts from this chapter (e.g. Kusaha breach, JEEViKA, Census density) and turn each into a three-sentence "fact + meaning + link" unit for a topic of your choice.

**Task 3 — Paraphrase drill (10 minutes).** Rewrite three of the "quotes to avoid" as one-line ideas without attribution, in your own words.

**Task 4 — Evidence triangle (15 minutes).** For the topic "Disasters test the strength of governance", write one Bihar, one India and one world example with one sentence each.

**Self-check rubric**

- [ ] Every quote I used has a speaker I am sure of; otherwise it has no name.
- [ ] Every number I wrote has a source and year.
- [ ] Each theme page has at least two Bihar examples.
- [ ] Each "fact + meaning + link" unit ends by connecting to the topic.
- [ ] My counter-views are fair, not straw arguments.
- [ ] I marked every fast-changing item "check latest" in my notebook.
