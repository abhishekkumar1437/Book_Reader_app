Environment news is steady and predictable: a climate COP every November, new Ramsar sites around World Wetlands Day (2 February), tiger and dolphin counts, IUCN Red List updates, new laws. BPSC asks the convention behind the news (year, place, purpose), the protected area behind the news (state, district), and the Bihar link. This chapter gives all three. The ecology basics are in the Geography chapter [Soils, Vegetation and Wildlife](/interview/bpsc-2027/geography/soils-vegetation-and-wildlife) and Bihar's wildlife in [Agriculture, Minerals and Natural Resources of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/agriculture-minerals-and-natural-resources-of-bihar).

> **In simple words:** A **convention** is a treaty that countries sign and then **ratify** (formally accept) to solve a shared problem — climate change, ozone loss, wildlife trade. Each convention holds a **COP (Conference of the Parties)**, a regular meeting of all countries that joined. A **protocol** is a later, more detailed agreement under a convention (Kyoto Protocol under the UNFCCC). Learn: **name — year — place — purpose — India's role.**

## Climate change: UNFCCC, Kyoto, Paris

| Treaty | Adopted | In force | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UNFCCC** — UN Framework Convention on Climate Change | **1992** (Rio) | **21 March 1994** | Secretariat **Bonn**; aim: stabilise greenhouse gas concentrations; principle of **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)** — rich countries that polluted first must do more; first COP: **Berlin 1995** |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | **1997** (COP3, Kyoto) | **16 February 2005** | Binding emission cuts for developed (**Annex I**) countries only; market tools: **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)**, joint implementation, emissions trading; first period 2008–12; second (Doha Amendment) 2013–20 |
| **Paris Agreement** | **12 December 2015** (COP21) | **4 November 2016** | Keep warming **well below 2 °C**, try for **1.5 °C**; every country gives a **Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC)**, updated every **5 years**; **Global Stocktake** every 5 years (first at COP28, 2023). **India ratified on 2 October 2016** (Gandhi Jayanti). The USA left again from January 2026 (check latest) |

India has offered to host the climate **COP33 in 2028** (check latest). "Which COP did India host?" needs the **convention name** too.

### India's climate commitments

| Commitment | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Net zero** | By **2070** (announced at COP26) |
| **Updated NDC (August 2022)** | Cut **emissions intensity of GDP by 45%** by 2030 from the 2005 level; **about 50% of installed electric power capacity from non-fossil sources** by 2030; additional carbon sink of **2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂-equivalent** through forests by 2030; LiFE as a mass movement |
| **Panchamrit (COP26)** | 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; 50% of energy needs from renewables by 2030; cut 1 billion tonnes of projected emissions by 2030; 45% intensity cut; net zero 2070 |
| **Milestone** | India reached **50% non-fossil installed power capacity in 2025**, five years before the NDC date (check latest figure) |

| **Green Credit Programme** | 2023 — rewards voluntary actions like tree planting |
| **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme** | 2023 — an Indian carbon market |

> **Mains angle:** For "India's approach to climate change", use the balance: India's **per-capita emissions are far below the world average**; historic responsibility lies with developed countries (CBDR-RC); India still acts strongly (renewables, ISA, green hydrogen, LiFE). Bihar angle: heat waves, floods and drought in the same year, falling groundwater in south Bihar; **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali Abhiyan (2019)** is Bihar's climate-adaptation programme (check latest — see [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar)).

## Ozone layer

| Treaty | Year | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Vienna Convention** | **1985** | Framework to protect the ozone layer |
| **Montreal Protocol** | **1987** (in force 1989) | Phase out **ozone-depleting substances** like CFCs; called the most successful environmental treaty; **16 September = World Ozone Day** |
| **Kigali Amendment** | **2016** (Kigali, Rwanda) | Phase down **HFCs** — they do not harm ozone but are strong greenhouse gases. **India's group freezes HFC use in 2028 and starts cuts from 2032**, reaching 85% reduction by 2047. India ratified in 2021 |

> **Common mistake:** HFCs **do not** destroy ozone. They were brought in to replace CFCs, but they trap heat. That is why a **climate** goal was added to an **ozone** treaty (Kigali).

## Biodiversity

| Treaty | Year | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)** | **1992** (Rio); in force 1993 | Secretariat **Montreal**; three goals: **conservation, sustainable use, fair sharing of benefits** from genetic resources |
| **Cartagena Protocol** | 2000 (in force 2003) | **Biosafety** — safe movement of living modified organisms (GM crops) |
| **Nagoya Protocol** | **2010** (in force 2014) | **Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)** — communities share profits from their biological resources |
| **Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF)** | **December 2022** (CBD COP15) | **23 targets** for 2030, including **"30×30"** — protect **30% of land and 30% of oceans by 2030** |
| CBD COP16 | 2024, Cali (Colombia) | "Cali Fund" for benefits from digital sequence information |

**India:** **Biological Diversity Act 2002** (amended 2023) → **National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai)**, State Biodiversity Boards, and **Biodiversity Management Committees** in local bodies, which keep **People's Biodiversity Registers**.

## Wetlands: the Ramsar Convention

- **Signed:** **2 February 1971** at **Ramsar, Iran** (→ **World Wetlands Day, 2 February**). In force 1975. Secretariat: **Gland, Switzerland** (with IUCN).
- **India joined:** **1 February 1982**. India's **first Ramsar sites** (designated 1981): **Chilika Lake (Odisha)** and **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan)**.
- **Montreux Record:** a list of Ramsar sites under threat. Indian sites on it: **Keoladeo** and **Loktak (Manipur)**. Chilika was on it and was **removed in 2002** after restoration.
- **India's total:** about **100 or more** Ramsar sites as of 2026 — sources differ (figures of 98, 100 and 103 have been reported). **Check the latest number** on the Ramsar Sites Information Service or MoEFCC. **Tamil Nadu** has the most sites. The **Sundarbans (West Bengal)** is the largest by area.
- **Ramsar COP15:** Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe (2025).
- **India's rules:** **Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017**; **Amrit Dharohar** scheme (2023) for Ramsar sites.

## Wildlife trade and migratory species

| Treaty | Year | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **CITES** — Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora | **1973**, **Washington** (in force 1975) | Secretariat **Geneva**; India joined **1976**. **Appendix I** = trade banned (most endangered, e.g. tiger); **Appendix II** = trade controlled by permits; **Appendix III** = listed by one country seeking help. CoP20 held in Samarkand, Uzbekistan (2025) |
| **CMS** — Convention on Migratory Species (**Bonn Convention**) | **1979** | Secretariat Bonn; India a party since 1983; **COP13 at Gandhinagar (2020)**, mascot "Gibi" — the **Great Indian Bustard** |
| **International Whaling Commission** | 1946 | Commercial whaling moratorium since 1986 |

## Land, chemicals and oceans

| Treaty | Year | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **UNCCD** — UN Convention to Combat Desertification | **1994**, Paris (in force 1996) | Secretariat Bonn; **17 June = Desertification and Drought Day**; **India hosted COP14 (2019)**; India's target: **restore 26 million hectares** of degraded land by **2030** |
| **Basel Convention** | **1989** | Movement and disposal of **hazardous waste** across borders |
| **Rotterdam Convention** | 1998 | **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** for trade in hazardous chemicals and pesticides |
| **Stockholm Convention** | **2001** | **Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)** like DDT |
| **Minamata Convention** | **2013** (in force 2017) | **Mercury**; named after Minamata, Japan, where mercury poisoning harmed many people |
| **BBNJ Agreement** ("High Seas Treaty") | Adopted **2023** | Biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction — marine protected areas on the high seas; reached enough ratifications in 2025 and came into force in January 2026 (check). India signed in 2024 |
| **Global plastics treaty** | Talks since 2022 (UNEA resolution) | Negotiations at Busan (2024) and Geneva (2025) ended without agreement (check latest) |

> **Memory trick:** **"B-R-S"** chemical conventions in order of years — **B**asel 1989 (waste moves), **R**otterdam 1998 (consent before trade), **S**tockholm 2001 (POPs). Alphabetical and chronological at the same time.

| Law | Year | Key facts |

### Types of protected areas

| Type | Law / body | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **National Park** | Wildlife Protection Act | No human activity like grazing; stronger than a sanctuary |
| **Wildlife Sanctuary** | Wildlife Protection Act | Some regulated activity allowed |
| **Conservation Reserve** and **Community Reserve** | Added by the **2002 amendment** | Buffer and corridor areas; community land |
| **Tiger Reserve** | **Project Tiger (1973)**; **NTCA (2006)** | Core + buffer zones. **58 tiger reserves** (as of 2026 — check); latest: **Madhav (Madhya Pradesh, 2025)** |
| **Elephant Reserve** | **Project Elephant (1992)** | Not a legal protected area by itself |
| **Biosphere Reserve** | MoEFCC; UNESCO **Man and the Biosphere (MAB, 1971)** | **18** in India; 12–13 are on UNESCO's World Network (check latest); first: **Nilgiri (1986)** |
| **Eco-Sensitive Zone** | Environment Protection Act | Buffer around protected areas |

**Tiger numbers:** the **All India Tiger Estimation 2022** gave at least **3,167** tigers (mean estimate **3,682**) — about **75% of the world's wild tigers**. The **sixth cycle** began in 2026; results were awaited as of October 2026 (check latest).

**Cheetah:** Project Cheetah — cheetahs from **Namibia** were released in **Kuno National Park (Madhya Pradesh)** on **17 September 2022**; more from South Africa in 2023; **Gandhi Sagar Sanctuary** (MP) became the second site in 2025.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Convention | A treaty between countries on a shared problem |
| Protocol | A detailed follow-up agreement under a convention |
| Ratify | To formally accept a treaty so it becomes binding on a country |
| COP | Conference of the Parties — the regular meeting of all countries in a convention |
| NDC | Nationally Determined Contribution — each country's own climate pledge under Paris |
| CBDR-RC | Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities |
| Net zero | Emitting no more greenhouse gas than is removed from the air |
| Emissions intensity | Emissions per unit of GDP |
| Loss and damage | Help for countries already harmed by climate disasters |
| Oxbow lake | A curved lake formed when a meandering river cuts off one of its loops |
| Montreux Record | Ramsar list of wetlands under threat |
| Echolocation | Finding objects by sending sound and hearing the echo (used by dolphins and bats) |
| Core and buffer zone | Strictly protected centre of a reserve, and the surrounding area where limited activity is allowed |

**1. Wetlands of Bihar are both an ecological asset and a livelihood resource. Discuss with reference to the state's Ramsar sites and suggest measures for their conservation.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Bihar's floodplains hold thousands of wetlands (chaurs, maans, oxbow lakes); Ramsar recognition since 2020.
- Body:
  - Sites: Kanwar (Begusarai), Nagi and Nakti (Jamui), Gokul Jalashay (Buxar), Udaipur Jheel (West Champaran), Gogabil (Katihar — check).
  - Ecological value: flood buffering, groundwater recharge, migratory birds on the Central Asian Flyway, fish breeding.
  - Livelihoods: fishing, makhana, water chestnut (singhara), eco-tourism.
  - Threats: encroachment for farming, siltation, agricultural runoff, drying, invasive weeds, land disputes (Kanwar).
  - Measures: Wetlands Rules 2017, Amrit Dharohar, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali (renovating ponds and ahars-pynes), community management through Biodiversity Management Committees, eco-tourism, clear boundaries.
- **Conclusion:** Wetland conservation in Bihar is also flood management and rural income policy.

**2. Critically examine India's climate commitments under the Paris Agreement in light of the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

**3. "The Gangetic dolphin is an indicator of the health of the Ganga." Explain, and discuss conservation efforts with special reference to Bihar.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** National Aquatic Animal (2009); Endangered (IUCN); blind, echolocating.
- Body: why indicator (needs deep, clean, connected water); threats (dams and barrages, sand mining, pollution, fishing nets, ship traffic on National Waterway-1); efforts (Vikramshila sanctuary 1991, Project Dolphin 2020, National Dolphin Research Centre Patna, Namami Gange, dolphin survey 2025); community role (fisherfolk as "dolphin mitras"); more needed (flow regulation, sand-mining control, bycatch-free nets).
- **Conclusion:** Saving the dolphin means saving the river that sustains Bihar.
