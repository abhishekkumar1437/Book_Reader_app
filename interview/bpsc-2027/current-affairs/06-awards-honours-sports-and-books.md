Awards, sports and books are the "pure memory" part of current affairs. BPSC asks them in a few fixed forms: who won, in which field, for which work, at which venue, which trophy belongs to which sport. The winners change every year, but the facts about each award — who gives it, when it began, what it is for — do not. This chapter gives you those fixed facts, Bihar's links, and a simple tracker for the new winners.

> **In simple words:** For every award, learn **who gives it, since when, for what field, and one or two famous winners.** For every sports event, learn **venue, India's result and any record.** For books, learn **book ↔ author ↔ subject.** Then add the year's new names from the newspaper into the tracker at the end of this chapter.

## How these questions are framed

| Framing | What you need | Example (style only) |
|---|---|---|
| "X is given in which field?" | Field of the award | Dadasaheb Phalke Award → cinema |
| "Who gives the X award?" | Giving body | Jnanpith → Bharatiya Jnanpith trust |
| "Who received X in year Y?" | Winner (from your tracker) | The latest Jnanpith winner |
| "Match the award with the field" | Award ↔ field table | Pulitzer → journalism and literature |
| "Which trophy is related to which sport?" | Trophy ↔ sport table | Durand Cup → football |
| "Where were the Games held?" | Venue | Asian Games 2026 → Aichi-Nagoya, Japan |
| "Who wrote the book?" | Book ↔ author | *Maila Anchal* → Phanishwar Nath Renu |
| "Which person from Bihar received…?" | Bihar list | Bharat Ratna to Karpoori Thakur (2024) |

> **Prelims angle:** Many award questions are really static questions. "Which award is called the Asian Nobel?" (**Ramon Magsaysay**). "The first recipient of the Jnanpith Award was…" (**G. Sankara Kurup**, Malayalam, 1965). These never change. Learn them first; add the year's winners later.

## National civilian honours

| Honour | Instituted | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Bharat Ratna** | **2 January 1954** | **Highest civilian award**; any field of human endeavour (originally arts, literature, science and public service; widened in 2011); **maximum 3 a year** by rule (2024 was an exception with **5**); posthumous awards allowed from 1955; no cash; a peepal-leaf-shaped medallion; **about 53 recipients** (as of 2026 — check). First (1954): **C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C. V. Raman** |
| **Padma Vibhushan** | 1954 | Second-highest: "exceptional and distinguished service" |
| **Padma Bhushan** | 1954 | Third: "distinguished service of a high order" |
| **Padma Shri** | 1954 | Fourth: "distinguished service in any field" |

- Padma awards are **announced on the eve of Republic Day (25 January)** and presented by the President at Rashtrapati Bhavan, usually in March–April.
- The awards are **not titles**: **Article 18** forbids titles, and the Supreme Court in **Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (1995)** upheld the awards but said they must not be used as prefixes or suffixes to names.
- Since 2016, the public can nominate people for Padma awards online — many "unsung heroes" now receive them.

## Gallantry awards

| Wartime (in order) | Peacetime (in order) |
|---|---|
| **Param Vir Chakra** (highest; first recipient **Major Somnath Sharma**, 1947, posthumous) | **Ashoka Chakra** |
| **Maha Vir Chakra** | **Kirti Chakra** |

### The Nobel Prizes

- Set up by the will of **Alfred Nobel** (inventor of dynamite); first awarded **1901**.
- Fields: Physics, Chemistry, Medicine or Physiology, Literature, Peace; **Economics** added in **1968** by Sweden's central bank (Sveriges Riksbank) — first awarded 1969.
- **Peace** is given in **Oslo (Norway)**; the others in **Stockholm (Sweden)**. Announced in October, given on **10 December** (Nobel's death anniversary).
