Defence and space news gives BPSC some of its most direct current-affairs questions: "Exercise Yudh Abhyas is held between India and…", "Which mission studied the Sun from the L1 point?", "BrahMos is a joint venture with which country?". These answers stay the same year after year; only the newest test or launch is new. This chapter gives you the stable base so that the newspaper only has to add the latest item. For the science behind rockets and satellites, see [Space and Defence Technology](/interview/bpsc-2027/science/space-and-defence-technology) in the Science book.

> **In simple words:** When a missile is tested or a satellite is launched, remember four things: **name, type (what it does), who made it (DRDO, ISRO, a joint venture), and what is new (first test, longest range, first private launch).** For exercises, remember **partner country and service (army, navy, air force)**.

## India's missile family

### The IGMDP — where it began

The **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** was started in **1983** under **Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam** ("Missile Man of India") at **DRDO**. It aimed to build five missiles. The programme was formally closed in **2008** after its main goals were met.

> **Memory trick:** The five IGMDP missiles spell **"PATNA"**: **P**rithvi, **A**gni, **T**rishul, **N**ag, **A**kash. A Bihar reader will not forget it.

| Missile | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Prithvi** | Surface-to-surface, short range (about 150–350 km) | First IGMDP missile; **Dhanush** is the naval version |
| **Agni** | Surface-to-surface ballistic, medium to intercontinental range | The strategic (nuclear-capable) family |
| **Trishul** | Short-range surface-to-air | Development stopped |
| **Nag** | Anti-tank, "fire and forget" | Helicopter versions: **Helina** (Army), **Dhruvastra** (Air Force) |
| **Akash** | Medium-range surface-to-air | In service; **Akash-NG** next generation; exported (Armenia) |

### Strategic and tactical missiles in news

| Missile | Type | Range / key fact | Made by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agni-I to Agni-IV** | Ballistic, nuclear-capable | Ranges from about 700 km (Agni-I) to about 4,000 km (Agni-IV) | DRDO |
| **Agni-V** | Ballistic, near-ICBM class | Over **5,000 km**; **MIRV** test (**Mission Divyastra**, **11 March 2024**) — one missile carrying several warheads for different targets | DRDO |
| **Agni-Prime (Agni-P)** | Ballistic, canisterised (stored and fired from a sealed tube) | About 1,000–2,000 km; tested from a rail-based launcher (2025 — check) | DRDO |
| **K-15 (Sagarika), K-4** | Submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBM) | K-4 about 3,500 km; carried by the Arihant-class submarines | DRDO |
| **Shaurya** | Surface-to-surface | Land version of K-15 | DRDO |
| **Pralay** | Quasi-ballistic, short range | About 150–500 km; for the Army and Air Force | DRDO |
| **BrahMos** | **Supersonic cruise missile** (about Mach 2.8–3) | Joint venture of **DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya**; name from **Brahmaputra + Moskva** rivers; land, sea, air and submarine versions; range extended beyond the original 290 km; first export to the **Philippines** (deal 2022) | BrahMos Aerospace |
| **Nirbhay** | Subsonic long-range cruise missile | About 1,000 km; successor programme ITCM | DRDO |
| **Astra** | Beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile | Fired from Su-30MKI and Tejas | DRDO |
| **Rudram** | Anti-radiation missile (destroys enemy radars) | Air-launched | DRDO |
| **MRSAM / Barak-8** | Medium-range surface-to-air | Joint development with **Israel** (IAI) | DRDO + IAI |
| **QRSAM** | Quick-reaction surface-to-air | For the Army | DRDO |
| **Long-Range Anti-Ship Missile (LR-AShM)** | **Hypersonic** glide missile | First flight test **November 2024** (about 1,500 km class — check) | DRDO |

> **Prelims angle:** Learn the difference between **ballistic** and **cruise** missiles. A **ballistic missile** is rocket-powered only in the early part of its flight and then follows a high, curved (ballistic) path, like a thrown stone. A **cruise missile** flies low inside the atmosphere with a jet engine all the way, like an unmanned plane. **Agni and Prithvi are ballistic; BrahMos and Nirbhay are cruise.** **Supersonic** = faster than sound (Mach > 1); **hypersonic** = more than five times the speed of sound (Mach > 5).

## Space: ISRO and India's missions

### Institutions and history

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| **INCOSPAR** | 1962, under **Vikram Sarabhai** (Father of the Indian space programme) |
| **ISRO** | Founded **15 August 1969**; HQ **Bengaluru** |
| **Department of Space and Space Commission** | 1972 |
| **First rocket launch** | **21 November 1963**, from **Thumba** (near Thiruvananthapuram) — a US Nike-Apache sounding rocket |
| **Aryabhata** | **India's first satellite**, launched **19 April 1975** on a **Soviet** rocket |
| **SLV-3** | India's first satellite launch vehicle; put **Rohini** into orbit in **1980** (project director: Kalam) |
| **Rakesh Sharma** | First Indian in space, **1984** (Soviet Soyuz T-11) |
| **Spaceport** | **Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota** (Andhra Pradesh); a second spaceport at **Kulasekarapattinam** (Tamil Nadu) for SSLV (under construction — check) |
| **NSIL** (2019) | ISRO's commercial arm |
| **IN-SPACe** (2020) | Authorises and promotes private space companies |
| **Indian Space Policy** | 2023; FDI rules eased in 2024 |
| **First private rocket** | **Vikram-S** by **Skyroot Aerospace**, November 2022 (suborbital) |
| **ISRO Chairman** | **V. Narayanan** (from January 2025) — check latest |

> **Bihar connection:** India's first satellite is named after **Aryabhata**, the mathematician-astronomer of **Kusumapura (Pataliputra)** who wrote the **Aryabhatiya** (499 CE). **Taregna** near Masaurhi (Patna district) is linked with his observations; scientists gathered there for the **total solar eclipse of 22 July 2009**. Patna's **Aryabhatta Knowledge University** carries his name.

### Launch vehicles

| Vehicle | Full name | Use | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PSLV** | Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle | Satellites to polar / Sun-synchronous orbits | ISRO's "workhorse"; launched Chandrayaan-1, Mangalyaan, Aditya-L1; **104 satellites in one launch (2017)** |
| **GSLV** | Geosynchronous SLV | Heavier satellites to GTO | Uses an indigenous **cryogenic upper stage** |
| **LVM3** (GSLV Mk III) | Launch Vehicle Mark-3 | ISRO's heaviest rocket | Launched Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, OneWeb satellites; **human-rated version (HLVM3)** for Gaganyaan |
| **SSLV** | Small SLV | Small satellites, quick launch | Developmental flights 2022–24 |

> **Prelims angle:** **Cryogenic engine** = an engine using fuels cooled to very low temperatures (liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen). India's first indigenous cryogenic stage flew on **GSLV-D5 (2014)**.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Ballistic missile | Rocket-powered only at the start, then follows a high curved path |
| Cruise missile | Flies low with an engine the whole way, like a pilotless plane |
| Hypersonic | Faster than five times the speed of sound (Mach 5) |
| MIRV | Multiple Independently targetable Re-entry Vehicles — one missile, many warheads, many targets |
| Nuclear triad | Ability to launch nuclear weapons from land, air and sea |
| SSBN | Nuclear-powered submarine carrying ballistic missiles |
| ASAT | Anti-satellite weapon |
| Cryogenic engine | Rocket engine using super-cold liquid hydrogen and oxygen |
