Every month some index or report is released and newspapers print India's rank. BPSC turns these into easy-looking questions: "Who publishes the Global Gender Gap Report?", "India's rank in the Global Hunger Index 2026 was…", "Which state topped the SDG India Index?". The publisher and what the index measures stay the same for years; the rank changes every edition. This chapter gives you the stable part in tables and a short list of latest ranks to update.

> **In simple words:** An **index** is a single number made by combining many indicators (for example, hunger index = undernourishment + child stunting + child wasting + child deaths). A **report** is a document, often yearly, that may contain an index. Learn three things for each: **publisher, what it measures, India's latest rank.** The first two are stable. The third must be checked before the exam.

> **Common mistake:** Learning "India is 131st" without the publisher and the year. Exam questions ask the publisher more often than the rank, and a rank without a year is useless because it changes every edition.

Four things to check in any index news:

1. **Publisher** — which organisation, and is it a UN body, a think tank, an NGO or the Government of India?
2. **What it measures** — and the number of pillars or indicators if famous.
3. **Rank and number of countries** — "131st of 148" means more than "131st".
4. **Top and bottom** — the top country is often asked (Finland in happiness, Iceland in gender gap, Switzerland in innovation, Norway in press freedom — check each edition).

> **Prelims angle:** Questions sometimes give two correct-looking statements: "The Global Hunger Index is published by the FAO" (wrong — it is by **Welthungerhilfe and Concern Worldwide**) and "The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World (SOFI) is published by FAO with others" (correct). Index ↔ publisher pairs are the safest marks in current affairs.

## Global indices — the master table

### Human development, poverty and hunger

| Index / report | Publisher | What it measures | India — latest (as of 2026, check latest) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Human Development Index (HDI)** in the **Human Development Report** | **UNDP** | Three dimensions: **long and healthy life** (life expectancy), **knowledge** (mean and expected years of schooling), **decent standard of living** (GNI per capita, PPP). Scale 0–1 | HDR 2025 (data for 2023): **130th of 193**, value 0.685, "medium human development". Check for a newer HDR |
| **Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)** | **UNDP + OPHI** (Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative) | Health, education, living standards — 10 indicators | Check latest |
| **Global Hunger Index (GHI)** | **Welthungerhilfe** (Germany) + **Concern Worldwide** (Ireland) | Four indicators: **undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting, under-five mortality**. Lower score = less hunger | **GHI 2026: 100th of 122**, score 24.1, "serious" (2025: 102nd of 123). The Government of India has often disputed the method |
| **State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World (SOFI)** | **FAO** with IFAD, UNICEF, WFP, WHO | Hunger and malnutrition numbers | Check latest |
| **World Happiness Report** | **UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network (SDSN)**, Gallup and the Oxford Wellbeing Research Centre | Life evaluation from Gallup polls, explained by GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity, corruption | **2026: 116th** (2025: 118th); **Finland** has been first for many years (check latest) |
| **Sustainable Development Report / SDG Index** (global) | **SDSN** | Progress on the 17 SDGs | Check latest |
| **State of World Population** | **UNFPA** | Population trends | India became the **most populous country** in 2023 (UN estimate) |

## Indian national indices and reports

These are made by Indian bodies and rank **states**. They are the main way Bihar appears in index questions.

| Index / report | Publisher | What it measures | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SDG India Index** | **NITI Aayog** (with UN in India) | State progress on 16 SDGs; scores 0–100 | Fourth edition **2023–24** (released July 2024): India 71; **Kerala and Uttarakhand top (79)**; **Bihar lowest (57)** — check for a newer edition |
| **National Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)** | **NITI Aayog** (with OPHI and UNDP), based on **NFHS** data | 12 indicators across health, education, living standards | Progress Review 2023: **Bihar highest share of multidimensionally poor, 33.76% (2019–21)**, but also the **largest fall** (about 18 percentage points from 2015–16). NITI also said about **24.82 crore people** escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013–14 and 2022–23 (discussion paper, 2024) |
| **Export Preparedness Index** | **NITI Aayog** | States' export readiness | Check latest |
| **India Innovation Index** | **NITI Aayog** | State innovation | Check latest |
| **Fiscal Health Index** | **NITI Aayog** (first edition 2025) | State finances: expenditure quality, revenue, debt | Check latest Bihar position |
| **State Energy and Climate Index** | **NITI Aayog** | Energy access, efficiency, environment | — |
| **Good Governance Index** | **DARPG** (Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances) | Governance across 10 sectors | Last full edition 2021 (check latest) |
| **Business Reform Action Plan (BRAP)** | **DPIIT** | State ease of doing business reforms; now uses "Top achievers / Achievers / Aspirers" categories | Check Bihar's category |
| **Swachh Survekshan** | **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)** | Urban cleanliness survey (since 2016) | **Indore** topped for many years; check Patna and other Bihar cities |
| **Performance Grading Index (PGI)** | **Ministry of Education** | School education quality, state-wise and district-wise | Check latest |

| **State Food Safety Index** | **FSSAI** | Food safety performance of states | — |
| **ASER** — Annual Status of Education Report | **Pratham** (NGO) | Rural children's reading and arithmetic | ASER Centre surveys rural districts including Bihar |
| **India Justice Report** | Tata Trusts with partner organisations | Police, judiciary, prisons, legal aid by state | Check latest |
| **NFHS** — National Family Health Survey | **Ministry of Health** (IIPS Mumbai as nodal agency) | Health, nutrition, fertility; NFHS-5 (2019–21) | **Bihar had the highest TFR (about 3.0) in NFHS-5**; NFHS-6 results — check latest |
| **India State of Forest Report (ISFR)** | **Forest Survey of India**, Dehradun (biennial) | Forest and tree cover | ISFR 2023 released December 2024; Bihar's forest cover is low (under 10% of area) — check figure |

| **Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES)** | **NSO** | Consumption spending | 2022–23 and 2023–24 rounds — Bihar's spending among the lowest (check) |

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar often appears at the **bottom** of development indices (SDG India Index, MPI headcount, per-capita income, literacy in Census 2011 — **61.8%**, the lowest in India; female literacy **51.5%**). BPSC Mains wants the other half of the story too: Bihar has had **high growth rates** in several recent years, the **largest fall in multidimensional poverty**, big gains in roads, electrification and girls' school enrolment, and strong women's self-help groups (**JEEViKA**). Write both sides. Take the latest numbers from the **Bihar Economic Survey** and mark them with the year. See [Economy of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/economy-of-bihar).

> **Mains angle:** A typical GS question: "What does Bihar's position in the SDG India Index tell us? Suggest measures." Structure: what the index is (NITI, 16 SDGs, 0–100) → Bihar's score and rank (with year) → where Bihar does well (for example clean energy, peace and justice — check edition) and badly (poverty, education, health, industry) → reasons (high population density, floods, low urbanisation, low industrial base, low per-capita revenue) → measures (Saat Nischay, JEEViKA, health and education investment, industrial policy, flood management) → conclusion.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Index | A single number combining many indicators to compare countries or states |
| Indicator | One measured item, such as child stunting or life expectancy |
| Stunting | Low height for age in children — a sign of long-term undernutrition |
| Wasting | Low weight for height in children — a sign of acute undernutrition |
| PPP (purchasing power parity) | A way of comparing incomes after adjusting for price differences between countries |
| Multidimensional poverty | Poverty measured by many deprivations (health, education, living standards), not only income |
| Headcount ratio | The percentage of people who are poor by a given measure |
| Edition | The year a report is released (the data may be for an earlier year) |
| NFHS | National Family Health Survey — India's large health and nutrition survey |
| SDGs | 17 Sustainable Development Goals adopted by the UN in 2015, to be reached by 2030 |

### Mains questions

**1. "Global indices often produce headlines, but their methods are debated." Examine with reference to the Global Hunger Index and India's response.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** GHI by Welthungerhilfe and Concern Worldwide; four indicators; India in the "serious" category (latest rank with year).
- Body:
  - Method: undernourishment (one-third), child stunting and wasting (one-third), under-five mortality (one-third).
  - India's objections: three of four indicators are about children; undernourishment based on small samples; does not reflect PDS and free grain schemes.
  - Real concerns: child wasting and stunting are high (NFHS data), anaemia.
  - Use of indices: they focus attention and allow comparison; limits: data gaps, timing, weighting.
  - Policy responses: PM-POSHAN, Poshan Abhiyaan, PMGKAY, fortified rice, Anganwadi strengthening.
  - Bihar: high stunting in NFHS-5; JEEViKA nutrition work, ICDS reach.
- **Conclusion:** Treat indices as a mirror, not a verdict — improve data and act on real gaps.

**2. Bihar ranks low in several development indices but has shown rapid improvement in some. Analyse the reasons and suggest a way forward.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Cite SDG India Index (NITI, latest edition, Bihar's score) and NITI MPI (highest share, largest fall).
- Body:
  - Reasons for low base: high population density, floods in the north and drought in the south, small industrial base after the 2000 bifurcation, low urbanisation, migration.
  - Improvements: roads, electricity to all households, girls' enrolment (cycle scheme), JEEViKA women's SHGs, reduced poverty headcount.
  - Remaining gaps: learning levels, health workforce, nutrition, jobs, per-capita income.
  - Way forward: industrial and food-processing push (makhana, litchi), skilling, urban development, flood management with Nepal, better data.
- **Conclusion:** Indices show both the scale of the challenge and the possibility of fast change; Bihar's strategy must target its weakest indicators.
