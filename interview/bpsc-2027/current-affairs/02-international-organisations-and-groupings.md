Almost every international news item involves an organisation or a grouping: a summit, a new member, a new head, a report. BPSC asks the static facts behind these — headquarters, founding year, members, India's role — far more often than the news itself. This chapter gives those facts in tables you can revise quickly.

> **In simple words:** An international organisation is a body that countries create by agreement, usually with a permanent office (secretariat). A grouping is a looser club of countries that meets at summits, sometimes without a permanent office. Learn each one by five facts: **what it is, when it began, where its HQ is, who the members are, and what India does in it.**

> **Remember:** Member counts, heads of organisations and summit hosts change. Everything marked "(as of 2026 — check the latest)" must be updated from your newspaper before the exam.

## The United Nations system

The **United Nations (UN)** was created on **24 October 1945** (UN Day) when the UN Charter came into force. The Charter was signed at the **San Francisco Conference** on 26 June 1945. It replaced the **League of Nations** (1920, Geneva). The HQ is in **New York**. It has **193 member states**; the last to join was **South Sudan (2011)**. The **Holy See (Vatican)** and **Palestine** are non-member observer states.

**India** was a founding member: it signed the Charter in 1945 (as British India) and joined on 30 October 1945.

### The six principal organs

| Organ | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **General Assembly (UNGA)** | All 193 members, one vote each; regular session opens every September; a new President each year; decisions on "important questions" need a two-thirds majority |
| **Security Council (UNSC)** | **15 members**: **5 permanent (P5)** — USA, UK, France, Russia, China — with **veto** power; **10 non-permanent** members elected for **2-year** terms by the UNGA; main body for peace and security |
| **Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)** | 54 members elected for 3 years; coordinates economic and social work, specialised agencies |
| **Trusteeship Council** | Supervised trust territories; **suspended operation in 1994** after Palau became independent |
| **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** | **The Hague (Netherlands)**; 15 judges, 9-year terms; settles disputes between states. Indian judges have included Nagendra Singh (also President of the Court) and **Dalveer Bhandari** |
| **Secretariat** | Headed by the **Secretary-General**, appointed by the UNGA on the recommendation of the UNSC, for 5-year terms |

**India in the UNSC:** India has been a non-permanent member **eight times**: 1950–51, 1967–68, 1972–73, 1977–78, 1984–85, 1991–92, 2011–12 and 2021–22. India seeks a **permanent seat** and is part of the **G4** (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan), which supports each other's claims. The opposing group is **"Uniting for Consensus"** (often called the Coffee Club, led by Italy, with Pakistan among members).

**Secretary-General:** **António Guterres** (Portugal) has served since 1 January 2017; his second term ends on **31 December 2026**. The selection of his successor was under way in 2026 (candidates included Michelle Bachelet, Rafael Grossi and Rebeca Grynspan). **(as of October 2026 — check who was appointed and takes office on 1 January 2027.)**

> **Prelims angle:** A common trap: "The ICJ is in Geneva." Wrong — the ICJ and the International Criminal Court (ICC) are both in **The Hague**. The ICJ is a UN organ that hears **disputes between countries**; the ICC (Rome Statute 1998, working since 2002) tries **individuals** for genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity. **India is not a member of the ICC.**

> **Memory trick:** "**Rome feeds**" — the three food bodies (**FAO, IFAD, WFP**) are all in **Rome**. "**Nairobi is green**" — UNEP and UN-Habitat are in Nairobi, the only UN global HQs in the Global South. "**Vienna is atomic and industrial**" — IAEA, UNIDO (and OPEC, though not UN).

> **Mains angle:** "Reform of the UN Security Council" is a stable Mains theme. Points: the P5 reflect 1945 power; Africa and Latin America have no permanent seat; India is the most populous country, a large economy and a top UN peacekeeping contributor; the G4 and the African Union's Ezulwini Consensus want expansion; the veto blocks action (examples: conflicts where the Council could not act). Way forward: expansion in both categories, limits on veto use, regular review.

## G20

The **Group of Twenty** began in **1999** as a meeting of finance ministers and central bank governors after the **Asian financial crisis (1997)**. It became a **leaders' summit in 2008** after the global financial crisis (first summit: Washington).

- **Members:** 19 countries — Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Türkiye, UK, USA — plus the **European Union** and the **African Union**.
- The **African Union** became a **permanent member** at the **New Delhi summit (September 2023)** under India's presidency.
- **No permanent secretariat.** The **presidency** rotates every year; the past, present and next presidents form the **troika**.
- Members hold about 85% of world GDP, 75% of world trade and two-thirds of world population (commonly quoted figures).

## BRICS

- **Origin:** The term "BRIC" was coined in **2001** by Goldman Sachs economist **Jim O'Neill**. Foreign ministers first met in 2006; the **first summit was in 2009 at Yekaterinburg (Russia)**. **South Africa** joined in 2010 (first summit as member 2011), making it BRICS.
- **Expansion:** From **1 January 2024**, **Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and the UAE** joined. **Saudi Arabia** was invited; its status has been unclear. **Indonesia** joined in **January 2025**. Members as of 2026: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Indonesia, and Saudi Arabia (often listed) **(as of 2026 — check the latest)**.
- **Partner countries:** A new "partner" category was created at the **Kazan summit (2024)**. Partners include Belarus, Bolivia, Kazakhstan, Cuba, Malaysia, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Nigeria and Vietnam **(check the latest)**.
- **Institutions:** **New Development Bank** (Shanghai) and the **Contingent Reserve Arrangement** (a reserve pool for balance-of-payments stress), both agreed at **Fortaleza (2014)**.
- **India's chairs:** 2012, 2016, 2021 and **2026**. India hosted the **18th BRICS summit in New Delhi on 12–13 September 2026** (check the declaration's main points from your newspaper).

## Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

- **Origin:** The **Shanghai Five** (1996: China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan). With **Uzbekistan** it became the **SCO in June 2001** at Shanghai.
- **HQ:** Secretariat in **Beijing**; the **Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure (RATS)** is in **Tashkent**.
- **Official languages:** Russian and Chinese.
- **Members (10):** China, Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, **India and Pakistan (2017, Astana summit)**, **Iran (2023)**, **Belarus (2024)**.
- **India:** Observer from 2005; full member 2017; held the chair in **2023** (virtual summit, Iran admitted). India has not signed on to China's Belt and Road Initiative language in SCO statements.
- **Recent chairs:** Kazakhstan 2024 (Astana), China 2025 (Tianjin), Kyrgyzstan 2026 (Bishkek summit, 31 August–1 September 2026), **Pakistan for 2026–27** **(as of 2026 — check the latest)**.

> **Common mistake:** The SCO **secretariat is in Beijing**, not Shanghai. Shanghai is where it was founded — and where the **NDB** (BRICS bank) has its HQ.

## ASEAN

- **Founded:** **8 August 1967**, **Bangkok Declaration**, by five countries — Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand.
- **HQ:** **Jakarta**.
- **Members:** the five founders + Brunei (1984), Vietnam (1995), Laos and Myanmar (1997), Cambodia (1999) = 10; **Timor-Leste** was admitted as the **11th member in October 2025** **(check the latest)**.
- **India and ASEAN:** **Look East Policy (1991, P. V. Narasimha Rao)** → **Act East Policy (2014)**. India became a sectoral dialogue partner in 1992, full dialogue partner in 1995/96, summit-level partner in 2002, Strategic Partnership in 2012 and **Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in 2022**. The **ASEAN–India Trade in Goods Agreement** came into effect in 2010 (signed 2009); a review is under way (check latest).
- **Related forums:** ASEAN Regional Forum (1994, security dialogue); **East Asia Summit** (2005, India a founding participant); **RCEP** — trade deal of ASEAN + China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand; **India walked out in November 2019**.

> **Bihar connection:** Bihar's Buddhist sites — **Bodh Gaya, Rajgir, Nalanda, Vaishali** — are a cultural link with ASEAN countries such as Thailand, Myanmar, Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia. The new **Nalanda University** (Nalanda University Act 2010; new campus inaugurated 2024) was supported by East Asia Summit countries. Expect questions linking "Act East" and Buddhist circuit tourism.

> **Prelims angle:** "Which of these is a member of BIMSTEC but not SAARC?" → **Myanmar or Thailand**. "Which is in SAARC but not BIMSTEC?" → **Pakistan, Afghanistan, Maldives**.

> **Bihar connection:** **Nepal** shares a long border with Bihar (seven Bihar districts touch Nepal: West Champaran, East Champaran, Sitamarhi, Madhubani, Supaul, Araria, Kishanganj). Bihar's floods (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati) begin in Nepal; the **Kosi Agreement (1954)** and **Gandak Agreement (1959)** are India–Nepal treaties. **Raxaul–Birgunj** is a major trade point with an Integrated Check Post; the **Jaynagar (Bihar)–Kurtha (Nepal)** cross-border railway restarted in 2022. India–Nepal news always has a Bihar angle.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Specialised agency | An independent international organisation linked to the UN by agreement (e.g. WHO, FAO) |
| Veto | The power of each P5 member to block a UNSC decision by voting "no" |
| P5 | The five permanent members of the UN Security Council |
| G4 | India, Brazil, Germany and Japan — supporting each other for permanent UNSC seats |
| Secretariat | The permanent office that runs an organisation day to day |
| Troika | Past, present and next presidents of a grouping working together (used in G20) |
| Observer | A country or body that attends meetings but cannot vote |
| Bretton Woods institutions | The IMF and World Bank, created at the 1944 conference in Bretton Woods, USA |
| Dialogue partner | A country with which ASEAN holds regular formal talks |
| Export-control regime | A group of countries that agree to control the export of sensitive goods (MTCR, NSG) |
| Act East Policy | India's 2014 policy (upgrading the 1991 Look East Policy) of closer ties with South-East and East Asia |

### Mains questions

**1. Examine the significance of BIMSTEC for India's "Neighbourhood First" and "Act East" policies. How can Bihar benefit from stronger regional connectivity?** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** BIMSTEC (1997) links five South Asian and two South-East Asian Bay of Bengal states; Charter 2022 gave it legal identity.
- Body:
  - Bridge between Neighbourhood First and Act East; alternative while SAARC is stalled.
  - Sectors: India leads security; also trade, energy, transport, disaster management.
  - Connectivity projects: Kaladan multi-modal project, India–Myanmar–Thailand trilateral highway, BBIN.
  - Energy: grid links with Nepal and Bhutan (hydropower).
  - Challenges: slow FTA, Myanmar instability, low funding, small secretariat.
  - Bihar: border with Nepal, Raxaul ICP and Jaynagar–Kurtha rail; Buddhist tourism with Thailand and Myanmar; flood cooperation with Nepal; market access for makhana, litchi and textiles.
- **Conclusion:** A working BIMSTEC can make eastern India, including Bihar, a gateway rather than a periphery.

**2. "The UN Security Council reflects the world of 1945, not of today." Discuss with reference to India's claim for a permanent seat.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** UNSC — 15 members, P5 with veto, created in 1945.
- Body:
  - Unrepresentative: no permanent seat for Africa, Latin America or India.
  - India's case: most populous country, major economy, nuclear power with a no-first-use doctrine, long peacekeeping record, eight elected terms.
  - Veto paralysis on major conflicts.
  - Reform proposals: G4, African Union's Ezulwini Consensus; opposition by Uniting for Consensus.
  - Obstacles: Charter amendment needs two-thirds of the UNGA and all P5.
  - India's approach: G4 diplomacy, Global South voice (Voice of Global South Summit, G20 African Union entry).
- **Conclusion:** Reform is needed for legitimacy; India should build coalitions while showing leadership in global public goods.

**3. Assess the role of groupings like the Quad, BRICS and the SCO in India's multi-alignment foreign policy.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** India is in groupings with rival powers — "multi-alignment" or strategic autonomy.
- Body: Quad (maritime security, technology, vaccines, balancing China); BRICS (Global South finance, NDB, reform of global institutions); SCO (Central Asia, counter-terrorism, energy, Afghanistan); tensions (China in BRICS/SCO, Russia–West conflict); benefits (bargaining power, diverse partners, access to energy and technology); limits (consensus is slow, expansion dilutes BRICS).
- **Conclusion:** Multi-alignment serves national interest if India keeps clear red lines, such as on terrorism and sovereignty.
