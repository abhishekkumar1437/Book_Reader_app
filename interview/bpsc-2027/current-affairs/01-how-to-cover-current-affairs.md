This book was written in October 2026, so it cannot give you the news of 2027. What it gives you is the **method** for covering news and the **static background** that almost every current-affairs question stands on. BPSC asks "current events of national and international importance" in Prelims and again in Mains GS Paper I. In both papers, the question is about the news, but the marks usually come from the background.

> **In simple words:** News changes every day, but the background behind the news changes slowly. If you know what the SCO is, who publishes the Global Hunger Index, and what the Ramsar Convention does, then any new headline about them becomes easy. This book builds that background. Your daily newspaper adds the new facts on top.

## Why current affairs matters in BPSC

Current affairs is not one subject. It is a layer that sits on top of every subject.

| Paper | Where current affairs appears | What it needs from you |
|---|---|---|
| Prelims GS | "Current events of national and international importance" is its own syllabus line; news also shapes polity, economy, science and environment questions | Exact facts: names, places, dates, ranks, members, first/largest |
| Mains GS Paper I | Section 2: "Current events of national and international importance" | Understanding: causes, impact on India and Bihar, way forward |
| Mains GS Paper II | Polity, economy and science questions often start from a recent event (a new law, a budget, a mission) | Linking the event to the static syllabus |
| Essay | Topics like climate change, women's empowerment, technology | Recent examples and data to support arguments |
| Interview | "What is in the news today?" Questions on Bihar's recent developments | Calm, balanced opinion with facts |

> **Prelims angle:** In state PSC papers, current-affairs MCQs are usually **fact-based**: "Which country hosted…", "Which state/district got…", "Who is the author of…", "Which organisation publishes…". Many of them are really static questions with a news hook. For example, a news item says "India hosted the BRICS summit in 2026". The question may ask which country joined BRICS in 2025 or where the New Development Bank is headquartered. Both answers are static.

> **Mains angle:** Mains GS I current-events questions ask you to "discuss", "examine" or "critically analyse". You need the background (what the issue is), the present (what happened recently), the impact (on India and Bihar) and a way forward. Chapter 08 gives a one-page template for exactly this.

- **Layer 1 — the static background.** What is this organisation, law, index, mission, species, place? When was it created, by whom, with what aim? This layer is in this book (chapters 02–07) and in your subject books.
- **Layer 2 — the news fact.** What happened, when, where, who, and the one number that matters. This layer comes from your newspaper and monthly magazine.

When you read a news item, ask: "Do I know the background?" If not, look it up once, write it in your notes, and you will not need to look it up again.

> **Remember:** If you can explain the background of a news item in two lines, you have understood it. If you can only repeat the headline, you have not.

## What period to cover for a 2027 Prelims

BPSC does not announce a fixed period for current affairs. From the pattern of state PSC papers, a safe working rule is: **cover the 12–18 months before the Prelims, with the most weight on the last 12 months.**

This book follows the assumed plan in the [BPSC 2027 Roadmap](/interview/bpsc-2027/roadmap/how-to-use-this-roadmap): if you start on 1 November 2026, Day 300 (the day before Prelims) is about 27 August 2027. Shift the dates when the official Prelims date is announced on **bpsc.bihar.gov.in**.

| Period | Weight | How you cover it |
|---|---|---|
| March 2026 – October 2026 (before you start) | Medium | Back-fill from **monthly compilations** (one per month), not old newspapers. Do this during the CA backgrounders block (Days 183–196) and in spare time. |
| November 2026 – about July 2027 | High | Daily newspaper (1 hour), your own notes, monthly compilation |
| The last 4–6 weeks before Prelims | Medium–High | A "last months" compilation, then direct revision; news this close to the exam appears less often because the paper is set earlier |
| Older big events (2023–2025) | Low but real | Only the very big ones: G20 India presidency 2023, Chandrayaan-3, new criminal laws, census plans, major Bihar events |

> **Common mistake:** Students start current affairs from "January 2027" because it feels clean. BPSC papers are set months before the exam date, and questions often come from the year before. Cover at least from **March 2026** onwards.

> **Prelims angle:** Mains GS I asks about the period up to the Mains exam. For Mains (roughly 2–3 months after Prelims), extend your notes up to Mains and consolidate one full year by theme during the Mains sprint (Days 301–390).

## Sources: fewer and better

The biggest mistake in current affairs is too many sources. Choose one source per need and stay with it.

| Need | Use | How much time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| National and international news | **One** national English newspaper (The Hindu or The Indian Express) | 45–60 min a day | Read the front page, national, international, economy, science and editorial pages |
| Bihar news | **One** Bihar edition of a Hindi or English newspaper (for example, the Patna edition of a major daily) | 10–15 min a day | Bihar government decisions, new schemes, Bihar in national indices, cabinet decisions |
| Official facts | **PIB** (pib.gov.in) — Press Information Bureau | As needed | The government's own press releases; best for scheme details, launch dates, numbers |
| Bihar official facts | state.bihar.gov.in, department sites, **Bihar Economic Survey** (finance.bihar.gov.in) | As needed | Use the latest Survey (usually released with the state budget in February–March) |
| Monthly consolidation | **One** monthly current-affairs magazine or compilation from a reliable coaching publisher | 4–6 hours per month | Use it to check what you missed, not as a replacement for your own reading |
| Year-end facts | One yearbook-style source (for example, India Year Book by the Publications Division, or a standard annual GK book) | Once, in Phase 4 | For awards, sports, appointments of the year |
| Budget and Survey | Union Budget and Economic Survey of India (February), Bihar Budget and Bihar Economic Survey | Summaries only | Key numbers, new schemes, themes |

> **Common mistake:** Reading three newspapers, two magazines and five YouTube channels. You will read the same news five times and remember none of it. One newspaper + PIB + one monthly compilation + one Bihar source is enough.

> **Bihar connection:** BPSC regularly asks about Bihar's own recent developments — a new Ramsar site in Bihar, a GI tag for a Bihar product, a sports event in Rajgir, a new state scheme, Bihar's position in a NITI Aayog index. Keep a **separate Bihar section** in every month's notes. The [Bihar Special](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special) book has the static base; [Government Schemes and Policies of Bihar](/interview/bpsc-2027/bihar-special/government-schemes-and-policies-of-bihar) is where most Bihar news connects.

### What to read and what to skip in the newspaper

| Read carefully | Read quickly | Skip |
|---|---|---|
| Government decisions (cabinet, new laws, schemes) | Opinion columns — only the main argument | Party politics, election speeches, allegations |
| Supreme Court / High Court judgments on constitutional issues | Business pages — only policy, RBI, GDP, inflation, trade | Crime stories, accidents (unless a policy follows) |
| International summits and India's foreign relations | Sports — only major events, records, India's medals | Celebrity and film gossip |
| Reports and indices (India's rank, who published) | Science page — Indian missions and big discoveries | Stock-market daily movements |
| Environment: new protected areas, species, conventions | State news of other states — only if national significance | Local city news outside Bihar |
| Bihar: state decisions, projects, awards, indices | | |

How to read the newspaper in detail is also covered in the Exam Guide chapter [Reading the Newspaper and Making Notes](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/reading-the-newspaper-and-making-notes).

## The five questions for every news item

Before you write a note, answer five short questions. If you cannot answer them, the item is either not important or you need the background first.

1. **What happened?** One line.
2. **Where does it fit in the syllabus?** Polity, economy, environment, science, international relations, Bihar.
3. **What is the static background?** Organisation / law / index / place / species — two or three lines.
4. **What is the exam fact?** The one name, number, date or place an MCQ could ask.
5. **Mains value?** Is there a "why it matters" for India and Bihar? If yes, note 2–3 points.

> **Memory trick:** **W-S-B-F-M** — "**W**hat, **S**yllabus, **B**ackground, **F**act, **M**ains". Say it in your head while reading.

### Worked example

News: "Two wetlands in Bihar declared Ramsar sites" (this happened in 2025).

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What happened? | Gokul Jalashay (Buxar) and Udaipur Jheel (West Champaran) were added to the Ramsar list |
| Syllabus | Environment; Geography of Bihar |
| Background | Ramsar Convention 1971 (Ramsar, Iran); India joined 1982; sites are wetlands of international importance; Bihar's earlier sites Kanwar Jheel, Nagi, Nakti |
| Exam fact | District of each site; Bihar's total number of Ramsar sites (check latest) |
| Mains value | Wetlands as flood buffers in the Gangetic plain, fisheries, migratory birds, eco-tourism |

### Daily (1 hour)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 40 min | National newspaper — mark items with a pen or highlighter as you read |
| 10 min | Bihar newspaper — state decisions, projects, indices, awards |
| 10 min | Write notes in the **daily template** (Chapter 08) — 5–10 lines a day, not more |

Read the newspaper at a fixed time (morning is best). On busy days, never skip — do 20 minutes at least.

### Weekly (on every 7th day — the revision day)

- Read your 7 days of notes once.
- Check **PIB** for the week's important releases on topics you marked (scheme numbers, launch dates).
- Write 10 recall questions from the week's notes and answer them without looking.
- Move "background" items into your **static topic files** (organisations, indices, schemes, environment, defence/space, awards/sports — the six files that match chapters 02–07).

### Monthly (one sitting of 4–6 hours, first weekend of the month)

- Read one monthly compilation for the previous month. Tick what you already have; add only what is missing.
- Make a **one-page monthly summary** (template in Chapter 08).
- Update the tracker tables: schemes (Chapter 07), indices (Chapter 03), Bihar section.
- Redo the recall questions of the month.

> **Remember:** Notes are for revision, not for record-keeping. If a month's notes are longer than 10–12 pages, you are copying the newspaper. Cut.

## Categories of current-affairs questions

Most current-affairs questions in state PSC papers fall into a few groups. Knowing the groups tells you what to note.

| Category | Typical question idea | What to note |
|---|---|---|
| International organisations and summits | Host of a summit, new member, HQ, India's role | Host city/country, new members, theme, India's position |
| Indices and reports | India's rank; who publishes an index | Publisher, India's rank, top country, Bihar position in national indices |
| Government schemes | Launch year, ministry, beneficiaries, target | Name, year, ministry, aim, one key number |
| Appointments | Who heads an organisation | Name, organisation, from when — always "check latest" before the exam |
| Awards and honours | Who won, for what, which field | Winner, field, book/film name |
| Sports | Venue, winner, India's medals, records | Event, place, India's result, Bihar link |
| Science and technology | Mission name, aim, launch vehicle, first-of-its-kind | Mission, agency, vehicle, date, "first" |
| Defence | Exercise with which country, missile type | Exercise ↔ country, missile range/type |
| Environment | New Ramsar site, tiger reserve, species status, COP host | Place, state/district, convention |
| Places in news | Country/strait/city in a conflict or event | Location on map, neighbours |
| Bihar | State scheme, project, GI tag, ranking, event | District, year, department |
| Days and themes | "World Environment Day theme" | Date (stable); theme (changes yearly) |

> **Prelims angle:** Appointments and "themes of days" change every year and are easy to confuse. Note them only in the last 3–4 months before Prelims, in one sheet, and revise that sheet many times.

## Reading news with a map and a dictionary

- **Map:** Every international news place (a strait, a disputed region, a summit city) — find it on an atlas the same day. Note neighbours and water bodies. Map questions are common in Prelims.
- **Dictionary of terms:** Keep a running list of new terms (for example "loss and damage fund", "BRICS partner country", "carbon credit"). Write each in one simple line. This feeds the Key terms box of your monthly summary.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Reading news without notes | 10 minutes of notes every day, in a fixed format |
| Making notes but never revising | Weekly revision on the 7th day; monthly recall questions |
| Using too many sources | One newspaper, PIB, one compilation, one Bihar source |
| Ignoring Bihar news | Separate Bihar section every month |
| Learning ranks without the publisher | Always write "index — publisher — India's rank — top country" |
| Trusting old ranks or office holders | Mark every changeable item "check latest" and update before the exam |
| Starting current affairs late | Start on Day 1; back-fill older months from compilations |
| Treating current affairs as only Prelims | Note "Mains value" for big issues; write one CA answer a week from Day 197 |

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Current affairs | Events of the recent past (here, 12–18 months) that matter for the nation, the world or Bihar |
| Static background | The stable facts behind a news item — what an organisation, law or index is |
| PIB | Press Information Bureau — the Government of India's official press-release service |
| Monthly compilation | A magazine or booklet that collects one month's important news |
| Economic Survey | Annual government report on the state of the economy, released before the budget (Union and Bihar both publish one) |
| Tracker | A table you keep updating (schemes, indices, appointments) |
| Back-fill | Covering months you missed, using compilations instead of old newspapers |
| Mains value | Whether a news item can give points for a descriptive answer |

### Mains questions

**1. "Current affairs is a layer over the static syllabus, not a separate subject." Explain with examples how a BPSC aspirant should integrate news with static preparation.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** The syllabus lists current events separately, but most news questions are answered with static knowledge.
- Body:
  - Example 1 — a Ramsar site in Bihar: needs Ramsar Convention (1971), wetland ecology, Bihar geography.
  - Example 2 — a BRICS summit: needs BRICS history, NDB, India's foreign policy goals.
  - Example 3 — a new rural employment law: needs MGNREGA background, federal funding, rural economy of Bihar.
  - Method: two layers (background + fact); five questions (W-S-B-F-M).
  - Notes: daily notes feeding static topic files; trackers for schemes and indices.
  - Benefit for Mains: background gives analysis; news gives freshness; Bihar angle gives relevance.
- **Conclusion:** Integration saves time and gives depth; the aspirant reads news as an extension of the syllabus.

**2. Describe an efficient routine for covering current affairs for a state civil services examination, and the common mistakes to avoid.** (38 marks, ~300 words)

Model answer outline:
- **Intro:** Current affairs is vast; efficiency comes from limited sources and fixed routines.
- Body:
  - Sources: one national newspaper, PIB, one monthly compilation, Bihar paper and Economic Survey.
  - Daily: 1 hour with fixed split; short notes in a template.
  - Weekly: revision and recall test; move background to topic files.
  - Monthly: compilation to fill gaps; one-page summary; tracker updates.
  - Period: 12–18 months, back-filled from compilations.
  - Mistakes: too many sources, no revision, ignoring Bihar, old ranks, starting late.
- **Conclusion:** A small, repeated routine beats a large, irregular one.
