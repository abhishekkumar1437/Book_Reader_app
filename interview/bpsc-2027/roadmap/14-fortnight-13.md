## What you will learn

**Languages and literature.** **Maithili** (Eighth Schedule, 2003; poet **Vidyapati**, 14th–15th century), **Bhojpuri** (Bhikhari Thakur — "Shakespeare of Bhojpuri", play *Bidesia*), **Magahi**, **Angika**, **Bajjika**. Hindi writers: **Ramdhari Singh "Dinkar"** (*Rashmirathi*, *Kurukshetra*; **Jnanpith** for *Urvashi*), **Phanishwar Nath "Renu"** (*Maila Aanchal* — a famous regional novel set in Purnia), **Nagarjuna** (Hindi and Maithili poet, called "Janakavi").

**GI tags.** A **Geographical Indication** tag protects a product whose quality comes from its place. Bihar examples: Madhubani painting, Sujni embroidery, Sikki craft, Bhagalpuri silk, Shahi Litchi, Jardalu mango, Katarni rice, Magahi paan, Mithila Makhana, Silao khaja (check the latest list).

**Physiographic regions.** North Bihar plain (Ganga's north bank, very flat, flood-prone), South Bihar plain (drier, better drained), the Shiwalik/Someshwar hills in West Champaran, and the southern plateau fringe (Kaimur plateau, Rajgir hills, Gaya hills).

**Floods and droughts.** North Bihar floods because Himalayan rivers bring heavy rain and silt into a flat plain and embankments often breach. South Bihar suffers **drought** because rainfall is less reliable and the rivers are seasonal. A large share of Bihar's area is flood-prone — often cited as about three-quarters of north Bihar (check the latest disaster management figures).

**Minerals after 2000.** Most coal, iron, mica and copper areas went to Jharkhand. Bihar still has **limestone** (Rohtas, Kaimur), **pyrite** (Amjhore, Rohtas), **mica** traces in Nawada and Gaya region, and some other minor minerals.

**Administration.** Bihar has **9 divisions** and **38 districts** (as of 2026 — check the latest). Hierarchy: state → division (commissioner) → district (district magistrate) → subdivision (SDM/SDO) → block (BDO) → panchayat. Patna High Court, the Bihar Legislative Assembly (243) and Council (75), and BPSC are the key institutions.

**Key schemes.** Saat Nischay (1 and 2), JEEViKA, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali, prohibition (2016), **Mukhyamantri Balika Cycle Yojana** and **Poshak Yojana** (school girls), student credit card, Kushal Yuva Program, and others. Reservation changes after the 2023 caste survey were passed by the state but later struck down by the Patna High Court (2024); track the current status (as of 2026 — check the latest).

**Caste-based survey 2023.** Bihar's own caste-based survey; results released on **2 October 2023**. Reported shares: **Extremely Backward Classes about 36%**, **Backward Classes about 27%**, **Scheduled Castes about 19.7%**, **Scheduled Tribes about 1.7%**, unreserved (general) about 15.5% (verify the exact figures from the official report).

**Facts and figures.** Bihar Diwas — **22 March**. State animal — **gaur (Indian bison)**; state bird — **house sparrow**; state tree — **peepal** (check the official list for the state flower). Largest district by area — West Champaran; Census 2011 density 1,106 (highest in India).

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Evaluate the impact of prohibition (2016) in Bihar." Points: aims (women's demand, domestic violence, health, savings); law and enforcement; positive effects reported in surveys (less domestic violence, more spending on food and education — cite sources carefully); problems — illegal liquor and hooch deaths, large number of cases and jail crowding, revenue loss, tourism concerns; amendments to the law; way forward — de-addiction, focus on suppliers, community role; balanced conclusion.
