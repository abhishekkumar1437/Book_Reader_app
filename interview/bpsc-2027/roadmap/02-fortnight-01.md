Your goal this fortnight is to **understand the exam completely and build the daily habit**, then start history with the part of the past where Bihar was the centre of India: Magadha, the Buddha, Mahavira and the Mauryas. By Day 14 you should know the exam pattern by heart, have all notebooks ready, and be able to tell the story of ancient India from Harappa to Ashoka.

> **In simple words:** Week 1 is setup — learn the rules of the game, gather books, start reading the newspaper for 1 hour every day. Week 2 is the first real subject — ancient history, where Bihar (Magadha) gave India its first empire.

The **1-hour newspaper block starts on Day 1 and never stops**. Write short notes under syllabus heads (national, international, Bihar, economy, science, environment, sports and awards). The **45-minute GMA block** also starts on Day 1 with one reasoning chapter and one maths chapter on alternate days.

## What you will learn

**The CCE in one paragraph.** The BPSC Combined Competitive Examination (CCE) selects officers for Bihar posts such as Deputy Collector (SDM-level), DSP and many others. It has three stages: **Prelims** (one GS paper, qualifying), **Mains** (General Hindi qualifying; GS I, GS II and Essay of 300 marks each; an optional paper that is qualifying for general posts) and the **Interview** (120 marks). Final merit = 900 (Mains) + 120 (Interview) = **1,020**.

**Negative marking.** Each wrong Prelims answer costs **one-third of a mark**. So a blind guess among four options gains nothing on average (¼ × 1 − ¾ × ⅓ = 0) and adds risk, but a guess after removing two wrong options gains on average (½ × 1 − ½ × ⅓ = +⅓ mark). Practise the arithmetic now, so you never panic in the hall.

**Option E.** The 72nd CCE OMR sheet has a fifth bubble, **E**, that you must use in a defined way. The official 72nd CCE notice says E means "not attempted" (0 marks), and a fully blank row costs 1/3 mark like a wrong answer. Confirm the rule again in the 73rd notification and OMR instructions; never leave a row blank.

**Harappan civilisation.** A Bronze Age urban culture (roughly 2600–1900 BCE in its mature phase) with planned cities, grid streets, covered drains, baked bricks and standard weights. Key sites: Harappa (Ravi), Mohenjo-daro (Indus, Great Bath), Lothal (dockyard), Kalibangan (ploughed field, fire altars), Dholavira (water reservoirs). The script is still not deciphered.

**Vedic age.** The period of the Vedas (about 1500–600 BCE). The **Rigveda** is the oldest. Early Vedic people were mainly pastoral, with tribal assemblies like the **sabha** and **samiti**; later Vedic society became agricultural, kingdoms grew and the varna system hardened.

**Mahajanapadas and Magadha.** Buddhist texts (the Anguttara Nikaya) list **16 Mahajanapadas** (large states) of the 6th century BCE. **Magadha** (south Bihar) won because of iron from nearby hills, fertile Ganga plains, rivers for trade and defence, and strong capitals — **Rajagriha (Rajgir)** and later **Pataliputra**. Dynasties: Haryanka (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Udayin) → Shishunaga → Nanda.

**Buddhism and Jainism.** The Buddha gained enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya**, gave his first sermon at **Sarnath** and died at **Kushinagar**. Mahavira was born at **Kundagrama near Vaishali** and attained nirvana at **Pawapuri** (Nalanda district). Both rejected the authority of rituals and the Vedas, and spoke in people's languages (Pali, Prakrit).

**The Mauryas.** **Chandragupta Maurya** (with Chanakya/Kautilya, author of the Arthashastra) founded the empire around 321 BCE with Pataliputra as capital. **Megasthenes**, the Greek envoy, wrote **Indica**. **Ashoka** fought the Kalinga war (about 261 BCE), turned to **Dhamma** (a moral code of tolerance and non-violence) and spread it through rock and pillar edicts, mostly in **Brahmi** script.

**Vaishali and the Licchavis.** Vaishali was the capital of the **Vajji confederacy**, ruled by an assembly of clan heads. That is why textbooks call it one of the world's earliest **republics**.

## End-of-fortnight self-check

Answer aloud, without notes. Then check below.

1. How many questions, marks and minutes in the BPSC Prelims GS paper, and what is the negative marking?
2. What is the total mark for final merit and how is it made up?
3. You attempted 100 questions and got 70 right. What is your net score?
4. Where was a Harappan dockyard found?
5. Which is the oldest Veda?
6. What was the first capital of Magadha?
7. Who shifted the capital of Magadha to Pataliputra?
8. Where did the Buddha gain enlightenment, and where did Mahavira attain nirvana?
9. Where was the First Buddhist Council held and under which king?
10. Who wrote Indica, and in whose court was he an envoy?

**Mains question to write in full (250 words):** "Why did Magadha emerge as the most powerful Mahajanapada? Discuss." Use: geography (rivers, iron), fertile land, capitals, ambitious rulers, army with elephants, and end with how this laid the base for the Mauryan empire.

### Answers

1. 150 questions, 150 marks, 2 hours; one-third mark deducted per wrong answer (72nd CCE pattern — confirm in the 73rd notification).
2. 1,020 = GS I (300) + GS II (300) + Essay (300) + Interview (120). General Hindi and the optional paper are qualifying.
3. 70 − 30/3 = 70 − 10 = **60**.
4. **Lothal** (Gujarat).
5. The **Rigveda**.
6. **Rajagriha (Girivraja, modern Rajgir)**.
7. **Udayin** (son of Ajatashatru), who built Pataliputra (Kusumapura) as the new capital.
8. Buddha — **Bodh Gaya**; Mahavira — **Pawapuri**.
9. At **Rajagriha (Rajgir)**, under **Ajatashatru**, soon after the Buddha's death.
10. **Megasthenes**, the Greek envoy of Seleucus Nikator at the court of **Chandragupta Maurya**.

## If you fall behind

- Days 1–7 can be squeezed into 4 days: read the exam-guide chapters 1–3 fully and skim chapters 4–7, then come back to them on Day 14.
- In ancient history, compress the prehistory (stone ages) part to its quick-revision box. Keep the Harappan sites table.
- Do **not** compress Magadha, Buddhism, Jainism, the Mauryas or Ancient Bihar — BPSC asks these again and again because they are Bihar topics.
- Never skip the newspaper hour, even on a catch-up day.
