Your goal this fortnight is to **master the statistics section of GS Paper I** and then **outline the most likely Mains questions across the syllabus**. Statistics is the most "scoring" part of Mains: answers are either right or wrong, so a well-prepared candidate can take almost full marks there. It also sharpens your Prelims maths.

> **In simple words:** Statistics in BPSC Mains is about class-10 level: averages, middle values, spread of data, percentages and reading or drawing graphs. Learn each formula, solve 40+ problems, and you will gain easy marks. Then you plan answers for 50 likely Mains questions and write a full 3-hour paper.

The **1-hour newspaper block** continues daily; revision days 217 and 224 also cover the week's CA notes.

## What you will learn

**Mean (average).** Sum of values ÷ number of values. For grouped data, mean = Σ(f × x) ÷ Σf, where f is frequency and x the class mid-value.

**Median.** The middle value after arranging data in order. With an even count, it is the average of the two middle values. Use it when there are extreme values (incomes, land holdings), because the mean is pulled by extremes.

**Mode.** The value that appears most often. For a rough link between the three: **mode ≈ 3 × median − 2 × mean** (for moderately skewed data).

**Range.** Highest − lowest value. Quick but affected by extremes.

**Standard deviation (SD).** The typical distance of values from the mean. Steps: find the mean; subtract it from each value; square the differences; average them (this is the **variance**); take the square root. A small SD means values are close together.

**Percentages and percentage change.** Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. A rise from 80 to 100 is a **25%** rise, but a fall from 100 to 80 is a **20%** fall — the base changes.

**Ratio and proportion.** Dividing 64 in the ratio 3 : 5 gives 3/8 × 64 = 24 and 5/8 × 64 = 40.

**Graphs.** **Bar chart** — compare categories (district literacy rates). **Pie chart** — parts of a whole; each 1% = **3.6°** of the circle. **Line graph** — trend over time (Bihar's growth rate across years). **Histogram** — grouped frequency with touching bars. Always give a title, labelled axes, a scale and a source note.

**The rubric.** A self-marking checklist: answered the directive word? covered all parts? introduction and conclusion? Bihar example? data with year? diagram? within the word limit? neat? Score each line and track your weakest line week by week.

**Question bank by syllabus line.** For each GS I and GS II syllabus line, list 5–10 likely questions and write 5-minute outlines. These outlines become your fast revision in the Mains sprint.

## End-of-fortnight self-check

Solve aloud or on rough paper in under 10 minutes total.

1. Find the mean of 4, 8, 6, 10, 12.
2. Find the median of 3, 9, 7, 15.
3. Find the mode of 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 3, 5.
4. Find the range of 12, 5, 20, 9.
5. A district's literacy rate rose from 50% to 60%. What is the percentage increase?
6. In a pie chart, what angle shows 25%?
7. Divide 64 in the ratio 3 : 5.
8. The mean of 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 is 5. What is the standard deviation (population)?
9. Which average is best for data with extreme values?
10. Which graph is best to show Bihar's growth rate over ten years?

**Mains question to write in full (timed, 20 minutes):** Using the illustrative table below (invented numbers for practice), (a) find the mean and median literacy rate, (b) draw a bar chart, and (c) comment on the spread in two lines.

| District (illustrative) | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Literacy rate (%) | 72 | 58 | 64 | 50 | 66 |

### Answers

1. (4 + 8 + 6 + 10 + 12) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = **8**.
2. Ordered: 3, 7, 9, 15 → (7 + 9) ÷ 2 = **8**.
3. **3** (appears three times).
4. 20 − 5 = **15**.
5. (60 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = **20%** (a rise of 10 percentage points).
6. 25 × 3.6 = **90°**.
7. **24 and 40**.
8. Squared differences: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16 → sum 32 → variance 32 ÷ 8 = 4 → SD = **2**.
9. The **median**.
10. A **line graph**.

Practice table answer: mean = (72 + 58 + 64 + 50 + 66) ÷ 5 = 310 ÷ 5 = **62%**; ordered 50, 58, 64, 66, 72 → median **64%**; range 22 points — a wide gap between the best and worst districts.
