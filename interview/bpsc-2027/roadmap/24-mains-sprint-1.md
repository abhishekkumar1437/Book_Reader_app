Your goal in this sprint is to **move from MCQ mode to writing mode within three days of Prelims**. Do not wait for the Prelims result — it usually takes weeks, and Mains comes soon after. You start with three **diagnostic papers** to see where you stand, then settle into the weekly rhythm you will follow until Day 384: **one full GS I test, one full GS II test and one essay paper every week**.

> **In simple words:** Write the Prelims, rest one day, then start Mains. Take a full GS I, GS II and essay test in the first week to find your weak points. From week 2, study a GS topic, take full tests, check them honestly and rewrite weak answers.

The **1-hour current-affairs block** continues every day: now you are building **one year of current affairs** into Mains-ready themes. The **45-minute block** becomes General Hindi, essay outlines and optional-paper practice.

## What you will learn

**The Mains papers.** **General Hindi** (100, qualifying, at least 30%), **GS Paper I** (300), **GS Paper II** (300), **Essay** (300), and an **optional paper** (100, objective, qualifying for general posts). Each descriptive paper is 3 hours. Only GS I, GS II and Essay count in merit (900 marks), along with the interview (120).

**GS I syllabus lines.** (1) Modern history of India and Indian culture, with Bihar's role in the freedom struggle; (2) current events of national and international importance; (3) statistical analysis, graphs and diagrams.

**GS II syllabus lines.** (1) Indian polity with Bihar focus; (2) Indian economy and geography of India, with Bihar's economy and geography; (3) role and impact of science and technology in India's and Bihar's development.

**Diagnostic test.** A full paper taken before topic revision, only to measure your starting level. Low marks are normal; what matters is the list of weaknesses it gives you.

**Bihar Economic Survey.** Published each year by Bihar's Finance Department before the state budget (around February–March). It has chapters on the macro economy, state finances, agriculture, industry, infrastructure, banking, human development and more. Read the overview first and copy the key numbers with the survey year onto your data card.

**Modern history for Mains.** Mains does not ask "when" but "why" and "how significant". For each event (1857, Moderates, Swadeshi, Champaran) prepare: background → causes → course with Bihar's role → impact → significance.

**Optional paper.** For general posts the optional is a **100-mark objective, qualifying** paper (some special posts have a subjective optional that counts — check your notification). One 45-minute MCQ session a week keeps it safe.

**Mains question to write in full (timed, 300 words):** "'Champaran marked the beginning of mass politics in India.' Discuss." Points: earlier politics limited to the educated; Champaran brought peasants in; Gandhi's method of inquiry and non-violent defiance; local leaders who later led Bihar (Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha); success through the 1918 Act; template for Kheda, Ahmedabad and Non-Cooperation; limits (local, economic issue); conclusion.

### Answers

1. **GS I (300), GS II (300) and Essay (300) = 900**; with the interview (120) the merit total is 1,020.
2. **30%**.
3. Modern history and culture (with Bihar's role); current events; statistical analysis, graphs and diagrams.
4. Indian polity (Bihar focus); Indian economy and geography (with Bihar); role and impact of science and technology in India's and Bihar's development.
5. Bihar's **Finance Department**, every year before the **state budget** (around February–March).
6. To find your **starting level and weaknesses**, not to score high.
7. **Background → causes → course (with Bihar's role) → impact → significance**.
8. Peasants had to grow indigo on **3/20 of their land** for European planters.
9. Notified on **22 March 1912** (working from 1 April 1912); leaders included **Sachchidananda Sinha** and **Mahesh Narayan**.
10. **Kunwar Singh** of Jagdishpur (with the Danapur sepoys); there were also risings at Patna under Pir Ali.

## If you fall behind

- If Prelims tiredness lasts, take Day 303 lightly, but do not delay the GS I diagnostic beyond Day 305.
- If you can do only one test in a week, alternate GS I and GS II, and keep the essay every week.
- Never skip evaluation: an unchecked test teaches little.
