This is the **most important fortnight of history for BPSC**. The national movement and Bihar's role in it is a separate Prelims syllabus head and the first section of Mains GS Paper I. Your goal is to know the movement phase by phase, with a Bihar example for every phase. From **Day 29 the 30-minute answer-writing block starts**: one BPSC-style answer each day.

> **In simple words:** From 1857 to 1947 Indians fought British rule in many ways — armed revolt, petitions, boycotts, non-violent mass movements and revolutionary action. Bihar was at the centre: Kunwar Singh in 1857, Gandhi's first satyagraha at Champaran in 1917, and huge sacrifices in 1942.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** every day; revise the week's notes on Days 35 and 42.

## What you will learn

**The Revolt of 1857.** It began at **Meerut on 10 May 1857** and spread to Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi and Bihar. Causes: annexations (Doctrine of Lapse, Awadh 1856), heavy land revenue, attack on religious customs, and the **greased cartridge** issue for sepoys. Results: the Company's rule ended and the Crown took over under the **Government of India Act 1858** and Queen Victoria's Proclamation.

**Kunwar Singh.** Zamindar of **Jagdishpur** (Bhojpur district), about 80 years old in 1857. He led the Danapur sepoys, took **Arrah**, fought across UP and central India, and returned to Jagdishpur, where he died in **April 1858** after being wounded. Bihar remembers him as **"Veer Kunwar Singh"**.

**Wahabi movement.** An Islamic reform movement with **Patna** as its main Indian centre; it later took an anti-British shape, and the British crushed it with trials in the 1860s–70s.

**Tribal and peasant movements.** **Santhal Hul (1855–56)** under **Sidhu and Kanhu** against zamindars and moneylenders in the Rajmahal hills; **Munda Ulgulan (1899–1900)** under **Birsa Munda**; **Tana Bhagat movement (1914)** among the Oraons; **Indigo revolt (1859–60)** in Bengal.

**Moderates and Extremists.** The **Indian National Congress** was founded in **Bombay in December 1885** (A.O. Hume; first president **W.C. Bonnerjee**; 72 delegates). **Moderates** (Dadabhai Naoroji, Gokhale) used petitions and speeches. **Extremists** (Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal) wanted Swaraj and used boycott and **Swadeshi**. The Congress split at **Surat (1907)**.

**Bihar becomes a province.** Leaders such as **Sachchidananda Sinha** and **Mahesh Narayan** campaigned for a separate Bihar. The Delhi Durbar of **1911** announced it. The new province of **Bihar and Orissa** was notified on **22 March 1912** — celebrated every year as **Bihar Diwas** — and began working from 1 April 1912.

**Champaran Satyagraha (1917).** Under the **tinkathia** system, peasants in Champaran had to grow indigo on **3/20 of their land**. **Raj Kumar Shukla** persuaded Gandhi to come. Gandhi, with **Rajendra Prasad**, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, J.B. Kripalani and others, recorded peasants' statements. The government formed an inquiry committee with Gandhi as a member, and the **Champaran Agrarian Act (1918)** ended tinkathia. It was Gandhi's **first satyagraha in India**.

**Non-Cooperation to Civil Disobedience.** **Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)** → **Khilafat and Non-Cooperation (1920–22)**, withdrawn after **Chauri Chaura (February 1922)** → Swaraj Party (1923) → **Simon Commission (1928)** → Lahore Congress and Purna Swaraj (1929) → **Dandi March and Civil Disobedience (1930)** → Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931) → **Poona Pact (1932)**. In Bihar, **Bihar Vidyapith** and **Sadaqat Ashram** (Mazharul Haque) were set up in Patna during Non-Cooperation.

**Quit India (1942).** On **8 August 1942** the Congress passed the Quit India resolution in Bombay; Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"**. On **11 August 1942**, seven students were shot while trying to hoist the national flag on the **Patna Secretariat**; the **Shaheed Smarak (Saat Murti)** stands there. **Jayaprakash Narayan** escaped from **Hazaribagh jail** in November 1942 and organised underground resistance.

**Revolutionaries and socialists.** **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki** threw a bomb at **Muzaffarpur (1908)** meant for the judge Kingsford. The **Congress Socialist Party (1934)** had JP as a key leader. **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** founded the **Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha (1929)** and was the first president of the **All India Kisan Sabha (1936)**.

**Bihar after 1947.** Orissa had already separated on **1 April 1936**; **Jharkhand** was carved out on **15 November 2000**. The **JP Movement (1974)** began with students in Bihar; on **5 June 1974** at Gandhi Maidan, Patna, JP called for **"Sampoorna Kranti" (Total Revolution)**.

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Discuss the significance of the Champaran Satyagraha (1917) for the Indian national movement." Points: background of indigo and tinkathia; Raj Kumar Shukla; Gandhi's method (inquiry, non-violence, defiance of the order to leave); local leaders who emerged (Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha); the 1918 Act; impact — first test of satyagraha in India, peasants brought into national politics, model for later movements.

### Answers

1. At **Meerut**, on **10 May 1857**.
2. **Jagdishpur** (Bhojpur district, Bihar); he died in **April 1858**.
3. **Sidhu and Kanhu** (Murmu brothers).
4. **W.C. Bonnerjee**; at **Bombay** (December 1885).
5. **22 March 1912** (announced at the Delhi Durbar, December 1911); the day is celebrated as **Bihar Diwas**. The province began functioning from 1 April 1912.
6. A system under which peasants had to grow **indigo on 3 kathas out of every 20 (3/20)** of their land for European planters.
7. **Raj Kumar Shukla**, a peasant from Champaran.
8. Seven students were **shot dead by police** while trying to hoist the national flag on the Secretariat building; the Saat Murti memorial honours them.
9. **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati**, in **1929**.
10. On **5 June 1974** at **Gandhi Maidan, Patna**.
