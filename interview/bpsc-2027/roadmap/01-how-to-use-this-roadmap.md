This roadmap turns the BPSC 73rd Combined Competitive Examination (CCE) into a list of small daily jobs. You study **5½ hours a day** for **300 days up to Prelims**, then do a **90-day Mains sprint**. Every day in the next 29 chapters names the subject, the exact chapters to read in this collection, a reasoning or maths task, an answer-writing question, and a "done when" target, so you always know when a day is finished.

> **In simple words:** Read one subject at a time in the 3-hour block, read the newspaper every day, practise reasoning and maths every day, start writing Mains answers from Day 29, and revise every 7th day. Do this for 300 days and you reach Prelims with every subject read twice and revised many times. The 90 days after Prelims are for full Mains tests.

The exam pattern used here is the **72nd CCE** pattern (notification of May 2026). The 73rd notification is not out yet (as of October 2026). When it comes, check every detail on **bpsc.bihar.gov.in** and adjust the plan as shown below in "When the 73rd notification arrives".

### Daily routine (5½ hours)

| Block | Time | What you do | Days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main subject | **3 h** | The subject of the phase: read the chapter, make notes, answer its recall questions | Every day |
| Newspaper + current affairs | **1 h** | Read one national daily (Bihar edition if possible); write short notes under syllabus heads | Every day, Day 1 to Day 390 |
| General Mental Ability (GMA) | **45 min** | Reasoning and maths. Days 1–120: one reasoning chapter and one maths chapter on alternate days. Days 121–245: mixed timed sets. From Day 246: inside mocks and speed drills | Days 1–300 (Hindi, essay and optional practice after Prelims) |
| Answer writing | **30 min** | 1–2 Mains-style answers on what you studied (Days 1–28: short written recall instead) | From Day 29 |
| Revise yesterday | **15 min** | Read yesterday's notes and say the key points aloud without looking | Every day |

Suggested clock for a full-time learner: 6:00–7:00 newspaper; 7:30–10:30 main subject (with a 10-minute break each hour); 15 min revision of yesterday after breakfast; 4:00–4:45 GMA; 8:00–8:30 answer writing. Working learners can split the main block into 1½ h before work and 1½ h after.

### Weekly routine

- **Days 1–6 of each week:** new material in the main block, as the day-by-day table says.
- **Every 7th day (Day 7, 14, 21 …) is a revision day.** Revise the whole week's notes, redo every recall question you got wrong, write two longer answers (250–300 words), redo the week's wrong GMA questions, and revise the week's current-affairs notes. Do not learn new chapters on this day.
- **Monthly current-affairs compilation.** In this plan a "month" is 30 plan-days. On the last revision day of each month (Days 28, 56, 84, 119, 147, 175, 210, 238, 266, 294, 329, 357 and 385) turn the month's newspaper notes into one clean compilation using [Monthly Compilation Template and Revision](/interview/bpsc-2027/current-affairs/monthly-compilation-template-and-revision).

> **Remember:** A revision day is not a holiday. If you skip it, the week's reading leaks out of memory. If you must take a day off, take it after the revision day, not instead of it.

## How to study for a state PSC

**1. NCERT first, then the book chapter.** For history, geography, polity, economy and science, read the matching NCERT (Class 6–12) pages quickly before the chapter in this collection. NCERTs give simple language and the base facts; the chapters here add state-PSC depth and the Bihar angle. The full NCERT list is in [Booklist and Sources](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/booklist-and-sources).

**2. Make short notes, not copies.** Use one notebook (or one digital file) per subject. Write only what you might forget: names, dates, numbers, article numbers, lists. Keep a separate column or page called **"Bihar link"** in every subject, because BPSC asks Bihar-specific questions in every section.

**3. Revise on a cycle.** Use the **1-7-30 rule**: look at new notes again after 1 day (the daily 15-minute block), after 7 days (the revision day) and after about 30 days (the monthly round). Phase 4 then gives two full revision rounds. See [Revision and Mock Test Strategy](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/revision-and-mock-test-strategy).

**4. Practise active recall.** After reading, close the book and answer the chapter's recall questions aloud or on paper. Active recall means pulling facts out of memory instead of re-reading them. It feels harder, and that is why it works.

**5. Keep a mistake log.** Every wrong recall answer, every wrong mock answer and every forgotten fact goes into one notebook with the correct fact and the reason you got it wrong. In the last 15 days you will revise mainly from this log.

**6. Write answers from Day 29.** Prelims is qualifying only; your rank comes from Mains (900 marks) and the interview (120 marks). Writing one or two answers a day from Day 29 means you reach Phase 3 with about 250 answers already written.

**7. Read past papers.** Download the official question papers from bpsc.bihar.gov.in. Read them on Day 4 to see the style, and solve them subject-wise in Phase 4.

## How Prelims and Mains fit together

You do not prepare twice. Almost every Prelims subject feeds a Mains paper, so you read once with both exams in mind: facts for Prelims, explanations and the Bihar angle for Mains.

| Subject you read in Phases 1–2 | Prelims head | Mains paper it feeds |
|---|---|---|
| Modern history, national movement, culture | History of Bihar and India; Indian National Movement and Bihar's role | **GS I** section 1 |
| Ancient and medieval history | History of Bihar and India | Essay content and culture questions |
| Polity | Indian Polity (including Bihar) | **GS II** section 1 |
| Economy and geography | Geography; Indian Economy (including Bihar) | **GS II** section 2 |
| Science and technology | General Science | **GS II** section 3 |
| Current affairs | Current events | **GS I** section 2; essay |
| Maths and reasoning | General Mental Ability | **GS I** section 3 (statistics) |
| Bihar Special | All heads | Every paper |

> **Mains angle:** Prelims needs the fact; Mains needs the fact plus the "why", the impact and a Bihar example. When you make notes, add one line on why the fact matters. That line becomes your Mains content.

## How to read a fortnight chapter

Each of the next 29 chapters covers 14 days (the last Prelims fortnight and last sprint are shorter). The day-by-day table has six columns:

- **Day** — the plan-day number.
- **Main subject (3 h)** — the topic for the main block.
- **Read (links)** — the chapters in this collection to read. "(part 1)" and "(part 2)" mean read the first or second half of a long chapter.
- **GMA (45 min)** — the reasoning or maths chapter or practice set. After Prelims this column becomes Hindi, essay or optional-paper practice.
- **Answer writing / practice** — the recall task (Days 1–28) or the Mains question to write (from Day 29). Questions marked **Q:** are written in BPSC style.
- **Done when…** — a small test of whether the day is really finished.

After the table: **What you will learn** (plain-English meanings of the fortnight's main topics), a **Checklist**, an **End-of-fortnight self-check** with answers, and **If you fall behind**.

## When the 73rd notification arrives

The single rule: **Day 300 is the day before Prelims.** When BPSC announces the Prelims date, count back from it.

1. Write the Prelims date. Day 301 = Prelims day. Day 300 = the day before.
2. Find the plan-day you are on today. Count the real days left until Prelims. Compare with the plan-days left until Day 301.
3. **If you have more days than the plan** (Prelims is later): add the extra days to Phase 4. Use them for a third revision round, more past papers, and extra work on your two weakest subjects. Do not start new books.
4. **If you have fewer days than the plan** (Prelims is earlier): cut in this order, and stop cutting as soon as the days match:
   - Move General Hindi (Days 236–245) to the Mains sprint. It is qualifying and is not in Prelims.
   - Move the Essay block (Days 225–235) to the Mains sprint.
   - Shorten the GS answer-writing block (Days 197–224) to statistics only (Days 211–217), which also helps the Prelims GMA section.
   - Shorten Phase 4 Round 2 from 13 days to 7 days by revising two subjects a day.
   - Never cut the last 15 days.
5. **Mains date.** When the Mains date is announced, set Day 390 as the day before the first Mains paper. If the gap after Prelims is shorter than 90 days, drop weeks from the middle of the sprint (each sprint week = one GS I test, one GS II test, one essay paper). If it is longer, repeat sprint weeks 10–11.
6. **Re-check the rules.** Read the new notification for: exam pattern, the meaning of option E and the rule for blank rows, negative marking, age limits, and the cut-off date for age. Update your notes if anything changed.

> **Example:** You started on 1 November 2026, so your Day 300 is 27 August 2027. BPSC fixes Prelims on, say, 26 September 2027 (an invented date). Your new Day 300 is 25 September — 29 extra days. You add them to Phase 4: a third revision round and two more mocks a week.

## If you fall behind

Everyone falls behind sometimes: illness, family events, a hard chapter. Use these rules.

- **The 3-day rule.** If you are up to 3 days behind, use the next revision day to catch up and revise only lightly. If you are more than 3 days behind, compress (below) — do not just keep sliding.
- **Never skip the newspaper.** One hour a day is the minimum. Missed current affairs cannot be "read later" without a big cost.
- **Compress, do not cut, the core.** Read the "Quick revision" section and recall questions of a chapter instead of the whole chapter, and come back to the full chapter in Phase 4.
- **What is safe to compress first:** world geography details, ancient and medieval history beyond the Bihar parts, deep science derivations, and the second half of long question banks.
- **What you must not drop:** Bihar Special, modern history and the national movement, polity, the newspaper, mocks in Phase 4, and answer writing (even one answer a day).
- **Protect Phase 4.** It is the most important stretch for clearing Prelims. Borrow days from Phase 3 if you must (see the cutting order above), never from Phase 4.

Each fortnight chapter has its own "If you fall behind" section with exact topics to compress.

## Before Day 1 checklist

- [ ] Download the latest BPSC CCE notification and the official syllabus from bpsc.bihar.gov.in (72nd CCE now; the 73rd when released).
- [ ] Download the last few years' Prelims and Mains question papers from the same site.
- [ ] Collect NCERTs (Class 6–12): history, geography, political science, economics, science (Class 6–10) and the Class 11–12 physics, chemistry and biology basics you are comfortable with. Free PDFs are on ncert.nic.in.
- [ ] Subscribe to one national daily newspaper (Bihar edition if available) and keep one Hindi daily for Bihar news if you can.
- [ ] Bookmark state.bihar.gov.in, finance.bihar.gov.in (Bihar Economic Survey and Budget), pib.gov.in and the BPSC website.
- [ ] Buy or make: one notebook per subject (history, polity, geography, economy, science, Bihar), one current-affairs notebook, one mistake log, one answer-writing register, one GMA notebook.
- [ ] Print or copy the phase table above and stick it on your wall.
- [ ] Fix your daily clock (which hour for which block) and tell your family your study hours.
- [ ] Set up a simple tracker: one line per day with a tick for each block.
- [ ] Choose a weekly test source for Phase 4 and the Mains sprint (any test series or self-made papers from past questions).
