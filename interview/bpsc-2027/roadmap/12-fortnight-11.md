Your goal this fortnight is to **cover the biology that BPSC asks most — vitamins, diseases, body systems — and the technology topics that link to current affairs**. Biology and health questions are a steady block in every Prelims paper; technology feeds Mains GS II section 3, which asks about the **role and impact of science and technology in India's and Bihar's development**.

> **In simple words:** You learn what acids and metals are and how chemistry shows up in daily life, how cells and genes work, how the human body works, what we need to eat and which germs cause which diseases. Then you learn about ISRO, computers and AI, biotechnology and clean energy.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** daily. Day 147 is the **month-5 revision day** with the monthly compilation.

## What you will learn

**pH.** A scale from 0 to 14 that shows how acidic or basic a solution is. **Pure water = 7** (neutral); below 7 is acidic (lemon juice, vinegar — acetic acid, stomach — hydrochloric acid); above 7 is basic (soap, baking soda, lime water). Acidic soil is treated with **lime**; alkaline (usar) soil with **gypsum**.

**Groundwater contamination in Bihar.** **Arsenic** is found in groundwater in many districts along the Ganga; **fluoride** in parts of south Bihar; **iron** in parts of north-east Bihar. Long use causes skin lesions and cancer (arsenic) and bone and teeth problems (fluoride). Remedies: safe surface-water supply, deeper wells, treatment plants.

**Alloys.** Brass = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin; steel = iron + carbon; stainless steel = iron + chromium + nickel (+ carbon).

**Cell and genetics.** The **cell** is the basic unit of life. **DNA** carries genetic information; its double-helix structure was described by **Watson and Crick (1953)**. **Mitosis** makes two identical cells (growth); **meiosis** makes gametes with half the chromosomes. Humans have **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**. **Mendel** gave the laws of inheritance using pea plants.

**Genetic engineering.** Changing an organism's genes directly. Example: **Bt cotton** carries a gene from a soil bacterium (*Bacillus thuringiensis*) that kills bollworms. Newer tools like **CRISPR** edit genes precisely.

**Blood groups.** ABO system: **O negative** is the universal donor (for red cells); **AB positive** is the universal recipient. The heart has **four chambers**; arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins carry it back.

**Hormones.** Chemical messengers. **Insulin** (pancreas) lowers blood sugar — lack causes diabetes; **thyroxine** (thyroid) needs iodine — lack causes goitre; **adrenaline** (adrenal glands) prepares the body for "fight or flight"; **growth hormone** (pituitary, the "master gland").

**Vitamins and deficiency diseases.** A — night blindness; B1 — beriberi; B3 — pellagra; B12 — pernicious anaemia; **C — scurvy**; **D — rickets**; K — poor blood clotting. Minerals: **iron — anaemia**, **iodine — goitre**, calcium — weak bones.

**Diseases and pathogens.** Virus: COVID-19, measles, polio, rabies, dengue, AIDS, Japanese encephalitis. Bacteria: tuberculosis, typhoid, cholera, leprosy, tetanus. Protozoa: **malaria** (Plasmodium, spread by female Anopheles mosquito), **kala-azar** (Leishmania, spread by sandfly — Bihar has been a major focus area). **AES in Muzaffarpur**: Acute Encephalitis Syndrome cases among children, linked by studies to low blood sugar in undernourished children who ate litchi on an empty stomach; key measures — feeding children before sleep, quick glucose treatment, awareness.

**Ecology.** A **food chain** moves energy from producers (plants) to consumers; only about **10%** of energy passes to the next level (Lindeman's 10% law). **Biodiversity** is the variety of life; India is one of the 17 megadiverse countries.

**ISRO highlights.** **Chandrayaan-3** landed near the Moon's south pole on **23 August 2023** (now National Space Day); **Aditya-L1** (solar mission, launched September 2023) studies the Sun from the L1 point; **Gaganyaan** is the human spaceflight programme (as of 2026 — check the latest status).

**ICT and AI.** **Artificial intelligence** — computers doing tasks that need human-like thinking (recognising images, language). Uses in governance: grievance analysis, crop disease detection, traffic control. **Cyber security** protects data and systems from attacks.

**Energy and environment technology.** Solar, wind, biomass, small hydro, green hydrogen; waste-to-energy; e-waste recycling; pollution control (catalytic converters, electrostatic precipitators).

## End-of-fortnight self-check

1. What is the pH of pure water?
2. Which harmful element is found in the groundwater of many Ganga-belt districts of Bihar?
3. Brass is an alloy of which metals?
4. Who described the double-helix structure of DNA, and when?
5. Which blood group is the universal donor?
6. Deficiency of which vitamin causes scurvy, and of which causes rickets?
7. Which hormone controls blood sugar, and which gland makes it?
8. Which insect spreads kala-azar?
9. When did Chandrayaan-3 land on the Moon?
10. What does the 10% law in ecology say?

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Discuss the problem of malnutrition among children in Bihar and the steps taken to address it." Points: meaning (stunting, wasting, underweight, anaemia — cite the latest NFHS figures with year); causes — poverty, low maternal education, early marriage, poor sanitation, diet gaps; steps — ICDS/Anganwadi, PM POSHAN mid-day meals, Poshan Abhiyaan, JEEViKA nutrition work, food fortification, Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali and sanitation; way forward; conclusion.

### Answers

1. **7**.
2. **Arsenic**.
3. **Copper and zinc**.
4. **James Watson and Francis Crick**, **1953**.
5. **O negative**.
6. Scurvy — **vitamin C**; rickets — **vitamin D**.
7. **Insulin**, made by the **pancreas** (islets of Langerhans).
8. The **sandfly**.
9. **23 August 2023**.
10. Only about **10% of the energy** at one level of a food chain passes to the next level.
