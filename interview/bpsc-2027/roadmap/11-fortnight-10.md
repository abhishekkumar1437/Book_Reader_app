Your goal this fortnight is to **close Phase 1 cleanly and open Phase 2 with physics**. Days 127–133 finish economy and revise all four static subjects; Days 134–140 start General Science, which gives a large share of Prelims questions. Science questions in BPSC are usually about **everyday applications** — why the sky is blue, what an electric fuse does — not long derivations.

> **In simple words:** First you learn how India changed its economy in 1991 and what schemes Bihar runs. You then revise everything from Phase 1. Then physics begins: motion and force, heat, light and sound, electricity and magnets, and atoms and reactions — always with daily-life examples.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** daily. Science news (ISRO, health, technology) now goes into a separate page in your CA notebook.

## What you will learn

**The 1991 reforms.** In 1991 India faced a **balance-of-payments crisis** (very low foreign exchange reserves). The Narasimha Rao government, with **Manmohan Singh** as Finance Minister, started **LPG — liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation**: removing most industrial licences, opening to foreign investment, lowering import duties, devaluing the rupee, and reforming banks and taxes.

**UPI and the digital economy.** **UPI** (Unified Payments Interface), launched in **2016** by the **NPCI**, lets people send money instantly by mobile. With Aadhaar and Jan Dhan accounts it forms the "JAM trinity" used for **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)**.

**Bihar's schemes.** **Saat Nischay** (Seven Resolves, from 2015; part 2 from 2020) — e.g. tap water to every house (Har Ghar Nal Ka Jal), toilets, roads and lanes, electricity, youth support (student credit card), women's reservation in jobs. **JEEViKA** — the Bihar Rural Livelihoods Project (started 2006) that organises rural women into **self-help groups**. **Prohibition** — liquor banned since **April 2016**. **Jal-Jeevan-Hariyali** (2019) — water conservation and tree planting. Mark all scheme details "as of 2026 — check the latest".

**Newton's laws.** (1) A body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a force acts (**inertia** — passengers fall forward when a bus stops suddenly). (2) Force = mass × acceleration (**F = ma**). (3) Every action has an equal and opposite reaction (rocket launch, recoil of a gun).

**Work, energy, power.** **Work** = force × distance (unit **joule**). **Power** = work ÷ time (unit **watt**). Energy changes form but is not destroyed (law of conservation of energy). Pressure = force ÷ area (unit **pascal**) — that is why knives are sharp and camels have wide feet.

**Heat transfer.** **Conduction** (through solids — a spoon in hot tea), **convection** (through liquids and gases — sea breeze), **radiation** (no medium needed — sunlight). Dark surfaces absorb more radiation. Temperature conversion: °F = (9/5 × °C) + 32; body temperature 37 °C = 98.6 °F.

**Light.** Light travels at about **3 × 10⁸ m/s**. **Refraction** bends light (a pencil looks bent in water). **Total internal reflection** keeps light inside an **optical fibre** — the base of internet cables. **Myopia** (short-sightedness) is corrected by a **concave** lens; **hypermetropia** by a **convex** lens. The sky is blue because air scatters blue light more (**Rayleigh scattering**).

**Sound.** Needs a medium; travels fastest in **solids**, then liquids, then gases (about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C). Humans hear about **20 Hz to 20,000 Hz**; above that is **ultrasound** (used in medical scans), below is **infrasound**.

**Electricity.** **Ohm's law**: V = I × R. Power **P = V × I**. Electricity is sent over long distances at **high voltage** so that current and heat loss are small. A **fuse** melts and breaks the circuit when current is too high.

**Magnetism and nuclear physics.** A changing magnetic field produces current (**electromagnetic induction**, Faraday) — used in generators. **Fission** splits heavy nuclei (uranium) — used in nuclear power plants; **fusion** joins light nuclei — powers the Sun.

**Chemical reactions.** A **physical change** does not make a new substance (melting ice); a **chemical change** does (rusting, burning). Reaction types: combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, **oxidation and reduction**.

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Assess the impact of the 1991 economic reforms on the Indian economy. Did Bihar benefit equally?" Points: background crisis; key reforms; gains — higher growth, services boom, foreign investment, reserves, consumer choice; concerns — jobless growth, regional inequality, farm distress; Bihar — limited private investment, slow industrialisation, gains mostly after 2005 through public spending, roads and services; conclusion.

### Answers

1. **Dr Manmohan Singh** (Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao).
2. **NPCI** (National Payments Corporation of India), **2016**.
3. **April 2016** (Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016).
4. The **Bihar Rural Livelihoods Project** (started 2006, World Bank–supported), which organises rural women into **self-help groups** for savings, credit and livelihoods.
5. The **watt (W)**.
6. **Newton's third law** (action and reaction).
7. A **concave (diverging) lens**.
8. Air molecules **scatter blue light more** than other colours (Rayleigh scattering).
9. In **solids**.
10. At high voltage the **current is low**, so the **heat loss (I²R)** in wires is much smaller.
