Your goal this fortnight is to understand **how the government actually works** — at the Union level, in Bihar, and in villages and towns. This is the heart of Mains GS Paper II section 1, and BPSC especially likes questions on the **Bihar legislature, the Governor, the Patna High Court and Panchayati Raj in Bihar**.

> **In simple words:** India has a parliamentary system: the people elect the Lok Sabha, the majority forms the government led by the Prime Minister, and courts check that everyone follows the Constitution. States like Bihar copy this pattern with a Governor, a Chief Minister and a legislature. Villages and towns have their own elected bodies.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** daily — note every Bihar government decision in your "Bihar polity" page.

## What you will learn

**Council of Ministers.** Headed by the **Prime Minister**, appointed by the President. It is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (Article 75). The **91st Amendment (2003)** limits the total number of ministers to **15% of the Lok Sabha's strength** (and 15% of the Assembly in states, with a minimum of 12).

**Attorney General.** The government's chief legal adviser (**Article 76**); can speak in Parliament but cannot vote. The state equivalent is the **Advocate General** (Article 165).

**Parliament.** **Lok Sabha** — 543 elected seats now (the Constitution allows a maximum of 550 elected members); term 5 years; presided over by the **Speaker**. **Rajya Sabha** — permanent house; maximum **250** members including **12 nominated** by the President; one-third retire every two years; presided over by the **Vice-President** as Chairman.

**Money Bill (Article 110).** A bill only on taxes, borrowing or the Consolidated Fund. It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha; the **Speaker certifies** it; the Rajya Sabha can only delay it for **14 days**. **Joint sitting (Article 108)** settles a deadlock over ordinary bills; it is presided over by the Speaker. There is no joint sitting for Money Bills or Constitution amendment bills.

**Judicial review and basic structure.** Courts can strike down laws that violate the Constitution. In **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but **cannot destroy its basic structure**.

**Patna High Court.** Established in **1916**. A High Court can issue writs under **Article 226** — for Fundamental Rights and also "for any other purpose", so its writ power is wider than the Supreme Court's under Article 32.

**PIL (Public Interest Litigation).** A case filed in public interest by any person, even if not personally hurt. It grew in the late 1970s and 1980s (Justices P.N. Bhagwati and V.R. Krishna Iyer).

**The Governor.** Appointed by the President (**Article 155**); holds office "during the pleasure of the President". Has some discretion — for example in reporting a breakdown of constitutional machinery (Article 356) and in reserving bills for the President.

**Bihar legislature.** Bihar is **bicameral** (two houses): **Vidhan Sabha** with **243** elected seats and **Vidhan Parishad** (Legislative Council) with **75** members. Councils exist in only six states (Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh — as of 2026, check the latest).

**Emergencies.** **National emergency (Article 352)** — war, external aggression or armed rebellion ("internal disturbance" was replaced by "armed rebellion" through the **44th Amendment, 1978**). **President's Rule (Article 356)** — failure of constitutional machinery in a state. **Financial emergency (Article 360)** — never imposed so far. **S.R. Bommai (1994)** made President's Rule subject to judicial review and said the floor of the House is the test of majority.

**Panchayati Raj.** The **73rd Amendment (1992)** added **Part IX** and the **Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)**: a three-tier system, elections every five years, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission, at least one-third seats for women. The **74th Amendment** did the same for towns (**Part IXA, Twelfth Schedule, 18 subjects**). **Bihar Panchayati Raj Act 2006** gave **50% reservation for women** in panchayats — Bihar was the first state to do so.

**Constitutional bodies.** **Election Commission** (Article 324), **UPSC and State PSCs** like BPSC (Articles 315–323), **CAG** (Article 148), **Finance Commission** (Article 280). They are "constitutional" because the Constitution itself creates them.
