Your goal in this sprint is to **reach Mains at your peak: fast, neat and complete in every paper**. The last tests are taken at the **real exam time** and under exam rules. After each final test you spend a day correcting mistakes, not learning new topics.

> **In simple words:** Revise from your one-page sheets and outlines, fix your five weakest topics, take the final GS I, GS II, Hindi and essay papers exactly like the real exam, and correct what went wrong.

The **1-hour current-affairs block** continues as revision of your master file, plus a quick daily look at the news for any very big new event.

## What you will learn

**Final-test routine.** Use the same pen, the same watch, the same seating time and the same breakfast you will use on exam days. Plan your time per question from the number of questions and marks printed on your paper (check the latest pattern), and keep 10 minutes at the end to finish diagrams and underlining.

**Correction day.** For each final test, list: questions you could not finish (time problem), questions where content was thin (knowledge problem), and questions with poor structure (method problem). Fix each with one action: a time rule, a one-page addition, or a rewritten answer.

**Presentation.** Short paragraphs, numbered points, underlined keywords (sparingly), neat diagrams with a pencil, margins left as instructed. Write the question number clearly.

**General Hindi safety.** Final checks: letter format, précis length (about one-third), essay structure in Hindi, common grammar errors. The aim is a **safe pass with margin**, not a top score.

**Optional paper.** A final 50-MCQ revision of your optional subject; revise only the wrong ones afterwards.

1. What three kinds of problems do you list on a correction day?
2. How long should you keep at the end of a paper for finishing?

4. How long should a précis be?
5. What should you avoid doing in the last week before Mains?
6. Name the GS II syllabus sections.
7. What is the merit total, and what is it made of?
8. Which Bihar Act gives time-bound public services?
9. Which amendment created Part IX of the Constitution?
10. What is the meaning of "critically examine"?

**Mains question to write in full (timed, 300 words):** "Examine the fiscal position of Bihar." Points: revenue sources — share in central taxes (largest part), grants, own tax revenue (GST, stamp duty, excise loss after prohibition); expenditure — education, health, rural development; deficits within FRBM limits (quote the latest Budget figures with year); debt; issues — low own revenue, dependence on the Centre, committed expenditure; measures — tax base, efficiency, outcome budgeting; conclusion.

### Answers

1. **Time problems, knowledge problems and method problems**.
2. About **10 minutes**.
3. A **safe pass with a margin** above 30%.
4. About **one-third** of the original passage.
5. Starting **new sources** or new topics; cutting sleep.
6. **Indian polity (Bihar focus); Indian economy and geography (with Bihar); role and impact of science and technology** in India's and Bihar's development.
7. **1,020** = GS I 300 + GS II 300 + Essay 300 + Interview 120.
8. The **Bihar Right to Public Services Act, 2011**.
9. The **73rd Amendment (1992)**.
10. Look at **strengths and weaknesses** and give a **balanced judgement** with evidence.

## If you fall behind

- Never drop the final tests at exam time; if needed drop a correction day and do corrections on the revision day.
- If tired, shorten study days to 4 hours but keep the tests.
- No new topics after Day 378.
