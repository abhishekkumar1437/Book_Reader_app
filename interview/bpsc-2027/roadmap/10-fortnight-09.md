Your goal this fortnight is to **understand how the government earns and spends money, how farming and food security work, and where Bihar's economy stands**. Mains GS II section 2 asks directly about Bihar's economy, so start your **Bihar data card** now: growth rate, per capita income, sector shares, each with its source year.

> **In simple words:** The budget shows what the government earns (taxes) and spends; the gap is the deficit. GST is one tax on goods and services shared by the Centre and states. Farmers get support through MSP and food reaches the poor through ration shops. Bihar grows fast but starts from a low base, so its income per person is still among the lowest.

## What you will learn

**Deficits.** **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts. **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts); it shows how much the government must **borrow**. **Primary deficit** = fiscal deficit − interest payments. The **FRBM Act (2003)** sets targets to control deficits; Bihar has its own FRBM law.

**GST.** The **Goods and Services Tax** began on **1 July 2017** after the **101st Amendment (2016)**. It replaced many central and state taxes. The **GST Council** (Article **279A**) — chaired by the Union Finance Minister with state finance ministers — decides rates. GST is a **destination-based** tax: it goes to the state where goods are consumed, which helps consuming states like Bihar.

**Finance Commission.** A body set up every five years under **Article 280** to recommend how central taxes are shared with states (**vertical devolution**) and among states (**horizontal devolution**, using population, area, income distance, etc.). Bihar gets one of the largest shares because of its large population and low income. (The 16th Finance Commission covers 2026–31 — check its final numbers.)

**MSP and agricultural marketing.** **MSP** (minimum support price) is the price at which the government promises to buy certain crops; it is announced on the advice of the **CACP**. Bihar **repealed its APMC Act in 2006**, so it has no regulated mandis; farmers sell to private traders and the state procures paddy through **PACS** (primary agricultural credit societies).

**Food security.** The **National Food Security Act 2013** covers up to **75% of rural and 50% of urban** people; each eligible person gets **5 kg of foodgrains per month** at subsidised prices, and **Antyodaya** households get 35 kg per household. Under PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana foodgrains are now free (as of 2026 — check the latest).

**Poverty.** **Income poverty** counts people below a money line (Tendulkar and Rangarajan committees). **Multidimensional poverty** (MPI) looks at **health, education and living standards** together. NITI Aayog's national MPI showed Bihar with the highest share of multidimensionally poor people, though falling (check the latest report).

**Unemployment types.** **Disguised** (more people on a farm than needed — common in Bihar), **seasonal**, **structural**, **frictional**, **educated** unemployment. Large **out-migration** from Bihar is linked to low local jobs.

**Balance of payments.** The record of a country's transactions with the world. **Current account** — trade in goods and services, remittances, income. **Capital account** — investment (FDI, FPI), loans. **FDI** brings long-term control; **FPI** is portfolio money in shares and bonds.

**International organisations.** **IMF** and **World Bank** — created at **Bretton Woods (1944)**, headquarters **Washington DC**. **WTO** — started on **1 January 1995**, headquarters **Geneva**; India raises concerns about food stockholding and farm subsidies there.

**Economy of Bihar.** Bihar has had one of the **higher growth rates** among states in recent years, but its **per capita income is among the lowest** in India. The **services sector** has the largest share of its economy, agriculture employs most people, and industry is small. Use the **latest Bihar Economic Survey** (finance.bihar.gov.in) for current figures and write the year next to every number.
