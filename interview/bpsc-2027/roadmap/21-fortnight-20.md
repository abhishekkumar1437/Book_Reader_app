Your goal this fortnight is to **finish Round 1 and start the faster Round 2**. In Round 2 you revise **one whole subject in one day**, mainly from its question bank and your mistake log, and you take a mock **every other day**. Speed matters now: you should be recalling, not re-learning.

> **In simple words:** You finish the first revision with science-tech, Bihar facts, current affairs and maths-reasoning. Then you start the second revision, where each subject gets just one day, and you take a full mock every second day.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** daily. On Day 273 (end of Round 1) and Day 280 revise the week's CA notes as well.

## How Round 2 differs from Round 1

| | Round 1 (Days 246–273) | Round 2 (Days 274–286) |
|---|---|---|
| Unit per day | One block of a subject | One whole subject |
| Source | Notes + quick-revision boxes | Question bank + mistake log |
| Past papers | 25 questions a day | 30 questions a day |
| Mocks | 2 per week | Alternate days |
| Aim | Refresh everything | Speed and accuracy |

**The alternate-day rhythm.** Revision day → mock day → revision day → mock day. On a mock day, take the mock at the same time as the real exam (check your admit card later; recent BPSC Prelims have been held around midday), then analyse for an hour, then revise a small Bihar block.

## What you will learn

**GMA under time.** Prelims GMA questions should take under a minute each. Know your fastest methods: series (differences), coding (position values A = 1 … Z = 26), blood relations (draw a family tree), directions (draw arrows), percentages (fractions like 1/8 = 12.5%), averages (deviation method), time-speed-distance (km/h × 5/18 = m/s).

**Current affairs consolidation.** By Day 270 your master file should cover about 9 months. Revise it by heading: national, international, Bihar, economy, science, environment, sports and awards, appointments (marked "check latest").

**Bihar facts at speed.** Use flashcards for firsts, symbols, districts, rivers, Ramsar sites, GI tags, schemes with years, and Census 2011 numbers.

**Mistake log patterns.** By now you will see patterns: e.g. confusion between similar dates, wrong guesses in science, misreading "not correct" questions. Write one rule per pattern at the top of the log.

**Reading the question.** "Which of the following is **not** correct?" questions are a common trap. Underline "not" or "incorrect" on the question paper before you read the options.

## End-of-fortnight self-check

1. Convert 72 km/h into m/s.
2. What percentage is 1/8?
3. In letter coding with A = 1, what number is the letter M?
4. Find the next number: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
5. If 5 people's average age is 30 and a sixth person aged 36 joins, what is the new average?
6. What should you underline first in a "not correct" question?
7. How often are mocks taken in Round 2?
8. What is the main source in Round 2?
9. Which state of India has the highest population density as per Census 2011?
10. In which year did Bihar ban liquor?

**Mains question to write in full (250 words, one answer to keep the hand moving):** "Discuss the importance of the Bihar Right to Public Services Act, 2011 for good governance." Points: meaning of time-bound services; services covered; designated officers, appeals, penalties; impact — less corruption and delay, online RTPS counters; problems — awareness, staff shortage, digital divide; link to RTI and citizen charters; conclusion.

### Answers

1. 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s**.
2. **12.5%**.
3. **13**.
4. Differences 4, 6, 8, 10 → next 12 → **42**.
5. (5 × 30 + 36) ÷ 6 = 186 ÷ 6 = **31**.
6. The words **"not" / "incorrect"**.
7. On **alternate days**.
8. The **question banks** of each book and your **mistake log**.
9. **Bihar** (1,106 per km²).
10. **2016**.

## If you fall behind

- If a Round 2 subject day slips, revise that subject's quick-revision boxes in the afternoon after the next mock's analysis.
- Compress world geography and ancient history to their question-bank "last-week revision" boxes.
- Keep every mock; if you must drop one, drop a Round 2 revision day, never a mock.
