Your goal this fortnight is to **learn India's physical geography with Bihar layered on top of it**. BPSC asks many questions on Bihar's rivers, floods, soils, crops, protected areas and minerals, and Mains GS II has a full section on the geography of India and Bihar. By Day 98 you should be able to draw a schematic map of Bihar's rivers from memory.

> **In simple words:** India has mountains in the north, plains in the middle and a plateau in the south. Bihar sits in the middle Ganga plain: the Ganga flows west to east through it, Himalayan rivers like the Kosi and Gandak bring floods to the north, and the south is drier and borders the plateau. The monsoon decides how farming goes every year.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** daily, with special attention to monsoon, flood and farming news from Bihar.

## What you will learn

**Straits.** A strait is a narrow water passage joining two larger water bodies. Examples: **Strait of Malacca** (Andaman Sea – South China Sea; between the Malay Peninsula and Sumatra), **Palk Strait** (India – Sri Lanka), **Strait of Hormuz** (Persian Gulf – Gulf of Oman; oil route), **Strait of Gibraltar** (Atlantic – Mediterranean), **Bab-el-Mandeb** (Red Sea – Gulf of Aden).

**Physiographic divisions of India.** Six: the **Himalaya**, the **Northern Plains**, the **Peninsular Plateau**, the **Indian Desert**, the **Coastal Plains** and the **Islands**. The Himalaya has three parallel ranges: **Himadri** (Greater), **Himachal** (Lesser) and **Shiwalik** (Outer).

**Bihar's physical regions.** Bihar's area is about **94,163 km²**. It borders **Nepal** (north), **West Bengal** (east), **Uttar Pradesh** (west) and **Jharkhand** (south). Main regions: the **Shiwalik foothills** in the far north-west (West Champaran), the **north Ganga plain**, the **south Ganga plain**, and the **southern plateau fringe** (hills of Rajgir, Gaya, Kaimur).

**Himalayan vs peninsular rivers.** Himalayan rivers (Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra systems) are **perennial** — fed by snow and rain — and change course often. Peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Narmada, Tapi) are mostly **rain-fed** and flow in hard rock valleys.

**The Ganga in Bihar.** The Ganga enters Bihar near **Buxar (Chausa)** and leaves near **Kahalgaon–Pirpainti**. North-bank tributaries from the Himalaya: **Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak and Kosi** (the **Bagmati** and **Kamla** join the Kosi system; the **Mahananda** drains the far north-east). South-bank tributaries from the plateau: **Karmanasa, Son, Punpun and Kiul** (the **Falgu** at Gaya mostly runs dry and fades into the plain). The **Gandak** joins the Ganga near **Hajipur–Sonepur**; the **Son** joins it near **Patna**.

**Kosi — "Sorrow of Bihar".** The Kosi carries a huge silt load from the Himalaya, shifts its course often (it has moved far westward over about two centuries), and floods large areas of north Bihar. Embankments, the Kosi barrage (in Nepal) and the 2008 Kusaha breach are standard examples in answers.

**Monsoon.** The south-west monsoon reaches Kerala around **1 June** and Bihar around mid-June. It comes because land heats faster than sea, low pressure forms over north India, the **ITCZ** (a low-pressure belt near the equator) shifts north, and winds from the Indian Ocean bring rain. **El Niño** (warm Pacific waters) often weakens the Indian monsoon.

**Soils.** **Alluvial** (most widespread; northern plains; fertile — Bihar's main soil), **black** (regur; Deccan; cotton), **red and yellow**, **laterite** (heavy rain areas; leached), **arid**, **forest/mountain**. In Bihar, the north has young alluvium; there are **tal** (low-lying, waterlogged in rains) and **diara** (river-bank sand) lands.

**Protected areas.** A **national park** strictly protects one area; a **wildlife sanctuary** allows some human activity; a **biosphere reserve** protects a large ecosystem with core, buffer and transition zones. **Valmiki Tiger Reserve** (West Champaran) is Bihar's only tiger reserve.

**Bihar's crops and resources.** Rice, wheat and maize are the main cereals; Bihar is a leading producer of **makhana** (fox nut) and **litchi**. GI tags include **Shahi Litchi of Muzaffarpur**, **Mithila Makhana**, Jardalu mango (Bhagalpur), Katarni rice and Magahi paan (check the latest GI list). After Jharkhand was formed in 2000, most of the mineral belt went to Jharkhand; Bihar kept limestone, pyrite and some others.

**Minerals and energy in India.** Coal is found mainly in the **Damodar valley** (Jharkhand, West Bengal), Odisha and Chhattisgarh; iron ore in Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Karnataka. Most electricity still comes from coal (thermal power); renewable capacity is growing fast.

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Why is the Kosi called the 'Sorrow of Bihar'? Suggest measures for flood management in north Bihar." Points: Himalayan source, steep fall then flat plain, high silt load, course shifting, embankment failures (2008 Kusaha breach); impacts on people, crops, migration; measures — embankment repair, early warning, the proposed high dam in Nepal (needs India–Nepal cooperation), river interlinking proposals, flood-plain zoning, raised houses and flood shelters, crop insurance; conclusion — "living with floods" plus engineering.

### Answers

1. The **Palk Strait**.
2. **Himadri (Greater)**, **Himachal (Lesser)**, **Shiwalik (Outer)**.
3. About **94,163 km²**.
4. The **Kosi**.
5. **Ghaghara, Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Kosi** (Bagmati and Kamla belong to the Kosi system).
6. Any three of: **Karmanasa, Son, Punpun, Kiul**.
7. Near **Hajipur** (opposite Patna; Sonepur).
8. **Alluvial soil**.
9. **Valmiki Tiger Reserve**, **West Champaran**.
10. **Shahi Litchi**.
