Your goal this fortnight is to **turn the knowledge of Phases 1–2 into high-scoring Mains answers**. Mains decides your rank: GS I, GS II and Essay carry 900 of the 1,020 merit marks. You have been writing one answer a day since Day 29; now the answer-writing block becomes the main subject, and you write **two timed answers a day** and compare them with model answers.

> **In simple words:** An examiner gives marks for a clear structure, relevant points, Bihar examples, data and a sensible conclusion — not for long paragraphs. This fortnight you learn that pattern and practise it on every GS topic.

The **1-hour newspaper block** continues; GMA is now mixed timed sets. Day 210 is the **month-7 revision day** with the monthly compilation.

## What you will learn

**How Mains is evaluated.** Examiners read many copies quickly. They look for: (1) whether you answered **what was asked** (the directive word), (2) **coverage** of the main dimensions, (3) **structure** — introduction, body with sub-headings or points, conclusion, (4) **facts and examples**, especially from Bihar, (5) **presentation** — neat handwriting, underlining, diagrams.

**Directive words.** **Discuss** — explain different sides with examples. **Examine** — look at the topic in detail, with causes and effects. **Critically examine / analyse** — give merits and limitations and your judgement. **Evaluate / assess** — judge the value or impact, with evidence. **Describe** — give the features or story. **Explain** — make clear how or why.

**Introduction types.** (1) **Definition** — "Panchayati Raj is the system of rural local self-government…". (2) **Fact or data** — "Bihar's Census 2011 density of 1,106 per km² is the highest in India…". (3) **Context** — a historical or current event that leads into the question. Keep it to 2–3 lines.

**Body.** Break the question into parts and give each a short heading or numbered point. Use 5–8 points for a long answer. Put one **Bihar example** or one **data point with year** in at least two points.

**Conclusion.** A balanced closing line: a way forward, a summary judgement, or a link to a constitutional value or development goal. Never introduce a new argument in the conclusion.

**Diagrams.** Simple flowcharts, tables, schematic maps (Bihar's rivers, flood zones), and bar or pie sketches earn marks quickly. Draw them with a pencil and scale; label clearly.

**Bihar data card.** One page of 20 numbers you can use in many answers, each with **source and year**: population and density (Census 2011), literacy, sex ratio, growth rate and per capita income (latest Economic Survey), sector shares, women's reservation in panchayats (50%), and similar.

**Current-events answer frame.** Background → what happened → why it matters (issues) → impact on India and Bihar → way forward.

**Mains question to write in full (300 words, timed):** "Discuss the role of Bihar in the Quit India Movement." Points: 8 August resolution and arrest of leaders; 11 August 1942 Patna Secretariat firing; spread to districts — attacks on railways, telegraph and police stations; parallel governments and underground action; JP's escape from Hazaribagh and the Azad Dasta; repression and collective fines; role of students and women; significance — showed people's determination, prepared ground for independence.

### Answers

1. **Introduction, body, conclusion**.
2. Look at **merits and limitations** of the issue and give your own **balanced judgement** with evidence.
3. Any two of: a **definition**, a **fact or data point**, a **historical or current context**.
4. About **5–8** points.
5. BPSC is a Bihar exam; the syllabus asks for the Bihar angle, and Bihar examples show **relevance and depth**, which examiners reward.
6. It should never bring in a **new argument**; it should close and balance the answer.
7. **GS Paper I, GS Paper II and Essay** (300 marks each).
8. **30%** (it is qualifying; marks are not added to merit).
9. Its **source and year**.
10. A **schematic map** of north Bihar showing the Himalayan rivers (Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kosi) and flood-prone belts, or a **flowchart** of causes → impacts → measures.
