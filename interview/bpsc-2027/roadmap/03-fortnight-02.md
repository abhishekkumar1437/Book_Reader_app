Your goal this fortnight is to cover **about 1,800 years of history in 12 study days** — from the Shungas to the British land settlements — while keeping one eye on Bihar: Nalanda and Vikramshila, Sher Shah of Sasaram, Guru Gobind Singh's Patna, and the Battle of Buxar. By Day 28 you will also make your first monthly current-affairs compilation.

> **In simple words:** After the Mauryas, India split into many kingdoms, then came together again under the Guptas (a golden age of art and science). Turkish and Afghan rulers set up the Delhi Sultanate; the Mughals built an even bigger empire. Then European traders came, and the British slowly took over — Bihar's Buxar battle (1764) was the turning point.

Keep the **1-hour newspaper block** every day. Day 28 is the **first month-end revision day**: build your month-1 compilation.

## What you will learn

**Post-Mauryan kingdoms.** The **Shungas** (founded by Pushyamitra Shunga, about 185 BCE) replaced the Mauryas in Magadha. Foreign groups came from the north-west: Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Parthians and **Kushanas**. **Kanishka**, the greatest Kushana king, is linked with the **Shaka era (78 CE)**, the fourth Buddhist council in Kashmir, and Mahayana Buddhism. In the Deccan, the **Satavahanas** ruled; in the far south, the Sangam age produced Tamil poetry.

**The Gupta age (about 319–550 CE).** Founded by Sri Gupta; strengthened by **Chandragupta I** (Gupta era 319–320 CE), **Samudragupta** (Allahabad pillar inscription by Harisena) and **Chandragupta II "Vikramaditya"** (Chinese pilgrim **Fa-Hien** visited). **Kumaragupta I** founded **Nalanda Mahavihara**. Science: **Aryabhata** (zero, place value, earth's rotation); literature: **Kalidasa**. It is called "classical" because of these high standards in art, science and literature.

**Harsha and the Palas.** **Harshavardhana** (606–647 CE) ruled from Kannauj; **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)** studied at Nalanda and described Harsha's assemblies at Kannauj and Prayag. The **Palas** of Bengal and Bihar (founder **Gopala**, elected by nobles) were Buddhist patrons; **Dharmapala** founded **Vikramshila** (Bhagalpur district). The Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas fought the **tripartite struggle** over Kannauj.

**The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526).** Five dynasties: **Mamluk/Slave (1206), Khalji (1290), Tughlaq (1320), Sayyid (1414), Lodi (1451)**. **Iltutmish** organised the **iqta** system (land revenue assignments to officers) and silver tanka coins. **Alauddin Khalji** fixed market prices. **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** tried bold experiments (capital shift to Daulatabad, token copper currency). **Bakhtiyar Khalji** attacked Bihar around 1200 and is linked with the destruction of Nalanda and Odantapuri.

**The Mughals and Sher Shah.** **Babur** won the first battle of Panipat (1526). **Sher Shah Suri** (Afghan ruler from Sasaram, Bihar) defeated Humayun at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj (1540)**. His reforms: the silver **rupiya**, land measurement for revenue, roads (the route now called the Grand Trunk Road), **sarais** (rest houses), and a postal system. His **tomb at Sasaram** is a famous Indo-Islamic monument. **Akbar** set up the **mansabdari** system (ranks for officers) and the **Ibadat Khana** for religious debate. **Guru Gobind Singh** was born at **Patna (1666)**.

**Bhakti and Sufi.** **Bhakti** means loving devotion to God without rituals or caste barriers (Kabir, Ramananda, Chaitanya, Mirabai, Guru Nanak). **Sufism** is the mystical stream of Islam, organised in **silsilas** (orders) like Chishti and Suhrawardi; in Bihar, **Sheikh Sharfuddin Ahmad Yahya Maneri** of the **Firdausi** silsila is buried at Bihar Sharif.

**Vijayanagara and the Marathas.** Vijayanagara was founded in **1336** by Harihara and Bukka and was defeated at **Talikota (1565)**. **Shivaji** set up the Maratha kingdom with a council of eight ministers (**Ashtapradhan**) and collected **chauth** (one-fourth) and **sardeshmukhi** (one-tenth extra).

**Europeans and the British.** Order of arrival: **Portuguese (Vasco da Gama, 1498), Dutch, English, Danes, French**. **Plassey (1757)** made the English powerful in Bengal; **Buxar (22 October 1764)** — the English defeated the joint army of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II — and the **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** gave the Company the **Diwani** (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.

**Land revenue systems.** **Permanent Settlement (1793, Cornwallis)** — zamindars became owners and paid a fixed revenue forever; used in Bengal and Bihar. **Ryotwari** — direct settlement with cultivators (Madras, Bombay; Thomas Munro). **Mahalwari** — settlement with the village (north-west; Holt Mackenzie). **Drain of wealth** — Dadabhai Naoroji's idea that Britain took India's wealth without return.

## End-of-fortnight self-check

1. Which Gupta ruler founded Nalanda Mahavihara?
2. Which Pala king founded Vikramshila, and in which present district is it?
3. Which Chinese pilgrim visited during Chandragupta II's reign, and which one during Harsha's reign?
4. Who introduced the iqta system in an organised form?
5. In which battle did Sher Shah defeat Humayun in 1539, and in which state is the battlefield?
6. Where is Sher Shah Suri's tomb?
7. Where and when was Guru Gobind Singh born?
8. Name the three parties defeated by the English at Buxar.
9. What did the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) give the East India Company?
10. Who introduced the Permanent Settlement and in which year?

**Mains question to write in full (250 words):** "Evaluate the administrative and economic reforms of Sher Shah Suri." Points: revenue (measurement, crop share, patta and qabuliyat), currency (rupiya), roads and sarais, postal system, police and justice, army reforms; conclude that Akbar built on these reforms.

### Answers

1. **Kumaragupta I** (5th century CE).
2. **Dharmapala**; **Bhagalpur** district (Antichak).
3. **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** under Chandragupta II; **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)** under Harsha.
4. **Iltutmish**.
5. **Battle of Chausa**, in present **Bihar** (Buxar district).
6. **Sasaram** (Rohtas district, Bihar).
7. **Patna**, in **1666**.
8. **Mir Qasim** (Nawab of Bengal), **Shuja-ud-Daula** (Nawab of Awadh) and the Mughal emperor **Shah Alam II**.
9. The **Diwani** — the right to collect revenue — of **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.
10. **Lord Cornwallis**, in **1793**.
