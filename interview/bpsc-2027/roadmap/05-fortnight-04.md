## What you will learn

**Temple styles.** **Nagara** (north India) — curved tower called **shikhara**, no big boundary wall; example: Khajuraho, Konark. **Dravida** (south India) — pyramid-like tower called **vimana**, tall gateways called **gopurams**, walled enclosure; example: Brihadeeswara, Thanjavur. **Vesara** — a mix, seen in Karnataka (Chalukya and Hoysala temples).

**Buddhist architecture.** **Stupa** — a solid dome holding relics (Sanchi; Kesaria in East Champaran is among the tallest excavated stupas). **Chaitya** — prayer hall; **vihara** — monastery. Mauryan art is known for polished **pillars** with animal capitals (Lion Capital at Sarnath — the national emblem).

**Mughal art.** Architecture: Humayun's Tomb, Fatehpur Sikri, Taj Mahal (domes, arches, charbagh gardens, pietra dura inlay). Painting: miniature paintings, which reached a peak under **Jahangir**. **Patna Kalam** — a Bihar school of painting that grew in the 18th–19th centuries when painters from the Mughal tradition settled in Patna and painted everyday life.

**Classical dances.** Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu), Kathak (north India), Kathakali and Mohiniyattam (Kerala), Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh), Odissi (Odisha), Manipuri (Manipur), Sattriya (Assam).

**Bihar's culture.** Folk paintings: **Madhubani (Mithila)**, **Manjusha** (Bhagalpur, linked with the Bihula–Vishahari tale), **Tikuli** art, **Sikki** grass craft. Festivals: **Chhath** (worship of the Sun god), **Sama-Chakeva** (brother–sister festival in Mithila). Languages: Maithili, Bhojpuri, Magahi, Angika, Bajjika. **Maithili** was added to the **Eighth Schedule** by the **92nd Amendment (2003)**.

**Making of the Constitution.** The **Constituent Assembly** was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**. It first met on **9 December 1946**, with **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha** as interim (temporary) President; **Dr Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President on **11 December 1946**. **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the **Drafting Committee**. The Constitution was adopted on **26 November 1949** (Constitution Day) and came into force on **26 January 1950** (Republic Day).

**Preamble.** The introduction to the Constitution, declaring India a **sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic** and promising justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. The words "socialist", "secular" and "integrity" were added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)**.

**Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35).** Six groups: equality (14–18), freedom (19–22), against exploitation (23–24), religion (25–28), cultural and educational (29–30), constitutional remedies (32). **Article 32** lets you go directly to the Supreme Court; it uses **writs**: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto.

**Directive Principles (Part IV, Articles 36–51).** Goals the state should follow in making laws (borrowed from the Irish Constitution). They are **not enforceable in court** but are "fundamental in the governance of the country" (Article 37).

**Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA).** Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the **Swaran Singh Committee's** advice; ten at first, the eleventh (education of children aged 6–14) added by the **86th Amendment (2002)**.

**The President.** Elected indirectly by an **electoral college** of elected MPs and elected MLAs of states (and of Delhi and Puducherry). Removed by **impeachment** (Article 61) for violation of the Constitution.

**Mains question to write in full (300 words):** "Distinguish between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy. Can they be reconciled?" Points: Part III vs Part IV; justiciable vs non-justiciable; negative limits on the state vs positive goals; cases — Champakam Dorairajan (1951), Golaknath (1967), Kesavananda Bharati (1973), Minerva Mills (1980) and the idea of harmony between the two; Bihar example: land reform and DPSP-based welfare schemes; conclusion that both together aim at social justice.

1. Nagara — **shikhara**; Dravida — **vimana**.
2. The **92nd Amendment**, **2003** (along with Bodo, Dogri and Santhali).
3. **Dr Sachchidananda Sinha**.
4. **11 December 1946**.
5. Adopted **26 November 1949**; in force **26 January 1950**.
6. The **42nd Amendment (1976)**.
7. **Article 32** (right to constitutional remedies).
8. **Habeas corpus**.
9. **Ireland**.
10. The **86th Amendment (2002)**.
