Your goal in these last days is to **walk into each Mains paper calm, rested and with everything fresh in mind**, and to know what comes next: the **Interview (Personality Test)**, which carries **120 marks** in the 1,020-mark merit. The writing work is done; now you revise lightly and protect your energy, because Mains papers are spread over several days.

> **In simple words:** Revise only your one-page sheets, data card and outlines. Sleep well. Follow the checklist for each paper day. After the exams, rest a little, then start preparing your interview from your own application form (DAF) and Bihar.

The **current-affairs block** is now a short daily look at the news plus revision of your master file. Day 385 is the **last monthly compilation** (month 13).

## Mains-day checklist

**Before the first paper (Day 390)**
- [ ] Download and print the **Mains admit card** (two copies); check every paper's date, time and centre.
- [ ] Keep the **original photo ID** named on the admit card and any photos asked for.
- [ ] Keep **3–4 good pens** of the colour instructed, a **pencil, eraser, sharpener and a scale** for diagrams (if allowed — check the admit card).
- [ ] Keep a watch if allowed (no smart watch); a transparent water bottle if allowed.
- [ ] Read the instructions on the admit card about banned items.
- [ ] Know the route and travel time to the centre.
- [ ] Make a small envelope for each paper day with that day's one-page sheets.

**On each paper day**
- [ ] Light, familiar meal; reach early.
- [ ] Read the whole question paper and instructions first (2–3 minutes); mark the questions you will attempt.
- [ ] Plan time per question from the marks; write the easiest well-known questions first if allowed in any order.
- [ ] Use the answer structure: introduction → body points → conclusion; add a Bihar example, a data point with year, and a diagram where it fits.
- [ ] Leave space and write question numbers clearly.
- [ ] Keep 10 minutes at the end to complete unfinished answers in point form and check numbering.

**Paper-specific notes**
- [ ] **General Hindi:** read the précis and comprehension passages twice; keep the précis to about one-third; follow letter format; write clean Devanagari.
- [ ] **GS I:** attempt the statistics section with full working and neat graphs; history answers need Bihar's role; current-events answers need background → issue → impact → way forward.
- [ ] **GS II:** quote articles in polity; use data with year in economy; draw schematic maps in geography; link S&T to Bihar's development.
- [ ] **Essay:** plan for 10 minutes per essay; one essay from each section; quotes and Bihar examples; balanced conclusion.
- [ ] **Optional (objective):** read each question fully; follow the paper's marking instructions.

**Between papers**
- [ ] Do not discuss the finished paper; revise only the next paper's one-page sheets.
- [ ] Sleep 7–8 hours every night of the exam days.

## Interview preparation outline

The interview is a conversation with a board, usually lasting a short time, to judge your **personality, awareness and suitability for public service in Bihar**. Read [The Interview (Personality Test)](/interview/bpsc-2027/exam-guide/the-interview-personality-test) for the full guide. Start this work after Mains (or after the Mains result, if the gap is short).

**1. Your DAF (Detailed Application Form).** Most questions start from it. For each line — name, home district, school and college, subject, job, hobbies, sports, achievements — prepare 5–10 likely questions. Example: if your hobby is reading, know your last few books well.

**2. Your district and Bihar.** History, geography, economy, famous people, problems and recent government work in your home district; Bihar's major issues (floods, migration, education, health, industry, law and order) and schemes (as of the interview date — check the latest).

**3. Your service preference.** Why you want to be a Deputy Collector, DSP or another post; what the job involves; how you would handle a flood relief camp, a land dispute or a law-and-order situation.

**4. Current affairs.** The last 6 months of national, international and Bihar news, with your own balanced opinion on big issues.

**5. Your academic subject.** Basics of your graduation subject and its use in administration.

**6. Situation and ethics questions.** Answer with the law first, then fairness, then compassion; never promise to break rules.

**7. Mock interviews.** Do 3–5 mock interviews with honest feedback; record yourself to check speech speed, eye contact and posture.

**8. Dress and behaviour.** Simple formal clothes; greet the board politely; listen fully before answering; say "I do not know, sir/madam" honestly instead of guessing; stay calm and polite even under pressure.

## What you will learn

**Peak, not panic.** In the last days, energy and calm add more marks than one more chapter. Revise what you know so it comes out quickly in the hall.

**Paper-by-paper focus.** Each Mains paper day is its own small exam. Finish one, forget it, prepare the next.

**The DAF.** A detailed form filled by candidates who qualify Prelims (check BPSC's process for the 73rd CCE). Fill it honestly and carefully — the interview board reads it before you enter.

## End-of-fortnight self-check

1. How many marks does the interview carry, and what is the final merit total?
2. What should you do in the first 2–3 minutes of a Mains paper?
3. What three things should you add to answers wherever they fit?
4. How should you prepare between two paper days?
5. What is the DAF, and why does it matter?
6. Name four areas of interview preparation.
7. How should you answer a question you do not know in the interview?
8. In which order should you think through a situation question?
9. How many mock interviews are suggested?
10. What should your dress be for the interview?

**Mains question to write in full:** None — rest and revise. After Mains, write a one-page note: "My home district: history, economy, problems and solutions" — your first interview preparation.

### Answers

1. **120 marks**; final merit **1,020** (GS I + GS II + Essay 900 + Interview 120).
2. **Read the whole paper and instructions**, and mark the questions you will attempt.
3. A **Bihar example**, a **data point with year**, and a **diagram**.
4. Do **not discuss** the finished paper; revise only the **next paper's one-page sheets**; sleep well.
5. The **Detailed Application Form** with your personal, academic and other details; the interview board **asks many questions from it**.
6. Any four of: **DAF, home district and Bihar, service preference, current affairs, academic subject, situation and ethics questions**.
7. Say honestly, **"I do not know"**, politely, instead of guessing.
8. **Law first, then fairness, then compassion** — never break rules.
9. **3–5**.
10. **Simple, formal and comfortable** clothes.

## If you fall behind

- There is no catching up now. Revise only one-page sheets and the data card. Rest is part of the plan.
- If a Mains paper goes badly, do not let it spoil the next one — each paper is marked separately.
