## Set 1 — Number and letter series (Problems 1–16)

**1.** 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ? — (a) 125 (b) 127 (c) 129 (d) 131

*Solution:* Each term × 2 + 1: 3 → 7 → 15 → 31 → 63 → 63 × 2 + 1 = 127. **Answer: (b) 127**

**2.** 2, 9, 28, 65, 126, ? — (a) 215 (b) 216 (c) 217 (d) 218

*Solution:* Terms are n³ + 1: 1 + 1, 8 + 1, 27 + 1, 64 + 1, 125 + 1. Next 6³ + 1 = 217. **Answer: (c) 217**

**3.** 1, 5, 14, 30, 55, ? — (a) 85 (b) 89 (c) 91 (d) 96

*Solution:* Differences 4, 9, 16, 25 are squares (2², 3², 4², 5²). Next difference 6² = 36 → 55 + 36 = 91. **Answer: (c) 91**

**4.** 10, 18, 34, 66, 130, ? — (a) 258 (b) 260 (c) 262 (d) 250

*Solution:* × 2 − 2: 10 × 2 − 2 = 18, 18 × 2 − 2 = 34, … 130 × 2 − 2 = 258. **Answer: (a) 258**

**5.** 4, 12, 36, 108, ? — (a) 216 (b) 324 (c) 432 (d) 256

*Solution:* × 3 each time → 108 × 3 = 324. **Answer: (b) 324**

**6.** 5, 8, 13, 20, 29, ? — (a) 38 (b) 40 (c) 41 (d) 42

*Solution:* Differences 3, 5, 7, 9 → next 11 → 29 + 11 = 40. **Answer: (b) 40**

**7.** 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16, ? — (a) 21 (b) 22 (c) 23 (d) 24

*Solution:* Differences 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 → next 6 → 16 + 6 = 22. **Answer: (b) 22**

**8.** 6, 12, 21, 33, 48, ? — (a) 63 (b) 64 (c) 66 (d) 69

*Solution:* Differences 6, 9, 12, 15 (multiples of 3) → next 18 → 48 + 18 = 66. **Answer: (c) 66**

**9.** 1, 10, 3, 20, 5, 30, 7, ? — (a) 9 (b) 35 (c) 38 (d) 40

*Solution:* Two series: odd places 1, 3, 5, 7; even places 10, 20, 30 → next 40. **Answer: (d) 40**

**10.** Find the wrong term: 5, 10, 17, 26, 37, 50, 64 — (a) 17 (b) 37 (c) 50 (d) 64

*Solution:* Terms are n² + 1 for n = 2 to 8: 5, 10, 17, 26, 37, 50, 65. The last term should be 65. **Answer: (d) 64**

**11.** Find the wrong term: 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 242 — (a) 9 (b) 27 (c) 81 (d) 242

*Solution:* Powers of 3: 3⁰ … 3⁵ = 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243. **Answer: (d) 242**

**12.** B, F, J, N, R, ? — (a) U (b) V (c) W (d) X

*Solution:* Positions 2, 6, 10, 14, 18 (+4) → 22 = V. **Answer: (b) V**

**13.** AB, DE, GH, JK, ? — (a) LM (b) MN (c) NO (d) MO

*Solution:* Each pair is two neighbouring letters, and each new pair starts 3 after the last: A, D, G, J → M. So MN. **Answer: (b) MN**

**14.** Z, W, S, N, ? — (a) G (b) H (c) I (d) J

*Solution:* Positions 26, 23, 19, 14: differences −3, −4, −5 → next −6 → 14 − 6 = 8 = H. **Answer: (b) H**

**15.** ACE, FHJ, KMO, ? — (a) PRT (b) PQR (c) QSU (d) ORT

*Solution:* Positions (1, 3, 5), (6, 8, 10), (11, 13, 15): each group +5, and inside a group +2. Next (16, 18, 20) = P, R, T. **Answer: (a) PRT**

**16.** 0, 3, 8, 15, 24, ? — (a) 33 (b) 35 (c) 36 (d) 37

*Solution:* n² − 1 for n = 1 to 5: 0, 3, 8, 15, 24 → 6² − 1 = 35. **Answer: (b) 35**

## Set 2 — Coding–decoding (Problems 17–28)

**17.** If TABLE is coded as UBCMF, how is CHAIR coded? — (a) DIBJS (b) DIBKS (c) BGZHQ (d) DHBJS

*Solution:* Each letter +1 (T→U, A→B, B→C, L→M, E→F). CHAIR → D, I, B, J, S. **Answer: (a) DIBJS**

**18.** If DELHI is coded as IHLED, how is PATNA coded? — (a) ANTPA (b) ANTAP (c) APTNA (d) NAPTA

*Solution:* The word is written backwards. PATNA backwards = ANTAP. **Answer: (b) ANTAP**

**19.** If RAM is coded as IZN, how is SHYAM coded? — (a) HSBZN (b) HSAZN (c) HTBZN (d) GSBZN

*Solution:* Opposite letters (R18 ↔ I9, A ↔ Z, M ↔ N). S19 → H8, H8 → S19, Y25 → B2, A → Z, M → N. **Answer: (a) HSBZN**

**20.** If MOTHER is coded as 13-15-20-8-5-18, how is SISTER coded? — (a) 19-9-19-20-5-18 (b) 19-9-19-21-5-18 (c) 18-9-18-20-5-19 (d) 19-8-19-20-5-18

*Solution:* Each letter → its position. S19, I9, S19, T20, E5, R18. **Answer: (a) 19-9-19-20-5-18**

**21.** If ABC = 6 (sum of letter positions), what is BIHAR? — (a) 36 (b) 37 (c) 38 (d) 40

*Solution:* B2 + I9 + H8 + A1 + R18 = 38. **Answer: (c) 38**

**22.** If GOLD is coded as HNMC, how is SILVER coded? — (a) THMUFQ (b) THMWFQ (c) TJMUFQ (d) RHMUFQ

*Solution:* Shifts +1, −1, +1, −1 (G→H, O→N, L→M, D→C). SILVER: S+1 = T, I−1 = H, L+1 = M, V−1 = U, E+1 = F, R−1 = Q. **Answer: (a) THMUFQ**

**23.** In a code, "lo pi ka" means "eat fresh fruit", "ka mo ni" means "fruit is sweet" and "ni pi su" means "sweet fresh water". What is the code for "water"? — (a) pi (b) ni (c) su (d) lo

*Solution:* Sentences 1 & 2 share "fruit" = ka. Sentences 1 & 3 share "fresh" = pi. Sentences 2 & 3 share "sweet" = ni. In sentence 3, the remaining word "water" = su. **Answer: (c) su**

**24.** If + means −, − means ×, × means ÷ and ÷ means +, find 20 × 4 + 3 − 2 ÷ 6. — (a) 5 (b) 11 (c) 17 (d) 2

*Solution:* Replace signs: 20 ÷ 4 − 3 × 2 + 6. BODMAS: 20 ÷ 4 = 5; 3 × 2 = 6. 5 − 6 + 6 = 5. **Answer: (a) 5**

**25.** If "blue" is called "red", "red" is called "green", "green" is called "white" and "white" is called "yellow", what is the colour of milk? — (a) white (b) green (c) yellow (d) red

*Solution:* Milk is white, and "white" is called "yellow" in this language. **Answer: (c) yellow**

**26.** In a code, BEAT is written as 25120 (letter positions side by side). How is TEA written? — (a) 2051 (b) 2015 (c) 5201 (d) 20151

*Solution:* T = 20, E = 5, A = 1 → 2051. **Answer: (a) 2051**

**27.** If SYSTEM is coded as METSYS, how is PEOPLE coded? — (a) ELPOEP (b) PLEPEO (c) PLEOEP (d) LEPPEO

*Solution:* Write SYSTEM backwards: M, E, T, S, Y, S = METSYS. So the rule is complete reversal. (Option (b) PLEPEO comes from swapping the halves, which does not fit: SYSTEM with halves swapped would be TEMSYS.) PEOPLE backwards = ELPOEP. **Answer: (a) ELPOEP**

**28.** If 3 ★ 4 = 25 and 5 ★ 12 = 169, find 6 ★ 8. — (a) 48 (b) 100 (c) 196 (d) 14

*Solution:* a ★ b = a² + b²: 9 + 16 = 25 ✓, 25 + 144 = 169 ✓. 6 ★ 8 = 36 + 64 = 100. **Answer: (b) 100**

> **Common mistake:** Problem 27 shows why you must test the **simplest** rule first. "Complete reversal" explains SYSTEM → METSYS fully; a more complicated rule is not needed.

## Set 3 — Blood relations and direction sense (Problems 29–44)

**29.** Pointing to a girl, Raj says, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." How is the girl related to Raj? — (a) sister (b) niece (c) daughter (d) cousin

*Solution:* Raj is male, so his mother's only son is Raj himself. The girl is Raj's daughter. **Answer: (c) daughter**

**30.** A is the son of B. C is B's sister. D is C's son. How is D related to A? — (a) brother (b) cousin (c) nephew (d) uncle

*Solution:* C is A's aunt (B's sister). D is the aunt's son → A's cousin. **Answer: (b) cousin**

**31.** Introducing a boy, a girl says, "He is the son of the daughter of the father of my uncle." How is the boy related to the girl? — (a) brother (b) cousin (c) brother or cousin (d) nephew

*Solution:* The father of her uncle = her grandfather. The grandfather's daughter = her mother or her aunt. That daughter's son = her brother or her cousin. **Answer: (c) brother or cousin**

**32.** P $ Q means P is the father of Q; P # Q means P is the mother of Q; P * Q means P is the sister of Q. What does A # B $ C * D mean? — (a) A is the grandmother of D (b) A is the mother of D (c) A is the aunt of D (d) A is the sister of D

*Solution:* A # B → A is B's mother. B $ C → B is C's father. C * D → C is D's sister, so D is also B's child. A is the mother of D's father → grandmother. **Answer: (a)**

**33.** M is the wife of N. N is the only son of Mr O. P is the daughter of M and N. How is O related to P? — (a) father (b) grandfather (c) uncle (d) brother

*Solution:* O is N's father; P is N's daughter → O is P's grandfather. **Answer: (b) grandfather**

**34.** How is Ravi's mother's brother's only son related to Ravi? — (a) brother (b) nephew (c) cousin (d) uncle

*Solution:* Mother's brother = maternal uncle; his son = Ravi's cousin. **Answer: (c) cousin**

**35.** Kamla is the mother-in-law of Rekha, whose husband is Mohan. Kamla has only one son. How is Mohan related to Kamla? — (a) son (b) son-in-law (c) brother (d) nephew

*Solution:* Kamla is the mother of Rekha's husband (that is what "mother-in-law" means for a wife). Rekha's husband is Mohan → Mohan is Kamla's son. **Answer: (a) son**

**36.** Pointing to a photo, Arun said, "His father is the only son of my mother." Whose photo is it? — (a) Arun's brother (b) Arun's father (c) Arun's son (d) Arun's nephew

*Solution:* The only son of Arun's mother is Arun. So the person's father is Arun → the photo is of Arun's son. **Answer: (c)**

**37.** A man walks 4 km north, turns right and walks 3 km. How far is he from the start, and in which direction? — (a) 7 km, north-east (b) 5 km, north-east (c) 5 km, north-west (d) 1 km, east

*Solution:* North 4, then right (east) 3. Distance = √(16 + 9) = 5 km, towards north-east. **Answer: (b)**

**38.** Facing east, Priya turns left, then left again, then right. Which direction is she facing now? — (a) north (b) south (c) east (d) west

*Solution:* East → left → north → left → west → right → north. **Answer: (a) north**

**39.** In the morning, Ram stands so that his shadow falls directly behind him. Which direction is he facing? — (a) north (b) south (c) east (d) west

*Solution:* Morning sun is in the east; shadows fall west. The shadow is behind him → his back is to the west → he faces east. **Answer: (c) east**

**40.** Seema walks 15 m west, then turns right and walks 8 m. How far is she from the start? — (a) 23 m (b) 17 m (c) 7 m (d) 13 m

*Solution:* Facing west, right is north. West 15, north 8 → √(225 + 64) = √289 = 17 m (north-west). **Answer: (b) 17 m**

**41.** A goes 3 km north, 4 km east and then 3 km south. Where is he from the start? — (a) 4 km east (b) 4 km west (c) 10 km north-east (d) 5 km north-east

*Solution:* North 3 and south 3 cancel. Net: 4 km east. **Answer: (a)**

**42.** Neha walks 10 m north, turns right and walks 6 m, turns right and walks 10 m, then turns left and walks 4 m. Where is she from the start? — (a) 10 m east (b) 6 m east (c) 4 m west (d) 2 m east

*Solution:* North 10 → (0, 10). Right → east 6 → (6, 10). Right → south 10 → (6, 0). Facing south, left is east → 4 m → (10, 0). **Answer: (a) 10 m east**

**43.** If north-east is called south, south-east is called west, and so on, what is north called? — (a) south-east (b) south-west (c) north-west (d) east

*Solution:* North-east (45°) → south (180°): rotation +135°. Check south-east (135°) + 135° = 270° = west ✓. North (0°) + 135° = 135° = south-east. **Answer: (a) south-east**

**44.** A clock lies flat on a table so that at 12 noon its hands point north-east. At 1:30 p.m., in which direction does the hour hand point? — (a) north (b) north-east (c) east (d) south-east

*Solution:* At 1:30 the hour hand is 1.5 × 30° = 45° clockwise from the 12 mark. The 12 mark points north-east (45°), so the hour hand points at 45° + 45° = 90° = east. **Answer: (c) east**

## Set 4 — Ranking, order and seating (Problems 45–56)

**45.** Mohan is 18th from either end of a row. How many people are in the row? — (a) 34 (b) 35 (c) 36 (d) 37

*Solution:* Total = 2 × 18 − 1 = 35. **Answer: (b) 35**

**46.** In a class of 45, Rita is 15th from the top. What is her rank from the bottom? — (a) 30 (b) 31 (c) 32 (d) 29

*Solution:* 45 − 15 + 1 = 31. **Answer: (b) 31**

**47.** In a row of 40, A is 12th from the left and B is 18th from the right, and A is to the left of B. How many people are between them? — (a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 11 (d) 12

*Solution:* B from the left = 40 − 18 + 1 = 23. Between = 23 − 12 − 1 = 10. **Answer: (b) 10**

**48.** P is 9th from the left and Q is 14th from the right. They swap places, and P becomes 20th from the left. How many people are in the row? — (a) 32 (b) 33 (c) 34 (d) 29

*Solution:* P now stands where Q stood, so Q's old place = 20th from the left = 14th from the right. Total = 20 + 14 − 1 = 33. **Answer: (b) 33**

**49.** In a queue, Rahul is 5th from the front and Sohan is 7th from the back. There are 2 people between them. What is the minimum number of people in the queue? — (a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 10

*Solution:* Without overlap: 5 + 2 + 7 = 14. With overlap (Sohan ahead of Rahul): Sohan at 5 − 2 − 1 = 2nd from the front, and 7th from the back → total 2 + 7 − 1 = 8. Minimum = 8. **Answer: (a) 8**

**50.** A earns more than B but less than C. D earns more than C. E earns the least. Who earns the second highest? — (a) A (b) B (c) C (d) D

*Solution:* D > C > A > B > E. Second highest: C. **Answer: (c) C**

**51.** Among P, Q, R, S and T: Q is taller than only T. S is taller than R but shorter than P. Who is in the middle by height? — (a) P (b) Q (c) R (d) S

*Solution:* "Q taller than only T" → T shortest, Q second shortest. S > R and P > S → P > S > R. Order: P > S > R > Q > T. Middle (3rd): R. **Answer: (c) R**

**52.** Five people A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. A is at the left end. C is at the right end. E is immediately to the left of C. B sits between A and D. Who is in the middle? — (a) B (b) D (c) E (d) A

*Solution:* A = 1, C = 5, E = 4. B between A and D → B = 2, D = 3. Row: A B D E C. Middle: D. **Answer: (b) D**

**53.** Six people K, L, M, N, O and P sit around a round table facing the centre. K is opposite N. L is immediately to the left of K. M is immediately to the right of N. O is immediately to the right of K. Who sits between N and O? — (a) L (b) M (c) P (d) K

*Solution:* Seats 0–5 clockwise; K = 0, N = 3. Facing the centre, left = clockwise: L = 1. Right = anticlockwise: M = 2 (anticlockwise of N), O = 5 (anticlockwise of K). P takes seat 4, which is between N (3) and O (5). **Answer: (c) P**

**54.** In a circle of 8 people facing the centre, X is third to the right of Y. Where is Y with respect to X? — (a) third to the right (b) third to the left (c) fifth to the left (d) opposite

*Solution:* If X is 3 seats to Y's right (anticlockwise), then Y is 3 seats clockwise of X, i.e. third to X's left. **Answer: (b) third to the left**

**55.** In a queue of 25, Raj is 10th from the front. Shyam is 5 places behind Raj. What is Shyam's position from the back? — (a) 10 (b) 11 (c) 12 (d) 15

*Solution:* Shyam is 15th from the front. From the back: 25 − 15 + 1 = 11. **Answer: (b) 11**

**56.** In a class of 40, 8 students failed. Sonu passed and is 12th from the bottom among the students who passed. What is his rank from the top in the whole class? — (a) 20 (b) 21 (c) 22 (d) 29

*Solution:* Passed = 32. Sonu from the top among passed = 32 − 12 + 1 = 21. All failed students rank below every passed student, so his rank in the whole class from the top is also 21. **Answer: (b) 21**

## Set 5 — Syllogism and statements (Problems 57–66)

Options for 57–62: (a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Either I or II follows (d) Both I and II follow.

**57.** All roses are flowers. All flowers are plants. I. All roses are plants. II. Some plants are roses.

*Solution:* All + All → All roses are plants ✓. Its reverse, "Some plants are roses" ✓. **Answer: (d) Both**

**58.** Some cups are plates. No plate is a spoon. I. Some cups are not spoons. II. Some spoons are cups.

*Solution:* Some + No → Some cups are not spoons ✓. Spoons may have nothing to do with cups ✗. **Answer: (a) Only I**

**59.** All boxes are tins. Some tins are jars. I. Some boxes are jars. II. No box is a jar.

*Solution:* All + Some → no sure conclusion. I and II form a complementary pair on boxes–jars. **Answer: (c) Either I or II**

**60.** No tree is a fruit. All fruits are leaves. I. Some leaves are not trees. II. Some trees are leaves.

*Solution:* No + All → Some leaves (the fruits) are not trees ✓. Whether any tree is a leaf is unknown ✗. **Answer: (a) Only I**

**61.** Some doctors are nurses. Some nurses are teachers. I. Some doctors are teachers. II. Some teachers are nurses.

*Solution:* Some + Some → nothing between doctors and teachers ✗. II is the reverse of "Some nurses are teachers" ✓. **Answer: (b) Only II**

**62.** All pens are blue. No blue thing is red. I. No pen is red. II. All red things being pens is a possibility.

*Solution:* All + No → No pen is red ✓. II would make red things pens, which are blue — impossible ✗. **Answer: (a) Only I**

**63.** Statement: The school bus was late today because of a puncture on the way. Conclusions: I. A puncture can delay a bus. II. The school bus is always late. — (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

*Solution:* I is directly supported. II uses "always", which the statement does not support. **Answer: (a) Only I**

**64.** Statement: An agriculture department advisory says, "Use only certified seeds for a better harvest." Assumptions: I. Certified seeds can give a better harvest. II. Farmers can get certified seeds. — (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

*Solution:* The advice makes sense only if certified seeds help (I) and if farmers can obtain them (II). **Answer: (c) Both**

**65.** Statement: Cases of dengue are rising in the city. Courses of action: I. Spray against mosquitoes and remove standing water. II. Close all schools for one year. — (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both (d) Neither

*Solution:* I is practical and addresses the cause. II is extreme and impractical. **Answer: (a) Only I**

**66.** (A) Many people bought umbrellas this week. (B) The monsoon arrived early this year. — (a) A is the cause, B the effect (b) B is the cause, A the effect (c) Both are independent causes (d) Both are effects of a common cause

*Solution:* An early monsoon (rain) leads people to buy umbrellas. **Answer: (b)**

## Set 6 — Analogy, classification and Venn diagrams (Problems 67–78)

**67.** Bird : Nest :: Bee : ? — (a) Hive (b) Den (c) Burrow (d) Stable

*Solution:* A bird lives in a nest; a bee lives in a hive. **Answer: (a) Hive**

**68.** 4 : 17 :: 6 : ? — (a) 35 (b) 36 (c) 37 (d) 38

*Solution:* 17 = 4² + 1 → 6² + 1 = 37. **Answer: (c) 37**

**69.** 2 : 9 :: 3 : ? — (a) 27 (b) 28 (c) 26 (d) 10

*Solution:* 9 = 2³ + 1 → 3³ + 1 = 28. **Answer: (b) 28**

**70.** CAT : DBU :: DOG : ? — (a) EPH (b) EPI (c) CNF (d) EOH

*Solution:* Each letter +1 → D→E, O→P, G→H. **Answer: (a) EPH**

**71.** Book : Author :: Painting : ? — (a) Canvas (b) Painter (c) Colour (d) Gallery

*Solution:* A book is created by an author; a painting by a painter. **Answer: (b) Painter**

**72.** Odd one out: 13, 17, 19, 21, 23 — (a) 13 (b) 19 (c) 21 (d) 23

*Solution:* All are primes except 21 = 3 × 7. **Answer: (c) 21**

**73.** Odd one out: Patna, Gaya, Bhagalpur, Lucknow — (a) Patna (b) Gaya (c) Bhagalpur (d) Lucknow

*Solution:* Patna, Gaya and Bhagalpur are in Bihar; Lucknow is in Uttar Pradesh. **Answer: (d) Lucknow**

**74.** Odd one out: 8, 27, 64, 100, 125 — (a) 27 (b) 64 (c) 100 (d) 125

*Solution:* 8, 27, 64, 125 are cubes (2³ to 5³); 100 is not. **Answer: (c) 100**

**75.** Odd one out: BD, FH, JL, NQ — (a) BD (b) FH (c) JL (d) NQ

*Solution:* BD, FH, JL each skip one letter (+2). N → Q is +3. **Answer: (d) NQ**

**76.** Which Venn picture best shows Women, Mothers and Teachers? — (a) three separate circles (b) Mothers inside Women, and Teachers overlapping both (c) Women inside Mothers inside Teachers (d) three circles, each overlapping the others, none inside another

*Solution:* Every mother is a woman → Mothers inside Women. Some teachers are women, some are mothers, and some teachers are men → Teachers overlaps both but is not inside Women. **Answer: (b)**

**77.** Of 80 people, 45 read a Hindi paper, 40 read an English paper and 15 read both. How many read neither? — (a) 5 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 20

*Solution:* At least one = 45 + 40 − 15 = 70. Neither = 80 − 70 = 10. **Answer: (b) 10**

**78.** Which Venn picture best shows Pigeon, Bird and Dog? — (a) Pigeon inside Bird; Dog separate (b) all three overlapping (c) all three separate (d) Bird inside Pigeon; Dog overlapping

*Solution:* Every pigeon is a bird; no dog is a bird. **Answer: (a)**

## Set 7 — Puzzles, dice, cubes and mirror images (Problems 79–88)

**79.** A standard die shows 3 on top. What number is at the bottom? — (a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 2

*Solution:* Opposite faces of a standard die add up to 7 → 7 − 3 = 4. **Answer: (a) 4**

**80.** Two views of a die show (1, 2, 3) and (1, 4, 5). Which number is opposite 1? — (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 6

*Solution:* 1 is seen next to 2, 3, 4 and 5 — its four adjacent faces. The remaining face, 6, is opposite. **Answer: (d) 6**

**81.** A cube painted on all faces is cut into 216 equal small cubes. How many small cubes have exactly one painted face? — (a) 64 (b) 96 (c) 48 (d) 144

*Solution:* 216 = 6³ → n = 6. One face painted = 6(n − 2)² = 6 × 16 = 96. **Answer: (b) 96**

**82.** In Problem 81, how many small cubes have no painted face? — (a) 27 (b) 64 (c) 96 (d) 125

*Solution:* (n − 2)³ = 4³ = 64. **Answer: (b) 64**

**83.** A clock shows 10:25. What time does its mirror image show? — (a) 1:35 (b) 2:35 (c) 1:25 (d) 2:25

*Solution:* 11:60 − 10:25 = 1:35. Check: 10:25 + 1:35 = 12:00 ✓. **Answer: (a) 1:35**

**84.** The mirror image of a clock shows 5:50. What is the actual time? — (a) 6:50 (b) 6:10 (c) 7:10 (d) 5:10

*Solution:* 11:60 − 5:50 = 6:10. **Answer: (b) 6:10**

**85.** A triangle has 4 lines drawn from its top corner to its base. How many triangles are there in the figure? — (a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 20

*Solution:* k = 4 → (k + 1)(k + 2) ÷ 2 = 5 × 6 ÷ 2 = 15. (Single parts 5, pairs 4, triples 3, fours 2, all 1: 15.) **Answer: (c) 15**

**86.** How many squares are there in a 5 × 5 grid? — (a) 25 (b) 50 (c) 55 (d) 60

*Solution:* 25 + 16 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 55. **Answer: (c) 55**

**87.** A, B, C and D each have a different pet: cat, dog, parrot or fish. A has neither the cat nor the dog. B has the parrot. C does not have the cat. Who has the dog? — (a) A (b) B (c) C (d) D

*Solution:* B = parrot. The cat is not A's or C's (or B's) → D = cat. A has neither cat, dog nor parrot → A = fish. C gets the dog. **Answer: (c) C**

**88.** Five lectures P, Q, R, S and T are held on Monday to Friday, one a day. R is on Tuesday. P is on Friday. T is on the day after Q. S is not on Wednesday. On which day is Q? — (a) Monday (b) Wednesday (c) Thursday (d) Tuesday

*Solution:* Free days: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday. Q and T need two days in a row: only Wednesday–Thursday is possible (Tuesday and Friday are taken). So Q = Wednesday, T = Thursday, and S = Monday (S is not on Wednesday ✓). **Answer: (b) Wednesday**

## Set 8 — Arithmetic (Problems 89–134)

### Number system and simplification

**89.** The HCF of 84 and 126 is — (a) 21 (b) 42 (c) 14 (d) 63

*Solution:* 84 = 2² × 3 × 7; 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42. **Answer: (b) 42**

**90.** The LCM of 6, 8 and 10 is — (a) 60 (b) 80 (c) 120 (d) 240

*Solution:* 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³, 10 = 2 × 5. LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120. **Answer: (c) 120**

**91.** The smallest number which leaves remainder 4 when divided by 6, 9 or 15 is — (a) 94 (b) 49 (c) 184 (d) 64

*Solution:* LCM of 6, 9, 15 = 90. Number = 90 + 4 = 94. **Answer: (a) 94**

**92.** The unit digit of 2³⁵ is — (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 8

*Solution:* Unit digits of powers of 2 repeat 2, 4, 8, 6. 35 ÷ 4 leaves 3 → third in the cycle = 8. **Answer: (d) 8**

**93.** Which number is divisible by 9? — (a) 4,381 (b) 4,383 (c) 4,385 (d) 4,387

*Solution:* A number is divisible by 9 if its digit sum is. Digit sums: 16, 18, 20, 22. Only 18 is a multiple of 9. **Answer: (b) 4,383**

**94.** 48 ÷ 6 × 2 + 5 − 3 = ? — (a) 6 (b) 18 (c) 9 (d) 20

*Solution:* Left to right for ÷ and ×: 48 ÷ 6 = 8; 8 × 2 = 16. Then 16 + 5 − 3 = 18. **Answer: (b) 18**

**95.** 0.25 × 0.4 = ? — (a) 1 (b) 0.01 (c) 0.1 (d) 0.001

*Solution:* 25 × 4 = 100; there are 2 + 1 = 3 decimal places → 0.100 = 0.1. **Answer: (c) 0.1**

**96.** √1764 = ? — (a) 41 (b) 42 (c) 43 (d) 44

*Solution:* 1764 ends in 4 → root ends in 2 or 8. 40² = 1600 and 45² = 2025, so the root is 42. Check 42² = 1764 ✓. **Answer: (b) 42**

### Percentages and ratio

**97.** 30% of 450 = ? — (a) 125 (b) 135 (c) 145 (d) 150

*Solution:* 10% of 450 = 45; × 3 = 135. **Answer: (b) 135**

**98.** What percentage of 80 is 20? — (a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 40%

*Solution:* 20/80 × 100 = 25%. **Answer: (b) 25%**

**99.** A number is increased by 10% and then again by 10%. The total increase is — (a) 20% (b) 21% (c) 22% (d) 19%

*Solution:* 10 + 10 + (10 × 10)/100 = 21%. **Answer: (b) 21%**

**100.** The price of sugar rises by 25%. By how much must a family cut its consumption to keep spending the same? — (a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 30%

*Solution:* Cut = r/(100 + r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = 20%. **Answer: (b) 20%**

**101.** In an election between two candidates, the winner gets 60% of the votes and wins by 400 votes. Total votes cast are — (a) 1,600 (b) 2,000 (c) 2,400 (d) 1,000

*Solution:* Winner 60%, loser 40% → margin 20% = 400 → 100% = 2,000. **Answer: (b) 2,000**

**102.** Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 3. If 8 is added to each, the ratio becomes 7 : 5. The numbers are — (a) 20 and 12 (b) 25 and 15 (c) 15 and 9 (d) 10 and 6

*Solution:* (5x + 8)/(3x + 8) = 7/5 → 25x + 40 = 21x + 56 → 4x = 16 → x = 4. Numbers 20 and 12. Check (28/20 = 7/5 ✓). **Answer: (a)**

**103.** ₹2,100 is divided among A, B and C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 2. B gets — (a) ₹600 (b) ₹700 (c) ₹900 (d) ₹1,050

*Solution:* Total parts 7; one part = 300. B = 3 × 300 = ₹900. **Answer: (c) ₹900**

### Averages

**104.** The average of 7, 11, 15, 19 and 23 is — (a) 13 (b) 15 (c) 17 (d) 14

*Solution:* These are equally spaced, so the average is the middle term, 15. (Sum 75 ÷ 5 = 15.) **Answer: (b) 15**

**105.** The average of 10 numbers is 40. Later it is found that 50 was misread as 80. The correct average is — (a) 37 (b) 43 (c) 38 (d) 35

*Solution:* Wrong sum = 400. Correct sum = 400 − 80 + 50 = 370. Average = 37. **Answer: (a) 37**

**106.** The average weight of 8 people rises by 2.5 kg when a new person replaces one who weighs 65 kg. The new person weighs — (a) 75 kg (b) 80 kg (c) 85 kg (d) 90 kg

*Solution:* Total rises by 8 × 2.5 = 20 kg. New person = 65 + 20 = 85 kg. **Answer: (c) 85 kg**

### Profit, loss and discount

**107.** An article costing ₹600 is sold at a 15% loss. The selling price is — (a) ₹490 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹510 (d) ₹520

*Solution:* SP = 600 × 0.85 = ₹510. **Answer: (c) ₹510**

**108.** By selling an article for ₹1,150, a shopkeeper gains 15%. The cost price is — (a) ₹977.50 (b) ₹1,000 (c) ₹1,050 (d) ₹1,100

*Solution:* CP = 1,150 ÷ 1.15 = ₹1,000. **Answer: (b) ₹1,000**

**109.** Two successive discounts of 10% and 20% equal a single discount of — (a) 30% (b) 28% (c) 25% (d) 32%

*Solution:* Pay 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72 of the price → discount 28%. **Answer: (b) 28%**

**110.** A shopkeeper marks goods 20% above cost and gives a 10% discount. His profit is — (a) 10% (b) 8% (c) 12% (d) 6%

*Solution:* Take CP = 100. Marked 120; after 10% discount, SP = 108. Profit 8%. **Answer: (b) 8%**

### Simple and compound interest

**111.** Simple interest on ₹8,000 at 6% per year for 2½ years is — (a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹1,200 (c) ₹1,400 (d) ₹960

*Solution:* 8,000 × 6 × 2.5 ÷ 100 = ₹1,200. **Answer: (b) ₹1,200**

**112.** At what rate of simple interest does a sum double in 8 years? — (a) 10% (b) 12% (c) 12.5% (d) 8%

*Solution:* Doubling means interest = principal → 100 = 100 × R × 8 ÷ 100 → R = 12.5%. **Answer: (c) 12.5%**

**113.** Compound interest on ₹8,000 at 5% per year for 2 years is — (a) ₹800 (b) ₹820 (c) ₹840 (d) ₹810

*Solution:* A = 8,000 × 1.05² = 8,000 × 1.1025 = 8,820. CI = ₹820. **Answer: (b) ₹820**

**114.** A sum becomes ₹4,840 in 2 years at 10% per year compound interest. The sum is — (a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹4,200 (c) ₹4,400 (d) ₹3,800

*Solution:* P = 4,840 ÷ 1.21 = ₹4,000. **Answer: (a) ₹4,000**

### Time and work

**115.** A can do a job in 20 days and B in 30 days. Together they take — (a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 25 days

*Solution:* 20 × 30 ÷ (20 + 30) = 600 ÷ 50 = 12 days. **Answer: (b) 12 days**

**116.** 10 men can finish a job in 12 days. How long will 15 men take? — (a) 6 days (b) 8 days (c) 9 days (d) 18 days

*Solution:* Total work = 10 × 12 = 120 man-days. 120 ÷ 15 = 8 days. **Answer: (b) 8 days**

**117.** A and B can do a job in 8 days, B and C in 12 days, and C and A in 24 days. In how many days can A, B and C together do it? — (a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 10 (d) 12

*Solution:* Add the three daily rates: 1/8 + 1/12 + 1/24 = 3/24 + 2/24 + 1/24 = 6/24 = 1/4. This counts each person twice, so A + B + C = 1/8 per day → 8 days. **Answer: (b) 8**

**118.** Pipe A fills a tank in 20 minutes and pipe B in 30 minutes. Both together fill it in — (a) 10 min (b) 12 min (c) 15 min (d) 25 min

*Solution:* 20 × 30 ÷ 50 = 12 minutes. **Answer: (b) 12 min**

### Speed, distance and time

**119.** 90 km/h is equal to — (a) 20 m/s (b) 25 m/s (c) 30 m/s (d) 15 m/s

*Solution:* 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s. **Answer: (b) 25 m/s**

**120.** A train 200 m long runs at 72 km/h. How long does it take to cross a bridge 300 m long? — (a) 20 s (b) 25 s (c) 30 s (d) 15 s

*Solution:* 72 km/h = 20 m/s. Distance = 200 + 300 = 500 m. Time = 500 ÷ 20 = 25 s. **Answer: (b) 25 s**

**121.** Two trains 120 m and 180 m long run in opposite directions at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. How long do they take to cross each other? — (a) 10 s (b) 12 s (c) 15 s (d) 18 s

*Solution:* Opposite directions → add speeds: 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Distance = 120 + 180 = 300 m. Time = 300 ÷ 25 = 12 s. **Answer: (b) 12 s**

**122.** A man travels 30 km at 15 km/h and another 30 km at 10 km/h. His average speed is — (a) 12.5 km/h (b) 12 km/h (c) 13 km/h (d) 11 km/h

*Solution:* Equal distances → 2 × 15 × 10 ÷ 25 = 12 km/h. (Check: time 2 h + 3 h = 5 h for 60 km → 12 km/h ✓.) **Answer: (b) 12 km/h**

**123.** A boat goes downstream at 15 km/h and upstream at 9 km/h. The speed of the boat in still water and of the stream are — (a) 12 and 3 km/h (b) 13 and 2 km/h (c) 12 and 6 km/h (d) 24 and 6 km/h

*Solution:* Still water = (15 + 9) ÷ 2 = 12; stream = (15 − 9) ÷ 2 = 3. **Answer: (a)**

### Algebra, ages, clocks and calendars

**124.** The sum of the ages of A and B is 50 years. Five years ago their ages were in the ratio 3 : 2. A's present age is — (a) 27 (b) 29 (c) 30 (d) 31

*Solution:* Five years ago the sum was 50 − 10 = 40. Split 3 : 2 → 24 and 16. A now = 24 + 5 = 29. **Answer: (b) 29**

**125.** The angle between the hands of a clock at 7:20 is — (a) 90° (b) 100° (c) 110° (d) 80°

*Solution:* |30 × 7 − 5.5 × 20| = |210 − 110| = 100°. **Answer: (b) 100°**

**126.** The angle between the hands at 9:45 is — (a) 22.5° (b) 30° (c) 15° (d) 7.5°

*Solution:* |30 × 9 − 5.5 × 45| = |270 − 247.5| = 22.5°. **Answer: (a) 22.5°**

**127.** If 1 January 2027 is a Friday, what day is 1 March 2027? — (a) Sunday (b) Monday (c) Tuesday (d) Saturday

*Solution:* Days from 1 January to 1 March = 31 (Jan) + 28 (Feb; 2027 is not a leap year) = 59. 59 ÷ 7 leaves 3. Friday + 3 = Monday. **Answer: (b) Monday**

**128.** 1 January 2024 was a Monday. What day was 1 January 2025? — (a) Tuesday (b) Wednesday (c) Thursday (d) Monday

*Solution:* 2024 is a leap year (366 days = 52 weeks + 2 days) → 2 odd days. Monday + 2 = Wednesday. **Answer: (b) Wednesday**

### Probability, counting, data and mensuration

**129.** A bag has 5 red and 3 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. The probability that it is blue is — (a) 3/5 (b) 3/8 (c) 5/8 (d) 1/3

*Solution:* Favourable 3, total 8 → 3/8. **Answer: (b) 3/8**

**130.** In how many ways can the letters of GAYA be arranged? — (a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 6 (d) 4

*Solution:* 4 letters with A repeated twice → 4!/2! = 24/2 = 12. **Answer: (b) 12**

**131.** A school had 400 students in 2022, 500 in 2023 and 450 in 2024. The percentage increase from 2022 to 2023 was — (a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 10% (d) 12.5%

*Solution:* (500 − 400)/400 × 100 = 25%. **Answer: (b) 25%**

**132.** The area of a square whose diagonal is 10 cm is — (a) 100 cm² (b) 50 cm² (c) 25 cm² (d) 40 cm²

*Solution:* Area of a square = d²/2 = 100/2 = 50 cm². **Answer: (b) 50 cm²**

**133.** The perimeter of a rectangle is 40 cm and its length is 12 cm. Its area is — (a) 96 cm² (b) 84 cm² (c) 100 cm² (d) 108 cm²

*Solution:* 2(l + b) = 40 → l + b = 20 → b = 8. Area = 12 × 8 = 96 cm². **Answer: (a) 96 cm²**

**134.** In a group, 25 people like tea, 20 like coffee, 10 like both and 5 like neither. How many people are in the group? — (a) 40 (b) 45 (c) 50 (d) 35

*Solution:* At least one = 25 + 20 − 10 = 35. Total = 35 + 5 = 40. **Answer: (a) 40**
