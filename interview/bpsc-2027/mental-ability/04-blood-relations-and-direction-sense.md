This chapter joins two topics that BPSC asks often and that have the same secret: **draw it**. Blood-relation questions describe a family in words; direction questions describe a walk in words. In both, a 10-second sketch turns a confusing sentence into an obvious answer.

> **In simple words:** For relations, draw a family tree — one generation per row, a line for marriage, and + for male, − for female. For directions, draw the walk on paper with north at the top. Then just read the answer off the drawing.

## Part A — Blood relations

### The family-tree drawing

Use these symbols every time:

<div class="formula">

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A (+) | A is male |
| B (−) | B is female |
| A = B | A and B are married (husband and wife) |
| Vertical line down | parent → child (one generation lower) |
| Horizontal line, same row | brothers and sisters (siblings) |
| ? | gender not known — keep it open |

</div>

Keep each **generation** on its own row: grandparents on top, parents in the middle, children at the bottom. A relation question then becomes "how many rows up or down, and which side".

### Relation words you must know

| Relation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Paternal | from the father's side (paternal uncle = father's brother, *chacha*) |
| Maternal | from the mother's side (maternal uncle = mother's brother, *mama*) |
| Uncle | father's or mother's brother; also husband of an aunt |
| Aunt | father's or mother's sister; also wife of an uncle |
| Nephew / niece | son / daughter of your brother or sister |
| Cousin | child of your uncle or aunt |
| Sibling | brother or sister |
| Spouse | husband or wife |
| Father-in-law | father of your spouse |
| Brother-in-law | brother of your spouse, or husband of your sister |
| Daughter-in-law | wife of your son |
| Grandson / granddaughter | son / daughter of your son or daughter |

### The shortcut for "pointing to a man" questions

These questions have a chain: "He is the son of my paternal grandfather's only son." **Start from the far end of the chain** (the word next to "my") and walk back:

1. "my paternal grandfather" → the speaker's father's father.
2. "paternal grandfather's only son" → the speaker's father (he is that grandfather's only son).
3. "son of my father" → the speaker's brother (or the speaker himself).

> **Common mistake:** Do not assume a person's gender from a name you think is male or female unless the question gives a clue ("he", "she", "son", "mother", or a common name the question clearly treats as male/female). If gender is not given, the answer may be "son or daughter" — and the correct option will often say "cannot be determined".

## Solved examples — blood relations

**Example 1.** Pointing to a man, Rina says, "He is the son of my paternal grandfather's only son." How is the man related to Rina?

- Rina's paternal grandfather is her father's father. His only son → Rina's **father**.
- Son of Rina's father → Rina's **brother**.
- Answer: **brother**. (If the question said only "grandfather", he could be the maternal grandfather, and then the man would be Rina's cousin. Read such words carefully.)

**Example 2.** A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. E is D's mother. How is A related to D?

- C is the mother of B; A is B's sister, so C is also A's mother.
- D is C's father → D is A's maternal grandfather.
- A is female → A is D's **granddaughter**.

**Example 3.** Introducing a woman, Ramesh says, "Her mother is the only daughter of my mother." How is the woman related to Ramesh?

- "The only daughter of my mother" → Ramesh's **sister**.
- The woman's mother is Ramesh's sister → the woman is Ramesh's sister's daughter.
- Answer: **niece**.

**Example 4.** Pointing to a photograph, a man says, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son." Whose photograph is it?

- "My father's son": the speaker has no brother, so his father's only son is **the speaker himself**.
- "That man's father is me" → the man in the photo is the speaker's **son**.

**Example 5.** Rahul's father is the only son of Mohan's father. How is Mohan related to Rahul? (Mohan is male.)

- Mohan's father's only son = Mohan himself (Mohan is male and the father has only one son).
- So Rahul's father = Mohan. Mohan is Rahul's **father**.

**Example 6.** Pointing to a lady, Anil said, "She is the daughter of the only child of my grandmother." How is the lady related to Anil?

- The only child of Anil's grandmother → Anil's **father or mother** (Anil's parent on that side).
- The daughter of Anil's parent → Anil's **sister**.

**Example 7.** A is the father of B. C is the daughter of B. D is the brother of B. E is the son of A. How is E related to C?

- B is a child of A. E is also a son of A → E is a brother of B.
- C is B's daughter → B's brother is C's **uncle**.
- Answer: E is C's **uncle**.

**Example 8.** A is a sibling of B. B is the son of C. How is A related to C?

- B is C's son, and A is B's sibling → A is C's child.
- A's gender is not given → A is C's **son or daughter**. If an option says "cannot be determined" for "son" or "daughter" alone, that is the safe answer.

### Coded relations

Here symbols stand for relations. Decode one symbol at a time, from left to right.

**Example 9.** A + B means A is the mother of B; A − B means A is the brother of B; A × B means A is the father of B; A ÷ B means A is the sister of B. What does P + Q − R mean?

- P + Q → P is the mother of Q.
- Q − R → Q is the brother of R. So R is also P's child.
- Answer: **P is the mother of R**.

**Example 10.** Using the same code, what does P × Q ÷ R − S mean?

- P × Q → P is the father of Q.
- Q ÷ R → Q is the sister of R (so R is also P's child).
- R − S → R is the brother of S (so S is also P's child).
- Answer: **P is the father of S** (S's gender is unknown, so "son" or "daughter" cannot be said).

### A family puzzle

**Example 11.** There are six members in a family: A, B, C, D, E and F.
- B is the son of C, but C is not the mother of B.
- A and C are a married couple.
- E is the brother of C.
- D is the daughter of A.
- F is the brother of B.

Answer: (a) How many male members are there? (b) Who is the mother of B? (c) How is E related to D? (d) How many children do A and C have?

> **Remember:** English relation words in the options map to the Hindi words you use at home. Translate in your head: father's brother = chacha = paternal uncle; mother's brother = mama = maternal uncle; father's sister's husband = phupha; mother's sister's husband = mausa.

<div class="formula">

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Right turn | 90° clockwise: N → E → S → W → N |
| Left turn | 90° anticlockwise: N → W → S → E → N |
| About turn | 180°: N ↔ S, E ↔ W |
| Clockwise angles | from N: 45° = NE, 90° = E, 135° = SE, 180° = S, 225° = SW, 270° = W, 315° = NW |
| Shortest distance | Pythagoras: √(x² + y²), where x = net east–west, y = net north–south |
| Sun | rises in the east, sets in the west; shadow falls opposite the sun |
| Morning shadow | falls towards the **west** |
| Evening shadow | falls towards the **east** |
| Noon | the sun is overhead; there is almost no shadow (so noon questions are rare) |

</div>

### The shortcut: net displacement

Instead of drawing every step, keep two running totals:

- **x** = total east minus total west;
- **y** = total north minus total south.

Then the distance from start is √(x² + y²), and the direction is read from the signs: x > 0 and y < 0 → south-east, and so on.

> **Memory trick:** Common Pythagorean triples: **3-4-5, 6-8-10, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25**. Most direction answers use one of these.

- North 5 → y = +5. Turn right (facing north, right is east) → east 3 → x = +3.
- Turn right again (facing east, right is south) → south 9 → y = 5 − 9 = −4.
- Distance = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = **5 km**. x positive (east), y negative (south) → **south-east**.

**Example 13.** A man is facing north. He turns 90° clockwise, then 180° anticlockwise, then 45° clockwise. Which direction is he facing now?

- North + 90° clockwise → east.
- East + 180° anticlockwise → west.
- West + 45° clockwise → north-west.
- Answer: **north-west**.

**Example 14.** One morning after sunrise, Gita and Sita were talking face to face. Gita's shadow was exactly to her right. Which direction was Gita facing?

- Morning → sun in the east → shadows fall to the **west**.
- Gita's right side is west.
- If your right is west, you are facing **south**. (Facing south: right hand = west, left hand = east.)
- Answer: Gita faces **south** (and Sita faces north).

**Example 15.** In the evening, A and B stand back to back. A's shadow falls in front of him. Which direction does B face?

- Evening → sun in the west → shadows fall to the **east**.
- A's shadow is in front of A → A faces **east**.
- B stands back to back with A → B faces **west**.

**Example 16.** A walks 10 m east, turns left and walks 10 m, turns left and walks 20 m, then turns left and walks 10 m. Where is he from the start?

- East 10 → (10, 0). Left from east = north → (10, 10).
- Left from north = west, 20 m → (−10, 10). Left from west = south, 10 m → (−10, 0).
- Answer: **10 m west** of the start.

**Example 17.** Sohan walks 6 km south and then 8 km west. How far is he from the start, and in which direction?

- x = −8 (west), y = −6 (south).
- Distance = √(36 + 64) = √100 = **10 km**, towards the **south-west**.

**Example 18.** From his home, Raju goes 4 km east, 3 km north, 2 km west, 3 km south and 6 km east. Where is he now?

- x = 4 − 2 + 6 = +8. y = 3 − 3 = 0.
- Answer: **8 km east** of home.

**Example 19.** Ram walks 3 km north, then 4 km west. In which direction is his **starting point** from him now?

- Ram is 3 km north and 4 km west of the start → Ram is north-west of the start.
- The question asks the reverse: where is the start **from Ram**? The opposite of north-west → **south-east**. (Distance 5 km.)

> **Common mistake:** Read whether the question asks "where is he from the start" or "where is the start from him". These are opposite directions.

**Example 20.** Neha starts from a point, walks 20 m south, turns left and walks 15 m, then turns left and walks 20 m. How far and in which direction is she from the start?

- Facing south, left is **east** → 15 m east. Facing east, left is **north** → 20 m north.
- y = −20 + 20 = 0; x = +15.
- Answer: **15 m east**.

**Example 21.** If south-east is called north, north-east is called west, and so on, what is west called?

- South-east (135° from north) is renamed north (0°): every direction is rotated by 135° anticlockwise.
- Check: north-east (45°) − 135° = −90° = 270° = west ✓.
- West (270°) − 135° = 135° = **south-east**.

**Example 22.** A clock is placed so that at 3:00 the hour hand points north. In which direction does the minute hand point at 3:00?

- At 3:00 the minute hand is at 12 and the hour hand at 3: the minute hand is 90° anticlockwise of the hour hand.
- 90° anticlockwise from north = **west**.

> **Prelims angle:** BPSC direction questions are almost always "net distance and direction" (Pythagoras triples) or "which way is he facing". Shadow questions appear sometimes. Always draw: north up, east right.

## Common traps

1. **Left/right confusion when facing south or west.** Facing south, your right is west. Facing west, your right is north. Turn the paper if it helps.
2. **"From whom" reversal** (Example 19).
3. **Gender assumptions** in blood relations (Example 8).
4. **"Only son" logic:** "my father's only son" is the speaker himself if the speaker is male.
5. **Mixing generations:** always keep one generation per row.
6. **Shadows:** morning → west, evening → east. Not the other way.
7. **Coded relations:** decode left to right, one pair at a time, and carry the conclusion along.

## Quick revision

- Family tree: + male, − female, = married, one generation per row.
- Start "pointing" chains from the word next to "my".
- Gender not given → "cannot be determined".
- Right turn = clockwise 90°; left turn = anticlockwise 90°.
- Net x (east − west), net y (north − south); distance √(x² + y²).
- Triples: 3-4-5, 6-8-10, 5-12-13, 8-15-17.
- Morning shadow west; evening shadow east.
- Facing south: right = west; facing west: right = north; facing east: right = south.
- Renamed directions: find the rotation angle from one given pair and apply it.

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Blood relation | A family relation by birth or marriage |
| Generation | One level of a family (grandparents, parents, children) |
| Paternal / maternal | From the father's / mother's side |
| Sibling | Brother or sister |
| Spouse | Husband or wife |
| Clockwise | The direction a clock's hands move (N → E → S → W) |
| Anticlockwise | Opposite to clockwise (N → W → S → E) |
| Displacement | The straight-line distance and direction from start to end |
| Pythagorean triple | Three whole numbers that fit a² + b² = c², like 3, 4, 5 |

## Practice

### Problems

1. Pointing to a boy, Meena says, "He is the son of my father's only daughter." How is the boy related to Meena?
2. A is the brother of B. B is the daughter of C. D is the father of C. How is A related to D?
3. If A × B means A is the father of B, A + B means A is the wife of B, and A − B means A is the sister of B, what does P + Q × R mean?
4. X's mother is the sister of Y's father. How is X related to Y?
5. Introducing a man, a woman says, "His wife is the only daughter of my father." How is the man related to the woman?
6. A man walks 12 km south, turns left and walks 5 km. How far is he from the start?
7. Facing west, Ankit turns 135° clockwise. Which direction does he face now?
8. One evening, Suresh was walking towards his shadow. In which direction was he walking?
9. A person walks 8 m north, turns right and walks 6 m, turns right and walks 8 m. How far and in which direction is he from the start?
10. If north is called east, east is called south, and so on, what is north-west called?

### Answers

1. Meena's father's only daughter = **Meena herself**. The boy is her **son**.
2. B is C's daughter, A is B's brother → A is C's son. D is C's father → A is D's **grandson**.
3. P + Q → P is the wife of Q. Q × R → Q is the father of R. So P is the **mother** of R.
4. X's mother is Y's father's sister → X is the child of Y's aunt → X is Y's **cousin**.
5. "The only daughter of my father" = the woman herself. So she is the man's wife → the man is her **husband**.
6. South 12, then left (facing south, left is east) 5. Distance = √(144 + 25) = √169 = **13 km** (south-east).
7. West = 270°. 270° + 135° = 405° − 360° = 45° → **north-east**.
8. Evening shadow falls east. Walking towards the shadow → walking **east**.
9. North 8, right → east 6, right → south 8. Net: x = 6, y = 0 → **6 m east**.
10. Each direction is rotated 90° clockwise (N → E, E → S). North-west (315°) + 90° = 405° − 360° = 45° → **north-east**.
