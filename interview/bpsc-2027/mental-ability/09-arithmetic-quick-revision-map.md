The "basic numeracy" part of General Mental Ability is class 10 arithmetic. This shelf does not repeat it: the [Quantitative Aptitude](/interview/quant-aptitude/how-to-prepare) book already teaches every topic with formulas, shortcuts and practice sets. This chapter is your **map**. For each topic it tells you what BPSC asks, how deep to go, and shows 2–3 solved examples at exactly the BPSC level. Read the linked chapter once in full, then use this page for revision.

> **In simple words:** BPSC arithmetic is not hard maths. It is percentages, ratios, averages, profit and loss, interest, work, speed, simple number facts, clocks and calendars, and small tables — each asked in a one- or two-step word problem. Learn the one key formula per topic and practise until you can solve each in under a minute.

## How deep to go

<div class="formula">

| Priority for BPSC | Topics |
|---|---|
| High — learn fully | number system, simplification, percentages, ratio, averages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, time and work, speed and distance, clocks and calendars, data interpretation, sets |
| Medium — basics only | algebra (linear equations, ages, identities), mensuration (area, volume of basic shapes), geometry (angles, triangles), permutation–combination and probability (basic counting) |
| Low — one idea | trigonometry and heights (45° and 30°/60° basics only) |

</div>

Percentages, ratios, averages and data interpretation from this chapter are its base. The Mains side is taught in [Statistics, Graphs and Diagrams](/interview/bpsc-2027/mains-answer-writing/statistics-graphs-and-diagrams).

## 1. Number system

**Read:** [Number System](/interview/quant-aptitude/number-system). **BPSC asks:** HCF and LCM word problems, divisibility, unit digit of a power, simple remainders.

<div class="formula">

| Key fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| HCF × LCM | = product of the two numbers (for two numbers only) |
| Unit-digit cycles | 2: 2,4,8,6 · 3: 3,9,7,1 · 7: 7,9,3,1 · 8: 8,4,2,6 · 4: 4,6 · 9: 9,1 · 0,1,5,6 stay the same |
| "Leaves remainder r in each case" | LCM + r |

</div>

**Example 1.** Find the HCF and LCM of 12, 18 and 30.

- 12 = 2² × 3; 18 = 2 × 3²; 30 = 2 × 3 × 5.
- HCF = lowest powers of common primes = 2 × 3 = **6**.
- LCM = highest powers of all primes = 2² × 3² × 5 = **180**.

**Example 2.** What is the unit digit of 7⁴⁵?

- The unit digits of powers of 7 repeat every 4: 7, 9, 3, 1.
- 45 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 1 → same as 7¹ → unit digit **7**.

**Example 3.** Find the smallest number which, when divided by 12, 15 and 20, leaves remainder 5 in each case.

- LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60.
- Number = 60 + 5 = **65**.

## 2. Simplification and approximation

**Read:** [Simplification and Approximation](/interview/quant-aptitude/simplification-and-approximation). **BPSC asks:** BODMAS expressions, fractions of a quantity, approximate values.

**Example 4.** Simplify 18 + 6 ÷ 3 × 2 − 4.

- BODMAS: division and multiplication first, left to right: 6 ÷ 3 = 2; 2 × 2 = 4.
- 18 + 4 − 4 = **18**.

**Example 5.** Find 2/3 of 3/4 of 480.

- 3/4 of 480 = 360. 2/3 of 360 = **240**. (Or 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2 → half of 480.)

**Example 6.** Approximately, 49.8 × 20.2 = ? (a) 900 (b) 1,000 (c) 1,100 (d) 1,200

- Round: 50 × 20 = 1,000. (Exact value 1,005.96.)
- Answer: **(b) 1,000**.

## 3. Percentages

**Read:** [Percentages](/interview/quant-aptitude/percentages). **BPSC asks:** increase/decrease, successive change, "more than / less than", population growth.

<div class="formula">

| Key formula | Detail |
|---|---|
| Successive changes a% and b% | net = a + b + ab/100 (use − for a decrease) |
| A is r% more than B → B is less than A by | r/(100 + r) × 100 % |
| Growth at r% for n years | P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ |

</div>

**Example 7.** A price is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. What is the net change?

- Net = 20 − 20 + (20 × −20)/100 = −4 → a **4% decrease**.

**Example 8.** A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what percentage is B's salary less than A's?

- 25/(100 + 25) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.

**Example 9.** A town's population is 50,000 and grows by 10% a year. What will it be after 2 years?

- 50,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 50,000 × 1.21 = **60,500**.

## 4. Ratio, proportion and alligation

**Read:** [Ratio, Proportion and Alligation](/interview/quant-aptitude/ratio-proportion-and-alligation). **BPSC asks:** dividing an amount in a ratio, combining ratios, mixing two prices.

**Example 10.** Divide ₹1,200 between A and B in the ratio 3 : 5.

- Total parts = 8. One part = 1,200 ÷ 8 = 150.
- A = 3 × 150 = **₹450**; B = 5 × 150 = **₹750**.

**Example 11.** A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5. Find A : B : C.

- Make B the same: multiply the first ratio by 4 and the second by 3 → A : B = 8 : 12, B : C = 12 : 15.
- A : B : C = **8 : 12 : 15**.

**Example 12.** In what ratio must rice at ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹60 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹45 per kg?

- Alligation: (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper) = (60 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 15 : 5 = **3 : 1** (cheaper : dearer).

## 5. Averages

**Read:** [Averages](/interview/quant-aptitude/averages). **BPSC asks:** average of numbers, a number removed or added, the teacher's age type.

**Example 13.** Find the average of the first 10 natural numbers.

- Sum = 10 × 11 ÷ 2 = 55. Average = 55 ÷ 10 = **5.5**.

**Example 14.** The average of 5 numbers is 20. When one number is removed, the average of the rest is 18. Which number was removed?

- Sum of 5 = 100. Sum of 4 = 72. Removed = 100 − 72 = **28**.

**Example 15.** The average age of 30 students is 14 years. When the teacher's age is included, the average becomes 15. Find the teacher's age.

- Total with teacher = 31 × 15 = 465. Total of students = 30 × 14 = 420.
- Teacher = 465 − 420 = **45 years**.

## 6. Profit, loss and discount

**Read:** [Profit, Loss and Discount](/interview/quant-aptitude/profit-loss-and-discount). **BPSC asks:** profit %, selling price after discount, two items sold at the same price.

**Example 16.** An article bought for ₹400 is sold for ₹500. Find the profit percentage.

- Profit = 100. Profit % = 100/400 × 100 = **25%** (always on cost price).

**Example 17.** The marked price of a fan is ₹800. A discount of 15% is given. Find the selling price.

- Discount = 15% of 800 = 120. Selling price = **₹680**.

**Example 18.** Two articles are sold at ₹990 each, one at 10% profit and the other at 10% loss. Find the overall result.

- Cost of the first = 990 ÷ 1.1 = 900. Cost of the second = 990 ÷ 0.9 = 1,100.
- Total cost 2,000; total sale 1,980 → **loss of ₹20, i.e. 1%**.
- Shortcut: same selling price, same x% profit and loss → loss % = x²/100 = 100/100 = 1%.

## 7. Simple and compound interest

**Read:** [Simple and Compound Interest](/interview/quant-aptitude/simple-and-compound-interest). **BPSC asks:** SI, CI for 2–3 years, CI − SI difference.

<div class="formula">

| Formula | Detail |
|---|---|
| Simple interest | SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 |
| Compound amount | A = P(1 + R/100)ᵀ; CI = A − P |
| CI − SI for 2 years | P × (R/100)² |

</div>

**Example 19.** Find the simple interest on ₹5,000 at 8% per year for 3 years.

- SI = 5,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = **₹1,200**.

**Example 20.** Find the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per year for 2 years.

- A = 10,000 × 1.1² = 12,100. CI = **₹2,100**.

**Example 21.** Find the difference between CI and SI on ₹5,000 at 10% for 2 years.

- P × (R/100)² = 5,000 × (0.1)² = 5,000 × 0.01 = **₹50**.

## 8. Time and work

**Read:** [Time and Work](/interview/quant-aptitude/time-and-work). **BPSC asks:** two people working together, one leaving, pipes and cisterns.

**Example 22.** A can finish a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. How long will they take together?

- Work per day: 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6.
- Together: **6 days**. (Shortcut: ab/(a + b) = 150/25 = 6.)

**Example 23.** A and B together finish a job in 12 days. A alone takes 20 days. How long does B alone take?

- B's work per day = 1/12 − 1/20 = 5/60 − 3/60 = 2/60 = 1/30 → **30 days**.

**Example 24.** A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours; another empties it in 9 hours. If both are open, when is the tank full?

- Net per hour = 1/6 − 1/9 = 3/18 − 2/18 = 1/18 → **18 hours**.

## 9. Time, speed and distance

**Read:** [Time, Speed and Distance](/interview/quant-aptitude/time-speed-and-distance). **BPSC asks:** unit conversion, trains crossing poles and platforms, average speed.

<div class="formula">

| Formula | Detail |
|---|---|
| Distance | speed × time |
| km/h → m/s | × 5/18 |
| Train crossing a pole | time = train length ÷ speed |
| Train crossing a platform | time = (train + platform length) ÷ speed |
| Average speed, equal distances at x and y | 2xy/(x + y) |

</div>

**Example 25.** Convert 72 km/h into m/s.

- 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s**.

**Example 26.** A 150 m long train runs at 54 km/h. How long does it take to cross (a) a pole (b) a 300 m platform?

- 54 km/h = 54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s.
- (a) 150 ÷ 15 = **10 s**. (b) (150 + 300) ÷ 15 = **30 s**.

**Example 27.** A man goes from Patna to a village at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. What is his average speed?

- Equal distances → 2 × 40 × 60 ÷ (40 + 60) = 4,800 ÷ 100 = **48 km/h** (not 50).

## 10. Algebra (basics)

**Read:** [Algebra](/interview/quant-aptitude/algebra). **BPSC asks:** linear equations in word problems (ages, numbers), simple identities.

**Example 28.** A father is three times as old as his son. After 10 years he will be twice as old as his son. Find their present ages.

- Let son = S, father = 3S. After 10 years: 3S + 10 = 2(S + 10) → 3S + 10 = 2S + 20 → S = 10.
- Son **10 years**, father **30 years**.

**Example 29.** If x + 1/x = 3, find x² + 1/x².

- Square both sides: x² + 2 + 1/x² = 9 → x² + 1/x² = **7**.

## 11. Geometry and mensuration (basics)

**Read:** [Geometry](/interview/quant-aptitude/geometry) and [Mensuration](/interview/quant-aptitude/mensuration). **BPSC asks:** angles of a triangle, area and perimeter of rectangles and circles, volume of a cube or cuboid.

**Example 30.** The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find them.

- Sum = 180°. Parts = 9 → one part = 20°.
- Angles = **40°, 60°, 80°**.

**Example 31.** Find the area and circumference of a circle of radius 7 cm (π = 22/7).

- Area = πr² = 22/7 × 49 = **154 cm²**. Circumference = 2πr = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = **44 cm**.

**Example 32.** A rectangle is 12 cm long and 5 cm wide. Find its diagonal.

- Diagonal = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = **13 cm**.

**Example 33.** Find the volume and total surface area of a cube of edge 3 cm.

- Volume = 3³ = **27 cm³**. Surface area = 6 × 3² = **54 cm²**.

## 12. Trigonometry and heights (one idea)

**Read:** [Trigonometry and Heights](/interview/quant-aptitude/trigonometry-and-heights). **BPSC asks:** rarely; at most the 45° case.

**Example 34.** A tower casts a shadow equal to its own height. What is the angle of elevation of the sun?

- tan θ = height ÷ shadow = 1 → θ = **45°**.

## 13. Data interpretation

**Read:** [Data Interpretation](/interview/quant-aptitude/data-interpretation). **BPSC asks:** a small table or bar chart with 2–4 questions on percentage change, average, ratio.

**Example 35.** Rice production in a district (lakh tonnes): 2021 — 40; 2022 — 50; 2023 — 45. Find (a) the percentage increase from 2021 to 2022, (b) the percentage change from 2022 to 2023, (c) the average production.

- (a) (50 − 40)/40 × 100 = **25% increase**.
- (b) (45 − 50)/50 × 100 = **10% decrease**.
- (c) (40 + 50 + 45) ÷ 3 = 135 ÷ 3 = **45 lakh tonnes**.

> **Common mistake:** Percentage change is always on the **earlier** (base) value. From 50 to 45 is a 10% fall; from 45 back to 50 is an 11.1% rise.

## 14. Permutation, combination and probability (basics)

**Read:** [Permutation, Combination and Probability](/interview/quant-aptitude/permutation-combination-and-probability). **BPSC asks:** arrangements of letters, handshakes, dice and coin probability.

**Example 36.** In how many ways can the letters of PATNA be arranged?

- 5 letters with A repeated twice → 5!/2! = 120/2 = **60**.

**Example 37.** Five people shake hands with each other once. How many handshakes?

- Choose 2 of 5: 5C2 = 5 × 4 ÷ 2 = **10**.

**Example 38.** Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the sum is 7?

- Favourable: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1) = 6. Total 36.
- Probability = 6/36 = **1/6**.

## 15. Number series, clocks and calendars

**Read:** [Number Series, Clocks and Calendars](/interview/quant-aptitude/number-series-clocks-and-calendars) (series reasoning is in [Number and Letter Series](/interview/bpsc-2027/mental-ability/number-and-letter-series)). **BPSC asks:** angle between hands, day of the week for a date, days after n days.

<div class="formula">

| Formula | Detail |
|---|---|
| Clock angle at H:M | \|30H − 5.5M\|; if above 180°, subtract from 360° |
| Hands overlap between H and H + 1 | at (60/11) × H minutes past H |
| Odd days | ordinary year 1, leap year 2; 100 years 5, 200 years 3, 300 years 1, 400 years 0 |

</div>

**Example 39.** Find the angle between the hands at 3:30.

- |30 × 3 − 5.5 × 30| = |90 − 165| = **75°**.

**Example 40.** At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock do the hands coincide?

- (60/11) × 4 = 240/11 = **21 9/11 minutes past 4**.

**Example 41.** Today is Monday. What day will it be after 61 days?

- 61 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 5. Monday + 5 days = **Saturday**.

**Example 42.** On what day was 2 October 1869 (Mahatma Gandhi's birth)?

- Up to 1800: 1600 years → 0 odd days; 1601–1800 (200 years) → 3. Total 3.
- 1801–1868: 68 years with 17 leap years (1804 … 1868) → 68 + 17 = 85 → 85 mod 7 = 1. Total 4.
- 1869 up to 2 October: Jan 31 + Feb 28 + Mar 31 + Apr 30 + May 31 + Jun 30 + Jul 31 + Aug 31 + Sep 30 + 2 = 275 → 275 mod 7 = 2. Total 6.
- 6 odd days → **Saturday** (0 = Sunday, 6 = Saturday).

## 16. Sets and Venn diagrams

**Read:** [Sets, Venn Diagrams and Logical Numeracy](/interview/quant-aptitude/sets-venn-diagrams-and-logical-numeracy) (diagram questions are in [Analogy, Classification and Venn Diagrams](/interview/bpsc-2027/mental-ability/analogy-classification-and-venn-diagrams)).

**Example 43.** Of 100 students, 60 study Hindi, 50 study English and 20 study both. How many study neither?

- At least one = 60 + 50 − 20 = 90. Neither = **10**.

**Example 44.** n(A) = 20, n(B) = 25, n(C) = 30, n(A∩B) = 8, n(B∩C) = 10, n(A∩C) = 6, n(A∩B∩C) = 3. Find n(A∪B∪C).

- n(A∪B∪C) = 20 + 25 + 30 − 8 − 10 − 6 + 3 = **54**.

## Quick revision

- HCF = lowest common powers; LCM = highest powers; "remainder r in each case" → LCM + r.
- Unit-digit cycles of 2, 3, 7, 8 have length 4.
- Successive % change: a + b + ab/100. "r% more" → "r/(100 + r) % less".
- Alligation: (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper).
- Profit % on cost price; same SP with ±x% → loss x²/100 %.
- SI = PRT/100; CI − SI (2 years) = P(R/100)².
- Work together: ab/(a + b). Pipes: subtract the emptying rate.
- km/h × 5/18 = m/s; average speed for equal distances = 2xy/(x + y).
- Clock angle |30H − 5.5M|; overlap at (60/11)H minutes past H.
- Odd days: ordinary 1, leap 2; 0 = Sunday.
- n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B).

## Key terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| HCF | Highest common factor — the largest number dividing all given numbers |
| LCM | Least common multiple — the smallest number divisible by all given numbers |
| BODMAS | Order of operations: Brackets, Of, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction |
| Cost price (CP) | The price at which something is bought |
| Selling price (SP) | The price at which it is sold |
| Marked price | The price written on the item before discount |
| Principal (P) | The money lent or borrowed |
| Alligation | A quick rule to find the ratio for mixing two prices |
| Odd days | Days left over after complete weeks |
| Union (∪) | Members in at least one of the sets |

## Practice

### Problems

1. Find the LCM of 8, 12 and 18, and the HCF of 36 and 48.
2. Find 15% of 240 + 25% of 120.
3. Two numbers are in the ratio 4 : 7 and their sum is 165. Find the numbers.
4. The average of 6 numbers is 15. When a seventh number is added, the average becomes 16. Find the seventh number.
5. An article is bought for ₹250 and sold for ₹300. Find the profit percentage.
6. Find the simple interest on ₹4,000 at 5% per year for 4 years.
7. A can do a job in 12 days and B in 24 days. How long will they take together?
8. How far will a car go in 2½ hours at 36 km/h?
9. Find the angle between the clock hands at 2:20.
10. What is the unit digit of 3²²?
11. A coin is tossed twice. What is the probability of getting two heads?
12. If 1 October 2026 is a Thursday, what day is 2 October 2026?

### Answers

1. 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3² → LCM = 2³ × 3² = **72**. 36 = 2² × 3², 48 = 2⁴ × 3 → HCF = 2² × 3 = **12**.
2. 36 + 30 = **66**.
3. One part = 165 ÷ 11 = 15 → **60 and 105**.
4. 7 × 16 − 6 × 15 = 112 − 90 = **22**.
5. Profit 50 → 50/250 × 100 = **20%**.
6. 4,000 × 5 × 4 ÷ 100 = **₹800**.
7. 12 × 24 ÷ (12 + 24) = 288 ÷ 36 = **8 days**.
8. 36 × 2.5 = **90 km**.
9. |30 × 2 − 5.5 × 20| = |60 − 110| = **50°**.
10. Cycle of 3: 3, 9, 7, 1. 22 ÷ 4 leaves 2 → **9**.
11. Outcomes HH, HT, TH, TT → 1/4.
12. The next day → **Friday**.
