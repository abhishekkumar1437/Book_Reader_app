# WORLD.md — बहाव (The Drift) (Canonical)

## Vocabulary (use these exact words)
- **बहाव** — the Drift. The sea of abandoned imaginations.
- **मसौदा** (pl. मसौदे) — a draft/world. Frozen at the second of abandonment.
- **लिखित** — what was actually written/imagined about a being or place. Absolute inside its draft.
- **रिक्त** — a gap: something never written. Only gaps can be claimed.
- **दावा** — a claim (see CLAIM_SYSTEM.md).
- **रेखा** — ink-line that appears on the inner left wrist per claim. Count = claims made.
- **सफ़ेदी** — the Blank. White nothing at every draft's unwritten edges. It moves.
- **पाठक** — the Reader. Felt as the pressure of being looked at. Never speaks.
- **संशोधक** — Proofreaders. Grey, numbered, a thin red line across the eyes. They erase contradictions (विरोध) with a strike-through: the thing gets a line through it and dims out.
- **हाशिया** — margin: an edge where two drafts bleed into each other. Scraps float here (half-poems, loading screens, prayers).
- **काटा हुआ दृश्य** — a deleted scene: a tunnel between drafts (and the only way a finished thing re-enters the Drift).
- **पहला पन्ना** — the first page: the exact moment/place a draft was first imagined. Standing on it at the right hour shows the maker's real moment. The way "ऊपर".
- **ऊपर** — up. The real world.
- **पूर्ण** — finished. No gaps. Cannot change, cannot be erased, cannot be eaten — and cannot stay: "पन्ना पलट जाता है" (the page turns; the finished thing is pulled out of the Drift).
- **अधूरा** — unfinished/half-made. Everyone in the Drift.

## How the Drift works (public knowledge among the half-made)
- Each draft keeps the physical laws its maker assumed (gravity, day/night) but only the *described* parts exist. Undescribed interiors are grey-smooth; undescribed streets end in सफ़ेदी.
- Beings have as much face/detail as was written. A boy written in three lines has a blurred face in water. A princess described for pages is sharp as glass.
- Time inside a draft loops on the day of abandonment unless travelers push it forward. Walking "deeper" into a draft (toward its first page) walks backward into its maker's past.
- Drafts bleed at margins. The village's sketched railway "leads nowhere" — in the Drift, a road with no destination leads to the nearest other draft.
- Natives usually cannot leave their draft if their लिखित pins them ("वह गाँव में रहती है"). Beings written with movement ("मोती हर जगह अर के साथ जाता है") can go anywhere. Travelers are rare: those whose text is thin enough, or who tore their pins.

## The Blank (सफ़ेदी) — SECRET MECHANICS (reveal Arc 5)
- It is not punishment. It is **indifference**: a draft closes at the speed nobody holds it in mind. Even its maker, at night, half-remembering, slows it.
- Resolve and attention are literal forces here. Ardh slows the Blank around him because one line still *holds* him: "वह मुझे बचाने आएगा" — a promise a tired 34-year-old never fully let go of.
- The Blank hums very faintly. Those who listen hear nothing. That's the horror.

## The Reader (पाठक) — SECRET (reveal Arc 5–6)
- Not a being. The Reader is the summed **attention** of everyone who ever loved an unfinished thing — including the makers at 3 a.m.
- The "law" (finished → real; unfinished → closed) is the Drift's misreading. The true law: **what is held, stays; what is declared done, goes real.** Both can be true of the same story.
- The Proofreaders are the Reader's reflex for consistency — the nitpicking part of attention. They can learn to *read* (accept) instead of erase. Katha is the first.

## The six drafts (texture bible)
1. **सुनहरी** (village) — Tara, age 12, from her नानी's village. Mustard fields, a well with no bottom, a potter with no hands (never described working), a temple with painted-on doors, one street ending in white. Railway sketched in pencil to the west. Smells of mustard and woodsmoke. Light: always late afternoon.
2. **अधजला शहर** (half-burnt city) — an aspiring writer, 23, three chapters of cyberpunk then quit. Neon that never finishes flickering. Rain described once, so it rains always. Streets with names, no interiors except a bar ("चौथा गियर"), a rooftop, a courier office. The author's **margin notes** float as grey text in alleys — including "ज़ोया बाद में धोखा देगी।" Everyone can read it.
3. **अनंत रण** (eternal war) — a retired soldier, 60s, writing the war novel he never could; died mid-chapter. Dawn camp of forty thousand on a ridge. Enemy never written: the army faces the Blank and calls it "दुश्मन". Bugles at every dawn. Soldiers have whole backstories (he loved them). Mud, tea, letters never sent.
4. **मुस्कान नगर** (smile-town) — a studio's cancelled kids'-cartoon pitch. Rule written on the pitch deck: "यहाँ सब हमेशा मुस्कुराते हैं!" In the Drift the rule rotted: smiles are fixed on; anyone who stops is "corrected" by **बंटी-मास्कॉट** (a round, soft, terrible thing). Candy colors, no shadows, songs on loop. The darkest arc.
5. **हेमगढ़** (the waiting kingdom) — Tara's नाना, 68, bedtime tale for Tara at 10; died before the hero arrived. A golden kingdom under siege by "the long winter"; the princess waits at the tower window for the hero. Written with love and detail: Purna is sharp as glass. The tower has a crack since she tore her ending.
6. **असली दुनिया** — an Indian metro city, present day. Tara 34, senior editor at a publishing house (she finishes other people's books all day). Half-made beings flicker here; most people see "someone". Only Tara sees Ardh fully.

## Also in the Drift
- **बिना-नाम स्टेशन** — a railway station from someone's abandoned horror short. Arc 1 waystation. Where अंत first scents Ardh.
- **काटे हुए दृश्य** of अंत's published novel ("कैवल्य") lie scattered in the Drift like torn pages — his roads.
