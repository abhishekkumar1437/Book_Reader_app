## CORE PREMISE

Every world that a human imagines with real love and then abandons does not vanish. It sinks into **बहाव (the Drift)** — an endless sea of half-finished drafts (**मसौदे**), each frozen at the second its maker gave up.
Half-made people live there. A knight with no face. A city whose streets end in white. A princess written to love a hero who never arrived.

Above the Drift: **पाठक (the Reader)** — a vast, silent attention that only turns pages. One law as the Drift understands it:
- A draft that becomes **पूर्ण (finished)** turns real and leaves the Drift forever.
- A draft that stays unfinished too long is **closed** — **सफ़ेदी (the Blank)** eats inward until nothing remains.

**अर्ध** wakes in a village that stops mid-street. One memory: a girl's voice — *"उसका नाम है अर—"* — then silence. She was twelve. She stopped writing him.

The Drift's only magic: **दावा (Claim)**. An unfinished being can declare what was never written about him, and it becomes true — permanently. Each claim closes a gap. A being with no gaps is finished, and finished things cannot change. Power is self-definition; every use is one step toward becoming fixed forever.

He crosses drafts to climb **ऊपर** — to the real world — to find the girl who made him and ask her to finish him.
On the way, women who were each written for someone else fall in love with the one thing nobody wrote.

## THE CENTRAL QUESTION
**Is it better to be real and done, or unfinished and free?**
The ending refuses the easy answer: *a story can be finished while its people stay open.*

## LOCKED ENDING (do not drift from this)
1. Ardh, Purna and Katha reach the real world through सुनहरी's first page (Arc 6).
2. Tara (34, burnt-out editor) first refuses, then reads the old notebook. Reveal: the back pages hold her **नाना's** handwriting — he wrote Purna's story (हेमगढ़) as a bedtime tale for 10-year-old Tara and died before the hero arrived. Grandfather wrote "a princess who waits for the hero". Granddaughter wrote "a hero who comes". Neither knew. Ardh and Purna were born from the same family.
3. अंत (the finished villain) hunts his own author in the real world to un-write himself. Climax at the author's book launch. Tara must write Ardh's missing parts (his face, as Purna describes it) so he has enough authority to fight a finished being. He wins. अंत hears his author say "मैंने तुझमें सबसे ज़्यादा ख़ुद को रखा था" — and chooses his written death.
4. Tara writes the last line: **"और वे कभी पूरे नहीं हुए।"** — the story is declared finished (real) while its people remain open (free). Ardh and Purna leave the Drift and the real world both — a mustard field that does not end, with no one reading.
5. Tara starts writing again: she finishes सुनहरी. Laali gets a whole life. The village is held, so it stops closing.
6. Katha, the Proofreader who learned to read instead of erase, stays in the Drift as its first reader. Final chapter is in her voice: she was the narrator all along.
7. Last image: Tara, 35, new notebook. First line: "एक लड़का था। उसका नाम था अर्ध।" — she keeps the half-name on purpose.

## TONE RULES
- Warmth > shock. Villains have wounds. Victories cost receipts.
- Romance is a spine, not a side dish: a ♥ beat at least every 3 chapters. Every heroine has her own goal that is not Ardh. No bickering-harem comedy beyond light sparks. Ardh is honest, not oblivious; choosing hurts people, and the story shows the hurt.
- Horror used sparingly, to deepen mystery (मुस्कान नगर arc is the darkest).
- No system windows, no levels, no truck, no "it was a simulation". The Reader never speaks in words.
- Each draft the heroes enter has a different texture/genre: mustard-village (child's sketch), neon half-burnt city (young writer), eternal war (old soldier's novel), smile-town (studio cartoon pitch), waiting-kingdom (grandfather's bedtime tale), real world.
