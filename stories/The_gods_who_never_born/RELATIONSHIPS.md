## ═══ FINAL UPDATE (ch 481–495 — saga complete) ═══
- **अथर्व↔the guests**: the welcome he practiced his whole life became his last act — he died saying it: "आ गए? अच्छा हुआ।" His welcome, their arrival, and his बदली are one scene (490) — the thesis of every relationship in the saga.
- **अथर्व↔देवयानी (d.)**: closed across the door — the rolling-pin-calloused hand that took his was hers; the son who waited a lifetime for no one to have to wait went to his mother like a child from play.
- **अथर्व↔ईशा**: the rooftop pact kept to the letter — she served the dawn thalis and looked up once; years later her own बदली came ON that bright spot. The saga's steadiest marriage ends as it lived: in service, without ceremony.
- **पहला↔the आने वाले**: eldest brother of all readers — formally named by अथर्व's letter, chanted by both worlds; his standing vow: first thali to every new reader, forever. His bond with the house: he entered the main ledger-lines (no longer only margins)।
- **पहला↔the last page**: resolved — it read him, he read it, and in the end they wrote each other: his name is in its signature line ("पहला — और उसके बाद के सब")।
- **दीया↔दस्तक**: the थाली passed ("दी नहीं — तेले पाथ लखी है")। Threshold→road: the craft-line continues past the saga's edge, deliberately unclosed.
- **बाँचू/सीढ़ी↔his victims**: the completed circle — they climbed OUT on him ("हम जिस पर चढ़कर आए, वह तुम थे"), gave him his finished name, and set him his sweet unending count: those who come DOWN the stairs. Victim-executioner → congregation-pujari → family. No forgiveness was ever spoken; something larger was done instead.
- **परछाईं↔everyone**: "पहले एक के साथ चलती थी। अब सबके।" The saga's first companion became its common ground — her line is the children's playground of two worlds.
- **The house↔the reader of the book (495)**: the final relationship, the one all others built — direct, open, unconditional: "थके हो?… कब से तुम्हारी थाली ढकी रखी है।" The reader is handed the pen: the next page is theirs. "ब्याज तुम हो।"
