1

P versus NP

open
Check an answer: n stepsA polynomial method: n²Try every subset: 2ⁿ
Subset sum: given n numbers, is there a subset adding to a target? Handed a subset, you can check it in n additions. Finding one, as far as anyone knows, means trying up to 2ⁿ subsets. P = NP would mean a fast way to find always exists whenever a fast way to check does.

The statement

P is the class of problems a computer can solve in polynomial time. NP is the class whose solutions can be verified in polynomial time. Is P equal to NP?

Why it matters

If P = NP, every lock whose key can be checked quickly can be picked quickly: encryption, scheduling, protein design and most of mathematics itself would become mechanical. Almost everyone expects P ≠ NP, which means some problems are inherently hard.

Status

Open since 1971. Known proof techniques (diagonalisation, natural proofs, algebrisation) have each been shown unable to settle it. Most experts expect it to outlast the century.

2

The Riemann Hypothesis

open
|ζ(σ + it)| for t from 0 to 62, computed live. On the critical line σ = ½ the curve touches zero again and again. Slide off the line and every zero lifts away. The hypothesis says that is true forever, for all of the infinitely many zeros.

The statement

Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) = Σ 1/nˢ has real part exactly ½.

Why it matters

The zeros control how the primes are spread out. Riemann's formula turns each zero into a wave, and adding the waves reproduces the exact count of primes. If a zero strayed off the line, the primes would have a hidden bias nobody has seen.

Status

Open since 1859. The first 10¹³ zeros have been checked by computer and all lie on the line. At least 40 percent of all zeros are proven to. A proof of the rest is the most wanted result in mathematics.

Yang–Mills existence and the mass gap

open
Quark and antiquark, 3D
drag to orbit
Energy stored in the colour field
Unlike electric field lines, the strong force squeezes into a tube whose energy grows with distance. Pull far enough and the tube snaps into a new quark pair rather than letting a quark go free. Physicists see this in every accelerator. The problem asks for a proof, from the equations, that this theory exists and that its lightest particle has positive mass.

The statement

Prove that quantum Yang–Mills theory exists on four-dimensional spacetime as a rigorous mathematical object, and that it has a mass gap: a smallest positive energy for any excitation above the vacuum.

Why it matters

Yang–Mills equations underlie the strong and weak nuclear forces. Physicists use them daily with astonishing accuracy, but the mathematics that justifies the calculations has never been built. A mass gap is why nuclear forces have short range and why gluons, which are massless in the equations, never appear alone.

Status

Open since 1954. Lattice computer simulations show the gap clearly. Rigorous constructions exist only in two and three dimensions. Four is the hard one.

4

Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness

open
Live fluid · drag through it to stir
A 2D solution of the Navier–Stokes equations, computed each frame. In two dimensions solutions are proven to stay smooth forever. In three, nobody knows whether a perfectly smooth flow can concentrate its energy into a point and blow up in finite time.

The statement

For the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations with smooth initial data, prove that smooth solutions exist for all time, or give an example where a solution breaks down.

Why it matters

These equations run weather forecasts, aircraft design, blood-flow models and climate simulations. Engineers trust them completely. Mathematicians cannot yet rule out that the equations themselves are incomplete, with singularities hiding at scales no simulation can reach.

Status

Open since 1845. Smoothness is proven in 2D and for small or short-time 3D flows. In 2024 Terence Tao's programme showed a modified version of the equations can blow up, which sharpens the question without settling it.

5

The Hodge Conjecture

open
A torus and its cycles, 3D
drag to orbit
Topology sees a shape through the loops and surfaces that cannot be shrunk away. On this torus two loops generate all the rest. The conjecture is about higher-dimensional shapes cut out by polynomial equations: it says every such cycle that "looks algebraic" by the calculus test really is built from pieces defined by polynomials.

The statement

On a smooth projective variety, every rational Hodge class is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic subvarieties.

Why it matters

It is a bridge between three languages: topology (shapes), analysis (harmonic forms) and algebra (polynomials). If true, a purely analytic calculation can tell you which geometric objects can be written down with equations. Much of modern number theory, including the Langlands programme, leans on this kind of bridge.

Status

Open since 1950. Proven for curves, surfaces and some special families. Known to be false over the integers, which is why the statement is about rational combinations.

6

The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture

open
Click two orange points to add them.
An elliptic curve with its rational points (fractions for both x and y) found by search. Adding two points: draw the line through them, take the third crossing, reflect it. Repeating this from one point can generate infinitely many. The conjecture predicts, from an analytic number called L(E,1), whether that happens.

The statement

The rank of an elliptic curve (how many independent points you need to generate all its rational points) equals the order of vanishing of its L-function at s = 1.

Why it matters

Finding rational solutions to equations is the oldest problem in number theory, going back to Diophantus. Elliptic curves are the first case where it is hard but structured, and the same curves secure most of today's internet traffic. BSD would turn an impossible search into a computation.

Status

Open since 1965. Proven when the rank is 0 or 1 (Gross, Zagier, Kolyvagin, and others). Nothing is known for rank 2 or higher, and no one can even prove ranks are bounded.

7

The Poincaré Conjecture

solved · Perelman, 2003
Ricci flow, 3D
drag to orbit
Ricci flow lets a shape's curvature spread out like heat. Hamilton proposed it; Perelman proved it can be run through its singularities and that any closed 3-manifold with no holes ends up as a round sphere. The white loop is the test: on a sphere, every loop shrinks to a point.

The statement

Every closed, simply connected three-dimensional manifold is homeomorphic to the three-sphere. In plain terms: if every loop on a finite 3D shape can be shrunk to a point, the shape is a sphere in disguise.

Why it matters

It classifies the simplest possible 3D universes. The method, Ricci flow with surgery, proved the far larger Geometrization Conjecture at the same time, sorting all 3-manifolds into eight geometries.

Status

Proven by Grigori Perelman in three papers posted online in 2002 and 2003. He declined the Fields Medal in 2006 and the million-dollar prize in 2010, saying Richard Hamilton deserved equal credit.
