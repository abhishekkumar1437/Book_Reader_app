What are dark matter and dark energy?

Spiral galaxy, 3D
Drag to orbit · visible stars in warm white
Observed rotation speedExpected from visible matter alone
Stars at the edge of a galaxy orbit far too fast for the matter we can see. Either there is five times more unseen mass, or gravity itself works differently at large scales.

What we know

Galaxies rotate, cluster and bend light as if they hold about five times more mass than their stars and gas. The universe's expansion has been speeding up for roughly 5 billion years.

What we don't

What the extra mass is made of. Forty years of underground detectors have found no particle. What the energy is that pushes space apart.

Leading ideas

Weakly interacting particles, axions, primordial black holes. For dark energy, a cosmological constant, or a field that changes over time. Modified gravity remains a minority view.

How does life begin from non-life?

Warm little pond
monomers drift and bond
Small molecules link into chains. A long enough chain becomes a template that copies itself. Whether this step happens easily, and how, is the gap. Chains of six or more here copy, which real chemistry has never been shown to do without help.

What we know

Amino acids and nucleobases form readily in early-Earth conditions and arrive on meteorites. Life was present on Earth within a few hundred million years of the oceans forming.

What we don't

How molecules first began copying themselves and competing. Whether it took a lucky accident or happens anywhere with water, carbon and energy.

Leading ideas

An RNA world where one molecule did both storage and catalysis. Alkaline hydrothermal vents as natural reactors. Wet-dry cycles on volcanic shores that concentrate and link molecules.

What is consciousness?

Neurons firing · on the left, what instruments record
Every spike on the left can be measured in millivolts. The experience on the right cannot be measured at all. The arrow between them is the explanatory gap.

What we know

Consciousness depends on the brain. Anaesthetics switch it off, damage changes it, and specific patterns of activity in the cortex track whether a person reports seeing something.

What we don't

Why any physical process is accompanied by experience at all. How to tell whether an octopus, a newborn or a language model has it.

Leading ideas

Global workspace theory (broadcast across the brain), integrated information theory (a measure of how unified a system is), and higher-order theories. A 2023 adversarial test left the first two both standing.

How do gravity and quantum mechanics fit together?

Curved spacetime, 3D
Drag to orbit
Relativity says mass bends a smooth sheet. Quantum theory says nothing is smooth at 10-35 metres. Both are right in their own domain, and no one has a theory that holds the sheet and the foam together.

What we know

General relativity passes every test, including gravitational waves and black hole images. Quantum field theory predicts the electron's magnetism to twelve digits.

What we don't

What happens at a black hole's centre or the first instant of the Big Bang, where both theories must apply and their maths produces infinities.

Leading ideas

String theory, loop quantum gravity, and newer approaches such as holography, where gravity in a volume equals a quantum theory on its boundary. None has made a prediction we can test.

Why is there more matter than antimatter?

The first microsecond
Pairs are born, meet and vanish into light. The real surplus was about one in a billion. That leftover is every atom that exists. The slider is exaggerated so you can see a survivor at all.

What we know

Energy makes matter and antimatter in equal amounts. The early universe should have annihilated to pure radiation. Instead, roughly one particle per billion survived.

What we don't

What tipped the balance. The known difference between matter and antimatter (CP violation in quark decays) is about a billion times too small to explain it.

Leading ideas

Extra CP violation among neutrinos, now being hunted at the DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande experiments. Heavy particles decaying asymmetrically in the first instant. Or physics we have not imagined yet.

Frank Drake's 1961 equation. The first three factors are now measured. The last four are guesses, and honest guesses span from a galaxy full of neighbours to a galaxy with only us.

What we know

Most stars have planets. Roughly one in five sun-like stars has a rocky planet in the habitable zone. That is billions of candidates in our galaxy alone.

What we don't

Whether life starts easily, whether intelligence follows, and how long technological societies last. One confirmed detection anywhere would change every one of those numbers.

Leading ideas

Look for biosignature gases in exoplanet atmospheres with JWST and its successors. Search for technosignatures across radio and optical bands. Drill the ice of Europa and Enceladus.

How does the brain store a memory?

One synapse
strength 1.0×
Press repeatedly. The connection strengthens, then slowly fades if left alone.
Long-term potentiation: a synapse used often grows stronger. This is the best-understood mechanism of memory, and it still does not explain how a single morning becomes a recollection you can replay forty years later.

What we know

Memories are held in patterns of strengthened connections across many neurons, with the hippocampus writing new episodes and the cortex slowly taking them over, mostly during sleep.

What we don't

How a specific memory is encoded in those weights, how it can be read back, why some last a lifetime while most fade, and what makes a memory feel like the past rather than a thought.

Leading ideas

Engram cells tagged and reactivated with light in mice. Protein switches that keep a synapse strong. Replay of neural sequences during sleep as the copying process.

8

Why do we sleep?

Sleep stageREM periods
A typical night. Deep sleep comes early, REM grows toward morning. We can map every minute of it, and still cannot say why a fruit fly deprived of this dies.

What we know

Every animal with a nervous system sleeps or does something like it. Sleep consolidates memory, clears metabolic waste from the brain, and resets synapses that grew during the day.

What we don't

Which of those is the reason sleep evolved, why it must involve losing consciousness, and why total deprivation is fatal in rats within weeks.

Leading ideas

The synaptic homeostasis hypothesis (sleep prunes). The glymphatic system (sleep washes). Or sleep as the default state, with waking the costly addition.

What happened before the Big Bang, and what is time?

Sixty orders of magnitude from the Planck time to today. Left of the Planck time our equations give infinities. Whether "before" even means anything there is an open question.

What we know

The universe was hot, dense and expanding 13.8 billion years ago. The cosmic microwave background is a photograph of it at 380,000 years old, and it matches theory to a part in 100,000.

What we don't

What happened in the first 10-43 seconds, whether there was a before, and why time runs one way when the underlying laws do not care.

Leading ideas

Cosmic inflation, a bounce from a previous contracting universe, eternal inflation spawning many universes, or time as an emergent effect of entropy rather than a fundamental thing.

Can we predict a cell from its genes?

Protein chain, 3D
orange: water-avoiding · blue: water-loving · drag to orbit
Shape is now predictable. Function, timing and what the cell does with it are not.
AlphaFold solved the shape of nearly every known protein. The next step, from a genome to a living, dividing cell simulated from first principles, is still out of reach.

What we know

The genetic code, the shape of 200 million proteins, and the wiring of a whole fruit fly brain. The simplest free-living cell has about 470 genes, and we can build it.

What we don't

What about 100 of those 470 genes do. Why one mutation causes disease in one person and not another. How to simulate even a single bacterium from its DNA alone.

Leading ideas

Whole-cell models that track every molecule. Foundation models trained on millions of single-cell profiles. Minimal synthetic cells built gene by gene until the model matches the dish.
