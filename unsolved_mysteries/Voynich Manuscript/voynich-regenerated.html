Transliteration (EVA)

The script

Eighteen glyphs, each drawn from a stroke recipe and jittered by hand-pressure so no two copies match. The labels under each glyph are the EVA letters (European Voynich Alphabet) that scholars use to transcribe the real script.

What this is

Every folio here is generated: the script, the plants, the star wheels, the pools and their bathers. Nothing is copied from the original. Change the seed and the whole codex is rewritten, page for page, into a book that never existed.

It is not a decipherment. The words mean nothing. That is also the leading theory about the real one.

How the words are shaped

Voynichese words have a rigid skeleton: an optional prefix such as qo- or o-, a tall gallows letter or ch/sh, a run of e, then one of a small family of endings like -aiin, -dy, -ol. Paragraphs tend to open with a gallows glyph. Line-final words are short. The generator follows those rules, so the transliteration reads like a page of the Beinecke transcription.

The real book

Beinecke MS 408 at Yale: about 240 vellum pages, carbon-dated to 1404–1438, bought by the Polish book dealer Wilfrid Voynich in 1912. Its sections are usually named herbal, astronomical, biological, cosmological, pharmaceutical and recipes, and this facsimile keeps that order.
